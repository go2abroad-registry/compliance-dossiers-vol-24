Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Service (VCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; national tax e-services portal (VCIR eFiling/Vanuatu eGovernment portal).

E-government filing portal

A Vanuatu International Company is not required to register for income tax because Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax, and an IC is statutorily exempt from direct taxation. To obtain a TIN/VAT registration where the IC makes any VAT-relevant supply or wishes to substantiate zero-rated export treatment, the IC must submit the prescribed VAT registration application to VCIR, accompanied by the IC certificate, certificate of incorporation, VFSC annual exemption receipt, registered agent confirmation, constitutional documents, non-resident ownership and beneficial ownership declaration, and bank account or inward remittance evidence. VCIR verifies that the IC is not carrying on domestic taxable activity in Vanuatu and issues the VAT registration or TIN reference; no check-the-box or income-tax classification filing is made.

VCIR verifies that the IC is not carrying on domestic taxable activity in Vanuatu and issues the VAT registration or TIN reference; no check-the-box or income-tax classification filing is made.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box, hybrid-mismatch, or entity classification election exists under Vanuatu law. The relevant statutory election for an IC is the International Company status election under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222]: while the IC remains exempt from all Vanuatu income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty and other direct taxes, it must not trade with Vanuatu residents, carry on a domestic business, or hold Vanuatu land other than under permitted exceptions. For an ordinary resident company, an election to be treated as a non-resident company under the Companies Act may be available, but that election does not apply to the standard Vanuatu IC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 4,000,000 annual taxable turnover; compulsory VAT registration threshold. Zero-rated supplies are generally included for threshold measurement only to the extent required by the VAT Act and administrative guidance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available to VAT-registered suppliers exporting goods from Vanuatu where the goods are physically exported within the statutory timeframe, and to suppliers providing international or offshore services to a non-resident recipient who benefits entirely outside Vanuatu. A Vanuatu IC supplying payroll SaaS automation and staff-compliance software to non-resident employers qualifies for 0% VAT on licence and subscription fees provided the services are not effectively connected to a Vanuatu establishment, the recipient is not a Vanuatu resident, and the benefit is not consumed in Vanuatu. The IC must evidence the non-resident customer relationship, performance outside Vanuatu, and foreign consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSDEC) or electronic export declaration proof
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or courier airway receipt for physical goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for physical goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank credit advice evidencing export proceeds
  • Duly executed SaaS licence agreement and service statement identifying the non-resident recipient and offshore place of benefit
  • VAT return supported by a zero-rating schedule and export-services or goods register

Vanuatu does not currently mandate a continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing clearance system. VAT-registered suppliers must issue a full VAT invoice within 28 days of the time of supply, retain electronic or paper records, and file VAT returns through VCIR-approved channels. There is no real-time pre-clearance digital stamping or mandatory tax-authority invoice clearance for ordinary domestic or export supplies.

VCIR may retrospectively audit zero-rating claims and, if documentary substantiation is deficient or export and inward remittance evidence is not obtained within the prescribed 180-day window, may assess unpaid VAT at 12.5%, add statutory interest and late-payment penalties, claw back refunds, and impose administrative and civil penalties. Knowingly creating false export documentation or false zero-rating claims may constitute tax and customs fraud, exposing the IC, its directors and its registered agent to criminal fines, imprisonment, loss of IC exemption, and deregistration by VFSC.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

VUV is a managed-float currency anchored by RBV to a trade-weighted basket (predominantly AUD and USD). Current-account payments - software subscriptions, payroll disbursements, professional fees - are freely convertible for residents through authorised dealers using standard declaration forms and genuine invoice support; no pre-approval is needed for arms'-length trade and service remittances. Capital-account flows (portfolio investment, resident lending to non-residents, security issues, outward capital repatriation) are restricted and require RBV prior approval for residents; however, an International Company is statutorily exempt from the Exchange Control Act under section 3(1) of the International Companies Act, and is not treated as a resident for exchange-control purposes. Practical convertibility frictions are therefore bank-side, not RBV-side: authorised dealers demand full UBO disclosure, source-of-funds evidence, and commercial rationale for high-value wires; the offshore VUV market is thin, so conversions above USD 1 million may take 24-48 hours to price. Profit and dividend remittance friction is minimal because Vanuatu levies no income tax, withholding tax, dividend tax, or capital gains tax on an IC; the only effective drags are correspondent-bank compliance screening, FX spread, and uncommitted bank fees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

RBV exchange-control practice (Export of Goods Notices issued under the Exchange Control Act) requires resident exporting companies to: (1) register each physical export through an authorised dealer before shipment (export declaration); (2) receive the full invoice proceeds in the country within six months of export; and (3) offer to the authorised dealer any foreign-currency proceeds in excess of an approved working balance, with no fixed statutory conversion ratio - the operative duty is surrender of surplus foreign currency rather than a percentage-based mandatory conversion. Proceeds from services and intangible exports (software, payroll processing) are treated as current-account receipts and must be repatriated and declared to the banking system within the same six-month accounting horizon; exporters may maintain approved foreign-currency accounts for legitimate working needs. Because the Stage 4 IC is exchange-control-exempt, the legal surrender rules do not apply to its export proceeds; nevertheless, to preserve bank confidence and avoid look-alike pass-through flags, the IC treasury policy will mirror the six-month repatriation discipline, link every inbound USD credit to a numbered SaaS invoice, and keep no surplus USD beyond 120 days of operating expenses in the Vanuatu account.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) as central bank and exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 75] and the National Payment System legislation; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) regulates non-bank finance and administers the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] registry; commercial banks operate as RBV-authorised dealers for foreign exchange.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer-pricing regime and no Local File/Master File or CbCR thresholds exist in Vanuatu law; the arm's length principle is not codified, and no VUV-based documentation trigger applies. Intra-group pricing for the payroll software should nevertheless be documented using OECD principles as defensive evidence for foreign revenue authorities.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to BVI or Cayman. However, an IC must maintain a registered office and a VFSC-licensed registered agent in Vanuatu, keep adequate accounting records, and satisfy the AML/CFT Act and Beneficial Ownership reporting rules. VFSC supervisory practice tests substance through registered-agent KYC, record inspection, and the prohibition on domestic Vanuatu business; a pure shell without a registered agent or records is liable to deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unauthorised domestic (local) business

Statutory sanction: Default fine imposed by the Registrar under the International Companies Act Cap 222; compulsory registration as a local company with loss of IC exemption; the VFSC may strike the company off the International Companies Register.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be jointly and severally liable for the default fine and for the company's debts arising from the unlawful local business; persistent breach permits the Registrar to petition the court for winding-up.
Unlicensed payment services and client-fund custody

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence: fines up to VUV 50,000,000, court-ordered confiscation/forfeiture of funds, refusal or revocation of licences, and voidability of the underlying payment contracts; the entity may be barred from the financial services sector.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly conduct unlicensed money transmission face personal criminal prosecution for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act, personal civil liability to disgorge client funds, travel bans, and imprisonment.
AML/CFT and beneficial-ownership non-compliance

Statutory sanction: VFSC enforcement under the AML/CFT Act: administrative penalties, formal censure, revocation of the registered agent's licence, mandatory reporting to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit, and de-registration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal prosecution for money laundering (predicate offence: fraud or tax evasion upstream), with fines of up to VUV 50,000,000, imprisonment, and asset-freezing orders; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation.
Tax-exempt marketing abuse and foreign tax evasion assistance

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu's Tax Information Exchange Agreements and mutual-assistance legislation permit foreign authorities to obtain books and records through the VFSC; false declarations to the Registrar attract perjury penalties; the Domestic AML regime criminalises facilitation of foreign tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to cross-border civil tax claims, gross-negligence penalties in client jurisdictions, criminal referral through mutual legal assistance, and civil restitution orders enforced by Vanuatu courts.
Late annual filing and strike-off
