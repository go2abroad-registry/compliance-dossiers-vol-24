Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue (VCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Government of Vanuatu; online portal for VAT/e-filing: https://www.finance.gov.vu; customs clearance portal: ASYCUDA World; company registry, licensing and tax-exemption certification: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate an International Company ('IC') under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] through a VFSC-licensed registered agent; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office certificate, memorandum and articles, and the VFSC IC number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the IC is a resident-exempt company: pursuant to s.100 of the International Companies Act, it is wholly exempt from all present and future national, island and municipal taxes, duties and imposts of Vanuatu, including corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, estate or gift tax, and stamp duty on its instruments, shares and transfers. The exemption covers the IC's assets, income, transactions and operations conducted outside Vanuatu; however, the IC remains within the territorial ambit of the Value Added Tax Act No. 7 of 1998 in respect of goods imported into Vanuatu and for any Vanuatu-sourced supply made in breach of its offshore operating restriction.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime. Available statutory elections: (i) voluntary VAT registration under s.10 of the VAT Act where taxable supplies are expected to reach 80% of the compulsory threshold; (ii) an IC may elect under s.8(2) of the International Companies Act to be bound by the Companies Act [Cap. 191] and lose its exempt status, thereby entering the ordinary municipal business-licence and VAT framework; (iii) an IC may adopt optional by-law provisions (e.g., Part 11 arrangements for fund administration) without altering its exempt tax classification; and (iv) for cross-border purposes, the VFSC will issue a certificate of tax residence/ exemption for use by the foreign head-office or regulatory counterparties, without creating any domestic tax liability.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 2,500,000 (two million five hundred thousand vatu) of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; because financial supplies (principal dealing in securities, forex and derivatives, clearing and market-making) are exempt supplies under the VAT Act, a proprietary HFT IC has no mandatory VAT registration duty unless it procures or supplies non-exempt goods/services locally.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero per cent VAT applies only to: (a) the export of goods from Vanuatu, including the re-export of HFT servers, network equipment, and financial data hardware, provided the goods physically leave Vanuatu and the supplier retains documentary proof of export; and (b) 'international services' as narrowly defined — services supplied to a non-resident for the direct benefit of that non-resident while outside Vanuatu, or services physically performed on goods to be exported. Proprietary trading gains, market-making financial services, and exchange-clearing services supplied to a foreign counterparty are exempt supplies by nature (not zero-rated), so they are outside the scope of Vanuatu VAT and the IC cannot charge output VAT or recover input VAT on related costs.

  • ASYCUDA World Electronic Customs Export Declaration (EX-DEC) bearing the unique EDN number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the exporter as the IC and discharge port outside Vanuatu
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list cross-referenced to the export declaration
  • Original VAT tax invoice supporting the local purchase or importation of the exported equipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the 180-day period
  • Customs Export Permit and/or VFSC no-objection for controlled or dual-use goods, where applicable

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping or API clearance regime is in force. VAT invoices must be issued sequentially, in paper or unsecured PDF, containing the prescribed particulars (seller name/address, VAT ID, invoice number, date of supply, description, consideration and tax amount). VAT returns and payments are lodged electronically through the VCIR e-file portal; import and export declarations are processed and cleared exclusively through ASYCUDA World with no separate government e-invoicing API.

The VCIR Commissioner may audit any VAT refund or zero-rating claim for up to five years from the relevant tax period. Where zero-rating or refund is claimed without genuine export evidence, the disputed VAT is clawed back with a shortfall penalty of 200% of the tax intentionally evaded under s.70 of the VAT Act, plus late-payment penalties at 30% per annum under s.69; knowingly submitting false export declarations, forged bills of lading or fictitious inward remittance certificates is an offence punishable by a fine not exceeding VUV 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to two years under s.78. In addition, a fraudulent export claim constitutes carrying on business in Vanuatu contrary to s.6 of the International Companies Act, empowering the VFSC to strike the IC off the register and revoke its statutory tax exemption, after which the company becomes liable to municipal licence fees and standard 12.5% VAT on any local supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) is partially convertible: current-account trade and service payments are liberalized under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 47] and can be approved at Authorized Dealer level, while capital-account transactions require RBV exchange-control no-objection clearance. The RBV operates a tightly managed de facto basket peg (USD, EUR, AUD, NZD, JPY) with a thin domestic money market; there is no deep onshore interbank market for large VUV notional. Non-resident International Companies may hold and operate foreign-currency (USD/EUR/AUD) accounts and repatriate profits offshore without VUV conversion, provided funds derive from foreign-source trading activity and are supported by audited financial statements and a reporting accountant certificate. VUV-denominated domestic receipts are subject to authorized-dealer conversion and RBV documentary review. Repatriation friction arises chiefly from KYC/AML substantiation rather than statutory remittance taxes; there is no capital gains tax or dividend withholding tax for non-resident IC shareholders. Practical treasury advice: keep more than 95% of operating liquidity in USD in offshore-compatible correspondent accounts and use VUV solely for domestic operating expenses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory rule: resident exporters must offer all export proceeds to an RBV Authorized Dealer within 3 months of shipment or contractual due date and surrender 100% of any VUV proceeds through the banking system; retention in foreign currency is allowed only with a General Exchange Control Authority license or in approved Foreign Currency Accounts. For this non-resident International Company conducting offshore proprietary algorithmic trading, proprietary trading capital and HFT settlement proceeds are not Vanuatu export proceeds and are not subject to the 3-month surrender or conversion mandate; they may be held and settled freely in USD/EUR/AUD. To preclude misclassification, the IC must declare non-resident exchange-control status to its Authorized Dealer, document all trading revenues as foreign-source, and submit an annual exchange-control return through the VFSC-registered agent. Any Vanuatu-source domestic receipts must be converted and surrendered onshore under the standard timeline.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) - Central Bank and Exchange Control Authority; Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) - Financial Services Licensing and AML/CFT Supervisor

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Assessed as LOW-MEDIUM: viable only as an operational payout channel, not as a banking or clearing rail. Vanuatu has no domestic EMI licensing or passporting regime for foreign digital EMIs; the VFSC does not recognize Wise, Payoneer or Airwallex e-money accounts as regulated bank deposits. Such EMIs may be used for non-core disbursements if licensed in their home jurisdiction and connected to the IC underlying bank account, but the primary USD treasury, HFT margin and counterparty settlement must run through SWIFT/Fedwire bank rails. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot hold client funds as deposits under Vanuatu law, cannot provide VUV conversion without an Authorized Dealer license, and cannot substitute for exchange-control declaration. Rating: LOW for capital custody and settlement finality; MEDIUM for operational payout support.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: route shareholder and investor capital exclusively via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers to the IC foreign-currency account at an RBV Authorized Dealer (ANZ or BSP), using explicit purpose codes: 2330 (funds received from parent - capital increase) for equity injections and 2420 (loans from affiliates) for shareholder debt. Complete the VFSC inward investment declaration and obtain bank exchange-control inward registration before value date. Deliver a funding packet comprising incorporation certificate, beneficial-ownership register, board resolution authorizing account and signatories, share allotment document or promissory note, and source-of-funds evidence. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% - Vanuatu does not levy corporate income tax; International Companies (ICs) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1991 (IC Act, Cap 222) are statutorily exempt from all income, profit, capital gains, and distribution taxes.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Vanuatu imposes no dividend withholding tax; an International Company may distribute profits, dividends, and capital to non-resident shareholders free of any Vanuatu withholding, income, or remittance tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None - Vanuatu has no transfer pricing legislation, no local file/master file thresholds, and no statutory contemporaneous documentation requirement. The arm's length principle is not codified in Vanuatu domestic law; the practical reference point is the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines where a foreign tax administration asserts a PE, treaty, or anti-avoidance adjustment.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2019 applies to Vanuatu International Companies and foreign-owned entities conducting 'relevant activities'. Proprietary algorithmic high-frequency trading for the IC's own account is generally not a listed relevant activity; however, related intra-group treasury support, financing of trading inventory, or holding of subsidiary trading entities may be reclassified as financing-and-leasing or holding-business, triggering a requirement to demonstrate: (i) direction and management in Vanuatu; (ii) adequate local employees and premises; (iii) local operating expenditure; and (iv) substantive income-generating activity proportionate to the trading business.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal Code (Cap 153) false-declaration and fraud offences: fines of up to VUV 500,000 and imprisonment up to seven years for natural persons; the VFSC may strike off the IC and reverse its tax exemption with retroactive effect; the foreign jurisdiction will reassess the hidden profits with penalties and surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false declarations are personally liable for restitution, civil damages, and criminal penalties; a foreign revenue court will pierce the corporate veil under fraud or sham doctrines; international travel bans and cross-border asset freezing may issue under FATF-linked mutual legal assistance.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Act 2019 fines of up to VUV 5,000,000 plus daily penalties of VUV 100,000; suspension or surrender of the VFSC registration; adverse classification by the FATF/Global Forum leading to international countermeasures against the entity.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing arises where directors transact the IC's business with intent to defraud creditors or tax authorities; directors may be ordered to make personal restitution of all foreign tax evaded, disqualified from acting as Vanuatu company officers, and, in egregious cases, extradited from Vanuatu under the Extradition Act.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2016: corporate fines up to VUV 50,000,000 and imprisonment of individuals up to 15 years; forfeiture of all proceeds; VFSC licence revocation and deregistration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal money-laundering charges, personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and prohibition from holding VFSC-regulated positions; the corporate veil is pierced to recover fraudulently moved trading assets held in the names of nominees or related trusts.
Unlicensed Financial Dealing

Statutory sanction: VFSC licensing statutes impose administrative fines, suspension of trading permissions, and criminal referral; foreign trading venues may terminate; OFAC and FINRA enforcement may follow for abusive trading through jurisdictions with opaque ownership.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior traders are personally liable for market-abuse penalties, disgorgement of profits, and extradition requests from the affected exchange jurisdictions; fraudulent trading while the IC was insolvent renders directors personally liable for the company's entire trading debts.
Sanctions Evasion and Integrity of Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Sanctions-infringement offences carry US civil penalties of up to USD 20,000,000 per violation, criminal fines, denial of clearing access, and inclusion of the entity on the OFAC SDN list; Vanuatu may seize the local assets upon a mutual legal assistance request.

Director personal liability: Directors face OFAC designation, visa bans, secondary sanctions, and criminal prosecution in the jurisdiction of the payment rail; the corporate veil is disregarded as the IC is treated as a mere instrumentality of the sanctioned principal, resulting in personal liability for all cleared transaction values.
