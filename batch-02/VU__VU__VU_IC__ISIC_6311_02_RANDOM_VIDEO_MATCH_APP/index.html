UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Vanuatu AML/CFT and UBO obligations are governed by: (i) the Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing Act No. 23 of 2016, as amended, which defines a 'beneficial owner' as any natural person who ultimately owns or controls 25% or more of the shares, voting rights, or ownership interest, or who otherwise exercises control over the legal person; (ii) the International Companies Act No. 32 of 1992 [Cap 222], as amended, which requires every International Company to maintain a VFSC-licensed registered agent and registered office in Vanuatu and to keep accurate registers of directors, shareholders, and beneficial owners; (iii) the Companies Act [Cap 191]; (iv) the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission Act No. 10 of 2016; and (v) directives and guidance issued by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission ('VFSC') and the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit ('VFIU'). The AML/TF Act imposes mandatory customer due diligence, beneficial-ownership verification, suspicious-transaction reporting, and record-retention duties on licensed agents and reporting entities; failure to identify a natural-person UBO is a mandatory ground for refusal of the client relationship.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Acceptable. An International Company operating a remote video-matching and cross-border social telephony business may satisfy its statutory registered-office obligation solely through the premises of a VFSC-licensed registered agent in Vanuatu; no employees, equipment, or servers need be located at the registered address. Shared-office, virtual-office, and professional-mailbox configurations are therefore permissible at the pre-incorporation stage, provided the registered agent maintains custody of the statutory registers, UBO records, and service-of-process facilities. This arrangement does not, by itself, satisfy any future economic-substance or licensing test if the company later applies for a VFSC financial or securities dealing licence.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Administration (VRCA), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Republic of Vanuatu; International Company (IC) statutory registration is administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC). Tax e-portals: VRCA VAT e-filing and ASYCUDA World customs clearance, accessible via the national Government portal at www.gov.vu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company is first incorporated with the VFSC, obtaining a VFSC Registry Number and Certificate of Incorporation. If the IC makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu or imports commercial goods, the foreign entity must file VAT Form 1 (Application for Registration) with VRCA together with certified constitutional documents, proof of physical address, and officer/shareholder particulars; VRCA issues a unique 8-digit VAT identification number endorsed on the VAT registration certificate, typically within 10 business days after verification. For customs transactions, a trader code is obtained electronically through ASYCUDA World upon presentation of the VAT certificate and, where onshore activity exists, a valid Business Licence. Where the IC conducts wholly offshore business, no local business licence or VAT ID is required; a VFSC Registry tax-status letter is used to evidence statutory exemption.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company is first incorporated with the VFSC, obtaining a VFSC Registry Number and Certificate of Incorporation. If the IC makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu or imports commercial goods, the foreign entity must file VAT Form 1 (Application for Registration) with VRCA together with certified constitutional documents, proof of physical address, and officer/shareholder particulars; VRCA issues a unique 8-digit VAT identification number endorsed on the VAT registration certificate, typically within 10 business days after verification. For customs transactions, a trader code is obtained electronically through ASYCUDA World upon presentation of the VAT certificate and, where onshore activity exists, a valid Business Licence. Where the IC conducts wholly offshore business, no local business licence or VAT ID is required; a VFSC Registry tax-status letter is used to evidence statutory exemption.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu levies no income, capital gains, withholding, or stamp tax, so no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections exist under local law. Available elections include: (1) voluntary VAT registration and group VAT registration under Sections 8-10 of the Value Added Tax Act [CAP 250], allowing input tax recovery; (2) reclassification election under Sections 14-15 of the International Companies Act [CAP 222] to convert an IC into a domestic company, or vice versa, with VFSC and Ministerial consent; (3) redomiciliation election enabling a foreign-incorporated company to transfer its registration into the VFSC registry as an International Company; and (4) election to apply zero-rating to qualifying export contracts by submitting a written export-determination schedule at the time of supply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration is triggered where taxable turnover exceeds VT 4,000,000 (approximately USD 33,000) in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted below that threshold. Non-resident suppliers of remote and digital services are within the Vanuatu VAT net only where the supply is made for the direct benefit of a person in Vanuatu, and registration is required if the same VT 4,000,000 threshold is met through Vanuatu-sourced supplies; no separate low-value digital-services registration threshold currently exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered person may apply 0% VAT to: (a) goods physically exported from Vanuatu under a customs export declaration, ordinarily removed within 30 days of supply or within a 60-day extended period approved by VRCA; (b) services supplied for the direct and sole benefit of a non-resident recipient located outside Vanuatu, including cross-border social-telephony and video-matching platform services where no consumer in Vanuatu receives the benefit; and (c) supplies to exempt international organisations. The zero-rating claim requires contemporaneous evidence that the goods left Vanuatu or that the service benefit was received offshore, retained in the taxpayer's VAT working file.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) and VRCA/ASYCUDA clearance endorsement evidencing physical export from Vanuatu
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to a foreign destination with the consignee located outside Vanuatu
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency credit advice from a licensed Vanuatu bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds from overseas
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration particulars
  • Executed export-services contract, platform usage report for the offshore jurisdiction, and non-resident customer attestation for zero-rated services

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API validation, or digital stamping regime is currently in force. VAT-registered persons must issue an original tax invoice within 28 days of supply for all taxable supplies exceeding VT 1,000, showing sequential numbering, the VAT registration number, a description of the supply, and the 12.5% tax split; invoices may be manual, printed, or generated through the taxpayer's own ERP system. Customs export and import declarations, however, are cleared in real time through ASYCUDA World, which requires electronic lodgement and digital risk-based validation before goods are released.

VRCA may audit zero-rated export supplies within the 5-year statutory assessment window, extended to 7 years where returns were not filed; exporters must produce the full documentary bundle, including bank credit evidence and customs departure records. Misdeclared or fraudulent zero-rating entails the following: additional tax of up to 25% of the short-paid VAT for careless errors, rising to 100% for intentional evasion; statutory interest on refunded or clawed-back amounts; cancellation or retrospective de-registration with recovery of all previously refunded input VAT as a debt due; and criminal liability for tax fraud under Section 65 of the VAT Act, carrying a fine of up to VT 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. Additionally, VFSC may strike off or impose monetary penalties on an International Company for false declarations, without prejudice to money-laundering charges under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu's currency, the vatu (VUV), is fully convertible for current-account purposes; Vanuatu is an IMF Article VIII-member jurisdiction and maintains no material exchange restrictions on resident current payments. Capital-account transfers for residents require RBV-approved dealer filings. International Companies are statutorily non-resident and fall outside the resident exchange-control perimeter, meaning inward equity contributions, trade payments, and profit remittances can circulate freely in VUV or foreign-currency accounts without RBV pre-approval, provided the company remains non-resident and does not transact with Vanuatu residents in vatu.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters are obliged to surrender export proceeds through an RBV-Authorized Dealer within 21 days of receipt, with a mandatory conversion ratio of 100% into VUV (subject to RBV sector waivers). Non-resident International Companies are exempt from the surrender regime; however, commercial banks will require a sworn declaration of non-residency and the underlying commercial invoices to prevent the surrender obligation from being mechanically applied to operating inflows.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) as the central monetary and exchange-control authority, acting under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 61] and the Financial Institutions Act; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) regulates International Companies separately for corporate registry and AML/CFT purposes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility rating. Global Tier-1 digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Stripe Treasury) systematically decline VU-registered International Companies due to the residual FATF/EU AML risk memory of Vanuatu and the cross-border social-telephony product profile. Vanuatu has no domestically recognized EMI license class, so offshore EMIs can only be used as settlement wallets rather than as primary clearing accounts; a compliant structure is a BSP/ANZ operational bank account feeding a USD settlement wallet provided by a UK/EEA EMI licensed in a FATF-compliant state, with clear segregation of client funds and no crypto on/off ramps.

Equity injection must be driven by an MT103 SWIFT wire from the ultimate parent or investor to a VUV/USD-denominated corporate account at BSP/ANZ in Port Vila with beneficiary field referencing the VFSC registration number, and SWIFT field 77B stating purpose code EQUI (equity injection).
