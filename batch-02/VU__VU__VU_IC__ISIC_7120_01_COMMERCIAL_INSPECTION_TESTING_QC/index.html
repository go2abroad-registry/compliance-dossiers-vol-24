Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official portal and e-services: www.dcir.gov.vu; corporate registry and IC licensing: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) www.vfsc.vu

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate International Company (IC) with VFSC and obtain Certificate of Incorporation; IC is statutorily exempt from all income, withholding, capital gains, and stamp taxes. Step 2 — Determine if the IC makes taxable supplies within Vanuatu: if annual taxable turnover of local supplies exceeds VT 10,000,000, apply to DCIR for VAT registration on Form VAT-01 with incorporation certificate, business license, director/beneficial owner identification, and declared local business premises. Step 3 — For a purely offshore service model (non-resident clients, no local supplies), no VAT/TIN registration is statutorily required; a Certificate of Incorporation plus VFSC Good Standing certificate serves as tax identity for banking and counterparty due diligence. Step 4 — Issue invoices referencing the IC registration number; no separate tax file number (TFN/TIN) is issued if no VAT registration is triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: International Companies are exempt from income tax, corporation tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax; no Tax_Exempt_Zone certificate required — statutory exemption under Companies and International Business Companies Act 2012; VAT is the only material indirect tax and applies only on Vanuatu-sourced taxable supplies above the registration threshold.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election regime exists in Vanuatu. No hybrid mismatch or look-through elections apply because the IC is not subject to income tax at any jurisdictional level. Voluntary VAT registration below the VT 10,000,000 threshold is available at the taxpayer's option, and a VAT registration can be cancelled if taxable supplies cease or fall permanently below the threshold; no other classification elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 annual taxable turnover of supplies made within Vanuatu (approximately USD 83,000); foreign-sourced offshore service fees from non-resident principals do not count toward the threshold, and zero-rated export supplies are excluded from the threshold computation

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under the VAT Act where the supplier is a VAT registered person and (a) the goods are exported from Vanuatu in accordance with Customs export procedures, or (b) the services are supplied to a person who is not resident in Vanuatu and are performed wholly or principally outside Vanuatu, or (c) the services are directly related to exported goods. Pre-shipment factory inspection, testing, and quality control services performed in a foreign country (e.g., at client factories in China, India, or Vietnam) for offshore principals qualify for zero rating, subject to contemporaneously documented supply contracts, inspection reports, and foreign-invoice evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Vanuatu Customs Entry Number) for any physical goods, samples, or calibration equipment temporarily exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods out of Vanuatu (where applicable)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Service Agreement / Inspection & Testing Contract with the non-resident client specifying offshore performance
  • Detailed Inspection Report / Test Certificate / Quality Audit Report identifying the foreign manufacturing site
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT advice) or interbank transfer evidence showing foreign-source payment received by the IC

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime is in force in Vanuatu as of the current effective date. VAT registered entities must issue sequential tax invoices containing full seller/buyer identification, tax invoice number, date, description, quantity, price, VAT amount, and the words 'Tax Invoice'. Paper, PDF, and standard accounting software invoices are acceptable; DCIR retains audit rights to require electronic submission of invoice data upon inspection.

DCIR audit triggers include invalid zero-rating documentation, unsupported invoices, and mismatched foreign remittance records. Penalties under the VAT Act [CAP 296]: late payment penalty of 50% of the unpaid tax, additional 25% penalty where tax remains unpaid more than 30 days after demand, and monthly interest at 1.5% on arrears; mis-declaring output tax as zero rated to obtain a tax benefit constitutes a serious offence punishable by fines up to VT 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 3 years. The Commissioner can issue retrospective assessments within 5 years of the relevant supply and claw back credited input VAT with penalty and interest on a joint and several basis for officers of the IC.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains an open, freely convertible current account without exchange control legislation. Payments and transfers for current international transactions are unrestricted, and the Vatu (VUV) is convertible via authorised commercial banks. Capital account transactions are equally open: foreign investors and International Companies may inject capital and repatriate profits, dividends, and loan service payments without prior RBV approval. The only practical compliance hurdles are anti-money laundering due diligence expectations at the commercial bank level and correspondent bank screening for USD clearing. Profit remittances are not subject to a statutory tax or additional permit, provided the underlying source of funds is verified and appropriate supporting documentation is presented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory export proceeds surrender requirement in Vanuatu. No statutory timeline exists for repatriation of export receipts, and there is no prescribed conversion ratio into VUV. Exporters and service providers, including inspection and testing agencies, may retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts at domestic commercial banks or convert to VUV at prevailing market rates as operational preference dictates.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low–Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms do not hold any Vanuatu banking licence and cannot access the VUV clearing or RBV settlement infrastructure. The VFSC does not maintain an electronic-money institution licensing category; an offshore EMI operating without a local banking licence would likely breach the Financial Transactions Reporting Act or the Banks Act if it held client funds locally. An EMI may be used as a secondary USD/EUR corridor for trade disbursements, but it cannot serve as the primary treasury rail because it lacks Vanuatu IBAN/VUV capability and presents elevated correspondent de-risking risk. Vanuatu-registered International Companies should therefore use a tier-1 commercial bank as the base clearing nexus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are cleared through the Vanuatu commercial bank's correspondent network, typically in USD via major US clearing banks. The routing protocol requires the remitter to use SWIFT MT103 with a precise purpose narrative, for example 'Equity Capital Injection for International Company VU_IC – ISIC 7120 Inspection Services' or 'Shareholder Loan for Working Capital – Factory Inspection Agency'. The bank will verify the beneficiary name against the VFSC certificate of incorporation and the registered agent's records. Before settlement, the client must provide an account-opening letter, a board resolution authorising the account, a copy of the inward remittance advice, and a breakdown of the capital injection. Amounts above VUV 5,000,000 or USD 50,000 trigger enhanced due diligence, including a source-of-funds questionnaire. The bank may obtain the SWIFT GPI tracking reference and screen all parties against UN, OFAC, or EU sanctions lists.Outbound disbursements from the inspection and quality-control entity are routed through SWIFT MT103 for fee settlements to overseas calibration laboratories, technical consultants, and trade settlement counterparties. For larger transactional activity, a Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary LC) can be opened via the Vanuatu bank and issued through its correspondent banker. The preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 because it is efficient for service-based fees; an irrevocable documentary LC is used only when the inspection agency is effecting payment for verified physical goods, and it must be supported by shipping documents, inspection certificates, and a conformity assessment report. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax; an International Company (VU_IC) is expressly exempt from all Vanuatu taxes, duties, fees and levies on income, assets, transactions and operations under section 92 of the International Companies Act [Cap.222].

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — no withholding tax is imposed on dividends declared by a Vanuatu International Company to non-resident shareholders, irrespective of the shareholder's residence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None — Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing legislation, no Local File / Master File thresholds, no APA regime, and no vatu-denominated documentation requirement because no income tax base exists.

Economic substance scope: No Vanuatu Economic Substance Regulations apply; pure ICs face no local substantive quota, headcount, or expenditure thresholds. VFSC nevertheless requires a licensed registered agent, registered office, statutory registers, and a compliant Beneficial Ownership Register. Where the IC invoices factory-inspection work performed in the United States, China, or the EU, foreign CFC / anti-conduit / permanent-establishment rules may recharacterise the structure; the inspection contracts, pricing policies, and management decisions must therefore evidence real offshore commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Foreign tax evasion / conduit misuse

Statutory sanction: No domestic Vanuatu income-tax sanction exists; however, VFSC may strike off the IC for false declarations, and Vanuatu may exchange information under its TIEAs. The foreign jurisdiction may impose CFC attribution, permanent-establishment reclassification, withholding assessments, and penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false 'no local business' declarations face fines up to VT 500,000 and/or 12 months imprisonment; under the Proceeds of Crime Act, courts may issue restraint orders, travel bans, and confiscation orders over directors' assets connected to fraudulent conduct.
Ultra vires local activity (exemption forfeiture)

Statutory sanction: Immediate loss of the section 92 tax exemption; retroactive assessment of all Vanuatu taxes and duties including VAT on local supplies; forced deregistration; and a fine not exceeding VT 1,000,000 under the International Companies Act for conducting prohibited local business.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly liable for debts and distributions arising from the prohibited local business; the Registrar may disqualify them from acting as directors of any Vanuatu company for up to 5 years.
Beneficial ownership register non-compliance (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty up to VT 5,000,000 on the IC, plus daily default fines of VT 10,000 per day; repeated non-compliance results in automatic dissolution and VFSC information-sharing with foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Officers face fines up to VT 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years for knowingly submitting false beneficial-ownership particulars; travel bans may be imposed during AML investigations.
VAT evasion and zero-rating abuse

Statutory sanction: Evasion penalty up to 200% of the tax evaded; interest at 3% per month on arrears; refund clawback; criminal fine up to VT 1,000,000 or imprisonment up to 7 years for intentional fraud.

Director personal liability: The Commissioner may disregard the corporate façade and recover unpaid VAT directly from directors; directors who sign fraudulent VAT returns face criminal prosecution and confiscation of personal assets.
Shell status and economic substance attack abroad

Statutory sanction: No domestic Vanuatu penalty; the source jurisdiction may recharacterise the IC as fiscally transparent, apply transfer-pricing adjustments with penalty rates up to 40%, and list the entity for enhanced scrutiny in foreign tax-risk registries.

Director personal liability: Foreign courts may treat the directors as de facto directors of a phantom company and hold them personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes; entry bans and extradition exposure arise where withheld employee taxes or social contributions were diverted through the structure.
