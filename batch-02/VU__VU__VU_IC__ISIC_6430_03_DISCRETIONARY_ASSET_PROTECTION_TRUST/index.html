Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The International Companies Act 1992 prescribes no minimum paid-up capital for an International Company limited by shares. However, the VFSC Trust Company Licence requires the applicant to demonstrate adequate unimpaired net assets (typically at least VT 2,000,000 or an equivalent bank guarantee acceptable to the VFSC), held in a segregated fiduciary escrow account at a VFSC-licensed bank before fiduciary administration of discretionary asset-protection spendthrift trusts may commence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

(a) Voluntary VAT registration election under s.11(3) of the Value Added Tax Act No. 12 of 1998 for persons below the VT 5,000,000 threshold; (b) Election under VAT Act s.7(2) to zero-rate cross-border supplies of fiduciary services to non-residents, subject to contemporaneous documentary substantiation; (c) IC Act s.6 statutory exemption from all present and future Vanuatu taxes, stamp duties, and exchange-control regulations, with a 20-year non-impairment guarantee from incorporation date; no check-the-box election exists because the IC tax-exempt status is automatic and non-elective; hybrid-mismatch elections are inapplicable as Vanuatu has no income tax regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 5,000,000 (approximately USD 42,000) of taxable aggregate turnover in any consecutive 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s.7 of the VAT Act 1998 to: (a) goods exported from Vanuatu; (b) services supplied to a non-resident where the benefit is consumed wholly outside Vanuatu, including cross-border fiduciary administration of irrevocable offshore spendthrift trusts, trust accounting, and asset-protection structuring performed from Vanuatu; and (c) international transport services. The IC must maintain contemporaneous evidence showing the recipient is a non-resident, the services are not consumed in Vanuatu, and the consideration is received from outside the Vanuatu VAT jurisdiction; input VAT attributable to the zero-rated supply is refundable under the normal VAT refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged in ASYCUDA World (for physical goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing export movement
  • Commercial Invoice describing the zero-rated fiduciary supply
  • Executed Fiduciary Administration Agreement with the non-resident trust settlor or trustee
  • Certified copy of the Discretionary Asset Protection Trust Deed evidencing governing law and situs
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT confirmation of overseas fee payment
  • Non-resident status declaration from the counterparty beneficiary

Vanuatu is in phased deployment of electronic fiscal devices (EFDs) under the VAT (Electronic Fiscal Devices) Regulations 2022; VAT-registered taxpayers must use certified EFD software and transmit transaction data to the Vanuatu Revenue Management System (VRMS); full real-time fiscal clearance and mandatory digital stamping are not yet universally operational; ASYCUDA World is used for customs declarations and import tax stamping; an IC with no Vanuatu taxable supplies and no VAT registration has no e-invoicing transmission obligation.

No mandatory FX repatriation deadline applies because ICs are statutorily exempt from the Vanuatu Exchange Control Act under s.6 of the International Companies Act 1994; notwithstanding, abuse of export zero-rating is subject to the VAT Act penalty regime: civil penalty up to 200% of the tax shortfall on a fraudulent claim, interest at the prescribed ministerial rate on all retrospective assessments, and a 5-year assessment period (extended to 7 years for fraud or wilful default); criminal conviction for fraudulent export VAT claims may result in a fine not exceeding VT 5,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years, plus forfeiture of wrongly refunded input credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) operates under a conventional pegged arrangement against an undisclosed foreign-currency composite, with the RBV managing convertibility. Vanuatu maintains full current-account convertibility and a largely liberalized capital account: since the Exchange Control (Amendment) Act 2006, prior ministerial consent for capital inflows, income remittances, and repatriations has been abolished. Non-resident beneficiaries may freely receive trust distributions without FX quota or approval. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are unrestricted for foreign parties. Practical constraints are not legal but operational: (i) banks must report balance-of-payments statistics for any single transfer above VUV 10 million (approximately USD 85,000); (ii) cash transactions above VUV 1 million trigger mandatory currency transaction reports; (iii) USD and other foreign-currency settlements rely on correspondent banks subject to OFAC screening; and (iv) FATF increased monitoring (grey-list, June 2024) has intensified enhanced due diligence on all VU legal entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirement applies to foreign-currency receipts; the 2006 amendments abolished all statutory direction to convert export proceeds into VUV and likewise removed any deadline for repatriation of such proceeds. For the subject discretionary trust (which generates no trading export revenue), any incidental foreign-currency receipts may be held indefinitely in Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) at licensed RBV-regulated banks, subject only to record retention, OFAC/RBV screening, and aggregate reporting thresholds. Mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [CAP 322], is the sole authority for exchange control administration, monetary policy, and prudential licensing of all deposit-taking financial institutions. Exchange control is formally governed by the Exchange Control Act [CAP 71] as substantially liberalized by the Exchange Control (Amendment) Act 2006. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) separately supervises trust and company service providers (including the licensed trustee entity of this discretionary asset protection trust) under the Trust Companies Act [CAP 60] and the International Companies Act [CAP 222], while the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) monitors AML/CFT reporting under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 13 of 2014.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Vanuatu has no domestic EMI licensing framework for digital-only payment institutions, and leading offshore EMIs and FX-scale platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury, Revolut Business, Payoneer) routinely decline Vanuatu-domiciled International Companies due to the FATF grey-listing of June 2024, perceived high money-laundering risk of the trust sector, and correspondent-bank de-risking. A licensed VU trustee should treat EMIs only as downstream payment beneficiaries, never as the primary account bank. If digital rails are mandatory for operations, a parallel operating entity in an acceptable clearing jurisdiction should be used solely for treasury flows, with the VU IC remaining the legal owner of the trust assets.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Settlement funding is injected via SWIFT MT103 from the settlor's account to the trustee's VUV or USD account at a licensed Vanuatu bank. The remittance narrative must read 'Settlement of Discretionary Trust (VU IC) – Capital Contribution', with the trust name and settlement deed number. Vanuatu does not operate an official purpose-code registry; the remitter's bank should classify the flow under an international convention code such as 'Gift between family members' or 'Capital transfer – other private' while the receiving bank maps it to BoP 'Capital account – other private transfers' for RBV statistics. Acceptable verification for the receiving bank comprises the notarized settlement deed, trustee resolution, source-of-funds pack, and For full traceability the injected funds must originate from the settlor's personal or wholly-owned treasury account, never from unrelated third parties, and any inbound amount above VUV 10 million will trigger BoP reporting and enhanced EDD verification.Beneficiary distributions are cleared through SWIFT MT103 wires in USD, AUD, EUR, or GBP from the trust's Vanuatu bank account to the beneficiary's global bank. Each wire is preceded by (i) a trustee distribution resolution naming the beneficiary and the power exercised; (ii) protector's countersignature if the trust instrument so requires; (iii) re-confirmation of the beneficiary's identity, CDD status, and bank details; (iv) OFAC, EU, and UN sanctions screening at the sending bank and in the correspondent chain (typically ANZ Melbourne, Westpac, or a New York-based USD clearer); and (v) compliance with the trust's 12-month cash-flow plan. For commercial disbursements (atypical for a pure asset-protection trust), Commercial Letters of Credit at sight and documentary collections are obtainable through ANZ or NBV, but their use reclassifies the entity as a higher-risk trading trust and should be avoided. Vanuatu has no domestic EMI licensing framework for digital-only payment institutions, and leading offshore EMIs and FX-scale platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury, Revolut Business, Payoneer) routinely decline Vanuatu-domiciled International Companies due to the FATF grey-listing of June 2024, perceived high money-laundering risk of the trust sector, and correspondent-bank de-risking. A licensed VU trustee should treat EMIs only as downstream payment beneficiaries, never as the primary account bank. If digital rails are mandatory for operations, a parallel operating entity in an acceptable clearing jurisdiction should be used solely for treasury flows, with the VU IC remaining the legal owner of the trust assets.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A — VUV 0. Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing legislation, no Local File or Master File obligation, and no VUV-denominated documentation threshold. The International Company is outside the domestic tax net; TP compliance may be required solely under the consolidation and audit rules of a foreign parent or beneficiary jurisdiction.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu applies OECD-compliant economic substance rules through the International Companies (Economic Substance) regulations administered by the VFSC. An International Company conducting a relevant activity must demonstrate adequate employees, premises, expenditure, and management and control in Vanuatu. For a discretionary asset-protection trust, the licensed Vanuatu trustee must maintain substantive decision-making records and fiduciary governance; pure passive holding or trust entities may satisfy a reduced economic substance requirement through a local registered agent, registered office, and properly documented trustee meetings.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Foreign Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu itself imposes no income tax penalty for foreign tax evasion, but the VFSC may deregister an International Company used for unlawful purposes, and foreign authorities may impose accuracy penalties, understatement penalties, and interest. Domestically, fraudulent abuse of corporate and trust forms is punishable under the Penal Code as fraud, with consequential civil recovery orders enforceable by the Vanuatu Supreme Court.

Director personal liability: Directors, trustees, and settlors who exercise effective control may be personally assessed in the foreign high-tax jurisdiction if the trust iis disregarded as a sham. Vanuatu mutual legal assistance mechanisms allow asset freezing, travel bans, and extradition; domestic fraud penalties include imprisonment up to seven years and court-ordered restitution.
Unlicensed Fiduciary / Trust Company Activity

Statutory sanction: Under the Fiduciary and Trust Companies Act 2014, unlicensed fiduciary activity is a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to VUV 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to two years. The VFSC may also strike off the offending International Company and apply for a court order freezing the trust assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly participate in unlicensed fiduciary activity are personally liable for the penalty. Courts may pierce the corporate veil, order repayment of all fees received, place a travel ban on responsible officers, and issue management disqualification orders.
AML/CFT / Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2016 and VFSC AML/CFT Rules, corporate penalties may reach VUV 30,000,000; individuals face imprisonment up to 15 years. The VFSC may suspend or revoke the trustee licence and confiscation orders may attach to the entire trust corpus.

Director personal liability: Directors, Chief Compliance Officers, and Money Laundering Reporting Officers bear personal criminal and civil liability. Sanctions include imprisonment, disqualification, disgorgement of fees, personal fines up to VUV 10,000,000, travel bans, and freezing orders during investigation.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The VFSC may deregister the International Company under the International Companies Act 1992 and impose administrative penalties of up to VUV 5,000,000. A false economic-substance declaration triggers additional penalties under the Vanuatu economic substance rules, and trust distributions may be voided as ultra vires or invalid contracts.

Director personal liability: Courts may disregard the corporate and trust structure and hold directors personally liable for breach of fiduciary duty, requiring restitution of diverted assets. Directors may be disqualified for up to ten years and face foreign enforcement of civil judgments against their personal estates.
Fraudulent Conveyance / Asset-ProTection Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the common law fraudulent conveyance principles applied in Vanuatu, the transfer may be set aside as void. Foreign courts applying their own voidable-transaction and clawback legislation may enter judgment directly against the trust corpus, and Vanuatu courts will assist in judgment enforcement and asset seizure under international mutual assistance procedures.

Director personal liability: The settlor, directors, and trustees may be held personally liable to creditors for the value of assets fraudulently transferred, plus punitive damages and interest. Travel bans, contempt orders, and imprisonment may be imposed for refusal to repatriate assets.
