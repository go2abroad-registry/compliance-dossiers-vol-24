Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR) of Samoa, operating the Tax Administration System (TAS) e-filing portal at www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) must first obtain its Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of International Companies; thereafter it files a Business Registration Application with the Ministry for Revenue, attaching the certificate, constitution, registered-office address in Apia, and notarized director/shareholder identification. The MfR issues a 9-digit Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and, where required, a separate VAGST registration number within 5 business days of complete submission. Non-resident entities with no Samoan-source income may register for TIN-only status and remain outside the income-tax net.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone - The IC is treated as non-resident and tax-exempt under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended); it pays only a flat annual government fee and is exempt from income tax, stamp duty, estate duty, and exchange-control approvals, provided no business is transacted within Samoa and no income arises from Samoan sources. Any local-source income (e.g., storage or brokerage connected to Samoan property) becomes assessable under the Income Tax Act 2012. — A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) must first obtain its Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of International Companies; thereafter it files a Business Registration Application with the Ministry for Revenue, attaching the certificate, constitution, registered-office address in Apia, and notarized director/shareholder identification. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - The IC is treated as non-resident and tax-exempt under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended); it pays only a flat annual government fee and is exempt from income tax, stamp duty, estate duty, and exchange-control approvals, provided no business is transacted within Samoa and no income arises from Samoan sources. Any local-source income (e.g., storage or brokerage connected to Samoan property) becomes assessable under the Income Tax Act 2012.

Available election forms

The IC may file an irrevocable written election with the Ministry for Revenue to be taxed as an ordinary resident company, thereby forfeiting tax-exempt status and becoming subject to the Income Tax Act 2012 at the then-applicable statutory corporate rate; hybrid-mismatch and US-style check-the-box elections are not recognized. The IC may elect segregated-cell treatment under the International Companies (Amendment) Act 2008 to ring-fence the used-excavator trading pool from other activities, and may elect special-purpose-vehicle status to clarify non-resident tax treatment to counterparties and banks.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 of annual taxable turnover - mandatory registration applies to any person making taxable supplies above this threshold in any 12-month period; registration below the threshold is voluntary. Non-resident suppliers of remote/digital or imported services must register if total supplies to Samoan consumers exceed WST 60,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available to a VAGST-registered trader exporting used hydraulic excavators, rollers, and associated earthmoving equipment where the goods are physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply under a valid customs export entry, and title passes to a non-resident buyer. Export-related services (e.g., overseas freight brokering, inspection fees invoiced to a foreign buyer, and international arbitration support) are zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident outside Samoa at the time of supply, the service is performed outside Samoa, and the benefit is enjoyed wholly outside Samoa. The trader must substantiate export status within the filing period and reflect the zero rating in the VAGST return with full documentary retention.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa SAD) with official exit stamp and Customs-coded goods description
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing delivery to the overseas consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed commercial bank confirming hard-currency settlement of the export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and signed export packing list referencing the IC's TIN and VAGST registration number
  • Certificate of Origin (where a preferential tariff claim is made) and MfR export clearance certificate
  • Survey/inspection report (e.g., SGS or equivalent third-party condition report) covering the used machines, to substantiate valuation and arm's-length price

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control mandate currently applies. VAGST-registered businesses must issue sequentially numbered tax invoices in the prescribed MfR format, file monthly returns through the Tax Administration System (TAS) or its certified API-connected accounting software, and remit net VAGST by the 28th day of the following month; invoice and customs data must be retained for 7 years and are subject to MfR automated risk-scoring and cross-matching with Samoa Customs entry records.

Samoa Customs and the Ministry for Revenue coordinate through the ASYCUDA World single-window platform; suspected overvaluation or fictitious exports trigger a joint audit. Where zero-rating is disallowed, the MfR assesses VAGST at 15% on the full export consideration, claws back input credits, and applies a late-payment penalty of 50% of the unpaid tax plus interest under the Tax Administration Act 2014. Fraudulent export claims are punishable by an evasion penalty of up to 200% of the tax avoided, public naming of the taxpayer, and imprisonment for up to 7 years; the Customs Service may independently impose forfeiture of the goods and a false-declaration penalty not exceeding WST 100,000 per customs entry.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is a non-fully-convertible, managed-float currency. Current account transactions, including trade settlements and service payments, are permitted without a general licence but require submission of supporting trade documentation through an authorised dealer. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, share subscriptions, related-party loans, and profit repatriation by non-resident shareholders of a Samoa International Company (IC), require prior written approval or filing with the Central Bank of Samoa under the Exchange Control Act 1976. Remittance of after-tax profits from ICs is generally legally permissible, but the practical hurdles include formal Board-resolved dividend or distribution declarations, audited financial statements evidencing retained earnings, a tax clearance certificate from the Samoa Ministry of Revenue, and confirmation that no outstanding Exchange Control obligations exist. Dividends distributed by an IC whose underlying income is sourced wholly outside Samoa and that is tax-exempt under the International Companies Act 2012 may be remitted without domestic withholding tax, but the Central Bank will still require a signed undertaking that the funds do not derive from any locally prohibited activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations, all export receipts from the sale of used construction machinery, excavators, and rollers by a Samoa IC must be repatriated to Samoa and offered for conversion into WST through an authorised dealer within 30 days from the date the foreign currency is credited to an offshore collection account or received by the exporter, whichever occurs first. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the export proceeds unless the Central Bank of Samoa issues a prior exemption certificate permitting the holder to retain the foreign currency for up to 12 months for the direct settlement of verified import obligations or the servicing of approved external debt. For WST-denominated transactions, exporters must declare the value on the applicable Exchange Control form and attach invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, and proof of physical movement of goods.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

The prospective viability of offshore fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) is Low. Samoa's legal and regulatory framework does not currently license standalone cross-border digital banks or independent EMIs. An offshore EMI may be used only as a supplementary payment collection instrument, not as a bank-clearing rail, if it is contractually backed by a licensed correspondent bank and it does not hold or intermediate the principal trading account of the Samoa IC. The IC's primary treasury infrastructure must remain anchored in a licensed Samoa commercial bank. Legal constraints include the Central Bank's regulatory advisory powers, the Anti-Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act 2000, and bilateral correspondent-bank compliance expectations under CFATF and FATF standards.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard Samoa corporate income tax rate for resident companies and branches); 0% effective statutory rate for an International Company (IC) that complies with the International Companies Act 1987 and does not carry on business in Samoa

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by an International Company: section 200 of the International Companies Act 1987 exempts all IC dividends from Samoan income tax and withholding tax, so no non-resident dividend WHT arises on distributions from a compliant IC. The standard Samoa statutory non-resident dividend WHT is 15% but applies only to resident trading companies, never to a compliant IC distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within one calendar month following each anniversary of the date of incorporation; the annual government licence fee becomes payable at the same time. The Registrar issues a renewal notice, but no grace period exists for the fee.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the balance date (for the standard 31 December year-end, the return is due by 30 June of the following year); the Commissioner of Revenue may extend the deadline only on application made before the due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File or Master File thresholds exist under Samoan law; the arm's length rule in section 79 of the Income Tax Act 2007 applies only to taxable resident entities. As a risk-based benchmark, a used-excavator International Company engaged in related-party transactions should prepare a contemporaneous transfer pricing memorandum where annual related-party turnover exceeds WST 2,000,000 (approximately USD 740,000) or where a single related-party excavator sale exceeds WST 500,000; below these thresholds a concise contract-and-margin note is maintained. The Samoan exemption does not relieve the IC of the need to evidence arm's length pricing in the customer's high-tax jurisdiction.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not operate a codified Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE or the BVI; there is no ESR filing and no substance score. Instead, Samoa enforces substance through the International Companies Act 1987 prohibition against carrying on business in Samoa, the annual compliance certificate, mandatory beneficial ownership reporting, and AML/CFT supervision by the Central Bank of Samoa. Internationally, downstream tax authorities will apply OECD BEPS Action 5, controlled foreign company rules, substance-over-form, and general anti-abuse rules to challenge a used-excavator trading IC that lacks genuine management, staff, decision-making, a real bank account, inventory risk, and operational function outside Samoa. The IC must therefore maintain a demonstrable commercial role in the market where the functions are performed, such as actual dealer inventory, inspection yard exposure, credit risk, and independent negotiation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Up to a 150% evasion shortfall penalty plus interest under the Tax Administration Act 2007; criminal conviction attracts fines up to WST 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to seven years. The customer-country tax authority may reassess the economic owner on the full resale margin, deny DTA benefits, and impose additional foreign penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the IC is a mere facade: directors and ultimate beneficial owners become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest and penalties; asset-freeze orders and travel bans are available in Samoa and through mutual assistance channels; imprisonment and director disqualification of up to ten years may follow.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Deregistration and striking-off by the Registrar of International Companies under the International Companies Act 1987; in the destination state, application of CFC and anti-avoidance rules, reallocation of profit to the beneficial owner, imposition of tax at the applicable corporate rate, penalties and late-interest, and denial of tax residence certificates. The EU and OECD framework may classify the IC as a shell with no entitlement to treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Director disqualification in Samoa and in the customer jurisdiction; personal assessment for the tax reallocated where the corporate entity is dissipating assets; cross-border recovery against foreign director assets; visa and immigration restrictions in multiple treaty partner jurisdictions.
AML/CFT Violations (Beneficial Ownership and Wire / SWIFT LC Flows)

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's AML/CFT Act, corporate fines of up to WST 1,000,000 and individual fines of up to WST 500,000; mandatory suspicious transaction reporting, transaction freeze orders, confiscation of the excavator sale proceeds, and forced termination of the registered agency relationship leading to deregistration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed Money Laundering Reporting Officer face imprisonment of up to fourteen years for money laundering offences; personal confiscation orders, travel bans, listing on sanctions databases, and permanent prohibition from managing any Samoan international company or trust.
Transfer Pricing Fraud / False Intercompany Margin

Statutory sanction: In Samoa, where the entity is taxable, the Commissioner may adjust the price to the arm's length standard, impose shortfall penalties of 20% to 150% and interest; in the destination market, primary adjustments, corresponding dividend or royalty withholding taxes, late-interest and penalties of 10% to 100% may apply. Mutual agreement procedure under the relevant DTA can relieve double taxation only if both tax administrations accept the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing disclosures face personal fines, perjury referral, and joint liability for the adjusted tax where the company is judgment-proof; repeated wilful conduct is prosecuted as tax evasion and may result in custodial sentences.
Breach of IC Tax-Exempt Status (Carrying on Business in Samoa)

Statutory sanction: The IC immediately becomes taxable in Samoa on its Samoan-source income at 25%, with mandatory tax registration, a 10% late-filing penalty, monthly interest at 2%, and up to a 150% evasion penalty for concealment; the Registrar may compulsorily convert the IC to a domestic company or strike it off the register.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify the annual compliance certificate while knowing business was conducted in Samoa commit a false declaration offence: a fine of up to WST 20,000 plus imprisonment under the Tax Administration Act; personal liability is imposed for all unpaid corporate tax and penalties of the company.
