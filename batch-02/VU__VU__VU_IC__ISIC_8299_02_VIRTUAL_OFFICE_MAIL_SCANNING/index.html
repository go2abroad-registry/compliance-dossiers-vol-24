Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department (CIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, through its Value Added Tax Division; corporate registration is administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC). Official online portal: https://www.gov.vu and VAT online services via the CIR portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) must obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department. The application requires: (1) submission of Form TIN-01 (Application for Tax Identification Number) to the CIR, (2) certificate of incorporation issued by VFSC, (3) certified copy of the IC's constitution, (4) registered office address in Vanuatu, (5) identity documents of directors/shareholders (passport, proof of address), and (6) if the IC intends to undertake a taxable activity or hold a business licence, the proposed business licence application or approval. Processing takes approximately 5-10 business days. Although the International Company is tax-exempt from domestic income tax, a TIN may be issued for administrative, customs, business licence, and VAT compliance purposes. If the IC is wholly non-resident and has no Vanuatu-source taxable activity, the IC may not be automatically required to obtain a TIN; however, for virtual office/mail scanning services supplied to international clients, a TIN and VAT registration are recommended to sustain zero-rated export treatment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no US-style check-the-box election and no OECD hybrid mismatch election mechanism. Under the International Companies Act, an IC may elect in its constitution to maintain confidentiality of shareholders and directors. An IC may also elect to convert to a domestic 'local company' by filing a change-of-status election with the VFSC and CIR, thereby subjecting itself to Vanuatu's domestic tax and VAT rules; such an election is voluntary and must be approved by the VFSC Registrar. Hybrid entity classification is not recognized for domestic tax purposes; there is no deemed disposition or 'dual resident' election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 (five million vatu) annual taxable turnover for a taxable activity; presumptive registration is required for importers of taxable goods or services irrespective of turnover, and a person may voluntarily register for VAT with CIR approval. International Companies providing offshore services solely to non-residents are generally treated as outside the Vanuatu VAT net, but if any local turnover such as mail scanning services to domestic addressees arises, the IC must monitor the VUV 5,000,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) exported goods that leave Vanuatu under customs control within the period prescribed in the Value Added Tax Regulations, and (ii) exported services provided to a non-resident customer for consumption wholly outside Vanuatu, including virtual office administrative services, mail receiving and scanning, and other back-office services rendered by an International Company. To qualify, the supplier must be a registered VAT person, the supply must meet the definition of an export under VAT law, and the supplier must retain documentation evidencing the destination, exportation, and receipt by the non-resident. Services that are physically performed in Vanuatu but whose economic use occurs offshore (such as digitized mail scanning sent to a foreign virtual mailbox subscriber) are capable of zero-rating if the sole recipient is a non-resident and no Vanuatu resident benefits from the service. Advance rulings from the CIR are recommended for novel digital-scope service arrangements.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form E1, electronic or paper SAD) endorsed by the Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Vanuatu
  • Commercial invoice and VAT tax invoice issued by the registered supplier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank documentary evidence of foreign exchange receipt from the non-resident purchaser
  • Packing list / shipping manifest for goods exports; for service exports, a dated service contract and delivery receipt confirming offshore receipt and use

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory electronic invoicing regime is currently in force in Vanuatu. VAT-registered suppliers must issue a tax invoice containing the prescribed particulars (supplier name and TIN, recipient name, date, description, VAT amount, VAT-exclusive value, and VAT rate). Invoices may be issued in paper or electronic format, but there is no government API integration, digital stamping, or real-time clearance requirement. VAT returns can be submitted online via the CIR e-filing system; however, the tax authority has not implemented continuous transaction controls or clearance mandates.

CIR and Vanuatu Customs apply strict documentary audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. If the required customs documentation, shipping evidence, or inward remittance proof is not available within 180 days of supply, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 12.5%, and the registered person must self-assess and remit the VAT. Fraudulent zero-rating is treated as tax evasion under Section 52 of the VAT Act, attracting a fine of up to VUV 5,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years. Civil penalties may include a late payment penalty of 2% per month of the unremitted VAT, a default assessment based on 150% of the suppressed tax, interest at the statutory rate, and the automatic clawback of any previously refunded VAT. Subsequent third-party facilitators (including promoters of sham export structures) may be jointly and severally liable, and repeat offenders face suspension or cancellation of VAT registration and the business licence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is fully convertible for current-account payments and profit remittances. No exchange-control approvals, gross reserve retention requirements, or repatriation licences apply to a Vanuatu International Company. The practical barrier is banking counterparty due diligence, not convertibility. Dividend and fee distributions can be made to non-resident shareholders and contractors provided the underlying source-of-funds and business documentation is available to the clearing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies to export receipts earned by a Vanuatu International Company. Free disposal of proceeds in VUV, USD, AUD, or any permitted settlement currency is allowed without RBV approval, subject only to internal bank anti-money-laundering verification and accurate recordkeeping under the FTRA.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap 125]; external exchange control matters are additionally referenced to the Exchange Control Act [Cap 121] and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act (FTRA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Vanuatu does not operate a domestic electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing framework, and the RBV does not authorise non-resident digital EMIs to provide local account opening, clearing, or payment acceptance services. Overseas EMIs can be used receptively for low-value recurring client inflows via Stripe/card rails, but tier-1 Vanuatu banks will not treat such EMIs as compliant settlement counterparties for export proceeds, tax remittance, or capital injections. Any such inbound collection must be swept to a licensed commercial bank through a referenced SWIFT/card-payout protocol.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol: The investor or shareholder initiates an SWIFT MT103 from a bank account that has completed its own KYC; the payment must clearly reference 'PAID-IN CAPITAL – INVOICE/SUBSCRIBER REF [unique ID]' and include the relevant capital subscription register. No exchange-control permit is required, but the receiving Vanuatu bank will verify source-of-funds documentation and align the purpose code with the International Company’s subscribed share capital or non-current shareholder loan. Funds are cleared directly to the segregated Capital Escrow Account whenever the transaction exceeds USD 10,000; smaller operating collections may route to the Operational Account after standard negative-news and counterparty-name screening.Outbound trade settlements are conducted primarily via SWIFT MT103 for software licensing, scanning subcontracts, and administrative services paid to overseas vendors. For physical equipment purchases and machinery importation, a documentary Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) is the preferred clearing rail, with the Vanuatu bank issuing against a confirmed client purchase order and shipping invoice. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual license renewal and exemption certificate confirmation due by the anniversary date of incorporation or the VFSC-designated renewal date; no income tax filing deadline applies.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
VFSC Annual Return / License Renewal Application Registered agent confirmation, registered office address, director and shareholder particulars, statement of no local domestic business activity. Non-resident beneficial owners must confirm maintained status as non-residents and that no business has been conducted with Vanuatu residents, to preserve the tax-exempt IC classification. Late renewal penalties imposed by VFSC: a default penalty of up to VUV 50,000 for failure to file annual return; continued default may lead to strike-off and dissolution. No daily surcharge for income tax exists given the zero-tax regime.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local currency threshold; Vanuatu International Companies are not subject to transfer pricing documentation obligations because there is no corporate income tax. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is nevertheless market practice for ultimate parent jurisdictions with CFC or income inclusion rules.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not currently apply EU-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, ICs must maintain a registered agent in Vanuatu and should avoid creating a local physical presence that could be deemed domestic trading income, which would trigger the income tax exemption loss and VAT registration risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Misuse of Tax-Exempt IC Status

Statutory sanction: Loss of exemption, retroactive income tax assessment, monetary penalties, and deregistration under the International Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they authorize undisclosed local trading; criminal fines and disqualification from management can apply.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: VFSC strike-off, revocation of license, and fines under the International Companies Act and Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fine and imprisonment for AML failures; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation of illicit funds flows.
AML / Counterfeiting of Corporate Gateway

Statutory sanction: Fines up to VUV 10 million and license suspension for registered agents; confiscation of funds and corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability with imprisonment up to 15 years; directors as beneficial owners risk travel bans and civil restitution orders.
