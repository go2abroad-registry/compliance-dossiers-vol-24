Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA) — statutory revenue authority established under the Samoa Revenue Authority Act 2013; administered through the SRA Tax Administration System (TAS) digital portal at https://www.sra.gov.ws; responsible for administration of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992, the Income Tax Act 2016, and related revenue laws.

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 does NOT obtain a Samoan TIN or VAGST registration number as part of its offshore formation; its statutory identifier is the certificate of incorporation and company number issued by the Registrar of International Companies within the Samoan International Finance Authority (IFA). Procedure for a foreign-owned IC: (1) instruct a licensed Samoan registered agent / company service provider; (2) file the constitution, Form 1 incorporation application, and a non-residence declaration with the IFA; (3) obtain the certificate of incorporation, tax exemption certificate, and annual license; (4) no step is required before the Samoa Revenue Authority unless the IC later breaches its non-resident status, elects domestic treatment, or carries on taxable activity in Samoa, in which case the IC must apply to the SRA for a TIN using the standard SRA TIN application form supported by the incorporation certificate, directors' register, and registered agent confirmation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Non-resident International Company; the IC is statutorily exempt under the International Companies Act 1987 from all Samoan taxes, including income tax, VAGST (GST), stamp duty, and exchange control, subject to the territorial limitation that the IC may not carry on business in Samoa, hold Samoan land or Samoan domestic company shares, or transact with Samoan residents except for prescribed administrative services; all wholesale principal-to-principal trading income from the bulk resale of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules is outside Samoa's territorial tax net.

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime. Available elections are: (1) the IC may elect in its constitution to adopt or exclude elective provisions of the International Companies Act 1987 (elective provisions mechanism); (2) the IC may elect by special resolution to be re-domiciled or converted into a Samoan domestic company, which prospectively extinguishes the tax-exemption certificate and subjects the entity to full Samoan taxation; (3) hybrid entity treatment is not cognizable in Samoa, so the classification analysis must be performed solely in the jurisdiction of the beneficial owner (e.g., a U.S. owner may check the box to treat the exempt IC as a disregarded entity); (4) the IC may elect its own internal accounting currency but may not elect any Samoan tax regime because no nexus exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (Samoa Tala) annual taxable turnover threshold for mandatory VAGST registration of any person carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa; an International Company engaged in offshore bulk petrochemical polymer resale has no Samoan taxable activity and therefore no VAGST registration obligation, regardless of global turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992, exports of goods from Samoa are zero-rated where the goods are physically removed from Samoa and ownership passes to a non-resident recipient within prescribed time limits; offshore services supplied to a non-resident recipient for use wholly outside Samoa are likewise zero-rated. The Samoan International Company does not rely on this VAGST zero-rating mechanism because the virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules never enter Samoan territory: inventory is held at third-country producer warehouses or bonded facilities, title passes on FOB/CIF or similar INCOTERMS at non-Samoan load ports, and the IC's gross margin is realized entirely offshore. Substantiation therefore focuses on proving the exempt non-resident status of the IC through its IFA certificate of tax exemption, invoice flows, warehouse and transportation documentation, and corporate substance records.

  • Samoan Customs Export Declaration (Cus-Exp Form) — required only if any goods are physically exported from Samoa; typically inapplicable to offshore principal-to-principal polymer resale, but retained where incidental Samoan-origin movements occur
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill / Air Waybill evidencing the third-country load port, ocean or air carriage, and consignee of the polyethylene and polypropylene granules
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credit advice evidencing settlement of the irrevocable or documentary LC proceeds into the IC's registered foreign-currency bank account
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and third-party weight/quality certificates issued at the load port
  • Underlying master purchase agreements and sale contracts with the polymer producer and downstream buyer, including INCOTERMS, price bases, and title transfer clauses

Samoa does not mandate real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital tax stamping; VAGST operates on a self-assessment basis using conventional commercial invoices. The SRA TAS portal supports electronic filing and payment of returns, but no API-based invoice clearance is imposed. For the exempt IC, standard commercial invoices, warehouse transfer documents, and SWIFT/LC records are retained as commercial and beneficial-ownership evidence rather than fiscal invoices.

If a registered VAGST person fraudulently or erroneously claims zero-rating on goods or services that do not qualify, the SRA may assess the full 15% VAGST due, together with late payment penalties and interest under the Tax Administration Act 2016, and may pursue criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax evasion, attracting fines and imprisonment for responsible officers. For the International Company, the IFA and SRA may jointly audit compliance with the non-residence conditions; any breach (e.g., domestic Samoan trading, unauthorized land holding, or sham LC structuring to disguise Samoan-source income) voids the tax-exemption certificate ab initio, exposing the IC to retrospective Samoan income tax and VAGST, civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax shortfall, and striking-off of the company. The principal international clawback risk is mischaracterization of the IC as a permanent establishment in the seller's or buyer's jurisdiction; this is mitigated by maintaining genuine offshore contracts, independent LC payment rails, and economic substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

WST is a partially convertible, tightly administered currency traded only through CBS-authorized dealers within Samoa. Current-account transactions such as trade settlements, service fees, commissions and dividend remittances receive routine bank-level approval subject to documentary proof and tax-clearance certificates. Capital-account transactions, including equity injections, shareholder-loan disbursements, capital repatriation and portfolio transfers, require individual CBS approval or bank approval within delegated limits, supported by verified corporate records and source-of-funds evidence. Profit remittance by a tax-exempt International Company is lawful but practically gated by the need to demonstrate that no transaction was conducted with Samoan residents, that all underlying trade documents are genuine, and that the beneficial owner is fully identified; otherwise the remittance is treated as a suspect offshore flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and CBS Exchange Control Guidelines, any merchandise physically exported from Samoa must be invoiced in a foreign currency and the exporter must repatriate the full export proceeds to an authorized dealer in Samoa within 30 days from the date of shipment, followed by mandatory surrender and conversion into WST at the prevailing commercial rate, unless a specific CBS retention, offset or exemption is granted. For the WS_IC petrochemical trading entity, goods will normally be drop-shipped from third-country resin producers to end-buyers without entering Samoa, so no Samoan customs export entry or statutory surrender obligation arises on those flows; nevertheless, any third-country receivables credited to a Samoan WST account will be documented by the bank as foreign-currency proceeds subject to source-verification and exchange-control reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, is the monetary authority and licensed regulator of all authorized dealers in foreign exchange. CBS administers the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Financial Institutions Act 1996, the International Banking Act 1987, and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007, and holds final authority over foreign-exchange approvals, authorized-dealer licensing, trade-payment clearing rules, and the managed-float policy of the Samoan Tala (WST) against a trade-weighted basket of USD, NZD, EUR and AUD.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for use as a primary clearing rail. Samoa has no licensed electronic-money-institution or digital-bank framework, and CBS does not supervise offshore EMIs; therefore the Samoa International Company cannot hold a locally regulated EMI licence and a third-country EMI (e.g., EU, FCA or HKMA-licensed) is treated as an unregulated third-party payment intermediary by Samoan banks. Legal constraints include: (i) funds entering Samoa must pass through a CBS-authorized dealer with complete sender/beneficiary identification and a valid purpose code, which an EMI payment trail rarely satisfies; (ii) exchange-control surrender and reporting obligations become impossible to reconcile if receivables are collected offshore; and (iii) Samoan banks will quarantine or reject inbound transfers from EMI aggregator accounts due to opacity risk. Permissible supporting role: use a regulated EMI solely as a low-value receivables collection point in a fully licensed foreign jurisdiction, immediately onward-settling to the Samoan operational account via direct bank-to-bank MT103, with the EMI never named as the ultimate originator of trade payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder contributions must be wired by SWIFT MT103 with an unmistakable purpose narrative: for equity, use 'Shareholder Capital Contribution / Equity Injection', accompanied by a board resolution and the IC's register of shareholders; for borrowings, use 'Shareholder Loan', supported by an executed interest-bearing facility agreement. Under the Exchange Control Act 1976, inward remittances to a CBS-authorized dealer do not require pre-approval when the funds originate from a non-resident regulated bank, the beneficiary is the International Company, and the funds are credited to a foreign-currency account with the remitter name exactly matching the registered shareholder. The onboarding bank will hold the first inward transfer for verification and will require: (i) the registered agent's incumbency certificate; (ii) the directors' resolution authorizing account opening and fund receipt; (iii) the charter and certificate of good standing; and (iv) a sanctions-screen of the remitter. Funds in USD should remain in a USD-denominated operational account to avoid unnecessary WST conversion and re-conversion spreads.Outbound trade payments to third-country resin suppliers will use two complementary rails. First, open-account settlements via SWIFT MT103 from the Samoa USD account, with a narrative containing the supplier's invoice number, trade date, Incoterms rule and vessel or container reference; transactions above the authorized-dealer threshold are escalated to CBS with pro-forma invoice and proof of underlying import. Second, documentary settlements through Irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit issued or confirmed by BSP Samoa or ANZ Samoa, using MT700/MT707/MT740 messages routed via New York or Sydney correspondent banks, with complying presentation of full bills of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, insurance certificate and independent inspection certificate; given the bulk-polymer margin model, sight LCs are preferred over usance LCs except where a 30–60 day supplier credit to the IC is expressly documented. Standby Letters of Credit (MT760) may be issued for performance security where the buyer requires them. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: International Companies engaged in distribution and service centre activity must complete an Economic Substance return where total gross income exceeds WST 3,000,000 per annum. A Samoan Local File will be required where related-party cross-border transactions exceed WST 10,000,000 per annum; a Master File threshold applies at WST 50,000,000 in aggregate covered transactions.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2020 apply. The wholesale distribution of petrochemical polymers is treated as a 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity. The IC must demonstrate: (a) it is managed and controlled in Samoa through board meetings and minutes; (b) it maintains an adequate physical office in Samoa; (c) it employs adequate full-time employees in Samoa; (d) it incurs adequate operating expenditure in Samoa; and (e) its core income-generating activities are performed in Samoa. Failure to meet these conditions allows SIFA to revoke the tax exemption and reclassify the IC as a resident taxable company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Sham Offshore Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act and Tax Administration Act, the Samoa Ministry of Revenue can disregard the IC's separate existence, assess the entity as a resident company at 27% on worldwide income, impose a 150% civil penalty on the tax avoided, and apply interest from the date the tax should have been paid. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion carries a fine of up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment for up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Samoan Registered Agent may be held jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax, penalties, and interest; the court may issue travel bans preventing departure from Samoa, order asset freezing, make directors personally liable for unlawful dividends, and disqualify directors from acting in Samoa for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: SIFA will strike the company off the International Companies Register and revoke the tax exemption certificate under the Economic Substance Regulations; SIFA may levy a penalty of WST 200,000 and require the company to file and pay Samoan corporate income tax on its entire prior exempt income.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false economic substance declarations are personally liable for fines up to WST 100,000; the court may order restitution to SIFA, compel production of personal tax records, and prohibit the director from being a director of any Samoan company for five years.
Money Laundering and AML Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: The Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018 imposes a maximum penalty of WST 4,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; Samoan correspondent banking relationships may be terminated, accounts frozen, and the IC dissolved for cause.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability, confiscation of both Samoan and foreign assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act, travel bans, and extradition risk in jurisdictions with mutual legal assistance treaties with Samoa.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Ministry of Revenue may make a transfer pricing adjustment reallocating additional taxable income to Samoa, impose a penalty equal to 25% of the adjustment amount, and add a monthly late payment penalty of WST 10,000 until compliant documentation is submitted.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing certificates or knowingly approve intercompany pricing without lawful economic support may be served Director Penalty Notices, rendering them personally liable for the adjusted tax debt; they may also be barred from serving as officers of a Samoan company.
Failure to File Annual Return and Licence Fee

Statutory sanction: Automatic strike-off and dissolution under the International Companies Act; the IC loses legal capacity to hold assets, sue, or contract; bank accounts held in Samoa are frozen; reinstatement requires payment of all arrears plus WST 250 reinstatement fee and a late penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for debts incurred while the company is dissolved, and the Samoan Registered Agent may resign and notify SIFA, triggering immediate dissolution; courts may award restitution to creditors and prohibit the directors from forming any new Samoan company for up to five years.
