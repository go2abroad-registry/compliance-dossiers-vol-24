Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA) under the Ministry of Finance; official online portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws (SRA e-Services tax filing system). The corporate registrar for International Companies is the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) acting through the Registrar of International Companies under the International Companies Act 1987.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Samoa International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 via a licensed registered agent; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and Memorandum and Articles (no minimum capital requirement). Step 2: Pay the annual SIFA government license fee and maintain the registered office and resident agent; confirm statutory non-resident status. Step 3: Apply to the Samoa Revenue Authority for a TIN and VAGST registration number only where the IC makes taxable supplies with a Samoan nexus; file the prescribed application with certified incorporation documents, registered agent certificate, passports of directors/shareholders, and registered office proof. Step 4: A pure offshore IC earning no Samoan-source income is not required to file Samoan income tax returns and remains compliant through the SIFA annual return and license fee payment alone.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Samoa. Permitted elective mechanisms: (1) voluntary VAGST registration despite turnover below the WST 60,000 threshold under the VAGST Act 1991, s.10; (2) application to the Commissioner for a tax exemption or relief certificate; (3) election by an IC to convert into a domestic Samoan company, thereby forfeiting offshore exemption, or to continue/redomicile to another jurisdiction; (4) election of tax accounting period and accounting basis. Samoa has not enacted OECD hybrid mismatch rules, so no corresponding entity-classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 (Samoan Tala) annual taxable turnover; mandatory registration applies when actual or expected taxable supplies exceed the threshold, with voluntary registration permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAGST rate applies to: (a) goods physically exported from Samoa within the prescribed period (generally 28 days of supply) and supported by customs-verified export documentation, including consumer electronics sold online and delivered to foreign customers; (b) services performed outside Samoa, or supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and receives the benefit offshore, including e-commerce fulfillment, logistics coordination, and digital marketing services; (c) supplies directly connected with exported goods (freight and insurance). The IC must retain documentary evidence of export and offshore payment; failure to substantiate the export treatment recharacterizes the supply as taxable at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime is in force in Samoa. VAGST returns and payments must be filed electronically through the SRA e-Services portal. Taxpayers must issue and retain sequential tax invoices that accurately reflect the VAGST treatment applied; export invoices must be cross-referenced to customs and shipping evidence and maintained for audit inspection.

SRA audit teams may assess export zero-rating claims within 6 years, extended to 12 years where fraud is alleged. A disallowed zero-rating is reassessed at 15% VAGST, with a late payment penalty of 10% of the unpaid tax plus monthly interest of 2%. Under the Tax Administration Act 2012, shortfall penalties range from 20% for failure to take reasonable care up to 100% for deliberate evasion. Fraudulent export claims attract a fine of up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years under the VAGST Act 1991, and trigger retrospective clawback of all refunded input credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is not fully freely convertible on the capital account; Samoa operates a managed currency arrangement supervised by the CBS, with current-account trade payments normally processed by licensed authorised dealers on production of genuine commercial documents, while capital-account movements require CBS approval or delegated authorised-dealer consent. WS_IC is a tax-exempt International Company whose online retail treasury may be held in USD outside Samoa or in a designated foreign-currency account at a licensed Samoan bank; such funds can remain offshore without WST conversion. Repatriation of USD into Samoa is permitted if supported by source-of-funds and invoice evidence, but conversion into WST for local expenses requires exchange-control declaration and authorised-dealer review. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted only out of audited available distributable profits; any activity that creates Samoa-source trading income, a physical branch, or routine domestic WST clearing may bring the IC within the domestic tax and licensing statutory regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and CBS Notices, exporters of goods or services from Samoa must receive full export proceeds through an authorised dealer in Samoa within 30 days of export and surrender 100% of the foreign currency for WST unless a CBS exemption permits retention in foreign currency. For WS_IC operating as an offshore online retailer whose goods are sourced from third-country suppliers and delivered directly to consumers outside Samoa, the receipts do not constitute Samoan export proceeds and need not be converted into WST. If WS_IC invoices from Samoa, uses a Samoan customs entry, or receives settlement into a Samoan-domiciled bank account, the authorised dealer will characterise the funds as export proceeds and apply the standard surrender and repatriation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) – Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa, Exchange Control Division

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex or Stripe Treasury may be used as collection processors for PayPal, Stripe and card payments, but they cannot operate as the principal settlement bank for WS_IC because Samoa's banking and exchange-control framework requires any entity holding funds, converting WST or providing cross-border payment services to a Samoan-registered entity to hold a banking/EMI licence approved by the CBS. The permissible model is: Stripe and PayPal settle in USD into WS_IC's licensed bank account or a non-Samoan treasury account in WS_IC's name; the EMI acts only as a card-acquiring and payout conduit and must never hold the entire merchant treasury overnight or provide WST conversion without a local authorised dealer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loans must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from a regulated bank account of the shareholder to WS_IC's USD or multi-currency account at a licensed Samoan bank. The receiving bank must obtain: the certificate of incorporation/registration, board resolution approving share allotment, share application documents, UBO register, source-of-funds declaration, and the required CBS exchange-control declaration or authorised-dealer capital-account approval. If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, an executed loan agreement, interest-rate schedule, repayment terms and external-debt registration with the authorised dealer are required. Funds credited to a foreign-currency account and not converted into WST may be treated as offshore funds and remain outside the surrender obligation. Use of Stripe, PayPal or an EMI as the originating remitter for capital contributions is not acceptable; capital must originate from a licensed deposit-taking institution.Trade disbursements for consumer-electronics inventory are executed over two permitted routes: direct SWIFT MT103 transfers from WS_IC's USD account to approved supplier bank accounts, or a documentary/commercial letter of credit issued by the Samoan bank via SWIFT MT700 and confirmed by a top-tier Hong Kong or Singapore bank to mitigate country and bank risk. Each payment must reference the purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/air waybill, Incoterms and shipment route. Stripe and PayPal settlements should be swept daily into WS_IC's designated USD settlement account and only then applied to trade payments. WST local clearing is reserved for Samoan payroll, rent, utilities and local overheads; conversion from USD to WST must be executed by an authorised dealer with supporting CBS documentation. For high-value bulk purchases, a standby LC (SWIFT MT760) may back payment obligations. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold is prescribed for an exempt International Company. If the company is assessed as a resident (e.g., after loss of IC status), the Samoa Revenue may require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for any related-party transaction exceeding WST 1,000,000 in a fiscal year, in line with OECD transfer pricing guidelines

Economic substance scope: The International Companies Act 1987 does not impose an explicit economic substance test. However, Samoa's general anti-avoidance rule and international tax transparency standards require that an International Company have genuine economic presence in Samoa, including proper books and records, board meeting minutes, and a real decision-making function; a purely passive e-commerce shell with no substance and no registered agent will be exposed to challenge by both Samoa Revenue and foreign tax authorities

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of International Company tax exemption; reassessment at the 27% corporate income tax rate; penalties up to 200% of tax understated and monthly interest of 1.5%; criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 2012 with fines up to WST 1,000,000

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes and penalties; a court may impose travel bans pending settlement; imprisonment for up to 10 years under the Samoa Crimes Act 2013
Shell Company / Sham Transactions

Statutory sanction: Court-ordered strike-off and dissolution under the International Companies Act 1987; confiscation of corporate assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2018; refusal by foreign courts and tax authorities to recognize the corporate veil; placement on international shell-company blacklists

Director personal liability: Civil restitution orders for the full amount of tax avoided; disqualification from serving as a director for up to 10 years; extradition possible for related foreign tax or money laundering offenses
AML / Counter-Financing of Terrorism (CFT) Breach

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties up to WST 500,000 per violation under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007; suspension or revocation of the agent's license; freezing of bank and payment-rail accounts; referral to the FATF for compliance review

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines of up to WST 250,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years; travel bans may be imposed during the investigation; a conviction results in a permanent ban from being a director of any Samoa-licensed entity
Transfer Pricing / Substance Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment to the arm's length price by Samoa Revenue; penalty of 50% of the additional tax assessment; default interest; publication of the taxpayer's name on the tax offences register

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for tax compliance are personally liable for the penalty if the mispricing is driven by their deliberate instructions; criminal prosecution for perjury in certifying transfer pricing documents; imprisonment for up to 3 years
