Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue, Government of Samoa (MOR) – Taxpayer Services; online portal: Samoa Tax Administration e-Services via Ministry of Revenue website (https://www.revenue.gov.ws)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing as an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 must first obtain a Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of International Companies. The IC then applies to the Ministry of Revenue for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using Form TIN-01, submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, constitutional documents, registered agent confirmation, and beneficial owner identification documents. The MOR issues the TIN within 5–10 business days. An IC holding a valid International Company tax exemption certificate is generally exempt from income tax but must maintain a TIN for customs, VAGST, and statistical reporting purposes if any import/export transactions occur.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Foreign entities establishing as an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 must first obtain a Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of International Companies. The IC then applies to the Ministry of Revenue for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using Form TIN-01, submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, constitutional documents, registered agent confirmation, and beneficial owner identification documents. The MOR issues the TIN within 5–10 business days. An IC holding a valid International Company tax exemption certificate is generally exempt from income tax but must maintain a TIN for customs, VAGST, and statistical reporting purposes if any import/export transactions occur.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election is available under Samoa domestic law. International Companies are statutorily tax-exempt under the International Companies Act 1987. The IC may elect to be treated as a non-resident company for exchange control purposes, but no tax status election exists. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 (Samoan Tala) in taxable supplies per 12-month period; non-resident International Companies without a fixed establishment in Samoa are not required to register unless they make taxable supplies in Samoa, in which case the WST 60,000 threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible movable goods (including non-ferrous copper cathodes) are zero-rated under the VAGST Act 2008. To claim the 0% rate, the IC must be a registered VAGST person, the sale must be to a person outside Samoa, the goods must be physically exported from Samoa within the statutorily prescribed period, and the IC must hold contemporaneous documentation proving export. Offshore services supplied to non-resident customers may also qualify if performed outside Samoa. The IC must not be engaging in domestic consumption of the goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs Service approved form)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent transport document such as Air Waybill or CMR) issued to the non-resident consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a licensed Samoan commercial bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

Samoa does not currently mandate real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. Tax invoices must be issued in accordance with VAGST Act 2008 and retained in electronic or paper form for audit purposes. No API-based clearance or government digital invoicing stamping regime is in effect.

The Ministry of Revenue and Samoa Customs Service conduct risk-based audits of export zero-rating claims. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating declarations are subject to retrospective assessment for unpaid VAT at 15%, interest accruing from the date of supply, and civil penalties of up to 150% of the tax amount. Criminal prosecution may arise under the Tax Administration Act 2002 and Customs Act 2014, carrying fines and imprisonment terms. Input tax credits are clawed back where export documentation is missing or where goods were diverted to domestic use. The limitation period for recovery is generally 3 years for reasonable care situations, extendable to 7 years for fraud or willful default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is fully convertible for current-account transactions under a managed float, but capital-account inflows and outflows by resident entities require CBS approval, licensing, or registration. The WS_IC, as a non-resident International Company incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987, benefits from the statutory exchange-control exemption (section 128), rendering the Exchange Control Act inapplicable to its offshore copper cathode trading and capital movements. Profit remittance for non-resident ICs is similarly unblocked for non-Samoan-source earnings; however, any Samoan-source income (e.g., storage or logistics invoiced by a Samoan branch) would require CBS remittance approval, tax clearance from the Ministry of Revenue, and applicable withholding tax certification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoan resident exporters must repatriate export proceeds within 30 days of shipment and surrender them to an authorized dealer for conversion into WST unless a CBS-approved foreign-currency account exemption is held. The WS_IC is outside this surrender regime because it is a non-resident IC conducting offshore commodity arbitrage; nonetheless, all export proceeds routed through a Samoan banking relationship must be supported by full trade documentation (sale contract, invoice, bill of lading, weight and assay certificates) to demonstrate that the flows are genuine international commerce and not disguised domestic earnings.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) operating under the Exchange Control Act 1976, Exchange Control Regulations 1977, and Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (as amended), with exclusive regulatory authority over authorized dealers and the cross-border capital account for Samoa.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Samoa maintains no statutory fintech EMI or digital banking licensing framework; non-bank payment service providers require a money remittance licence under the Money Remittance Act 2011 and CBS approval. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Airwallex) cannot access Samoan domestic clearing or hold WST-denominated accounts without a licensed local intermediary, and any EMI receiving Samoa-sourced funds without a CBS licence would contravene Samoan law. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable on a statutory basis; Samoa does not impose mandatory Local File or Master File documentation for International Companies operating under the zero-tax offshore regime. Transfer pricing documentation may nonetheless be required by the offshore customer or intermediary jurisdiction (e.g., parent-company country), and prudent practice mandates contemporaneous intercompany documentation.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations apply to International Companies conducting geographically mobile activities. Wholesale trading in copper cathodes is not a specified 'relevant activity' under the core ESR categories (banking, insurance, shipping, IP holding, headquarters, distribution and service centers), but if the company claims foreign tax residence, the foreign tax authority's economic substance rules will be applied by that jurisdiction. Where actual management and control are found to be exercised in Samoa, the entity may be deemed resident and subject to ESR compliance including adequate physical presence, local directors, and income-generating activity testing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Tax-Residence Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the International Company as a Samoa-tax-resident entity, imposition of income tax at the applicable Samoan corporate rate on deemed Samoa-source income, and penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged. The International Companies Registrar may also strike off the company and the registered agent may be fined.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations of foreign residence or falsify the annual declaration are personally liable for civil restitution, disqualification from serving as directors for up to five years, and potential imprisonment for up to seven years under the criminal provisions of the International Companies Act and the Tax Administration Act.
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Compulsory striking-off from the International Companies Register, forfeiture of the annual license, and prohibition on carrying on business in Samoa. If the company engages in transactions with Samoan residents or derives Samoan-source income, back taxes and late payment penalties are assessable.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner may be jointly and severally liable for all corporate debts and unpaid fees where the court pierces the corporate veil. Directors may be subject to travel bans pending investigation and personally ordered to restore misappropriated funds.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Freezing of bank accounts by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit; seizure of proceeds; civil forfeiture proceedings; fines up to WST 2 million per violation; referral to the Samoa Attorney General for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may be criminally prosecuted for money laundering and terrorism financing. Sanctions may extend to personal travel bans, asset freezes, extradition to sanction-imposing states, and custodial sentences of up to 14 years.
Fatal Tax Filings / Fraudulent Annual Declaration

Statutory sanction: Void filing, imposition of a surcharge of 50% of any tax that would have been payable, late payment interest of 12% per annum, and criminal fines of WST 25,000 per false declaration.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability upon the signing directors and registered agent; imprisonment up to 10 years for tax evasion by false statement; personal liability for outstanding corporate penalties and restoration of wrongfully obtained tax advantages.
