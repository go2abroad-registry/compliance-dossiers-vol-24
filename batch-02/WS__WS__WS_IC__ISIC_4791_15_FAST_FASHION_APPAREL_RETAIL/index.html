Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue, Government of Samoa (Maota o Tupe Lafu), taxpayer self-service portal: Samoa National Integrated Revenue Information System (NIRIS) accessible via https://www.revenue.gov.ws; the Samoa International Financial Authority (SIFA) administers registration and annual fees for International Companies.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must file a Corporate TIN Registration Application (Form TIN-01 Corporation) with the Ministry of Revenue, attaching certified copies of the certificate of incorporation or registration, constitutional documents, register of directors and shareholders, Samoa registered office or agent details, and a statement of expected local activities. The Ministry issues a 9-digit Samoa TIN upon approval. For a pure offshore International Company, the SIFA company registration number suffices and the entity is not obliged to hold a tax TIN unless it derives Samoan-source income, registers for VAGST, or employs local staff.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not maintain a check-the-box, fiscal-transparency, or hybrid-mismatch election regime. An International Company automatically enjoys a statutory tax exemption under the International Companies Act 1987-1988; a written exemption certificate or tax ruling may be obtained from the Minister of Revenue where desired, and an IC may elect to redomicile out of Samoa or convert to a domestic company under the Income Tax Act 2016, thereby bringing itself within the standard territorial tax net.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of annual taxable supplies (or as gazetted by the Minister of Revenue); supplies made outside Samoa by a tax-exempt International Company do not count toward the threshold and do not trigger VAGST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for tangible goods physically exported out of Samoa where the transfer of ownership passes to a non-resident and the goods leave Samoa within 28 days of supply or invoicing, and for services supplied directly to a non-resident located outside Samoa or performed entirely offshore. An International Company engaged exclusively in offshore e-commerce of fast-fashion apparel and footwear is outside the VAGST net; recovery of input VAGST is limited to inputs attributable to taxable or zero-rated supplies.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration lodged via ASYCUDA World with the Samoa Customs Division
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export to a destination outside Samoa
  • Commercial Invoice showing HS classification, values, and VAGST-exclusive pricing
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds through a licensed commercial bank in Samoa
  • Packing List and, where required, Certificate of Origin

Samoa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or an e-invoicing API clearance platform. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue sequentially numbered tax invoices stating supplier name, TIN, date, description, VAGST-exclusive value, and VAGST amount. Electronic invoicing through approved accounting or point-of-sale software is accepted; e-filing of VAGST returns is effected through NIRIS, and the Ministry of Revenue has indicated phased implementation of mandatory e-invoicing aligned with regional digitalisation.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to post-clearance audit and cross-matching between ASYCUDA customs records and foreign-exchange receipts reported by banks. An incorrect or fraudulent claim attracts a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded plus interest; knowing evasion is a criminal offence punishable by fine of up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years under the VAGST Act 2008. Retrospective clawback of zero-rated amounts for up to 7 years, cancellation of VAGST registration or customs export privileges, and seizure of the goods are additional statutory consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa has accepted the obligations of Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, thereby guaranteeing current-account convertibility and prohibiting exchange restrictions on payments and transfers for international trade, services, and remittances. The domestic currency, the Samoan Tala (WST, ISO 4217: WST), is a thinly traded, non-internationalized currency managed by the CBS against a composite basket of trading-partner currencies (primarily USD, NZD, and AUD); offshore WST liquidity is negligible. Capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, portfolio flows, and intercompany transfers) remain subject to CBS exchange-control approval or registration and must be flagged through an authorized dealer with the appropriate purpose code on the SWIFT cover. For the subject fast-fashion e-commerce International Company (WS_IC): profit remittance and dividend distribution hurdles are minimal because the IC is tax-exempt and non-resident under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended); there is no Samoan dividend withholding tax, no capital-gains tax, and no profit-attribution requirement. The residual hurdle is documentary: any remittance of funds through a Samoan licensed bank is classified under exchange-control capital purpose codes, and CBS reporting is triggered where WST conversion or resident beneficiaries are involved. Practical mitigation: execute the full receivables/payables treasury waterfall in USD outside Samoa in order to bypass WST convertibility friction entirely and preserve the IC's non-resident exchange-control exemption.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Samoan resident exporters, the Exchange Control Ordinance 1973 requires that the full value of exported goods be received within the statutory period (administratively applied by CBS as a 90-day repatriation ceiling from the date of shipment or performance) and that all foreign-currency export proceeds be surrendered or offered for conversion to an authorized dealer, unless a foreign-currency retention account has been pre-approved by CBS. The mandatory conversion ratio for resident exporters is effectively 100% surrender of convertible export receipts, and the export declaration, commercial invoice, waybill, and proof of payment must be retained for exchange-control verification. For the non-resident International Company (WS_IC) selling fast-fashion apparel and footwear through Klarna BNPL and Shopify Payments rails: the IC is a tax-exempt offshore legal entity that is NOT a Samoan resident for exchange-control purposes, provided it earns no WST-denominated local revenue, employs no Samoan resident personnel, and maintains no local trading premises. Consequently, the statutory surrender timeline and mandatory conversion ratios do not apply, and e-commerce sales proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD in an offshore treasury account. Structural caution: if ownership or control is ever deemed to be Samoan-resident, or if a Samoan-resident director exercises operating control, CBS could re-characterize the IC as resident and assert surrender obligations; the structure must continuously document non-resident status, offshore treasury holdings, and the absence of Samoan economic nexus.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS; Samoan: Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, acting as the exchange-control authority pursuant to the Exchange Control Ordinance 1973 and subsidiary Exchange Control Regulations, working in coordination with the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 for AML/CFT supervision of licensed banks and the Samoa International Financial Services Authority (SIFSA) for regulation of International Companies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW with material legal restrictions. Samoa (WSM) is not a supported settlement jurisdiction for the two specified target payment rails: Shopify Payments does not onboard Samoan-domiciled entities, and Klarna BNPL (managed-payout) channels are unavailable to Samoan legal entities; both rails must in practice be accessed through a supported merchant-of-record entity (e.g., a Hong Kong or Singapore operating company) that nets settlement proceeds and remits the balance to the IC's treasury account via SWIFT MT103. Global fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and similar) do not directly list Samoa as a supported business-registration jurisdiction, and most EMI KYC policies decline tax-exempt offshore International Companies absent substantiated economic activity. However, a Samoan IC may be onboarded by a UK/EU-regulated EMI that accepts offshore corporate clients where: (i) the EMI's risk appetite tolerates the structure; (ii) full UBO disclosure with notarized passport and proof-of-address is provided; and (iii) evidence of genuine e-commerce operations (marketplace dashboard, supplier contracts, logistics trail, domain ownership) is furnished. Legal constraint: any EMI account must be ring-fenced in USD or EUR, must never be denominated in WST, and must not accept Samoan-resident deposits or payments. The recommended blueprint is a hybrid: a licensed Samoan Tier-1 bank account serving as the domestic compliance anchor and correspondent gateway, paired with an offshore USD EMI or bank account executing the e-commerce receivable waterfall, thereby resolving the absence of direct Shopify/Klarna settlement on Samoa ISIC 4791 rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol for WS_IC: equity or shareholder-loan injections must originate from the identifiable UBO's or shareholder's personal/corporate account and be wired to the IC's designated USD operational account via SWIFT MT103. The wire must carry an explicit memo reference (e.g., 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - WS_IC' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN PER AGREEMENT DATED [X]'), the correct purpose code (aligning with the Samoan exchange-control capital category and, for correspondent banks, OECD/EBCD code 'C400' equity or 'C810' non-trade loan), and a cross-reference to the board resolution authorizing the allotment or borrowing. The receiving authorized dealer will classify the flow as a non-resident capital receipt (CAP code); where the funding originates from a Samoan family trust, the IC must verify that no Samoan-resident source of funds is involved and provide the trust deed extract. Required documentation bundle: board resolution; capital contribution or loan agreement; UBO identification; remitting bank's source-of-funds confirmation; certified copy of the IC's share-register allotment entry; and, where a licensed Samoan bank receives the funds, the exchange-control declaration form required by CBS. All inbound funds should remain in USD to avoid WST conversion and preserve the non-resident treasury profile.Outbound clearing rails for the fast-fashion trade settlement waterfall: (1) Routine supplier payments for garments, textiles, packaging, and freight are executed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the IC's USD operational account to Tier-1 East-Asian apparel factories, each payment referenced to the corresponding purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading; (2) Bulk seasonal inventory procurement is settled via irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (issued at ANZ/BSP Samoa and confirmed by a negotiating bank in Hong Kong or China), using 60-90-day usance drafts, clean-on-board bills of lading, and third-party inspection certificates; (3) Buyer-side settlement through Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL: because Samoa is not a supported settlement jurisdiction, the monetizing entity accesses these rails through a merchant-of-record operating company in a supported jurisdiction, which nets transaction and BNPL fees, then remits the weekly treasury balance to the IC's USD account via MT103 with memo 'E-COMMERCE SETTLEMENT - PERIOD [DATE]'; (4) Digital-operational disbursements (hosting, advertising, payment-gateway fees, software subscriptions) are cleared from the same USD account with matched invoices and gateway card statements; (5) All outbound beneficiaries are subject to OFAC and FIU sanctions screening, with name-and-address matching against supplier invoices in the documentary trail.On-shore USD/WST deliverable forward contracts, available only through CBS-licensed authorized dealers and only for genuine current-account or trade exposure; in practice unavailable to a non-resident International Company without underlying exchange-control documentation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on WST/USD booked in offshore FX hubs (Singapore, Hong Kong, London) where a counterparty is willing to price the thin off-shore WST market; administratively feasible but economically relevant only for discrete structural WST expenses rather than the main USD e-commerce flow.Cross-currency swaps (USD/WST) permitted by CBS only where a genuine underlying WST-denominated obligation exists (e.g., local Samoan service invoices or payroll); requires CBS exchange-control approval plus a full documentary underlying trail demonstrating commercial purpose.Natural balance-sheet hedging: because the IC's revenues (USD, EUR, GBP via Shopify, Klarna, and other BNPL rails) and its major costs (East-Asian factory invoices) are predominantly USD-denominated, the optimal hedge is natural offsetting: invoice all suppliers in USD, hold treasury reserves in USD, and maintain only a small WST operating buffer at a licensed Samoan bank for incidental local administrative costs.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW with material legal restrictions. Samoa (WSM) is not a supported settlement jurisdiction for the two specified target payment rails: Shopify Payments does not onboard Samoan-domiciled entities, and Klarna BNPL (managed-payout) channels are unavailable to Samoan legal entities; both rails must in practice be accessed through a supported merchant-of-record entity (e.g., a Hong Kong or Singapore operating company) that nets settlement proceeds and remits the balance to the IC's treasury account via SWIFT MT103. Global fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, and similar) do not directly list Samoa as a supported business-registration jurisdiction, and most EMI KYC policies decline tax-exempt offshore International Companies absent substantiated economic activity. However, a Samoan IC may be onboarded by a UK/EU-regulated EMI that accepts offshore corporate clients where: (i) the EMI's risk appetite tolerates the structure; (ii) full UBO disclosure with notarized passport and proof-of-address is provided; and (iii) evidence of genuine e-commerce operations (marketplace dashboard, supplier contracts, logistics trail, domain ownership) is furnished. Legal constraint: any EMI account must be ring-fenced in USD or EUR, must never be denominated in WST, and must not accept Samoan-resident deposits or payments. The recommended blueprint is a hybrid: a licensed Samoan Tier-1 bank account serving as the domestic compliance anchor and correspondent gateway, paired with an offshore USD EMI or bank account executing the e-commerce receivable waterfall, thereby resolving the absence of direct Shopify/Klarna settlement on Samoa ISIC 4791 rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa does not prescribe a formal published local-currency Local File/Master File threshold; the Income Tax Act 2015 s.76 mandates OECD-aligned arm's-length pricing with contemporaneous documentation required for any cross-border related-party transaction. For practical enforcement, a Samoan IC engaging in intra-group procurement or brand licensing of more than WST 1,000,000 per annum should maintain robust transfer pricing documentation.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Samoa's International Companies Economic Substance Rules (2020/2021) require an IC deriving relevant income from distribution and/or service activities (including digitally facilitated fast-fashion retail) to maintain real management in Samoa, hold board meetings in Samoa, have Samoa-resident officers, and keep adequate records. The fast-fashion e-commerce IC should preserve a physical operational footprint and payroll in Samoa to support its tax-holiday profile; the economic substance doctrine is enforced through annual reporting to the Samoa International Companies Registry and the Revenue authority.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Offshore Shell Abusing the IC Tax Holiday

Statutory sanction: Full reassessment of worldwide income at the 27% corporate rate, penalties of up to 200% of the under-assessed tax, criminal fines of up to WST 10,000, and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Tax Administration Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors face joint and several civil restitution for the unpaid tax, appointment of a liquidator by the Revenue Commissioner, issuance of a departure prohibition order preventing travel, and imprisonment on conviction where nominee structures are proven to be fraudulent.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner is empowered to adjust the transfer price to the arm's length amount, disallow all associated deductions, and impose an adjustment penalty of 50% of the net tax shortfall; uncertainty assessments may extend to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate transfer pricing declarations and related-party invoices are personally liable for the deprived revenue, are barred from holding directorships of Samoan companies, and may face criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years for knowingly creating false and misleading records.
AML and Payment-Rails Compliance Failures

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines up to WST 2,000,000, natural-person fines up to WST 100,000, imprisonment of up to 10 years, freezing of the IC's bank accounts, and deregistration of the company by the International Companies Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally sued by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit for compensation and confiscation of personal assets; individuals involved face asset-freezing orders, travel bans, and custodial sentences; deliberate evasion of AML controls lifts the corporate veil and renders directors principals to the offence.
Economic Substance Shortfall / Shell Exposure

Statutory sanction: Retrospective loss of the tax exemption, assessment of 27% corporate tax on all exempt income accrued during the substance-failure period, a penalty of WST 100,000, and automatic notification to foreign tax authorities through Samoa's Exchange of Information cooperation; persistent failure leads to strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors are issued personal notice of enforcement, held jointly and severally for the reassessed tax, subjected to a prohibition on leaving Samoa pending restitution, and criminally liable if the substance declarations are certified as knowingly false.
