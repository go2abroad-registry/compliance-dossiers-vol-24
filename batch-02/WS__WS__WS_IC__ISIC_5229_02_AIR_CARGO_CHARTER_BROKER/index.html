Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa; online taxpayer portal: https://www.mfr.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) obtains a Samoa Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by submitting to the MfR: (i) certified certificate of incorporation issued under the International Companies Act 1987; (ii) registered office confirmation; (iii) notarized passport copies of all directors and shareholders; (iv) completed TIN application form; and (v) proof of appointment of the licensed registered agent. The MfR issues the TIN within 5-10 business days; the IC then files annual returns with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL) Registry through the licensed agent.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned International Company (IC) obtains a Samoa Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by submitting to the MfR: (i) certified certificate of incorporation issued under the International Companies Act 1987; (ii) registered office confirmation; (iii) notarized passport copies of all directors and shareholders; (iv) completed TIN application form; and (v) proof of appointment of the licensed registered agent. The MfR issues the TIN within 5-10 business days; the IC then files annual returns with the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL) Registry through the licensed agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists. The International Companies Act 1987 grants an automatic statutory exemption from Samoan income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty and VAGST on international business income; an IC may elect to redomicile or convert to a domestic company, thereby entering the standard resident tax net, but such election is irrevocable for one tax year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered person may apply the 0% rate to: (i) goods exported from Samoa outside any free zone; and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply, where the direct benefit is consumed outside Samoa. Air cargo charter brokerage fees for international legs qualify where the principal contract evidences a foreign destination and the broker retains shipping documents.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API clearance is yet mandatory in Samoa; taxpayers file VAGST returns electronically through the MfR e-services portal, and invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form subject to retention for at least 7 years.

Samoa permits VAGST refunds within 45 days of application; fraudulent zero-rating or false export documentation attracts penalties under the VAGST Act 2003 of up to WST 100,000 fine and/or up to 7 years imprisonment, plus reassessment clawback for up to 8 years from the year of assessment, with interest at the statutory rate.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is partially convertible. Current-account flows such as freight payments, commissions and insurance are liberally approved through CBS-licensed authorized dealers, but capital-account transactions including equity injections, intragroup debt and external transfers exceeding WST 200,000 equivalent must either obtain CBS exchange control approval or rely on a statutory exemption. International Companies incorporated under the International Companies Act 2012 are exempt from the Exchange Control Act 1971 in respect of their foreign-currency, non-resident offshore business; the air-cargo charter brokerage can accordingly hold USD brokerage commissions offshore or in a licensed USD account in Samoa without profit-remittance restriction or Samoan withholding tax. Any WST conversion or payment to a Samoan resident, however, still passes through an authorized dealer and remains monitorable by the Central Bank of Samoa.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Samoan resident exporters, the Exchange Control Act 1971 and CBS administrative directives impose mandatory repatriation and 100% surrender of export proceeds into WST through an authorized dealer within three (3) months of export entitlement or receipt, unless a specific CBS retention or exemption approval is granted. The air-cargo charter-broker IC is treated as a non-resident under the International Companies Act 2012, so USD charter commissions and disbursement recoveries are not export proceeds subject to the surrender rule and may be retained in a designated USD account. To preserve this treatment, the IC must not book Samoan-source invoicing through a resident branch or permanent establishment, and the licensed Samoan registered agent must maintain the IC records exclusively in the offshore exempt segment.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Falefa), acting jointly with the Minister of Finance as the exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1971 (as amended), and as the AML/CFT supervisor under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary clearing rail; Medium only as a supplementary offshore USD collection vehicle. Samoa has no fintech EMI licensing category, and the Exchange Control Act 1971 channels resident payment business through CBS-authorized dealers. Practical legal constraints: (1) Samoan banks generally reject inbound MT103 transfers from unregulated digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business or Currenxie due to source-of-funds opacity and correspondent de-risking; (2) the EMI nostro chain is fragile for USD clearing; and (3) a CBS review will not accept an EMI as evidence of regulated banking status. If used, the EMI must provide per-transaction beneficiary and purpose data and the IC must periodically sweep balances into the primary Samoan USD account by SWIFT MT103.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank directly to the IC's USD account at the Samoan authorized dealer, with a memo line stating 'Capital injection - International Companies Act 2012 exchange-control exempt' and the ISIC 5229 air-cargo charter brokerage purpose. Pre-funding verification requires: (i) board resolution allotting shares; (ii) updated share register and register of directors; (iii) registered agent certificate; (iv) CBS exchange-control declaration notification where the aggregate injection exceeds WST 200,000 equivalent; (v) three-month bank statements and source-of-funds declaration from the subscribing shareholder; and (vi) certified directors' and signatories' passports and powers of attorney. Samoa does not use IBAN; beneficiary identification is the bank's SWIFT BIC plus the IC's USD account number, and the receiving bank must reject the inbound MT103 if the narrative lacks the purpose code or references a mismatched beneficiary legal name. Equity proceeds may be held in USD without CBS surrender until deployed for charter disbursements.Operational trade disbursements covering charter hire, block-space prepayments, aviation fuel, overflight and landing fees, insurance premiums and brokerage commissions are cleared via SWIFT MT103 against the bank's USD nostro in New York, with Fedwire or CHIPS used for same-day USD value where the beneficiary bank requires it. Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) are used for material prepayments typically above USD 100,000 equivalent under ACMI and block-space agreements; the documentary set comprises the signed charter agreement, air waybill or GSA acceptance, airline invoice, insurance certificate, and aircraft registration or air operator certificate extract. All outbound disbursements are matched to an underlying charter event, pass a senior compliance sign-off, and are reported to the Central Bank of Samoa through the authorized dealer's monthly balance-of-payments return. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% standard corporate income tax rate on Samoa-source income of resident domestic companies; 0% for International Companies on all international business income by statutory exemption.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident Samoa company to non-resident shareholders; 0% for International Companies paying dividends to non-resident shareholders by express exemption under s.199 of the International Companies Act 1987.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 6 months after the fiscal year-end balance date (standard balance date 31 December; return due 30 June); self-assessment payment due on the same date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 31 days following each anniversary of the date of incorporation, not the fiscal year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Assessable Income) Gross Samoa-source assessable business income derived during the income year. International Company: enter 0 where all receipts derive from offshore cargo charter brokering and no Samoa-source services are performed; attach a schedule categorizing receipts by source. Late filing: penalty of 20% of the tax payable (minimum WST 200); late payment interest accrues at 2% per month on unpaid balances; false or misleading returns attract an additional 100% penalty on the shortfall and prosecution of directors.

Box 3 (Deductions Relating to Exempt Income) Operating expenses including charter hire, fuel, brokerage commissions, communications, and registered agent fees allocable to exempt income. Claims must not reduce non-exempt taxable income; maintain segmented profit-and-loss accounts consistent with the IC audited financial statements. Late filing: penalty of 20% of the tax payable (minimum WST 200); late payment interest accrues at 2% per month on unpaid balances; false or misleading returns attract an additional 100% penalty on the shortfall and prosecution of directors.
Box 4 (Withholding Tax Offset) Samoa withholding taxes withheld on dividends, interest, or royalties derived by the IC from domestic sources. Attach Form WHT certificates and reconcile to the IC bank statements; exempt ICs rarely complete this box. Late filing: penalty of 20% of the tax payable (minimum WST 200); late payment interest accrues at 2% per month on unpaid balances; false or misleading returns attract an additional 100% penalty on the shortfall and prosecution of directors.
Section A (Company Particulars) Registered name, company number, registered office address in Samoa, and licensed registered agent details. Ensure the registered agent in Samoa is a SIFSA-licensed Trust Company; any agent change must be endorsed within 14 days. Late filing fee of WST 100 per month after the anniversary date; persistent default for 6 months exposes the IC to administrative strike-off from the register and forfeiture of assets to the Crown; directors are personally liable for unpaid annual fees and penalties.
Section B (Shareholders and Directors) Full name, address, nationality, and shareholding percentage of each shareholder; details of directors and secretary. Non-resident beneficial owners may be natural persons or corporate shareholders; the beneficial ownership register must be current and available for inspection under the International Companies beneficial ownership requirements. Late filing fee of WST 100 per month after the anniversary date; persistent default for 6 months exposes the IC to administrative strike-off from the register and forfeiture of assets to the Crown; directors are personally liable for unpaid annual fees and penalties.
Section C (Statement of No Local Business) Declaration that the IC did not carry on business with Samoa residents or derive Samoa-source income during the year. A false declaration converting local trading into an IC wrapper invalidates the exemption and triggers retrospective taxation and deregistration. Late filing fee of WST 100 per month after the anniversary date; persistent default for 6 months exposes the IC to administrative strike-off from the register and forfeiture of assets to the Crown; directors are personally liable for unpaid annual fees and penalties.
Section D (Annual Fee and Penalty Schedule) Government annual fee and SIFSA filing acknowledgment for the current year. The annual government fee is generally assessed at WST 1,000 or equivalent USD and must be settled before the return is accepted. Late filing fee of WST 100 per month after the anniversary date; persistent default for 6 months exposes the IC to administrative strike-off from the register and forfeiture of assets to the Crown; directors are personally liable for unpaid annual fees and penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No formal Samoa transfer pricing documentation threshold is legislated for International Companies; however, the Income Tax Act 1974 ss.81-83 authorizes the Commissioner to reallocate international transactions to an arm's length basis. SIFSA expects gross related-party charter and brokerage flows exceeding WST 2,000,000 per annum to be supported by contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and functional analysis) aligned to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations apply through the International Companies Act 1987 and SIFSA administrative guidance. An International Company must maintain a registered office and licensed registered agent in Samoa and demonstrate effective management in Samoa through board meetings or written resolutions. For an air cargo charter broker, acceptable substance evidence includes: senior management meetings in Samoa, charter contracts signed by Samoa-resident officers, billing and collection routed through Samoa bank accounts, and a commensurate local payroll and office footprint. Failure to maintain substance allows SIFSA to strike off the IC or reclassify it as a Samoa-resident taxable company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Abuse of Tax-Exempt Status (Domestic Revenue Contamination)

Statutory sanction: Retroactive withdrawal of the s.199 exemption; back-assessment at the 27% corporate rate for all years involved; a 100% shortfall penalty and 2% monthly late-payment interest; deregistration of the IC under the International Companies Act 1987.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the false exemption claim are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; criminal conviction for tax evasion carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to WST 100,000.
Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus 2% monthly interest; seizure of bank accounts; criminal conviction imposing a fine not exceeding WST 200,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is statutorily permitted: directors and shadow directors are personally liable for restitution of the shortfall where the IC is insolvent; travel bans and asset freezing orders may be issued by the Supreme Court.
Shell Company / Hollow Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SIFSA strike-off and dissolution; loss of limited liability protection; revocation of the registered agent's license for the IC; inclusion on a publicly listed struck-off register damaging bank and counterparty standing.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally sued by creditors if the IC traded with willful disregard for substance requirements; the court may disqualify directors for up to 5 years and issue deportation or travel-ban orders where the director is non-resident.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Financing of Terrorism Failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory penalties for body corporates up to WST 1,000,000 plus daily default fines; freezing of accounts; suspension or revocation of the air cargo brokerage license; requirement to appoint a SIFSA-approved compliance officer and external auditor.

Director personal liability: Directors, senior managers, and compliance officers are personally exposed to criminal money laundering charges carrying imprisonment up to 14 years and property forfeiture; travel bans can be imposed pending prosecution, and professional disqualification orders terminate director service.
