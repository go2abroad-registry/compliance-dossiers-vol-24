Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official portal: https://customs.gov.vu; TIN/VAT registration administered via VRC e-Service; company registration administered by Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 222].

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned entity: (1) incorporate as an International Company (IC) through a VFSC-licensed registered agent, obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation and statutory registers; (2) apply to VRC for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) only if the entity carries on a taxable activity in Vanuatu, using Form VAT-1; an IC that conducts no domestic supply, has no Vanuatu physical establishment, and derives income exclusively from offshore business is not obliged to obtain a TIN because Vanuatu levies no national income tax; (3) obtain a municipal Business Licence only if physically operating a business in Vanuatu, which an IC is generally prohibited from doing; (4) if importing goods or equipment into Vanuatu, the importer must hold a TIN and register with Customs for clearance purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (International Company zero-tax regime): Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax, personal income tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax, gift tax, or withholding tax on ICs; the only indirect tax exposure is VAT on taxable imports and local taxable supplies, plus stamp duty on instruments relating to land situated in Vanuatu. — Foreign-owned entity: (1) incorporate as an International Company (IC) through a VFSC-licensed registered agent, obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation and statutory registers; (2) apply to VRC for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) only if the entity carries on a taxable activity in Vanuatu, using Form VAT-1; an IC that conducts no domestic supply, has no Vanuatu physical establishment, and derives income exclusively from offshore business is not obliged to obtain a TIN because Vanuatu levies no national income tax; (3) obtain a municipal Business Licence only if physically operating a business in Vanuatu, which an IC is generally prohibited from doing; (4) if importing goods or equipment into Vanuatu, the importer must hold a TIN and register with Customs for clearance purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (International Company zero-tax regime): Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax, personal income tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax, gift tax, or withholding tax on ICs; the only indirect tax exposure is VAT on taxable imports and local taxable supplies, plus stamp duty on instruments relating to land situated in Vanuatu.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, transparent-entity, or hybrid-mismatch elections exist under Vanuatu law because there is no corporate income tax regime; entities below the VAT registration threshold may voluntarily elect to register under the Value Added Tax Act No. 6 of 2014 to recover input VAT, and taxable suppliers may elect to apply the simplified invoicing rules; Vanuatu has no tax treaty network, so treaty-based hybrid planning is unavailable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 10,000,000 annual taxable turnover for suppliers of goods; VUV 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover for suppliers of services; voluntary registration is permitted for businesses below the thresholds; non-resident digital service suppliers may be subject to registration or reverse-charge obligations when supplying services to Vanuatu-resident businesses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (1) exported goods where legal title passes to a non-resident and the goods are physically exported from Vanuatu within a prescribed period; and (2) services supplied to a non-resident for the direct use and economic benefit of the non-resident outside Vanuatu, including offshore B2B logistics-fleet telematics and SaaS subscriptions, provided no Vanuatu-resident person receives the benefit; the supplier must obtain and retain evidential documentation of export or offshore use, and zero-rated supplies entitle the supplier to full credit for attributable input VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED) lodged in ASYCUDA World and endorsed by Vanuatu Customs
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or endorsed courier receipt evidencing physical export movement
  • Commercial invoice and executed service agreement evidencing supply to a non-resident and offshore use
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing settlement from the foreign counterparty
  • Telematics/SaaS usage records and server-access logs demonstrating that services were consumed outside Vanuatu

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is in force in Vanuatu; registered taxpayers must issue prescribed VAT invoices or simplified invoices under the VAT Act and file monthly VAT returns with the VRC, retaining source records for audit; customs declarations are processed through Vanuatu's ASYCUDA World single-window system.

VRC audit powers allow reversal of unsupported zero-rated claims with VAT, interest, and penalties up to 150% of the evaded tax, and criminal prosecution of directors under the VAT Act sections 67 to 69; false Customs declarations attract seizure of goods, fines, and imprisonment under the Customs Act [Cap 71]; Vanuatu imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline, so the 180-day period functions as a banking/AML compliance convention and documentary expectation rather than a domestic clawback trigger; retrospectively falsified documentation may also result in deregistration, public naming by VRC, and liability under anti-money-laundering legislation administered by the VFSC.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains current-account convertibility: longitudinal subscription, royalty, and service payments may be made without prior central bank approval. Capital-account transactions are reportable but not prohibited; inward capital increases into a non-resident International Company are allowed through an authorized dealer, although the receiving bank may require an internal approval mandate above VUV 10,000,000. Conversion of trade-value receipts into VUV is not obligatory; the Vatu floats and is not blocked for legitimate corporate flows. Profit-remittance hurdles are evidentiary, not statutory: an exempted Vanuatu IC can distribute dividends to non-resident shareholders with zero withholding tax, but the distributing bank requires a board resolution declaring the dividend, a current share register entry, an updated VFSC certificate of good standing, and a signed copy of the IC's exempt-status confirmation from the registered agent before initiating the SWIFT wire.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory export surrender ratio for a non-resident International Company. For a Vanuatu resident exporter, full proceeds must be repatriated to Vanuatu through an RBV-licensed authorized dealer within 180 days of negotiation of the invoice; no VUV conversion is required and the proceeds may be retained in foreign currency if the exporter can demonstrate a balance-sheet justification. For the VU IC engaged in logistics SaaS, export proceeds consist of cross-border telematics subscription fees; the company should evidence repatriation by monthly SWIFT GPI sweeps from its PSP or settlement bank, and must retain invoices and purpose codes for five years in order to satisfy an authorized dealer's exchange-control reporting obligation. The statutory default of 180 days applies only to residents; the IC is treated as non-resident and may maintain 100% foreign-currency balances offshore without surrender.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), acting as the exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 60]; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) separately licenses and supervises banks and financial service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium — conditionally feasible. A Vanuatu International Company is not statutorily barred from maintaining an account with a non-resident EMI, but the RBV's exchange-control practice expects all resident-sourced settlements to pass through an authorized dealer. Where the IC is truly non-resident, an offshore EMI can be used as a collection-only sweep account for Stripe/PSP clearings; however, major low-cost EMIs often reject shell or economic-substance-light entities, and correspondent banks route funds only if underlying KYC files show shareholders and beneficial owners. Clearance into the primary VU bank is cleanest via an SWIFT MT103/GPIB sweep; direct EMI-to-EMI flows without a bank intermediary are not accepted by Vanuatu banks and may trigger source-of-funds review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use an SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's home banking institution, addressed to the receiving Vanuatu authorized dealer (for example, ANZ's central treasury or BSP's Port Vila branch) with ISO 20022 purpose code 'INTC' or 'SHAR' (for equity subscription) and free-text field 70 'CAP_IC/01 - SHARE CAPITAL INCREASE - BENEFICIAL OWNER NAME' to avoid a generic transfer. If the receiving bank uses RBV reporting forms, complete the 'Capital Transfer / Source of Funds' declaration with the exact purpose: 'Equity injection for telematics logistics SaaS platform'. The 77B/Regulatory reporting field may state: 'REG/INIT/Fund from ABC BANK account in country X; no intermediary; not crypto; if a chain of correspondents is unavoidable, verify each intermediary with a full chain of title from the original bank to the beneficiary. On the receiving side, file a signed board resolution (approving issue of non-cash consideration shares if appropriate), updated register of members, and the IC's share certificate book entry to evidence the capital injection.SWIFT MT103 (GPIB) is the primary clearing rail for all operational disbursements, using UETR-enabled messages and field 70s that state the purpose: 'SUPP - software & hosting costs', 'ROYL - patent/telematics royalty', or 'PCOM' for distribution commission. Where the disbursement funds a genuine physical fleet-hardware purchase (e.g., GNSS/GPS units, in-cab tablets), a commercial or standby letter of credit can be issued by the account-holding VU bank, confirmed by a top-tier correspondent, with sight drafts and inspection documents; for routine disbursements an irrevocable LC is over-engineered. For low-value subscription refunds to fleet operators, use SEPA instant/local real-time rails only when the counterparty is in an eligible jurisdiction; otherwise route through SWIFT with 'RFDN/REFND' purpose. Late VFSC filing triggers a rehabilitation fee, penalty of up to 10% of the annual license fee, and possible administrative strike-off after 6 months of delinquency.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not cast in local currency because Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax and operates no transfer-pricing statute for ICs; if the IC becomes a VAT-registered Vanuatu-resident enterprise, no Local File/Master File obligation exists under current domestic law.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu’s Economic Substance Regulations apply to ICs performing relevant activities (finance, leasing, headquarters, holding, distribution/service center, IP holding). A logistics fleet dispatch SaaS directed solely at non-resident customers is generally outside the listed relevant activities; however, any booking of revenue through a Vanuatu branch or related-party IP licensing could bring the operation into scope and require board meetings, physical office, expenditure, and local substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / trading with residents while claiming IC exemption

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of IC tax exemptions, retro-active VAT assessment at 12.5% (subject to current VU VAT law), customs-duty reassessment, plus administrative fines imposed by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission.

Director personal liability: Directors may personally owe restitution for unlawful avoided VAT and be disqualified from management; culpable directors risk arrest and imprisonment under Vanuatu criminal law for filing false declarations.
Shell company / sham economic substance

Statutory sanction: Striking-off of the IC, confinement to a VFSC enforcement docket, and monetary penalties for non-compliance with Economic Substance Regulations or International Companies Act record-keeping duties.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil, imposing civil restitution and travel bans; directors or shareholder-beneficiaries can be ordered to compensate creditors and may face custodial sentences for fraudulent misuse of corporate register.
AML/CFT failure and suspicious transaction non-reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Vanuatu’s Financial Transaction Reporting and Money Laundering acts, with fines, account seizure, and possible imprisonment (up to 20 years for aggravated money-laundering cases).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to file suspicious transaction reports may incur criminal liability and be jailed; the court can order confiscation of personal assets and issue international travel bans pending extradition.
False public filings / forgery

Statutory sanction: Rescission of the certificate of incorporation, fine of up to VUV 1,000,000 (or other statutory amount), and mandatory disclosure to foreign competent authorities through international EOI requests.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution to defrauded third parties; deliberate forgery carries custodial penalty; travel bans are routinely sought during extradition and mutual legal assistance proceedings.
