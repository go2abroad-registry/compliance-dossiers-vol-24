Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue, Government of Samoa (Inland Revenue Division); official online portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws. Corporate and International Company registry oversight: Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), https://www.sifa.gov.ws.

E-government filing portal

A Samoa International Company (IC) is an offshore exempt entity and is not required to obtain a Samoa TIN or VAT registration number. Its statutory tax identity is the certificate of incorporation and certificate of good standing issued by SIFA; no revenue-ministry TIN is issued unless the IC is compulsorily registered for a local taxable activity (which an IC is generally prohibited from carrying on). If a foreign entity nevertheless requires a Samoa VAT/TIN identifier for a permitted limited purpose, it must submit Form VAT-1 (Compulsory Registration), together with SIFA certificate, articles of association, directorship details, and a local physical or registered-office address, to the Ministry of Revenue. A TIN/VAT number is then issued within the turn-around time applied by the Ministry, subject to anti-avoidance review.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election, hybrid-mismatch election, or look-through classification exists under Samoa tax law. The applicable elections are corporate-law based: (a) an IC may elect in its articles to avail itself of the tax-exempt status conferred by the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended); (b) it may by special resolution elect to continue or re-domicile to another recognized jurisdiction; (c) it may elect to register as a domestic company, which prospectively surrenders the offshore tax exemption but does not create a retroactive Samoan tax liability for the period of exempt status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 50,000 (Samoa tala) of taxable supplies made in Samoa during the current or any preceding consecutive 12-month period. An International Company operating as a pure offshore e-commerce vendor of pet supplies to non-resident customers does not, by reason of a registered office in Samoa alone, have a Samoan VAT registration nexus; the threshold applies only to persons carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa, which an IC is statutorily barred from doing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT Act 2000, exports are zero-rated where the goods are physically exported from Samoa by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf within the time limits prescribed by the VAT regime, the supply is made to a purchaser resident outside Samoa, and the supplier retains documentary evidence of export. For services, zero-rating applies to Services supplied to a person not resident in Samoa where the benefit is derived entirely outside Samoa. A Samoa International Company, being an exempt offshore entity, is not a VAT-registered taxable person; therefore it neither charges VAT on overseas online pet-supplies sales nor claims export VAT refunds. If an IC were wrongly or compulsorily VAT-registered, it may zero-rate genuine export supplies only on the complete customs and banking documentation set described below.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration (electronic SED or Customs Export Entry) showing the exporter of record and the foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing physical departure from Samoa customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list matching the export declaration
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT credit advice, or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Certificate of origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed) and any supplier/exporter contract or purchase order
  • Proof of export within the statutory VAT deadline (e.g., cargo manifest, customs clearance stamp, or freight carrier departure record)

Samoa does not operate a mandatory continuous transaction control regime, real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoice stamping, or API-based e-invoicing requirement. VAT-registered suppliers must issue a tax invoice containing the supplier's name and address, VAT registration number, date, description, quantity, price, and VAT chargeable. VAT returns are filed electronically or in paper through the Ministry of Revenue's prescribed forms; no separate electronic clearance bride interface is required for standard exports.

The Commissioner of Revenue may conduct retrospective audits of zero-rated export claims, including inspection of customs export records, transportation documents, bank FX flows, and counterparty contracts. Where zero-rating is found to have been claimed fraudulently or without sufficient export evidence, the claim is set aside, the VAT becomes due from the original supply date, and all interest and penalties accrue as provided under Samoa tax and customs laws. The Revenue may claw back any refund or credit previously granted, set it off against other taxpayer liabilities, and pursue the debt as a civil tax debt without limitation periods for fraud. Administrative penalties apply for false declarations, failure to keep records, and unregistered taxable activity, and the directors/officers of the supplier may face personal liability and criminal prosecution for tax evasion. The burden of proving that the goods were exported and consideration remitted from outside Samoa rests entirely on the claimant, and the Ministry of Revenue is not bound by any prior clearance or refund decision when fraud is subsequently discovered.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is convertible for current international transactions; Samoa maintains no exchange restrictions on payments and transfers for current-account purposes, but all inward and outward transfers are subject to statutory reporting and exchange-control monitoring by the Central Bank of Samoa. Capital-account transactions, including equity injections, shareholder loans, assignment of shares, and liquidation repatriations by non-residents, require prior CBS exchange-control approval or authorization processed through a licensed commercial bank. Profit/dividend remittances are treated as current transfers once the underlying financial statements are audited, filed, and tax compliance is evidenced; for a Samoa International Company operating in the offshore ring-fence, transactions are executed in foreign currency, which minimizes local WST conversion friction. Principal remittance hurdle: absent a clean UBO register, audited accounts, and a CBS-approved capital verification file, dividend and capital repatriation instructions may be suspended at the correspondent-bank level for de-risking reasons.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Where the IC physically exports goods from Samoa, the Exchange Control Regulations require export proceeds to be received through a licensed Samoan commercial bank within ninety (90) days of shipment, and, unless a foreign-currency-account retention exemption is granted by the CBS, 100 percent of repatriated proceeds must be offered for conversion into WST at the prevailing CBS-determined commercial rate within the prescribed surrender window. For the target ISIC 4791 model (electronic retailing of pet supplies), inventory is ordinarily drop-shipped from third-country suppliers to end-buyers without Samoan customs export, so no export surrender obligation is triggered; the IC must nevertheless maintain documentation proving the absence of territorial export and must repatriate to Samoa any sales proceeds that are booked or received in Samoa within the standard repatriation window.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with strict legal boundaries. Samoa does not license foreign digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) as banks; EMIs such as Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer, Wise, and Airwallex may be used only as unlicensed payment-collection and disbursement switches, never as the primary treasury depository. Legal constraints: (i) any EMI activity that accepts Samoan-resident funds in WST without a CBS banking or money-remittance licence would constitute unlawful deposit-taking/money transmission; (ii) global EMIs routinely freeze accounts of Samoa International Companies lacking economic-substance documentation; and (iii) card-network rules restrict the EMI to merchant-of-record fronting rather than corporate treasury clearing. Recommended architecture: Stripe/PayPal as card-acquiring rails sending net settlements via automated payout to the ANZ or BSP Samoa account, with EMI accounts used solely for supplier payables denominated in third-country currencies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share-capital and subordinated-shareholder-loan injections enter Samoa exclusively by SWIFT MT103 to a foreign-currency account in the IC's legal name at a licensed Samoan bank. Wire memo (field 70) must state 'INWARD CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / WS_IC / ISIC 4791'; the licensed bank codes the transfer under Balance-of-Payments category 5211 (Equity Capital) on its CBS exchange-control return. Verification steps: remitter identity matched to the UBO register, certified board authorization, and source-of-funds records. Routing via third-country shell banks or digital-EMI pass-through accounts must be avoided because it breaks the correspondent audit trail and triggers de-risking. Capital receipt must be booked to the IC capital ledger and the CBS authorization/confirmation retained for future dividend and liquidation remittances.Supplier and logistics disbursements clear by: (i) SWIFT MT103 priority remittance for high-value supplier invoices, intercompany loan servicing, and tax/government fees; (ii) commercial letters of credit (irrevocable sight L/Cs) issued through the ANZ/BSP Samoa trade desks or their offshore parent banks for large inventory purchase orders; and (iii) push-pull settlement instructions to Stripe/PayPal for payment-gateway fees and refund obligations. Correspondent clearing is effected in USD, EUR, or AUD through the parent banks' New York, Sydney, and Auckland corridors; WST-denominated outward current payments require CBS exchange-control settlement approval only in non-trade cases. Every outward instruction must be sanctions-scanned (OFAC/EU) and reference-matched to underlying purchase orders and commercial invoices.Deliverable OTC forward contracts (USD/WST, USD/AUD, and EUR/USD) with ANZ Bank Samoa or Bank of South Pacific Samoa, limited to underlying transactional exposure and requiring bank-supported UBO documentation.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing WST executed with non-resident OECD-licensed bank counterparties, permitted only with prior CBS notification/approval as a capital-account derivative.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between USD card-collection flows and the functional settlement currency, permitted where the swap maturity matches the operating cash-flow cycle and the counterparty is a CBS-recognized international bank.
