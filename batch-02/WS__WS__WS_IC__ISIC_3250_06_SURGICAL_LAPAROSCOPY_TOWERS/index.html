Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue, Government of Samoa (official portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws; taxpayer online services and electronic VAGST filing are available through the Ministry's Samoa Taxpayer Online System)

E-government filing portal

For a Samoa International Company ('IC'): (1) obtain the certificate of incorporation and International Company status certificate from the Registrar of Companies; (2) if the IC will make any taxable supplies in Samoa, obtain a TIN/VAGST registration by filing the prescribed registration application through the Ministry of Revenue online portal; (3) provide the IC incorporation number, local registered agent details, registered office address, expected annual turnover, and description of business activities; (4) the Ministry of Revenue issues a tax identification number and/or VAGST registration number. An IC transacting exclusively offshore is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax and VAGST and is not otherwise required to complete annual national income-tax registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — For a Samoa International Company ('IC'): (1) obtain the certificate of incorporation and International Company status certificate from the Registrar of Companies; (2) if the IC will make any taxable supplies in Samoa, obtain a TIN/VAGST registration by filing the prescribed registration application through the Ministry of Revenue online portal; (3) provide the IC incorporation number, local registered agent details, registered office address, expected annual turnover, and description of business activities; (4) the Ministry of Revenue issues a tax identification number and/or VAGST registration number. An IC transacting exclusively offshore is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax and VAGST and is not otherwise required to complete annual national income-tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity-classification election exists in Samoa. The IC's tax-exempt status is conferred non-electively under the International Companies Act 1987. An IC may elect to surrender its international status with the approval of the Registrar, thereby becoming an ordinary Samoa company subject to entity-level income tax and VAGST. VAGST-registered businesses may elect grouping or tax-period arrangements under the Tax Administration Act 2012. Samoa does not currently provide hybrid-mismatch or pass-through elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory threshold; voluntary registration is permitted for suppliers making lower-value supplies; importers may be required to register when importations exceed the prescribed minimum).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAGST-registered exporters may zero-rate tangible goods (including Ultra-HD 4K minimally invasive rigid endoscopes and surgical laparoscopy towers) where the goods are physically exported from Samoa and the exporter retains documentary evidence of shipment to the non-resident buyer. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident recipient, where the benefit of the service is consumed entirely outside Samoa, may also be zero-rated. In each case the supply must be made to an offshore recipient and invoiced in the ordinary course; local supplies in Samoa are taxable unless a schedule exemption applies. An International Company operating outside Samoa is outside the VAGST net, but where it incurs Samoan VAGST on inputs, a zero-rated export pathway permits refund via the Ministry of Revenue.

  • Samoa Customs export declaration (Customs Export Entry/Exit statement)
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing export from Samoa to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT wire transfer evidence confirming receipt of export proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • VAGST zero-rated sales invoice (where the exporter is VAGST-registered)
  • Certificate of origin or any certificate or export permit required for medical device exports

Samoa has not mandated real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API clearance, or government-issued digital stamping for commercial invoices. A VAGST-registered supplier must issue invoices containing prescribed particulars (supplier name and address, VAGST registration number, invoice date and number, description and quantity, consideration, and VAGST charged). VAGST returns and payments are filed electronically through the Ministry of Revenue's online system, but no per-invoice government pre-clearance is currently enforced.

The Ministry of Revenue may require export documentation evidence before allowing or retaining any zero-rating benefit and may review refund claims for up to the statutory document-retention period (generally not less than five years). Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating claims attract reassessment to the full VAGST shortfall, interest at the prescribed statutory rate, and civil penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2012, including penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall. Knowingly providing false or misleading customs or tax documentation may expose officers to criminal fines and imprisonment. Any refund paid is recoverable as tax due, with interest and penalty, if the export is not completed, the prescribed evidence is not produced, or the export consideration is not received within the required period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa accepted IMF Article VIII obligations (2011), confirming free convertibility for current-account payments and transfers. Current-account transactions (trade settlement, services, dividends, and branch profits) are remittable through authorized dealers without CBS case-by-case approval, subject to standard supporting-documentation checks. Capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, debt repayment other than trade credit) remain subject to CBS licensing or authorized-dealer delegated limits. For the tax-exempt International Company (International Companies Act 1987), dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are not subject to withholding tax, provided audited financial evidence and a valid tax-exemption certificate are filed with the bank; the bank makes a delegated-authority determination and files an exchange-control declaration with CBS for statistical purposes. WST is not a deliverable hard currency offshore, so clearing and settlement are ultimately executed in USD, AUD, or NZD. The practical convertibility hurdle is not legal prohibition but the bank's AML/KYC evidence requirements and the 2-5 business day CBS reporting window for larger capital movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1975, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa through an authorized dealer within six (6) months of the date of export (or such extended period as CBS may permit). The statutory default is that 100% of foreign-currency export proceeds must be offered for conversion into WST. However, an International Company may obtain CBS approval to maintain a designated Foreign Currency Account (FCA) at a Samoan bank, permitting retention of USD proceeds where the underlying contract, counterparties, and payment flows are entirely non-resident. Practically, the CBS and authorized dealers grant blanket FCA retention for International Companies with a SIFA-registered agent, audited books, and monthly/quarterly exchange-control reporting, provided the proceeds are not used to fund domestic WST expenditure.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, with exclusive foreign-exchange administration under the Exchange Control Act 1974, the Exchange Control Regulations 1975, and delegated authority to authorized dealers (licensed commercial banks). The CBS operates a managed-float currency regime for the Samoan Tala (WST), which is referenced against a trade-weighted basket of AUD, NZD, USD, EUR, and JPY.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low/Prohibited in substance. Samoa has no digital bank, EMI, or virtual-asset licensing regime, and the CBS does not recognize foreign electronic-money institutions as authorized dealers. Samoan commercial banks will not accept or execute instructions to/from unlicensed EMI wallets (including EEA-licensed EMIs or crypto on/off-ramps) due to correspondent-bank de-risking (APG mutual evaluation scrutiny). The only compliant cross-border rails are SWIFT MT103/MT202 via a Samoan authorized dealer and its correspondent banking relationships (typically BSP's AUD/USD corridor through Australia). An offshore EMI may be used only as a non-banking aggregator for reconciliation, never as a settlement rail, and must be declared to the Samoan bank in the KYC file to avoid account freezing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing sequence: (1) The non-resident shareholder executes a board resolution and share-subscription agreement referencing the International Company registry number and the endoscope-manufacturing business purpose (ISIC 3250); (2) the shareholder remits USD/AUD via SWIFT MT103 from its own bank account to the Samoa IC's dedicated Capital Escrow Account at BSP/NBS/SCB, with the SWIFT field 70 remittance information stating 'Equity injection under International Companies Act 1987 - WS_IC'; the remitter name in field 50 must be the legal entity's exact name as registered, not a nominee or alias; (3) the receiving bank files an Exchange Control Declaration (Capital Import) with the CBS via the delegated-authority portal, attaching the subscription agreement and BO register; (4) CBS approval or deemed approval is typically issued within 2-5 business days for amounts up to the authorized-dealer delegated ceiling (approximately WST 2 million equivalent; higher amounts go to CBS case-by-case); (5) after approval, escrowed funds are released to the operational USD account; (6) the bank issues a Certificate of Capital Import, which serves as the documentary anchor for future dividend repatriation and for any FX hedge application. The IC must not co-mingle trade receipts with the capital injection; separate sub-accounts are mandatory to facilitate CBS audit.Required clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 telecommunication wires for supplier payments (component imports for 4K laparoscopy towers, optics, cables, sterile packaging) up to the authorized-dealer delegated limit, supported by a proforma invoice, purchase order, and contract; (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by BSP/NBS/SCB on an irrevocable, confirmed basis for higher-value shipments, with the IC as applicant and the Chinese/EU/US component manufacturer as beneficiary; documents must include commercial invoice, bill of lading, insurance certificate, and packing list; (c) standby LCs (MT760) for distributor performance guarantees where hospital tenders require it; (d) direct USD wires to EU/US clearing via BSP's correspondent banking corridor (Australia-based), typically 2-5 business days. All outbound disbursements above the delegated ceiling require prior CBS exchange-control approval and must cite the IC reference and the underlying ISIC 3250 purchase order number. Partial prepayments above 30% are disfavored; the bank will require an L/C or third-party escrow to mitigate trade-based money-laundering risk. USD inflows from medical-device buyers may be routed via TT Wire without an LC, but the bank must see the purchase order and the IC's sales invoice (MT103 plus supporting documents retained in file for 7 years).Onshore FX spot contracts (USD/WST, AUD/WST, NZD/WST) executed with the authorized dealer for same-day settlement of trade obligations.Onshore FX forward contracts (maximum tenor 12 months) on USD/WST, AUD/WST, and NZD/WST, permitted against underlying trade documentation (export proceeds or import payables) and limited to 100% of the documented exposure; these are offered by BSP in practice, with two-way pricing against the CBS official rate.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing WST, permissible only with prior CBS notification and proof of genuine economic exposure (hedging, not speculation); market depth is extremely thin and execution is rarely available, so most ICs rely on multi-currency accounts to achieve natural hedging.Cross-currency swaps with a licensed commercial bank for IC debt obligations, subject to CBS capital-account approval; in practice, Samoa's onshore market does not support such instruments, and the IC should instead denominate its intercompany loan in USD to avoid WST conversion risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 of aggregate controlled transactions (or 30% of gross revenue if lower) requires a contemporaneous Local File; a Master File is expected when the worldwide group revenue exceeds WST 100,000,000. Exempt ICs file the economic substance annex instead of an income-tax TP schedule but should keep the same transfer pricing documentation available for substance review.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's ESR under the International Companies (Economic Substance) (Amendment) Act 2018 requires every IC carrying on a relevant activity (including distribution and service centres or IP holding) to demonstrate adequate board management, employees, and physical expenditure in Samoa proportionate to the activity. If the IC merely invoices sales of 4K laparoscopy towers and all manufacturing is performed by an offshore affiliate, a pure distribution activity exists and the substance threshold must be met in Samoa by a local office or by a compliant outsourced service provider; otherwise the IC will be treated as non-compliant and subject to exchange of information reporting.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at 27% plus a 200% penalty on tax shortfall; administrative penalty up to WST 1,000,000 for false declarations; deregistration of the IC; referral to the criminal courts for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are jointly and severally liable, may be disqualified for 10 years, and may be imprisoned for up to 7 years; the court may also issue a travel ban and asset freeze pending prosecution.
Shell Company Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the International Companies Register, forfeiture of the exempt status, refund of any distributions illegally made, and a fine of up to WST 100,000 under the International Companies Act.

Director personal liability: The court will pierce the corporate veil; directors and shadow shareholders receive a civil restitution order covering the diverted profits and are personally liable for all liquidation costs and unpaid supplier invoices.
AML / Medical Device Licensing / Sanctions

Statutory sanction: FIU administrative fine up to WST 500,000, criminal fine up to WST 1,000,000, forfeiture of the transaction funds, cancellation of the registered agent, and reporting to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit as a suspicious transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize unlicensed exports or sanctions evasion risk up to 10 years imprisonment, personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and being named in an AML enforcement action by Samoa's FIU.
Transfer Pricing Contravention

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income, additional tax of 200% of the shortfall, interest at 8% per annum, and a WST 50,000 penalty for failure to file the contemporaneous Local File.

Director personal liability: Where the TP manipulation is fraudulent, directors can be made personally liable for the adjusted tax, barred from managing companies, and prohibited from leaving Samoa until the tax debt is settled.
