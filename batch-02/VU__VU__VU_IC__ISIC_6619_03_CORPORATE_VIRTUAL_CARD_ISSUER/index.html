Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; portal: https://customs.invanuatu.gov.vu

E-government filing portal

A foreign or offshore entity must submit the VAT registration application (Form VAT-1) together with certified constitutional documents, proof of physical or registered address, VFSC registration certificate (for International Companies), and bank account details. The application is filed with the Comptroller of Customs and Inland Revenue. An International Company formed under the International Companies Act is exempt from national income tax and is not ordinarily required to obtain a TIN; however, it must maintain its VFSC registration number and may optionally obtain a VAT ID only if it carries on nonexempt taxable activities in Vanuatu, which is inconsistent with the offshore restriction.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign or offshore entity must submit the VAT registration application (Form VAT-1) together with certified constitutional documents, proof of physical or registered address, VFSC registration certificate (for International Companies), and bank account details. The application is filed with the Comptroller of Customs and Inland Revenue. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no check-the-box election. International Companies automatically receive a statutory tax exemption under the International Companies Act, unless they violate the offshore activity restriction and become subject to domestic VAT. There are no hybrid mismatch elections because Vanuatu imposes no income tax on offshore entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 (Vanuatu Vatu) of taxable supplies in any 12-month consecutive period; offshore International Companies providing no domestic taxable supplies are not registrable

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT-registered supplier may zero-rate exports of goods where the goods are physically exported outside Vanuatu within the prescribed period, transport services connected with those exports, and supplies of services to a non-resident where the benefit of the services occurs wholly outside Vanuatu. An International Company with the restricted offshore business model is not a VAT-registered trader and therefore cannot practically claim input VAT refunds; any incidental export-like supply would fall under the tax-exempt offshore status rather than the VAT zero-rating mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-reporting, digital stamping, or continuous invoice-control mandate is in force. VAT-registered businesses must issue compliant paper or electronic VAT invoices. Export declarations are processed through the ASYCUDA World customs administration system without a mandatory third-party fiscal clearance API.

Fraudulent or erroneous claims of export zero-rating trigger retrospective assessments, with the full VAT deducted as input credit clawed back plus late-payment penalties and interest. Making a false declaration to the Customs authority or VAT administration constitutes an offence punishable by monetary penalties up to three times the tax evaded and, for serious cases, imprisonment. An International Company that attempts to use zero-rating while breaching the offshore restriction risks loss of the statutory tax-exempt status, retroactive taxation, and VFSC regulatory sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is administered against a trade-weighted basket of the AUD, USD and SDR. Vanuatu observes current-account convertibility and IMF Article VIII obligations; current payments for trade, services, dividends and interest are unrestricted. Capital-account operations are liberalized but resident entities may need RBV approval or reporting under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 65]; a VFSC-registered International Company (IC) is non-resident and statutorily exempt from exchange-control approval, removing remittance, dividend and capital-repatriation hurdles provided AML/KYC conditions are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must offer all foreign currency export proceeds to an RBV-authorized dealer within 3 months of the entitlement date and convert 100% unless an RBV exemption is granted. The Exchange Control Act excludes International Companies from the resident definition, so the VU IC may retain export, card-interchange and programme-fee proceeds in VUV or any permitted foreign currency account without surrender or mandatory conversion.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) - central bank and exchange-control authority; Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) - delegated regulator for Financial Dealers Licensing Act [Cap 228] and non-bank financial supervision; National Payment System Act oversight with RBV.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW-MEDIUM. Vanuatu has no electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing regime and no passporting for foreign EMIs; taking VUV deposits would require a banking licence under the Banking Act [Cap 112]. Offshore EMIs can act only as non-deposit-taking card programme managers/processors under a VFSC-reviewed servicer due-diligence file and a written programme-management agreement, with settlement accounts held at, or sub-accounted through, an RBV-authorized domestic bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing legislation, no arm's-length documentation thresholds, and no local-file/master-file rules for International Companies.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu International Companies are subject to economic substance expectations under the VFSC licensing framework and related Company/ESR rules. A B2B virtual card issuer must maintain real economic presence in Vanuatu - including local physical office, eligible decision-makers, board minutes, employment substance, and activity consistent with the VFSC financial services licence. Passive letterbox arrangements are not tolerated in licensed financial services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misuse of Tax-Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Vanuatu law and the International Companies Act Cap. 222, with statutory fines up to VT 10,000,000, deregistration of the IC, confiscation order, and permanent prohibition from holding a VFSC licence.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the IC is a mere instrument of fraud. Directors are exposed to civil restitution to affected stakeholders, travel bans, disqualification from management, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Unlicensed Virtual Card Issuance / Banking Activity

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed conduct triggers VFSC enforcement action, including immediate licence suspension, civil penalties, freezing orders, forced remediation, and referral to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit for money-laundering and terrorist-financing investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers face personal criminal liability, director-ban orders, and travel bans. Personal restitution liability attaches where funds of cardholders or merchants were mishandled.
AML / KYC Programme Failure

Statutory sanction: Under anti-money-laundering legislation and VFSC rules, failure to maintain required AML controls attracts monetary penalties, revocation of the VFSC licence, and designation as a prohibited person for financial services. Regulators may also impose enhanced supervision costs on the institution.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit AML failures are individually liable for civil penalties and criminal prosecution; imprisonment is available for aggravated non-compliance. Personal assets may be subject to civil forfeiture in successful enforcement proceedings.
Economic Substance / Shell Company Arrangement

Statutory sanction: VFSC may impose economic-substance penalties, strike the IC off the Register, and transmit adverse notices to foreign tax authorities and international regulators. The company can be forced into compulsory winding-up.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute sham substance arrangements face personal fines, disqualification orders, travel bans, and civil liability for regulatory costs and client losses. The corporate veil will be disregarded where substance was artificially manufactured to shield another jurisdiction's taxable business.
