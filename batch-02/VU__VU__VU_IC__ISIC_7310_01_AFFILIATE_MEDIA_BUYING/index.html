Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs & Inland Revenue Service (VCIRS), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila; the statutory authority for value added tax, customs, and excise. Customs export declarations are lodged through the ASYCUDA electronic clearance gateway; company registration and annual licence fees are administered separately by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), www.vfsc.vu. There is no national online VAT e-filing portal; VAT registration and returns are processed on a paper or direct-submission basis at the VCIRS VAT Office.

E-government filing portal

Since Vanuatu imposes no income, payroll, capital gains, or withholding tax, the only national statutory tax identifier available to a foreign-owned entity is the VAT Registration Number (VATRN) issued under the Value Added Tax Act No. 14 of 2011 (as amended). Procedure: (1) Incorporate the VU International Company under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] through a licensed VFSC registered agent and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, registered agent, and registered office details; (2) determine whether the IC carries on a 'taxable activity' in Vanuatu — outbound affiliate media buying for non-resident advertisers is a zero-rated export supply, so compulsory registration is triggered only when taxable supplies made in Vanuatu exceed VUV 5,000,000 in any 12-month period; (3) if registration is required or desired (for input VAT recovery), file the VCIRS VAT registration application (Form VAT-01) with a certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation, registered agent address, principal place of business, description of taxable supplies, and a 12-month turnover estimate; (4) on approval, the Commissioner issues a VAT Registration Certificate containing the unique VU VATRN, to be quoted on all tax invoices and customs export entries; (5) obtain an exporter code from Vanuatu Customs for ASYCUDA filings; and (6) obtain the applicable Provincial/Municipal Business Licence (e.g., Shefa Province / Port Vila Municipality) as a separate local-permit requirement. A non-registered IC has no VATRN and cannot issue tax invoices or reclaim input VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — A VU International Company is incorporated as a distinct legal person under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] and operates in a zero-income-tax jurisdiction: no corporate income tax, personal income tax, capital gains tax, branch tax, or withholding tax is imposed, and the IC Act grants exemption from stamp duty and other levies on international instruments. The entity's only national tax exposures are value added tax at 12.5% on taxable supplies made in or imported into Vanuatu, customs/fiscal import duties on physical imports, the fixed VFSC annual licence fee, and provincial business licence fees. Because no income tax exists, there is no domestic reclassification of the IC as a pass-through entity, no CFC or territorial-source regime, and no restriction on foreign media-buying income.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, CFC, or hybrid-mismatch election exists because Vanuatu has no income tax law and no comprehensive double-tax-treaty network. Elective mechanisms available under Vanuatu law: (a) voluntary VAT registration under the Value Added Tax Act No. 14 of 2011 — an IC making zero-rated export supplies may register to obtain a VATRN and recover input VAT on local operating costs (registered-agent fees, bank charges, hosting, professional services); (b) an election of VAT filing frequency (monthly or quarterly) based on the registered supplier's turnover bracket; (c) under the IC Act, an International Company may apply to VFSC to maintain its tax-exempt and stamp-duty-exempt status and, conversely, may elect to convert or re-domicile into a domestic company, which is generally not advisable for tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 (approximately USD 42,000) of aggregate taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period for a domestic supplier carrying on a taxable activity in Vanuatu; compulsory registration applies once the threshold is met, and voluntary registration is available for zero-rated exporters. Non-resident digital-service suppliers are subject to a parallel simplified registration obligation under recent amendments when supplies to Vanuatu consumers exceed the same threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value Added Tax Act No. 14 of 2011, a VU IC may claim the 0% export rate where: (a) supplied goods are physically removed from Vanuatu by the supplier, evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration; or (b) supplied services are rendered to a recipient who is not resident in Vanuatu and the services are not performed in connection with land situated in Vanuatu or goods situated in Vanuatu at the time of supply (including international transport and services directly facilitating exported goods). The affiliate media-buying model (digital ad arbitrage, affiliate lead generation, media placement procurement) qualifies as an export of services when the contractual counterparty is a foreign advertiser or affiliate network, the platforms and delivery channels are operated outside Vanuatu, and the commercial benefit inures outside Vanuatu. The supply must be reported on the VAT return as zero-rated rather than exempt, and input VAT on local overheads remains recoverable in the same return.

Registered suppliers must issue a 'tax invoice' containing the prescribed particulars (supplier name and VATRN, recipient name and address, date of supply, description, VAT-exclusive value, rate, and VAT amount or zero-rating statement); invoices may be issued on paper or as electronic documents with sequential numbering. VAT returns are filed in paper or Excel form at the VCIRS VAT Office. Physical exports are declared through the ASYCUDA customs clearance system, which is the sole electronic filing gateway relevant to export zero-rating.

VCIRS retains full audit and self-assessment powers over zero-rated claims and may retrospectively raise default assessments whenever the export documentation bundle is incomplete, the non-resident recipient cannot be verified, or payment has not originated from abroad. Consequences: the supply is re-characterised as standard-rated and assessed at 12.5% with penalty tax; the VAT Act prescribes graduated penalties for incorrect returns and late payment, rising to penalty tax up to 200% of the VAT shortfall for deliberate evasion, plus criminal fines (statutorily up to VUV 3,000,000) and imprisonment (up to three years) for directors or representatives who sign false zero-rating returns. Fraudulent claims also trigger clawback of input VAT credits, cancellation or suspension of the IC's VATRN, revocation of the VFSC international-company licence for compliance failures, and potential joint liability of the registered agent. Vanuatu has no exchange-control legislation and therefore no statutory repatriation deadline; nonetheless, the 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is applied by local practitioners and auditors as an export substantiation proxy, and failure to repatriate within that window may impair documentary proof of a genuine transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains no exchange-control legislation. The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is freely convertible on both current and capital account transactions, and there is no requirement for central bank approval for external payment transfers or profit remittance. International Companies may remit dividends and service proceeds without administrative restriction, subject only to the standard anti-money-laundering transaction monitoring of the authorized dealer bank. The VUV exchange rate operates under a managed arrangement against a basket of major trading-partner currencies, and all spot conversions must be executed through a licensed commercial bank or authorized foreign-exchange dealer under the Banking Act (Cap 62).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Vanuatu imposes no statutory timeline for export-receipt repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio into VUV. Export and service proceeds, including affiliate media-buying revenue, may be retained in USD or AUD in foreign-currency accounts and are freely remittable. The only compliance requirement is that the SWIFT payment narrative accurately reflects the underlying commercial transaction and that the beneficiary maintains supporting invoices and media-delivery records for bank transaction-monitoring purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium practical viability for a Vanuatu International Company. Vanuatu has no statutory Electronic Money Institution (EMI) category; retail payment services and deposit-taking are licensable activities under the Banking Act (Cap 62) and the Financial Dealers Licensing Act, requiring an RBV license or authorized dealer status. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and specialized multi-currency platforms generally refuse Vanuatu International Companies unless the entity maintains a local agent relationship, provides a physical business substance affirmation, and satisfies the EMI's correspondent-bank restrictions. A USD account through a licensed offshore EMI can be used only as a pass-through funding channel to the VAN-based operational bank, never as the primary treasury settlement account for regulated trade disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound funding is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the corporate treasury account (e.g., Mercury, Wise Business, or a licensed US/EU bank) to the VU commercial bank's correspondent account, using the beneficiary International Company's name and BIC. The payment narrative must state 'capital contribution / subscription of shares' with reference to the board resolution, and the beneficiary bank requires pre-clearance of the UBO certificate and source-of-funds memorandum prior to credit. For USD inbound, the wire is cleared over the New York correspondent rail and then credited either in USD or converted to VUV at the authorized dealing rate; any conversion must be captured in a treasury audit log.Outbound disbursements for media inventory and affiliate payouts are cleared primarily by SWIFT MT103 direct beneficiary transfer in USD or AUD, with same-day or one-day settlement to the BIC of Meta, Google, TikTok, or major affiliate networks. Commercial letters of credit are not appropriate for digital media inventory; they may be used only for any goods-based affiliate or dropshipping flows, with VU commercial banks acting as confirming institution subject to trade documentation (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading). All SWIFT payments must carry a precise purpose-of-payment code and be supported by stored invoices and delivery evidence to avoid correspondent freeze risk.Deliverable USD/VUV outright forward contracts (plain vanilla, 1-12 month tenors) permitted through authorized dealer banks for genuine economic hedging of receivables and payablesDeliverable USD/AUD forward contracts for cross-currency treasury management and interchange-rate protection between the VU operational account and USD-denominated media disbursementsCross-currency swaps (USD/AUD) executed through licensed commercial banks under confirmed underlying exposure; VUV non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible nor available in this jurisdiction given the full convertibility of the VUVForeign-exchange swap contracts between USD and VUV for liquidity smoothing purposes, subject to margin and collateral terms agreed at the commercial bank's dealing room

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium practical viability for a Vanuatu International Company. Vanuatu has no statutory Electronic Money Institution (EMI) category; retail payment services and deposit-taking are licensable activities under the Banking Act (Cap 62) and the Financial Dealers Licensing Act, requiring an RBV license or authorized dealer status. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and specialized multi-currency platforms generally refuse Vanuatu International Companies unless the entity maintains a local agent relationship, provides a physical business substance affirmation, and satisfies the EMI's correspondent-bank restrictions. A USD account through a licensed offshore EMI can be used only as a pass-through funding channel to the VAN-based operational bank, never as the primary treasury settlement account for regulated trade disbursements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% - Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax at any level; an International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, exchange control and all similar state impositions under the International Companies Act [Cap.222].

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Vanuatu imposes no dividend withholding tax on any distribution by an International Company to any shareholder, resident or non-resident.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None - Vanuatu has not enacted transfer-pricing legislation; no Local File, Master File or Country-by-Country threshold in VUV exists, and the VFSC imposes no TP documentation requirement. Practical documentation obligations flow from corporate records and AML/BO retention rules only.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations. De facto substance requirements are (i) the IC Act prohibition on domestic Vanuatu business and onholding Vanuatu land; (ii) VFSC AML/BO record substance - statutory registers, board minutes, written resolutions and source-of-funds records; and (iii) realistic organisational substance under foreign CFC, exit-tax and bank KYC rules. For an ISIC 7310 performance-media arbitrage vehicle, core commercial functions - ad inventory contracting, bid management, reconciliation and risk assumption - must be performed outside Vanuatu, with the VU IC functioning as a genuine offshore principal rather than a letterbox.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Exemption Abuse and Domestic Trading

Statutory sanction: Loss of IC tax-exempt status; exposure to any applicable Vanuatu taxes, VAT at 12.5% on in-scope supplies, VFSC deregistration and strike-off; fines and restoration penalties under the International Companies Act and VFSC Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and UBOs can be personally assessed for unpaid taxes and penalties if the exemption is pierced; false statutory declarations attract criminal fines and imprisonment, while cross-border investigators may impose travel bans and asset freezes during recovery and extradition proceedings.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Arbitrage

Statutory sanction: VFSC AML/BO sanctions, agent license suspension, compulsory strike-off for non-renewal, and deregistration; foreign tax administrations may recharacterise the whole arrangement, impute the revenue to the principal, impose CFC inclusions, exit assessments, and 50-100% accuracy-related penalties on the affected group.

Director personal liability: Directors of the group and of the VU IC face civil restitution, personal deficiency assessments, director penalty notices, travel bans and disqualification; deliberate sham arrangements can lead to fraud prosecutions and imprisonment.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Offences under the Vanuatu Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing framework and the Money Laundering Act: substantial fines per breach, VFSC enforcement action including license revocation, freezing of bank accounts and confiscation of proceeds; foreign regulators may initiate mutual legal assistance and asset restraint.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and the UBO can be prosecuted personally for money-laundering facilitation; penalties include imprisonment, confiscation orders, civil restitution to defrauded advertisers, and travel bans pending extradition or criminal trial.
Affiliate Fraud and Cross-Border Tax Evasion Red Flags

Statutory sanction: No domestic Vanuatu TP penalty exists, but the principal jurisdictions (US, Australia, Singapore, UK, EU) may apply CFC, transfer-pricing and general anti-avoidance rules with penalties of up to 100% of the underpaid tax; civil fraud actions by advertisers; VFSC deregistration; payment-rail bans by banking partners.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal deficiency notices, fraudulent-trading liability, clawback of distributions, civil restitution, passport seizure and travel bans; willful evasion of foreign tax or mail/wire fraud in the affiliate scheme is indictable with long imprisonment terms and confiscation of personal assets.
