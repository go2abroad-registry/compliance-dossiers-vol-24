Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue Authority (VRA) — official tax and VAT administrator; portal: https://www.vra.gov.vu. Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) — company registry and international company licensing; portal: https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) must first be incorporated under the International Companies Act through VFSC. If the IC makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu, it must apply for VAT/TIN registration with the VRA by submitting the VAT registration application form, certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, beneficial ownership identification, and physical address confirmation. VRA issues a TIN/VAT ID upon approval. An IC that does not make local taxable supplies has no mandatory TIN requirement; a Tax Exemption Certificate may be obtained from VFSC for statutory exemption documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — International Company is exempt from Vanuatu corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty on offshore income; it is not subject to entity-level tax unless it makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu that trigger VAT obligations.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime exists under Vanuatu law. Statutory elections are limited to optional VAT registration for small businesses and the voluntary application for a VFSC Tax Exemption Certificate. No partnership or transparent-entity elections are provided.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 2,000,000 (VT 2,000,000) annual taxable turnover in Vanuatu triggers compulsory VAT registration; voluntary registration is permitted for entities making zero-rated export supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Vanuatu are zero-rated provided proof of export is obtained. Services, including public relations, media communication and advisory work performed for non-resident clients, are zero-rated where the recipient belongs outside Vanuatu, the services are supplied for use or benefit wholly outside Vanuatu, and there is no connection with taxable supplies in Vanuatu.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for physical goods; not required for pure services)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (where physical PR collateral, media kits, or printed matter is shipped)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a Vanuatu bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • VAT zero-rated export invoice with non-resident customer declaration and underlying service contract

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate exists in Vanuatu. VAT-registered businesses must issue sequential tax invoices with statutory particulars; invoices may be paper or electronic. VRA encourages electronic filing through its online portal but does not require government API integration or pre-clearance of invoices.

The VRA may conduct a retrospective audit of zero-rated export claims; if documentation is deficient or the export is determined to be a local supply, VAT at 12.5% is clawed back with statutory interest and penalties up to 200% of the unpaid tax. Fraudulent zero-rating may attract criminal fines of up to VUV 500,000, director disqualification, and imprisonment for up to 7 years; records must be retained for at least 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains a liberalised exchange control regime under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 112] administered by the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu, qualified by only formal notification and transparency obligations for cross-border transfers exceeding VUV 10 million. Current account transactions are fully convertible; capital account transactions, including direct investment, portfolio investment, and profit remittance, are freely permitted with no prior approval for ICs enjoying tax-exempt status. Profit remittance hurdles are practically non-existent for a licensed International Company; however, the receiving financial institution may request underlying contracts, invoices, and proof of source of funds to comply with VUV anti-money laundering and counter-financing of terrorism obligations. Statutory withholding tax does not apply to non-resident ICs, and dividends, royalties, and service fees can be repatriated in full without conversion restrictions. No surrender rules apply to export proceeds; the exporter may retain foreign currency in offshore or resident accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms that have no physical presence in Vanuatu are not locally licensed by the VFSC or the RBV. They can only be used for non-resident liquidity purposes if the entity maintains a parallel domestic correspondent clearing capability. The RBV does not recognise such EMIs as authorised dealers in VUV, so any VUV clearing must route through a resident commercial bank. For a USD/EUR/AUD-denominated international PR agency, established EMIs such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or MultiPass are legally acceptable for non-VUV treasury consolidation, provided they satisfy 3rd-party AML/KYC due diligence and the beneficial ownership documentation is aligned with VFSC records. Cross-border EMI accounts cannot be used as the sole bank account for statutory annual returns or for any transaction requiring VUV settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for initial share capital must be routed via SWIFT to an authorised dealer bank in Vanuatu (e.g., ANZ or BSP) using purpose code 'G301 - Direct Investment' (or its local equivalent) when exceeding VUV 10 million. The remitting bank must include full legal name of the IC, BIC of the receiving bank, and both the sending and receiving account numbers. Supporting documents, including the share subscription resolution, bank confirmation of share proceeds, and RBV electronic declaration, must be transmitted to the receiving bank on the same business day. There is no minimum capital requirement for an International Company; a prudent initial injection of USD 20,000 is recommended to satisfy bank residual risk thresholds. No exchange control prior approval is needed; post-transaction filing is handled by the bank.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and operating expenses are executed through standard SWIFT MT103 over the correspondent banking network. For commercial LC transactions (e.g., procurement of production assets), the WC/BPU006 documentary credit via MT700 series is available through tier-1 banks. The firm may also use USD-AUD and EUR-VUV clearing corridors. Payments to overseas consultants and subcontractors must be supported by an executed service agreement, an invoice referencing ISIC 7310, and a settlement advice. The RSB reporting threshold of VUV 10 million applies; above this threshold, banks will pre-validate the transaction and attach the e-declaration receipt. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% - Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax; no Income Tax Act or CIT assessment regime exists in Vanuatu law.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an International Company. Dividends may be declared in USD or any foreign currency and paid gros to non-resident shareholders without VRC involvement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The VFSC annual licence fee is payable on the anniversary of incorporation of the IC; the registered agent in Port Vila must lodge the annual fee and the prescribed annual return before the anniversary date to avoid late fees.

Deadline: · Frequency:

VAT returns are due on or before the 20th day of the month following the end of the monthly tax period; nil returns are compulsory for all registered persons.

VUV 5,000 plus VUV 1,000 per day); late payment: interest at 2% per month; VAT evasion: civil penalty up to 150% of the tax under the VAT Act [Cap. 248], with directors and officers exposed to prosecution and custodial sentencing.
Box 4 Net VAT payable or refund claimed. Any refund is subject to VRC review; the IC must maintain full local documentary trail and a Vanuatu bank account if a refund is claimed. Late filing: fixed penalty plus daily surcharge (approx. VUV 5,000 plus VUV 1,000 per day); late payment: interest at 2% per month; VAT evasion: civil penalty up to 150% of the tax under the VAT Act [Cap. 248], with directors and officers exposed to prosecution and custodial sentencing.
Box 5 Declaration and signature. Must be signed electronically by an authorised local representative with a valid VRC e-services credential. Late filing: fixed penalty plus daily surcharge (approx. VUV 5,000 plus VUV 1,000 per day); late payment: interest at 2% per month; VAT evasion: civil penalty up to 150% of the tax under the VAT Act [Cap. 248], with directors and officers exposed to prosecution and custodial sentencing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Local File or Master File regime is legislated in Vanuatu, and VFSC does not require a transfer pricing study for the annual licence. However, where the IC books intercompany PR retainer, media placement, or crisis communication revenue exceeding VUV 10,000,000 (approx. USD 85,000) per annum, the foreign parent's CFC/GILTI tax authority will apply its own transfer pricing and economic substance tests; a contemporaneous OECD-based TP documentation set should therefore be prepared and updated annually.

Economic substance scope: Following the November 2019 OECD FHTP review, Vanuatu applies economic substance expectations to International Companies generating geographically mobile income, including marketing, public relations, consultancy, and communication strategy services. For the IC to defend its 0% tax position, the core functions - strategic direction, client relationship management, media content approval, and board oversight - must be genuinely performed in Vanuatu with local employees on payroll, a physical office in Port Vila, regular board meetings at the registered office, and records of operational control. VFSC may request an annual substance declaration and financial statements demonstrating that the IC is not a bare nominee shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: VFSC strike-off and dissolution under s.121 IC Act [Cap. 222]; adverse FHTP/EU listing status. Foreign: Subpart F/GILTI/CFC inclusion of the full retained profit, IRC s.482 reallocation with penalties up to 40% of the understated foreign tax, denial of treaty benefits, and potential criminal tax evasion referral.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders are jointly and severally liable for recharacterised foreign taxes and penalties; courts may issue worldwide asset freezing orders, travel bans, and director disqualification orders; in egregious cases custodial sentences of up to 25 years apply under foreign money laundering statutes.
Money laundering / AML failure

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: corporate fines up to VUV 100,000,000 (approx. USD 850,000), confiscation of assets, loss of banking correspondence relationships, and VFSC revocation of the IC licence; foreign banks may terminate correspondent arrangements and report the IC to FATF-aligned FIUs.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face individual fines up to VUV 100,000,000 and/or imprisonment for money laundering offenses (up to 25 years); travel bans and passport surrenders are routinely imposed during pending FIU investigations.
Tax evasion via artificial profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: prosecution for fraudulent misrepresentation to VFSC, fine, and deregistration. Foreign: 100% CFC/GILTI inclusion, transfer pricing adjustment, penalty equal to 100% of the tax on the shifted profit, and foreign criminal tax evasion charges where the invoices are found to be sham documents.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the sham retainer agreements are personally liable for civil restitution of shifted taxes, subject to travel bans, extradition requests, and imprisonment in the operating country; Vanuatu courts may also enforce foreign revenue judgment debts in certain mutual assistance channels.
Unlicensed local business activity / VAT evasion

Statutory sanction: VRC: retroactive VAT registration, penalty of 100% of the evaded VAT, interest at 2% per month, and prosecution under the VAT Act [Cap. 248]; Ministry of Finance business licence fines; the IC loses its offshore exempt character.

Director personal liability: The resident director responsible for the company's administration is personally assessed for the unpaid VAT and may be imprisoned for up to 2 years; the IC's offshore tax exemption is revoked retroactively.
False declaration to VFSC / non-payment of annual fees

Statutory sanction: VFSC deregistration and dissolution; fines up to VUV 50,000,000 (approx. USD 425,000) for false declarations; all contracts issued in the IC's name after strike-off are void, causing unsecured creditor claims against the foreign group.

Director personal liability: Directors continuing to trade after strike-off are personally liable for the company's post-dissolution debts, may be disqualified from acting as directors in Vanuatu, and face criminal fines and imprisonment for fraudulent trading; travel bans are available to facilitate enforcement.
