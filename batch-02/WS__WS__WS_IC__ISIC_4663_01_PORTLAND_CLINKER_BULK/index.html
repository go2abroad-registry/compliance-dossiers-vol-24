Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is mandatory; shares may be issued with no par value and in any currency. Capital duty is exempted. Escrow deposit rules apply only where a financier requires a bankable asset guarantee; such escrow is held with a licensed commercial bank in Samoa and is not a statutory incorporation condition.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Samoa Fire and Emergency Services Authority (FESA) for any transit warehouse; structural load inspection by the Ministry of Works, Transport and Infrastructure (MWTI) for racking or storage platforms; occupational health and safety clearance under the Labour and Employment Relations Act 2013 covering dust exposure monitoring, personal protective equipment, and emergency response planning.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is mandatory; shares may be issued with no par value and in any currency. Capital duty is exempted. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa; online portal: MfR Tax Administration System (https://www.revenue.gov.ws). Offshore company registrar: Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA).

E-government filing portal

A Samoa International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from national income tax, VAGST, and stamp duty on offshore operations under the International Companies Act 1987 (s.203), and therefore does not obtain an income tax TIN. Formation is completed with SIFA, which issues a Certificate of Incorporation and IC registration number. If the IC carries on a taxable activity within Samoa with turnover exceeding SAT 80,000 per 12 months, it must register for VAGST with the MfR on the prescribed form (supported by the SIFA certificate, memorandum and articles, and registered agent confirmation) and receive a TIN. A purely offshore IC trading cement clinker by ocean vessel is outside the VAGST registration net.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box or hybrid branch election. An IC may voluntarily convert to an Ordinary Company under the Companies Act 2001 (with SIFA and MfR notifications), thereby electing into Samoa corporate income tax (27%) and VAGST (15%). The Tax Administration Act 2012 and Samoa's OECD BEPS-aligned provisions, including its MLI position, govern hybrid mismatch outcomes; no elective multi-jurisdictional classification regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 80,000 per rolling 12-month period on taxable supplies made in Samoa (compulsory registration); voluntary registration available below the threshold; an IC with exclusively offshore exports of clinker has no domestic nexus and is not registrable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAGST Act 1996, a registered person may apply the 0% rate to goods exported from Samoa where the goods are exported within the statutory period (generally 30 days from supply, or such time as the Commissioner allows) and documentary proof of export is retained. Bulk Portland cement clinker exported by ocean vessel qualifies as exported goods. Services rendered to non-residents for the offshore storage, handling, and freight of clinker are zero-rated where the services are supplied and performed outside Samoa. An exempt IC transacting offshore is outside VAGST entirely and is not required to file export refund claims.

  • Samoa Customs Service Export Declaration (SGD)
  • Negotiable Ocean Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential claims are made)
  • Loading port weight certificate and vessel manifest extract

No real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping mandate exists in Samoa. VAGST returns and payments are filed electronically through the MfR Tax Administration System. Commercial invoices, packing lists, and negotiable ocean bills of lading constitute the operative documentation; no government-approved e-invoicing platform is required for bulk clinker export sales.

Under the Tax Administration Act 2012, shortfall penalties reach 100% of the tax evaded for deliberate conduct and 150% for deliberate and concealed conduct, plus a late payment penalty of 2% per month and statutory interest; criminal prosecution may lead to fines and imprisonment. VAGST refunds attributable to zero-rated exports are subject to pre-payment and post-payment audit; any amount wrongfully refunded is clawed back with interest and penalties. SIFA may strike an IC off the register for breach of the International Companies Act 1987, and the IC exemption is forfeited where the entity engages in non-licensed domestic activity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for resident companies (Samoa Income Tax Act 2015); 0% for International Companies (ICs) under the International Companies Act 2008

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid by an International Company; dividends paid to non-residents by a resident company are not subject to Samoan withholding tax when not attributable to a permanent establishment in Samoa

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 5,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions (goods, services, financing or IP) triggers mandatory contemporaneous TP documentation (Local File) for domestic CIT filers; no TP documentation threshold applies to exempt ICs because they are outside the CIT net, but the IC must be able to demonstrate arm's length conduct if it transacts with related parties that claim deductions abroad

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2020 apply to International Companies engaged in relevant activities, including 'distribution and service centres'; the IC must maintain real economic presence in Samoa: local management and board meetings, adequate physical premises, full-time employees proportionate to the distribution activity, and core income-generating activities conducted in Samoa; an annual economic substance declaration must be filed with the Registrar through the registered agent within 12 months of the financial year end; pure holding or low-risk procurement structures without local staff and premises will fail the substance test and face sanctions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Upon conviction: fine up to WST 500,000 and imprisonment up to seven years; the court may also order recovery of any tax that would have been payable plus interest and a penalty of 150% of the evaded tax

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the tax, penalties and costs if they signed false declarations; a director guilty of tax evasion may be disqualified from acting as a director in Samoa for up to five years, subjected to a travel ban, and imprisoned for up to seven years
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to WST 100,000 for the company; repeated non-compliance results in automatic strike-off, compulsory winding up, and forfeiture of the exemption certificate; the registered agent must file a compliance report and may be fined WST 10,000 per non-compliant client

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow a substance failure are jointly and severally liable for the penalty and the costs of winding up; they may be prohibited from incorporating or managing any Samoa entity for up to five years and may be required to post security before leaving Samoa
Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's AML/CFT Act 2018: on conviction, imprisonment up to fourteen years and a fine up to WST 1,000,000; the company's bank accounts are subject to freezing and forfeiture of the proceeds; the IC's registration may be cancelled if its agent fails to conduct customer due diligence

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face extradition-compliant criminal liability, personal asset forfeiture, travel bans pending trial, and civil restitution orders; directors who fail to maintain adequate beneficiary records are personally fined up to WST 50,000 and disqualified from holding any financial services licence
Unlicensed Domestic Trading / Breach of IC Act 2008

Statutory sanction: The offending contract is void and unenforceable in Samoa; the IC is liable to a fine up to WST 25,000 plus forfeiture of any profit derived from the prohibited transaction; the Registrar may strike off the IC and impose a lifetime ban on re-registration

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for all debts arising from the void contract and for restitution to the Samoan counterparty; knowing breach is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment up to two years and a personal fine up to WST 25,000; the director may be subject to a garnishee order on overseas assets through reciprocal enforcement
