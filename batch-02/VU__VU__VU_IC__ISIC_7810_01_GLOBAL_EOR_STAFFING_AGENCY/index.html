Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Government of Vanuatu; official portal: Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue e-services (https://www.customsinlandrevenue.gov.vu as provided by the Government of Vanuatu).

E-government filing portal

For an International Company (IC) formed under the Vanuatu International Companies Act, the IC must be registered with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) through a licensed registered agent. The VFSC-issued Certificate of Incorporation is the foundational corporate/tax identifier. If the IC carries on any Vanuatu domestic taxable activity or exceeds the VAT registration threshold, it must apply to the Commissioner of Customs and Inland Revenue for a VAT Registration Number / Tax Identification Number, submitting the VFSC certificate, registered agent confirmation, beneficial ownership declaration, local contact details, and evidence of taxable activity. For wholly offshore operations, the IC relies on its VFSC tax-exempt status and annual renewal; no separate CIT or income tax registration is required because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The Vanuatu International Company is a non-resident, tax-exempt legal form. It is exempt from Vanuatu corporate income tax, stamp duty, VAT on offshore transactions, exchange control, and capital-based levies. Tax liability exposure is limited to any accidental domestic taxable activity, customs/VAT on physical imports into Vanuatu for domestic use, and annual VFSC fees.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no US-style check-the-box election. An International Company may elect to continue/convert into a domestic company under the Vanuatu Companies Act, thereby losing IC tax-exempt status. An IC may voluntarily register for VAT even below the statutory threshold. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable because Vanuatu does not impose corporate income tax or a general deduction regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 3,000,000 of taxable turnover in a consecutive 12-month period; International Companies undertaking wholly offshore business are outside the scope of Vanuatu VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Vanuatu qualify for 0% VAT upon physical export, evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration and removal from Vanuatu. Offshore services supplied by the IC to non-resident EOR clients, such as global staffing/contractor payroll services, are treated as outside the scope of Vanuatu VAT when the services are not performed or consumed in Vanuatu and the beneficiary is a non-resident. The IC must substantiate the non-resident status of the foreign client, the offshore service contract, and the absence of domestic use. Input VAT refunds on local costs are available only to the extent the IC is VAT-registered and uses those costs to make taxable or zero-rated supplies; wholly offshore ICs normally have no Vanuatu input VAT recovery because no corresponding domestic taxable activity exists.

  • Customs-approved Export Declaration / Export Entry with clearance status
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment out of Vanuatu
  • Commercial/Proforma Export Invoice with Incoterms
  • Packing List and proof of export/removal
  • Foreign client contract or service agreement for offshore EOR/contractor payroll services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing payment in foreign currency from the non-resident client

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, digital stamping, or continuous fiscal clearance regime is currently in force in Vanuatu. VAT-registered persons must issue prescribed VAT tax invoices for taxable supplies and keep source records, customs export declarations, transport documents, and payment evidence for statutory review. VAT returns are filed through the Department of Customs and Inland Revenue's approved e-filing/paper filing procedures, with export zero-rating substantiation retained for inspection.

The Commissioner of Customs and Inland Revenue may retrospectively assess VAT on any supply claimed as zero-rated or outside scope if documentation is incomplete or the destination/beneficiary is not substantiated. Reassessments can claw back previously refunded input VAT, with statutory penalties for underpayment, late payment interest, and additional assessments up to the tax evaded. Fraudulent zero-rating or false export declarations are criminal offenses and may result in fines, imprisonment, loss of the IC's VFSC license, and revocation of tax-exempt status.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is a floating convertible currency for current account purposes, including payroll remittances and operational trade payments. Capital account transactions, including inbound shareholder capital injections into an International Company, are freely permitted for non-residents, but large outbound capital transfers and cross-border loans may require prior RBV approval through an authorized dealer bank. Vanuatu International Companies are treated as non-resident for exchange control purposes and may hold foreign currency accounts, enabling unrestricted dividend and profit remittances provided the company is not conducting prohibited domestic business with Vanuatu residents. There are no material hurdles to current account convertibility or outward profit remittance for a properly licensed Global EOR/Staffing entity transacting exclusively with overseas clients and contractors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. There is no export proceeds surrender regime under the Exchange Control Act or RBV directives. Exporters and International Companies may retain foreign currency receipts in designated foreign currency accounts with authorized dealers without any obligation to convert into VUV within a specified period. This facilitates flow-through payroll funding and offshore invoicing for an International Company engaged in global EOR services.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), also administering the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 61]

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not licensed or regulated by the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu and cannot independently provide clearing or settlement in VUV without a local authorized dealer. Wise and similar global EMIs are viable only through a commercial arrangement with a Vanuatu-regulated bank or as a supplementary disbursement channel for payroll to employees in low-risk jurisdictions. The core treasury architecture should maintain a primary VUV and USD account with a Tier 1 onshore bank, with offshore EMIs used exclusively for sub-15,000 USD contractor payments where expedited cross-border delivery is commercially justified. Legal constraint: an EMI that is not an authorized dealer cannot perform exchange control functions, issue documentary credits, or hold client money as a bank in Vanuatu.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be initiated from the corporate shareholder bank account using SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code corresponding to direct investment capital contribution under IMF BPM6 (Category 521 for equity capital). The payment must be routed to the International Company's VUV corporate bank account or dedicated foreign currency account with an authorized dealer in Vanuatu. Supporting documents required for RBV auditing and bank compliance: board resolution approving the capital injection, constitutional documents, shareholder register, and the bank's capital escrow account opening checklist. The bank will verify that the source bank is in good standing, the remitter is the sole or listed shareholder of the VU IC, and that no funds are being routed through prohibited channels. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (nil) — Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax on International Companies, whether locally or offshore sourced.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions by an International Company to resident or non-resident shareholders.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — Vanuatu's zero-CIT regime prescribes no Local File or Master File thresholds and has no transfer-pricing legislation. For ESR purposes, any relevant activity conducted in Vanuatu must be supported by records evidencing adequate premises, employees, and VUV-denominated expenditure proportionate to the activity.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) (Amendment) Act 2019 applies to Vanuatu International Companies carrying on relevant activities: banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, pure holding, intellectual property holding, and distribution or service centres. A third-party EOR agency providing global staffing and contractor payroll to unrelated clients is not a listed relevant activity; however, payroll/EOR services rendered to connected group undertakings fall within the service-centre category and require physical presence, qualified employees, premises, and expenditure in Vanuatu commensurate with the activity. Pure holding companies enjoy reduced substance requirements. Non-compliance attracts administrative penalties up to VT 1,000,000, escalation fines, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion — VAT and municipal levy fraud

Statutory sanction: Value Added Tax Act (No. 4 of 2009): penalty of 100% of the tax evaded plus default interest; criminal fines up to VT 10,000,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; municipal business licence forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax loss; conviction triggers disqualification from VFSC-regulated functions, travel bans may be imposed during investigation, and restitution orders attach to personal assets.
Shell company and false economic substance declaration

Statutory sanction: International Companies (Economic Substance) (Amendment) Act 2019: administrative penalty up to VT 1,000,000; repeated default draws fines up to VT 10,000,000 and administrative strike-off; a false statement under the IC Act is a criminal offence.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if the default results from their direction; courts may order disgorgement of fiscal benefits as civil restitution; offending directors are placed on the VFSC disqualified list and barred from acting in any VFSC-regulated entity.
AML/CFT failure in licensed activity

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act (No. 7 of 2017): regulatory fines up to VT 5,000,000 per breach against the IC and beneficial owners; freezing orders; suspension or revocation of VFSC registration and licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal liability for knowingly permitting money laundering — imprisonment up to 5 years, fines up to VT 5,000,000, travel bans, and international mutual-assistance proceedings.
Corporate veil-piercing and fraudulent preference

Statutory sanction: International Companies Act (Cap. 222) and applicable insolvency principles: restitutionary awards, statutory fines up to VT 500,000, director disqualification orders, and disclosure of VFSC records to foreign tax authorities under information-exchange mechanisms.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil, rendering directors personally liable to restore withheld contractor salaries; personal insolvency with asset freezes, and criminal proceedings for fraudulent trading.
Information exchange and international cooperation non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Exchange of Information (International Assistance) Act (Cap. 284): monetary penalties for non-compliance, injunctive enforcement, and strike-off upon VFSC order; adverse findings may be shared with treaty partners.

Director personal liability: Officers obstructing an information request face fines up to VT 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 12 months; directors' foreign assets become traceable under international confiscation proceedings.
