Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election is available under Samoan law. The exemption under the International Companies Act 1987, s.173 (complete relief from income tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, and withholding taxes, together with exemption from filing any income returns) is automatic, irrevocable, and not open to an entity-classification election. The only available election is voluntary registration for VAGST under the VAGST Act 2004, which may be elected for refund recovery on local input costs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of annual taxable supplies in Samoa; voluntary registration permitted below threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Goods: physical export from Samoa within 30 days of supply with title passing to a non-resident located outside Samoa, supported by customs and transport evidence; (2) Offshore POD retailing services: e-commerce order-taking, customer acquisition, and fulfilment agency services supplied to a non-resident with the place of supply outside Samoa and benefit wholly consumed offshore (covering scenarios where the proximate print/fulfilment operation occurs abroad) qualify as zero-rated offshore services. A VAGST-registered IC may claim a refund of input VAGST on local costs attributable to such zero-rated export or offshore service supplies.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration (Form EX-001) — required where goods are physically exported from Samoa
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill dated within 30 days of the tax invoice, evidencing the Samoa export movement for goods zero-rating
  • Commercial tax invoice marked 'Zero Rated — Export / Offshore Services', showing supplier TIN and the non-resident customer's foreign address
  • Inward remittance certificate or foreign currency bank credit advice (PayPal/Stripe settlement reports accepted only when corroborated by bank statements) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Print-on-demand production and freight invoices substantiating input VAGST claimed in the refund application

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate exists in Samoa. A valid tax invoice must show the supplier TIN and recipient TIN, issue date, unique sequential number, description, quantity, unit price, and VAGST at 15%; electronic invoices are accepted and all records must be retained for 5 years. VAGST returns are filed online through the MfR e-services portal on a monthly or quarterly basis, but the MfR applies retrospective audit clearance rather than per-invoice government authorization.

The MfR audits zero-rated claims by tracing goods to customs export records; failure to substantiate the zero rate recharacterizes the supply as taxable at 15% with output VAGST assessed. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2012 range from 25% of the deficiency for failure to take reasonable care up to 75% for intentional disregard or concealment, plus monthly late-payment interest; refunds may be retrospectively clawed back by assessment for up to 5 years, without limitation in fraud cases. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating carries fines of up to double the tax evaded and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years, and unrepatriated proceeds beyond the 180-day window separately constitute an Exchange Control Act 1976 offence with Central Bank of Samoa penalties and increased customs audit exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a partially convertible managed-float currency. Current-account transactions, including trade settlements and marketplace payouts, are statutorily delegated to licensed authorized dealers and are routinely approved. Capital-account transactions, such as equity injections and shareholder loan repayments, require CBS approval on a case-by-case basis; approval is routinely granted for bona fide International Company treasury flows. An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is deemed non-resident for exchange-control purposes, may contract and hold balances in any currency, and operates USD-denominated clearing accounts outside the WST conversion net. Profit remittance: dividends paid by an IC to non-resident shareholders are exempt from all Samoan taxes and transferable without a CBS capital-account permit when funded from foreign-currency balances; conversion of WST dividends requires only a declaration to an authorized dealer, not a CBS permit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender ratio applies to an IC's export or trade proceeds under the Exchange Control Act 1976; an IC is expressly exempt from the resident exporter's duty to sell foreign currency to an authorized dealer. The statutory export-proceeds timeline requiring repatriation within 6 months of shipment applies only to resident Samoan exporters; a POD IC selling via Stripe and PayPal to end-customers is treated as having no deemed export from Samoa. Any incidental WST-denominated sales proceeds must be credited to an authorized-dealer WST sub-account with a declaration of trade origin; 100% of foreign-currency proceeds may be retained in the IC's USD clearing accounts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), exercising delegated authority from the Minister of Finance under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and prudential supervision over licensed authorized dealers under the Financial Institutions Act 1996.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Newly incorporated IC with no trading history and no local staff; beneficial ownership obscured by a Hong Kong corporate trustee as sole shareholder.

Root cause: Newly incorporated IC with no trading history and no local staff; beneficial ownership obscured by a Hong Kong corporate trustee as sole shareholder.

Defense evidence: (i) certificate of incorporation and certificate of good standing issued by the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA); (ii) register of directors, share register and registered-office consent, each certified by the licensed Samoan registered agent; (iii) formal introduction letter from the registered agent confirming the IC's administration, statutory filings and non-resident status; (iv) UBO declaration with passport, residential address proof and professional background; (v) written source-of-wealth statement covering the founder's capital injection path; (vi) board resolution naming account signatories.
Payments arriving exclusively through Stripe and PayPal settlement rails in aggregated, platform-origin batches, creating third-party-transfer misclassification risk.

Root cause: Payments arriving exclusively through Stripe and PayPal settlement rails in aggregated, platform-origin batches, creating third-party-transfer misclassification risk.

Defense evidence: (i) executed merchant service agreements with Stripe and PayPal naming the IC or its merchant subsidiary as registered payee; (ii) 3-6 months of settlement statements from both PSPs cross-referenced to the USD account credits; (iii) order-level data export showing SKU, custom design file, print-file record and fulfillment tracking per payout; (iv) operational narrative describing the POD cycle - order received, design uploaded, print facility dispatched, no physical inventory in Samoa; (v) evidence that sales terms, invoices and storefront do not represent Samoa as place of supply.

Beneficial owner or signatory matching OFAC, EU or UN sanctions screening due to common-name hits in mainland China or adjacent jurisdictions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use an equity injection rather than shareholder debt to avoid any CBS capital-account permit and to strengthen the IC's capital base for merchant underwriting. The shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 from the originating bank in USD to the IC's USD account at the Samoan tier-1 bank or the approved offshore EMI, with remittance field 70-79 stating 'Capital Injection / IC Act 1987 / No WST Conversion'. The IC's director issues a certified board resolution authorizing the account opening and recording the share issuance; the bank files CBS Form ECB/1 as a notification under the exchange-control exemption rather than as an approval application. The injecting shareholder attaches the source-of-wealth package; funds remain unallocated until the SIFA share register is updated and a certificate of incumbency is produced. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Companies; 27% for resident ordinary companies

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by an International Company to non-residents (exempt under the International Companies Act); ordinary Samoa companies pay 15% non-resident dividend withholding tax unless reduced by treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 5,000,000 in annual controlled transactions triggering mandatory Local File and Master File; also requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where a Samoa IC enters into related-party cross-border transactions above WST 1,000,000, in accordance with Income Tax Act 2012 Part IX and OECD TP Principles.

Economic substance scope: Samoa enacts Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019, applicable to Samoa International Companies carrying on relevant activities such as distribution and service businesses, including print-on-demand retail. The IC must demonstrate: (a) management meetings in Samoa where strategic decisions are made, (b) an adequate physical presence and qualified employees in Samoa, (c) core income-generating activities performed in Samoa, and (d) adequate operating expenditure in Samoa proportional to the level of business. Election of tax exemption does not remove ESR obligations and fails to maintain substance results in penalties and forced redomiciliation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and deliberate misreporting of income

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of tax understated, late payment interest of 10% per annum, a monetary penalty of up to WST 50,000, and criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 2012; on conviction, fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax under the statutory restoration and liability rules. They may face criminal fines, travel bans through the Ministry of Customs and Revenue, asset freezing orders, and disqualification of directors for up to 10 years.
Artificial shell company with no economic substance

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with the Economic Substance Regulations triggers a penalty of up to WST 1,000,000; the IC may be struck off the Samoa International Companies Register, have its exemption certificate revoked, and be reported to foreign tax authorities through spontaneous exchange of information. The company may also face a WST 20,000 annual default penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be held personally liable for penalties if they caused the company's substance failures. The Registrar can issue a disqualification order, barring the individual from managing any Samoa company for 5 years, and may impose a personal fine of up to WST 50,000 for continuing non-compliance.
Money laundering and AML/CFT violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2018, breaches carry a monetary penalty of up to WST 10 million or 3 times the value of the transaction; plus confiscation orders. Criminal conviction can impose imprisonment from 10 to 25 years for serious money laundering.

Director personal liability: Company directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners of the Samoa IC are automatically liable for AML penalties involving their decisions. The High Court can issue a travel prohibition, freeze passports, and order repatriation of all funds; directors who knowingly process illicit proceeds are personally subject to extradition and imprisonment sentences.
Failure to file annual returns and misrepresentation to the Registrar

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties of WST 500 plus WST 50 per month of default; false statements to the Registrar attract a fine of WST 25,000 and, on conviction, imprisonment of up to 5 years. Persistent non-filing leads to automatic strike-off and the corporate veil removed upon restoration.

Director personal liability: Directors who continue to trade after striking-off are personally liable for all debts and taxation of the IC; the Registrar may obtain a court order for the director's disqualification and for civil recovery from personal assets. Failure to disclose beneficial ownership can result in a custody sentence of up to 3 years.
