Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Ministry for Revenue; e-services portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain a Samoan TIN by submitting a TIN registration application to the Samoa Revenue Authority, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered office particulars, corporate register extract, and identification of directors and officers. An International Company engaged solely in offshore business is not compulsorily registered for income tax or VAGST; registration is required only if it undertakes taxable local activities. If VAGST registration is required, the applicant files the standard VAGST registration form with the SRA, declares expected annual turnover, and receives the TIN and VAGST registration certificate upon approval, typically within 10-15 business days. A local agent may be appointed for service of notices and filing of returns.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election is available under Samoa domestic law. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 in taxable supplies in any 12-month period; compulsory VAGST registration once the threshold is exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical goods supplied in the course or furtherance of a taxable activity are zero-rated for VAGST when they are exported from Samoa and the exporter retains evidence of export within the prescribed time. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and performed entirely outside Samoa, or otherwise fall within the international services zero-rating category. For a subscription box service, direct despatch of the physical box from Samoa to a foreign subscriber supports the 0% rate; boxes supplied from a pre-positioned overseas warehouse are outside the scope of Samoa VAGST but may trigger foreign indirect taxes.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SRA Customs, Form CED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing Samoa port of export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice proving settlement in foreign currency within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for subscription box contents
  • Proof of foreign delivery (courier proof of delivery or destination customs clearance)

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists. Samoa does not currently impose mandatory e-invoicing, SRA API reporting, digital stamping, or pre-clearance of tax invoices. VAGST invoices may be issued on paper or electronically and must contain the prescribed particulars under VAGST regulations.

SRA may audit zero-rated export claims retrospectively for up to 5 years, and without limitation where fraud or deliberate evasion is suspected. Unsupported, falsified, or improperly substantiated zero-rating claims are fully clawed back with late-payment interest and applicable civil penalties. Civil shortfall penalties may be imposed at 50% of the tax avoided, and criminal prosecution may result in fines up to WST 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Directors and officers of the International Company may be jointly and severally assessed where fraudulent export documentation is knowingly used.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST, ISO 4217) is a partially convertible currency administered within an Article XIV exchange-restriction framework and managed against a trade-weighted currency basket of major partners (USD, AUD, NZD, EUR, JPY). Current-account payments for genuine goods and services are generally authorized by the authorized dealers under delegated CBS limits, subject to documentary verification (commercial invoices, transport and insurance documents). Capital-account transactions, including inward direct investment, repatriation of subscribed capital, shareholder loans and dividend/profit remittances, require prior CBS approval or an authorized-dealer application on prescribed exchange-control forms; resident foreign-owned companies may remit statutory profits after audit and tax clearance, while an International Company (IC) is treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes and may retain foreign-currency receipts in a CBS-approved Foreign Currency Account. Conversion of such receipts into WST is restricted to genuine Samoan local expenditure, and any WST-denominated distribution or transfer to non-residents triggers the standard capital-repatriation approval matrix.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1970, a resident exporter must obtain the relevant exchange-control permit for the export, repatriate the full export proceeds to Samoa within 90 days of shipment, and surrender 100% of such proceeds to an authorized dealer for conversion into WST immediately upon the earlier of repatriation or receipt, unless a foreign-currency retention approval has been issued by the CBS. For the non-resident Samoa International Company conducting subscription-box e-commerce, physical goods are typically shipped directly from third-country suppliers to end consumers, so no Samoa customs export declaration or surrender event arises on the physical flow; where goods pass through Samoa customs, a deemed export declaration must be lodged and any WST-converted proceeds must be surrendered under the standard rule. Retention of USD subscription receipts in the IC's Foreign Currency Account requires a general exchange-control license or CBS letter of approval, which should be secured during bank onboarding to eliminate deemed-surrender exposure and to permit unrestricted offshore treasury operations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS); statutory mandate derived from the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, the Exchange Control Act 1970 and the regulations and directives promulgated thereunder, together with the Financial Institutions Act 1996 (as amended). All foreign-exchange transactions in Samoa must be routed through CBS-licensed authorized dealers, being the licensed commercial banks, and no resident or non-resident enterprise may maintain external accounts or process trade settlements outside the Samoan banking system without prior CBS authorization.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Samoa does not maintain a domestic e-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime for non-bank entities; only CBS-licensed commercial banks qualify as authorized dealers under exchange control. Regulated digital processors (Stripe, Authorize.Net, Payoneer, Wise) may be deployed as customer-facing card-acquiring and payment-aggregation collection rails, but they must not function as the IC's principal treasury settlement account because funds would then be held outside the Samoan banking system in an unlicensed foreign entity, creating exchange-control contraventions and a bankability failure at onboarding. Viability rating: HIGH as a collection layer with settlement directly into the IC's USD account at a Samoan authorized dealer; LOW as a substitute for a Samoan commercial bank treasury and liquidity account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contribution is remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's foreign bank to the IC's Capital Escrow or Operational USD account at a CBS-licensed authorized dealer; the MT103 remittance information must include the shareholder's legal name, the IC registration number and the narrative Equity Contribution / Share Subscription or Unsecured Shareholder Loan. In parallel, the IC files a board resolution accepting the injection, updates the statutory share register and submits any required CBS exchange-control or foreign-investment application where the threshold applies. Subscription-box collections from retail customers are batched by the card processor through network settlement into the IC's USD clearing account; no Samoa exchange-control event arises while the receipts remain in USD, provided the Foreign Currency Account retention approval has been obtained.Clearing rails for trade settlements comprise: (1) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for routine supplier payments (boxes, components, freight) from the IC's USD account to beneficiary banks globally, each matched to a commercial invoice; (2) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued by SWIFT MT700 for large repetitive procurement tranches in China and Asia, payable at sight or on a deferred basis against shipping documents; (3) Documentary Collections transmitted via SWIFT MT400 for mid-value shipments where letter-of-credit cost-benefit is adverse. The authorized dealer must receive the underlying purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list and transport evidence before releasing funds; WST disbursements for domestic corporate costs (registered agent fees, CBS levies, legal and accounting fees) are converted at the authorized dealer's spot rate under the IC's approved conversion allowance.Outright deliverable FX forwards with the Samoan authorized dealing bank, typically up to 12 months tenor, supported by genuinely committed USD, AUD and NZD subscription cash flows and supplier payment schedules, with CBS documentation evidencing underlying trade exposure.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) with the dealing bank or an approved international counterparty under a CBS no-objection or prior approval, employed to align USD-denominated subscription revenues with CNY-denominated supplier costs and to manage residual WST exposure.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on WST/USD obtainable only through international dealing desks with prior CBS notification; practical utility is limited because the tala is trade-weighted-basket pegged and the domestic deliverable forward window is operationally narrow.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File / Master File threshold is expressly prescribed in Samoan Tala (WST); Samoa's Income Tax Act 2015 enforces the arm's length principle and MaF may demand contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation regardless of transaction size. A prudential compliance threshold for a subscription-box IC is preparation of Master File / Local File documentation where controlled cross-border transactions exceed WST 5,000,000 annually.

Economic substance scope: Samoa applies Economic Substance Regulations for International Companies under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended 2018) and SIFA Substance Rules. Relevant activities include 'distribution and service centre' business, which captures a subscription-box model involving warehousing, order fulfillment, logistics coordination, and customer service. A compliant IC must (1) perform core income-generating activities in Samoa, (2) maintain adequate physical offices, employees, and expenditure in Samoa, (3) be managed and controlled in Samoa, and (4) file an annual economic substance return. An IC whose subscription-box operations are fully performed abroad fails the substance test, loses the exemption protection, and becomes liable to information exchange and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Samoan-Source Income Concealment

Statutory sanction: Assessment of corporate income tax at 25% on concealed income; penalty surcharge of 150% of the tax avoided; interest at 10% per annum; criminal prosecution for tax evasion carrying a fine up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Tax Administration Act 2015; possible deregistration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax where the corporate form is used as a sham; the Supreme Court may issue asset restraint orders and travel bans; directors face criminal conviction, disqualification from managing Samoan companies, and civil restitution of diverted profits.
Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: SIFA monetary penalties for late or false economic substance filings; compulsory exchange of information with foreign tax authorities; automatic revocation of tax-exempt privileges; strike-off from the register with loss of legal capacity; potential inclusion of the entity on international harmful-tax-practices listings.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false substance declarations are personally liable for fines and imprisonment; nominee directors face indemnity claims and asset freezes; directors of a struck-off company are jointly liable for the company's unpaid fees and penalties in civil proceedings.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fines and imprisonment under the Samoa Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Financing of Terrorism Act 2007; freezing orders on the IC's bank accounts and payment processor accounts (Stripe, Authorize.Net); revocation of the registered agent's license and deregistration of the IC; administrative penalties applied by SIFA.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face individual criminal liability for money laundering and terrorist financing offences with custodial sentences up to 14 years; civil forfeiture of personal assets; travel restriction orders issued on application of the FIU; lifelong prohibition from acting as a director in Samoa.
False Tax Returns / Fraudulent Records

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 150% of the undercharged tax plus interest; prosecution for forgery and false statements; cancellation of the IC's registration by the Registrar; withdrawal of tax-exempt status with retroactive assessment at 25%; referral to the Samoan prosecutorial authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for restitution of all penalties and unpaid taxes; criminal conviction leading to imprisonment; extradition risk where conduct involves foreign customers' funds; permanent ban from holding directorships in Samoa and from engaging licensed agents.
