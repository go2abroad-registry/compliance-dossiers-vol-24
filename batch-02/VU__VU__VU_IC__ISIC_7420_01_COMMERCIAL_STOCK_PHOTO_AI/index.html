Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Department of Customs and Inland Revenue; online portal: https://customsinlandrevenue.gov.vu

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities are not subject to a general income tax registration requirement in Vanuatu. The Vanuatu International Companies Act (Cap. 222) requires an International Company (IC) to file its registered office and agent details with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC). For VAT purposes, only a business making taxable supplies above the VAT registration threshold must apply to the Vanuatu Department of Customs and Inland Revenue for a VAT identification number. A foreign entity with no Vanuatu source income and no local fixed establishment has no statutory TIN obligation; a Tax Identification Number is issued only upon VAT registration or employer PAYE registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu generally imposes no income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, or estate duty on International Companies. There is no check-the-box election because Vanuatu does not levy corporate income tax. An IC may elect to register for VAT voluntarily once it meets the turnover threshold, but no hybrid mismatch elections are available in domestic law. Under the International Companies Act, an IC may not elect for taxation as a resident company; the statutory tax exemption is automatic and non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 4,000,000 (four million vatu) annual taxable turnover; voluntary registration permitted below this threshold; exporters of goods and certain services may register to claim zero-rating and input VAT recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the Value Added Tax Act 2013 (Cap. 231) to: (a) the export of goods from Vanuatu, provided the exporter retains proof of physical export; (b) international transport of passengers or goods; (c) supplies of certain offshore services to non-residents, including the supply of digital goods, royalty-like payments for the use of intellectual property, and services physically performed outside Vanuatu; and (d) supplies to a person carrying on business outside Vanuatu where the services are directly related to goods outside Vanuatu or are for direct use outside Vanuatu. A stock photography and generative visual assets platform selling digital image licenses to non-resident customers is generally eligible for zero-rating as an export of services, provided the supplier is a VAT-registered person and holds contemporaneous documentary evidence of the recipient's non-resident status and the offshore performance or consumption of the service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (from Vanuatu Customs Service) for physical export of goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (delivery proof for physical goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange receipt evidence from a Vanuatu licensed bank)
  • VAT Export Invoice with VAT registration number of supplier
  • Contract or license agreement evidencing the non-resident customer and offshore usage of digital assets

Vanuatu does not currently operate a mandatory electronic invoicing clearance (e-invoicing) regime with real-time fiscal APIs or digital stamping. VAT-registered persons must issue a tax invoice containing prescribed particulars (seller name and VAT registration number, invoice number, date, description, consideration excluding VAT, VAT amount, and total). Records must be maintained in Vanuatu or be accessible electronically; no real-time government clearance or pre-approval of invoices is required. The Department of Customs and Inland Revenue may require periodic electronic lodgment of VAT returns, but there is no central e-invoicing database.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims are subject to reassessment by the Department of Customs and Inland Revenue, which may claw back the unrecovered VAT together with interest. Penalties under the Value Added Tax Act include an initial penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall for intentional evasion or fraud, plus late payment penalties of 2% per month or part-month. Criminal prosecution is possible for knowingly furnishing false documentation, with fines and imprisonment. The Department has the power to require contemporaneous bank records and export documentation; failure to produce the mandated bundle within the statutory audit window results in automatic denial of the zero-rating claim and retrospective VAT assessment at 15%.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains a liberal foreign exchange regime with no statutory exchange controls on current account or capital account transactions applicable to Vanuatu International Companies. The Vanuatu vatu is convertible through Reserve Bank of Vanuatu authorized dealers into USD, AUD, NZD, EUR, JPY, and other major currencies, though offshore VUV liquidity is narrow and treasury settlement for cross-border stock-media licensing is normally denominated in USD. Profits, dividends, royalties, and capital repatriation from an International Company are not subject to Vanuatu exchange-control approval, withholding tax, or a profit remittance quota; however, the paying bank and receiving correspondent bank will still apply anti-money laundering source-of-funds and sanctions screening before release.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandated conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines in Vanuatu. An International Company may lawfully retain gross licensing and stock-photo export receipts in any permissible foreign currency ledger, including USD, in a domestic commercial bank or authorized dealer. The Reserve Bank of Vanuatu does not compel conversion into VUV and does not require a conversion permit for receipts attributable to an International Company operating outside the Vanuatu domestic economy.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. A Vanuatu International Company may use a licensed foreign fintech EMI or digital cross-border payment platform for outbound licensing, disbursement, or wallet collection, but such an EMI cannot operate as a deposit-taking institution inside Vanuatu without a Reserve Bank of Vanuatu license. Offshore EMIs are most viable for USD or EUR treasury concentration outside Vanuatu, while the primary corporate bank account should remain at a Vanuatu authorized dealer to facilitate proof of funds, CRS/AEOI reporting status, and correspondent banking clearance. VUV-denominated wallet balances must not be held through an unregulated offshore EMI because VUV settlement remains subject to RBV-supervised banking channels.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial equity and shareholder loan funding should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's licensed bank directly to the International Company's USD settlement account at a Vanuatu authorized dealer. The remittance instruction must include a clear purpose code such as 'Direct investment in share capital' or 'Capital contribution to non-resident International Company', together with the full legal entity name and Vanuatu corporate registration number. The receiving bank will conduct a source-of-funds verification and may require the board resolution authorizing the share issuance, the executed share subscription agreement, and the stated consideration per share. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu does not impose corporate income tax at the national level. An International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act [CAP 222] is statutorily exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, dividend withholding tax, and stamp duty on worldwide income, irrespective of profit distribution.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an International Company to any shareholder, resident or non-resident. Distributions may be paid in any freely convertible currency; no government approval, no capital duty, and no exchange control applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File / Master File threshold in VUV exists because Vanuatu does not impose corporate income tax and has no Transfer Pricing Act. Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019, an IC carrying on a relevant activity involving related parties must nevertheless maintain contemporaneous accounting records demonstrating arm's length character, retain them for 7 years, and produce them to the Commissioner within 21 days of demand. If the IC belongs to a foreign group with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million, the ultimate parent's OECD Country-by-Country Report must include the Vanuatu entity. Practical threshold for a stock-photo/AI-asset IC: any related-party royalty, service fee, or distribution arrangement, regardless of amount, triggers documentation discipline.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 and associated Regulations apply to ICs conducting a 'relevant activity' (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, finance and leasing, headquarters, holding company, IP business, distribution and service centre). A commercial stock-photo and generative-AI visual asset marketplace selling to unrelated third-party end customers through Stripe and PayPal does not ordinarily constitute a relevant activity, because the 'distribution and service centre' head requires provision of goods or services to related parties. To retain non-relevance, the IC must ensure (i) all material contracts are with unrelated customers, (ii) the beneficial owner is not the sole licensee of the AI model, and (iii) any licensed-in IP from an affiliate is paid at arm's length royalty. If the self-assessment changes to 'relevant', the IC must demonstrate substantial core income-generating activities in Vanuatu: at least one board meeting in Vanuatu per year, a resident director, a company secretary, physical premises, local payroll, and adequate operating expenditure. Failure to file the ESR declaration or to satisfy the substance test is a criminal offence triable both against the company and its officers.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Offshore Tax Evasion / Foreign CFC Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: In Vanuatu: no local tax charge arises, but VFSC may strike off the IC if it discovers domestic business activity or false declarations. In the shareholder's jurisdiction: reassessment of all digital-asset royalty income at the shareholder level (e.g., U.S. federal rate 21% plus state income tax), 20–40% accuracy-related penalties, statutory interest, criminal referral for willful evasion (up to 5 years' imprisonment in the U.S.), and equivalent EU/UK GAAR counteraction with double-tax elimination denied.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false non-resident declarations face personal fines up to VUV 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years under the International Companies Act. Foreign courts may impose civil restitution against the beneficial owner as shadow director, issue travel bans, and pierce the corporate veil to attach personal assets where the IC is a mere alter ego.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Corporate penalty up to VUV 2,000,000 and daily default penalty up to VUV 100,000; mandatory referral by VFSC to the Financial Intelligence Unit and the OECD Global Forum; inclusion on the EU Annex II 'grey list' (Vanuatu was listed in October 2024) triggers enhanced due diligence by EU financial institutions and loss of EU withholding benefits and grant eligibility.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for unpaid penalties; the Vanuatu Supreme Court may issue travel bans and asset-freezing orders; making a false ESR declaration is punishable by imprisonment up to 2 years; the veil is pierced where the corporation operates as the shareholder's instrumentality, exposing personal assets to civil restitution.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Corporate fine up to VUV 1,000,000; suspension or revocation of the registered agent's licence and the IC's good standing; confiscation of proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act; aggravated money-laundering conviction carries a fine up to VUV 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal prosecution, personal confiscation orders, freezing of foreign bank accounts, extradition under Vanuatu mutual assistance treaties, and travel bans; failure to file an STR is a standalone offence punishable by up to 2 years' imprisonment; compliance officers and directors are personally accountable for knowledge-based failures.
Late or Non-Filing / De-registration

Statutory sanction: Administrative strike-off and dissolution; all company assets, including the stock-photo IP portfolio and AI-model copyrights, vest in the Republic of Vanuatu as bona vacantia; the IC loses legal capacity to sue or enforce contracts while struck off; restoration is possible only by Supreme Court order with full arrears, penalties, and legal costs paid.

Director personal liability: Directors who trade, transfer IP, or dissipate assets after strike-off are jointly and severally liable for the company's debts; foreign courts may impose travel bans pending resolution; distribution agreements and Stripe/PayPal merchant accounts terminate automatically on loss of good standing, causing irrecoverable revenue loss.
Misuse of Tax-Exempt Status / Prohibited Domestic Business

Statutory sanction: Retroactive loss of the tax exemption: the IC becomes assessable to 15% VAT, Business Licence fees, PAYE on local employees, customs duties on imported equipment, and any future Vanuatu corporate tax; VFSC may order compulsory winding-up for breach of the non-resident business prohibition; administrative fines apply for each prohibited act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize domestic business are personally liable for unpaid taxes, fees, and fines; the Court may disqualify directors for up to 5 years and issue civil restitution orders for profits derived from the prohibited activity; in egregious cases, the directors face criminal prosecution and imprisonment.
