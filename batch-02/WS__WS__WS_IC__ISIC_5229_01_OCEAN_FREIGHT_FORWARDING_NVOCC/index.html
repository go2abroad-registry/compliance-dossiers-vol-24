Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR) of the Independent State of Samoa, operating under the Tax Administration Act 2019; taxpayer e-services portal 'Molimauina' at https://molimauina.gov.ws; customs clearance administered by the Samoa Customs Service via the Asycuda World single-window system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) obtains a Samoan Tax Identification Number (TIN) by filing Form TIN-01 Application for Registration with the Ministry for Revenue. The IC must be incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 and maintain a licensed registered agent and registered office in Samoa. Submission requires: certified copy of the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, register of directors and shareholders, notarised passports of beneficial owners, an IC registered agent certificate, and a board resolution authorising TIN registration and appointment of a local tax representative where relevant. Non-resident applicants are registered immediately; documents in multilingual form are accepted in English or certified translation. There is no statutory minimum capital requirement. TIN issuance precedes VAGST registration and customs exporter code activation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no US-style check-the-box election. Under the International Companies Act 1987, an IC may by special resolution elect to convert to a local domestic company (triggering full Samoan income taxability and loss of offshore exemption), redomicile to another jurisdiction, or migrate into Samoa. For indirect tax, an IC may elect to register for VAGST on a voluntary basis below the WST 100,000 threshold or seek group registration for related entities. No hybrid mismatch elections arise for ICs because the offshore regime grants exemption from income tax and there is no controlled-foreign-company or hybrid instrument offset mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory); voluntary registration is permitted for persons making taxable supplies below the threshold; foreign entities supplying services to Samoa may be required to register if imports of services exceed the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAGST is zero-rated on (i) export of goods removed from Samoa (Schedule 1, VAGST Act 2009); (ii) international transport of goods, including ocean freight and NVOCC services rendered as principal or agent in connection with goods carried from Samoa; (iii) services physically performed outside Samoa and supplied to a non-resident; (iv) services ancillary to the export or international transit of goods, including inland haulage, port handling, containerisation and freight forwarding, provided the supplier obtains and retains documentary evidence that the service was performed for an export consignment. The supply must be invoiced to a non-resident recipient and paid for through a licensed Samoan bank to evidence foreign currency consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/Export Entry) filed via Asycuda World and processed by Samoa Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading (original, telex release, or express release) evidencing the NVOCC as contract carrier for the export consignment
  • Commercial export invoice and freight invoice issued by the NVOCC showing zero-rated VAGST treatment
  • Packing list and cargo manifest for the ocean shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice from a licensed Samoan commercial bank proving receipt of the export freight proceeds in convertible foreign currency

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing regime is in force. VAGST-registered entities must issue compliant tax invoices (VAGST Act s.30) showing supplier name and TIN, recipient name, description of supply, VAGST amount and rate. Monthly VAGST returns and payments are filed electronically via the Molimauina portal, with no statutory digital stamping or API-based fiscal clearance. Customs export declarations must be lodged electronically through the Asycuda World single-window platform. New rules require retained source records in electronic format to satisfy MfR audit queries.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to post-clearance audit by the Ministry for Revenue and Samoa Customs. Substantiation failure or fraudulent claims trigger: (i) a shortfall penalty of 50% of the tax understated, increased to 100% for intentional evasion, plus a 25% late payment surcharge (Tax Administration Act 2019, ss.73-78); (ii) criminal prosecution with fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to seven years (VAGST Act s.74); (iii) retrospective clawback of the unsubstantiated VAGST refund with monthly interest at 2%; (iv) cancellation of VAGST registration and denial of tax clearance certificates; and (v) exchange-control offences under the Exchange Control Regulations 1976 enforced by the Central Bank of Samoa, including daily penalties for non-repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days and revocation of the IC's offshore banking licence in egregious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoa tala (WST) is convertible for current-account transactions through licensed authorized dealers with standard documentary support and CBS reporting. Capital-account transactions require prior CBS approval or registration. A Samoa International Company (WS_IC) is treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes; therefore, its external foreign-currency receipts, including ocean freight and NVOCC service revenues earned wholly outside Samoa, may be held offshore without mandatory conversion. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders are structurally permitted from external accounts and do not create onshore WST conversion pressure. The principal hurdles are the need for a CBS-licensed bank to be the settlement agent, strict economic substance documentation for dividend outflows, and the prohibition of using foreign currency for domestic Samoan transactions without dealer conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident Samoan entities, export proceeds from goods and services must be repatriated through an authorized dealer within 30 days of receipt, and 100% of repatriated proceeds must be offered for conversion into WST unless a specific 'external account' exemption is obtained from CBS. For the WS_IC NVOCC/Freight-Forwarding International Company, being non-resident, freight income originated from international ocean carriage and brokerage is external income and is not subject to the resident surrender requirement. However, any WST-denominated collections generated from Samoan domestic cargo or onshore logistics invoices will be classified as resident-source transportation revenue and will trigger the 30-day surrender rule with a mandatory conversion ratio of 100% unless the International Company applies for and receives a CBS non-resident account designation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Exchange Control Regulations, together with the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Act 2019 (as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary treasury settlement rail; Medium as an ancillary disbursement channel only. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie and similar platforms are not licensed or regulated by the Central Bank of Samoa and cannot directly access the Samoa clearing system, the local WST real-time settlement, or CBS reporting. Their use is legally constrained to white-label correspondent arrangements where the underlying value transfer is ultimately booked through a CBS-licensed commercial bank. Operating the NVOCC treasury solely through an unlicensed EMI creates a high AML/CFT non-compliance risk and may trigger unauthorized money-transmission exposure under Samoan law.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder capital contributions must be sent by SWIFT MT103 to the WS_IC's USD or WST settlement account at a CBS-licensed commercial bank. The remittance must be annotated with the purpose code corresponding to 'foreign direct investment' or 'intercompany loan', the precise legal name of the International Company as beneficiary, and the underlying evidence, including the board resolution, share subscription document or loan agreement. The receiving bank will perform balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting to CBS. For capital injections in WST, or where the bank receives foreign currency and converts to WST, a Samoa authorized-dealer transaction report is required; however, external USD capital injection into a USD-denominated offshore account held within the bank may be retained in USD without conversion.For payment of ocean carriers, foreign haulage agents, port terminal operators and overseas logistics technology providers, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers and commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) issued through ANZ Bank Samoa or BSP Samoa. Correspondent-dollar clearing is commonly executed via U.S. correspondent banks. Payments to Philippine, Chinese, Fijian and New Zealand counterparties should use the MT103 with clear freight-invoice references and cargo identifiers. For onshore WST payments, the account must be debited in WST and settled locally; for foreign-currency payments, disbursement is made directly from the USD or other foreign-currency current account, avoiding unnecessary dual conversion. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by an exempt Samoan International Company to non-resident shareholders. Ordinary resident companies paying dividends to non-residents are subject to a 15% dividend withholding tax unless a Double Taxation Agreement reduces the rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No later than six months after the company's fiscal year-end. For a 31 December accounting period, the return and supporting financial statements are due by 30 June of the following calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Related-party cross-border transactions exceeding WST 1,000,000 in a tax year trigger contemporaneous Transfer Pricing documentation, including a Local File and, if consolidated group revenue exceeds the applicable Group threshold, a Master File. For a tax-exempt International Company operating as an NVOCC, SIRCA may require the same documentation where such transactions involve commissions, container slot charters, or freight rebates with related parties and threaten to erode the Samoan tax base.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's International Company regime does not impose full Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to Caribbean offshore centre rules, but the International Companies Act requires a registered office in Samoa, a licensed registered agent, maintenance of books and records, and commercially identifiable functions. For ocean freight forwarding ICs, SIRCA and foreign tax authorities will examine whether the IC has real decision-making, staff, bank accounts, and operational capacity. A pure mailbox NVOCC without substance is vulnerable to reclassification as a shell company, strike-off in Samoa, and CFC or anti-avoidance attack in the shareholder's residence jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Use of International Company Exemption

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2015 and Tax Administration Act 2012, SIRCA may issue a shortfall penalty of up to 100% of the tax avoided, impose late-payment charges up to 40%, and prosecute directors criminally. The Court may impose a fine of up to WST 1,000,000 and a term of imprisonment not exceeding 7 years, or both.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents who sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. SIRCA can issue enforcement certificates, freeze bank accounts, impose travel bans, and remove directors from the International Company register.
Shell Company and Abusive Redomiciliation Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Registrar of International Companies may cancel the certificate of incorporation, strike the company off the Register, and impose a fine not exceeding WST 50,000 plus daily default penalties. The company loses the right to enforce contracts in Samoan courts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable to creditors and to third-party cargo claimants where the company was used as an instrument of fraud. The Registrar may disqualify directors for up to 5 years and bar them from managing any Samoan company.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing legislation and the International Companies (Beneficial Ownership) rules, a company may be fined up to WST 500,000, have its bank accounts subject to freezing orders, and have its registered agent penalised and deregistered. Persistent breaches trigger regulatory review by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise undocumented payments or false UBO declarations face imprisonment of up to 5 years, personal fines, and confiscation of assets. Travel bans may be imposed during criminal investigations.
Transfer Pricing and Economic Substance Misstatement

Statutory sanction: SIRCA may disregard the arrangement, reattribute income to a taxable Samoan entity or to the entity exercising real functions, impose a substantial-compliance penalty of up to 50% of the underpaid tax, and charge interest. The exemption of the IC may be revoked retroactively if the transaction is found to be a sham.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the IC to enter into artificial arrangements may be personally assessed for unpaid tax and penalties where they knowingly participated in the avoidance scheme. Civil restitution actions may be brought against them for the benefit of the revenue authority.
DTA and Residency Shopping Abusive Treaty Claim

Statutory sanction: The foreign tax authority may deny treaty benefits, impose the full domestic withholding tax rate, and request exchange of information from Samoa under the tax treaty or the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance. Samoa may strike the IC off the Register for making false residency declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors may become personally liable for the unpaid foreign withholding tax and fines under the source country's tax law. A finding of tax fraud may lead to extradition proceedings and imprisonment in the source jurisdiction if the conduct rises to deliberate domestic criminal tax evasion.
