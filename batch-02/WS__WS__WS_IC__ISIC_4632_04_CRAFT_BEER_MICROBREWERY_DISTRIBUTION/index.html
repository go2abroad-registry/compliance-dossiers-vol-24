Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue of Samoa (MOR), National Taxpayer Services, https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) obtains statutory tax identity by filing the prescribed Incorporation Application with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) and registering the IC with the Ministry of Revenue through Form TIN-01. Where the IC engages in a taxable activity (importation, local supply, or export), the IC must also obtain a VAGST registration number by filing Form VAGST-01 and the accompanying supporting documents; for pure offshore exempt business, the IC may obtain a Tax Exemption Certificate instead of an active income-tax TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa law does not apply a US-style check-the-box election. The IC enjoys automatic statutory tax exemption under the International Companies Act 2014 for non-resident-source income and maintains that exemption by complying with the annual license fee regime. For indirect tax purposes, an IC may elect to voluntarily register for VAGST to claim input credits on export-related costs; no hybrid mismatch elective mechanism is relevant because the entity is treated as tax-transparent in its home jurisdiction only if such treatment is established by the foreign corporate law and recognised under Samoa tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover; voluntary registration permitted for exporters below the threshold to recover input VAT on production and distribution costs

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods such as craft beer and cold-chain keg inventory qualify for VAGST zero-rating when the supplier exports the goods from Samoa within 28 days of supply, retains evidence of physical export, and supplies the goods under a contract with a non-resident recipient. Direct offshore services and ancillary international distribution services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Samoa also qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is outside Samoa and the services are not supplied to a person in Samoa for consumption in Samoa.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through ASYCUDA World
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing foreign-currency payment repatriation
  • Original VAGST tax invoice and zero-rating certification
  • Certificate of Origin (Form A or non-preferential Samoa origin certificate, as applicable)
  • Cold-chain transport temperature logs for keg beer, where required by Samoa Customs or the destination market regulator

Samoa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal electronic invoicing or digital tax stamping for beer exports. Businesses must issue compliant VAGST tax invoices and export documentation, and all export declarations are processed through the Samoa Customs Service using the ASYCUDA World electronic single-window system, which serves as the de facto clearance and audit trail.

Samoa Customs and the Ministry of Revenue may retrospectively deny zero-rating and claw back VAGST refunds plus statutory late payment interest where export evidence is incomplete, untimely, or fraudulent. Knowingly providing false export declarations or fabricated freight documentation attracts civil penalties of up to 200% of the tax shortfall, criminal fines up to WST 100,000, imprisonment of up to 7 years, and loss of the IC's statutory tax exemption status if the offending activity taints the offshore character of the company.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa applies a managed exchange-rate framework with statutory exchange control. Current-account convertibility is broadly available: import/export payments, distribution fees, freight, insurance and trade settlement are processed by authorized dealers against commercial documents and bank reference checks, aligning with Samoa's Article VIII obligations with the IMF. The Samoan Tala (WST) is non-deliverable offshore; all conversion must occur through an onshore authorized dealer. Capital-account convertibility is selective: resident entities require CBS licensing for external borrowings and outbound remittances above WST 500,000-equivalent. A non-resident International Company (WS_IC) is carved out of the exchange-control perimeter where it maintains exclusively foreign-currency accounts and has no dealing in Samoan Tala; profit remittance and distribution to settlor/trust beneficiaries are then documentary rather than license-intensive, subject only to the depository bank's anti-money-laundering verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters, CBS directions require collection of export proceeds through an authorized dealer within 90 days of shipment and surrender of 100% of the foreign-exchange value into WST unless a foreign-currency retention permit has been granted for up to 50% working-capital cover. For the Samoa IC operating as a non-resident craft-beer wholesaler, export receipts from sales of cold-chain kegs outside Samoa are outside the surrender perimeter and may remain in USD in the IC's operational account. Where the IC is the actual Samoan exporter of record for customs purposes, receipts must still route through a licensed dealer with the export licence, bill of lading, and brewery invoice; only the resident cost portion is subject to mandatory WST conversion and CBS reporting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS; Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), administering the Exchange Control Act 1975, the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 together with reserve bank directives for authorized foreign-exchange dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium with material legal constraints. Samoa does not operate an e-money or payment-institution licence category, and a Samoa International Company may not itself hold itself out as a payment service provider. Cross-border digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud-type MSB rails) generally maintain Banking Act 2005-licensed counterparties outside Samoa and commonly apply exclusion lists covering Pacific offshore jurisdictions; they also require a funded inbound SWIFT transfer from a licensed Samoa bank, which duplicates the clearing chain and introduces depository uncertainty for the correspondent. Recommended construct: keep the licensed Samoa bank as the account-holder of record and use the fintech EMI only as a discrete USD pay-in/payout rail for refunds to distributors, never as the primary treasury hub.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended compliant routing: settlor/trust treasury in Hong Kong or Singapore executes a SWIFT MT103 customer transfer to the WS_IC's USD demand-deposit account at ANZ Bank (Samoa) Limited in Apia; if the value date exceeds USD 250,000, the sending bank should pre-advise with a bank reference confirming the underlying trust distribution. The transfer narrative must state 'International Company capital contribution — family trust distribution' and carry BPM6 purpose code 3100 (equity injection) for share capital, or 3900 (other investment — shareholder loan) for debt funding. Funds retained in USD are outside the CBS exchange-control perimeter; if converted to WST for local cold-chain payroll, the authorized dealer records a foreign-exchange declaration at the counter. Verification relies on the account-opening KYC file, the issued-share register, the capitalisation board resolution and the banker's reference from the remitting trust.Trade settlements for hop, malt, keg and refrigerated container purchases should run over SWIFT MT103 customer transfers from the authorized Samoan dealer (ANZ or BSP) to the supplier's EU/US account; for contracts above USD 250,000 prefer an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued under UCP600, at sight or 90 days usance, with required documents comprising commercial invoice, cold-chain bill of lading or air waybill, brewery health and phytosanitary certificates, insurance certificate and certificate of origin. Local direct-debit rails are used solely for Apia cold-chain utilities, warehouse rent and quarantine fees; cross-border disbursements must never be delegated to unlicensed money transmitters or cash couriers. The exporting IC must retain the CBS export dossier (delivery notes, export licence, customs declaration) for the authorized dealer's periodic reporting.Forwards — outright USD/WST forward contracts with an authorized dealer, tenor capped at 12 months and strictly supported by an export sales contract or purchase order (CBS trade-backing rule).NDFs — non-deliverable forwards referencing the Samoan Tala are not permissible under Samoa exchange control; there is no onshore NDF market and offshore WST NDFs are treated as prohibited speculative rings.Currency swaps — cross-currency USD/EUR and USD/GBP swaps arranged through an authorized dealer's international treasury desk with CBS notification are permissible for cold-chain import/export working capital; WST cross-currency swaps are not offered onshore.

Master / Local File threshold: No Samoa-code threshold for ICs; commercial benchmark aligns with OECD local file / master file when related-party transactions exceed WST 2,000,000 in a year.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations. An IC must maintain a registered office and locally licensed registered agent in Samoa, but no genuine economic activity requirement or staff/expenditure ratios apply; this makes the IC an acceptable low-risk holding/distribution vehicle only when no domestic Samoan trade occurs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Strike-off of the IC, forfeiture of property by the Registrar, and financial penalties of up to WST 100,000

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; court-ordered travel bans and imprisonment for up to 7 years apply.
Shell Company Misuse / Domestic Trade Abroad

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the IC tax exemption, retroactive corporate income tax at 27% on all domestic-source profits, and 150% surcharge on the tax shortfall

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil: joint and several civil liability for the full tax arrears, director disqualification, and potential custodial sentence for fraudulent trading.
AML

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to WST 1,000,000, regulatory suspension of the IC, and cancellation of the agent's license

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal fines, forfeiture of unlawful gains, and imprisonment of up to 10 years for knowing money-laundering facilitation.
