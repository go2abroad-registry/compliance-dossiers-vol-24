E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must apply for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the Corporate Registration Form (IR-C1), certified certificate of incorporation, attested constitutional documents, and proof of appointment of the local registered agent. International Companies receive a TIN upon incorporation under the International Companies Act 1987. A separate VAGST registration must be filed with the SRA when taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold; foreign entities may appoint a locally resident fiscal representative for VAGST compliance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Samoa in accordance with the Customs Management Act are zero-rated when the supplier holds the export documentation and the goods are exported within the statutory time period. Services, including customs brokerage and forwarding services, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident customer and the direct benefit is outside Samoa; however, brokerage services physically performed in Samoa connected to imported goods, or services directly for the benefit of a resident importer, are standard-rated. Zero-rating for a customs brokerage house should be documented by identifying the non-resident principal, showing the direct link to the exported goods, and retaining all clearance records showing the goods exited Samoa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED) submitted in ASYCUDA World with broker reference code
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Samoan commercial bank showing foreign exchange repatriation for the export supply
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order identifying the non-resident customer and the zero-rated brokerage fee
  • Certificate of Shipment / customs release authorization or electronic Customs Permit confirming goods left the territory

Samoa has not implemented a national real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate as of the current date. VAGST invoices must contain prescribed particulars and be issued within the statutory timeframe. Electronic invoicing is permitted through standard B2B systems. All import and export clearance is executed electronically through the SRA's ASYCUDA World customs management system, which provides an API/Gateway interface for customs brokers; brokers must hold a valid broker license and digital machine-readable card linked to the ASYCUDA World user profile. No fiscal stamping is required.

False or fraudulent zero-rating claims attract an automatic penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall plus interest, and civil penalties under the VAGST Act and Customs Management Act may be imposed up to WST 200,000 or three times the duty/VAGST evaded, whichever is greater. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment for up to seven years. Retrospective clawback applies for four years from the end of the accounting period in respect of ordinary errors; there is no time limit for fraud or deliberate misstatement. The Commissioner of the Samoa Revenue Authority is empowered to seize assets and impose maritime or border compliance penalties on the exporting principal and the broker as a licensed customs agent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a managed, thinly traded currency and is not deliverable offshore. Current-account convertibility is available for genuine trade and service payments through authorised dealers, subject to documentary proof and CBS reporting thresholds; capital-account remittances (dividends, capital repatriation) require CBS approval unless the IC exemption applies. Profit-remittance hurdle: to preserve the exemption, the customs brokerage IC must invoice, collect and settle exclusively in USD or AUD; collecting WST from domestic importers would trigger onshore exchange control, jeopardise the IC's tax-exempt status and require a separate Samoan-licensed entity. The blueprint therefore keeps the IC treasury 100% foreign-currency and routes domestic duty flows through a licensed local intermediary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Samoan-resident exporters, export proceeds must be repatriated into Samoa within 6 months of shipment and, where directed by an authorised dealer under s.7 of the Exchange Control Act 1976, be offered for sale into WST promptly upon receipt. This statutory surrender rule has no application to an exempt IC, provided brokerage commissions are earned offshore and invoiced in foreign currency; each incoming remittance should carry a brokerage contract reference to evidence non-resident service provision. No WST receipts may be booked into the IC's operating account.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, is the statutory foreign exchange and payments authority; day-to-day administration is delegated to authorised dealers (the licensed commercial banks) under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Foreign Exchange Regulations 1977. An International Company (IC) holding a valid international company certificate from the Samoa International Financial Authority (SIFA) is exempt from the Exchange Control Act 1976 for transactions concluded in foreign currency and performed wholly outside Samoa (exemptions under the International Companies Act 2017). Any WST-denominated transaction with a Samoan resident falls back within CBS jurisdiction and destroys the exemption.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary clearing rail; Medium only as an ancillary collections and payout channel. Offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA, EU-licensed, MAS-licensed) are not supervised by the Central Bank of Samoa and cannot engage in WST settlement; their utility is limited to holding USD/GBP/EUR float for foreign broker disbursements. Practical constraints: EMIs impose jurisdiction-based enhanced due diligence on Samoan ICs, require a reference letter from BSP or SCB, and cannot route funds to the Samoa Revenue Authority or Samoan importers. Recommended use is a USD payout wallet with a top-tier SWIFT-enabled EMI for disbursements below USD 10,000, mirrored monthly into the BSP Samoa account for CBS audit trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for subscribed capital: the beneficial owner remits USD by SWIFT MT103 to the IC's BSP Samoa operational account (or SCB escrow account) with the purpose description 'IC Share Capital Contribution — International Companies Act 2017'; the receiving bank performs enhanced due diligence and wires above USD 1,000,000 are held pending the share allotment resolution, updated share register and source-of-funds evidence. Verification bundle: Certificate of Incorporation, allotment board resolution, updated share register, SIFA maintenance receipt. No Samoan capital-import certificate is required. Any accidental WST credit must be immediately converted to USD or AUD to preserve the offshore-transaction exemption.Clearing rails in priority order: (1) overseas broker/agent fees and foreign customs authority payments — SWIFT MT103 direct from BSP Samoa via its correspondent network (BSP Port Moresby or ANZ/HSBC Singapore) with evidence of the underlying brokerage contract; (2) US border clearance payments — Automated Broker Interface (ABI) and ACH credits executed through the bank's USD correspondent account and reconciled against ABI release records; (3) duty payments to Samoa Revenue Authority — electronic transfer to SRA's CBS treasury account referencing the ASYCUDA World declaration number, routed exclusively through a licensed local intermediary in WST; (4) commercial letters of credit to foreign bonding houses for high-value customs bonds — sight LC issued by BSP Samoa with 30% cash margin. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File equivalent) is mandatory where the aggregate value of related-party transactions exceeds WST 500,000 per annum; a Country-by-Country Report is required only where consolidated group revenue meets the OECD threshold and Samoa has an active competent-authority exchange arrangement in force.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations under the International Companies Act 1987 apply to relevant activities, including 'distribution and service centre' activity, which covers customs clearance brokerage and bonded transit management. A Samoa IC must maintain within Samoa: (i) direction and management of the activity, (ii) a minimum number of qualified full-time employees with appropriate skills, (iii) adequate annual operating expenditure, and (iv) physical office premises. An annual economic substance declaration with supporting evidence must be filed with SIFA; a customs brokerage IC lacking on-island staff, premises, and management will be treated as a substance-less shell and exposed to penalty, information exchange, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2017: penalty tax of up to 150% of the tax shortfall, statutory interest accrual, criminal fines upon conviction, and confiscation of the benefit derived. False customs declarations separately trigger Customs Act penalties, suspension of the customs brokerage license, and permanent debarment from the broker interface.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face piercing of the corporate veil for deliberate evasion: civil restitution orders against personal assets, travel bans pending tax investigation, and imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SIFA administrative penalties under the International Companies Act 1987: fines per violation, power to compel dissolution, compulsory exchange of information with foreign tax authorities, and removal from the International Companies Register (strike-off).

Director personal liability: After strike-off, directors remain personally liable for company debts incurred during the non-compliant period; foreign tax jurisdictions may re-characterize profits and issue personal tax assessments; travel bans may be imposed under mutual assistance requests.
AML/CFT Breach (Broker Payment Rails and Trade-Based Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Money Laundering Prevention Act and SIFA AML directives: fines per contravention up to WST 100,000, revocation of AML compliance approval, freezing orders on bank accounts, and debarment from the Automated Broker Interface and wire payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability: imprisonment of up to 14 years for willful money laundering facilitation, personal fines, disqualification from acting as a director, and asset forfeiture orders.
Prohibited Domestic Trading by an International Company

Statutory sanction: Loss of the IC tax exemption with retrospective re-assessment at the 27% CIT rate plus penalties; fines under the International Companies Act; mandatory conversion to a domestic company or de-registration; revocation of any customs brokerage license by the Samoa Revenue and Customs authority.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorized the prohibited domestic trade are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, customs duties, and penalties; wage arrestment, asset seizure, and travel restriction orders may be imposed during recovery proceedings.
