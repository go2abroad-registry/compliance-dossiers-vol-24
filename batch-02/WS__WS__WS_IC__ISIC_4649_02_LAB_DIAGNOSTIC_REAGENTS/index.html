Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

International Companies Act 1987 (Samoa), as amended — Part II, ss. 3–14 (incorporation of international companies); ss. 69–75 (directors and officers); Part X, ss. 101–109 (registered office and registered agent); non-application of the Companies Act 2001 to international companies under s. 189.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Incorporation of an International Company (Form IC-A) together with the executed Memorandum and Articles of Association and the consent of the licensed registered agent.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. An International Company may be incorporated with a single share of no par value; no minimum paid-up capital, capitalization, or escrow deposit requirement is imposed by the International Companies Act 1987.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Samoa Fire and Emergency Services Authority (SFESA) following inspection of warehouse, cold-storage chambers, electrical installations, and emergency exits; structural load inspection certificate by a Samoan licensed engineer confirming compliance with the Building Code of Samoa; and occupational safety and health clearance from the OSH Division, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour covering workplace hazard assessment, spill-response kit availability, personal protective equipment, and staff training records.

Paid-in capital rule: None. An International Company may be incorporated with a single share of no par value; no minimum paid-up capital, capitalization, or escrow deposit requirement is imposed by the International Companies Act 1987.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Apia; SRA Taxpayer e-Services portal (sra.gov.ws) for income tax and VAGST filing; customs declarations filed through Samoa Customs Division via ASYCUDA World; the Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) administers foreign exchange repatriation requirements.

E-government filing portal

Foreign owners must engage a licensed Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) registered agent to incorporate the International Company under the International Companies Act 1987. Upon SIFA incorporation, the SRA automatically issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN); no minimum capital, no local residency, and no physical presence in Samoa are required. The IC may apply for a specific exemption certificate and may voluntarily register for VAGST only if it makes taxable supplies in Samoa, subject to SRA Commissioner approval. Standard processing is 1–3 business days after submission by the agent.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — an International Company transacting exclusively with non-residents and deriving no Samoan-source income is exempt from income tax under the International Companies Act 1987, s.193 and the Income Tax Act 2015; territorial scope excludes offshore wholesale distribution of IVD/ELISA diagnostic reagents; VAGST applies only to supplies consumed in Samoa. — Foreign owners must engage a licensed Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) registered agent to incorporate the International Company under the International Companies Act 1987. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — an International Company transacting exclusively with non-residents and deriving no Samoan-source income is exempt from income tax under the International Companies Act 1987, s.193 and the Income Tax Act 2015; territorial scope excludes offshore wholesale distribution of IVD/ELISA diagnostic reagents; VAGST applies only to supplies consumed in Samoa.

Available election forms

Samoa has no US-style 'check-the-box' election. The IC's tax classification is fixed by statute: it is an opaque, tax-exempt Samoa entity. The International Companies Act 1987 permits constitutional contracting-out elections on corporate administrative matters (share transfer restrictions, director quorum, redemption mechanics), which do not alter tax status. Foreign owners may obtain hybrid characterization only through the application of their home-jurisdiction classification rules to the Samoa IC; Samoa imposes no anti-hybrid or CFC look-through rules on the IC for non-residents.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration is triggered where taxable supplies exceed WST 100,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration below threshold is subject to SRA Commissioner approval; an IC trading offshore with no Samoan taxable supplies has no VAGST registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of clinical diagnostic reagents (HS 3822.00) is zero-rated if the goods are physically exported from Samoa to a non-resident within 30 days of supply, the exporter holds an approved Customs Export Declaration, and the sale is invoiced to and settled by a non-resident customer. For offshore-to-offshore trading where the IC purchases from a foreign manufacturer and sells to a foreign buyer without goods entering Samoa, the supply falls entirely outside VAGST scope, achieving an effective 0% outcome without Samoan zero-rating formalities.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document filed via ASYCUDA World)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Samoa within 30 days of supply
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (with HS 3822.00 classification, batch/lot references, and ISIC 4649 description)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed Samoa commercial bank evidencing repatriation of export sales proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin (Form E or applicable preferential certificate) where tariff preference is claimed in the destination market
  • IVD conformity documentation for the ELISA diagnostic reagents (e.g., EU CE-IVD certificate or ISO 13485 certificate) to substantiate the bona fide commercial transaction

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping mandate exists. VAGST returns must be filed electronically through SRA e-Services on a monthly or quarterly basis depending on turnover volume; import and export declarations are processed electronically through ASYCUDA World. There is no prescribed e-invoice schema beyond standard commercial documentation, but electronic books and records must be retained for 5 years for audit purposes.

Under the Tax Administration Act 2012, reckless or fraudulent export zero-rating claims trigger penalty tax of 150% of the tax shortfall, monthly late-payment penalties, and retrospective clawback of all VAGST input credits, with a 5-year audit window. Criminal prosecution under the VAGST Act 1992 for false declarations carries fines up to WST 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days breaches the Exchange Control Regulations administered by the Central Bank of Samoa and attracts separate monetary penalties, exchange-control sanctions, and potential revocation of the IC's exchange-control authorization.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains a dual-system exchange control regime. The Samoa tālā (WST) is convertible for genuine current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, royalties) through licensed authorized dealers, subject to documentary verification and anti-money-laundering compliance. Capital-account transactions, including equity injections, inter-company loans and portfolio investments, require specific CBS approval unless conducted through an exempt International Company. For a tax-exempt International Company under the International Companies Act 2012, profit remittance is generally unrestricted if the underlying funds originate from offshore trading and are not sourced from onshore Samoa revenue. Residual hurdles include the need for audited financial statements, beneficial-ownership declarations, and Central Bank statistics reporting via authorized banks. The practical convertibility of WST is thin because Samoa is a small-dollar economy; the effective operating currency for the IVD reagents distribution business should be USD, held in offshore clearing accounts to avoid WST liquidity friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The CBS Exchange Control Regulations require resident exporters to repatriate all export proceeds to Samoa through an authorized dealer within 30 days of receipt and to surrender 100% of such proceeds into a WST account unless a specific exemption is granted. Non-resident International Companies that do not carry on business within Samoa and whose export goods are never landed in Samoa are generally exempt from the mandatory surrender requirement, provided they maintain full documentary trails proving onward shipment from third-country manufacturers to overseas buyers. Any temporary holding of export proceeds in a Samoa domestic bank account attracts the surrender rule; therefore, the recommended structure is to route export receipts through an offshore USD settlement account and only bring WST into Samoa for statutory license fees, professional fees and minimal operational expenses.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) — Falefa, Apia, Samoa (under the Central Bank Act 1984, Exchange Control Act 1971 and Exchange Control Regulations 1981)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Samoa does not maintain a fintech electronic-money-institution (EMI) or crypto-asset licensing framework, and the CBS prohibits non-bank entities from holding client funds for payment processing. Offshore digital EMIs, payment facilitation platforms and virtual-asset service providers face legal inability to obtain a Samoan banking relationship or an authorized-dealer license. Even for cross-border USD settlement, an offshore EMI cannot be used as the primary clearing backbone for a Samoan International Company if the company is required to demonstrate a physically licensed Samoan bank account for regulatory reporting and substance evidence. The only viable use is a non-core, secondary payment gateway for receiving select customer card payments, provided funds are swept daily into a licensed bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound routing for an equity injection to the Samoan International Company is a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wire from the parent company or shareholder to the IC's USD operational account, referencing the company registration number and the purpose code 'G.I.2 Equity Capital Contribution' under the CBS balance-of-payments classification. The remittance advice must include the IC's legal name, bank account number, and the beneficiary bank's SWIFT code. Verification evidence must include the executed share-subscription agreement, board resolution approving the capital injection, source-of-funds letter from the injecting shareholder, and the IC's updated register of members. The corresponding bank must receive a copy of the CBS exchange-control approval or, for an exempt International Company, a legal cover letter confirming the exemption from CBS capital-control approval. If the capital injection is for working capital to pay third-party reagent suppliers, it should additionally be supported by the underlying supplier contract to prevent the transaction being blocked by the receiving bank as a suspicious structured payment.The primary outbound clearing rail for trade settlements is SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfer from the Samoan IC's USD account at ANZ/BSP to supplier accounts globally, using purpose code 'G.1.1.1 Imports of Goods' for reagent purchases and 'G.2.1.1 Business Services' for logistics and insurance. For high-value or transaction-intensive trade to customers in the Asia-Pacific region, the preferential rail is a commercial documentary credit opened by a top-tier Asian correspondent bank (e.g., a Chinese or Singapore bank) against the IC's purchase order, with the LC denominated in USD, sight or 30-day terms, and full set of shipping documents. The Samoan IC may also use the Samoa-based bank as advising bank for an outward LC issued by its own account, but the smoothness of settlement materially improves if the IC maintains a sub-limit or cash-collateral pledged account with the issuing correspondent bank. Real-time gross settlement within Samoa is not available for USD; instead USD clearing is executed over the New York CHIPS/Fedwire network via the correspondent bank chain, while WST settlement, if ever required, is handled through the Central Bank of Samoa's interbank settlement system.Outright USD/WST forward contracts with a licensed authorized dealer, limited to the settlement of onshore operating expenses in SamoaNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing WST for offshore entities that do not hold WST cash flowsCross-currency swaps between USD, EUR and NZD for matching multi-currency supplier receivables/payables, subject to CBS counterparty and tenor guidelinesPlain-vanilla FX options (call/put) on USD/WST for protecting rare repatriation events, excluding complex structured products

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory transfer pricing documentation threshold applies to a tax-exempt International Company. Prudent contemporaneous documentation benchmark: prepare a Local File when annual related-party cross-border transactions exceed WST 1,000,000, and a Master File when consolidated group revenue exceeds WST 10,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations apply only where the International Company engages in a geographically mobile relevant activity, such as intellectual property holding, banking, financing, insurance, fund management, or headquarters operations. Wholesale distribution of laboratory diagnostic reagents is a non-mobile trading activity; sufficient substance is evidenced by a Samoa registered office, a resident registered agent, genuinely conducted management and decision-making, and contracts and invoicing for customer and supplier logistics.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion shadow profits

Statutory sanction: Back assessment of all undeclared tax, late payment penalties of up to 25%, daily interest accrual, and criminal fines for deliberate tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly assist the evasion may be made personally liable for restitution; travel bans and imprisonment of up to 7 years are available under Samoan tax statutes.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike the International Company off the register; banks may close accounts under know-your-client rules; the Samoan tax authority may re-characterise the transactions as performed by the underlying principal.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face civil liability, director disqualification and professional indemnity exposure if they permit the company to operate as a sham; the corporate veil may be pierced to impose restitution.
AML / sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Samoan Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Act 2019, including asset freezing, confiscation, and mandatory reporting to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents may be personally fined, have their registration cancelled, and face imprisonment for money laundering or financing of unauthorized parties; corporate incorporation does not insulate personnel.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make a discretionary transfer pricing adjustment, impose underpayment interest, and apply documentation penalties of up to 40% of the adjusted tax amount.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false transfer pricing declarations or knowingly approving mispriced intercompany invoices may incur civil recovery, fraudulent trading liability, and personal director disqualification.
