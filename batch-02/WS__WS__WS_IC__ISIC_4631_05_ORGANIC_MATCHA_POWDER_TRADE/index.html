Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Ministry for Revenue (MfR; Matagaluega o Tupe Maua a Samoa), Apia, Samoa. Sole national tax and customs authority administering the Tax Administration Act 2011, the VAGST Act 1994 and the Customs Act 2014. Online portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws; e-filing at https://efiler.revenue.gov.ws; customs clearance through ASYCUDA World managed by the Samoa Customs Service.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoan International Company (IC) obtains its statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by filing MfR form TIN-01, the IC certificate of incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, certificate of good standing, registered office and registered agent particulars, and director/shareholder identification. Application is submitted at https://www.revenue.gov.ws or physically at the MfR Headquarters in Apia, with issuance typically completed within 3-5 business days for standard compliance files. The Registrar of International Companies concurrently issues the certificate of tax exemption under the International Companies Act 2016. The TIN is a mandatory prerequisite for opening bank accounts, filing VAGST returns, lodging customs declarations and claiming export zero-rating.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Samoa International Company is treated as an offshore tax-exempt vehicle: profits derived from business carried on outside Samoa, including cross-border procurement and distribution of organic matcha powder to non-resident buyers, are entirely outside the Samoa income tax base. Samoa corporate income tax is not imposed on foreign-source trading profit, dividends, interest, royalties or capital gains; the IC remains within the VAGST and customs net solely for import/export compliance, and only Samoa-sourced supplies would attract Samoan taxation.

Available election forms

Samoa does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections, tax-transparent entity elections, hybrid mismatch elections or VAGST group registration. An IC may elect to be governed by the International Companies Act 2016 rather than the Companies Act 2012, obtain a Certificate of Exemption from the Registrar of International Companies, and voluntarily register for VAGST to preserve input-credit and export zero-rating rights. Entity classification for foreign-tax purposes (e.g., Form 8832 check-the-box in the United States) is a matter of the parent jurisdiction's law and carries no Samoan filing consequence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAGST registration is triggered when the actual or reasonably expected total value of taxable supplies in any 12-month period exceeds WST 100,000 (approx. USD 35,000); voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. Zero-rated export supplies count toward the threshold for registration purposes. A non-resident supplier making taxable supplies to persons in Samoa is also registrable. An International Company engaged solely in offshore matcha trading should register once turnover exceeds the threshold in order to lawfully issue tax invoices, claim input credits and support 0% export treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAGST rate applies to: (i) goods physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply, remaining outside Samoa and not subsequently returned; (ii) services physically performed outside Samoa; and (iii) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply. The matcha exporter must prove that title and risk passed outside Samoa, that the goods left Samoa within the statutory window under an approved export declaration, and that export proceeds are repatriated through the licensed banking system. The claim is evidenced in the VAGST return together with the full export documentation bundle; unsupported claims are re-assessed at 15%.

  • Cargo Export Declaration (CED) approved through ASYCUDA World bearing the customs export reference and exit confirmation
  • Original commercial invoice and packing list describing organic matcha powder, net weight, unit value and HS classification (green tea matcha under HS 0902.10)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical carriage from Samoa (Apia port/airport) to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice issued by a licensed Samoan commercial bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds within the statutory repatriation period
  • Certificate of Origin issued where the foreign importer claims preferential tariff treatment (optional but recommended)

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, SAF-T filing or continuous controls regime applies in Samoa. VAGST returns and payments are submitted electronically via the MfR EFiler portal; customs declarations are lodged exclusively via ASYCUDA World. All invoices issued by a registered person must contain the supplier's name and TIN, VAGST registration number, date, description and tax amount. Unregistered exporters clearing goods use ordinary commercial invoices and packing lists without fiscal branding.

The Ministry for Revenue cross-matches every zero-rated export claim against ASYCUDA export records, carrier documents, banking inflows and counterparty contracts. Where zero-rating is claimed without adequate proof, the export is recharacterized as a standard-rated domestic supply, the 15% VAGST is clawed back retrospectively, and the shortfall attracts late-payment penalty interest of 20% per annum. Fraudulent or deliberately false export documentation is subject to a 150% shortfall penalty, fines up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment under the Tax Administration Act 2011 and VAGST Act 1994; the Commissioner may also recover clawed-back input credits as a debt due, and directors of the IC bear personal liability for knowingly false claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a managed-float exchange-rate framework with Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) intervention; it is not a freely internationally convertible offshore currency. Samoa has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, thereby permitting unrestricted current-account payments for goods, services, and trade-related transfers through authorized dealers. Capital-account transactions by resident domestic entities—including inward portfolio flows, foreign loans, and outward direct investment—are generally subject to CBS exchange-control approval or notification. A non-resident-owned International Company (WS_IC) transacting wholly in foreign-currency offshore accounts is treated as outside the domestic exchange-control perimeter for current-account trade payments and profit remittances. Dividend and profit-repatriation hurdles for WS_IC are therefore limited to bank-level source-of-funds documentation, audited or accountant-certified reserve evidence, and applicable CBS authorized-dealer reporting forms; no prior CBS consent is mandated for remittance of declared dividends where the IC holds valid tax-exemption status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident trading entities, gross export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to Samoa through an authorized dealer within 180 days of the date of shipment (or such extended period as CBS permits in writing), and the proceeds are subject to a mandatory conversion requirement into WST at the prevailing onshore market rate unless an exemption certificate is obtained. International Companies holding a valid Certificate of Registration under the International Companies Act 2012 are exempt from the mandatory-surrender and conversion rules and may retain all export receipts in foreign-currency accounts for trade settlement, treasury, and disbursement purposes. Where any such foreign-currency export proceeds are subsequently converted into WST or introduced into the domestic clearing system, the conversion event is treated as a domestic foreign-exchange transaction and triggers standard CBS reporting, timing, and onboarding verification requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and exercising exchange-control supervisory authority pursuant to the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1985, as amended. The Bank also administers banking-license requirements for all authorized dealers and custodian institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for full in-country clearing and custody. Samoa does not maintain a statutory digital-only EMI or neobank licensing regime; non-bank PSPs, offshore EMIs, and digital treasury platforms cannot hold Samoan-licensed accounts, cannot clear in WST, and may not serve as the exclusive banking partner for a WS_IC. A foreign EMI may be used as a supplementary outward-payment channel for low-value supplier disbursements in foreign currency only where a licensed Samoan authorized dealer remains the primary clearing bank, and where the EMI executes settlements via a correspondent banking network acceptable to the CBS. The EMI route materially increases AML/CFT scrutiny on transaction narratives and reconciliation for trade-finance files.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) from the shareholder's home bank into the WS_IC's dedicated foreign-currency account maintained at one of the licensed Samoan authorized dealers. The recommended remittance narrative in field 70 is 'Share capital contribution' or 'Direct investment equity' with corresponding CBS balance-of-payments classification 5101 (equity) or 5201 (intercompany shareholder loan). The remitting bank must provide the full legal name, registered address, and account number of the corporate shareholder; the Samoan receiving bank will require the shareholder resolution, certificate of incumbency, UBO source-of-wealth file, and confirmation that no CBS approval is required for offshore-to-offshore foreign-currency flows. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (domestic resident companies); effective rate 0% for International Companies under s.198 International Companies Act 1987

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Domestic statutory rate: 15% on dividends paid to non-residents under the Income Tax Act 2012. International Company distributions: 0% - dividends or capital distributions paid by a Samoa IC to non-resident shareholders are exempt from withholding tax under the s.198 IC Act exemption, provided the IC derives no Samoa-source income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No formal Local File or Master File monetary thresholds are prescribed in Samoa; the Income Tax Act 2012 imposes a general arm's length obligation on related-party transactions. For the tax-exempt IC, TP documentation is not tax-driven, but a simplified contemporaneous benchmark memorandum using wholesale matcha distribution comparables should be retained to evidence price setting in any MFR audit or OECD exchange of information request.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2021 applies to ICs carrying on 'relevant activities', which expressly includes distribution and service centre businesses. A Samoa IC trading organic matcha must show, on an annual Economic Substance Return, that (i) core income-generating activities - procurement negotiation, inventory risk assumption, pricing decisions, and logistics co-ordination - are performed in Samoa; (ii) adequate physical premises and full-time employees (or more than one part-time employee) exist in Samoa; and (iii) operating expenditure incurred in Samoa is proportionate to distribution income. Failure to do so is a fatal compliance breach.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive IC Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty up to 150% of evaded tax; mandatory interest; criminal fine up to WST 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Tax Administration Act 2012; the Court may also order revocation of exempt status and winding up of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent who signed false declarations are jointly and severally liable for assessed tax, the 150% penalty, and costs; the Court may issue travel bans preventing departure from Samoa and restitution orders against personal assets; directors face imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to WST 250,000 per year of non-compliance plus daily default penalties under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2021; the Registrar may strike off the IC; details are spontaneously exchanged with the ultimate parent jurisdiction under the OECD framework, triggering foreign tax audits and recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Each director is personally liable for the unpaid ESR penalty; directors face fines up to WST 50,000 and disqualification from acting as a director of any Samoan company for up to 5 years; after strike-off, company assets vest in the State while directors remain liable to creditors for civil restitution.
AML / Money Laundering via Trade Mis-Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WST 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 15 years for money laundering under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (as amended); asset forfeiture covering trade proceeds; the resident agent's licence may be revoked and the IC's bank account frozen by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the mis-invoicing scheme are criminally liable as principals, facing the same 15-year imprisonment term, personal fines, travel bans, passport seizure, and civil restitution orders; the corporate veil is pierced where the IC was a mere instrumentality of the beneficial owner's fraud.
Failure to Maintain Registered Agent / Registered Office

Statutory sanction: Immediate fee doubling and automatic strike-off after 3 months; the struck-off IC's tax exemption is retrospectively removed, exposing all offshore income to 27% corporate tax plus penalties; reinstatement requires a court application and payment of all outstanding fees and taxes plus a reinstatement fee of WST 500.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for all debts and contractual obligations incurred after the company knew it lacked a registered agent; continued trading while struck off constitutes fraudulent trading, rendering directors liable for civil restitution, disqualification, and imprisonment up to 3 years.
