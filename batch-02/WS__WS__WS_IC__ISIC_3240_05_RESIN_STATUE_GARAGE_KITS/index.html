Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue, Government of Samoa (MoR) is the competent tax and customs administration (official portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws). The Samoa Customs Service operates within the MoR and processes all goods declarations through the ASYCUDA World electronic system. The Registrar of International Companies, part of the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), administers International Company incorporation, licensing, and annual franchise fees. The Central Bank of Samoa administers exchange-control and foreign-currency repatriation rules.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC): (1) file the charter/statutory documents with the Registrar of International Companies and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, maintaining a Samoa registered office and registered agent; (2) for the IC's offshore business, no income tax or VAGST registration is required because the entity is tax-exempt; (3) if a Samoan bank account, local contracting, or counterparty due diligence requires a number, the IC files the MoR standard TIN application together with the Certificate of Incorporation, certificate of incumbency, register of directors and shareholders, beneficial-ownership declaration, and a statutory declaration confirming no business with Samoa residents, no Samoan-source income, and no Samoan landholding; (4) the MoR issues a TIN or, upon request, a letter confirming tax-exempt status, typically within 5-10 business days. Any entity carrying on a taxable Samoan activity rather than offshore business must register for income tax and VAGST before commencing that activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. Under the International Companies Act 2012, the IC is a non-resident limited company and is exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, VAGST, stamp duty, estate duty, gift duty, and all other Samoan taxes; it files no Samoan income tax or VAGST returns for offshore business and pays only the fixed annual government franchise fee. Ordinary resident Samoa companies are taxable on worldwide income at the standard corporate rate of 27%, with a legislated multi-year phase-down toward 9%.

Available election forms

Samoa has no US-style check-the-box regime. The IC may: (a) elect by special resolution to convert to a domestic company under the Companies Act 2001, thereby exiting the tax-exempt zone and becoming subject to full income tax and VAGST registration, or a domestic company may convert into an IC if eligible; (b) elect to continue (redomicile) out of Samoa or receive a foreign company by continuation into Samoa; (c) rely on entity-classification elections made in the foreign parent jurisdiction (e.g., U.S. Form 8832), although Samoa's Income Tax Act 2015 general anti-avoidance, CFC, transfer-pricing, and substance-over-form rules may re-characterize hybrid mismatches or conduit arrangements; no express hybrid-mismatch election or safe harbor exists in Samoan law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tālā (WST) is not fully convertible and is managed under a central bank administered exchange control framework. Current account transactions, including trade payments, service fees, and profit remittances, are generally approved on a documentary basis, but capital account flows require prior Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) authorisation via an authorised dealer. For a Samoa International Company (IC) operating under the International Companies Act 2012, all treasury transactions may be settled in foreign currency (USD, EUR, AUD) without WST conversion, provided the IC maintains non-resident status, does not conduct domestic business with Samoan residents, and does not acquire Samoan land. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are free from Samoan withholding tax under the IC tax-exempt regime. However, authorised dealers will typically require an undertaking confirming the beneficiary’s non-resident status, a board resolution, a tax exemption certificate, and a completed Samoa International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) declaration before processing any outbound WST conversion or cross-border profit transfer. Practical remittance hurdles include enhanced source-of-funds diligence on shareholder injections where the underlying wealth originates from jurisdictions outside the FATF-recognised network, and a possible 48-hour hold on transactions exceeding USD 500,000 pending CBS reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under regulation 17 of the Exchange Control Regulations 1971, all goods exported from Samoa must be paid for through an authorised dealer and the full foreign exchange proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa within 180 days from the date of bill of lading or date of shipment, whichever is later. Unless an exemption is obtained from the Central Bank of Samoa, the mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the export value into WST on the date the proceeds are received by the authorised dealer. For an International Company engaging in manufacturing resin statues for export, a blanket exemption may be applied for through the authorised dealer to retain 100% of export receipts in a designated USD or EUR retention account, provided the IC exports exclusively through a bonded manufacturing model, maintains no WST operational liabilities, and submits an annual export schedule and external auditor confirmation to the CBS.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Fa'aputugatupe Tutotonu o Samoa), operating under the Exchange Control Act 1970 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1971

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for foreign digital EMI / cross-border payment fintechs such as Airwallex, Ebury, MultiPass, or Currencycloud, provided they operate through an authorised partner bank in a FATF-equivalent jurisdiction. Legal constraints: Samoa does not have a domestic EMI licensing regime, so offshore EMIs cannot provide WST clearing, Samoan domestic settlement, or direct connectivity to the Central Bank of Samoa. 0% effective statutory rate for the International Company (Tax-Exempt Zone) under the International Companies Act 1987; the general resident corporate headline rate is 27% under the Income Tax Ordinance 2015, which does not apply to the IC

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions made by a Samoa International Company (statutory override in the International Companies Act 1987); Samoa's general non-resident dividend withholding tax is 15% under the Income Tax Ordinance 2015, but it does not apply to an exempt IC

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has no codified Local File/Master File monetary threshold in the Income Tax Ordinance; International Companies are outside the income tax net and are not statutorily required to maintain TP documentation. As best-practice governance for the resin statue business, maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) where connected-party transactions exceed WST 1,000,000 per annum or where the IC invoices related-party sales of polyresin statues

Economic substance scope: Samoa applies economic substance expectations to International Companies under the International Companies Act 1987 and administrative guidance aligned with the EU Code of Conduct Group criteria. The IC must demonstrate: a physical registered office in Samoa, a licensed resident Registered Agent, adequate local books and records, board meetings or written resolutions for decision-making regarding the resin statue distribution business, and core income-generating activities conducted in Samoa relative to the entity's role. A pure 'mailbox' IC without substance risks recharacterisation, loss of the tax exemption, and listing-related sanctions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unauthorised Domestic Trading

Statutory sanction: Retroactive imposition of 27% corporate income tax on all income derived since the prohibited trading began, plus late payment penalties of 10% and a daily surcharge of 0.2%; fines not exceeding WST 10,000 under the International Companies Act; compulsory deregistration of the IC

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax and penalties; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent trading, leading to civil restitution orders, disqualification from acting as a director for up to 10 years, travel bans, and imprisonment not exceeding 5 years
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Blacklisting by the EU and Samoa's financial services regulator; loss of the IC's Tax-Exempt Zone status with retroactive tax assessments; fines up to WST 50,000; freeze of bank accounts connected to the entity

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for debts incurred while the IC was acting in violation of the economic substance expectations; disqualification orders and prohibition from reincorporation in Samoa; asset tracing and civil recovery proceedings
AML/CFT Breach (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to WST 1,000,000 for the corporate entity; confiscation of transaction funds under the Proceeds of Crime Act; revocation of the Registered Agent's license; the entity may be struck off as an unacceptable financial vehicle

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners face criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 14 years, personal fines, forfeiture of personal assets, and international travel bans pending investigation
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment of the taxable base in counterparty jurisdictions with an associated 20% penalty on the additional tax and back-dated interest; the Samoa IC loses its exemption if the arrangement is found to be an abusive shell; global anti-avoidance measures may deny treaty and exemption benefits

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the sham documentation are jointly liable for the penalty tax; cross-border cooperation (ATO, IRD, OECD exchange of information) can expose them to personal recovery actions and criminal indictment for tax fraud
