Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – Samoa International Companies are statutory non-resident offshore entities. They are exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, and all other Samoan taxes on offshore/international business income, including income from the manufacture and export of military tactical vehicles to non-resident defense buyers. The IC is not taxed on world income derived outside Samoa, and no withholding tax applies on distributions to non-resident shareholders. Local-source business in Samoa is prohibited and would forfeit the exemption.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Samoa. Elections available: (1) An IC may elect in its constitution to be a 'company limited by guarantee' or 'company limited by shares' and may alter its authorized capital, with corresponding changes to the annual SIFA fee; (2) An IC may elect to convert into an ordinary company under the Companies Act 2001 or into a foreign company, thereby waiving the offshore tax exemption and becoming taxable under the Income Tax Act 2012; (3) An IC may elect to issue shares in any currency and maintain accounts in a foreign currency, which reinforces its non-resident, tax-exempt character; (4) No hybrid mismatch election is available under Samoan law; Samoa has adopted only limited BEPS-aligned rules that do not presently override the IC exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 – compulsory VAGST registration is triggered when taxable supplies in Samoa exceed WST 100,000 in any 12-month period. International Companies that only make offshore supplies remain outside the VAGST net; registration is voluntary for annual taxable turnover below WST 100,000 but is required to claim input credits on local procurement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible movable goods, including all-terrain armored military tactical vehicles classified under ISIC 3040, are zero-rated under VAGST Act 1992 section 14 when the goods are exported by the supplier or for the supplier's account within 28 days of supply and are not subsequently re-imported. Zero-rating also extends to freight, insurance, and handling services directly related to the exported vehicles, as well as services physically performed on movable goods for export (e.g., military fit-out and armor installation) when performed for a non-resident recipient. Defense end-use requires an export authorization under Samoa's Exchange Control Act 1975 and applicable arms export permits; failure to produce the end-user certificate and destination certificate invalidates the zero-rated claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EDN) filed via ASYCUDA World by the IC or its licensed customs broker
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of the vehicles to the foreign defense buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 issued by an authorized dealer confirming receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Tax invoice meeting VAGST Act 1992 section 32 requirements with an explicit zero-rated supply notation
  • End-User Certificate and export permit for military tactical hardware issued by the relevant Samoan authority

Samoa has no mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API filing, or digital stamping regime. Registered persons must issue a standard tax invoice under VAGST Act 1992 section 32 containing the supplier name and address, VAGST registration number, invoice number, date of supply, description, quantity, price, and VAGST amount. Defense export invoices are contractual documents supporting customs clearance via ASYCUDA World; no digital stamp is affixed by the tax authority.

The Ministry for Revenue and the Central Bank of Samoa may retrospectively audit export zero-rating claims where the goods are not exported within 28 days, the proceeds are not received within 180 days, or documentation is deficient or fraudulent. Clawback is measured at the standard 15% VAGST rate on the full supply value, plus interest. Penalties under the VAGST Act 1992 and the Tax Administration Act 2012 include shortfall penalties up to 150% of the tax evaded for deliberate concealment, civil penalties for each false statement, and criminal fines and imprisonment for officers in the case of fraudulent zero-rating schemes. The Customs Service may also suspend the IC's export license and require security or guarantees for future shipments. For International Companies, abuse of the offshore exemption through fictitious export transactions additionally triggers revocation of the IC status by SIFA, rendering the entity taxable on all income from inception.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is not freely convertible offshore. Current account payments are generally permitted through authorized dealers, but capital account transactions require CBS prior approval. Profit remittance from a non-resident International Company (IC) is unrestricted if funded by offshore foreign currency source and not involving resident counterparties; however, any conversion to WST for local settlement triggers CBS documentation requirements. ICs operating purely in foreign currency with no local revenue face low remittance friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must surrender foreign exchange export proceeds to an authorized dealer within six months from shipment and convert to WST unless an exemption is granted by CBS. Non-resident International Companies are automatically exempt from surrender and conversion obligations when export receivables are denominated in foreign currency and are not derived from resident business or local currency resources. The IC should maintain a CBS approval letter or SIFA-issued exemption certificate to evidence compliance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), operating under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and the Exchange Control Act 1973; delegated authority for exchange control administration and enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low. Samoa does not operate a digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licensing regime. Offshore fintech firms providing cross-border payment services are not authorized to clear through Samoa’s real-time gross settlement or local interbank rails. Such EMIs may be used incidentally for ancillary payments via overseas correspondent banks, but cannot serve as a primary liquidity or clearing conduit for the IC.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% for ordinary resident companies (Income Tax Act 2015); 0% for International Companies (ICs) registered under the International Companies Act 1987 (ICA 1987), which are tax-exempt on all qualifying offshore income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Samoa's statutory dividend withholding tax is 20% for dividends paid by ordinary resident companies to non-residents (Income Tax Act 2015). An International Company is fully exempt from this tax: distributions by the IC to its non-resident shareholders carry 0% Samoa dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No explicit WST monetary threshold is gazetted in Samoa's Income Tax Act 2015. In administrative practice, the Ministry of Revenue expects contemporaneous Master File and Local File documentation where cross-border related-party turnover exceeds approximately WST 1,000,000 per annum. A tax-exempt International Company is excluded from the Samoan corporate income tax net and therefore owes no Samoan TP documentation; if the IC's exemption is revoked, TP documentation becomes mandatory from the first year of taxable status.

Economic substance scope: Samoa applies economic substance rules under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2020, enacted following Samoa's EU listing review. An IC carrying on a 'relevant activity' — banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centers, holding-company functions, or intellectual-property holding/development — must meet Samoa substance tests, including physical presence, locally based decision-making, and adequate employees and expenditure. Manufacture of armored security personnel transport vehicles (ISIC 3040) is not a listed relevant activity; the IC is therefore outside the enhanced substance regime as long as it confines itself to offshore manufacturing, export logistics, and government/diplomatic procurement. The IC must still keep board minutes, registries, and records at the Samoa registered office and must respond to the annual economic substance notification where directed by SIFA. If the IC owns product-related IP and derives royalty income from that IP within an intra-group arrangement, the high-value IP core-income tests can apply and would require demonstrable strategic functions, personnel, and decision-making in Samoa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abusive Use of the International Company Exemption

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the Tax Exemption Certificate; back-assessment at 25% corporate income tax on undeclared Samoan-source income for up to 8 years; late payment penalties and daily surcharges; strike-off and cancellation of IC registration; denial of treaty benefits and mandatory exchange of information with foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for unpaid tax where the corporate veil is pierced; fines up to SAT 100,000; imprisonment up to 5 years for false statutory declarations; travel/exit restriction orders may be issued while tax liabilities remain outstanding; disqualification from acting as a director of any Samoa company.
AML/CFT Violations on Defense-Tactical Hardware Flows

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's AML/CFT regime, corporate fines up to SAT 500,000 and employee fines up to SAT 200,000 per violation; freezing and forfeiture of assets; cancellation of the special defense-hardware license; referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit and foreign enforcement counterparts.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved face imprisonment up to 14 years, personal financial penalties, travel bans to prevent flight, and confiscation orders over personal assets; professional debarment of the registered agent may follow a breach of reporting obligations.
Unlicensed Defense Manufacturing and Export Control Breach

Statutory sanction: Confiscation and forfeiture of the goods; fines up to SAT 1,000,000; suspension and permanent blacklisting of the IC from Samoan registry benefits; cancellation of the special license; possible referral for criminal prosecution under trade-control legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal conviction and imprisonment for unlawful export of defense hardware; civil restitution to affected parties; personal disqualification orders; departure prohibition and asset seizure to enforce judgments.
Regulatory Default: Non-Filing and False Substance Representation

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike off and dissolve the IC; late penalty fees accrue daily; the IC loses the protection of limited liability for obligations incurred after dissolution; restoration requires payment of arrears, penalties, and a restoration fee of up to SAT 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who consented to trading while the company is dissolved may be held personally liable for company debts; sanctions include director disqualification, fines for continued non-compliance, and potential criminal liability for knowingly making false compliance declarations.
