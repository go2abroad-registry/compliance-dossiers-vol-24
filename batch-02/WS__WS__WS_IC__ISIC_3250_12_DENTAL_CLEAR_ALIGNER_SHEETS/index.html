Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Apia, Samoa; official taxpayer portal: SRA Online Services (https://www.sra.gov.ws); entity registry: Samoa International Companies Registry under the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC): (1) Appoint a licensed registered agent in Samoa and incorporate the IC under the International Companies Act 1987 via the MCIL Registry; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Registration of International Company. (2) Submit to the Samoa Revenue Authority the SRA Taxpayer Registration Application (Form T1) attaching the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, register of directors and shareholders, registered office certificate, and passport copies of directors. (3) File a non-resident business declaration confirming the IC will not carry on business in Samoa with Samoan residents and will not derive gross income from sources within Samoa, thereby preserving the statutory tax-exempt regime. (4) The SRA issues a single Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) in electronic form within 5-10 working days; the IC is recorded as 'Non-Resident Tax-Exempt International Company' and no VAGST registration number is issued. (5) If the IC later makes taxable supplies in Samoa or to Samoan residents, the SRA must be notified within 14 days and mandatory VAGST registration applies once annual turnover reaches WST 60,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box pass-through election and no OECD hybrid mismatch election under the Income Tax Ordinance 2015. The only statutory election: an International Company may voluntarily file an SRA notice to surrender its tax-exempt status and be treated as a resident company for income tax and VAGST purposes; the election is irrevocable for at least five Samoan tax years and triggers full domestic tax obligations. An IC may also elect to exclude resident ownership or to issue bearer shares per its charter without changing the tax-exempt default. No partnership pass-through classification exists for ICs; entity-level taxation arises only upon a valid resident-election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 (annual taxable turnover of supplies made in Samoa in any 12-month period; a person making only zero-rated exports of goods is nonetheless eligible for voluntary registration; tax-exempt International Companies are outside the charge unless resident-election is made)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods (thermoformed multilayer dental clear-aligner sheets and finished aligners): (i) the IC supplies the goods in the course of its export-oriented business to a buyer who is outside Samoa and not a Samoan resident; (ii) the goods are physically removed from Samoa by the IC or its agent within 28 days after the day of supply, or within any longer period approved by the SRA Commissioner; (iii) the IC retains the complete statutory export evidence bundle; (iv) the goods are not re-imported into Samoa or diverted for local consumption without SRA consent. Offshore services (e.g., aligner design data services, IP licensing to a non-resident distributor): zero-rating applies where the recipient is a non-resident outside Samoa at the time of supply, the services are performed outside Samoa and not for a Samoan permanent establishment, and the benefit is enjoyed wholly outside Samoa; services relating directly to Samoan land or physical personal property in Samoa are ineligible.

  • SRA Customs Export Declaration (electronic cargo declaration) bearing the approved export entry number and exit stamp of the Samoan Customs post
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the dental-aligner goods from Samoa within the statutory 28-day window
  • Commercial invoice and underlying purchase order from the overseas buyer, with full buyer identity and offshore delivery address
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed Samoan bank, evidencing credit of export proceeds in foreign currency from the offshore buyer
  • Certificate of Origin (Form A or non-preferential certificate) where preferential tariff treatment or buyer customs clearance requires it
  • VAGST export tax invoice annotated 'Zero-Rated Export — VAGST Act 2007, s. 12(1)'

Samoa currently imposes no national mandatory real-time e-invoicing, no fiscal data clearance to government APIs, and no digital stamping requirement. The SRA requires every registered taxable person to issue a VAGST tax invoice within 28 days of supply, showing the seller's TIN, VAGST registration number, supply date, description, quantity, tax-exclusive value, and the 15% VAGST amount. Zero-rated export invoices must be clearly annotated 'Zero-Rated Export — VAGST Act 2007, s. 12(1)' and include the overseas buyer's details. Compliance is monitored through periodic SRA desk audits, customs export cross-matching, and VAGST return reconciliation rather than real-time transactional clearance.

The SRA and Samoan Customs conduct targeted audits of export zero-rating claims, cross-matching export declarations, bills of lading, IRC bank records, and VAGST returns. Where zero-rating is claimed without statutory substantiation: the Commissioner disallows the claim, reassesses the supply at the standard 15% VAGST, and issues an assessment for the tax shortfall plus a late-declaration penalty of 25% of the shortfall and deficit interest at 2% per month compounded from the original due date; the Commissioner may reassess within 7 years from the date of the fraudulent or wilful-default claim. Criminal sanctions under the VAGST Act 2007 and Customs Act 2014: corporate fines up to WST 50,000; directors and officers individually liable to fines up to WST 20,000 and/or imprisonment for a term up to 2 years. Fraudulent export declarations also constitute exchange-control offences under the Exchange Control Act 1972 (failure to repatriate export proceeds within the prescribed period attracts monetary penalties up to the value of the export proceeds) and money-laundering predicate offences under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act 2018, exposing the IC and its beneficial owners to asset forfeiture and debarment from Samoan licensed banking channels.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a controlled but trade-convertible currency. The CBS administers the Exchange Control Regulations 1976/1983 and enforces a bifurcated regime: current account transactions are generally freely convertible with documentary verification, while capital account transactions require express CBS approval. For a Samoa International Company (IC), profit remittances in the form of dividends, reinvested earnings, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds are conceded as current account transfers once the IC is fully tax-exempt and possesses a CBS-approved tax exemption certificate. In practice, remittances above WST 200,000 equivalent are subjected to a CBS exchange control form with underlying audited financial statements, board resolutions, and evidence of regard for the non-resident beneficial owners. The WST is not an offshore deliverable currency; offshore clearing is limited to NASDAQ-traded WST crosses through tier-1 correspondent banks, with final settlement onshore in WST or an approved foreign currency account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The CBS Exchange Control Policy requires all export proceeds from physical movement of dental clear aligner sheets out of Samoa to be credited to a licensed Samoan bank account within 30 calendar days of payment receipt, or 90 calendar days of shipment if financing is under an open account facility. The mandatory surrender and conversion ratio is 100% of export receipts into WST unless the exporter holds a general or special CBS foreign currency retention exemption. For a Samoa IC with operational foreign currency cash cycles, CBS may grant a standing exemption allowing up to 100% retention in USD/EUR for up to 180 days, with quarterly reconciliation and proof of ongoing intercompany trade.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate. Samoa's financial sector perimeter requires any entity holding client funds or executing remittances in Samoa to be licensed or expressly recognized by the CBS. Offshore digital EMIs like Revolut Business or Wise are not standalone licensed in Samoa and may not act as the primary clearing bank for a Samoa IC. They can serve as auxiliary disbursement rails for supplier payments only if the IC maintains a local account at a licensed commercial bank and obtains CBS approval for each outbound transfer exceeding WST 200,000 equivalent. Because dental-aligner sheet production involves raw polymer imports and premium orthodontic returns, reliance on unregulated EMIs is not advisable as the substantial treasury backbone.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% for resident companies under the Samoa Income Tax Act 2015; 0% effective rate for a qualifying International Company with no Samoan-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends and distributions paid by a Samoa International Company to non-resident shareholders because the IC is tax-exempt and distributions are outside the Samoa income tax system. By contrast, a pure Samoa domestic company paying a dividend to a non-resident would be subject to the domestic dividend withholding tax rate of 15% unless a treaty applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold is codified in the Samoa Income Tax Act; however, the Samoa Revenue Authority may demand contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for any cross-border related-party transaction regardless of amount. A prudent Samoa International Company transacting in dental clear aligner sheets or IP royalties with connected parties should prepare OECD-compliant Local File and Master File documentation when annual related-party turnover exceeds WST 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Samoa Economic Substance Regulations apply to International Companies carrying on relevant activities, including distribution and service centres, IP holding, financing, leasing, headquarters, shipping, investment funds, banking and insurance. A dental clear aligner IC that licenses intellectual property to contract manufacturers or resells finished aligner sheets through related parties must demonstrate Samoan direction and management, adequate local employees and physical premises, and performance of core income-generating activities in Samoa. An IC limited to passive equity holding of a dental-aligner subsidiary benefits from a reduced substance requirement but must still file the annual economic substance return. Failure to satisfy the tests can trigger monetary penalties, exchange of information to foreign tax authorities, and deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham International Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the income as Samoan-source and taxable at the 25% corporate rate; imposition of unpaid tax, late payment interest, and an evasion penalty of up to 50% of the tax underpaid; prosecution under the Income Tax Act 2015 with criminal fines registrable against the corporate estate and possible deregistration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly authorised false filings can be personally assessed where the corporate veil is pierced; civil restitution of evaded tax, travel bans pending recovery, disqualification from serving as a director, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Mandatory economic substance filing penalties, a direction to amend the IC's business structure, exchange of information with foreign tax authorities, strike-off and deregistration of the IC, and revocation of the IC tax exemption with retroactive assessment.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors jointly liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; directors also face personal financial penalties, travel restrictions, disqualification orders, and civil claims by creditors and revenue authorities.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The Samoa Revenue Authority may adjust taxable income to the arm's length value, disallow excess deductions, impose a transfer pricing adjustment penalty of 25% of the underpaid tax, and charge interest from the original due date; continued abuse can trigger professional conduct investigations of the Samoan tax agent.

Director personal liability: Where transfer pricing manipulation is knowingly directed by board members, those directors are personally liable for the corporate tax shortfall, loss of the benefit of limited liability, and may be subject to criminal fines, a travel ban, and imprisonment for tax-related fraud.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Civil and criminal penalties under the Samoa Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2007, including fines up to WST 200,000 for the IC and suspension of the licensed Samoan registered agent; the IC may be deregistered and reported to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and trustees can be made personally liable for money laundering offences, with asset forfeiture, court-ordered travel bans, surrender of passports, and imprisonment up to 14 years; the corporate veil is set aside where a company is used as an instrument of concealment.
