Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. The Samoa IC is an entity-level offshore exempt company: under ICA 1987 s.192, no Samoan income tax (standard corporate rate 27%), no capital gains tax, no stamp duty, no transfer duty, and no withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties or distributions applies to the IC's income, profits, gains, distributions, or to dealings in its shares, debentures or marketable securities, provided the IC does not carry on business in Samoa and does not transact with Samoan residents. Distributions to shareholders are equally exempt. The exemption is permanent and entity-level, not a pass-through election. The IC cannot access Samoan foreign tax credits or bilateral treaty relief unless the Minister of Finance issues a discretionary declaration under ICA 1987 s.192(3), the anti-treaty-shopping rule.

Available election forms

Samoa does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime and has no hybrid-mismatch or CFC elections for International Companies. Available statutory mechanisms: (1) ICA 1987 s.192(3) Ministerial declaration — the Minister of Finance may, by notice, declare an IC to be resident for the purposes of a double tax agreement, thereby exposing the IC to Samoan income tax only on Samoan-source income; the IC cannot self-elect into or out of this status. (2) Voluntary VAGST registration under the VAGST Act 2013 s.8(3), available with MfR approval only to persons making taxable supplies in Samoa; a wholly offshore IC has no taxable supplies and is not registrable. (3) Remission of penalties by the Commissioner for reasonable-cause non-compliance under the applicable Tax Administration legislation. There is no election to convert an IC into an ordinary taxable Samoan company, no group consolidation election, and no hybrid-finance or hybrid-entity election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is freely convertible on current-account transactions under Samoa's IMF Article VIII status (accepted 1999); domestic capital-account transfers require Central Bank of Samoa approval through authorised banks. Samoa International Companies are statutorily excluded from the Exchange Control Act 1976, so the IC may freely hold, convert, and remit USD, CNH, and WST without CBS approval or profit-remittance licensing. Practical remittance hurdles are non-statutory: no Stripe or Shopify Payments payout market in Samoa, correspondent-bank de-risking of the WST corridor, and a narrow cleared-bank network (principally BSP and Bank of Samoa). No Samoan withholding tax, dividend tax, or capital-gains tax applies to a non-resident shareholder of an IC on repatriated profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident trading companies, CBS exchange-control policy requires export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days of shipment and, where converted, surrendered through the licensed banking system at the prevailing interbank rate. International Companies are outside the statutory exchange-control regime, so no mandatory surrender ratio or repatriation deadline applies to the IC's keyboard-keycap export receipts; USD and CNH balances may be held offshore without obligation to convert to WST. If the IC clears earnings through a domestic WST account, the CBS normal-dealing rule and bank customer-dealing rates apply to any conversion.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), licensed banking supervisor and administrator of the Exchange Control Act 1976 and Exchange Control Regulations 1977; the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) separately supervises international companies, licensed registered agents, and international-company AML/CFT compliance under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low direct viability. Mainstream offshore EMIs (Airwallex, Dukascopy, Mercury, Currencycloud) routinely decline Samoa-incorporated entities because of Samoa's offshore-centre profile, its historic FATF increased-monitoring listing (removed 2021), and current EU Code of Conduct Group Annex II observation. Legal constraints: a Samoa IC cannot hold an EMI licence and cannot open an e-money account without a bank reference, a SIFA-licensed registered agent's attestation, and a full UBO documentation pack; the EMI must satisfy the e-money and AML/CFT rules of its home jurisdiction. Viable alternative: retain the Samoa IC as trading and IP principal while routing receivables settlement through a supported-jurisdiction intermediary, or use the BSP treasury network as a directly sponsored channel.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (standard resident company rate, Income Tax Act 2012); International Companies (ICs) carrying on exclusively offshore business are statutorily exempt from all Samoan income tax, yielding a 0% effective rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (statutory rate on Samoan-source dividends paid to non-residents, Income Tax Act 2012); 0% for dividends and capital distributions paid by an International Company by operation of the s.184 exemption.
