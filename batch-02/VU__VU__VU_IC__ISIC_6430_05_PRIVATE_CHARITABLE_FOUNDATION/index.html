Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), Companies Registration Division, Rue Bougainville, Port Vila, Vanuatu (www.vfsc.vu)

Governing statute

International Companies Act [Cap 222] of Vanuatu, ss. 6-17 (Registration and Incorporation), ss. 24-27 (Memorandum and Articles), s. 34 (Registered Office and Registered Agent); the objects clause is additionally constrained by the endowment and charitable purpose rules applicable to private charitable foundations under Vanuatu law.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form ICA-1 (Application for Registration as an International Company), submitted with the executed and notarized Memorandum and Articles of Association, Consents of Registered Agent, Director and Secretary; the Certificate of Incorporation is issued under the seal of the VFSC Registrar.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an International Company under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]. The memorandum need only state an authorized share capital; a nominal subscription of one share at par (USD 1.00) is sufficient. No escrow deposit, capital contribution or paid-up capital verification is required for a non-trading charitable endowment vehicle.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance from the Vanuatu Fire and Emergency Services for the Port Vila registered office (smoke alarms, emergency exits, portable extinguishers); occupancy permit confirming the leased floor is approved for office use under the Vanuatu Building Code; structural load inspection certificate issued by a Vanuatu-licensed engineer where the office is located above ground level; workplace OHS registration and first-aid compliance with the Department of Labour under the Employment Act and applicable occupational health and safety regulations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official taxpayer portal: https://vrc.gov.vu; customs portal: https://customs.gov.vu; electronic services via the 'My VRC' online filing platform for VBN, VAT registration, and VAT returns.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate an International Company (IC) with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] (expedited same-day incorporation; zero minimum paid-up capital; local registered agent required). Step 2: Apply to VRC for a mandatory Vanuatu Business Number (VBN) for any entity with a Vanuatu nexus (free of charge; online form plus passport, registered agent letter, and certified constitutional documents). Step 3: Assess VAT registration obligation: an IC with annual taxable turnover in Vanuatu exceeding VT 5,000,000 must submit a VAT registration application within 21 days after the end of the month in which the threshold is exceeded; VRC issues a distinct VAT registration number. Step 4: A pure offshore charitable endowment IC that makes no taxable supplies in Vanuatu is not VAT-registrable, but must still hold a VBN and file the annual VFSC compliance declaration. Step 5: Foreign entities appoint a local registered agent for all statutory filings; non-resident directors and shareholders are permitted without a local tax presence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - An International Company is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes, including corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, estate or gift duty, and stamp duty (International Companies Act [Cap 222] s.7). Vanuatu imposes no general income tax regime on any resident or non-resident entity. The sole material indirect exposure is Value Added Tax at 12.5% on taxable supplies made within and in connection to Vanuatu; offshore endowment and grant management services supplied to non-resident founders fall outside this net.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or tax classification election exists because Vanuatu levies no income tax. Applicable statutory elections: (i) voluntary VAT registration under s.8(3) of the VAT Act for an IC making below-threshold but taxable supplies in Vanuatu, subject to a minimum 24-month registration commitment; (ii) election under the International Companies Act to convert an IC into a domestic company under the Companies Act [Cap 191], which terminates IC tax-exempt status; (iii) election to register as a Foundation under the Foundations Act 2016 to operate expressly as a private charitable endowment vehicle; (iv) no hybrid mismatch or CFC elections are recognized in Vanuatu law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 5,000,000 (Vanuatu Vatu) annual taxable turnover in any 12-month period; registration mandatory from the first day of the month following the threshold breach; voluntary registration available for lower-turnover entities making taxable supplies; no registration required for purely offshore/non-Vanuatu supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s.11 of the VAT Act to: (a) exported goods physically delivered outside Vanuatu within 28 days of supply (or such longer period as approved by VRC); (b) exported services supplied to a non-resident customer where the services are wholly performed outside Vanuatu or the economic benefit is enjoyed entirely outside Vanuatu; (c) international transportation of goods or passengers; and (d) supplies to gazetted international organizations. For an International Company engaged in private charitable endowment management, the 0% rate applies to offshore advisory, custodial, and grant-distribution services rendered to non-resident founders, funders, and philanthropic beneficiaries, provided the service contract provides for performance outside Vanuatu and all consideration is received in foreign currency from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ECS Single Administrative Document) certified by Vanuatu Customs Service
  • Commercial invoice / VAT tax invoice expressly identifying the supply as zero-rated export
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading (marine) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical removal from Vanuatu (goods only)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign exchange from the non-resident customer
  • Executed contract / grant agreement with the non-resident grantor and, for offshore services, a written declaration of performance location outside Vanuatu

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory e-invoicing API, and no digital stamping regime exists in Vanuatu. A VAT-registered supplier must issue a statutory tax invoice (paper or electronic) containing prescribed particulars: supplier name, VBN and VAT registration number, customer name, date of supply, description, quantity, VAT-exclusive price, VAT amount, and total; the invoice must be issued within the period prescribed by the VAT Act (not exceeding 28 days after supply). Electronic invoices are permissible, but there is no Government pre-clearance or interoperability mandate; invoices must be retained for at least five years for VRC audit.

VRC may audit and reassess export zero-rating claims within five years of the relevant VAT return date. Failure to substantiate the zero-rating results in a deemed taxable supply at 12.5% with additional tax of up to 200% of the shortfall, statutory interest, and retrospective clawback of any refunded input VAT. Fraudulent zero-rating, false invoicing, or refund abuse constitutes a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to VT 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years; directors, officers, and foundation trustees are jointly and severally liable. The assessment limitation period is extended in cases of fraud or evasion. Vanuatu imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark should be written into grant and endowment agreements to satisfy audit expectations and international anti-money-laundering standards.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains a liberalised current account and capital account with no general exchange control restrictions. The Vatu (VUV) is convertible for current account transactions (trade, interest, dividends, grants, and charitable disbursements) and for most capital transactions, subject to anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing obligations. Profit or endowment remittance hurdles are minimal; the principal statutory requirements are compliance with the International Companies Act, the Charitable Associations (Incorporation) Act, and submission of accurate underlying documentation to resident banks. Beneficial ownership disclosure and source-of-funds verification are the primary practical hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds under Vanuatu law. Residents and non-resident entities may retain export receipts in foreign currency accounts, and the VUV is freely movable. Nonetheless, for anti-money laundering surveillance, banks will require a declared purpose code and retention of commercial invoices or grant agreements for any repatriated funds.

Central bank supervision: The Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) is the ultimate monetary and financial stability authority; however, day-to-day oversight of bank licensing and foreign exchange activities is also administered through the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) for non-deposit-taking financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium but highly constrained. Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) service providers may offer virtual IBANs or e-wallets to a Vanuatu International Company; however, they are not regulated by the RBV and face an uncertain legal status for handling charitable grant funds. VFSC-licensed money transmitters may be used for non-core ancillary transactions, but the primary treasury infrastructure should rely on VFSC-registered banks or fully licensed foreign banks with physical Vanuatu branches. Legal constraints include WMD and proliferation financing screening, FATF mutual evaluation sensitivities, and the practical refusal of many global EMIs to service Vanuatu tax-exempt international companies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The standard inbound lane for capital injection or donor advances is via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire equivalent) denominated in USD, AUD, NZD, EUR, or GBP, using beneficiary account details at ANZ, BRED, NBV, or BSP. For Vanuatu International Company endowment assets, the recommended purpose code is 'G3R – Transfers of a social nature' for true donations and grants, or 'D2R – Capital transfers' where the injection is structured as an endowment capital contribution. The bank will require a Board Resolution certifying the source of funds, a copy of the donor agreement or deed, and identification of every remitting entity. No VFSC prior approval is required for inbound funds, but the bank reserves the right to quarantine funds pending VFI screening.Outbound grant disbursements and programme-related payments are cleared through standard correspondent banking rails: SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments, and Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for any procurement of goods or services in furtherance of the charitable programme. For high-value grant distributions, banks may settle through the RBV's national payment system for VUV-denominated domestic payments; for cross-border grants, the beneficiary bank must be fully identified and the recipient's charitable or NGO status documented. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% - Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax; an International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from all national taxes, including income tax, capital gains tax, dividend tax and stamp duty.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an International Company to non-resident shareholders; no deduction at source is permitted or required under Vanuatu law.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six (6) months after each anniversary of the date of incorporation of the International Company.

Deadline: · Frequency:

ESR-1 must be filed within twelve (12) months after the end of the accounting period; the initial ESR notification must be filed within six (6) months of incorporation or of commencing a relevant activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None statutorily prescribed - Vanuatu has no income tax statute and no formal transfer-pricing regime. As prudent ESR-aligned practice, intercompany financing, endowment-management fees, or royalty flows with related parties exceeding VUV 50,000,000 (approximately USD 420,000) per annum should be documented on an arm's-length basis. The ESR applies to all relevant activities without a monetary de minimis threshold.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (No. 140 of 2021) apply to all Vanuatu International Companies conducting a 'relevant activity', including fund management and holding business. A private charitable foundation managing only its own endowment is treated as a Holding Business and satisfies substance through: a registered office in Vanuatu, a resident director or licensed agent, local premises and staff or contracted agent services, board meetings held in Vanuatu, and adequate records of grant-distribution decisions. If the foundation administers third-party endowment funds for a fee, it falls within Fund Management and must meet the additional CIGA test (core income-generating activities performed in Vanuatu) and must not hold itself out as a passive tax-exempt vehicle.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False ESR Declaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to VUV 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment for false ESR statements; daily default penalties; VFSC strike-off and revocation of the tax-exempt certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for restitution of misapplied foundation funds, disgorgement of fees obtained through false declarations, travel bans pending investigation, and custodial sentences for fraudulent misrepresentation.
Shell Company / Defective Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR non-compliance determination with penalties up to VUV 10,000,000 and continuing daily fines; strike-off and dissolution of the IC; loss of good standing for treaty attestation purposes.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for restoration costs and third-party debts under a veil-piercing theory; director disqualification; adverse cross-border enforcement where the structure is used to evade foreign tax.
AML/CFT Breach in Charitable Grant Flows

Statutory sanction: Enforcement under the Vanuatu AML/CFT Act and VFSC AML Rules: monetary penalties against the foundation, forfeiture of tainted funds, suspension or revocation of VFSC registration or licence.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability for willful blindness, imprisonment for serious offences, personal asset restraint orders, travel restrictions, and disqualification from serving as directors of any regulated financial entity.
Unauthorized Fund Management / Unlicensed Financial Dealing

Statutory sanction: Penalties for unlicensed financial dealing, compulsory winding-up of the foundation, claw-back of all fees received with interest, and formal VFSC enforcement notices.

Director personal liability: Personal liability for repayment to defrauded grantors or investors, disgorgement of profits, criminal prosecution for carrying on licensable financial activity without a licence, and director disqualification orders.
Beneficial Ownership Concealment and Nominee Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines under the International Companies Act and VFSC beneficial-ownership rules, with daily default penalties; strike-off and deregistration; denial of the tax-exempt certificate; statutory reporting to foreign tax authorities under exchange-of-information arrangements.

Director personal liability: Deemed joint and several liability for all foundation debts where the register is false; fines and imprisonment for false statutory declarations; travel bans and seizure of assets upon contravention.
