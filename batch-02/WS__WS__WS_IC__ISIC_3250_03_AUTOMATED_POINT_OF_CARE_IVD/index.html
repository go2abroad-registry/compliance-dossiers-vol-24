Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA); official portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws/; electronic filing via the SRA Taxpayer Access Portal (TAP)

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is automatically treated as a non-resident tax-exempt entity and does not require an income tax TIN for offshore operations. If the IC nevertheless becomes a VAGST registrant by reason of making taxable supplies in Samoa, it must file the SRA taxpayer registration application and the VAGST registration form (Form VAGST 001) to obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAGST registration number. For purely offshore IVD manufacturing and distribution activities, no Samoan tax registration is required; any application is only voluntary or triggered by a Samoa domestic VAT nexus, customs licences, or a tax exemption certificate application.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAGST) applies to goods that are exported by the supplier from Samoa to a non-resident consignee outside Samoa and to services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time the services are performed, provided the services are not used in Samoa. Manufactured microfluidic cartridges, immunoassay analyzers, and point-of-care IVD systems qualify for zero-rating when title transfers and the goods are dispatched from Samoa under a valid export customs declaration. The exporter must retain documentary proof of physical export and payment from the overseas buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs electronic export entry/CUSDEC)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Samoa
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Purchase Order or Supply Contract naming the non-resident buyer
  • Packing List and certificate of origin where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation of foreign currency settlement

No mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API interoperability, or digital stamping regime is currently imposed under Samoa VAGST law. Registered suppliers must issue prescribed VAGST tax invoices bearing the supplier’s name, TIN, invoice date, description, VAGST amount, and total consideration. Returns and VAGST payments must be filed electronically through the SRA Taxpayer Access Portal (TAP); paper or PDF record retention remains acceptable.

Although Samoa has no explicit statutory FX repatriation deadline, SRA audit practice treats 180 days as the benchmark for export settlement. Exporters must preserve export documentation for 7 years. False or inadequate export substantiation will cause zero-rating to be reversed; the exporter must immediately repay all VAGST input credits previously claimed on the export supply, plus interest and late-payment penalties. Knowingly providing false or misleading export evidence constitutes an offence under the VAGST Act and Tax Administration Act, punishable by fines up to WST 50,000, imprisonment, and the SRA’s power to revoke registrations and tax exemption certificates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

WST is not freely convertible offshore; CBS operates a managed float with no liquid offshore WST market. Current-account payments, including trade goods and services connected to the IVD manufacturing export chain, are convertible through CBS-licensed authorized dealers upon presentation of underlying contracts, invoices, and customs evidence. Capital-account transactions, including equity injection, intercompany debt, and profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders of the International Company (IC), require prior CBS approval or a standing facility issued in favour of the IC; approval is routinely granted where operations remain offshore with no Samoan resident counterparty, the IC holds a valid tax-exemption certificate from the Samoa Ministry of Revenue, and the capital is sourced from a non-sanctioned overseas parent. Remittance friction is therefore documentary rather than substantive: profit repatriation requires audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate, and the bank's confirmation that the funding has been cleared through the CBS green-corridor mechanism.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory baseline under Exchange Control Regulations 1977: all export proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa within 6 months of shipment date and the authorized dealer must surrender 100% of receipts, being the mandatory conversion ratio, into WST at the prevailing official mid-rate within 5 banking days of receipt, unless a specific retention exemption is granted. For an offshore IC, CBS issues a standing exemption permitting 100% retention of export proceeds in USD or EUR inside the designated Foreign Currency Account, effectively eliminating forced WST conversion; the mandatory conversion ratio for qualifying ICs is therefore 0%. The authorized dealer files monthly CBS returns itemizing each export credit with the exemption reference; any subsequent shareholder dividend or intercompany remittance drawn from such proceeds remains subject to CBS capital-account notification thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Falepuna Apia; statutory mandate under the Exchange Control Act 1976, Exchange Control Regulations 1977, and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism Act 2018

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low and not legally viable as the settlement hub. Samoa has no Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing or passporting regime, and the Exchange Control Act 1976 requires all inward and outward cross-border payments by ICs to be routed through CBS-licensed authorized dealers. Unlicensed offshore digital EMIs, such as Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, and similar providers, are therefore unable to hold lawful custody of the IC's treasury; funds moved through an EMI into a Samoa bank account would be flagged as unlicensed money transmission, inviting CBS inquiries, delayed clearance, or forced repatriation. Recommended architecture: the Samoa authorized dealer remains the sole legal clearing and custody layer, while EMIs may be engaged only as non-custodial invoicing, reconciliation, or API orchestration tools outside the settlement path, with no account balance held offshore.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity injections and shareholder loans must arrive via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's banking institution to the IC's designated Foreign Currency Account at the Samoa authorized dealer. The wire must carry the CBS capital-account purpose code (Category C.1, Increase of Paid-in Capital or Foreign Investment) and be accompanied by the completed CBS Exchange Control Application (Form EC-9), board resolution, share-allotment minutes or loan agreement, tax-exemption certificate from the Samoa Ministry of Revenue, and the bank's sanctions release. No WST conversion is required; funds remain in USD. For recurring injections, the IC should obtain a standing CBS green-corridor facility, under which the authorized dealer auto-notifies CBS within 48 hours of each credit and releases funds to the FCA without case-by-case approval.Settlement of trade obligations, including microfluidic polymer resins, antibody reagents, optical modules, and analyzer components, is executed across two primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers from the IC's USD FCA to supplier accounts, accompanied by commercial invoice, packing list, and CBS threshold notification; and (ii) SWIFT MT700 commercial letters of credit issued through the Samoa dealer's international division, under UCP600 terms at sight or 60 to 120 days after shipment, for high-value procurement, with MT798 amendment messages for documentary discrepancies. Correspondent clearing uses Bank of Samoa's USD Nostro at a tier-1 United States correspondent and ANZ's global network; a dual-corridor policy requiring two independent correspondent channels is recommended to avoid concentration and clearing-failure risk. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for a resident company engaged in onshore Samoa-source business; 0% for a qualifying Samoa International Company under the International Companies Act 1987, s.133, provided the company does not derive Samoa-source income and complies with the non-resident status conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by a qualifying International Company to non-resident shareholders under the International Companies Act exemption; the general non-resident dividend withholding tax under the Samoa Income Tax Act 2015 is 15% for dividends paid by a resident taxable company to a non-resident shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 30 days after each anniversary of the date of incorporation; if the anniversary falls on a non-business day, the next business day applies.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months after the end of the company's fiscal year; for a standard 31 December year end, the deadline is 30 April unless an extension is granted by the Commissioner of Revenue.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa Statutory TP Notice, s. 5: mandatory Local File and Master File where the total annual controlled transaction value exceeds WST 1,000,000 or where the International Company claims an exemption for related-party manufacturing, distribution, or licensing revenue exceeding WST 2,000,000. Record retention period is ten years.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Notice 2021, issued under the Samoa International Companies Act 1987, imposes an economic substance condition for a company to retain tax exemption. An International Company engaged in automated point-of-care IVD manufacturing must demonstrate in Samoa or in its residence territory: (i) premises and equipment adequate for the income-generating activities; (ii) full-time professional employees, including a qualified quality assurance officer; (iii) real decision-making for procurement, production, and sales; and (iv) a core income-generating activity that is the manufacturing of microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers. A company failing the substance test is treated as resident and taxed at 27%, and the exemption is retrospectively revoked.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Unlawful Use of International Company Exemption

Statutory sanction: Imposition of 27% corporate income tax on the entire Samoa-source turnover, non-resident withholding tax on deemed distributions, penalty of up to 150% of the tax shielded, late payment surcharges of 1.5% per month, and a criminal fine of WST 200,000 for filing a materially false statement.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under the International Companies Act s.144 and the Samoa Income Tax Act 2015 s.118. Directors who signed the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties. Criminal conviction may carry imprisonment up to seven years, and the Ministry of Revenue may obtain a travel ban and asset freezing order against the directors.
Shell Company and Evasion of Economic Substance Rules

Statutory sanction: Denial of the International Company tax exemption, assessment at 27% on deemed Samoan-source revenue, an economic substance penalty not exceeding WST 1,000,000, and administrative strike-off from the register. Tax authority may redomicile the company as a Samoa taxable resident.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling beneficial owners can be made personally liable for the tax shortfall plus an additional civil restitution equal to 100% of the evasion. Travel bans under the Samoa Revenue Administration Act 2015 apply when tax arrears exceed WST 50,000, and directors may be disqualified from managing any company in Samoa for up to ten years.
AML/CFT Violation and Undisclosed Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Samoa Money Laundering Prevention Act 2013: a fine not exceeding WST 2,000,000 for corporate entities, confiscation of the property involved in the transaction, and cancellation of the company's international company certificate. The Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit must be notified of any suspicious transaction over WST 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the licensed registered agent face criminal liability if they knowingly allow layering of funds. Imprisonment up to fourteen years, unlimited pecuniary penalties, personal travel bans, and the court may make a disqualification order preventing service as a director in Samoa for life.
Misrepresentation to the Registrar and Fraudulent Annual Returns

Statutory sanction: Penalty under the International Companies Act 1987 s.166: fine not exceeding WST 50,000 for each false statement, striking-off of the company, and forfeiture of all rights derived from the certificate. The Ministry of Revenue may in addition impose tax penalties of 20% of any claimed exemption amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent who knowingly executed a false annual return are guilty of an indictable offense; upon conviction, imprisonment up to five years, restitution of fees and taxes avoided, and personal liability for the company's debts and contracts entered into during the period of unlawful operation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Base Erosion Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: A transfer pricing adjustment under the Samoa Income Tax Act 2015 recharacterizes the transactions at arm's length, disallows excessive deductions, imposes a 35% penalty on the adjusted tax understatement, and may treat the offshore payments as deemed dividends subject to non-resident withholding tax at 15%.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for preparing and signing tax computations can be held personally liable for the penalty amount up to WST 500,000. The corporate veil may be ignored where the arrangement has no commercial substance, and directors may be prohibited from leaving Samoa pending full payment of the adjusted liability.
