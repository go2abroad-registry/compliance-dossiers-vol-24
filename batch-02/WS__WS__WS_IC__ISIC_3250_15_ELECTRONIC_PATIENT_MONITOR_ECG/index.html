Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Samoa law. The International Company is statutorily exempt from Samoan income, corporate, stamp, capital gains, and withholding taxes. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 100,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (VAGST Act 2008); international companies supplying goods or services wholly outside Samoa are outside the charge to VAGST and are not required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAGST-registered person zero-rates a supply of tangible goods (including electronic patient monitors) where the goods are exported from Samoa by the supplier or on the supplier's instructions, and the goods are removed from Samoa no later than 30 days after the taxable supply. Services directly and exclusively for exporting the goods, and certain offshore services supplied to non-resident recipients, are also zero-rated. Evidence must demonstrate movement of goods out of Samoa and receipt of foreign-currency payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form CED/Samoa Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill) evidencing Samoan port/airport departure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT MT103/MT700 bank credit evidence)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Manufacturer’s Certificate of Origin for the ECG monitor
  • VAGST zero-rating substantiation schedule maintained by the exporter

No real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing API mandate exists. Registered persons must issue a VAGST tax invoice showing supplier name and TIN/VAGST number, recipient details, date, description, taxable amount, rate, and tax, and must retain records for at least seven years. PDF/e-invoicing is permitted; there is no digital stamp, SAF-T, or continuous transaction control.

The Samoa Ministry of Revenue may, during audit, recapture VAT previously zero-rated where (i) goods were not physically exported within statutory deadlines, (ii) customs documentation is missing or contradictory, (iii) FX proceeds were not repatriated or credited within 180 days, or (iv) the customer/supply chain was part of a circular or refund fraud. Penalties include the full 15% VAGST clawback, a retrospective shortfall penalty not exceeding 150% of the tax for deliberate or negligent conduct, late payment interest up to 2% per month, and, for fraudulent export documentation, criminal liability under the Tax Administration Act 2015 with fines up to SAT 200,000 and imprisonment up to seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoa Tala (WST) is a central-bank-managed floating currency operating within a crawling band against a trade-weighted basket dominated by the AUD, NZD and USD. Current-account payments for bona-fide cross-border trade in goods and services are freely convertible upon presentation of standard commercial documentation; no FX auction or allocation rationing applies. Capital-account transactions, including portfolio investment, offshore borrowing and resident-entity direct investment, remain restricted and require CBS approval or reliance on a standing general permission. For the subject WS_IC, a non-resident International Company incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended), the statute exempts a non-resident international company from the Exchange Control Act in respect of foreign-currency operations. Consequently, inbound equity capital, trade settlements, dividend and profit remittances, and intercompany treasury movements in USD or EUR may flow freely without CBS approval. Profit-remittance hurdles crystallise only if the IC maintains a WST-denominated account, invoices Samoan-domiciled customers, or converts foreign currency into WST for onshore expenditure; such onshore legs require audit evidence, a Samoa Ministry of Revenue tax clearance where applicable, and the CBS-prescribed exchange-control permission application.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Samoan-resident exporters the Exchange Control Regulations 1971, as read with CBS General Permission instruments, impose a mandatory repatriation and surrender structure: export proceeds must be received within 180 days from the date of shipment, must be denominated in an approved foreign currency, and, once received, must be offered for conversion to WST through an authorised dealer within 30 days of receipt; the statutory conversion ratio is 100% unless the CBS grants a foreign-currency-account retention permit or an export-incentive exemption for defined trade contracts. The WS_IC International Company is treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes, and the physical goods flow for the ECG patient-monitor value chain (third-country component supply, sub-assembly and final calibration, followed by export to regional distributors) does not enter or leave Samoan customs territory. The effective surrender obligation is therefore 0%, and 100% of USD-denominated export proceeds may be retained in the offshore operational account. This status must be reconfirmed annually through the registered agent's non-resident declaration and, where requested by the receiving bank, a CBS advance ruling letter.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), established by the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; the sole monetary authority and foreign-exchange administrator. Exchange control is administered under the Exchange Control Act 1970, the Exchange Control Regulations 1971, the Exchange Control (Amendment) Act 1994 and the CBS Foreign Exchange Control Policy 2008. The Central Bank licenses commercial banks as authorised dealers and delegates routine current-account approvals to them; capital-account transactions above delegated bank limits require CBS prior consent.

Domestic companies: final non-resident withholding tax of 10% on dividends under the Income Tax Act 2006. International Companies: 0% — dividend distributions by an exempt IC are free of all Samoan withholding tax and income tax by operation of Section 7 of the International Companies Act 1987.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 2,000,000 (circa USD 745,000) in aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation for domestic resident companies; a Master File is required where group turnover exceeds WST 40,000,000. An exempt Samoan IC does not file TP documentation domestically, but must keep transfer-pricing records in Samoa evidencing arm's-length pricing of its patient-monitoring assembly and calibration services in case a foreign parent CFC or transfer-pricing audit re-characterises the IC's profits.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Rules, an IC must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (for this scope: contract manufacturing, calibration and quality control of multi-parameter bedside telemetry monitors) are undertaken in Samoa, or in a jurisdiction imposing equivalent substance requirements, with adequate physical premises, employees and annual expenditure in Samoa. Non-compliance attracts a penalty of up to WST 10,000 per violation, exchange-of-information disclosures to foreign tax authorities and cancellation of the IC's tax-exemption status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misuse of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2006: recovery of evaded tax plus a civil penalty of up to 200% of the evaded amount plus prescribed interest; wilful evasion is a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to WST 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise false declarations are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of tax underpaid; the Commissioner may issue garnishee orders and travel bans; convicted directors face imprisonment, disqualification from acting as directors and cross-border enforcement flagging.
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: International Companies Act 1987 and Economic Substance Rules: deregistration and striking-off of the IC, cancellation of the tax-exemption certificate, a monetary penalty of up to WST 50,000 and notification to the OECD Inclusive Framework with a consequent black-listing or downgrade risk.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made personally liable for debts incurred while the IC traded without substance; courts can order reimbursement of distributed profits, civil restoration, travel bans and director disqualification orders.
AML / Money Laundering and Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Samoa AML/CFT Act 2016: money laundering is punishable by a fine of up to WST 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 25 years; failure to report a suspicious transaction attracts a fine of up to WST 500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent face criminal liability for knowing participation; sanctions include asset confiscation, travel bans, permanent disqualification from acting as a director, imprisonment and INTERPOL red-notice exposure.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and CFC Attribution

Statutory sanction: In Samoa, the exempt IC bears no domestic income tax, but the Registrar may adjust records, deregister the IC and activate exchange-of-information notifications to treaty partners; abroad, the mispriced profit is re-characterised as a dividend subject to foreign dividend withholding or CFC tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements face personal liability in civil recovery proceedings in the parent jurisdiction as shadow directors; foreign tax authorities may obtain travel bans and personal assessment orders pending final adjustment.
