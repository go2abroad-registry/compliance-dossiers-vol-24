Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (Inland Revenue Division), Government of Samoa; taxpayer portal: the Ministry for Revenue Online Taxpayer Services portal

E-government filing portal

Foreign legal entities obtain a Samoa Tax Identification Number (TIN) by filing a TIN registration application with the Ministry for Revenue, accompanied by the International Company certificate of incorporation, registered agent certificate, registry search result, directorship and shareholding disclosure, and evidence of the registered office in Samoa. For a non-resident International Company, TIN registration is required only for payroll withholding, VAGST registration, or customs clearance purposes; a separate VAGST registration number is issued when the entity makes taxable supplies in Samoa in excess of the statutory threshold or voluntarily requests registration. The standard SOP is: (1) submit the registration form through the Ministry for Revenue online portal or paper lodgment; (2) provide certified passport copies of each director and shareholder; (3) state the entity classification as a tax-exempt International Company; and (4) receive the TIN certificate and, if applicable, the VAGST registration certificate.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (the Samoa International Company is exempt from Samoan income tax and stamp duty under the International Companies Act, as amended; VAGST and customs duty arise only on imports physically entering Samoa or on taxable supplies made in Samoa, not on offshore export sales) — Foreign legal entities obtain a Samoa Tax Identification Number (TIN) by filing a TIN registration application with the Ministry for Revenue, accompanied by the International Company certificate of incorporation, registered agent certificate, registry search result, directorship and shareholding disclosure, and evidence of the registered office in Samoa. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (the Samoa International Company is exempt from Samoan income tax and stamp duty under the International Companies Act, as amended; VAGST and customs duty arise only on imports physically entering Samoa or on taxable supplies made in Samoa, not on offshore export sales)

Available election forms

No general check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime exists under Samoa law. The tax-exempt status of an International Company attaches automatically by incorporation and does not require an affirmative income tax election. Available statutory elections are limited to: (a) voluntary VAGST registration for a person whose taxable supplies are below the compulsory threshold; (b) an election to apply zero-rating to export supplies; and (c) the structural election to incorporate under the International Companies Act rather than the domestic Companies Act, which fixes the territorial tax exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT/WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover (Samoa Tala); foreign non-resident exporters of off-price finished garments must evaluate Samoa place-of-supply and consumption-based nexus rules before assuming a VAGST registration obligation

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Finished garments exported from Samoa, including off-price branded overstock apparel sold by a wholesale liquidation trader, qualify for the 0% VAGST rate when the supplier sells the goods under an export contract, the goods are physically removed from Samoa by or on behalf of the supplier, the risk and title in the goods pass to the overseas buyer, and the exporter retains contemporaneous shipping and payment evidence. Offshore services such as liquidation agency services, marketing, logistics coordination, and trade facilitation supplied to non-resident buyers are also zero-rated where the recipient is outside Samoa, the services are not rendered to a Samoan resident, and the place of effective consumption is outside Samoan territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / authenticated SWIFT wire incoming evidence
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Evidence of foreign buyer identity and purchase order

No real-time fiscal clearance, no national e-invoicing clearinghouse, no API clearance requirement, and no digital stamping mandate currently applies in Samoa. Taxpayers issuing VAGST invoices must nonetheless satisfy substantive invoice-content rules by stating the supplier name and VAGST registration number, recipient identification, date, description and quantity of goods, amount charged, the rate of tax, and the tax amount. Paper invoices and PDF invoices are accepted; the Ministry for Revenue has not yet issued a compulsory continuous-transaction-control clearance framework.

The Ministry for Revenue may retrospectively claw back zero-rated treatment and input tax credits if the exporter cannot substantiate the physical export, the foreign recipient, or the receipt of sales proceeds within the documentation retention period. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to statutory penalties, including immediate collection of unpaid VAGST, late payment interest, penalty tax for deliberate tax evasion (commonly assessed at a substantial percentage of the tax shortfall), and criminal prosecution before the Supreme Court of Samoa with potential fines and imprisonment. Exporters should retain the full customs, transport, warehousing, payment, incoterm, and invoice evidence bundle for at least five years, or longer if required by applicable anti-money-laundering record-keeping rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is not a freely convertible offshore currency. Exchange control is administered by the Central Bank of Samoa under the Exchange Control Act and accompanying regulations. Current account payments for bona fide trade in goods are generally approved through authorized commercial banks using exchange control forms and supporting trade documents. Profit remittance in the form of dividends may be cleared upon evidence of audited financial statements, tax clearance, and corporate solvency. Capital account inflows, including equity injections and shareholder loans, require prior exchange control approval through the authorized bank or by direct Central Bank application. The practical risk for a finished garments off-price wholesale trader is low-to-moderate for USD-based trade flows, provided the underlying supply contracts and invoices are transparent. Off-market convertibility of WST is thin; therefore USD is the preferred settlement currency via correspondent banking. There are no material restrictions on maintaining USD accounts domestically, but any Samoan-resident entity settlement through non-authorized channels would violate exchange control requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa does not currently impose a statutory percentage surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds into WST. However, all export receipts must be repatriated to a Samoa-authorized dealer bank within the underlying bill of lading period, generally within 180 days from the date of export, unless the Central Bank grants an extension. Export invoices must be submitted with the Exchange Control form through the receiving bank, including shipping documents, commercial invoices, and evidence of the physical goods flow. Without an active surrender obligation, proceeds may remain in USD in a Samoa bank account, but the company must declare the receipt and the bank must evidence that the funds arise from genuine merchandise exports. Holding proceeds offshore without prior Central Bank authorization may constitute an exchange control violation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Limited viability for fully offshore EMI banking. Samoa's financial licensing framework, including the Financial Institutions Act 2021 and the Central Bank of Samoa regulations, generally requires a licensed presence for money transmission and deposit-taking services with a domestic nexus. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, or Currencycloud may be used as supplementary collection or disbursement tools for non-Samoan trade flows, but they cannot be used as the primary regulated settlement channel for export proceeds attributable to the Samoa company. Any inbound proceeds to a Samoa company must be routed to an authorized dealer bank and reported through exchange control forms. Fintech platforms can provide USD collection accounts offshore for certain buyers, but final physical settlement should be cleared through a Samoa Licensed Commercial Bank. Legal risk arises if an offshore EMI is used to obscure beneficial ownership or evade the exchange control declaration regime. The recommended approach is a secondary EMI account for buyer-side EUR/USD receipts followed by periodic SWIFT MT103 transfers to the Samoa bank, with each transfer supported by underlying invoices.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital generally routes via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the shareholder's overseas bank to the company's USD account at a Samoa authorized dealer bank. For a Samoa International Company, the bank must receive evidence of the underlying equity or shareholder loan, the board resolution, and the account/instruction letter. The remittance narrative should include 'WS_IC capital injection / ISIC 4641 finished garments wholesale' with the appropriate purpose code. There is no mandatory central bank pre-approval for smaller equity injections under the delegated authority threshold, but the receiving bank will report on the Exchange Control form and may require additional UBO declarations. Compliance safeguards: use only the exact legal name of the company, include the Samoa bank SWIFT BIC, and avoid splitting payments to circumvent thresholds. The bank may require that the shareholder's account is in the same beneficial ownership name.Outbound trade disbursements for purchasing finished garment lots from overseas suppliers are cleared via SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers or documentary Letters of Credit for larger and first-time transactions. For routine off-price liquidation trading, the preferred rail is an MT103 direct onward payment from the Samoa bank to the supplier's bank, accompanied by a stated purpose 'trade payment for finished garments / inventory clearance'. Commercial Letters of Credit provide a safer route when dealing with unknown suppliers and should be confirmed by a reputable outside bank. ACH is acceptable for small recurring payments to US-based service providers and escrow-type settlement platforms. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — International Company (IC) statutory exemption on all international business income under section 141 of the International Companies Act 1987; the Samoa domestic standard corporate income tax rate is 27% for entities not holding IC status.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends and distributions made by an International Company to any shareholder, including non-residents, pursuant to the International Companies Act exemption; 15% final withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a domestic Samoan company to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has no codified stand-alone transfer-pricing statute in force for International Companies, but the Commissioner of Inland Revenue adopts the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and applies the arm's-length standard under the Income Tax Act general valuation and anti-avoidance powers. Local File-equivalent documentation is mandatory where connected-party cross-border transactions exceed WST 1,000,000 (approximately USD 370,000) per annum; a Master File and Samoa Country-by-Country report are required where the Samoa-resident ultimate parent group has consolidated group revenue above WST 35,000,000 (approximately USD 13,000,000).

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations, as amended, impose a mandatory economic substance test on every International Company performing a relevant activity. Relevant activities include banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centre, holding company, and intellectual property business. The off-price wholesale liquidation of finished garments (ISIC 4641) will be treated as a distribution and service centre to the extent that purchasing, inventory risk-taking, marketing, or resale functions are directed from Samoa. The company must demonstrate in Samoa: (1) direction and management, evidenced by board meetings held and minutes kept in Samoa; (2) an adequate physical office; (3) full-time employees proportionate to its income; and (4) operating expenditure proportionate to its income. Pure equity holding companies benefit from reduced substance requirements. Failure exposes the company to fixed penalties up to WST 100,000, reassessment of profits, automatic exchange of information, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil: 100% shortfall penalty on the unpaid 27% tax, a 2% monthly late-payment surcharge, and reassessment of all worldwide income. Criminal: fine up to WST 100,000 (corporate fine up to WST 250,000) and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the filing was directed by an officer: directors become jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties, and costs; prosecution carries travel bans, freezing of foreign assets, and imprisonment of the responsible director.
Shell Company Abuse and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty up to WST 100,000 per contravention plus daily surcharge; the regulator may strike the company off the register and exchange information with the foreign tax administration of the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Samoan registered agent are liable for the penalties where they participated in the facade; disqualification from acting as a director for up to 10 years, travel-ban enforcement pending examination, and personal restitution of fees improperly saved.
Breach of Tax-Exempt Perimeter (Domestic Taint)

Statutory sanction: Immediate forfeiture of IC exemption; assessment on worldwide income at 27%, penalty surcharge up to 20% of the tax assessed, deregistration, and a fine up to WST 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for restitution of the tax avoided on a joint-and-several basis; the court may order asset tracing, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years for a knowing contravention.
