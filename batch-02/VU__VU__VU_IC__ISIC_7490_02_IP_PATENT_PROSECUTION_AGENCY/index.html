Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC) - Department of Customs and Inland Revenue, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila; e-filing portal: Vanuatu Revenue Management System (VAT and TIN administration); International Company licensing: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), Port Vila

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu national taxes, so no income-tax TIN is required; the IC obtains a VFSC registration number and Certificate of Incorporation through a licensed registered agent. For VAT purposes, an IC that wishes to claim input credits or issue zero-rated invoices must procure a TIN or VAT registration number from VRC by filing the VAT registration form, attaching the VFSC Certificate of Incorporation, registered-agent due-diligence confirmation, local address or bank details, and anticipated turnover declaration; non-resident shareholders may complete the process by power of attorney through the VFSC-registered agent, with no physical presence in Vanuatu required.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — An International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu national taxes, so no income-tax TIN is required; the IC obtains a VFSC registration number and Certificate of Incorporation through a licensed registered agent. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or analogous regime exists in Vanuatu; therefore no check-the-box election is available or required, and hybrid mismatch elections are inapplicable. ICs are automatically tax-exempt under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] and, where invoked, may obtain a further tax-exemption certificate under the Vanuatu Economic Development Act. An IC may voluntarily elect VAT registration under the VAT Act [Cap 248]; compulsory registration arises only upon crossing the VT 5,000,000 taxable-supplies threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 5,000,000 (five million vatu) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; voluntary registration available; exports of goods and supplies of services to non-residents are zero-rated

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Section 6 of the VAT Act [Cap 248] where goods are physically exported from Vanuatu within the prescribed period or services are supplied to, and wholly enjoyed by, a non-resident outside Vanuatu. Patent-prosecution and international-trademark agency services delivered to overseas clients qualify as zero-rated exported services provided the IC maintains signed service contracts, invoices non-resident counterparties in foreign currency, performs no supply to Vanuatu residents, and retains sufficient evidence that the service was rendered outside the Vanuatu domestic market.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged in ASYCUDA World (goods exports only)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical removal from Vanuatu (goods exports only)
  • Commercial invoice and signed service contract or engagement letter naming the non-resident client
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT or MT103 credit evidence proving fees were paid from offshore funds
  • VAT zero-rated schedule and tax-invoice register substantiating each export claim

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory electronic-invoicing clearance regime applies in Vanuatu. Registered persons must issue tax invoices under Section 24 of the VAT Act [Cap 248] showing the supplier name, TIN or VAT number, invoice date, description of supply, VAT amount, and total; VAT returns are filed electronically through the VRC Revenue Management System, and goods customs declarations are lodged through the ASYCUDA World system.

Vanuatu imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day convention is applied by banks and auditors as the evidentiary benchmark for inward receipts supporting export zero-rating. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims expose the IC to VAT reassessment, statutory interest, late-payment penalties of up to 25% of the tax due, and criminal fines for false documentation under the VAT Act; confirmed dealing with Vanuatu residents additionally breaches the International Companies Act, triggering VFSC strike-off, director disqualification, and registered-agent licence consequences, with retrospective clawback of input credits and zero-rating relief for the statutory limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a liberalized exchange control regime administered by the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu. The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, with no general prohibitions on foreign-currency accounts held by residents or non-resident entities. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation from an International Company may be effected freely through authorized banks without prior central bank approval, subject to submission of standard documentary evidence (e.g., audited financial statements, board resolution, tax clearance where applicable). The regime creates no material bottleneck for patent prosecution fee flows, as service-related current payments and operational disbursements are outside administered surrender requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export receipts or service revenue in foreign currency to the central bank. Exporters and service providers, including intellectual property prosecution agencies, may retain receivables in foreign currency accounts with licensed domestic banks. The only compliance condition is that foreign-currency earnings from exports or cross-border services must be declared through the banking channel on the relevant inward remittance form, and documentary support (invoice, contract, or POA) must be retained for bank verification. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to VUV.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex can be used as supplementary payment rails for low-value, non-escalatory disbursements (e.g., annuities, small filing fees), but they are not licensed as banks or deposit-taking institutions in Vanuatu. They cannot lawfully provide the full fiduciary escrow or account-holding function required for capital injection escrow or large outbound transfers. Their use is restricted to the 'target_payment_rails' category of fintech conduits, and any use must be backed by a domestic bank account for ultimate settlement. Legal constraints include the Financial Dealers Act and the International Companies Act prohibition on conducting banking business without an RBV license, the inability of an EMI to issue SWIFT-based legal payment undertakings, and exposure to de-risking if funds flow to high-risk patent office jurisdictions without an underlying invoice trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to fund the Vanuatu International Company's operational escrow and disbursement accounts must be routed through a licensed RBV-authorized commercial bank using SWIFT MT103 or equivalent wire transfers. The sender must provide the Vanuatu receiving bank with the remitter's identity, source-of-funds documentation (shareholder loan agreement, equity subscription agreement, or client retainer), and the relevant purpose code for the transaction. For capital subscriptions, the purpose code 'S001 – Establishment of a foreign-invested enterprise' or 'S010 – Equity capital contribution' is used, while for pre-payments for patent prosecution services the code 'S020 – Service transaction payment' applies. The receiving bank performs a verification of the remitter's KYC profile, the matching of the beneficiary name to the IC's board resolution, and confirmation that the transaction does not violate the sanctions list. Upon receipt, the funds are held in an operational or escrow account segregated from any proprietary balances, with the correspondent relationship routed through ANZ or BSP's offshore banking groups (e.g., ANZ Australia, BSP Papua New Guinea, or a New York/London correspondent) for USD clearing.Outbound trade disbursements for patent prosecution fees, PCT filing costs, and international trademark registrations are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 payments from the IC's foreign-currency account at an RBV-licensed bank to official patent offices, law firms, or annuities service providers. The payment instruction must include a precise beneficiary description, the invoice or reference number of the official fee, and a purpose code reflecting 'royalties or service fees' as appropriate. Commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) are not a standard clearing rail for IP service disbursements and are generally not advisable; the prevailing global protocol is direct MT103 wire settlement with a full audit trail. Same-day value is achievable for patent office payments in the United States and Europe using the US or EU correspondent clearing networks, while PCT fee payments to WIPO may be settled via the MT103 to the Bank for International Settlements or approved commercial banks. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VUV 0 (no corporate income tax and therefore no Local File or Master File transfer pricing compliance threshold in Vanuatu). ESR recordkeeping obligations may apply irrespective of revenue where an IC carries on a relevant activity; the VFSC may request financial statements, invoices, bank records, and payroll records on a risk-factored basis.

Economic substance scope: Under Vanuatu's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2019, every International Company that carries on a relevant activity must demonstrate adequate economic substance in Vanuatu. A patent prosecution agency is ordinarily classified as carrying on a 'service business' if it earns fee income for PCT patent prosecution and trademark registration services; such an entity must maintain a physical office in Vanuatu, full-time qualified employees, proportionate operating expenditures, and board meetings held and minuted in Vanuatu. If the same IC holds, licenses, or otherwise administers patent/trademark intellectual property and receives royalty-style income, it is classified as an IP business and is subject to a more stringent substance test requiring demonstrable decision-making and development activity in Vanuatu. Pure equity holding entities enjoy lighter obligations but must still file an ESR return and maintain a registered office and records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abusive Tax Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Offence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2020, as amended, and the Proceeds of Crime Act; the VFSC and the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit may impose corporate fines up to VUV 20,000,000, seize illicit proceeds, strike the company off, and report the arrangement to foreign tax authorities under a Tax Information Exchange Agreement (TIEA).

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced on grounds of fraud; directors and beneficial owners are personally liable for civil restitution of diverted income, are subject to court-ordered asset freezes and travel bans, and may face imprisonment for money-laundering offences of up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Violation of the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 and the International Companies Act (Cap. 222); the VFSC may impose penalties up to VUV 5,000,000, issue a non-compliance direction, strike the company off, and notify the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false economic substance declarations are personally liable for penalties and restitution of illegal distributions; courts may issue travel bans and disqualification orders; imprisonment of up to 10 years may be imposed if fraudulent misrepresentation is established.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Vanuatu Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2020 and VFSC AML Code; corporate penalties reach VUV 25,000,000, with mandatory confiscation of funds; natural persons are liable to fines and imprisonment for up to 10 years; the IC may lose its exempt status and be struck off.

Director personal liability: Directors, shareholders, and the registered agent are treated as culpable principals for AML offences; the corporate veil is pierced, requiring disgorgement of all fees; travel bans and asset confiscation orders may be issued pending criminal proceedings, and imprisonment can be extended to 15 years for aggravated laundering.
