Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue Services (VRS), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management (MFEM); taxpayer e-portal and e-filing via the VRS self-service portal (vrs.gov.vu).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company (IC) is incorporated through a licensed registered agent at the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC). The VFSC Certificate of Incorporation and registered agent file constitute the primary corporate identifier. Because the IC enjoys a statutory tax exemption under Section 145 of the International Companies Act [Cap 221] and has no Vanuatu-source income, no national TIN/VAT ID is ordinarily required. If the IC later establishes a physical Vanuatu presence or makes local taxable supplies, it must apply to VRS on Form VAT-1 (accompanied by the incorporation certificate, registered agent confirmation, and projected turnover) to obtain a VAT registration number; a municipal Business Licence from the Port Vila or Luganville council is separately required.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company (IC) is incorporated through a licensed registered agent at the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists because Vanuatu imposes no income tax on any entity. Available elections are limited to: (i) voluntary VAT registration under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 245] where the annual turnover threshold is not yet met; (ii) an application to VRS confirming the IC's tax-exempt status under Section 145 of the International Companies Act, which guarantees the imposition of no future tax for 20 years from incorporation; and (iii) election of monthly versus quarterly VAT return filing periods where permitted by the Commissioner. Vanuatu has no CFC or hybrid entity classification regime, and no DTA network is applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 10,000,000 (ten million vatu) annual taxable turnover in any 12-month period; registration is compulsory once the threshold is exceeded, with voluntary registration available for lower-turnover suppliers of taxable goods and services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) exported goods physically removed from Vanuatu; and (ii) supplies of services to non-resident persons where the benefit of the services is received wholly outside Vanuatu, including cross-border tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services supplied by an IC to offshore clients. Domestic VAT-registered suppliers must treat such supplies as zero-rated and retain documentary evidence that the recipient is non-resident and that the performance and benefit sit outside Vanuatu. A pure International Company exercising the statutory exemption does not enter the VAT net at all, and its offshore service revenue is neither taxable nor VRS-assessable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (approved outward cargo manifest lodged with Vanuatu Customs and Border Control)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of goods from Vanuatu
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT MT103 bank credit advice) evidencing full receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, national e-invoicing, continuous transaction control (CTC), or mandatory digital stamping regime is in force. A registered person must issue a compliant VAT tax invoice in paper or PDF form showing supplier name and VAT ID, recipient details, date, description of supply, net value, VAT rate, and VAT amount. VAT returns and payments must be filed with VRS electronically or by paper within the statutory filing window; failure to file or pay attracts late penalties and interest. Customs export clearance is transacted through the Vanuatu Customs and Border Control system using a cargo manifest and export declaration.

VRS may audit zero-rated claims and retrospectively disallow the zero rate where the customs, invoicing, or outward-receipt bundle is incomplete, recharacterizing the supply as a taxable local supply and assessing VAT at 12.5%; statutory late penalties and daily interest accrue on any underdeclared output tax. Knowingly false zero-rating declarations are treated as tax evasion under the VAT Act and Penal Code, exposing the entity and its officers to substantial fines, imprisonment, recovery of evaded tax, and cancellation of the IC's exemption certificate. The Section 145 IC exemption does not shield fraudulent VAT filings or fictitious export transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains IMF Article VIII status with a fully liberalised current account: the Vatu (VUV) is freely convertible for trade in services, cross-border advisory fees, and profit or income remittances, subject to an authorised-dealer routing rule. Capital-account transactions require exchange-control approval via the RBV acting through authorised dealers; approval is routinely granted for genuine corporate treasury structures. The International Company (IC) is statutorily tax-exempt, with zero income, capital gains, and withholding taxes on remittances; the operative remittance hurdle is bank-level AML/KYC and source-of-funds verification rather than statutory conversion restrictions. Non-resident accounts may be credited freely by authorised dealers, while debiting to resident accounts triggers enhanced scrutiny. The VUV is managed against a basket of major currencies, providing de facto stabilisation for USD- and AUD-denominated treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, the Exchange Control Regulations require the exporter to receive payment within 6 months of the date of export and to offer the foreign-currency proceeds for sale or deposit to an authorised dealer in Vanuatu within a further 3 months; no mandatory conversion ratio applies, and retained foreign-currency balances are permitted with authorised-dealer approval. For ISIC 7020 cross-border advisory services, there is no surrender timeline or conversion mandate; consultancy fees in foreign currency must be routed through a Vanuatu authorised dealer and evidenced by an underlying engagement contract and invoice, with any VUV/USD conversion executed at the prevailing market rate. ICs are categorised as non-resident for exchange-control purposes when substantially owned by non-residents, further relaxing repatriation constraints.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), exercising exchange-control powers delegated under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 63] and the Exchange Control Regulations [Cap 63]; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) separately licenses International Companies and financial-service providers under the International Companies Act [Cap 222], the Financial Dealers Act [Cap 285], and the International Banking Act [Cap 165], with AML/CTF supervision under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 13 of 2017.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Remote-only offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise-style platforms) do not support Vanuatu International Companies, and Vanuatu has no dedicated EMI licensing regime. A foreign EMI wishing to serve a VU IC would require authorisation under the Financial Dealers Act [Cap 285] or the International Banking Act [Cap 165], imposing local substance, capital, and compliance obligations that erode the light-touch offshore advantage. The recommended architecture is direct Tier-1 bank account relationships for settlement, complemented by domestic Visa/Mastercard card acquiring; digital treasury dashboards are permissible only as a reporting overlay and must never hold settlement funds in a non-licensed wallet.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound funds route by SWIFT MT103 to the IC's settlement account at a Tier-1 Vanuatu bank. For share-capital injection, the payment narrative must state 'Share capital subscription - International Companies Act [Cap 222]' and be supported by a board resolution and share-register extract; no statutory minimum capital applies, but a USD 5,000 operating buffer is recommended to satisfy bank onboarding thresholds. For consultancy fees, the narrative must state 'Advisory services - ISIC 7020' and reference the underlying engagement contract and invoice number; the receiving bank's compliance team matches the narrative against the contract before crediting. Every inbound wire is screened against OFAC/EU/UN sanctions lists; the remitting bank must not be domiciled in a sanctioned jurisdiction, and intermediary banks that would require U-turn transactions must be avoided.Operational disbursements (subcontractors, software subscriptions, travel, professional fees) are executed via SWIFT MT103 with full purpose narrative referencing the corresponding invoice. Card-based disbursements utilise the bank's Visa/Mastercard issuing programme for low-value, high-frequency treasury payments such as SaaS and online expenses. All outbound instructions require dual-approval (two authorised signatories) under the IC's board-approved payment mandate. For any goods-related structuring work, Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) are available with the IC as applicant or beneficiary; VUV-denominated domestic disbursements settle through the national payment and clearing system. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu does not impose corporate income tax, branch profit tax, capital gains tax, payroll tax, or social security contributions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividends; distributions by an International Company to non-resident shareholders are made gross, with no domestic deduction or withholding obligation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None applicable. Vanuatu maintains no transfer pricing statute, no arm's length documentation rules, and no local or master file thresholds. The only relevant turnover benchmark is the VAT registration threshold of VT 4,000,000 (approximately USD 33,000) per annum, which applies only to supplies to Vanuatu-resident clients. AML/CFT rules nevertheless require retention of invoices, contracts, and bank records for a minimum of five years.

Economic substance scope: No Economic Substance Regulations are currently codified in Vanuatu. However, the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices and the EU listing process have pressed Vanuatu toward substance requirements, and commercial practice already mandates: a VFSC-licensed registered agent; a physical registered office in Vanuatu; board resolutions and decision-making records; and genuine invoicing and bank flows. A letterbox IC billing large advisory fees with no operational reality faces global bank de-risking, VFSC strike-off, and adverse exchange-of-information reporting.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Because no corporate income tax exists, fiscal evasion is prosecuted under the VAT Act (penalty tax up to twice the shortfall, monthly interest at 1%, and criminal fines) and under the Penal Code for obtaining a financial advantage by deception. VFSC may impose civil fines for false statutory filings and refer matters to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or authorize deception are personally liable for civil restitution, court-ordered fines, disqualification as directors, and imprisonment at the discretion of the Vanuatu Supreme Court; travel bans may be imposed pending asset recovery.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: While no Economic Substance Regulation is yet in force, global financial intermediaries classify such structures as high-risk shells; VFSC strikes off ICs that fail to maintain a registered agent or pay fees; banks close accounts and correspondent banking relationships are denied.

Director personal liability: Directors face cross-border enforcement through Tax Information Exchange Agreements and mutual legal assistance; foreign tax authorities may assert promoter penalties and secondary liability, and Vanuatu courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraud or wrongful trading, making directors personally liable for corporate obligations.
Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act No. 13 of 2014, as amended, registered agents and financial institutions must report suspicious transactions to the FIU; breaches attract substantial fines, license revocation, and asset-freezing orders. Money laundering offenses are prosecuted criminally with custodial penalties at the discretion of the courts.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are criminally liable for laundering the proceeds of foreign tax crimes; penalties include imprisonment, civil forfeiture of traced funds, travel bans, and search-and-seizure powers exercised by the FIU.
VAT Registration and Collection Failure

Statutory sanction: VAT Act penalties include a civil penalty of up to double the tax evaded, monthly interest at 1%, compulsory registration with back-assessment of tax, and separate fines for failure to file returns.

Director personal liability: The VAT Act imposes personal liability on directors who sign false returns; criminal conviction for evasion carries fines and imprisonment, and directors may be disqualified from acting as officers of any Vanuatu company.
Facilitation of Cross-Border Client Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu cooperates through Tax Information Exchange Agreements and the OECD Global Forum peer-review framework, permitting exchange of beneficial ownership and banking information; the EU may relist Vanuatu as a non-cooperative jurisdiction if substance standards are not met, triggering defensive measures affecting the entire IC structure.

Director personal liability: Directors may face extradition or prosecution at the request of foreign states under mutual legal assistance treaties; foreign courts may pierce the corporate veil and impose personal liability for unpaid foreign taxes, fraud penalties, and criminal imprisonment in the client's home jurisdiction.
