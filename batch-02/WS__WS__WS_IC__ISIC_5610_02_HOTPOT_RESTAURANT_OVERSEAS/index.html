Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MFOR), Apia, Samoa, headed by the Commissioner of Inland Revenue; official e-services portal: https://www.mfor.gov.ws (tax registration, return filing, payment and tax clearance certifications).

E-government filing portal

A Samoa International Company (IC) is incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 through a SIFA-licensed registered agent and receives a Company Number; no MFOR tax identification number (TIN) is automatically issued. A standard IC carrying on business wholly outside Samoa is exempt from all Samoan taxes and is not required to hold a TIN or file Samoa income tax/VAGST returns. If the IC later derives Samoa-source income, carries on a taxable activity in Samoa, or makes taxable supplies in Samoa, the registered agent must file an MFOR taxpayer registration application accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, constitution, registered agent certificate, and director details; MFOR then issues the TIN. For banking and AML due-diligence purposes, the registered agent may obtain from MFOR a tax clearance or tax-exemption certificate confirming the IC's offshore exempt status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Samoan law. Statutory elections available under the International Companies Act 1987 and the Companies Act 2015 include: (i) election to convert an International Company into a domestic company; (ii) election to migrate or redomicile an International Company out of Samoa; and (iii) election of a domestic company to register as an International Company. Any such election is lodged through the licensed registered agent with the Registrar and notified to MFOR, and it commences or terminates the tax-exempt status accordingly; a Samoan International Company has no transparent or pass-through tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period for a person carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa; a non-resident carrying on a Samoan taxable activity is registrable on the same threshold and voluntary registration is permitted. A tax-exempt International Company operating wholly offshore has no VAGST registration nexus in Samoa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the VAGST Act 2007 to: (i) goods exported from Samoa; (ii) services supplied to a non-resident recipient outside Samoa, including offshore group support, royalties, and hotpot condiment supplies to the overseas branch; and (iii) supplies in connection with foreign-going vessels or aircraft. Over-the-counter dining sales by the overseas restaurant are outside the scope of Samoan VAGST entirely and fall within the host country's VAT/GST system. Where the IC is not registered in Samoa, no Samoan VAGST refund mechanism is triggered; where registered, creditable input tax may be refunded on production of export evidence.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration (electronic customs entry)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical export from Samoa
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-currency credit advice
  • Certificate of origin where preferential duty treatment is claimed
  • Sanitary or phytosanitary certificate for exported food products

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API mandate, or digital stamping requirement exists under Samoan law. Registered suppliers must issue tax invoices (paper or electronic) containing the particulars prescribed under the VAGST Act 2007 and must file VAGST returns and payments through the MFOR e-Services portal.

Samoa law does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is applied in this dossier as the prudential compliance benchmark used to substantiate export zero-rating. If MFOR determines that a zero-rating claim is unsupported or fraudulent, the tax is clawed back at the standard 15% rate together with shortfall and late-payment penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2014 and the VAGST Act 2007; deliberate evasion is subject to fines of up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment, and any Samoan registration of the International Company may be suspended.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains Article VIII status for current account convertibility, allowing unrestricted payments for trade and services in Samoan Tala (WST). Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment equity injections, intercompany debt, and issuance of guarantees, require prior CBS approval or notification via authorised dealers. Profit and dividend remittances are permitted for approved investments upon submission of audited financial statements, tax clearance from the Samoa Revenue Authority, and a completed CBS foreign exchange transfer form; no material withholding hurdle applies when the entity is a tax-exempt International Company, but documentary compliance is strictly enforced.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must submit foreign exchange receipts to an authorised dealer in Samoa within 30 days of receipt. All export proceeds received in foreign currency are subject to a mandatory conversion ratio of 100% into WST unless the exporter holds a CBS-approved Foreign Exchange Retention Account or has obtained a specific exemption for reinvestment or payment of approved trade liabilities. Retention privileges are discretionary and require a written application demonstrating underlying commercial need.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) acting under the Central Bank Act 1984 and the Exchange Control Act 1976, together with the Foreign Exchange Regulations 1990

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI licences are not recognised in Samoa. Unlicensed fintech EMIs cannot access the local clearing system, are not authorised dealers under the Exchange Control Act, and may not lawfully open WST-denominated accounts or process Samoan merchant settlements. An overseas-licensed EMI may only serve as an auxiliary payment collection vehicle outside Samoa, with all net funds repatriated through a licensed Samoan commercial bank using a formal wire transfer and CBS purpose code.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity wiring must be directed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the entity's WST or USD capital escrow account at an authorised dealer in Samoa. The remittance must designate the CBS purpose code for inward direct investment, typically 'direct investment - equity capital', and must be accompanied by a completed CBS foreign exchange declaration (Form RPE-1 or the current equivalent) submitted by the receiving bank. Prior CBS approval for the investment is required where the aggregate equity injection exceeds the exchange control authorisation threshold; the company must file the relevant application before the funds arrive. For any funding from a related overseas hotpot restaurant, intercompany loan documentation and an intra-group capital transfer application are necessary to prevent misclassification as a trade payment.Payments to overseas suppliers for hotpot condiments, packaging, and equipment are cleared via SWIFT MT103 messages from the Samoan operational account to the beneficiary's bank. All outbound wires require a CBS foreign exchange form identifying the trade purpose code and, where the payment is in a foreign currency, the entity must source the funds from an approved foreign currency account or buy WST against the eligible trade invoice. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No codified Local File or Master File threshold is specified in the Samoa International Companies Act; the Samoa tax administration may require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed WST 1,000,000, particularly for management fees, brand royalties, and hotpot condiment supply arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Samoa’s Economic Substance Regulations apply to International Companies carrying on relevant activities such as intellectual property holding, banking, insurance, shipping, and headquarters services. An overseas direct-operated hotpot restaurant chain typically does not conduct such relevant activities in Samoa; however, if the IC holds hotpot trade names, recipes, or condiment brand IP and derives royalty income, it must demonstrate management and control in Samoa, adequate premises, local employees, and operating expenditure in accordance with the substance regulations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Exemption Claims

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to WST 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax shortfall plus monthly interest at 1.5%; revocation of the tax exemption certificate and reassessment at the standard 27% corporate rate.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties, face director disqualification, and may be subjected to travel restriction orders by Samoan courts until all liabilities are settled.
Shell Company Abuse and Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Refusal or retroactive denial of tax exemption, strike-off and dissolution of the IC, publication on the SICO non-compliance register, and loss of limited liability protection for secured creditors.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance declarations may be fined up to WST 500,000 and banned from managing a company for 5 years; shareholders’ assets traceable through nominee arrangements are recoverable under the Proceeds of Crime Act.
AML Breach and Structuring

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa’s Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act, entity fines up to WST 2,000,000, individual fines up to WST 500,000, freezing and seizure of funds, and criminal imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are personally prosecutable for failing to report suspicious transactions; personal bank guarantees and overseas assets can be subjected to tracing and enforcement through mutual legal assistance.
Withholding Tax and Transfer Pricing Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 100% of the withholding tax not collected, monthly late-payment interest, disallowance of the deduction for the payer, and loss of IC tax exemption for misclassified Samoan-source payments.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if they knowingly authorize under-withholding or mispricing, cannot rely on non-resident status to shield personal liability, and may face travel bans and civil restitution until the tax authorities recover the unpaid amount.
