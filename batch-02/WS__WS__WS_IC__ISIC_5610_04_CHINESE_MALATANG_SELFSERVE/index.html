Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa (successor to the Samoa Revenue Authority; merged with the former Customs Division), headquartered in Apia. Official taxpayer portal at www.revenue.gov.ws (MfR e-Services); customs and trade facilitation handled through the Samoa National Single Window (SNSW) running on ASYCUDA World.

E-government filing portal

Under the Tax Administration Act 2012 (s.28), every person carrying on a business in Samoa must obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) before commencing activity. The foreign-owned Samoa International Company (WS_IC) procures its TIN through a licensed registered agent: (1) the agent files the Taxpayer Registration Application (Form TIN/TA-01), together with the VAGST registration form where the IC expects taxable supplies in Samoa exceeding WST 100,000 in any 12-month period; (2) the application bundle comprises the Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Registered Office, certified copy of the Constitution, director/shareholder registers, and a certificate of good standing issued by the Samoa International Companies Registry; (3) MfR issues the TIN within approximately 5-10 business days; (4) where the IC relies on the statutory offshore-exempt regime, the agent submits the Tax Exemption Declaration / Certificate request under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) so the entity is recorded as tax-exempt; (5) a Samoa Business Licence under the Business Licences Act 2004 is required only if the IC conducts physical commercial activity inside Samoa - a pure offshore master-licensing operation with only a local registered office does not trigger that licence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections for the WS_IC: (a) election to convert from international-company status to a domestic company (thereby accepting Entity_Level_Taxation at the standard company rate) if it chooses to conduct onshore Malatang operations; (b) election for a 5-year income-tax holiday under the Economic Development Incentives regime for qualifying onshore licensed restaurant or franchise-support activity; (c) VAGST group registration election under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 1992 for related Samoan entities; (d) reconstruction/amalgamation rollover elections under the Income Tax Act 2018 for intra-group reorganisation of franchise IP; (e) hybrid mismatch: no comprehensive check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch alignment election is available under Samoan domestic law; foreign-hybrid outcomes are governed by ordinary source and residence rules of the Income Tax Act 2018.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (VAGST Act 1992, s.11); voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold; imported services supplied by a non-resident to a Samoan recipient may attract a recipient reverse charge, which is relevant to Samoan franchisees importing the Malatang brand licence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the VAGST Act 1992 for (a) goods exported from Samoa, (b) services supplied to a non-resident person for use and enjoyment wholly outside Samoa, and (c) international freight and transport. The WS_IC's master-franchise licence and royalty income derived from non-resident Malatang franchisees qualifies as exported services at 0% where the licensed IP is exploited entirely outside Samoa and the franchisee is not physically present in Samoa. The claim must be substantiated with the executed Master Franchise Agreement, documentary proof of each franchisee's non-residence (e.g., foreign business registration certificate), the 0%-rated VAGST tax invoice, and bank credit evidence of the royalty wire; VAGST input credits attributable to the zero-rated export activity are refundable to the IC.

  • Customs Export Declaration (lodged through ASYCUDA World) - required only for physical export of goods; a service-export declaration / zero-rated supply schedule flagged on the MfR portal replaces it for pure licensing royalties.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill for tangible goods; for the Malatang master-licence, the executed Master Franchise Agreement, the 0%-rated VAGST tax invoice, and the franchisee's certificate of non-residence / foreign business registration.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT MT103 / credit advice evidencing receipt of franchise royalties in the WS_IC's Samoan bank account within the statutory repatriation window.

Samoa has no mandatory real-time electronic-invoicing or fiscal-clearance regime; no API-based digital stamping or e-invoice clearance platform exists. Taxpayers must issue and retain VAGST tax invoices for all taxable supplies (VAGST Act 1992, ss.23-24) and file returns electronically via the MfR e-Services portal; physical exports and imports are cleared electronically through ASYCUDA World via the Samoa National Single Window, which requires advance electronic customs declarations.

MfR audits zero-rated export claims against the full documentation bundle and bank trail. Failure to substantiate triggers retrospective reversal of the zero-rating, clawback of all related input-credit refunds, and reassessment at 15% VAGST plus interest. Under the Tax Administration Act 2012, shortfall penalties escalate to 150% for intentional evasion, 90% for recklessness and 40% for failure to take reasonable care; criminal prosecution for false or misleading export declarations can lead to substantial fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years, and fraudulent customs declarations additionally attract penalties and forfeiture under the Customs Act 2014. Non-repatriation of export royalties within 180 days exposes the IC to exchange-control penalties under the Exchange Control Act 1976 (administered by the Central Bank of Samoa). In addition, the International Companies (Amendment) Act 2017 economic-substance rules apply: an IC claiming zero-rated offshore royalties without adequate substance or exchange-of-information compliance risks revocation of the statutory tax exemption and consequential grey-listing sanctions that deny treaty or zero-rating benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a managed float with exchange controls administered by the CBS. Capital account transactions by residents are restricted and require prior CBS approval; current account transactions such as royalty and service payments are generally permitted but must be effected through an authorized dealer (licensed commercial bank). Non-resident-owned International Companies (ICs) that conduct business exclusively in foreign currency and do not transact with Samoan residents enjoy broad exemptions from exchange controls. Profit remittance hurdles are accordingly low, provided the IC maintains a designated foreign-currency account, submits a certified board resolution for each dividend or royalty outflow, and demonstrates absence of any Samoan taxable activity via the IC tax-exemption certificate. Convertibility for ICs is thus effectively assured for current account flows and foreign-currency capital movements, while any incidental WST conversion remains subject to CBS approval and standard authorized-dealer oversight.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBS exchange-control practice, exporters of goods and services must repatriate export proceeds to Samoa within 180 days from the date of shipment or service completion, and surrender 100% of the proceeds to an authorized dealer within 30 days of receipt. A general or specific exemption is available for International Companies that are non-resident-owned and operate in foreign currency; such entities may retain export proceeds in an approved foreign-currency external account without mandatory surrender. For a Malatang fast-casual franchise generating royalty income from China, the exemption should be invoked by describing each remittance as 'franchise royalty – foreign services' and maintaining a documented trail of underlying service agreements to avoid inadvertent mandatory conversion.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) – Exchange Control Department, operating under the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Central Bank Act 1984

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g. Payoneer, Wise, and similar electronic money institutions) are not licensed banks in Samoa and are not recognized as authorized dealers under the Central Bank of Samoa's exchange-control framework. Their viability for a Samoa IC is Low to Medium. A small number of established EMIs accept Pacific-registered companies, but they impose enhanced due diligence and usually require an existing physical-bank relationship for outgoing wires. Legal constraints include the CBS rule that all Samoan-resident payments must be routed through an authorized dealer; EMIs cannot hold WST or provide MT103 clearing on behalf of Samoan ICs. EMIs may be used only as a supplementary receiving mechanism for foreign-currency royalty inflows when the underlying funds do not touch the Samoan domestic banking system, and never as the sole treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from a Chinese shareholder or parent will be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas remitting bank to the IC's USD-denominated operational account at a Samoan authorized dealer. The wire must carry a purpose code aligned with Samoa's BOP-compliant classification, specifically code 'EQUI' or the equivalent designation for 'Direct Investment Equity Capital'. The receiving bank will verify the identity and sanctions status of the remitter, the compliance of the 72 additional remittance information lines, and will require the IC to present a board resolution authorizing the capital increase, an updated register of members, a source-of-funds declaration, and any underlying share-subscription or contribution agreement. Since the IC is non-resident-owned, no prior CBS approval is required for the deposit of foreign currency, but the bank conducts an internal exchange-control check and may require a written statement from the IC's local agent confirming that the funds will not be converted into WST without CBS consent.Outbound trade and royalty disbursements will be cleared through two primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for routine franchise royalties, supply-chain deposits, and intangible service fees; and (ii) an irrevocable commercial letter of credit for large equipment purchases such as Malatang cooking stations, self-serve broth dispensers, and mechanical packaging units. For MT103 instructions, the IC's Samoan bank will transmit funds from the USD operational account to the Chinese beneficiary's bank using purpose code 'FRAN' / 'Royalties' or the CBS-mapped BOP service category, attaching invoice numbers and the reference to the underlying license agreement. For letter-of-credit transactions, the Samoan bank issues the LC at the IC's request backed by funds or a cash margin, with documents including commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and quality certificate. Economic substance scope: Samoa International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 apply to the IP business of master licensing self-serve broth bowl franchises. The IC must have a physical office in Samoa, a sufficient number of full-time employees, adequate operating expenditure, core decision-making activity in Samoa, and must file an Annual Economic Substance Declaration.
