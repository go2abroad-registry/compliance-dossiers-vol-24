Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), official portal https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) must engage a licensed local registered agent to incorporate under the International Companies Act 1987 and obtain a Certificate of Incorporation. The registered agent then files an application with the Samoa Revenue Authority through the SRA Taxpayer Registration portal or in person at the SRA headquarters in Apia, submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered agent confirmation, director and shareholder particulars, and proof of registered office in Samoa. The SRA issues a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and, where relevant, a VAT registration number if the IC opts for voluntary VAT registration. For International Companies recognised under the International Companies Act, the tax identification is administered through the SRA's non-resident taxpayer registry for offshore entities, with the registered agent designated as the filing intermediary.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

International Companies are not subject to Samoa income tax on offshore business income and therefore no check-the-box election is statutorily required for Samoan tax purposes. However, the IC may voluntarily elect to register for Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST) under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 1998, as amended, in order to reclaim VAGST on qualifying local inputs or to issue tax invoices for locally consumed supplies. Hybrid mismatch elections are not applicable because Samoa has not adopted OECD hybrid mismatch rules in its domestic International Companies Act, and the IC is an opaque, exempt entity that may nonetheless be treated as a controlled foreign corporation or flow-through entity under the shareholder's home jurisdiction; such foreign classification is governed by foreign law and does not alter the Samoan entity's status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Samoa qualify for zero-rating under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act if the goods are physically exported from Samoa within the meaning of the Act, the supplier has evidence of exportation, and the goods are supplied under a contract for sale or exchange where title passes to a person outside Samoa. The zero-rating criterion for a containerized bitumen/asphalt wholesale trading operation requires that the goods are removed from Samoa by the overseas buyer or on behalf of the overseas buyer, through a qualifying customs export declaration. Offshore services supplied to a non-resident recipient outside Samoa are zero-rated where the services are supplied to a person who is not in Samoa at the time of supply and the services are not physically performed in Samoa; however, International Companies engaged in offshore trading activities generally fall outside the VAGST net altogether and do not require a refund claim because they are not registered for VAGST. A registered IC may claim input tax credits or refunds on any VAGST paid on local supplies acquired for the purpose of making zero-rated exports, subject to SRA verification and the filing of a VAGST return for the relevant period.

  • Customs Export Declaration submitted electronically via Samoa Customs ASYCUDA World
  • Bill of Lading (original or certified true copy) in the name of the Samoan exporting entity or its agent
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods as containerized penetration grade bitumen/asphalt under HS 2713.20 or 2714.90, as applicable
  • Packing list evidencing container counts and weights
  • Evidence of payment or inward remittance certificate (or bank SWIFT/TT advice) showing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Certificate of origin (if required by the receiving customs jurisdiction)
  • Copy of the registered agent's or exporter's VAGST registration certificate (only if registered and making zero-rated supplies)

There is currently no mandatory real-time electronic fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for VAGST invoices in Samoa. Taxpayers must issue compliant VAGST tax invoices containing prescribed particulars: supplier name, TIN, invoice serial number, date, recipient details, description of goods, quantity, price, VAGST amount, and total consideration. The SRA is progressively upgrading its electronic filing and taxpayer portal, but no API-based clearance or government-authorised digital fiscal stamp is legally mandated. Customs export documentation is processed through the Samoa Customs Service using the ASYCUDA World electronic customs management system, which provides electronic export declarations and facilitates cross-agency data exchange with the SRA.

The SRA may conduct a VAT/VAGST audit or customs duty verification and compare export declarations, bills of lading, shipping manifests, and offshore payment records. If an exporter fraudulently zero-rates goods that are not exported, or fails to repatriate export proceeds within the applicable statutory or administrative deadline, the SRA may assess the unpaid VAGST at 15% plus interest, impose penalties under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act (including late payment penalties, evasion penalties, and in serious cases criminal fines), and retrospectively claw back input credits claimed. Claims of fraudulent zero-rating may result in prosecution before the Samoan courts with fines up to the amount prescribed by the Tax Administration Act and imprisonment in aggravated cases. Additionally, the customs authorities may seize or forfeit goods and impose customs penalties for false declarations, and the Financial Intelligence Unit may apply anti-money-laundering penalties where the arrangement discloses proceeds of crime or a fictitious export transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a managed-float currency pegged to a composite basket of the currencies of Samoa's principal trading partners. Current-account transactions for bona fide trade in goods are fully convertible through authorized dealers (commercial banks) against documentary proof comprising invoices, packing lists, bills of lading and related payment instructions; bitumen/asphalt imports require no import license, and no product-specific restriction applies to ISIC 4661. The capital account is restricted for resident entities, but Samoa International Companies (ICs) are treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes; therefore WS_IC may maintain foreign-currency transaction accounts (USD, NZD, AUD, EUR), fund them from any non-resident source, and remit dividends, shareholder-loan principal, and trade settlements without prior Central Bank approval provided all flows are routed through a licensed authorized dealer, correctly narratived, and reported in the dealer's periodic exchange-control returns. If the IC were to transact in WST or receive WST from resident Samoan customers, conversion to foreign currency at the authorized-dealer window would require current-account document verification. The principal profit-repatriation hurdle is not exchange control but compliance documentation: because the IC is tax-exempt under the International Companies Act 1987, no Ministry of Revenue tax clearance is required for dividend remittances, yet the paying bank will demand a board dividend declaration, internally reviewed or audited financial statements, an up-to-date beneficial-ownership register, and a source-of-funds confirmation letter from the directors in order to discharge obligations under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa's exchange-control guidelines require resident exporters to bring export proceeds into Samoa through an authorized dealer within six months of the shipment date; proceeds are normally credited to a prescribed Foreign Currency Account and a mandatory conversion portion (commonly a 50% band per credit, as directed by the Central Bank) must be surrendered into WST unless a standing exemption has been granted. WS_IC, being a non-resident International Company, sits outside the surrender and conversion obligation provided the bitumen sales are invoiced in a foreign currency (USD) and title passes outside Samoa under CIF/FOB destination terms; the IC must nevertheless hold the Central Bank of Samoa's standard non-resident IC foreign-currency classification letter issued through SIFA and must maintain export sales contracts, commercial invoices, bills of lading, and reconciled foreign-currency account statements for inspection by the authorized dealer and SIFA.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), Apia, operating as the consolidated monetary authority and exchange control administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; day-to-day supervisory control over onshore commercial banks rests with the Banking Supervision Department, while prudential oversight of offshore banks and of the International Company regime is exercised jointly with the Samoa International Financial Authority (SIFA) and the Registrar of International & Foreign Companies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, with material legal constraints. Samoa has no electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime; unlicensed overseas digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Mercury) cannot issue a Samoa-domiciled account and generally reject entities lacking a physical presence, local tax ID, or utility bill in the EMI's operating jurisdiction. A Samoa IC may be eligible for a foreign EMI account only if it maintains an operating subsidiary or qualifying proof-of-address in that jurisdiction, and even then the EMI will apply enhanced scrutiny to an offshore/tax-exempt structure, apply lower transaction velocity limits, and may suspend funds pending beneficial-ownership screens. The architecture should therefore treat EMIs exclusively as a secondary collection vehicle for open-account sales in the EU/UK corridor, never as the primary treasury rail; all core inbound and outbound flows must clear through a licensed authorized dealer in Samoa to remain reconcilable with the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007, and SIFA expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial equity and shareholder loans must be remitted to WS_IC's BSP Samoa USD account from the parent/shareholder bank outside Samoa (e.g., Singapore, Hong Kong, EU, UAE) via SWIFT MT103 using a direct-investment purpose narrative; field 70 must carry a wording such as 'INV/LOAN – WS_IC – SHAREHOLDER CAPITAL INJECTION – BITUMEN TRADING'. The receiving authorized dealer completes the Central Bank of Samoa's exchange-control declaration form for any single inward credit equivalent to WST 500,000 or more. Verification by the bank comprises the corporate extract, board resolution approving the capital injection and signatory mandate, shareholder register, source-of-funds evidence from the remitting bank, registered-agent confirmation of beneficial owners, and, for a shareholder loan exceeding USD 2,000,000, a signed facility deed with an arm's-length interest rate. The SWIFT GPI tracking reference and the full MT103 copy must be archived for AML audit; no prior Central Bank approval is required for non-resident IC capital injections, but equity versus debt must be clearly labelled in the corporate records to prevent future attribution and thin-capitalization challenges.The primary clearing rails are (1) irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit opened by buyer banks in Nigeria/UAE/Asia under UCP 600, transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT707 (MT710 where the issuing bank is not a member), and confirmed at BSP Samoa or Pacific Commercial Bank; the IC presents the full documentary set under the LC and is paid upon compliance through the USD nostro via CHIPS/Fedwire. (2) Direct telegraphic transfers for open-account settlements executed as SWIFT MT103 with field 23B 'SSTD' against matched invoices. (3) Pre-export finance, whereby the IC assigns LC proceeds to the financing bank using a SWIFT MT760 guarantee where required. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, with material legal constraints. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a Samoa International Company (IC) on all worldwide offshore-source income; Samoa's resident corporate headline rate is 25% under the Income Tax Act 2015, with a 15% concessional rate available to qualifying manufacturing, agriculture, fishing, tourism and ICT companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Samoa does not levy withholding tax on dividends paid by an exempt International Company; distributions of offshore profits to non-resident shareholders are made free of any Samoan tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa does not maintain a formal Local File or Master File documentation threshold for exempt ICs; however, the Commissioner may adjust intercompany pricing to reflect the arm's-length standard under the Income Tax Act 2015. For economic substance purposes, an IC with gross income from relevant activities exceeding WST 250,000 per annum must file a full Economic Substance Return; ICs at or below that de minimis threshold file a reduced notification only. A bitumen wholesale trading entity (ISIC 4661) above the threshold must document its Samoan value chain, including the fully burdened distribution margin, personnel costs, premises costs, and sampling of comparable independent distribution margins.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 requires an IC to satisfy Samoan economic substance where it carries on a 'relevant activity'. Wholesale trading of drummed and bagged penetration grade bitumen falls within the 'distribution and service centre' category. The IC must demonstrate in Samoa: (i) a physical office; (ii) at least one full-time locally resident executive who manages procurement, pricing, invoicing, and logistics; (iii) operating expenditure in Samoa proportionate to the wholesale margin; and (iv) strategic decisions taken by the board at meetings held in Samoa. Pure back-to-back contracting, remote approval chains, and reliance on foreign fulfilment without local decision-making will fail the test, trigger penalties, and expose the structure to spontaneous exchange of information to the ultimate parent's tax jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Economic Substance Return

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019, a false return is an offence punishable by a fine of up to WST 100,000 plus daily default penalties, revocation of the certificate of compliance, and mandatory spontaneous exchange of information to the ultimate parent jurisdiction; under the tax administration framework, intentional misstatement attracts shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Each director who signs or causes a false ESR or income declaration is personally liable to the penalty regime; fraudulent declarations additionally expose directors to criminal prosecution, imprisonment of up to 5 years, disqualification from managing any Samoa company, and civil restitution to the Revenue for the evaded tax.
Shell Company / Absence of Substance

Statutory sanction: The entity is rebuttably presumed to lack the substance required for IC tax-exempt status; the Registrar will decline renewal and strike the company off, and the asset-protection benefit collapses because assets located in Samoa vest in the Crown; the full ownership and financial profile will be exchanged under AEOI/CbCR frameworks to the foreign parent's tax authority.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who append signatures without exercising fiduciary oversight face statutory fines and disqualification; beneficial owners using a shell to evade the home jurisdiction face reassessment, GAAR recharacterization, civil recovery, and coordinated enforcement through mutual assistance in tax collection.
AML-CTF / Beneficial Ownership Registry Breach

Statutory sanction: Proceedings under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Financing of Terrorism Act 2018: business-registration suspension, asset-freezing orders issued by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit, fines of up to WST 1,000,000 for the entity together with confiscation, and independent fines against the registered agent for failure to file suspicious activity reports.

Director personal liability: Directors who conceal the principal, sign false KYC attestations, or knowingly move proceeds of customs fraud on asphalt imports face imprisonment of up to 7 years for money laundering; civil confiscation may reach personal assets, and the court may impose travel bans pending the conclusion of the investigation.
Aggressive Structuring / Treaty and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: Samoa's participation in the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance enables spontaneous exchange of ESR, banking, and beneficial-ownership data; the foreign parent's tax authority applies its general anti-avoidance rule and transfer-pricing adjustments, reverses the profits, imposes understatement penalties of up to 100% of the tax avoided, and denies treaty benefits to the conduit IC.

Director personal liability: Where the foreign authority pierces the corporate veil, controlling directors and shareholders are jointly and severally liable for reassessed tax, statutory interest, and penalties; cross-border mutual assistance in recovery permits attachment of personal assets and travel bans in both the foreign jurisdiction and Samoa.
