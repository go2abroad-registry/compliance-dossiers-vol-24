Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa, combining the Inland Revenue Division and Samoa Customs; official taxpayer portal at www.revenue.gov.ws.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not operating as an exempt International Company must first complete business registration with the Samoa Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour through the Companies Office, then submit the Ministry for Revenue Taxpayer Registration form (paper at the Apia office or via the MfR e-services portal) together with: certified certificate of incorporation or equivalent, constitution, registered-office certificate, identification of directors/shareholders, and notarized power of attorney for the Samoan representative. The MfR then issues a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and, where taxable activity has Samoan nexus, a Value Added Goods and Services Tax registration certificate. An exempt International Company under the International Companies Act 1987 may obtain an administrative TIN through the same non-resident channel, but it is not required to file income tax or VAGST returns for exempt offshore operations and no VAGST registration is required for zero-rated export-only supplies.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign entity not operating as an exempt International Company must first complete business registration with the Samoa Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour through the Companies Office, then submit the Ministry for Revenue Taxpayer Registration form (paper at the Apia office or via the MfR e-services portal) together with: certified certificate of incorporation or equivalent, constitution, registered-office certificate, identification of directors/shareholders, and notarized power of attorney for the Samoan representative. The MfR then issues a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and, where taxable activity has Samoan nexus, a Value Added Goods and Services Tax registration certificate. An exempt International Company under the International Companies Act 1987 may obtain an administrative TIN through the same non-resident channel, but it is not required to file income tax or VAGST returns for exempt offshore operations and no VAGST registration is required for zero-rated export-only supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

An International Company may, by special resolution filed with the Registrar of International Companies, elect to convert into an ordinary local company, thereby voluntarily subjecting itself to income tax at the standard corporate rate (27%) and to the standard VAGST regime. Samoa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is governed by the International Companies Act 1987 and the Income Tax Act 2012. No separate hybrid-mismatch election is available; Samoa's BEPS-aligned rules treat exempt International Companies as non-resident for treaty and controlled-foreign-company analysis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of taxable supplies made in Samoa over any consecutive 12-month period; optional registration permitted for lower-turnover exporters

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically removed from Samoa as export cargo, and supplies made directly for the operation or maintenance of international aircraft, including bonded in-flight meals delivered to the airline ramp at the airport, qualify for zero-rating. The supplier must hold documentary evidence of physical exportation, of the non-resident recipient's status, and must satisfy the Ministry for Revenue that the goods were not supplied to a Samoan resident for consumption in Samoa. Excess input VAGST attributable to zero-rated export supplies is refundable, subject to documentary substantiation and the statutory refund process.

  • Samoa Customs electronic export declaration (ASYCUDA World export entry) covering the HS in-flight catering product classification
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical exportation from Samoa
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing foreign-currency receipt from the airline counterparty
  • Certificate of origin, if required in the airline's import jurisdiction

No real-time fiscal clearance, SAF-T filing, or digital stamping mandate exists in Samoa. Registered suppliers must issue paper or electronic VAGST tax invoices containing the supplier name, address and TIN, sequential invoice number, date, description of supplies, the zero-rated and 15% split, and the amount of tax charged. Customs export entries are filed electronically through the Samoa Customs ASYCUDA World system, which serves as the fiscal clearance reference for export zero-rating.

The Ministry for Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims for up to seven years from the end of the relevant tax period. A shortfall penalty of up to 100% of the unpaid tax, daily late-payment penalties, and retrospective reassessment at 15% apply to unjustified or inadequately documented zero-rating. Fraudulent claims expose the entity to clawback of refunds, cancellation of VAGST registration, monetary penalties, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Tax Administration Act 2019 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992, with imprisonment terms for individuals involved in deliberate evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) operates under a non-fully-convertible, administratively controlled regime. Current-account payments, including trade settlements, freight, fuel, catering raw-material imports, and demonstrable service fees, are routinely approved with documented underlying contracts and tax clearance. Profit and dividend remittances by a tax-exempt International Company are allowable only where audited profits, substance evidence, and the registered agent's good-standing certificate are presented; capital-account transactions (equity injection, shareholder loans, capital repatriation, and any pledge of local assets) require prior CBS approval on a case-by-case basis. In practice, foreign-currency accounts (USD, EUR, AUD, NZD) may be held only with CBS consent, and WST is fully convertible for approved trade payments but not freely available offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must receive export proceeds within 3 months from the date of export and must immediately offer the foreign currency to an authorized dealer. Bonded in-flight catering deliveries to airline operators are treated as export-like supplies requiring the operator to obtain a CBS 'retention of export proceeds' consent so that USD-denominated airline invoices may be retained in a designated foreign-currency operating account; any non-approved balance must be converted into WST within the statutory period. Failure to comply triggers exchange-control penalties and freezing of banking facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Falefa, Apia, established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; foreign exchange is administered via the Exchange Control Regulations 1984 and the Financial Institutions Act 2005. All cross-border payment and FX transactions must be routed through a licensed authorized dealer. Accounts for non-resident International Companies require explicit CBS permission for foreign-currency retention and outward remittance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Samoa has no dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) or digital banking licensing regime; the CBS licenses only commercial banks and licensed money-remittance businesses under the Financial Institutions Act 2005. An offshore digital EMI cannot act as the primary treasury account holder in Samoa and should be used only as an upstream collection vehicle outside Samoa; all settlement, payroll, and FX execution must pass through a tier-1 authorized dealer bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a valid Samoa International Company (statutory exemption under International Companies Act 1987 (ICA 1987) s.183); standard resident corporate headline rate is 27% under the Income Tax Act 2014 (Samoa)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends and other distributions paid by a valid International Company to non-residents (ICA 1987 s.183(2); no duty to withhold); 15% final withholding tax applies only where a domestic Samoa company distributes dividends to non-residents under the Income Tax Act 2014

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File / Master File monetary threshold exists under Samoa law; the Income Tax Act 2014 applies the arm's-length principle to all related-party cross-border transactions, and the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 requires all ICs to maintain substance records irrespective of value; as best practice, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation should be prepared where annual related-party catering procurement, management, or royalty flows exceed WS$1,000,000 per annum, given the high audit risk of airport-located taxpayers

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Samoa, having removed its preferential IC regime from OECD scrutiny, enacted economic substance rules through the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 and ICA 1987 ss.13A-13D. An IC deriving income from a 'relevant activity', which includes provision of services such as airline inflight catering, must in any income year: (i) be managed and controlled in Samoa (board meetings held and minutes kept in Samoa); (ii) maintain adequate physical premises, a resident workforce, and operating expenditure in Samoa proportionate to the catering activity; and (iii) carry on core income-generating activities (food production and ramp logistics) in Samoa. A confidential economic substance declaration must be filed annually with the Registrar; failure leads to penalty, reclassification, or deregistration, and the revenue authority may upwardly adjust profits at the 27% rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Accounting

Statutory sanction: Assessment at 27% on the full concealed profit plus an evasion penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall and interest at 2% per month; criminal prosecution may result in a fine of up to WS$100,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the registered agent are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax where they directed or assisted the concealment; the court may impose a departure prohibition order, freeze personal bank accounts, and sentence directors to custodial terms; a conviction additionally disqualifies the director from managing any Samoa company for 10 years.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty of up to WS$100,000 per relevant activity year; the Commissioner may reclassify the IC as a resident taxpayer, taxing worldwide income at 27% with no allowance for the IC exemption; the Registrar may cancel the Certificate of Incorporation and publish the name of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the re-assessed corporate tax where the substance declaration was knowingly false; the court may strike the director from the Samoa Register of Directors for 3 years, issue a travel ban, and order restitution of profits extracted as management fees within the look-back period.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory penalties under the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU): up to WS$100,000 for the entity and WS$25,000 for the licensed agent per violation; the FIU may suspend the IC's bank accounts and file a suspicious transaction report; criminal conviction can attract up to 5 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal fines, asset freezing orders, and, where money laundering is proven, imprisonment; the registered agent's licence may be revoked for enabling the concealment, and the IC is compulsorily wound up with directors bearing the costs.
Unlawful Local Business Operation (Exceeding IC Licence Scope)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to WS$50,000 under ICA 1987 and a cease-and-desist order; each month of unlicensed operation is a separate offence; the Commissioner may additionally withdraw the IC's tax exemption and assess all catering revenue at 27% as Samoa-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly trade without the licence are personally liable for the unpaid annual government fees, licence penalties, and strike-off costs; the Minister may petition the court for 5-year disqualification of directors from holding any ICA company office and seek enforcement of the penalty against personal assets.
