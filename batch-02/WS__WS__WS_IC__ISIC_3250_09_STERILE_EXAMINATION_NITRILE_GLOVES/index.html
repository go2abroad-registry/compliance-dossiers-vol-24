Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (Matagaluega o Tupe Maua), Government of Samoa; official portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws. Administering the Income Tax Act 2015, the Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST) Act 2008, the Customs Act, and the Tax Administration Act 2012.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company obtains its Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the following procedure: (1) incorporation by a licensed registered agent with the Registrar of International Companies, Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), under the International Companies Act 1987; (2) submission of Form TIN-01 (Taxpayer Registration) to the Ministry for Revenue, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered agent certificate, proof of registered office in Apia, certified passport copies of directors/shareholders, and the company's constitutional documents; (3) issuance of a 9-digit TIN by the Ministry for Revenue within 3-5 business days; (4) where required, separate registration for VAGST (Form VR-1) if the company makes taxable supplies in Samoa exceeding the annual threshold, and obtaining a Customs importer/exporter code from Samoa Customs. The International Company is exempt from income tax on its offshore business and pays only the annual international company government fee to SIFA.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned Samoa International Company obtains its Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the following procedure: (1) incorporation by a licensed registered agent with the Registrar of International Companies, Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), under the International Companies Act 1987; (2) submission of Form TIN-01 (Taxpayer Registration) to the Ministry for Revenue, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered agent certificate, proof of registered office in Apia, certified passport copies of directors/shareholders, and the company's constitutional documents; (3) issuance of a 9-digit TIN by the Ministry for Revenue within 3-5 business days; (4) where required, separate registration for VAGST (Form VR-1) if the company makes taxable supplies in Samoa exceeding the annual threshold, and obtaining a Customs importer/exporter code from Samoa Customs. The International Company is exempt from income tax on its offshore business and pays only the annual international company government fee to SIFA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a check-the-box or OECD hybrid-mismatch election regime. The International Company automatically enjoys statutory tax exemption under section 196 of the International Companies Act 1987, covering income tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, estate duty, gift duty, and withholding tax on distributions to non-residents. Available statutory elections include: (i) voluntary VAGST registration under the VAGST Act 2008 for exporters or persons carrying on taxable activities in Samoa below the WST 100,000 compulsory threshold; (ii) elective conversion or continuation of an International Company into a domestic limited company under the Companies Act 2001, thereby subjecting the entity to the territorial Income Tax Act 2015 and standard Samoan company taxation; (iii) election to file an economic substance declaration under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Amendment Act for relevant activities; and (iv) group registration for VAGST purposes where related companies carry on taxable activities in Samoa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (Samoa Tala) of taxable supplies per annum; compulsory registration applies to any person carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa whose annual turnover exceeds this threshold. Offshore-to-offshore business of an International Company is generally outside the VAGST net as non-Samoa supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Samoa by a VAGST-registered exporter are zero-rated under the VAGST Act 2008 where: (i) the goods physically depart Samoa within 28 days of supply; (ii) the exporter retains conclusive evidence of export in the form of a Customs export declaration and bill of lading or air waybill; (iii) the consideration is receivable in convertible foreign currency and is not diverted for domestic use; and (iv) the goods are not subsequently returned to Samoa or consumed locally. Offshore services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Samoa, including international trading, logistics coordination, and re-invoicing of nitrile glove consignments, are also zero-rated or outside the scope of VAGST, provided the services are not performed in Samoa for a Samoan resident. Input VAGST attributable to zero-rated export activities is creditable, and the excess is refundable by the Commissioner of Revenue, typically within 30 days of lodgment of the return, subject to satisfactory substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document lodged electronically via ASYCUDA World)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and VAGST tax invoice
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)

Samoa currently imposes no mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based clearance mandate. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices within 28 days of supply and retain records for 7 years; electronic invoices are accepted. Customs export declarations must be lodged electronically via the ASYCUDA World single-window platform (Customs Export Entry / Single Administrative Document), and VAGST returns (Form VAGST-01) are e-filed through the Ministry for Revenue Tax Administration System portal.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to substantive audit by the Ministry for Revenue and Samoa Customs. Under the Tax Administration Act 2012, failure to substantiate zero-rated export treatment results in assessment of VAGST at the standard 15% rate plus shortfall penalties of 20% for lack of reasonable care, 75% for gross carelessness, or 150% for intentional evasion, together with late payment penalties of 1.5% per month compounded and statutory interest. Fraudulent export zero-rating, including circular container movements, false bills of lading, phantom shipments, or unrepatriated proceeds, constitutes a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 7 years, with retrospective clawback of input credits and assessed VAGST for periods of up to 7 years, and no time limit in the case of fraud. Additionally, the offending International Company may lose confidentiality protections under the International Companies Act 1987 and face SIFA administrative sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a partially convertible currency regime for the Samoan Tala (WST). Current-account payments for genuine imports of raw materials and trade-related services are screened by authorized dealers and are generally permissible upon submission of supporting commercial documents and Exchange Control Form A. Capital-account inflows, such as equity injections and shareholder loans into a resident Samoan branch of an International Company, require prior CBS Exchange Control approval unless routed through a licensed External Account. Profit remittances and dividend distributions by a tax-exempt International Company (WS_IC) are treated as offshore transactions under the International Companies Act and are not subject to Samoan withholding tax, but the beneficial owners must still satisfy CRS/FATCA reporting, and the bank must verify that the funds have clear provenance. The Tala is not freely convertible offshore; there is no liquid offshore WST market, so all conversions for operational purposes must be executed through a CBS-authorized dealer at the daily indicative rate. In practice, international treasury efficiency is achieved by maintaining a foreign-currency External Account and by settling cross-border obligations in USD or CNY outside Samoa.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBS Exchange Control Rules, exporters of medical PPE, including sterile powder-free nitrile examination gloves under ISIC 3250, must repatriate the full foreign-currency invoice value to Samoa through an authorized dealer within 90 days from the bill of lading date unless a prior exemption authority to retain proceeds in an External Account has been granted by the CBS. Upon repatriation, unless the exporter holds a written foreign-currency retention facility approved by the dealer and the CBS, 100% of the surrendered proceeds must be converted into WST at the prevailing bank-buying rate. For shipments settled by documentary letters of credit routed through a Samoan clearing bank, the permitted settlement window is extended to 180 days to accommodate the LC maturity, but the conversion requirement remains mandatory at the point of liquidation. An exporter may apply for standing exemption to retain USD export receipts in a designated External Account for direct repayment of imported nitrile polymer, machinery installments, and foreign freight, subject to quarterly verification by the Exchange Control Division.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) acting as the Exchange Control Authority pursuant to the Exchange Control Act 1970, the Exchange Control Regulations 1974, and the Money Laundering and Prevention of Money Laundering Act 2007, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Samoa does not license electronic money institutions as authorized dealers under the Exchange Control Act. Offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully perform WST conversion, export-proceeds surrender, or trade-facilitation clearing because they are not connected to the Central Bank of Samoa Settlement System (CBSS). An EMI can be used peripherally as a receivable-collection tool for e-commerce PPE sales outside Samoa, subject to the funds being swept directly to a CBS-licensed operational account and subject to enhanced due diligence by the Samoan clearing bank. The overall feasibility rating is Low to Medium only where the EMI has robust correspondent banking through ANZ or BSP and holds no WST liability in Samoa.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward equity contributions and shareholder loans for the WS_IC must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the bank’s Samoan clearing code, quoting purpose code P1101 (Investment Capital under International Companies Act) and supported by a board resolution and share-allotment form. Where the investing entity is a Samoan resident or where the influx will be converted into WST to pay local wages, a prior Exchange Control Application Form (EC-A) must be approved by CBS within 5 business days. For investments above WST 100,000, the receiving bank must issue a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate or Capital Import Certificate to the CBS prior to applying funds to the WS_IC’s Capital Escrow Account. If the capital is held in USD for onward trade payments, it will remain in a Designated External Account; conversion to WST for local disbursement will be executed only against ECB-confirmed invoices.Outbound trade settlements for raw-material imports and freight will be cleared through the Samoan banking system using SWIFT MT103/MT103+ for TT open-account transactions, commercial standby and sight letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT701) for large polymer shipments, and documentary collections under URC 522 for mid-sized distributor deliveries. All payments exceeding USD 50,000 must be supported by a completed Exchange Control Form B, the underlying purchase order, a customs-supplier invoice, and a copy of the import license for nitrile polymer. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa applies the OECD arm's length standard to resident taxpayers under the Tax Administration Act 2015. The tax-exempt IC is outside the Samoan TP net, but the contract-manufacturing affiliate in the production jurisdiction must hold contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation where related-party turnover exceeds the local threshold (commonly the lesser of WST 5,000,000 aggregate related-party turnover or WST 1,000,000 intra-group transactions, aligned with OECD TP documentation practice).

Economic substance scope: Samoa has not enacted BVI/Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, OECD Global Forum membership and the 2008 International Companies Amendment Act impose substantive elements: licensed registered agent, registered office in Samoa, prohibition of bearer shares, and retention of accounting records at the registered office. For the nitrile-glove trade flow, the IC must evidence board decisions, contract negotiation, invoicing and cash settlement outside Samoa; a purely passive letterbox IC risks recharacterization as a sham in the counterparty jurisdiction and exclusion from banking and payment-rail facilities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration (Samoa domestic law)

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalties of 100-200% of tax evaded; monetary fines of up to WST 500,000 against the company; criminal conviction with imprisonment of up to 5 years; deregistration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall where funds were distributed as dividends before settlement; travel restrictions and disqualification orders may apply.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the arrangement; denial of the exemption and withholding relief; imposition of Samoan corporate tax at 27% plus penalties if Samoan-source income is found; strike-off by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Judicial lifting of the corporate veil: directors ordered to disgorge diverted profits; personal liability for unpaid taxes; arrest warrants or travel bans pending foreign investigations.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML/CFT Act 2018)

Statutory sanction: Company fines up to WST 2,000,000; confiscation of proceeds; revocation of the registered agent's license; freezing of bank accounts; criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 14 years for responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability for officers who knowingly participate in laundering; asset forfeiture, worldwide travel bans and extradition exposure under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Regulatory Non-Compliance with the International Companies Act 1987

Statutory sanction: Late penalty charges; Gazette strike-off and deregistration; restoration fees of WST 500 plus all arrears; director disqualification in egregious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors remain personally liable for company debts contracted after the strike-off point; management of a struck-off IC is an offense punishable by fine and imprisonment; travel bans may assist creditor recovery.
