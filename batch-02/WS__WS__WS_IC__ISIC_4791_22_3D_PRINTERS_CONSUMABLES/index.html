Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Motootua, Apia, Samoa; official administration and e-filing portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws (SRA e-Services). International company incorporation, licensing, and annual fees are separately administered by the Registrar of International Companies within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Nexus screening: A Samoan International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from Samoan income and capital taxes on offshore profits; TIN procurement is obligatory only where the IC (i) makes VAGST-taxable supplies in Samoa, (ii) physically clears goods through Samoan Customs (import VAGST), or (iii) otherwise incurs a Samoan tax liability (e.g. licensed local business). Step 2 - Application: Submit the SRA Application for Tax Registration via SRA e-Services or in person at the SRA taxpayer service centre, attaching (a) the MCIL-issued Certificate of Incorporation of the International Company, (b) certified copy of the charter and memorandum of association, (c) certified copy of the registered office and registered agent confirmation, (d) register of directors and shareholders, (e) letter of appointment of the local registered agent, and (f) a short description of the online retail scope (ISIC 4791). Step 3 - Issuance: SRA validates the application and issues a 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN), normally within 10 business days; the same TIN serves income tax, VAGST, and customs accounts. Step 4 - VAGST registration: where annual taxable turnover in Samoa reaches WST 150,000, or where the IC elects voluntary registration, file the VAGST registration within 21 days of the obligation arising and obtain a VAGST registration certificate. No minimum capital, local shareholding, director residency, or physical presence is required for an International Company; the registered agent satisfies statutory presence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The Samoa International Company operates as a tax-exempt offshore entity under the International Companies Act 1987 (s.175 exemption), outside the scope of the Samoan Income Tax Act 2012. It is treated as non-resident and non-domiciled for Samoan tax purposes, attracting zero income tax on offshore business profits, no capital gains tax, no gift or estate duty, no stamp duty, and no withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees paid to non-residents. Samoan-source taxation would arise only where the IC obtains a licence to carry on business in Samoa, where VAGST applies to domestic supplies, or where goods physically enter Samoa and import VAGST and customs duty become exigible.

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through/entity-classification regime, nor a hybrid-mismatch deduction election. The statutory options that a Samoan IC may elect are: (1) Voluntary VAGST registration under s.13 of the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 1999, permitting a person making taxable supplies below the WST 150,000 threshold to register and recover input credits; (2) Election to convert an International Company into a domestic company under the International Companies Act 1987, which forfeits the IC tax-exempt status and subjects the entity to normal corporate income tax on worldwide income; (3) Application to the Registrar of International Companies for a licence authorising the IC to carry on business in Samoa, triggering tax residence-type exposure on Samoan-source income and full VAGST obligations; and (4) Election of accounting periods and cash or invoice basis for VAGST returns where sanctioned by the SRA. No anti-hybrid or transparent-entity election exists in Samoan domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 150,000 annual value of taxable supplies made in Samoa (VAGST Act 1999, s.12); voluntary registration is available below the threshold and registration is mandatory for a non-resident person making supplies in Samoa that exceed the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods, including desktop 3D printers, resin filaments, and maker consumables, are zero-rated at 0% VAGST where the supply is a direct export, namely the supplier exports the goods from Samoa within 28 days after the date of supply and retains conclusive proof of physical exportation. Services qualify as zero-rated exported services where the recipient is a non-resident, the services are delivered for the benefit of a person outside Samoa, and the services are consumed wholly outside Samoa. In the typical Samoan IC drop-ship model, where 3D printers and filaments are procured from overseas factories and shipped directly to foreign end-customers without the goods ever entering Samoa, the supply is outside the scope of Samoan VAGST, because no supply occurs in Samoa; in that case no VAGST registration, zero-rating claim, or input credit arises. Where goods are physically imported into Samoa and subsequently re-exported, import VAGST is payable at the border and can be reclaimed as an input credit against the zero-rated export supply, provided the exporter holds the full export documentation bundle.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World electronic export entry) endorsed by the Samoa Customs Service
  • Commercial sales invoice showing full goods description, quantity, price, Incoterms, and overseas customer identity
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical carriage from Samoa to the stated overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice proving receipt of the foreign currency export proceeds
  • Purchase order or sales contract, packing list, and any SRA-requested zero-rating support schedule

No real-time fiscal clearance, compulsory digital stamping, or nationwide electronic invoicing mandate (B2B or B2C) is currently operative in Samoa. Compliance is return-driven: VAGST-registered persons must file monthly VAGST returns and remit tax electronically through the SRA e-Services portal, and must issue tax invoices, credit notes, and adjustment notes satisfying the content and record-keeping requirements of the VAGST Act 1999 and the Tax Administration Act 2012. Physical export and import transactions are processed through the ASYCUDA World electronic single-window of the Samoa Customs Service; the customs export declaration is the principal control document for export zero-rating. The absence of e-invoicing clearance does not relax the documentary burden at audit, and all backup documentation must be retained for the statutory record period.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to ex-post audit by the Samoa Revenue Authority under the Tax Administration Act 2012 and the VAGST Act 1999. Where the export documentation bundle is incomplete, internally inconsistent, or the goods cannot be matched to an endorsed customs export entry, the SRA will re-characterise the supply as a standard-rated taxable supply, assess VAGST at 15%, and recover the tax together with statutory late-payment penalties that accrue monthly, and shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax avoided for intentional evasion. False zero-rating claims, forged customs entries, fictitious bills of lading, and sham export structures constitute criminal offences attracting fines and imprisonment, retrospective reassessments for extended periods where fraud or evasion is established, and clawback of any VAGST refunds already paid, with interest. The SRA additionally applies anti-avoidance powers and may cancel or suspend VAGST registration and, where applicable, recommend suspension of an International Company licence for serious or repeated non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a managed float with active CBS market intervention. Current account transactions through authorized dealers are generally permitted without prior CBS approval, except that amounts exceeding the prescribed threshold require an exchange control declaration on CBS Form EC-1. Capital account inflows and outflows, including equity subscription and dividend repatriation, require prior exchange control approval unless covered by a general permission applicable to International Companies. The WST is not an internationally delivered currency; trade and treasury settlements must clear through USD, AUD, EUR, or TWD correspondent accounts. Dividend profit remittance from the tax-exempt 'WS_IC' is not subject to Samoan withholding tax, but the payor bank will require audited financial statements evidencing distributable reserves and a CBS clearance for exemption from exchange control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1972, and applicable CBS Notices, all export proceeds arising from online retail sales of goods must be received through an authorized dealer within 90 days from the date of shipment or provision of service. E-commerce receivables from Stripe and Shopify Payments must be repatriated into the IC's CBS-authorized external foreign currency account without unauthorized diversion to personal or third-party accounts. There is no mandatory fixed percentage conversion of export receipts into WST; however, the exporter must either offer the receipts for conversion into WST or justify their retention in an external foreign currency account on bona fide trade grounds, with all supporting invoices, logistics waybills, and platform settlement reports retained for a minimum of seven years.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), constituted under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, acting as the exchange control and monetary authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2021.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorized to carry on deposit-taking, money transmission, or treasury servicing in Samoa without a CBS license under the Financial Institutions Act 1996. Moreover, neither Stripe nor Shopify Payments supports Samoa as a direct merchant jurisdiction, so the compliant treasury architecture requires a merchant-of-record, payment facilitator, or intermediary e-commerce holding entity incorporated in a Stripe-/Shopify-supported jurisdiction (e.g., Hong Kong, Singapore, or United Kingdom). All net collections from such facilitators must be settled via SWIFT into the Samoa IC's external account at a CBS-licensed authorized dealer; holding operating liquidity at any unlicensed offshore EMI creates a regulatory breach, FX evasion presumption, and automatic rejection during Samoan bank KYC underwriting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory transfer pricing documentation thresholds are specified in Samoa's Income Tax Act; international companies are outside the income tax net. If an IC is reclassified as a Samoa resident due to actual management and control in Samoa or a permanent establishment, the Ministry of Revenue may require Local File and Master File on-demand for related-party cross-border transactions exceeding WST 750,000 in annual gross payment.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations impose an economic substance test on ICs undertaking 'relevant activities', including distribution and service centre functions. An online retail operation selling desktop 3D printers and resin filaments is considered a distribution and service centre if it performs procurement, storage, logistics, or customer service through a Samoa branch. The entity must demonstrate a physical office in Samoa, qualified employees, core income-generating activity conducted in Samoa, and adequate operating expenditure. Failure to do so results in loss of tax exemption, penalties, and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Accounting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all income at 27% corporate rate; late payment penalty of 2% per month; penalty under Income Tax Act 1974 and Crimes Act 2016 of up to WST 100,000 and 7 years imprisonment; strike-off of the IC and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false declarations lose limited liability; they are personally liable for unpaid taxes, civil restitution, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Samoa company for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under the ESR, a deficiency determination requires a corrected return, loss of exemption, an additional 10% penalty on net income, and deregistration of the IC; the assets may escheat to the Government of Samoa.

Director personal liability: The Registrar may obtain a court order making directors jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid fees, penalties, and applicable taxes; travel bans and prohibition on acting as a director for up to 10 years may also be imposed.
Anti-Money Laundering / Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Financing of Terrorism Act 2018, fines for legal persons up to WST 500,000 and imprisonment of individuals up to 14 years; confiscation of all proceeds linked to the offending transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge or wilful blindness to money laundering or sanctions breaches are personally criminally liable; they may face immediate travel bans, cross-border extradition, and civil forfeiture.
International Company Regime Abuse / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Retroactive denial of treaty benefits, recovery of under-withheld tax at up to 15%, penalty of 100% of the tax underpaid, and deregistration of the IC for failure to hold a valid annual license.

Director personal liability: Directors abstaining from oversight are liable for reckless trading under the Companies Act; the Ministry of Revenue may bring a civil claim for restitution, apply for passport surrender, and obtain a restraint order over personal assets.
