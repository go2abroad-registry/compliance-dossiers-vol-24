Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management - statutory administrator of VAT, excise, and customs (portal: https://customs.gov.vu); Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) - sole registry for International Companies, licensed agents, and tax-exemption certification (portal: https://vfsc.vu).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company obtains legal existence through VFSC incorporation, not through an income-tax registration, because Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no withholding tax. Steps: (1) engage a VFSC-licensed registered agent in Port Vila; (2) reserve the company name and file the Memorandum and Articles of Association with VFSC under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]; (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and, optionally, a VFSC tax-exemption confirmation; (4) if any domestic Vanuatu taxable supplies are made (e.g., local office lease or local subcontracting), register with VRC for VAT once taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period reaches or exceeds VT 4,000,000; a purely offshore AI data-labeling and RLHF data-curation BPO serving non-resident clients remains outside the Vanuatu VAT base. The VFSC company registration number serves as the principal statutory identifier; no separate TIN is issued for income-tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - the International Company is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes and duties on profits, income, capital, and transactions arising outside Vanuatu under s. 5 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222]; the exemption also covers shares, debentures, and transfers and is incompatible with conducting business with Vanuatu residents or deriving Vanuatu-source revenue.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or OECD hybrid-mismatch election exists because Vanuatu has no corporate income tax base. Available statutory elections: (1) VFSC tax-exemption certification evidencing freedom from stamp duty and revenue imposts; (2) shareholder election by 75% resolution to re-domicile a foreign company into Vanuatu as an International Company with continuity of exempt status; (3) election to convert the International Company into a Vanuatu domestic company, which revokes exempt status and subjects Vanuatu-source activity to VAT, the 0.5% Consumer Compensation Levy, and municipal business-license fees; and (4) election to enhance limited-liability and share-structure provisions subject to VFSC beneficial-ownership registration. Any election touching resident-source activity triggers VRC and VFSC review.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 4,000,000 in taxable supplies per rolling 12-month period (approximately USD 33,000-35,000); registration is compulsory once the threshold is met, with voluntary registration permitted below the threshold. A purely offshore export-oriented International Company is not 'making supplies in Vanuatu' and therefore does not cross the threshold and holds no VRC VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods and services supplied to recipients belonging outside Vanuatu qualify for the 0% rate under the export provisions of the Value Added Tax Act 1998. For a computer-vision bounding-box annotation and RLHF data-curation BPO, the applicable tests are: (i) the recipient of the training-data and annotation services belongs outside Vanuatu; (ii) the contract, annotation delivery logs, and invoices squarely evidence offshore destination and foreign consumption; (iii) no material benefit of the service is consumed inside Vanuatu; and (iv) export proceeds are traceable to the foreign principal. A pure-export International Company sits entirely outside the VAT base and therefore does not charge output VAT, file VAT returns, or claim input-VAT refunds; the zero-rating mechanism is practically relevant only for a mixed-activity entity that holds a domestic VAT registration and exports services while seeking input-VAT recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Asycuda World filing) with applicable tariff or H.S. classification
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or equivalent digital delivery evidence for the goods element
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing foreign-currency settlement of export proceeds
  • Signed service contract and commercial invoice identifying the non-resident recipient of the AI data-labeling / RLHF service
  • Zero-rated VAT tax invoice compliant with s. 18 of the VAT Act and proof of service delivery (annotation batch reports, model-training data logs, client acceptance certificates)
  • Bank-account statement traces reconciling receipt currency, counterparty, and the 180-day repatriation window

Vanuatu operates no real-time fiscal clearance system, no digital stamping, no SAF-T schema, and no government API pre-clearance gateway. A VAT-registered supplier must issue a prescribed tax invoice under the VAT Act containing supplier and buyer identification, description and quantity of the supply, tax-exclusive price, VAT at 15%, invoice date, and serial number, and must retain records for seven years. Electronic invoices are accepted on VRC audit but are not subject to any live clearance or approval mechanism.

VRC may audit zero-rated export claims against the foreign-recipient contract, delivery evidence, and inward-remittance trace. Failure to substantiate the export within the 180-day repatriation convention triggers: assessment of output VAT at 15% on the gross consideration; a late-payment penalty of 10% of the assessed tax; monthly interest at approximately 2% per month; and civil penalties under the VAT Act, with wilful evasion or knowingly false documentation constituting a criminal offence carrying fines of up to VT 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to two years, together with retrospective clawback of input-VAT credits and denial of zero-rating for the entire engagement. For the International Company vehicle, VFSC may strike off the company or revoke the tax-exemption certificate for breaches of the non-residence conditions, such as contracting with a Vanuatu-resident party or maintaining a domestic physical presence, with retrospective assessment of all taxes that would otherwise have applied, plus business-license arrears, VAT, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a liberalized exchange-control regime. Both current account and capital account transactions are generally freely permitted, and the Vatu (VUV) is convertible through licensed commercial banks for legitimate trade and investment purposes. No statutory pre-approval is required for routine capital injections, loan repayments, or profit repatriation by International Companies. Profit remittance relies primarily on standard know-your-customer and source-of-funds verification by the banking system, with no material conversion restrictions; practical market depth for VUV is limited, so USD and AUD are the dominant settlement currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds into Vatu and no fixed statutory conversion ratio. Cross-border receipts may be retained in foreign currency in an offshore-compliant account or converted as needed. The only compliance requirements relate to accurate transaction documentation, beneficial-ownership declarations, and record retention for anti-money laundering oversight under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) - the central monetary authority and supervisory body for banks, foreign-exchange dealers, and payment service providers, exercising exchange-control and AML/CFT oversight under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act, the Financial Transactions Reporting Act, and applicable exchange-control regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low to Medium. Under Vanuatu's Financial Dealers Licensing Act and the Banking Act, holding client funds, operating a payment account, or providing money-transfer services from within Vanuatu requires licensing by the RBV. Unlicensed offshore digital EMIs are not viable as primary treasury accounts; however, established global EMIs such as Wise and PayPal Payouts may be used peripherally as disbursement rails if the Vanuatu International Company maintains a funded commercial bank account at a tier-1 VU bank and the EMI satisfies its own internal international-corporate onboarding checks. The legal constraint is that the EMI must not purport to be a licensed Vanuatu bank or money remitter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VUV 0 (not applicable). Vanuatu has no corporate income tax and has enacted no transfer pricing documentation threshold; no Local File or Master File regime exists. The operative threshold is instead the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019, which requires an IC carrying on a relevant activity to maintain contemporaneous records demonstrating adequate economic substance and arm's-length allocation of income.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 and its subsidiary regulations transpose internationally agreed substance requirements onto International Companies. Covered relevant activities include banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, holding company, intellectual property, and distribution and service centres. An AI data-labeling and RLHF BPO IC rendering services exclusively to unrelated clients and performing all processing abroad falls outside the relevant-activity net; however, if the same IC invoices related group companies, the distribution and service centre head may apply, requiring a physical office, qualified local personnel, and local operating expenditure proportionate to revenue. A fully remote BPO that cannot satisfy those physical-presence tests should either declare tax residence in the shareholder's jurisdiction, thereby invoking the foreign-tax-resident exclusion, or avoid interposition in related-party payment chains.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Conspiracy to Defraud Foreign Revenue

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu domestic law imposes no CIT sanction, but the VFSC invokes good-standing and anti-avoidance powers: administrative penalty up to VUV 500,000, compulsory deregistration, and formal notification to the foreign tax authority through Vanuatu's exchange-of-information framework, as Vanuatu participates in CRS and maintains TIEA arrangements with Australia, New Zealand, France, and EU member states.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false VFSC declarations face criminal fines and up to 12 months' imprisonment under the International Companies Act; where concealment of foreign tax liabilities is proven overseas, the foreign parent entity is exposed to its domestic penalties and directors may be subjected to restraining orders and travel restrictions pending asset recovery proceedings.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Evasion

Statutory sanction: Breach of the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 attracts an initial VUV 300,000 penalty, escalating to VUV 500,000 for continuing non-compliance, followed by mandatory strike-off of the company and a bar on re-registration under the same name; the registered agent itself faces license suspension for enabling the evasion.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: a director who fails to ensure ESR filings becomes jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid penalties; the VFSC may disqualify the director from managing any Vanuatu company for up to 5 years, with compliance compelled through civil contempt proceedings.
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Failures

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2017 criminalizes laundering of proceeds of crime with imprisonment of up to 20 years and/or a fine of up to VUV 500,000; ancillary contraventions, including a false beneficial ownership declaration, attract summary corporate penalties of up to VUV 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are prosecuted as principals where the IC lacked an AML compliance framework or where a director personally authorized the tainted payout route; sanctions extend to seizure and forfeiture of the IC's bank balances, including frozen PayPal and Wise receivables, freezing orders over foreign-held assets, and judicial travel bans enforced by the Vanuatu Police pending extradition or international arrest operations.
False Annual Return Declarations to the Registrar

Statutory sanction: Making a false statement under the International Companies Act 2012 is a criminal offence carrying a fine of up to VUV 500,000 and, upon indictment, imprisonment for up to 12 months; the company is struck off and verified beneficial ownership information is forwarded to foreign authorities under Vanuatu's information exchange commitments.

Director personal liability: A nominee director is personally exposed even where instructions originated from offshore executives; the law treats the registered director as the statutory author of the declaration. Personal liability includes civil restitution to the company's creditors, directorial disqualification, enforcement of judgments against personal assets in Vanuatu, and custodial sentences; no corporate indemnity overrides the director's statutory non-delegable duties.
