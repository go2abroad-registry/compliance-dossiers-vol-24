Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue Authority (VRA) within the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila. The VRA administers the Value Added Tax Act, Customs Act, and other indirect tax legislation; electronic interface is provided through the Vanuatu Revenue System portal. Company registration for International Companies is administered separately by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) under the International Companies Act.

E-government filing portal

Vanuatu does not operate a general national tax identification number or income-tax registration system. A foreign entity that only holds a Vanuatu International Company (IC) status obtains its corporate identity from the VFSC Certificate of Incorporation and has no automatic income-tax identifier. If the entity nevertheless carries on or intends to carry on a prescribed Vanuatu taxable activity (largely incompatible with IC status), it must submit the standard VAT registration application form to the VRA, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office details, banking confirmation, and estimates of taxable turnover. Upon approval the VRA issues a dedicated VAT registration number. A foreign entity that is not registered and does not make taxable supplies in Vanuatu has no statutory obligation to procure a TIN/VAT ID for general inbound trade or offshore factoring activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box, claw-back, or hybrid mismatch election is available under Vanuatu law. The Vanuatu International Company is automatically and irrevocably tax-exempt for the duration of its valid offshore status. A foreign investor may choose structurally between a local Vanuatu company (which may be subject to VAT registration on local taxable supplies) and an International Company (tax-exempt offshore vehicle), but there is no elective passthrough or entity-level tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Vatu (VT) 10,000,000 annual taxable turnover; registration is compulsory once the threshold is met or exceeded and may be voluntary below the threshold in limited circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available where the supply qualifies as an export under the VAT Act and the underlying transaction is genuinely offshore or exported. Goods must be exported from Vanuatu in accordance with the Customs Act, with title and effective control passing to a non-resident and the goods physically leaving Vanuatu. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient for use wholly outside Vanuatu, or where the supply consists of international transport or services directly connected with exported goods. A Vanuatu International Company engaged exclusively in cross-border trade and reverse factoring should not incur local VAT, provided no Vanuatu taxable supply is made and no service is rendered to a Vanuatu resident recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Export/CusDec)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Copy of the underlying factoring or trade finance contract

Vanuatu does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing APIs, or digital stamping. Registered persons must issue compliant VAT tax invoices, retaining supporting export documentation. The VRA is entitled to require electronic filing of VAT returns through the Vanuatu Revenue System, but no continuous transaction-control or government clearing house requirement applies.

Zero-rated export claims are subject to retrospective customs and VAT audit. If documentary evidence is insufficient or the alleged export did not occur, the VRA may reclassify the supply as taxable at 12.5% VAT, disallow the refund, impose statutory late-payment interest and civil penalties, and assess additional tax. Knowingly submitting false zero-rating declarations or falsifying export documentation constitutes a criminal offence under both the VAT Act and the Customs Act, potentially exposing responsible officers to fines and imprisonment. In practice, the VRA applies strict evidentiary review to refund claims, and clawback exposure remains open for periods covered by non-filed or fraudulent returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, so current-account payments and transfers are free of exchange-control restriction. The capital account is materially liberalized: a non-resident International Company (VU_IC) may freely receive equity injections, distribute profits, and remit factoring proceeds without prior RBV approval, provided each transaction is routed through an authorized dealer bank (ADB) with an accurate BoP purpose code. The VUV is a managed-float currency with limited offshore market depth and is not deliverable in any major clearing hub; in practice liquidity is cleared in USD, AUD, and EUR. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders of an IC are exempt from Vanuatu income tax and withholding tax, leaving exchange-control declaration as the only friction point.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations, resident exporters must ensure that export consideration is remitted to Vanuatu within 6 months of shipment and credited to a foreign-currency account held with an ADB; surplus foreign currency must be offered for conversion into VUV unless the RBV grants a specific exemption. The VU_IC itself is non-resident and therefore not subject to export surrender; however, where it purchases receivables from resident Vanuatu suppliers, the assigned invoice stream must include a contractual warranty that the underlying exporter has complied with the 6-month surrender timeline so that collection proceeds can be routed directly to the VU_IC's escrow account without violation of the RBV regime.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap 125], jointly administering the Exchange Control Act [Cap 38] and the Exchange Control Regulations. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) separately licenses the factoring entity as a Financial Dealer under the Financial Dealers Licensing Act [Cap 88], while the RBV remains the designated authority for all foreign-exchange, authorized-dealer, and cross-border clearing approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low (restricted). Vanuatu has no local fintech-EMI license category that grants direct access to the national VUV clearing system. Offshore digital EMIs would require a VFSC Financial Dealer Licence and typically cannot secure sponsored settlement accounts at local ADBs. The efficient route is a standard corporate escrow account at a tier-1 ADB combined with the ADB's regional USD nostro clearing capability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial capital injections are wired via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the investor's international bank to the VU_IC's designated USD/VUV escrow clearance account at the onshore ADB. Every transfer must (i) quote a BoP purpose code corresponding to an equity or capital-subscription inflow, (ii) reference the executed share-subscription agreement, and (iii) where the transfer exceeds the ADB's reporting threshold (typically VUV 10 million or equivalent), be accompanied by the RBV exchange-control notification form. VUV-denominated landing is discouraged; the USD account should be used as the primary liquidity anchor and spot-converted only when VUV disbursements are necessary.Factoring payouts and trade settlements are executed through SWIFT MT103 with full invoice-reference fields, cleared against the ADB's USD nostro lines. Where the underlying transaction involves a matched documentary export, a confirmed, irrevocable sight or usance commercial letter of credit (LC) may be issued through the ADB to the selling supplier, with the VU_IC as LC applicant. Straight MT103 transfers remain the default rail for reverse-factoring prepayments and repayment of assigned receivable collections. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no withholding tax, no payroll tax, and no stamp duty on International Company instruments)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by an International Company to its non-resident shareholders, and it does not impose a branch remittance tax on distributions from a registered foreign company's Vanuatu branch.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold applies in VUV because Vanuatu has no corporate income tax and therefore no TP adjustment mechanism. Notwithstanding, the Economic Substance Regulations impose a qualitative substance threshold on relevant activities. For a factoring entity holding trade receivables and issuing short-term financing against invoices, the annual ES return requires disclosure of: (a) board meeting minutes and quorum held in Vanuatu, (b) physical premises with permanent local staff, (c) senior management physically present in Vanuatu, and (d) total operating expenditure in Vanuatu commensurate with the factoring volume. There is no de minimis revenue threshold; any IC with a relevant activity is captured.

Economic substance scope: The Vanuatu Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2020 apply to every International Company engaged in a relevant activity. Factoring and reverse factoring of trade receivables are categorised under the 'financing and leasing' head. The entity must demonstrate: (1) management and control exercised in Vanuatu (at least two board meetings per year physically in Vanuatu, with minutes signed in-country), (2) adequate physical premises and employees proportionate to the factoring book, (3) operating expenditure in Vanuatu proportionate to the income booked, and (4) strategic and operational decisions taken in Vanuatu, including credit risk analysis, funding decisions, and collection supervision. Outsourcing of core functions to a foreign affiliate is permissible only if a local compliance officer supervises and local records are retained. The annual ESR return is filed simultaneously with the VFSC annual return; VFSC may request documentation and levy penalties for non-compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: VUV 5,000,000 financial penalty under the Economic Substance Regulations; a further VUV 100,000 per month of continuing default; mandatory strike-off from the VFSC register; potential referral to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit, and listing of the entity and its beneficial owners as non-compliant with the OECD Inclusive Framework, which can lead to additional foreign countermeasures.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign a false ESR declaration are personally liable for the penalty up to VUV 5,000,000 and jointly and severally liable for the entity's unpaid penalties; the corporate veil will be pierced if the entity is a façade for the foreign parent; directors may be disqualified for up to 10 years and banned from leaving Vanuatu at the request of the VFSC.
Anti-Money Laundering / Terrorism Financing (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act and the Vanuatu Proceeds of Crime Act: fines up to VUV 10,000,000 and imprisonment up to 20 years; confiscation of the factoring proceeds and the escrow account balances; revocation of the VFSC licence; freezing of all money held in the Vanuatu banking system.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are criminally liable if they act recklessly or with intent to facilitate money laundering; the court may impose imprisonment up to 20 years, corporate director disqualification, and travel bans; personal assets traceable to the offence are subject to civil confiscation; the registered agent may also be criminally fined if the agent failed to report suspicious transactions in relation to the factoring portfolio.
Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Offence under Section 76 of the International Companies Act 1992: fine up to VUV 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years, plus a daily penalty of VUV 10,000 for continuing false certification; VFSC shall cancel the certificate of incorporation; the Attorney General may petition for conditional sanctions; foreign tax authorities may impose secondary penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise a false return face imprisonment of up to 2 years, personal fines, and additional public scrutiny; if the IC is used as a sham to evade foreign taxes, foreign civil courts may pierce the veil and hold directors personally liable for restitution to the defrauded revenue authority; bank accounts held by the director in Vanuatu are subject to freezing orders.
Unlicensed Financial Services / Banking Activity

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed financial dealings: monetary penalty of up to VUV 5,000,000 per contravention; VFSC official warning and prohibition order; possible liquidation by court order if the factoring book is exposed; the obligation to refund all affected trade counterparties. Unlicensed deposit-taking activity is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment as well as fines.

Director personal liability: Directors of an unlicensed factoring business are personally exposed to criminal liability for each day of unauthorised activity, restitution to all defrauded creditors, disqualification under the Companies Act registry, and potential travel bans while the Financial Intelligence Unit conducts the investigation.
