Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) for International Company registration and statutory exemption certification; Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Service (VRCS), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management (MFEM), for VAT registration, returns, and compliance. Official self-service portal: VRCS Online Services (e-filing of VAT registrations and returns).

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes, stamp duties, and charges; no income tax TIN is required because Vanuatu does not levy income tax. For indirect tax, the IC must register for VAT with VRCS and obtain a 9-digit VAT registration number if it makes taxable supplies to Vanuatu residents or its annual taxable turnover reaches the statutory threshold; registration is effected through the VRCS VAT registration form and online portal. An IC whose activities are confined to non-resident counterparties relies on the VFSC tax-exemption certificate in lieu of tax ID procurement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the International Company is exempt by force of statute from corporate income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, and all other national taxes and charges, provided it does not carry on business with Vanuatu residents, does not own land in Vanuatu, and remains within the International Companies Act [Cap 222] conditions.

Available election forms

Section 60 election under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]: the IC may elect to be treated as a local company, thereby waiving its statutory tax exemptions and becoming subject to the VAT Act and general Vanuatu revenue law. No check-the-box or pass-through entity election exists under Vanuatu law; OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch election mechanisms have not been enacted domestically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 (ten million vatu) annual taxable turnover under section 7 of the VAT Act; mandatory registration applies once the 12-month rolling turnover equals or exceeds the threshold, with voluntary registration permitted below it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and supplies rendered for the direct benefit of non-residents are zero-rated under section 11 of the VAT Act. Overseas market-entry consultancy services are zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident person and the services are not supplied for use or enjoyment in Vanuatu; the supplier must retain documentation evidencing the non-resident recipient, the export character of the supply, and the absence of local enjoyment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (approved export entry filed with Vanuatu Customs under the Customs Act [Cap 121])
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods (where goods are exported)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/telex credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in vatu or foreign currency
  • VAT export tax invoice issued at the 0% rate in sequential invoice order
  • Export Service Agreement / Consultancy Contract identifying the non-resident client and the scope of services

No real-time e-invoicing, API fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force. VAT-registered suppliers must issue sequentially numbered VAT tax invoices and retain them for audit inspection. VAT returns are submitted electronically through the VRCS e-filing portal. A fiscal digitalization program introducing mandatory e-invoicing remains under policy consideration but is not yet legislated.

VRCS may reopen assessments within three years, extended indefinitely in cases of fraud or gross negligence. Fraudulent export zero-rating or refund claims constitute an offence under section 69 of the VAT Act, punishable by a fine of up to VT 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment of up to two years, together with forfeiture of the benefit obtained. Where zero-rating is reversed on audit, the taxpayer must repay the 12.5% VAT that would have applied, with penalty tax of up to 100% of the tax evaded and monthly late-payment interest under the VAT Act and general revenue administration law.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates an open foreign exchange market without exchange-control restrictions on current payments for a tax-exempt International Company. The Vatu is convertible on a controlled but non-domiciled basis; capital account convertibility is permitted for foreign-owned entities, but all inward remittances must pass through a licensed bank's anti-money laundering due diligence and be supported by an underlying consultancy agreement. Profit remittances and dividend payments are not subject to exchange controls or withholding taxes for an International Company, provided beneficial ownership and source-of-funds declarations are maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu does not impose statutory export proceeds surrender rules or mandatory conversion ratios under the Exchange Control Act for International Companies. Receipts from overseas market-entry consultancy services may be retained in foreign currency in permitted external accounts or converted at prevailing market rates through licensed authorized dealers, subject to bank-level KYC. No mandatory timeline applies to the repatriation of service export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) and Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Vanuatu International Companies may onboard with Tier-1 regulated digital EMI platforms, such as Wise for Business, for outbound B2B disbursements; however, Vanuatu has no domestically regulated EMI market. The underlying legality depends on the EMI's home-country licence and compliance with Vanuatu's anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing framework. Several EMIs reject Vanuatu-registered International Companies due to traditional shell-company perception; this risk is mitigable with economic substance evidence, physical director profiles, audited financial statements, and clear client-settlement history. An EMI should be used as a disbursement-only clearing rail, not as a primary deposit-taking bank, because it offers no Vanuatu-denominated account or deposit-insurance protection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections should be routed through a licensed Vanuatu commercial bank using SWIFT MT103 from the overseas parent or funding entity. The payment should reference the consultancy service agreement and an appropriate economic-purpose code such as consultation, management fees, or overseas market-entry services. Vanuatu does not mandate a national transaction purpose code, but the receiving bank must record the economic nature as an overseas market-entry consultancy fee and retain the underlying contract. Funds are initially credited to a Capital Escrow Account pending completion of KYC verification and beneficial-ownership confirmation; thereafter, transferred to the Operational Account for use in consultancy fee payments, local operating expenses, and outbound disbursements.Primary clearing rails include SWIFT MT103 for fee, supplier, salary, and intercompany disbursements; Commercial Letters of Credit for documentary trade settlements in market-entry and procurement transactions; SWIFT R-charge or SHARE-charge instructions where correspondent fees need to be controlled; and the domestic Vanuatu interbank clearing system for any VUV-denominated local disbursements. A contravention of the International Companies Act may also expose the company and every knowing officer to a statutory fine of up to VT 1,000,000 and a continuing daily penalty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable – Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax and has no domestic transfer-pricing documentation law, no arm’s-length statutory threshold, and no Local File/Master File obligation expressed in Vatu. Foreign transfer-pricing compliance may be triggered only if the International Company has a permanent establishment or taxable presence in another country under that country’s tax law.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu applies an economic substance framework derived from the OECD harmful tax practices standard. An International Company engaged in a relevant activity, including related-party service-centre activity, holding business, financing/leasing, headquarters, distribution, shipping, banking, insurance, fund management, or intellectual property business, must demonstrate adequate economic substance in Vanuatu. A pure overseas market-entry consultancy performed for unrelated clients from outside Vanuatu by non-resident staff generally falls outside the listed relevant activities; however, the IC must still maintain a registered office and registered agent in Vanuatu and must not falsely declare a non-relevant status if management, back-office, or advisory functions are in fact rendered to related parties. Substance evidence includes VFSC-registered premises, employees in Vanuatu, board meetings held in Vanuatu, and records of key decision-making in Vanuatu.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Sham Consultancy Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu does not levy income tax, but the underlying fraudulent conduct is prosecutable under the Penal Code Act [Cap. 135]. False pretences, forgery, and fraudulent invoicing may result in imprisonment and fines. The VFSC may strike off the company, and tainted proceeds may be restrained and confiscated under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly sign false invoices, backdated agreements, or false statements of work are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, civil restitution orders, asset freezing, and travel bans. The corporate veil will be pierced where the IC is a mere instrumentality of fraud.
Shell Company and False Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: A false declaration under the International Companies Act is a criminal offence carrying a maximum fine of VT 1,000,000 plus continuing daily default penalties. Where the economic substance regulations apply, the VFSC may impose additional monetary penalties, require corrective filings, and strike off the company for persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Each director who signs a false declaration may be individually fined and disqualified. During investigation, the Supreme Court may impose a travel ban on the directors. Directors may be ordered to contribute personally to the outstanding penalties and to make restitution for losses caused by the misrepresentation.
Money Laundering and AML/CTF Breach

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is a serious offence under the Proceeds of Crime Act. Conviction is punishable by substantial imprisonment and fines, with the laundered property itself subject to confiscation. The Financial Transactions Reporting Act also imposes regulatory monetary penalties on entities and agents that breach customer due diligence and suspicious transaction reporting rules.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and beneficial owners who control the flow of tainted funds face personal criminal liability. Courts may issue freezing orders, travel bans, and confiscation orders. Restitution can be ordered in the amount of the laundered funds, and imprisonment is available for those convicted.
Unlicensed Regulated Financial Activity

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed financial dealing is an offence. The VFSC may issue a cease-and-desist order, impose administrative fines, wind up the International Company, and deregister the entity. Contracts entered into in breach of the licensing regime are unenforceable, and profits derived from the unlicensed business may be disgorged.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate without a licence are personally liable for regulatory penalties, disgorgement, and civil claims by aggrieved counterparties. The VFSC can ban the directors from managing any Vanuatu entity, and the court may impose travel bans pending criminal proceedings.
False Beneficial Ownership and Nominee Abuse

Statutory sanction: Knowingly giving false information in a document required by the International Companies Act is a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to VT 1,000,000 and imprisonment; the Registrar may strike off the company and report the matter to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit. Sanctions and international asset-freeze obligations may also apply if the concealed person is subject to UN or foreign sanctions.

Director personal liability: The beneficial owner cannot hide behind a nominee. Both the actual controlling person and the nominee director signing the false return are personally liable to fines, imprisonment, travel bans, and civil claims. Restitution may be ordered to the defrauded party, the Crown, or foreign authorities under mutual legal assistance.
