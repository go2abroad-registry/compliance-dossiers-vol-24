Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa; online taxpayer portal administered by the Ministry for Revenue (formerly Samoa Revenue Authority) at https://www.revenue.gov.ws; all VAGST registrations, filings, and tax returns are processed through the MfR e-services platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) obtains its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) by filing a 'Company Tax Registration' application with the Ministry for Revenue. Required documents include: (i) Certificate of Incorporation issued under the International Companies Act 1987; (ii) Memorandum and Articles of Association; (iii) registered agent certificate of representation in Samoa (licensed by the Samoa International Finance Authority); (iv) directors and shareholders identification, address, and passport copies; (v) statement of registered office in Samoa. For ICs without public domestic nexus and no income sourced in Samoa, the TIN is issued on a non-resident/tax-exempt designation basis. The Ministry for Revenue issues the TIN electronically within 5-10 business days after submission of a complete application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The International Company is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax under the International Companies Act 1987 and is generally treated as a non-resident company for tax purposes; there is no US-style check-the-box pass-through election. The IC may elect in its constitution to be designated a 'non-resident company' (offshore/exempt) confirming that its commercial activities are conducted exclusively outside Samoa. Hybrid mismatch elections under the Income Tax Act 2019 are unavailable to non-resident exempt companies; a 'resident election' is technically possible only where central management and control are in Samoa, which is generally avoided to preserve tax-exempt status. No entity classification election is required because the IC is taxed at entity level but subject to zero rate of income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (approx. USD 37,000) annual taxable turnover in Samoa. An International Company conducting online resale of vintage luxury goods has VAGST registration obligations only if it makes taxable supplies in Samoa exceeding the WST 100,000 threshold. Supplies by a non-resident IC to customers outside Samoa are outside the scope of VAGST or zero-rated; remote digital/e-commerce services into Samoa are subject to VAGST on the import of services reverse-charge basis where the local recipient is VAT-registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating at 0% VAGST applies to: (i) physical export of goods from Samoa where the supplier retains evidence of export within 28 days and title has passed to a non-resident buyer; (ii) supply of a service directly in connection with the export of goods; (iii) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and the benefit of the service occurs wholly outside Samoa; (iv) international transport of goods. For an online reseller of vintage luxury goods, zero rating is available only where physical goods are actually shipped from Samoa to a foreign customer under a valid customs export declaration; consignment or overlapping storage models must be documented with a complete chain of custody.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form Cus-Ex-01) endorsed by Samoa Customs for physical departure of goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing ocean/air export from Samoa to foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and purchase/sales documentation evidencing transfer of title and value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank/escrow settlement statement evidencing foreign currency payment
  • Packing list and, where applicable, certificate of insurance for the consignment

Samoa does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system or mandatory e-invoicing business-to-government (B2G) API clearance as of 2025. There is no digital stamping requirement for invoices. However, all VAGST-registered persons must file VAGST returns and supporting transactional summaries electronically through the Ministry for Revenue e-services portal. VAGST tax invoices must include prescribed particulars: serial number, date, seller TIN, buyer TIN (where applicable), description, quantity, price, VAGST amount, and the phrase 'Tax Invoice'. The system is audit-based rather than clearance-based; conservation of complete invoice and export records is mandatory to support zero-rating claims.

False or unsupported zero-rating claims are treated as tax evasion under the Tax Administration Act 2019 (Samoa). Penalties include: (i) a tax shortfall penalty of 150% of the VAGST avoided; (ii) late payment penalty of 2% per month on the unpaid amount; (iii) statutory interest accruing from the original due date; (iv) retrospective clawback of VAGST for a period of up to 5 years (10 years in cases of fraud or wilful misrepresentation); and (v) criminal sanctions including imprisonment of up to 3 years and fines, with personal liability extending to directors and registered agents who participate in fraudulent exports or sham transactions.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) holds limited but generally workable convertibility for current-account transactions (trade goods, services, interest, dividends, and normal remittances) through licensed commercial banks under delegated CBS authority; capital-account transactions remain subject to prior CBS approval or authorized-dealer consent. An International Company registered under the International Companies Act 1987 is statutorily treated as a non-resident and is expressly exempt from the Exchange Control Act 1976, allowing it to maintain and settle balances in any freely usable currency (USD, EUR, AUD, NZD) without mandatory conversion or CBS capital-account approval. Practical frictions remain: any WST-denominated payment by an IC to a Samoan resident or any Samoan-source settlement routed through a local commercial bank will trigger standard CBS balance-of-payments reporting, threshold-based exchange-control declarations, and enhanced due diligence for luxury goods sellers; profit or capital repatriation to non-resident shareholders, however, faces no legal conversion hurdle because the IC can hold proceeds directly in USD or other foreign currency offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters, the Exchange Control Regulations require full repatriation of export proceeds within three months of shipment and surrender/ conversion into WST through a licensed commercial bank unless a CBS foreign-currency account permit is obtained. An International Company is exempt from the surrender and repatriation regime by operation of Section 198 of the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended), so vintage luxury resale receipts collected via offshore payment gateways (Stripe, Escrow Pay) may remain in foreign currency without forced conversion. If, however, the IC chooses to receive WST proceeds from local consignors or settle domestic Samoan expenses in WST, those flows must be cleared through a licensed Samoan bank, which will apply CBS declaration thresholds (above WST 100,000 per transaction or aggregation per month) and suspicious-transaction reporting under the AML/CFT Act 2018.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), acting under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1989, the Exchange Control Act 1976, and the Exchange Control Regulations 1980 as the sole administrator of exchange control and monetary oversight in Samoa

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Samoa does not operate a digital EMI licensing regime, and no local fintech can legally provide bank-grade payment/settlement services to an International Company; overseas EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currenxie) generally decline Samoan ICs due to correspondent-bank risk, FATF-style island-jurisdiction restrictions, and inability to verify the IC's exchange-control exemption in their automated onboarding engines. An offshore EMI can be used only as an ancillary settlement conduit if (a) the IC maintains its primary operational account with a licensed Samoan commercial bank, (b) the EMI is licensed in a reputable jurisdiction (UK FCA, HK MSO, Singapore MAS), and (c) EMI balances are swept daily to the primary bank account; using an unlicensed EMI as principal treasury custodian would breach the AML/CFT Act 2018 reporting-institution mapping and undermine corporate veil protection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity funding must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 (for amounts below USD 10 million) or MT202 for interbank covering, from the shareholder's licensed bank directly to the IC's ANZ/BSP Samoa account or designated foreign-currency account. The remittance must cite CBS balance-of-payments purpose code 1120 (direct investment — equity capital) or 1121 (equity injections) and a plain-text description reading 'Equity subscription — International Company capital — [Company Registration Number]'. The receiving bank will require a CBS Exchange Control declaration (EBI form) where the inward amount exceeds WST 100,000 or where funds originate from a jurisdiction flagged by the Samoa FIU; the bank will also request the share allotment certificate, shareholder register extract, and board resolution within 5 business days of credit. Funds routed through third-party crypto, cash, or non-bank money remitters will be rejected or suspended pending STR filing.For supplier and consignor settlements, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 sent from the IC's Samoan or foreign-currency account with purpose code 2110 (trade credit settlement) or 2310 (other goods and services), referencing the commercial invoice and consignment agreement. For large-batch replenishment of authentic vintage inventory (e.g., lots from European auction houses or Japanese luxury dealers), a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by ANZ/BSP under UCP 600 (with advising or confirming bank in the exporter's jurisdiction) ensures title-coupled payment; the required document set is commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill/parcel manifest, independent authentication certificate, insurance certificate, and packing list. E-commerce collections from customers close via Stripe and Escrow Pay settlement rails (card networks, SEPA/ACH, A2A bank debits) and are swept daily to the primary bank account with a 14-day conditional-release window for authentication disputes. Physical inventory movement leverages courier clearing rails (DHL Express, FedEx International Priority, CSV/APAC couriers) with shipment-level value declarations and a customs broker instruction clarifying 'vintage used luxury goods — ISIC 4791' to avoid import misclassification.Spot FX contracts with licensed commercial banks (ANZ, BSP) for immediate conversion of USD/EUR/AUD receipts into WST only when domestic settlement is required; International Companies are exempt from mandatory surrender and may keep all FX offshore.Outright deliverable forwards with maturity not exceeding 12 months for covering current-account payables (consignor settlements in EUR or JPY), obtainable from ANZ/BSP on production of underlying invoices; CBS treats such forwards as legitimate continuous covering and does not require pre-approval for the exempt IC.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible or market-practicable for WST because no recognized offshore deliverable WST market exists; the IC should instead manage exposure by maintaining multi-currency pockets (USD as base, EUR/JPY for purchasing) and matching currency of receivable to currency of payable.Cross-currency swaps are generally unavailable for International Companies in Samoa; only CBS-licensed banks may execute them, and only for balance-sheet hedging with documented underlying exposure and central-bank approval; speculative swap or option positions are prohibited and would jeopardize the IC's exchange-control exemption.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium viability. Samoa does not operate a digital EMI licensing regime, and no local fintech can legally provide bank-grade payment/settlement services to an International Company; overseas EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currenxie) generally decline Samoan ICs due to correspondent-bank risk, FATF-style island-jurisdiction restrictions, and inability to verify the IC's exchange-control exemption in their automated onboarding engines. An offshore EMI can be used only as an ancillary settlement conduit if (a) the IC maintains its primary operational account with a licensed Samoan commercial bank, (b) the EMI is licensed in a reputable jurisdiction (UK FCA, HK MSO, Singapore MAS), and (c) EMI balances are swept daily to the primary bank account; using an unlicensed EMI as principal treasury custodian would breach the AML/CFT Act 2018 reporting-institution mapping and undermine corporate veil protection.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (resident corporate income tax on Samoan-source income); a non-resident International Company is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax and bears an effective rate of 0% on its foreign-source vintage luxury resale profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Samoa's domestic dividend withholding tax rate is 15% for non-resident shareholders, but it applies only to dividends sourced in Samoa. A non-resident International Company distributing profits accumulated from foreign-source e-commerce turnover is outside the Samoan dividend withholding net; the effective rate is 0%. Under the Samoa-New Zealand Double Tax Agreement (DTA), the residual Samoan rate is 15% (5% where the recipient is a company holding 10% or more of the voting power); the Samoa-Australia DTA allows 15% (5% for a company owning at least 10% of the voting power). These treaty rates are irrelevant to a tax-exempt IC making a pure non-Samoan distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 2,000,000 (approximately USD 760,000) of aggregate related-party transactions in a tax year triggers the obligation to prepare and retain a Local File under Samoa's Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Rules; a Master File is required where the consolidated global group turnover of the IC and its associates exceeds WST 50,000,000, or where the group satisfies the OECD materiality threshold for the Master File. A non-resident International Company that does not carry on business in Samoa and derives no Samoan-source income is generally outside the domestic documentation obligation, but the parent group must nevertheless maintain a full transfer pricing file at its foreign headquarters, anchored by a resale-price or transactional-net-margin analysis supporting the IC's commission on the online luxury resale transactions.

Economic substance scope: Applicability of Economic Substance Regulations (ESR): Samoa enacted the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2021, operative from 1 July 2021, in alignment with the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices and EU listing standards. An International Company carrying on a relevant activity, expressly including distribution and service, intellectual property holding, and pure equity holding, must demonstrate economic presence in Samoa. The doctrine requires: (i) board or senior management meetings held in Samoa; (ii) the engagement of an appropriately qualified Samoan resident officer or manager; (iii) a physical registered office staffed to perform core income-generating activities (CIGA); and (iv) adequacy of Samoan expenditure proportionate to the IC's income. For the vintage luxury resale vehicle, the CIGA are inventory sourcing, consignment listing, payment processing, and logistics orchestration through foreign online marketplaces; the IC must be able to prove that any claimed exemption does not rest solely on the Samoan tax status, and must keep board minutes, substance declarations, and outsourced service agreements at the registered office.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Fronting

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Income Tax Act 2015, deliberate evasion attracts a shortfall penalty of 150% of the tax avoided plus late payment interest at 6.3% per annum; the International Companies Act Section 204 imposes fines of up to WST 100,000 on officers for filing false returns; the Ministry of Revenue may revoke the tax exemption certificate retrospectively and the entire exempt period may be re-assessed.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under the common-law sham doctrine; directors and the beneficial owner are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, any penalties, and the IC's debts if the entity is insolvent. Directors may be made subject to departure prohibition orders, travel bans, and asset freezing orders under the Tax Administration Act, and on criminal conviction face imprisonment of up to seven years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of WST 10,000 per relevant activity per fiscal year, a daily default penalty of WST 500 for continued non-compliance, and a mandatory strike-off order from the Companies Registry. The Registrar may also exchange information automatically with the beneficial owner's home jurisdiction under Samoa's participation in the OECD Global Forum and the Pacific Islands Tax Administration exchange network.

Director personal liability: The Court may enter a personal contribution order against the directors and registered agent for the company's unpaid penalties where the IC has been dissolved; individual directors may be disqualified from acting as corporate officers in Samoa for up to five years, and licensed agents may be debarred, which in practice freezes the IC's bank accounts and Stripe/Escrow payouts.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: The AML/CTF Act 2007 (as amended 2017) imposes civil penalties of up to WST 2,000,000 on the entity and its principals for institutional non-compliance; the Proceeds of Crime Act 2016 authorizes forfeiture and confiscation of the entire luxury goods inventory if the assets derive from predicate offences; the Financial Intelligence Unit may restrict the IC's bank accounts and report the entity to international financial intelligence networks.

Director personal liability: Directors and accountable persons face criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering, structuring, or false AML/CTF declarations; travel bans, passport seizure, asset freezing, and the blocking of all payment rails follow as statutory incident relief; the personal assets of controllers may be confiscated under the proceeds-of-crime framework.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Rules and Section 67 of the Income Tax Act 2015 authorize the Commissioner to adjust the consideration to arm's length, impose a 40% uplift penalty on the adjusted amount, and levy gross-rated failure penalties of WST 50,000 for non-production of documentation, plus late payment interest.

Director personal liability: Where the adjustment creates a tax debt that the IC cannot pay, the Commissioner may pursue directors under the voidable-arrangement provisions; personal liability attaches for the adjusted tax; directors face departure bans from Samoa until the debt is settled, and enforcement may be directed against the IC's bank accounts and the Escrow balances held for the associated buyers.
Corporate Registry False Return / Fraud and Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Section 228 of the International Companies Act 1987 imposes a fine of up to WST 50,000 and two years' imprisonment on officers for knowing false statements; the Registrar may strike-off the company and report the matter to the Financial Intelligence Unit; the IC's bank and escrow accounts may be frozen pending correction of the beneficial ownership record.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal conviction, automatic disqualification from managing any Samoan company for five years, and joint and several liability with the company for all outstanding fees, penalties, and tax liabilities; the disqualification order extends to the director's capacity as trustee of any associated family trust holding the IC shares.
