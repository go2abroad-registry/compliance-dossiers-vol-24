Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR) of the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; electronic portal: Vanuatu Electronic Single Window / ASYCUDA World and gov.vu online services

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from Vanuatu income tax and therefore does not pursue a national income-tax TIN. Establishment is effected through a licensed VFSC registered agent: (i) reserve and file the IC's memorandum and articles under the International Companies Act; (ii) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation/Registration issued by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission; and (iii) if the IC will supply taxable supplies in Vanuatu or import/export dutiable goods, apply through the DCIR for a VAT registration number/customs owner code and such number is stated on all VAT invoices. The Certificate of Incorporation and VFSC annual filing receipt are the primary fiscal identifiers for banks and counterparties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Vanuatu law for an IC; all ICs are automatically non-resident, tax-exempt entities. An IC can be re-domiciled/continued as a locally formed company under the Companies Act, but that conversion is a legal entity-action and does not constitute an elective tax classification. In foreign jurisdictions, the IC may be transparent or opaque by the foreign rules, but such treatment has no domestic Vanuatu election consequence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Vt 5,000,000 (approx. USD 43,000) of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available to persons making exclusively zero-rated supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for zero-rating when a VAT-registered supplier exports tangible goods directly to a non-resident buyer outside Vanuatu and retains proof of export. Services (including cross-border remittance/clearing services) qualify as zero-rated exported services when provided to a non-resident principal and not performed for a benefit consumed within Vanuatu; financial intermediation fees in connection with the transfer of funds from a non-resident remitter to a Vanuatu beneficiary may be treated as non-taxable exempt financial services or zero-rated if the supply is concluded on the non-resident's account and the remuneration is not attributable to Vanuatu activities. The IC must hold written service agreements, SWIFT advice, interbank charges, and the banking end-user attestation demonstrating that the principal is abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World generation)
  • Bill of Lading/Airway Bill or equivalent transport document for goods; SWIFT MT103/MT202 and clearing-house settlement reports for remittance services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the VFSC-licensed bank or foreign-bank correspondent

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is imposed on VAT invoices. Import/export declarations are cleared electronically via the Vanuatu Customs ASYCUDA World system. VAT invoices must be computer-generated or manual and retained for at least 7 years; registered remittance agents are expected to maintain SWIFT MT103/MT202 records and local cash-pickup settlement files. There is no statutory API-connect mandate to an e-invoicing platform.

Fraudulent or unjustified zero-rating causes disallowance of the zero-rate and output VAT at 12.5% plus penalties: a 10% late-payment fine, monthly interest of 1.5% or such statutory rate, and criminal fines up to Vt 1,000,000 with imprisonment for tax evasion. Input VAT credits may be retrospectively clawed back from prior returns (generally a three-year assessment window extended indefinitely for fraud). DCIR reconciles export declarations, shipping evidence, bank SWIFT/repatriation receipts and beneficiary-cash pickup records; remittance agents that cannot produce the inward remittance certificate and FX repatriation evidence will have the VAT zero-rating reversed and may be removed from the money-transfer licence.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains a fully liberalized exchange system: the vatu (VUV) is freely convertible on both current and capital account, the Republic has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, and there are no exchange-control approvals, surrender requirements, or restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, or capital repatriation by International Companies. The de facto constraints are operational: VUV is thinly traded, the commercial market is concentrated in two major banks, and large FX conversions require 48-hour lead time and are executed against a managed float maintained by the RBV. Profit remittance is subject only to standard bank documentary review (audited financial statements) and, where applicable, the money transmitter's segregated client funds rule.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Vanuatu imposes no mandatory repatriation period, no conversion percentage, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds or remittance receipts. All receivables may be held in any convertible currency in offshore, onshore, or foreign bank accounts indefinitely. For remittance operators, RBV license conditions require that customer funds be segregated in a designated client money/trust account; settlement to beneficiaries must be executed within standard T+1 to T+2 commercial terms, and transactions at or above VT 1,000,000 are reportable to the VFIU as currency/threshold transaction reports.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 286] (1989), exercises central banking, banking supervision, and exchange administration functions. The Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit (VFIU), embedded within the RBV, supervises reporting institutions under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 2002 [Cap. 270]. Money changers and money transmitters are regulated by the RBV under the Financial Dealers Act [Cap. 63] and the Financial Dealers (Money Changers and Money Transmitters) Regulations (Order No. 44 of 2001), which constitute the licensing regime for cross-border remittance service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium. Following removal of Vanuatu from FATF increased monitoring (June 2024), VU International Companies are in principle acceptable to tier-2 electronic money institutions (e.g., Currenxie, Airwallex, Dukascopy), but a money-transmission business model triggers hard MSB restrictions: EMIs reserve the right to decline remittance corridors, prohibit aggregated payouts, cap monthly throughput, and terminate on transactional velocity grounds. Legally, an EMI cannot access the VUV domestic clearing rails, cash payout agent network, or VFIU-aligned trust account structure; it can serve only as a supplementary USD/EUR/GBP SWIFT collection rail, never as the primary treasury bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No threshold applies (VUV 0 / not applicable). Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing documentation, master-file, or local-file legislation because it levies no corporate income tax. Foreign tax authorities may nevertheless require TP records under their own laws when the Vanuatu IC is a controlled overseas entity.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Vanuatu's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), enacted through the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act and VFSC guidance, require an International Company carrying on a relevant activity to demonstrate substance proportionate to its income. A licensed cross-border remittance business is likely to fall within the 'Financing or Leasing' category insofar as it provides money transmission, settlement, or liquidity management, or within 'Headquarters' or 'Distribution and Service Centre' if it performs intragroup processing activities. The IC must be managed and directed in Vanuatu, maintain a physical office, employ adequate full-time staff, and incur operating expenditure in Vanuatu proportionate to its revenue. An annual economic substance notification/return must be filed with VFSC; non-compliance leads to financial penalties and automatic exchange of information with the tax authorities of the entity's ultimate beneficial owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (VAT and Stamp Duty)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to VUV 5,000,000 plus 100% penalty on the tax shortfall and interest; imprisonment up to 5 years; asset seizure; deregistration from VCIR; publication of assessments and director disqualification.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct evasion are jointly and severally liable for unpaid VAT, interest, and penalties. Civil restitution, travel bans pending collection proceedings, and custodial sentences apply; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud.
Unlicensed Money Remittance / Financial Dealer Offence

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Dealers Licensing Act [Cap. 262], summary conviction attracts fines up to VUV 2,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; on indictment, fines up to VUV 10,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years. VFSC may freeze settlement bank accounts, revoke the IC's licence, and order restitution to affected remitters.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are personally liable for intercepted funds, subject to personal fines, account freezing orders, travel bans, and imprisonment. Where the company is a mere façade, the court will disregard limited liability and enter judgments against the directors personally.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Evasion

Statutory sanction: VFSC may impose administrative penalties from VUV 1,000,000 to VUV 10,000,000 per relevant activity; continuing offences incur daily fines, licence revocation, and strike-off. The economic substance file is automatically exchanged with foreign tax authorities, exposing the group to joint audits.

Director personal liability: Nominee and real directors are jointly liable for false substance declarations and all resulting liabilities. Travel bans, confiscation of corporate records, director disqualification, and imprisonment for knowingly misleading VFSC may follow; the corporate veil is pierced because the shell lacks substantive business reality.
AML/CFT Compliance Failures

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2014, reporting entities are liable to fines of up to VUV 20,000,000 and officers to imprisonment up to 15 years. The VFSC may suspend or cancel the Financial Dealer's Licence, confiscate assets, and require disgorgement of fees.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability if they knowingly permit AML/CFT failures. Remedies include civil asset forfeiture, freezing of foreign and local accounts, travel bans pending trial, disqualification, and imprisonment.
Fraudulent Misdirection and Misappropriation of Client Funds

Statutory sanction: Civil liability for full restitution plus punitive damages; criminal fraud penalties up to VUV 10,000,000 and imprisonment up to 12 years; court-ordered winding up, receivership over accounts, and prohibition orders against the company.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable to trace and return misappropriated remitter funds. Courts pierce the veil where funds were diverted through related companies; personal bankruptcy, travel bans, director disqualification, and custodial sentences apply.
