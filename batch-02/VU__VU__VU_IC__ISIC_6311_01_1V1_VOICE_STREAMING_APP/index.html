Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Authority (VRCA), Lini Highway, Port Vila, Republic of Vanuatu; official web portal: https://www.vrca.vu. International Company incorporation and registry oversight: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), Port Vila; https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] through a VFSC-licensed registered agent; obtain the VFSC Certificate of Incorporation, Constitution, registered office certificate, and Certificate of Good Standing. Step 2: An IC is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu income, capital gains, withholding, stamp, and indirect taxes; consequently, VRCA does not ordinarily issue a TIN or VAT registration number to an exempt IC, and the VFSC registration number is the de facto statutory identifier. Step 3: Should the IC propose to make a taxable supply in Vanuatu (an activity generally outside the permitted offshore purpose of an IC), the IC must file Form VAT-01 with VRCA, supported by the Certificate of Incorporation, Constitution, registered-agent consent, annual turnover projection, and proof of operational address; VRCA then issues a VAT/TIN number within approximately 7 to 14 business days. Step 4: All filings are made through the registered agent in Port Vila; no fully digital non-resident self-registration portal exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch or CFC election regime. The IC tax exemption is automatic, territorial, and non-elective: it applies only to offshore-sourced income and to transactions with non-residents. As an elective corporate-law matter, an IC may by special resolution approved by VFSC convert into, or redomicile as, a domestic company under the Companies Act [Cap 191], which immediately terminates the statutory tax-exempt status. Conversely, an eligible foreign company may elect to continue as an IC and thereby enter the exemption regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 of annual taxable turnover in Vanuatu (Value Added Tax Act [Cap 252]); voluntary registration is available below the threshold; an IC supplying only offshore digital services to foreign end-users sits outside the VAT net and is not registrable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 252] and the VAT zero-rating rules, exported goods and exported services qualify for the 0% rate when the recipient is a non-resident of Vanuatu who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply, and the services are not performed, used, or enjoyed in Vanuatu. For the Voice Streaming App business, subscription and in-app purchase revenues settled through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are supplies effected through non-resident platform operators to foreign end-users; the services are performed and enjoyed entirely outside Vanuatu, and all consideration is settled in foreign currency. The supply therefore qualifies as an exported service, and in parallel the International Company is statutorily exempt from VAT, creating no registration, output tax, or customs interface in Vanuatu.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Form 4A) lodged by a licensed Vanuatu customs agent
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Vanuatu (for goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency exchange receipt issued by a Vanuatu licensed commercial bank
  • Foreign-currency commercial invoice and packing list
  • For digital services: Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console payout statements, non-resident IP and geolocation logs, executed service agreement, and store-level transaction reports
  • Counterparty non-residence declaration or equivalent documentary evidence where requested by VRCA

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, continuous-transaction-control system, mandatory API e-invoicing, or digital stamping obligation is in force in Vanuatu. A VAT-registered supplier must issue standard paper or electronic tax invoices containing supplier name, VAT ID, invoice date, description, quantity, price, and applicable tax treatment; VAT returns and summary schedules may be filed electronically or on prescribed forms, but individual invoices are not cleared or pre-approved by VRCA.

VRCA may audit zero-rating claims throughout the statutory record-retention period; a claim that lacks the required customs, payment, or foreign-receipt evidence is reassessed at the standard VAT rate of 15% with default interest, penalty tax, and potential revocation of the zero-rating facility. Knowingly false zero-rating declarations attract criminal fines and imprisonment for natural persons and directors under the VAT Act, and for an exempt IC, mischaracterization of local supplies as exports can trigger retroactive loss of IC tax-exempt status and full taxation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Vanuatu maintains no system of mandatory prior exchange-control approval for foreign direct investment, capital injections, dividends, profits, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds payable to non-resident shareholders of a Vanuatu International Company (VU-IC). A VU-IC may lawfully hold and use USD, EUR, AUD, NZD, GBP and VUV accounts with RBV-licensed banks. Bank reporting of large transactions is purely for Financial Intelligence Unit statistics and AML/CTF supervision; it does not constitute exchange-control restriction. The only practical friction is the bank's own customer due diligence and source-of-funds verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timeline for converting export receipts into Vatu under Vanuatu law. A VU-IC engaged in the export of digital voice-streaming services may retain 100% of foreign-currency earnings indefinitely in an approved foreign-currency account. The sole documentary mandate is that the underlying export contracts, invoices and settlement records be available to the bank and the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit if a suspicious-transaction report is required. No RBV conversion or repatriation certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), acting as the central bank and prudential regulator under the Vanuatu Banking Act [Cap. 68], the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 191], and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act [Cap. 255], together with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) for International Company registration and AML/CFT supervision. The RBV issues banking licences, oversees licensed commercial banks and clearing system settlement, and administers Vanuatu's exchange-control liberalisation in conformity with its IMF Article VIII obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low as a primary treasury rail; Medium only as an auxiliary payout connector where a licensed sponsor bank stands behind the EMI. Vanuatu has no dedicated electronic-money institution licensing framework, and offshore fintech EMIs are not regulated by the RBV or VFSC. Most responsible EMIs reject a VU-IC because the entity is tax-exempt, non-resident, and lacks audited local substance. A foreign EMI may be used solely as a downstream disburse-and-collect API with full reconciliation into a licensed Vanuatu commercial bank account, but not as the legal account repository for the VU-IC's share capital or operating liquidity. Any such EMI arrangement must be vetted for FATF grey-list alignment, sanctions screening, and beneficial-ownership transparency before implementation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's banking institution to the VU-IC's USD capital escrow account at an RBV-licensed Vanuatu bank. The wire narrative must explicitly state 'Share Capital Contribution — VU-IC — Share Subscription Agreement [reference/date]' so that the receiving bank can classify the transfer as direct investment equity and distinguish it from trading settlement. Pre-notification to the beneficiary bank is mandatory, and the opening compliance file must include: the board resolution authorising issuance of shares and opening of the bank account, the executed subscription agreement, the Company's register of members, source-of-funds confirmation from the remitting bank, and a shareholder-certified identification pack. Vanuatu imposes no exchange-control authorisation threshold and no minimum capital deposit verification for International Companies.For operational trade settlements, the preferred clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer; batch payroll and supplier payments are executed through host-to-host MT101 files and settled by the Vanuatu bank's correspondent banks in Australia, New Zealand or France. Commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are permissible for physical equipment acquisition (e.g. servers, VoIP hardware) but are not applicable to routine digital voice-streaming payments; for those, verified MT103 wires to licensors, hosting providers, legal counsel and technology contractors are sufficient. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements arrive as MT103 or ACH-equivalent credits into the VU-IC's operational account, and the treasury function shall thereafter make onward MT103 payments to group service providers using clearly referenced purchase invoices. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Vanuatu domestic law does not prescribe Local File or Master File thresholds. However, under the Economic Substance (International Companies) Act 2019, every International Company deriving more than VUV 30,000,000 (approximately USD 250,000) in gross annual revenue from a relevant activity must prepare an ESR substance file, including intercompany agreements, transfer pricing analyses and audited financial records. The documentation must demonstrate that the arm's-length remuneration of offshore founders, licensors and sub-contractors is commensurate with the functions performed in Vanuatu.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (International Companies) Act 2019 and accompanying Regulations require Vanuatu International Companies carrying on a relevant activity to satisfy a 'managed and directed' test, which is fulfilled through: (1) board meetings held in Vanuatu at least twice per year, with a quorum physically present; (2) at least two local directors or qualified employees based at the registered office; (3) a physical office and filing systems located in Vanuatu; (4) records of strategic decisions made in Vanuatu; and (5) an annual ESR return confirming that core income-generating activities for the voice-streaming app are not merely outsourced shell operations. Pure holding or zero-tax structures without these attributes are deemed non-compliant and lose the tax exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Zero-Tax Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the IC as a resident company; imposition of corporate income tax on all historical profits; ESR fines up to VUV 5,000,000 plus VUV 500,000 per month until compliance; strike-off of the company; and publication on the VFSC non-compliant list.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the company's unpaid taxes and penalties through civil restitution; wilful evasion triggers disqualification orders, departure prohibition orders (travel bans), and criminal prosecution carrying custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Compulsory deregistration under the International Companies Act; forfeiture of the annual fee; penalties of up to VUV 1,000,000 imposed jointly on the company and the registered agent; loss of preferential status for all contracting parties.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for outstanding fees and penalties, a five-year prohibition from acting as a director of any VFSC-regulated entity, and possible extradition from treaty partners where fraud is found.
AML / Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Asset freezing orders issued by the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit; civil penalties up to VUV 10,000,000 under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 14 of 2017; criminal imprisonment of up to 12 years for money laundering and terrorist financing offences.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for repatriation of misappropriated assets, confiscation of personal assets, and imprisonment for predicate offences; non-resident directors are subject to Interpol red notices and removal of passive investor protections.
Cross-Border Digital Tax Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Denial of any treaty or exemption benefits, retroactive VAT levies at rates up to 21% in user jurisdictions, penalties equal to 100% of the unpaid foreign tax, and inclusion of the entity on public blacklists of shell arrangements.

Director personal liability: Foreign revenue authorities may pierce the corporate veil where the IC is merely an instrumentality; directors face personal assessments in the effective-management jurisdiction, travel bans within the EU/OECD framework, and criminal liability for tax fraud.
