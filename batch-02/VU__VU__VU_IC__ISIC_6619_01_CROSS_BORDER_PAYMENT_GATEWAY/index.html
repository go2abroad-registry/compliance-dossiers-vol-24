Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Government of Vanuatu; online filing and taxpayer services via the Vanuatu Integrated Taxpayer and Tax System (VITTS) accessible through the official national portal https://www.gov.vu; company incorporation and licensing registry for International Companies: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Foreign beneficial owners incorporate an International Company (IC) with the VFSC under the International Companies Act [CAP 222] and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office address, and first director/beneficial owner filings. Step 2: Apply to the DCIR for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) using the VFSC incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, registered agent address, and declaration of beneficial ownership; submission is made through the VITTS portal or in person at DCIR. Step 3: Because Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax, no income-tax registration or annual corporate return applies. Step 4: The IC is statutorily exempt from VAT, stamp duty, and all other national taxes under the International Companies Act; if the IC were to make local taxable supplies exceeding the VAT threshold (prohibited in substance for ICs), mandatory VAT registration would be triggered. A pure international payment-gateway IC remains outside the VAT net and requires no VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None applicable. Vanuatu has no corporate income tax, no check-the-box election, and no domestic hybrid-mismatch or entity-classification election regime. The International Companies Act [CAP 222] grants automatic exemption from all Vanuatu taxes and duties, excluding prescribed registration and annual fees. An IC may elect in its constitution to restrict shareholding to non-residents, elect to convert into a domestic limited company, or elect voluntary VAT registration where it intends to make taxable supplies to Vanuatu residents; no Pillar Two, CFC, or transparent-entity elections exist under Vanuatu law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration for any person whose taxable turnover exceeds VT 5,000,000 in any 12-month rolling period (approximately USD 42,000); voluntary registration permitted below the threshold; ICs engaging exclusively in international business are exempt from VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to: (a) goods physically exported from Vanuatu under a valid Customs export declaration where the goods leave Vanuatu within the period prescribed by the VAT and Customs law; (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply, provided the services are not directly linked to immovable property in Vanuatu and not performed on movable property located in Vanuatu at the time of performance — cross-border merchant acquiring, payment processing, clearing, settlement, and gateway platform services to foreign PSPs and banks qualify where the recipient is outside Vanuatu; (c) internationally supplied services incidental to exported goods, including freight, insurance, and handling. The exporter must prove the non-resident status of the customer, the foreign-currency payment, and the actual cross-border provision of services, and must claim the zero rate on a fully documented VAT return with supporting export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • VAT zero-rated export tax invoice
  • Executed cross-border services agreement and non-resident customer declaration

No real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API reporting, digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control regime exists in Vanuatu. Suppliers must issue a VAT tax invoice under section 14 of the VAT Act [CAP 246] showing the supplier's name and address, VAT registration number, invoice date, description of goods/services, taxable amount excluding VAT, and the VAT charged. Export zero-rated invoices must cross-reference the approved Customs Export Declaration; records must be retained for audit by DCIR.

Vanuatu maintains no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline, but a 180-day inward-remittance window is the prudent benchmark for export-invoice settlement and VAT refund substantiation. DCIR may audit export zero-rating and refund claims under the VAT Act [CAP 246] and Customs Act [CAP 249]; failure to produce the customs and payment evidence bundle within the statutory periods retroactively makes the supply taxable and exposes the claimant to repayment of the refund with penalty and interest. Fraudulent export claims, false invoices, forged customs declarations, or simulated foreign-payment flows attract penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded, retrospective clawback of all refunds paid, interest accrual from the date of payment, cancellation of the trader's VAT registration, and criminal prosecution where officers or beneficial owners are shown to have knowingly participated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions for International Companies; no exchange control permission is required for capital injection, profit repatriation, or trade payments. Offshore accounts denominated in USD, AUD, and NZD are widely accepted, and profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to the bank's own evidence requirements (e.g., confirmation of tax exemption status and audited financials).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements exist in Vanuatu. International Companies may retain export receipts in any foreign currency, hold them offshore, or convert freely. The only obligations relate to record-keeping and anti-money laundering transaction reporting.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) as monetary authority and prudential regulator for banks; foreign exchange administration is liberalized, with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) overseeing the licensing of financial dealers and payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMI entities can be structured under the Vanuatu Financial Dealers Act and regulated by VFSC, but obtaining transactional banking relationships with domestic tier-1 banks is highly constrained due to global de-risking. Most well-known offshore EMIs (e.g., Payoneer, Stripe, PayPal) do not provide direct merchant accounts for Vanuatu-incorporated ICs. Legal constraints include a mandatory local registered agent, substance requirements under the International Companies Act, and enhanced due diligence by correspondent banks that often results in a 'no touch' policy for payment aggregators.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to a VUV or USD operational account at a licensed local bank. Under Vanuatu's balance-of-payments classification, the intended purpose code for equity injection is '5101' (investment in offshore entity), and for pre-export working capital '5215'. The remitting party must confirm that the funds are not derived from criminal activity; the receiving bank will typically require a board resolution, a formal share issuance letter, and a copy of the VFSC license to verify corporate capacity.Trade settlements and merchant payouts are cleared via domestic RTGS for intra-Vanuatu flows and via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments. For high-value procurement, commercial letters of credit (MT700) confirmed by an international bank are recommended. USD clearing is executed through the US clearing infrastructure (Fedwire/CHIPS); direct Visa/Mastercard settlement requires a Principal or Principal via a third-party processor with settlement banks in the US or UK, as Vanuatu lacks a local card-clearing system.Plain-vanilla outright forwards (USD/VUV, AUD/VUV) with licensed local banksNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing USD/VUV with offshore bank counterparties, permissible due to absence of exchange controlsCross-currency swaps (USD/AUD/EUR) with international banking counterparties, subject to bank credit approval
