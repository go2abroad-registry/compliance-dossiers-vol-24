Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Mapusagafou, Apia; official portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company obtains a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the SRA Taxpayer Registration Application, the certified certificate of incorporation under the International Companies Act 1987, registered office and licensed registered agent confirmation, and identity documents for directors and shareholders; the application is filed through the licensed registered agent, and the TIN must be issued before any VAGST-registered trading activity commences.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Samoa International Company exempt from income tax and stamp duty under the International Companies Act 1987; non-resident owned; no exchange control restrictions; VAGST exposure arises only where the company registers for and makes taxable supplies within Samoa.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available; the company may elect to register voluntarily for VAGST if making taxable supplies other than zero-rated exports; may elect to apply the Income Tax Ordinance 2015 only upon ceasing exempt offshore status; no hybrid mismatch elections are available in Samoa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 80,000 taxable turnover in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Samoa may be subject to mandatory VAGST registration or reverse-charge/withholding rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for the 0% VAGST rate where exported within 28 days of supply, evidenced by a customs export declaration; B2B services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated where performed outside Samoa or directly to a non-resident recipient; the industrial MRO catalog e-commerce export model qualifies as a zero-rated export supply subject to valid shipping and payment evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs/SRA clearance record)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export within 28 days of supply
  • Commercial invoice / tax invoice issued to the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing payment from abroad
  • Purchase orders or contracts with the non-resident buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance or API stamping mandate; taxpayers must file VAGST returns and pay electronically via the SRA portal; standard tax invoices and electronic invoices are required to substantiate input tax credits; all digital records must be retained for at least 7 years.

Under the VAGST Act 2008 and the Tax Administration Act 2015, fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger a penalty of 150% of the tax avoided, a late payment penalty of 1.5% per month, retrospective clawback of refunds with statutory interest, criminal fines up to WST 10,000, and imprisonment; the SRA audit assessment window is 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa's currency (Tala, WST) operates under a managed float with exchange control restrictions 24 months compliant with the IMF Article VIII for current account transactions, which are freely permitted through authorized dealers subject to underlying document verification. Capital account flows (including non-resident direct equity injections, loans, and repatriation) require prior CBS approval unless a statutory exemption applies. For an International Company (IC) under the Companies Act 2001, a blanket non-resident exemption is available: the IC is treated as non-resident, may hold foreign currency accounts, and may remit dividends and operating profits freely without CBS consent or Samoan withholding tax, provided the IC is beneficially owned by non-residents, conducts no domestic business, and settles all transactions in foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must receive full proceeds within 6 months from shipment date and surrender the foreign currency to an authorized dealer in Tala, subject to CBS exemption directives. The Samoa IC, operating as a non-resident entity, is excluded from the mandatory Tala conversion under the standard non-resident foreign currency account facility; nevertheless, the bank will file Ex-1 Inward Remittance reports for statistics and to evidence that export receipts (B2B MRO e-commerce payments) are credited to the IC's foreign currency account within the 6-month statutory timeline to preserve the exemption.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) – Falemata'aga o Tupe Tutotonu, administering the Exchange Control Act 1976, Exchange Control Regulations 1977, and the Financial Institutions Act 2012; licensed authorized dealers are the sole channels for foreign exchange transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility with legal constraints. Samoa has no EMI licensing framework; the CBS only recognizes banks licensed under the Financial Institutions Act 2012, and offshore digital EMIs cannot obtain a Samoa banking licence or provide a domestic WST account. An offshore EMI (e.g., HK/UK/Singapore-regulated) may be acceptable as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail for the IC's USD B2B transactions, but local banks will not allow the EMI to clear via Samoa's domestic system, and the IC must still maintain a Samoa physical-bank account to maintain statutory substance and to satisfy the CBS's exchange-control reporting. Contractual and operational risk arise from the EMI's independent AML/CFT obligations and the absence of Samoa privy; therefore, use EMIs only for non-core merchant card settlement, never for escrow or capital buffers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from foreign shareholder is routed by SWIFT MT103 to the IC's USD operational account at a Samoa authorized dealer, with the purpose code 'Capital Injection – International Company (non-resident)' and a clear reference to the IC's Certificate of Incorporation. The receiving bank files the CBS inward-remittance form (Ex-1) and verifies source of funds (shareholder bank statement, board resolution approving capital contribution, and a capital-account ledger). The injection is exempt from CBS prior approval because the IC is non-resident and operates in USD; however, the bank will annotate 'Exchange Control Exempt – IC Account' on the credit advice to retain the exemption status. For payments exceeding USD 250,000 or from high-risk jurisdictions, the bank will request a specific legal opinion and a source-of-wealth statement before crediting.Clearing rails combine (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments and refunds to foreign vendors, (ii) SWIFT MT700/MT710 documentary credits for high-value MRO inventory purchases where the seller requires an LC, (iii) local cheques/EFTPOS for Samoan operational expenses (duty, agent fees), and (iv) global card-acquiring rails (Visa/Mastercard via a Tier-1 acquirer) for B2B customer card payments. The IC will maintain a liquidity buffer in a USD Nostro account in Apia and a secondary USD account at a Hong Kong or Singapore correspondent bank to ensure fast clearing for the Pacific Rim trade corridor; all disbursements above USD 50,000 will be dual-authorised and processed through the authorized dealer's electronic banking platform, and the CBS Ex-1 Outward Remittance report will be submitted automatically for statistical purposes.Outright FX forward (USD/WST) with a Samoa authorized dealer – for converting operational surpluses to Tala for local expenses, tenor up to 12 months, no premium, requiring a 10–20% margin.Offshore non-deliverable forward (USD/WST NDF) – permitted because the IC is non-resident and the WST spot market is thin; cash-settled in USD with a major international bank counterparty (HSBC/ANZ institutional desk) for hedging WST exposure without affecting Samoa's reserve management.Cross-currency swap (USD/WST) with a licensed commercial bank – available only for transactions exceeding USD 1 million and tenor of 1–3 years, subject to counterparty credit approval and CBS informational filing; used mainly for future inventory-financing facilities.FX options on USD/WST (limited) – permissible with the same non-resident ICS exemption, but constrained by market depth; authorize only via professional counterparties and board approval.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (standard resident company rate under the Samoa Income Tax Act 2015); effective 0% for a valid International Company (IC) on income derived outside Samoa.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions made by a valid tax-exempt International Company; the general Samoan dividend withholding rate for a taxable resident company payer is 15%, subject to any applicable DTA relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory Local File or Master File threshold applies to a tax-exempt Samoa IC. In a taxable-audit context, Samoa transfer-pricing rules require arm’s-length documentation where connected-party transactions exceed a relevant material threshold or where the Commissioner issues an information notice; the IC exemption removes routine documentation obligations for foreign-source trading.

Economic substance scope: Samoa’s International Companies economic substance framework applies to ICs conducting relevant activities such as distribution, service centre, financing, headquarters, shipping, or IP holding. A B2B MRO catalog e-commerce IC must demonstrate sufficient board governance, premises, employees, and business records proportionate to its revenue if it claims any Samoan economic substance or obtains a Samoan tax residence certificate; otherwise, it must document that its principal management and commercial functions occur outside Samoa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Exemption Abuse / Prohibited Samoan Business Activity

Statutory sanction: Loss of International Company tax exemption; income taxed at 27%; assessment of all Samoan-source taxes, interest, late payment penalties, and potential strike-off for unauthorized trading.

Director personal liability: Directors and persons controlling operations may be personally liable for unpaid tax where the IC is a sham, exposed to civil restitution, director disqualification, travel restrictions, and criminal fines.
Annual Registry and Fee Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late fee surcharge, automatic striking-off, cessation of legal capacity, and confiscation/vesting of company assets in the Registrar pending restoration.

Director personal liability: Directors who continue to trade while struck off are personally liable for contracts and can be subject to restoration penalties and disqualification orders.
Shell Company Misuse / Defective Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalties, financial penalties in WST, compulsory deregistration, and refusal of Samoan tax residence certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents who sign false substance declarations are subject to criminal fines, imprisonment, and civil claims for tax lost by any foreign beneficiary jurisdiction.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties, asset forfeiture, mandatory reporting sanctions, and revocation of the registered agent’s license under Samoa AML/CFT legislation.

Director personal liability: Company officers and nominee directors can face custodial sentences, travel bans, personal fines, and liability for money laundering where the IC is used as a conduit.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment, penalty tax on under-reported income, interest from the due date, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion where false invoices or records are produced.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false transfer pricing documentation may be held jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall, disqualified, and imprisoned where fraudulent evasion is shown.
