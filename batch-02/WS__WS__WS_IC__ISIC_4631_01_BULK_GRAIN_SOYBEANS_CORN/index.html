Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue, Government of Samoa (Matagaluega o Tupe Maua), Apia, Samoa. The Ministry is the single national tax and customs administration responsible for VAGST, income tax, stamp duty, and customs duty. Taxpayer-facing services are accessible through the Ministry of Revenue's online e-Services portal (Samoa Revenue Online Services), and customs lodgements are effected via the ASYCUDA World single-window system at Apia and the principal seaports and airport.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident beneficial owner of a Samoa International Company (IC): (1) appoint a licensed registered agent in Samoa as mandated under the International Companies Act 1987-1988; (2) file the memorandum and articles of association and the registered office/registered agent particulars with the Samoa International Financial Authority (SIFA); (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and pay the prescribed government incorporation and annual license fees; (4) obtain, where commercially prudent, a written tax-exemption or non-residence confirmation from the Ministry of Revenue evidencing that the IC is outside the Samoan income tax and VAGST net; and (5) note that no separate Samoan TIN is required for the offshore brokerage business. Only if the IC transacts taxable supplies within Samoa must it obtain a TIN from the Ministry of Revenue, register for VAGST once turnover reaches the threshold, and secure a domestic Business Licence.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (statutory offshore exemption; the IC is treated as a non-resident, tax-exempt entity in respect of all non-Samoan-source trading income and is prohibited from carrying on business with Samoan residents without forfeiting its exempt status). — For a non-resident beneficial owner of a Samoa International Company (IC): (1) appoint a licensed registered agent in Samoa as mandated under the International Companies Act 1987-1988; (2) file the memorandum and articles of association and the registered office/registered agent particulars with the Samoa International Financial Authority (SIFA); (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and pay the prescribed government incorporation and annual license fees; (4) obtain, where commercially prudent, a written tax-exemption or non-residence confirmation from the Ministry of Revenue evidencing that the IC is outside the Samoan income tax and VAGST net; and (5) note that no separate Samoan TIN is required for the offshore brokerage business. Only if the IC transacts taxable supplies within Samoa must it obtain a TIN from the Ministry of Revenue, register for VAGST once turnover reaches the threshold, and secure a domestic Business Licence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (statutory offshore exemption; the IC is treated as a non-resident, tax-exempt entity in respect of all non-Samoan-source trading income and is prohibited from carrying on business with Samoan residents without forfeiting its exempt status).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elective classification exists in Samoan law; the exemption of an International Company is statutory and automatic, not elective. The only material election is the surrender/revocation of exempt status: an IC may file a notice with SIFA under the International Companies Act 1987-1988 to convert into an ordinary domestic company, after which it becomes fully liable under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the VAGST Act 2008, and must obtain a Business Licence and register for VAGST when threshold turnover is reached. Samoa has not enacted dedicated hybrid-mismatch legislation beyond its BEPS Inclusive Framework commitments, so tax planning is confined to the statutory IC exemption and cannot duplicate deductions through hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 / WS$ 100,000 (Samoan Tala) of annual taxable supplies for a person carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa. A tax-exempt International Company engaged in offshore bulk grain brokerage of soybeans and yellow corn is outside the scope of VAGST because the supply of cargo brokerage services is performed entirely outside Samoa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Samoa are zero-rated, and services supplied to non-residents or performed entirely outside Samoa are zero-rated or otherwise outside the territorial scope of VAGST. For the Samoa IC (bulk vessel brokerage for soybeans (HS 1201) and yellow corn (HS 1005)), commission income from chartering and brokerage services negotiated and performed offshore is not subject to 15% VAGST; a VAGST-registered supplier claiming the 0% rate must demonstrate that the recipient is a non-resident not registered for VAGST in Samoa and that the service is consumed outside Samoa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document lodged in ASYCUDA World) for any goods physically transiting Samoan ports
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill, supported by the Charter Party, evidencing ocean shipment of the bulk grain cargo
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the soybeans/corn, quantity, grade, vessel, and consignee terms
  • Certificate of Origin and Phytosanitary Certificate (where the destination customs regime requires them)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT advice proving the export proceeds have been received through a licensed bank in Samoa in accordance with exchange-control requirements

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous-transaction-control e-invoicing mandate is currently in force in Samoa. The Ministry of Revenue operates an e-filing platform (Samoa Revenue Online Services) for TIN/VAGST administration, and all customs declarations must be lodged electronically in ASYCUDA World, but B2B invoices remain paper/PDF based. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices containing the prescribed particulars; there is no government API requirement for real-time invoice clearing.

Zero-rating is granted only on satisfactory documentary proof. In audit, if the exporter cannot substantiate the physical export or the offshore-recipient supply, the Ministry of Revenue retrospectively cancels the zero rating, assesses VAGST at 15%, imposes statutory late-payment interest, and recovers any refund paid. Deliberately false declarations by brokers or exporters attract evasion penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2012 (commonly up to 200% of the evaded tax) and criminal fines or imprisonment, while false customs export declarations may additionally trigger penal duties, forfeiture of cargo, or suspension of customs electronic lodgement privileges. The limitation period for reopening assessments and clawing back fraudulently claimed export refunds is generally four years, extended in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) operates under a managed float with exchange control administered by CBOS. Current account payments are generally permitted through authorized dealers (commercial banks), but supporting documentation and approved transaction purpose codes are required. Capital account transfers, including inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, and profit remittances by Samoan resident entities, require prior CBOS approval or must be processed through a licensed Samoa commercial bank under delegated exchange-control authority. An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 and classified as non-resident is treated as outside the Samoa exchange-control perimeter for its wholly offshore business; however, once an IC transacts in WST, maintains a domestic tala account, or receives funds into Samoa, convertibility is subject to the standard current/capital account review, meaning dividend and branch remittance requests must be supported by audited financial statements, tax status certificates, and a duly completed CBOS exchange-application form.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exported from Samoa, export proceeds must be repatriated to a licensed Samoan bank within 30 days of the date of export and 100% of the proceeds must be surrendered for WST at the prevailing market exchange rate unless CBOS has granted a specific retention or conversion exemption. This regime applies to Samoan-sourced exports of goods or commodities. For the WS_IC bulk grain and soybean/corn trading entity, the underlying goods are foreign-sourced and transshipped outside Samoa; accordingly, no statutory surrender obligation applies for the offshore contract proceeds, provided the IC does not maintain residency, the board and management are non-resident, and no funds are remitted into Samoa in settlement of the underlying trade.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBOS), acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI and e-money institutions are not licensed, registered, or recognized by the Central Bank of Samoa; therefore, no licensed Samoan clearing relationship can be established through an EMI. A WS_IC may maintain foreign-currency funds with an overseas EMI solely for non-Samoan operating payments, but this raises source-of-funds and sub-account scrutiny during any later Samoa banking onboarding. Samoan banks will not treat EMI statements as sufficient evidence of trade settlement, historical transactions, or beneficial-ownership proof.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for a WS_IC capital injection or shareholder loan is typically a SWIFT MT103/MT202 message from the contributor's bank to a Samoa licensed commercial bank's correspondent account, using the account-holder name, the IC, and the purpose code 'Capital contribution to non-resident International Company.' The receiving Samoa bank must obtain the standard exchange-control declaration if funds are to be converted to WST or credited to a WST account; if held in a non-resident foreign-currency account, CBOS approval is not required. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Companies (IC) under the International Companies Act 2007 tax exemption

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions made by a tax-exempt International Company to both resident and non-resident shareholders under the International Companies Act 2007 exemption. A domestic dividend withholding tax of 15% would apply only if the IC loses its exempt status and becomes taxable as a Samoa domestic company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory Local File or Master File threshold applies to a tax-exempt IC under Samoa law; prudent transfer pricing documentation should be retained for any related-party brokerage commission or commodity-supply transaction exceeding WST 250,000 (approximately USD 95,000) to defend the arm's length result in an audit or tax-exemption review.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations apply to International Companies conducting relevant activities. Bulk grain, soybean, and yellow corn brokerage is treated as a distribution and service centre activity; the IC must demonstrate: (i) a physical office and licensed registered agent in Samoa; (ii) effective management and control exercised in Samoa through local board meetings; (iii) adequate local qualified employees; and (iv) sufficient local operating expenditure. Failure to satisfy the economic substance test results in penalties up to WST 500,000, automatic exchange of information with foreign tax authorities, and potential strike-off of the IC.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abuse of IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: Assessment of back taxes at the prevailing corporate rate plus penalty of up to 100% and statutory interest; criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act with fines up to WST 500,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly arranged the sham are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid taxes; courts may pierce the corporate veil, order civil restitution of diverted profits, impose travel bans, and imprison directors if fraud is proven.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Annual penalty up to WST 500,000 under Samoa's economic substance regime, possible penalty based on gross income, compulsory reporting to foreign tax authorities, and deregistration by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents may be individually fined up to WST 100,000; a director who certifies false economic substance information may be barred from managing an International Company for 5 years and may face a travel ban pending investigation.
AML/CFT and Fraudulent Trade Finance

Statutory sanction: Offence under Samoa's Money Laundering and Prevention of Terrorism Act: fines up to WST 2 million, forfeiture of proceeds, and imprisonment up to 15 years; implicated banks may be fined and de-risked.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in laundering or predicate fraud are personally criminally liable; assets can be traced and confiscated, director passports may be seized through court-ordered travel bans, and principal offenders face imprisonment of 10-15 years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Invoice Fraud

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment of taxable profit, if any, plus late-payment interest; administrative penalty of up to 100% of understated tax; criminal fraud penalties of WST 500,000 if invoices are used to support fraudulent letter-of-credit draws.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false invoices and tax or transfer pricing declarations may be made personally liable for adjusted tax amounts; serious mispricing involving fraud exposes them to civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 7 years.
