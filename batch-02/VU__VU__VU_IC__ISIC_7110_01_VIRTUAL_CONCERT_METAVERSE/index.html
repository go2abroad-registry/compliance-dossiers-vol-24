Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Department (VRCD), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official tax portal: https://vrcd.gov.vu; corporate registry regulator: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company (IC) with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]; VFSC issues the Certificate of Incorporation and company number. Step 2: Apply to the Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Department (VRCD) for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) using the non-resident taxpayer registration form together with certified incorporation documents, registered agent details, and director/ultimate beneficial ownership declarations. Step 3: Assess VAT registration obligation under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 246]: register for VAT using Form VAT-01 if taxable supplies equal or exceed VT 5,000,000 in any twelve-month period, or voluntarily if the IC makes only zero-rated offshore digital supplies; VRCD issues the VAT registration certificate and VAT identification number. Step 4: Submit the IC annual return to VFSC confirming no Vanuatu-resident business activity or local asset holdings, and maintain ongoing VAT filings even where all supplies are zero-rated exports.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Vanuatu International Company is statutorily exempt from all income, corporate, capital gains, withholding, and stamp taxes under section 47 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222]. Vanuatu levies no national income tax, so the IC is not a pass-through in the traditional sense; it is a tax-exempt offshore vehicle. VAT is a distinct consumption tax: the IC becomes a VAT taxpayer only when making taxable supplies in Vanuatu or importing goods above the statutory threshold; supplies of digital services to non-residents outside Vanuatu are zero-rated or outside the scope of VAT.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no check-the-box or US-style hybrid entity election. Available mechanisms: (1) Voluntary VAT registration under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 246] for an IC making only zero-rated export supplies, enabling input credit recovery and simplified compliance; (2) Conversion of an IC into a domestic company with VFSC approval, thereby losing the tax-exempt status and becoming subject to domestic corporate taxation rules (no income tax is currently imposed); (3) Maintenance of the IC's offshore status by conducting business wholly outside Vanuatu, with no local establishment, so that supplies fall outside the Vanuatu VAT net; (4) Where foreign hybrid mismatch rules apply, no specific Vanuatu election exists; the IC's statutory exemption certificate and offshore structuring are the operative protections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 5,000,000 (five million Vatu) of taxable supplies in any consecutive twelve-month period; non-resident ICs supplying only zero-rated offshore digital services may nevertheless voluntarily register. Import VAT is payable at the border regardless of any turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 246], the export of goods is zero-rated, and services are zero-rated where the recipient obtains the benefit outside Vanuatu or the service is supplied to a non-resident who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply. For the virtual concert metaverse (ISIC 7110 / NAICS 711310), the IC's supplies of digital event access and ticket tokens qualify for zero-rating if: (i) the purchaser is a non-resident not present in Vanuatu at the time of supply; (ii) payment is received in foreign currency or USDC through offshore payment rails; (iii) the performance is delivered digitally via a global metaverse platform with no physical Vanuatu venue; and (iv) the IC maintains contemporaneous records demonstrating the offshore recipient and foreign receipt. No physical customs export declaration applies to intangible digital services; the zero-rating evidence bundle substitutes commercial and payment documentation for the customs export documentation used for tangible goods.

  • VFSC Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing confirming International Company tax-exempt status
  • Executed virtual-event ticketing and access agreement together with metaverse platform terms identifying each purchaser as a non-resident outside Vanuatu
  • Blockchain or platform ticket-minting sales ledger evidencing issuance of virtual concert access tokens to offshore buyers
  • Stripe and cryptocurrency (USDC) payment gateway settlement statements showing offshore receipt in foreign currency
  • Bank statements and inward remittance certificates demonstrating export proceeds repatriation within 180 days
  • VAT-compliant zero-rated tax invoices bearing the statutory declaration of export or offshore supply
  • Completed VRCD zero-rating substantiation schedule and annual return reconciliations

Vanuatu does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government-authorised e-invoicing, API-based tax clearance, or digital stamping for VAT invoices. Tax invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form provided they satisfy the content requirements of the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 246]. Customs import and export declarations are processed electronically through the ASYCUDA World customs management system, but this is not a VAT clearance mechanism. For the virtual concert metaverse, zero-rating substantiation relies on standard VAT invoices, payment gateway records (Stripe/USDC), and blockchain ticket-minting audit trails rather than any national e-invoicing infrastructure.

The VRCD may audit zero-rated claims, and retrospective assessments can claw back wrongly claimed zero-rating with interest, effectively recharacterising the supply as taxable at 12.5% VAT. Fraudulent export zero-rating or refund claims attract penalty tax of up to 200% of the tax evaded, daily default interest, criminal fines, and imprisonment for directors and other responsible persons. Failure to maintain the required documentation bundle, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory term, rebuts the zero-rating presumption and exposes the IC to full VAT assessment with penalties. As the International Company is otherwise tax-exempt, the principal compliance exposure is VAT, late-payment penalties, deregistration, and adverse VFSC regulatory findings affecting the IC's good standing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains a fully liberalized capital account with no exchange-control legislation, no current-account licensing, and no restriction on inward or outward remittances. The VUV is fully convertible for residents and non-residents, and the RBV operates a de facto conventional peg expressed against a trade-weighted basket comprising principally the AUD, USD, NZD, EUR, and JPY, publishing a daily official mid-rate. Profit and dividend remittances from an International Company (IC) are unrestricted; an IC is statutorily prohibited from carrying on business with Vanuatu residents and therefore repatriates exclusively to offshore counterparties. Foreign-currency accounts (USD, AUD, EUR, NZD) may be held freely by ICs, and there is no requirement to convert export or ticket-sale receipts into VUV, other than incidental settlements of bank charges and fees. Practical friction exists only in correspondent-bank KYC screening of outward USD wires and in bank-level due diligence on cryptocurrency-linked inflows, not in any RBV approval gate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None under Vanuatu law. There is no statutory timeline, minimum repatriation ratio, mandatory conversion obligation, or surrender requirement for export proceeds or foreign-currency revenue. A Virtual Concert Metaverse IC may lawfully retain 100% of ticket-sale proceeds, licensing fees, and USDC-derived fiat revenue in offshore USD accounts, offshore VUV accounts, or at an overseas EMI. Banks nonetheless impose private-contractual screening on cryptocurrency-linked inflows and may require a documented treasury policy for crypto-to-fiat conversions; these are bank policy requirements, not RBV controls, and do not alter the statutory position of full external convertibility.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 142] and operating as the monetary authority, the banker to licensed banks, and the licensing and supervisory authority for commercial banks under the Financial Institutions Act 2004. The RBV administers the exchange-rate framework for the national currency, the Vanuatu Vatu (ISO 4217 code VUV), and is flanked by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), which regulates International Companies and virtual-asset activity. Vanuatu has no separate foreign-exchange administration; foreign-exchange policy is executed entirely through RBV-authorized commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW for a pure Vanuatu IC. Major digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Mercury, Payoneer) generally impose 12-24 months of operating history, exclude or heavily scrutinize VU-incorporated entities due to residual offshore-vehicle and correspondent-banking risk, and none accept USDC directly. A VU IC with a VFSC-licensed registered agent, CRS self-certification, and audited financial statements may secure an account with Currenxie or Airwallex through an introducing intermediary only on evidence of economic substance and clean source of funds. Stripe does not support Vanuatu-incorporated merchants; a Hong Kong or Singapore SPV is required as merchant-of-record, settling to the VU IC via intercompany MT103 transfers. Legal constraints: the Financial Institutions Act 2004 prohibits unlicensed payment institutions from holding client funds in Vanuatu, so the IC must use EMIs licensed in their own jurisdictions (FCA, ASIC, MAS, FinCEN), and must not accept card settlements into the Vanuatu domestic VUV accounts without a full CDD trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route via SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's or SPV's bank account to the VU IC account held at a Vanuatu commercial bank, with the remittance narrative 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION - SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN - VU_IC'. Vanuatu does not operate a statutory purpose-code regime, but the beneficiary bank records the stated purpose for CDD; RBV foreign-investment approval is not required because the IC is an offshore entity under the International Companies Act and no controlled-business or foreign-investment clearance attaches to it. Supporting bundle: board resolution authorizing the share issuance or loan; subscription agreement or signed loan note; source-of-funds evidence for the remitter (six months of bank statements and wealth documentation); updated register of members after allotment; confirmation that the IC faces no minimum paid-up capital requirement. USDC-denominated capital injections cannot be accepted through domestic bank rails; the USDC must first be liquidated into USD fiat at a VFSC-licensed or exchange-regime-regulated VASP before wiring to the Vanuatu account.Primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 network through RBV-licensed commercial banks; USD transfers clear via correspondent banks in New York or Australia, and AUD transfers via Australian correspondents; VUV balances convert and settle domestically. Vanuatu imposes no exchange-control approval, no stamp duty on outward wire transfers, and no withholding tax at source for an IC, so full gross amounts may leave the country for content creators, licensing partners, technology vendors, and professional advisors. Commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are not required for low-risk digital services and should be reserved for high-value physical merchandise contingencies. For USDC disbursements to artists and technology vendors, use on-chain USDC clearing over Ethereum, Solana, or Stellar from the VU IC treasury wallet; fiat-bridge conversions above USD 50,000 must be executed through a regulated OTC desk, recorded in the AML/CFT log, and referenced to the corresponding on-chain transaction, never co-mingled with same-day MT103 settlement without documentary linkage. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW for a pure Vanuatu IC. Major digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Mercury, Payoneer) generally impose 12-24 months of operating history, exclude or heavily scrutinize VU-incorporated entities due to residual offshore-vehicle and correspondent-banking risk, and none accept USDC directly. A VU IC with a VFSC-licensed registered agent, CRS self-certification, and audited financial statements may secure an account with Currenxie or Airwallex through an introducing intermediary only on evidence of economic substance and clean source of funds. Stripe does not support Vanuatu-incorporated merchants; a Hong Kong or Singapore SPV is required as merchant-of-record, settling to the VU IC via intercompany MT103 transfers. Legal constraints: the Financial Institutions Act 2004 prohibits unlicensed payment institutions from holding client funds in Vanuatu, so the IC must use EMIs licensed in their own jurisdictions (FCA, ASIC, MAS, FinCEN), and must not accept card settlements into the Vanuatu domestic VUV accounts without a full CDD trail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — International Companies (ICs) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] are exempt from corporate income tax; Vanuatu levies no national corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no branch profits tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory dividend withholding tax — Vanuatu has no dividend withholding tax, no corporation tax on distributions, and no remittance tax. An IC may distribute profits to non-resident shareholders free of any Vanuatu withholding or final tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ACS and the annual government fee are due on or before each incorporation anniversary date. A short statutory grace period is customary; thereafter VFSC imposes late filing penalties and late fee surcharges. Failure to file and pay exposes the IC to automatic strike-off under s. 119 IC Act.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return and payment due within 21 days after the end of each calendar quarter. Non-resident suppliers of e-services to Vanuatu consumers must register under the non-resident VAT procedure, either directly or through a fiscal representative.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold exists — Vanuatu has no transfer pricing legislation, no OECD BEPS-aligned Local File/Master File filing obligation, and no transfer pricing penalty regime. Nevertheless, if the IC charges management fees, platform royalties, or ticket-asset licensing fees to an overseas parent or affiliate, the parent jurisdiction will apply its own thresholds (e.g., EUR 7.5 million intra-group services in the EU or USD 10 million in the US) and may request the IC's preparer-level documentation. A practical threshold: prepare contemporaneous benchmark documentation whenever annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed USD 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu has no locally codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) similar to the UAE/DE or Barbados regimes. However, substance is enforced indirectly through the AML/CFT Act, the VASP Act 2020, and international listing reviews. An IC operating a virtual concert ticketing business must demonstrate in Vanuatu: licensed registered agent, local registered office, board meetings or director resolutions respecting the IC's affairs, availability of accounting records, and, where digital token custody is involved, a VASP licence. Absence of real decision-making in Port Vila renders the IC a shell in the eyes of foreign CFC, GAAR, and late-tax regimes, and may trigger VASP licence denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Foreign CFC and GAAR Exposure

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: revocation of the tax exemption certificate, strike-off of the IC, and administrative fines for false declarations under the IC Act. Foreign jurisdictions: reclassification of the IC as a Controlled Foreign Corporation, full attribution of undistributed profit, underpayment interest, and substantial accuracy-related penalties (up to 50% of the tax shortfall in some jurisdictions).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations face personal tax assessments after piercing the corporate veil, civil restitution of diverted profits, and disqualification orders from VFSC. Where a warrant is issued by a foreign revenue authority, directors may encounter travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition-related detention.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: VFSC administrative dissolution of the IC, restoration penalties, and forfeiture of limited liability protection for debts incurred after the shell character is established. Under the AML/CFT Act and Proceeds of Crime Act, funds routed through a shell are subject to confiscation orders; financial institutions are required to de-risk the account once shell indicators are reported.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be jointly and severally liable for the IC's liabilities if the entity is treated as a sham; directors face fiduciary breach claims, confiscation of personal assets, and disqualification from managing any Vanuatu entity for a statutory period.
AML/CFT and Unlicensed VASP Operation

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed VASP activity is a criminal offence under the VASP Act, punishable by significant fines and terms of imprisonment. Money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act carries a maximum penalty of imprisonment up to 20 years and a fine of up to VUV 100 million. VFIU may issue freezing orders against the IC's merchant accounts and crypto wallets; VFSC may revoke all registrations and prohibit the entity from further trading.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners engaging in or wilfully blind to USDC laundering face personal criminal prosecution, imprisonment, travel restrictions, and placement on the VFIU watch-list. Corporate veil-piercing applies where the IC is used as an instrument of money laundering, rendering officers personally liable for civil restitution to victims.
VAT Evasion on In-Country Digital Supplies

Statutory sanction: The Revenue Commissioner can assess the uncollected 12.5% VAT, impose an additional tax shortfall penalty of up to 100% of the evaded VAT in aggravated cases, add statutory interest, and direct the payment processor to withhold remittances. The IC may also lose its tax exemption certificate for conducting local business without approval.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign VAT declarations concealing Vanuatu consumer ticket sales are personally liable for civil penalties; the Business Licence Act exposes unlicensed individuals to fines per day of unlicensed trading. Persistent evasion can trigger criminal conviction, restriction orders preventing departure from Vanuatu, and personal bankruptcy proceedings for restitution.
