Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MaAT), Government of Samoa; online portal: MaAT Revenue Online Services at https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

An International Company incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax and VAGST and therefore no income tax TIN is issued. The entity obtains its functional identifier through the Samoa Financial Services Authority licensed service provider and the Companies Registry; the International Company Registry Number serves as the corporate identifier. If the IC nevertheless engages in taxable domestic activity, it must apply for a VAGST TIN on the Ministry for Revenue registration form; however, under the IC's standard offshore tax-exempt status such VAGST registration is not applicable.

Default tax classification

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a check-the-box regime or hybrid mismatch election mechanism. An International Company can elect to become a licensed offshore bank, international trust, or offshore insurer only by obtaining a specific sectoral licence from the Financial Services Authority, which subjects the entity to tailored supervisory conditions without altering its offshore tax-exempt classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied by a VAGST-registered person are zero-rated where the goods are physically exported from Samoa within 30 days of supply; this includes cross-border e-commerce sales of dietary supplements and nutraceuticals. Offshore services supplied to non-residents, including digital commerce support, logistics coordination, and drop-shipment facilitation performed outside Samoa, also qualify as zero-rated supplies. An International Company in its standard tax-exempt status has no VAGST liability and does not need to claim zero-rating; if voluntarily VAGST-registered, it must retain documentary proof of physical export or offshore service delivery within Samoa's evidentiary rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged through Samoa Customs (Asycuda World)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Samoa
  • Commercial Invoice and VAGST tax invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Samoan licensed commercial bank
  • Export permit or sectoral licence for supplements and nutraceuticals where required by Samoa's quarantine and pharmaceutical control authorities

Samoa does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API-based digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls for VAGST. Registered suppliers must issue VAGST tax invoices containing prescribed particulars, supplier and purchaser TINs, VAGST-exclusive and –inclusive amounts, and the VAGST rate; electronic invoices in standard formats are accepted. Export clearances are processed through the Asycuda World single window at Samoa Customs, but this is not a domestic e-invoicing clearance mandate.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to retrospective assessment within an 8-year audit window, a 150% shortfall penalty, late-payment penalties, and interest exposure, as well as criminal prosecution under the VAGST Act 1998 and the Crimes Act 2016 with fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment. The Central Bank of Samoa also imposes exchange-control penalties on exporters who fail to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, including denial of export permits, monetary penalties, and potential suspension of licensed trading status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a managed exchange rate regime under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and Exchange Control Regulations 1981. The Samoan Tala (WST) is not freely convertible and is subject to transaction-specific approval. Current account payments for trade in goods, services, and profit remittances are generally permitted with documented substantiation. Capital account transactions, including inbound share subscriptions and outbound repatriation of capital, require Central Bank approval. Profit remittance from a Samoa International Company is allowed only after evidence of after-tax profits, audited financial statements, tax clearance from the Ministry of Revenue, and an approved FX-3 exchange control application. Nutraceutical e-commerce revenues invoiced in USD may be retained in foreign currency with Central Bank approval; conversion into WST must occur through an authorized dealing bank at the official exchange rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Samoa's Exchange Control Regulations, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa within 6 months from the date of shipment. 100% of the export proceeds must be credited to a Samoan licensed bank account. Where proceeds are received in foreign currency, 100% must be surrendered to an authorized dealer in exchange for Samoan Tala unless the Central Bank grants a specific exemption for working capital retention in USD. Regular repatriation reporting is required on a monthly or quarterly basis, including submission of underlying shipping and sales documentation supporting the receipts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Samoa has no standalone fintech or electronic money institution licensing framework. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut, or Wise are not recognized as authorized dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations and cannot provide regulated cross-border clearing services for a Samoa International Company without an underlying local bank account. A licensed Samoan commercial bank must serve as the primary clearing and settlement institution, with offshore EMIs used only as auxiliary payment collection channels and strictly subject to local bank compliance acceptance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be wired from the ultimate beneficial owner's bank account directly to the Samoa International Company's Capital Escrow Account via SWIFT MT103 using Balance of Payments purpose code 'K.2 – Capital Account / Share Capital Contribution'. The remittance must reference the shareholder's name and the entity registration number. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation (Local File and Master File) is mandatory where aggregate related-party transactions exceed WST 5,000,000 in an income year; below this threshold, an annual arm's-length declaration and comparability analysis must nevertheless be retained where any single controlled transaction exceeds WST 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has adopted OECD-compliant Economic Substance Regulations applicable to International Companies carrying on geographic mobile activities such as distribution and e-commerce; an IC must demonstrate core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Samoa - including local board meetings, a qualified local registered agent, substantive decision-making records, and a physical office - or the Ministry of Revenue may re-characterize the exempt income as fully taxable at 27%, disallow the annual-fee exemption, and impose back-dated assessments with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Misdeclared Source of Income

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of 200% of the underpaid amount plus monthly late-payment interest; criminal conviction may impose a fine of up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment for a term of up to 5 years; the IC's tax-exempt status is revoked retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations are personally jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, civil restitution, and any fraudulent-transfer recovery; travel bans may be imposed pending recovery and imprisonment of up to 5 years applies to wilful evasion.
Shell Company Abuse and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the entire net revenue as deemed Samoan-source income assessed at 27% with back-dated interest; imposition of a 50% accuracy-related penalty; cancellation of the International Company certificate and strike-off from the Register.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced upon proof of sham or façade; directors face personal assessments for the entire underpaid tax, restitution of diverted profits, a 3-year directorship disqualification, and a travel ban until all liabilities are settled.
AML/CFT Failure and High-Risk Merchant Processing

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to WST 500,000 for each violation, suspension or revocation of the merchant-processing licence, freezing of processor settlement funds, and referral to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit for criminal money-laundering prosecution carrying imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for civil restitution of laundered funds, personal fines equal to the transaction values involved, a 5-year industry ban, and imprisonment where knowledge of the AML breach is established.
Unlicensed Pharmaceutical and Sanitary Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Seizure and destruction of non-conforming products; fines up to WST 200,000 per product line; suspension of the trading licence; referral to the pharmaceutical regulator for criminal sanctions of up to 3 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors incur personal conviction, personal fines, prohibition from directing any licensed medical or food business, and civil liability for consumer harm claims where the corporate entity is insolvent.
Annual Return Default and Loss of Good Standing

Statutory sanction: Automatic strike-off from the International Companies Register, forfeiture of the tax-exempt status, restoration fee of WST 2,000, and disqualification of the entity from contracting with payment processors and banks.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid annual fees where the company is struck off, and for third-party contractual debts incurred after the company had ceased to be in good standing under the doctrine of de facto directorship.
