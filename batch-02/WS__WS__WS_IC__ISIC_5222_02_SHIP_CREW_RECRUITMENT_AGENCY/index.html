Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (Samoa), Apia; administers the Income Tax Act 2012, Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2011, Tax Administration Act 2016 and Customs Act 2014; taxpayer portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws (online TIN application and VAGST return filing via the Revenue Management System).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The Samoa International Company is a ring-fenced, statutorily exempt entity under ICA s.202, relieved from income tax, corporation tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty and all other Samoan taxes and duties, provided it does not carry on business within Samoa. The exemption is automatic and not subject to annual renewal, but the IC must maintain accounting records and may be required to file an annual return with the Registrar.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective pass-through mechanism exists under Samoa law; the ICA tax exemption is mandatory and not elective. Available elections are: (i) voluntary VAGST registration under VAGST Act 2011 s.10 where turnover is below the WST 100,000 compulsory threshold, allowing input-tax recovery on Samoan purchases; (ii) conversion from IC status to a domestic company under the Companies Act 2001 (registrable with the Registrar) if the entity wishes to conduct local activities and become subject to the Income Tax Act 2012; and (iii) election to acquire a local business or land licence under the International Companies (Amendment) Act 2015, which ring-fences the permitted local activity and subjects that specific activity to Samoan domestic taxation. Hybrid-mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act 2012 (as aligned with BEPS Action 2) operate by denial of double-deduction outcomes and do not confer any elective treatment on an IC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (Samoa Tala) annual value of taxable supplies; registration is compulsory for any person making taxable supplies above this threshold (VAGST Act 2011 s.10), voluntary registration is available below the threshold, and exports of goods and offshore services are disregarded for both nexus and threshold computation purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods and international services under VAGST Act 2011 s.31. Crew recruitment and vessel manning-agency services (ISIC 5222) are eligible for the 0% rate when: (i) the recipient is a non-resident shipowner, ship manager, or overseas crewing principal not registered for VAGST and not carrying on business in Samoa; (ii) the service is supplied from Samoa to the foreign vessel or offshore principal and is not connected with land in Samoa under the place-of-supply rules; (iii) the IC holds a written manning/recruitment agreement with the offshore principal identifying the vessel, flag state, crew complement and fee structure; and (iv) consideration is paid from outside Samoa. Where inputs in Samoa attract VAGST, the IC may claim a refund through the quarterly VAGST return; input tax on exempt or unregistered activities is not creditable.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based electronic-invoicing mandate is currently in force in Samoa. The Ministry for Revenue operates an electronic Revenue Management System (RMS) for VAGST return lodgement and payment, but invoice data transmission is not required at the point of issue. Tax invoices must contain the title 'tax invoice', supplier name, address and TIN, recipient name and TIN (where registered), description of supply, quantity, price excluding tax, VAGST amount, and issue date. All invoices and supporting export documentation must be retained for at least 7 years for audit inspection.

The Ministry for Revenue may audit zero-rated export claims at any time within the 7-year record-retention window. Where zero-rating is claimed fraudulently or without adequate export substantiation, the Ministry will (i) claw back VAGST at the 15% rate on the supply, (ii) impose late-payment interest of 10% per annum plus incremental penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2016 s.55 and VAGST Act 2011 s.49, (iii) apply administrative penalties of up to 25% of the tax sought where false documentation is tendered, and (iv) initiate criminal prosecution for tax evasion carrying fines of up to WST 200,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. The ICA s.202 tax exemption does not extend to VAGST on local supplies, and directors may be jointly and severally liable under ICA s.107 for reckless false statements. Discrepancies between export declarations, bills of lading and inward remittance certificates trigger automatic audit review and can suspend the IC's zero-rating status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is legal tender and freely convertible for current-account transactions, including crew recruitment agency service fees, subject to general Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) vetting. Capital-account transactions, including inward direct investments and shareholder loan injection, are non-resident and may be routed through offshore foreign-currency accounts without Samoan local exchange-control approval for International Companies. Profit remittance by a Samoa International Company is permitted where funds are derived from non-Samoan sources and no Samoan tax liability crystallizes; however, remittances incorporating Samoan domestic receivables attract current-account treatment, documentary verification of the underlying service contract, and Central Bank of Samoa notification thresholds. The practical convertibility hurdle is compliance substantiation rather than statutory inconvertibility; robust source-of-funds and source-of-wealth dossiers are indispensable for any conversion into WST or onward FX transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa does not impose a mandatory surrender ratio on export proceeds for International Companies. The Exchange Control Act 1978 requires resident exporters of goods to repatriate proceeds to the domestic banking system within 180 days and to account for the disposition of foreign exchange, but this rule does not apply to non-resident-owned International Companies that operate solely in foreign currency and maintain no WST domestic activity. Crew recruitment agencies classified as ISIC 5222 providing cross-border maritime staffing services are treated as exporters of services; to the extent receipts are lodged in a Samoan resident account, the bank will record the transaction under the current-account register but no prescribed conversion ratio applies. Where an offshore foreign-currency treasury account is used, no surrender obligation arises in Samoa, subject to a clean audit trail and customary periodic returns to the Central Bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Samoa's central bank and monetary authority)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Samoa has not identified a licensing regime for foreign fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs); only public bodies supervised by the Central Bank of Samoa may issue e-money. Consequently, a purely offshore digital EMI cannot independently operate a Samoan WST account or clear domestic wire transfers. Nevertheless, an International Company may lawfully use a reputable foreign EMI (authorised in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction) as a non-Samoan payroll collection and disbursement engine for USD, AUD, or NZD settlements provided no Samoan-resident counterparty is involved and the EMI itself does not hold itself out as a Samoan bank. The principal legal constraint is that such an EMI account does not constitute a Samoan banking relationship and therefore cannot be used to satisfy the SAMOA International Company statutory substance requirement or the bank-account-evidence milestones under a physical-presence licence application. A tier-1 Samoan bank account remains the anchor for compliance, licensing, and Central Bank interaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound founder or family-trust capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the Samoa International Company's tier-1 operational multi-currency account. The remitter must specify an unambiguous purpose code, typically 'GDR' (capital contribution) or '284' (trade credit), and must include the company name, share register reference, and a copy of the board resolution authorising the capital subscription or shareholder loan. The bank will require the Certificate of Incorporation, the register of shareholders, evidence of economic substance in Samoa (office lease, resident agent), and an audited source-of-funds statement if the amount exceeds the bank's threshold. For shareholder loans, the loan agreement should be notarised and apostilled; the funds must remain in the company's name and not be commingled with trust assets. Inbound transfers to the escrow/capital account should be structured to preserve traceability and to match the regulatory return to the Central Bank of Samoa, where applicable.Outbound crew payroll and subcontractor settlements are cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103 in USD, AUD, or NZD. For large-scale international seafarer labor placements, a commercial letter of credit (L/C) can be used as a settlement rail against delivery of crew mobilisation documentation, including STCW certificates, sickness and personal accident insurance, and repatriation confirmations. Standby letters of credit may also be used as performance guarantees in the crewing agreement. All outbound remittances are subject to the paying bank's sanctions screening, screening against the SAMOA/UN sanctions lists, and payment transparency rules under the AML/CFT Act. The bank will require underlying service contracts, tax-compliant invoices, and a boarded crew verification report. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be filed within 6 months after the end of the income year (a 31 December balance date is due by 30 June of the following year); final assessed tax is payable with the return; provisional installments fall due on 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March.

Due within 3 months after each anniversary of incorporation; the annual government fee is payable with the return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 (aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions requiring a Local File; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups meeting the OECD EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold where the ultimate parent is resident in Samoa).

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The International Companies (Economic Substance) Amendment Act 2018 and the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 impose a managed-and-controlled-in-Samoa test on ICs conducting relevant activities, including distribution and service centres. A crew-recruitment agency must perform its core income-generating activities in Samoa, maintain premises, local employees and operating expenditure commensurate with the activity, keep local records, and file an annual substance declaration; failure triggers information exchange, penalties, loss of exemption and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Loss of the statutory tax exemption; reassessment of 27% corporate income tax plus a 150% shortfall penalty and interest; administrative strike-off and deregistration; disclosure as a shell entity under exchange-of-information mechanisms.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance declarations are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties; courts may pierce the corporate veil, order civil restitution, impose a travel ban preventing departure from Samoa, and sentence directors to imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of back taxes for up to 7 years, 150% evasion penalty, statutory interest at 24% per annum, fines up to WST 100,000, and mandatory closure of the unlicensed operation.

Director personal liability: Directors with wilful blindness are personally liable for corporate tax debts; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 5 years, asset forfeiture and repatriation restrictions.
AML/CFT Breach in Crew Payroll Remittance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 500,000 per breach under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018, freezing and confiscation of payroll funds, revocation of the crew recruitment licence, and mandatory reporting to foreign supervisors.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines, disqualification, travel bans and asset restraint, and imprisonment for up to 7 years where funds are derived from criminal activity.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment, disallowance of deductions, 50% documentation shortfall penalty, statutory interest, and recharacterization of excess payments as deemed dividends subject to 15% withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-commercial pricing are liable for the resulting tax deficiencies; the corporate veil is pierced in cases of intentional interposition, allowing seizure of personal assets and travel restrictions pending settlement.
