Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), a semi-autonomous agency under the Ministry of Finance; taxpayer portal: SRA e-Services (https://www.revenue.gov.ws). Registration of International Companies is separately administered by the Registrar of International Companies within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is issued a certificate of incorporation and an IC number by the Registrar of International Companies. Because an IC is statutorily exempt from all Samoa taxes, it is not obliged to obtain a TIN unless it carries on prohibited local taxable activities in Samoa (which ICA 1987 restrictions otherwise bar). If such local activities arise, the IC must apply to SRA for a Tax Identification Number using the prescribed IRD-01 registration form, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered agent details, beneficial ownership identification, and notarized constitutional documents; SRA will issue the TIN and register the entity for GST if the relevant threshold is met.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone: The IC is classified as a non-resident offshore company under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended by the International Companies (Amendment) Act 1998) and is statutorily exempt from Samoa income tax, Goods and Services Tax, stamp duty, and all other Samoan taxes for a 20-year period, which exemption may be extended through a tax exemption certificate. The exemption remains valid only while the IC derives no Samoa-source revenue and does not carry on business with Samoan residents. — A foreign-owned International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is issued a certificate of incorporation and an IC number by the Registrar of International Companies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The IC is classified as a non-resident offshore company under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended by the International Companies (Amendment) Act 1998) and is statutorily exempt from Samoa income tax, Goods and Services Tax, stamp duty, and all other Samoan taxes for a 20-year period, which exemption may be extended through a tax exemption certificate. The exemption remains valid only while the IC derives no Samoa-source revenue and does not carry on business with Samoan residents.

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box, flow-through, or hybrid-mismatch election mechanisms. The only statutory elections applicable to a Samoa IC are: (i) applying for a Tax Exemption Certificate under the International Companies (Amendment) Act 1998 to secure the statutory 20-year tax holiday; (ii) electing to migrate/re-domicile as an ordinary resident company under the Companies Act 2019, which would irrevocably subject the entity to Samoa income tax and GST (10%) on its world-wide and Samoa-source income; and (iii) electing to commence onshore commercial activity with SRA registration and loss of offshore exempt status, an election that must be notified to the Registrar of International Companies and the Central Bank of Samoa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory GST registration applies where annual taxable turnover exceeds SAT 100,000; voluntary registration is available from SAT 60,000 annual turnover. Non-resident persons making taxable supplies of digital or remote services to consumers in Samoa may be required to register for GST regardless of turnover, subject to the non-resident supplier and reverse-charge provisions of the Goods and Services Tax Act 2021.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) the physical export of goods from Samoa where the supply is made within 28 days before or after the date of export and is substantiated by an outward customs declaration; and (ii) a supply of international/offshore services performed outside Samoa or supplied to a non-resident customer located outside Samoa at the time of supply, provided the services are not performed for a Samoan resident. For the virtual brand ghost kitchen operation, zero-rating is limited to demonstrable offshore sales involving the genuine cross-border movement of goods; delivery orders fulfilled and consumed within Samoa through UberEats or DoorDash remain taxable at the standard 10% GST rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration evidencing physical outward movement of goods from Samoa
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing export shipment from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing repatriation of export proceeds in accordance with Central Bank of Samoa exchange control

Samoa does not operate a mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping regime for GST. However, every GST-registered supplier, including ghost kitchen and virtual brand operators settling through UberEats and DoorDash payment rails, must issue a tax invoice with prescribed particulars at the time of supply, show the SRA GST registration number and itemized GST at 10%, and retain complete electronic records, accounting and delivery-log audit trails for a minimum of seven years. GST returns and payments are lodged electronically through SRA e-Services on the applicable periodic filing cycle.

SRA may audit zero-rated export claims retrospectively for up to seven years. Where zero-rating is claimed without genuine export or without the mandatory customs declaration and FX repatriation evidence, SRA will assess GST at 10% plus default interest, disallow and claw back all input tax credits claimed in connection with the export, and impose a civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall. In cases involving fraudulent intent or false documentation, criminal penalties apply: a fine of up to SAT 200,000 and/or imprisonment for up to seven years, together with suspension or revocation of the GST registration of the delivery operator.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a managed-float currency. Samoa has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, so current-account payments and transfers are unrestricted; residual exchange-control approvals under the Exchange Control Act 1976 apply mainly to capital-account transactions. International Companies (ICs) are treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes: they may freely maintain foreign-currency (USD) accounts, contract in any currency, and remit dividends, liquidation proceeds, and service fees offshore without CBS conversion h hurdles. Profit remittance is subject only to company-law solvency tests and IFSA annual return filing; no additional CBS exit tax or SDR surrender applies to ICs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must collect payment within 90 days from shipment date and surrender 100% of export proceeds to an authorised dealer in Samoa within 30 days of receipt, converted into WST unless a CBS exemption is granted. International Companies operating under the International Companies Act 1987 are exempt from the mandatory surrender and conversion regime because they are non-resident and prohibited from carrying on business with Samoan residents without CBS approval; accordingly their export/operating proceeds may be retained in USD or any approved settlement currency in offshore or domestic USD accounts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Falepuna, Apia), exercising statutory powers under the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Financial Institutions Act 2016, and the International Financial Services Authority (IFSA) for offshore International Company supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditionally viable (Medium feasibility). Samoa has no standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime under the Financial Institutions Act 2016; money transfer and payment services require a CBS money-remittance licence or an agency arrangement with a licensed Samoan financial institution. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA or Singapore MAS licensed) are legally usable for inbound platform payout aggregation on behalf of the IC, but they cannot serve as the master settlement account because CBS exchange-control reporting requires the final settlement account to be held at a Samoan authorised dealer. Recommended structure: use an FCA/MAS-licensed EMI as a virtual-account payment aggregator for UberEats/DoorDash payouts, then timely sweep to the master USD operational account at ANZ Samoa or BSP Samoa.

Due within 30 days after the anniversary of incorporation for the IC-AR1; the IC-TE1 nil income return is due no later than six months after the International Company's fiscal year-end (default 31 December).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions (Local File); WST 5,000,000 total revenue (Master File)

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Act 2019 applies to International Companies carrying on relevant activities. A ghost kitchen or virtual brand operation is a relevant activity if physically conducted in Samoa as a 'distribution and service center'. A purely digital brand generating orders abroad and settling via offshore delivery platforms has no Samoan physical nexus and incurs no ESR filing. Any local hiring, kitchen premises, or domiciled delivery logistics in Samoa triggers an ESR return requiring migration of management and control to Samoa, adequate local staffing, premises, and income proportionality.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Underpayment of 27% corporate tax on all Samoan-source profits, interest at 9% per annum, plus a penalty equal to 100% of the evaded tax. Criminal indictment may impose imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally assessed for unpaid taxes where the company is a sham or where dividends were paid in lieu of tax. Travel bans and departure prohibition orders can be issued; asset seizure covers disguised payments and related-party distributions.
Shell Company / ESR Violation

Statutory sanction: ESR noncompliance penalties up to WST 20,000 per return plus WST 5,000 per day of continuing breach. The Registrar may restrict bank accounts, suspend commercial authority, or strike the company off.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false ESR declarations are disqualified from managing a company for up to 5 years and may be ordered to pay restitution personally. Beneficial owners cannot rely on the corporate veil to shield assets where nominal directorship was used.
AML/CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 150,000 for the corporate entity and WST 75,000 for officers under the Samoa Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act; bank freezes and revocation of any financial service license.

Director personal liability: Directors with actual knowledge carry criminal liability up to 10 years' imprisonment; civil penalties attach to rejected wire transfers and complicit money laundering through delivery settlement accounts.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may adjust the controlled transactions, assess additional corporate tax, and levy a 20% penalty. A cross-border mispricing adjustment by a treaty partner triggers additional Samoan documentation penalties up to WST 10,000.

Director personal liability: Directors approving misleading contemporaneous evidence are jointly liable for penalties; they may personally be sued for indemnity by the affected operating company and face regulatory disqualification for neglect.
Failure to File Annual Return / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Daily late penalty of WST 100 up to WST 5,000; after 90 days the Registrar may strike off the IC. The Ministry of Revenue can issue a default assessment based on estimated platform sales turnover and may recover through garnishment.

Director personal liability: Directors lose representative immunity after strike-off; courts may impose restitution orders, travel restrictions, and a 3-year directorship ban, and release ultimate beneficial owners as jointly liable where false filings concealed actual business operations.
