Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa, which administers the Tax Administration Act 2012, Income Tax Act 2013, Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST) Act 1993, Customs Duty Act 2014 and Excise Duty Act 2013. The MfR combines the former Inland Revenue Department and Customs Division, operates the official taxpayer portal at www.revenue.gov.ws, and uses UNCTAD ASYCUDA World as the electronic customs single window.

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) must obtain a statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) from the Ministry for Revenue when it commences any taxable activity or importation in Samoa, or engages local staff; an otherwise pure offshore IC may be issued a TIN on application even without local nexus because a TIN is required before MfR will process VAGST refunds or zero-rating claims. The standard procedure is: (i) verify nexus or threshold, being commencement of taxable activity, WST 50,000 VAGST turnover, or any customs import; (ii) file the prescribed TIN application through the revenue portal with the Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of International Companies within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour, evidence of the Samoan registered office and registered agent, a certified extract of directors and beneficial owners, and proof of non-resident ownership; (iii) MfR issues the TIN and annotates the taxpayer register to reflect the IC's non-resident or exempt status; and (iv) the IC also obtains a customs client or declarant code in ASYCUDA World for any import or export transactions. The IC must keep its annual international company registration and government fee current, as lapse can invalidate the exemption.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is not a fully freely convertible currency under a liberalised capital account. Current account transactions are generally permitted through authorised dealers (licensed commercial banks) without individual CBS approval, subject to documentary verification and post-transaction reporting. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections for an International Company shipyard, loan advances, issuance of guarantees covering maritime instalments, and offshore hedging with derivatives, require either prior written CBS exchange control approval or an express exemption certificate obtained under the International Companies regime. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is structurally permitted and free of withholding tax for International Companies, but CBS and the clearing bank will apply practical hurdles: proof that the distributed profits originated from verified export proceeds or trade receivables, board resolution and distribution ledger, audited financial statements, and the absence of overdue domestic obligations. In practice, approvals are granted on a case-by-case basis and can take 5–15 business days; there is no blanket current-account convertibility guarantee for non-resident owned entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBS Exchange Control Regulations, resident exporters and, by extension, Samoa-registered International Companies maintaining Samoan-authorised-dealer accounts are required to repatriate export proceeds relating to Samoan-sourced trade to an authorised dealer in Samoa within 180 days from the bill of lading or invoice date. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the foreign currency invoice value into WST for export contracts executed wholly or partly within Samoa, unless a CBS exemption certificate is issued. For an International Company engaged in ocean-container-vessel construction where the vessel is fabricated in a foreign subcontracted shipyard, the export proceeds are classified as offshore trade receipts, and the surrender rule may be suspended or reduced to zero if the entity provides the underlying construction agreement, overseas cost breakdown, bill of lading, carrier contract, and a CBS exemption confirmation evidencing that the transaction does not create a net domestic WST cash flow requirement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) acting in its capacity as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Exchange Control Regulations made pursuant to the Foreign Exchange Act 1978, as amended and supplemented by CBS prudential and AML/CFT directives.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Samoa does not maintain a standalone electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime, and no offshore digital EMI is permitted to provide payment or clearing services in Samoa without a CBS banking or financial services license. An International Company may connect a foreign-licensed EMI only as a non-regulated front-end payment gateway, but the actual clearing and custody must be held through a CBS-licensed authorised dealer. In practical terms, the execution of maritime instalment LC and escrow arrangements requires a full commercial bank relationship in Samoa, and reliance on an unlicensed EMI would trigger adverse AML and correspondent de-risking outcomes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from a parent company or strategic investor must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 into a designated WST or USD account maintained with an authorised dealer in Samoa. The remitting bank must use an accurate purpose code — where available, 701110 for cash capital contributions or 702112 for equity capital in a finance/industry holding; for maritime instalments, the underlying invoice should be referenced under a transport equipment purpose such as 102101 (seagoing vessels). Prior to execution, the International Company must submit to the receiving bank a CBS exchange control approval or exemption certificate, a board resolution authorising the account and the remittance, a notarised copy of the investor's identity and constitutional documents, and the vessel construction contract or investment agreement evidencing the commercial transaction. The receiving bank will perform a funds-arrival verification, record the capital against CBS reporting thresholds, and issue a certificate of receipt for local corporate registry and audit purposes.Outbound trade disbursements to overseas shipbuilders, dry-dock subcontractors, engine suppliers, steel fabricators and classification surveyors should be settled under SWIFT MT103 for non-documentary operational payments, MT100/MT202 for interbank transfers covering letter-of-credit reimbursements, and a Samoa-law-governed irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (CL C) for staged vessel financing. The LC must be issued in favour of the subcontractor or equipment seller and advised through a UCP600-compliant channel (MT700/MT720). Where the LC maturity exceeds 180 days, CBS pre-approval of the deferred payment term is required. All outbound payments must be supported by the underlying construction invoice, signed schedule of work acceptance, shipping or delivery documents, and a negative media/sanctions screening result for the beneficiary. Payments directed to restricted shipyards or individuals are automatically blocked under CBS-approved sanctions screening.Vanilla forward contracts in USD/WST booked with a CBS-licensed authorised dealer, covering actual and committed trade receivables and payables for vessel construction and shipyard consumables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed in USD/WST through an offshore hedge counterparty, provided the underlying is an approved CBS-hedgeable exposure and settlement is made net in a permitted offshore clearing currency.Cross-currency swaps (USD/WST) whereby the International Company exchanges fixed or floating payments with an authorised dealer, subject to CBS prior approval, supporting matched debt-service and vessel-instalment cashflow conversion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable to a tax-exempt International Company; for any taxable Samoan affiliate, transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is mandatory where annual connected-party turnover exceeds WST 5,000,000, and a Master File is advisable where worldwide group turnover exceeds WST 100,000,000; failure to produce documentation attracts a penalty of WST 100,000 plus a 20% uplift on any assessed adjustment

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2019-2021 apply to International Companies carrying on relevant activities, which include shipping and maritime transport. A shipyard engaged purely in shipbuilding (ISIC 3011) is not itself a relevant activity, but the doctrine is triggered if the IC derives shipping income from vessel ownership, chartering, crew management or haulage. If triggered, the IC must demonstrate in Samoa: (1) board meetings with a quorum held in Samoa and strategic decisions recorded in Samoan minutes; (2) core income-generating activities performed in Samoa; (3) adequate employees, physical premises and operating expenditure in Samoa. Compliance is certified annually via Form IC-AR/ESD; no corporate income tax or payroll tax is imposed on the substantive operations of the exempt IC.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Fraud and False Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to WST 1,000,000 against the company and WST 100,000 against responsible officers, imprisonment up to 7 years, compulsory deregistration, and automatic recovery of tax at the 27% CIT rate on worldwide income for the non-compliant years; referral to foreign tax authorities under the multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for restitution of avoided tax and penalties; travel restriction orders under the Tax Administration Act; director disqualification for up to 10 years; foreign directors placed on immigration blacklists and exposed to extradition in aggravated fraud cases
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic substance non-compliance penalty of WST 10,000 up to WST 75,000, or WST 250,000 for a repeat offence; automatic exchange of information with the shareholder's tax jurisdiction under CRS; strike-off from the International Companies Register within 90 days of default; loss of limited liability for obligations incurred after the default date

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for all company debts incurred following the default; civil recovery proceedings by the Registrar for unpaid penalties; personal travel bans while penalties remain outstanding; custodial sentence up to 5 years for continuing refusal to remedy the substance failure
Anti-Money Laundering and Fraudulent Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the AML/CFT Act 2018 (as amended): fines up to WST 2,000,000, imprisonment up to 14 years, or both; revocation of any International Service Provider licence; freezing and forfeiture of vessel assets, letter of credit proceeds and escrow balances; confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime Act

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners prosecuted personally for money laundering; civil and criminal liability for arranging false letter of credit documentation; personal bankruptcy orders; prohibition from managing any Samoan International Company for up to 15 years; extradition exposure for non-resident directors acting with fraudulent intent
Failure to File Annual Return / Non-Payment of Annual Fee

Statutory sanction: Automatic 10% late-payment surcharge; mandatory termination of services by the registered agent; administrative dissolution after 6 months in default; penalty up to WST 5,000 plus a WST 1,000 restoration fee; unauthorized trading after deregistration constitutes an offence attracting a daily fine of WST 500

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid annual fees, penalties and all contracts entered into in the company's name after deregistration; Samoan courts may enforce judgments against personal assets and issue departure prohibition orders against non-resident directors
