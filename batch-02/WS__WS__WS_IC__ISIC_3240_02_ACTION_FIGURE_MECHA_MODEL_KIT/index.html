Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue of Samoa (MOR), Government of Samoa; official taxpayer portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws (STAR e-Services)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity is required to obtain a Samoa TIN only if it carries on a taxable activity in Samoa, derives Samoan-source income, or is required to withhold Samoan tax; a Samoa International Company (IC) is formed by lodging the registration application through a licensed international registered agent with the Registrar of International Companies (Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour / Samoa International Finance Authority), and the Certificate of Incorporation serves as the primary statutory identifier. Where a TIN is needed (bank account opening, VAGST registration, employer registration), the IC files the MOR Application Form for Taxpayer Identification Number with the Ministry of Revenue, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, Constitution, registered agent certificate, and Samoan office or agent address; the MOR issues the TIN within 10 business days. For the standard offshore IC in Tax_Exempt_Zone status conducting no Samoan taxable activity and deriving no Samoan-source income, no TIN, VAGST registration, or income tax filing is required; the only statutory payment obligation is the annual international company government license fee remitted through the registered agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box regime and has not enacted domestic OECD hybrid-mismatch rules. Available elections: (1) conversion of an IC to an ordinary domestic company under the Companies Act 2006, electing into the standard Samoa income tax and VAGST regimes; (2) continuance or redomiciliation of a foreign company as a Samoa IC under the International Companies Act 1987, electing into the offshore tax-exempt regime; (3) application to the MOR for a certificate of tax residence or non-residence for CRS, FATCA, and treaty relief purposes; (4) adoption of provisions in the IC's Constitution confining its business to offshore activities in order to preserve the statutory tax exemption. No election is available to treat the IC as a pass-through entity for Samoan income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 50,000 per annum: a person carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa whose taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed SAT 50,000 in any consecutive 12-month period must register for VAGST; non-residents register only if they make taxable supplies connected with Samoa, so the offshore IC selling mecha model kits exclusively to foreign buyers sits below the threshold and outside the Samoan registration nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under the GST Act when sold under a taxable supply in Samoa and physically exported within the statutory export window (generally 30 days, subject to extension for containerized shipments), provided the supplier retains full documentary proof of export. Services are zero-rated when performed outside Samoa, enjoyed wholly outside Samoa, or supplied to a recipient who is outside Samoa at the time of supply; the IC's offshore design, molding, assembly, and sale of ISIC 3240 action-figure mecha model kits to non-resident buyers is therefore outside Samoan VAGST and, where a Samoan-registered entity invoices the export, qualifies for the 0% rate with recovery of any attributable input credits. Refund claims are payable by the MOR within 30 days of a complete submission.

  • Samoa Customs Service Export Declaration (electronic CUS-EXP entry, HS 9503.00 attribution for toys and action figures)
  • Bill of Lading, House Bill of Lading, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement advice evidencing receipt of foreign customer funds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying SKUs, quantities, unit prices, and ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930 description
  • VAGST zero-rated tax invoice issued to the non-resident buyer
  • Manufacturer's or supplier's certificate of origin (required for PTA/EPA claims or buyer confirmation)

No real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (API), or digital stamping mandate exists in Samoa. A VAGST-registered business must issue a valid tax invoice within 7 days of supply, containing the prescribed particulars (supplier name and TIN, invoice number and date, description of goods, consideration, and VAGST separately stated). The MOR's STAR system supports electronic return filing and voluntary e-invoicing, but compliance is enforced through post-return audit rather than pre-clearance.

The MOR may reverse a zero-rating claim, claw back refunds, and re-assess VAGST at 15% plus statutory interest where export documentation is deficient, invoices are false, or goods are diverted into the Samoan domestic market. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2012 escalate from 20% of the shortfall for failure to take reasonable care, to 40% for recklessness, 80% for intentional disregard, and up to 150% for deliberate evasion or concealment, with monthly late-payment penalties on top up to the statutory cap. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent zero-rating, forged bills of lading, or fabricated Inward Remittance Certificates may result in significant monetary fines and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; for an International Company, the Registrar of International Companies may additionally strike the company off, and the offshore tax-exempt status can be revoked retroactively, rendering the entity assessable on its Samoan-source export margin.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is not freely convertible spot currency; CBS operates an exchange-control regime that distinguishes current-account transactions, which are largely delegated to licensed authorized dealers (commercial banks), from capital-account transactions, which require CBS approval or notification. For an International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 and conducting all business outside Samoa with non-resident counterparties, the exchange-control licensing burden is generally disapplied: the IC may hold and remit foreign currency through an offshore-express account without CBS conversion clearance. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful and normally unencumbered where the IC has no Samoan-resident creditor, no Samoan-source business income, and no WST-denominated liabilities. Practical hurdles arise when funds are converted into WST, when a Samoan resident is a counterparty, when the IC receives funds from a Samoan bank account, or when there is insufficient documentary evidence of the underlying non-Samoan trade; in such cases CBS may treat the remittance as resident profit repatriation and require board resolutions, audited financial statements, tax clearance, beneficial-ownership attestation, and balance-of-payments reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBS exchange-control framework, a Samoa-resident exporter must collect export proceeds within 90 days from the date of export and must surrender 100% of the declared foreign-currency export proceeds to a licensed authorized dealer, except where a specific retention approval or export-account facility has been granted by CBS. A Samoa International Company that manufactures or purchases action-figure and mecha-model kits entirely through offshore contract manufacturers, never imports the goods into Samoa customs territory, and settles suppliers and customers through offshore USD accounts is treated as outside the physical export surrender obligation. However, if any leg of the transaction touches a Samoan bank account, the collecting bank will apply the 90-day repatriation rule, require underlying invoices and customs or shipping evidence, and may compel conversion into WST unless the CBS retention exemption is obtained in advance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Apia, Samoa – statutory monetary authority and exchange-control administrator under the Exchange Control Act and the International Companies Act 1987.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a treasury depository; Medium as a peripheral receivable or e-commerce settlement side-rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex are not licensed banks or authorized dealers by CBS, and Samoa does not recognize non-resident EMI client accounts as protected deposits. A Samoa International Company may use an EMI for collecting PayPal and small retail customer receipts, but balances must be swept promptly to a tier-1 Samoan licensed bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital should be routed by the non-resident shareholder from its own bank account at a recognized international bank directly to the IC's USD account at a tier-1 Samoan licensed bank through SWIFT MT103. The wire must reference the Samoa IC registration number, the shareholder name, and the appropriate balance-of-payments purpose code for equity capital inward investment, typically mapped by the receiving bank as 'Direct Investment – Equity' or an equivalent CBS BOP capital-import code. Before remittance, the IC must adopt a board resolution approving the share subscription, issue share allotment letters, update the register of members, and prepare a source-of-funds dossier for the shareholder. The receiving bank will report any significant cash or wire transaction at or above the CBS threshold to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit and may request audited financials or a capital statement. If the funds are to be converted into WST, CBS exchange-control approval may be required; if the shareholder is a Samoan resident, the transaction is treated as a resident capital movement and will require additional CBS consent. For a standard non-resident capital injection retained in USD, no CBS capital-import approval is generally required and the funds can be immediately used for offshore trade payments.Outbound supplier payments for the high-precision steel injection molding and snap-fit plastic kit supply chain should use SWIFT MT103 for direct invoice settlements from the IC's USD account at the Samoan authorized dealer to the Chinese or Hong Kong supplier's USD account; the payment must reference the supplier invoice, purchase order, and ISIC 3240 goods description. For high-value mold tooling and first-article production molds, an irrevocable, confirmed at-sight Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Samoan bank and advised through the supplier's bank is recommended to mitigate performance and delivery risk. For recurring raw-material purchases, documentary collections with documents against payment or documents against acceptance can be used. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium as a treasury depository; Medium as a peripheral receivable or e-commerce settlement side-rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex are not licensed banks or authorized dealers by CBS, and Samoa does not recognize non-resident EMI client accounts as protected deposits. A Samoa International Company may use an EMI for collecting PayPal and small retail customer receipts, but balances must be swept promptly to a tier-1 Samoan licensed bank. Legal constraints include: exchange-control restrictions on routing funds through non-authorized financial institutions, AML/CFT travel-rule compliance, inability to obtain CBS confirmation for the EMI as a settlement rail for letters of credit, and heightened source-of-funds questions when large amounts are held offshore in an unregulated wallet. The EMI should never be used for capital injection, supplier letters of credit, or statutory liquidity evidence.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in Samoa — no Local File / Master File regime has been enacted; the Income Tax Act 2015 contains a general arm's length rule for cross-border related-party transactions of taxable persons; because a fully exempt International Company pays no Samoan income tax, no mandatory TP documentation threshold applies in WST, but contemporaneous pricing evidence is prudent for any transactions with Samoan counterparties or if exemption status is challenged

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019, adopted in response to EU Code of Conduct Group requirements, impose a substance test on ICs that carry on specified 'relevant activities' (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centre, intellectual property holding, and holding company). High-precision injection molding of action figure and mecha model kits (ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930) is not a specified relevant activity, so no local substance requirement in Samoa arises from manufacturing alone. Nevertheless, the IC must not be a mere shell: it must maintain a registered office and licensed agent in Samoa, keep proper books and records, be administered at a board level outside Samoa, and conduct no domestic Samoan business. If a manufacturing IC earns passive income or continues to hold royalty/licensing assets derived from the toy designs without operational substance in its jurisdiction of control, Samoan or foreign tax authorities may disregard the exemption or reclassify the entity as effectively managed in a higher-tax jurisdiction, triggering 27% taxation or aggressive anti-avoidance reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Exemption Forfeiture

Statutory sanction: The IC exemption is void ab initio for the year of the breach; the company is assessed on 27% corporate income tax on all income attributable to Samoan operations, plus non-resident withholding tax liabilities on any outbound payments; the Tax Administration Act 2015 imposes a civil penalty of up to 150% of the short-paid tax and a fine not exceeding 100 penalty units, and knowingly making a false declaration constitutes an offence punishable by imprisonment not exceeding 2 years

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly liable for the company's unpaid tax where they signed false declarations or directed the breach; the Ministry of Revenue may issue a departure prohibition order (travel ban) preventing any director or principal officer from leaving Samoa until the assessment, penalties, and interest are settled; judges may also award civil restitution against directors in proceedings brought by liquidators or creditors of the company
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019, a relevant IC that fails the economic substance test may be penalized up to WS$25,000, and a second-year failure may lead to deregistration by the Registrar; the entity may be treated as a sham for tax purposes, resulting in the imposition of 27% tax and penalties, and may be placed on international defensive-measure lists (EU Annex) that blacklist the structure for EU counterparties

Director personal liability: Directors who permit the company to operate with no economic substance or who sign certificates of good standing knowing the company is a façade are exposed to personal civil liability for abuse of process and fraudulent preference; the Registrar may disqualify directors for up to five years; regulators in the beneficial owner's home jurisdiction (e.g., China) may impose personal criminal or administrative penalties for cross-border tax evasion; travel bans may be imposed by Samoa for unpaid regulatory fines
Anti-Money Laundering and Financial Integrity Breaches

Statutory sanction: Under the Samoa AML/CFT Act 2018, a body corporate that fails to comply with customer due diligence, record-keeping, or suspicious transaction reporting obligations is liable to a fine not exceeding WS$1,000,000; the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) may impose a freezing order on the IC's bank accounts; wilful money laundering carries a custodial sentence of up to 10 years and confiscation of the proceeds of crime

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the registered agent are personally liable where the offence was committed with their consent, connivance, or neglect; sanctions include personal fines up to WS$250,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, forfeiture of commissions, and disqualification from serving as a director of any Samoa company; the director may also be subject to a restraint order over personal assets and a travel ban pending investigation
Dividend Stripping and Improper Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Ministry of Revenue may treat the entire distribution as a constructive dividend, impose 15% non-resident withholding tax on the gross amount, add a penalty of up to 150% for non-declaration, and charge interest at 2% per month; additionally, thin capitalisation or excessive interest payments may be disallowed as a deduction and recharacterized as hidden profit distributions

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or assist in unlawful repatriation without withholding are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax; they may be served with a departure prohibition order, have their passports flagged, and face personal summary proceedings for failure to ensure statutory deduction and payment; in egregious cases, criminal prosecution for false accounting or fraud could result in imprisonment up to 2 years, and the trustee of a Chinese family trust may face civil suit for breach of fiduciary duty by beneficiaries
