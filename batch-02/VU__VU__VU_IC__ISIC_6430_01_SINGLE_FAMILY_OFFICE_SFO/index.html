Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management of the Republic of Vanuatu; official portal and tax filings via https://dci.gov.vu; corporate registry and licensing counterpart: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), https://www.vfsc.vu

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act (Cap 222) is not required to obtain an income-tax registration because it is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes other than VAT on local taxable supplies. Where a VAT identity is required, the foreign beneficial owner or registered agent files a DCIR VAT Registration Form (Form 200 series) with the certified IC Certificate of Incorporation, certificate of registered agent, directorship register, and non-resident ownership declaration; DCIR issues a 7-digit VAT registration number and a Taxpayer Identification Number (TPIN) within approximately 14 business days. For a pure offshore single-family-office structure operating through private-banking wires and custody accounts, no municipal business licence (MBC) and no income-tax registration are triggered unless a physical office or local taxable activity is established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Vanuatu International Company receives an automatic statutory exemption from corporate income tax, personal income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, stamp duty, estate duty, and gift tax under section 62 of the International Companies Act (Cap 222); the entity is treated as fiscally transparent with no territorial-source nexus for offshore investment income; VAT registration applies only if the IC makes taxable local supplies exceeding the statutory threshold.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Vanuatu. The tax-exempt classification is obtained by election of the International Company form at incorporation before the VFSC, resulting in a Certificate of Exemption issued contemporaneously with the Certificate of Incorporation; the exemption is automatic for non-resident persons deriving no Vanuatu-source income and is not based on a treaty election because Vanuatu maintains no general double-taxation treaty network.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration where taxable supplies made or likely to be made in Vanuatu exceed or are likely to exceed VUV 10,000,000 (ten million vatu) in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is available below the threshold; foreign-service-only exporters are normally outside the charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) goods physically exported from Vanuatu within 28 days of the time of supply and evidenced by customs endorsement; and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person to the extent the services are performed wholly outside Vanuatu or are supplied for the direct benefit of the non-resident outside Vanuatu, which covers offshore family-office investment advisory, asset-custody coordination, trustee liaison, and fund-administration services rendered to non-resident family members; input VAT credits attributable to zero-rated exports are refundable through the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form V1) endorsed by a customs officer with the actual date of export
  • Bill of Lading or AirWaybill evidencing physical carriage of goods out of Vanuatu
  • Commercial invoice marked EXEMPT / ZERO-RATED cross-referenced to the export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export consideration in foreign currency
  • For offshore services: signed service agreement with the non-resident single-family-office beneficiary, detailed work-papers establishing performance wholly outside Vanuatu, and evidence of the beneficiary's non-resident status
  • VAT Form 200 zero-rated supplies schedule and input tax credit reconciliation worksheet

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-integrated e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate exists in Vanuatu as of 2025. Taxpayers must issue self-generated VAT tax invoices containing supplier and recipient identity, VAT registration numbers, date of supply, description, consideration, VAT-exclusive amount, VAT amount, and total; VAT returns (Form 200) and remittances are lodged with DCIR on a monthly or approved bimonthly basis; documentation must be retained for 5 years for audit verification.

DCIR audits zero-rating claims against the 28-day export window and the Exchange Control Act (Cap 60) requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Non-repatriation or documentary failure triggers retrospective clawback of the 0% rate, re-assessment of VAT at 15% from the original invoice date, interest at the statutory rate, and civil penalty tax of up to 200% of the evaded VAT for deliberate or reckless claims; criminal liability extends to fines of up to VUV 5,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraudulent documentation, with a 4-year retrospective assessment window and joint and several liability imposed on directors, the registered agent, and the beneficial owner.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is convertible on both current and capital account, and Vanuatu has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. There is no general exchange-control prohibition on bona fide inward or outward payments for trade, investment, dividends, interest, fees, or profit remittance by an International Company. The actual hurdles are documentary and compliance-driven: banks act as authorised dealers and must apply RBV reporting thresholds, AML/CFT checks, sanctions screening, source-of-funds verification, and beneficial-ownership disclosure. For the SFO structure, profit remittance is tax-neutral because the International Companies Act imposes no Vanuatu income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, or remittance tax; the only practical hurdle is presenting a valid board resolution, current company standing, and a credible source-of-funds record.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export-proceeds surrender rule applies. Vanuatu does not impose a statutory conversion ratio or a fixed surrender timeline for export receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts without converting to VUV. The operative obligation is administrative: the exporter must retain the underlying export evidence (invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration, inward remittance advice), and the receiving bank must report large or suspicious FX transactions to the RBV for balance-of-payments and AML/CFT oversight. If settlement occurs through a documentary letter of credit, disbursement follows the LC maturity calendar; for open-account trade, the bank will prudentially expect inward remittance within the contractual credit period. Books and records supporting export proceeds must be retained for at least five years under Vanuatu's AML/CFT framework.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) — the central bank, monetary authority, exchange control administrator, and prudential regulator of licensed banks under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 69], the Banking Act [Cap. 43], and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act. Operational licensing of financial services is shared with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary bank account, Medium as a supplementary settlement channel. Vanuatu has no standalone EMI licensing category; any e-money institution or fintech payment firm seeking to hold client funds or provide cross-border payment/custody services within Vanuatu must be authorised under the Banking Act, the Financial Dealers Act, or the AML/CFT registration regime supervised by the RBV/VFSC. An unregulated offshore digital EMI cannot open a domestic clearing account in Vanuatu, cannot issue vatu-denominated e-money, and will not be accepted by Vanuatu commercial banks as the sole settlement rail. The SFO may use a fully licensed foreign EMI only for low-value secondary disbursements, provided the EMI is backed by a RBV-licensed bank nostro account, has verifiable licensing, and the SFO's primary clearing remains at a Vanuatu or reputable international bank. Flows originating from crypto-only or unlicensed EMI platforms are likely to be rejected by Vanuatu banks.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Tax-exempt International Company with no local economic substance, no tax filings, and no physical presence in Vanuatu.

Root cause: Tax-exempt International Company with no local economic substance, no tax filings, and no physical presence in Vanuatu.

Defense evidence: Provide certificate of incorporation; memorandum and articles of association; current registered agent certificate; confirmation of no bearer shares; family office constitutional documents or trust deed; board resolutions authorising bank account opening and investment mandate; audited or management accounts; evidence of any Vanuatu substance (registered office, local professional adviser, directorship if applicable); commercial narrative showing the SFO's family-office activity; banking reference letters from previous financial institutions.
Beneficial ownership layer involving multiple trusts, nominees, or protected cell entities.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership layer involving multiple trusts, nominees, or protected cell entities.

Defense evidence: Deliver a signed ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) declaration; full structure chart from settlor/foundation through trustees to the Vanuatu IC; each trust deed's non-public redacted version or counsel-certified summary; protector and trustee resolutions; passport copies of all natural-person principals; tax residency certificates and CRS/FATCA registrations; confirmation that no bearer shares or undisclosed nominee shareholders exist.
Inbound capital injection from a high-risk jurisdiction, or deposit amount disproportionate to the SFO's known wealth record.

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from a high-risk jurisdiction, or deposit amount disproportionate to the SFO's known wealth record.

Defense evidence: Prepare a documented source-of-funds and source-of-wealth file: bank statements or sale deeds showing the exact funds; employment or dividend evidence for the family principal; independent tax records; audited financial statements of the investing family holding or trust; signed loan agreements or equity subscription letters; legal counsel opinion confirming the funds came from lawful sources; sanctions and adverse-media screening results. Use a single transparent wire and do not split funds to avoid structuring suspicion.
PEP status, family-member PEP exposure, or unusual outbound disbursements to third-party beneficiaries.

Root cause: PEP status, family-member PEP exposure, or unusual outbound disbursements to third-party beneficiaries.

Defense evidence: Submit completed PEP self-declaration and family relationship map; express sanctions/PEP checks; a transaction-flow memorandum explaining each investment or family distribution; supporting invoices, board minutes, or distribution resolutions; an undertaking to notify the bank of material changes; full signatory matrix and delegation rules. For third-party transfers, include the business rationale, counterparty due-diligence data, and copies of the underlying contract or family settlement document.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital must flow through a RBV-licensed bank using SWIFT MT103 or a correspondent bank wire, never through personal accounts or unregulated payment platforms. The receiving account should be pre-notified with a source-of-funds pack, board resolution, and confirmation of the purpose code: 'Capital Contribution', 'Shareholder Loan', 'Trust Loan', or 'Family Office Investment Funding'. The wire reference must show a clear originator/beneficiary relationship and a descriptive purpose (e.g., 'Capital injection for family office investment portfolio') to satisfy both the receiving bank's KYC review and RBV statistical reporting. The SFO should maintain a capital escrow account during the establishment phase and only convert escrow funds into operational/investment accounts after documentary verification is complete. Avoid multiple small wires from different entities on the same day; one auditable and transaction-level wire matching the corporate records is the strongest compliance posture.Use SWIFT MT103 for open-account disbursements to service providers, custodian banks, investment counterparties, and professional advisers. For physical-goods trade or high-value goods transactions, use a commercial letter of credit (MT700/MT707) cleared through a Vanuatu correspondent bank’s SWIFT network, with the trade invoice, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and insurance documents presented to the paying bank. Family-office investment settlements should be cleared through the bank's securities custody or foreign exchange desk using the VUV/AUD/NZD/USD nostro rails. Distributions to family beneficiaries may be processed as 'dividend/return-of-capital' or 'trust distribution' with a paid board resolution and underlying family deed; each outbound transaction must have an underlying invoice, settlement statement, contract, or distribution memo and must pass the bank's sanctions-screening and threshold-reporting rules.Outright/rolling FX forward contracts traded with an RBV-authorised dealer or licensed international bankNon-deliverable forward (NDF) on VUV against USD, AUD, NZD or EUR where physical VUV settlement is not availableCross-currency swap (CCS) between VUV and the SFO's offshore funding currency or investment currencyFX option/swaption, used strictly for hedging the SFO's identified currency exposures and documented under a board-approved derivatives mandate

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold in Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) applies. Vanuatu has no enacted transfer-pricing law and therefore imposes no Local File or Master File preparation requirement on an International Company.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The International Companies (Economic Substance) legislation requires Vanuatu International Companies to satisfy economic substance in respect of relevant activities. For a single-family office conducting pure private investment holding, the reduced substance test applies: the company must demonstrate adequate physical presence in Vanuatu, sustain a minimum level of operating expenditure and employees in Vanuatu, and ensure that core income-generating activities (investment management and business direction decisions) are performed in Vanuatu. Licence holders and registered agents will assess compliance; failure to satisfy the economic substance test may result in VFSC penalties, information exchange reports to the OECD, and ultimately strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Revenue and customs fraud

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Customs Act and indirect tax statutes may be imposed; offending goods may be seized; repeated or sophisticated concealment can trigger criminal prosecution leading to monetary penalties calibrated to the value of undeclared goods.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who knowingly participate in false customs declarations are personally liable to criminal fines; fraudulent conduct with intent to evade tax or duties may result in imprisonment under Vanuatu criminal and customs legislation.
Economic substance violation / shell company misuse

Statutory sanction: VFSC may impose administrative penalties, issue compliance directions, suspend the registered agent relationship, and deregister the International Company. Non-compliant entities may be reported to foreign enforcement authorities under exchange-of-information agreements, resulting in loss of tax-exempt status.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for penalties imposed on the company where they knowingly caused the economic substance shortfall. In egregious cases, civil proceedings can attach personal assets, and directors may be barred from acting as company officers in Vanuatu.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial ownership non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act and the Companies Acts include pecuniary penalties, disgorgement of unlawful funds, asset freeze, and criminal prosecution. VFSC may strike the company off and revoke registered agent licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability, including custodial imprisonment for money laundering offences, civil restitution of laundered amounts, and seizure/forfeiture of assets held in the family office. Pending investigation, a travel ban may be imposed on directors to secure jurisdiction.
Insolvency and director misconduct

Statutory sanction: Insolvency proceedings under Vanuatu law may be initiated; fraudulent preference transactions may be voided; the company may be compulsorily wound up and its directors disqualified from managing Vanuatu-registered entities.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for company debts incurred through reckless or fraudulent trading; courts may order contribution out of personal estates, restitution of misused assets, disqualification orders, and imprisonment for fraudulent trading.
