Conditional. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA) — official online portal: SRA ONLINE (online.sra.gov.ws); the SRA is the single competent tax administration for income tax, Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST), excise, and related filings in Samoa.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity first incorporates as an International Company under the International Companies Act 1987 through the Samoa International Companies Registry. For tax registration, the entity then submits the SRA TIN application form (Form TIN-1) together with the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office certificate, director/shareholder details, and a filed offshore activity declaration. The SRA issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN), which also serves as the taxpayer reference for any VAGST registration. An International Company that does not carry on business in Samoa and derives only non-Samoan-source income will ordinarily obtain a tax exemption certificate rather than an active taxable resident profile; if it later makes taxable supplies in Samoa, it must separately apply for VAGST registration on Form VAT-2 before undertaking such activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned entity first incorporates as an International Company under the International Companies Act 1987 through the Samoa International Companies Registry. For tax registration, the entity then submits the SRA TIN application form (Form TIN-1) together with the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office certificate, director/shareholder details, and a filed offshore activity declaration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box classification regime. An International Company may elect, and the Registrar may confirm, to operate under the International Companies Act 1987 as a non-resident, tax-exempt offshore entity; such election is documented through a tax exemption certificate and the company's offshore-status declaration. Samoa participates in BEPS Action 2 in respect of hybrid mismatch rules, but there is no substantive hybrid election available; an IC may only elect to be treated as an exempt offshore company and cannot elect pass-through treatment for Samoa income tax. The entity is also not eligible for the Income Tax Act 2012 thin-capitalisation or group relief elections unless it converts to a local company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; a foreign International Company providing only offshore freight-forwarding services to non-residents will generally be outside this threshold and not required to register for VAGST unless it physically supplies goods or services in Samoa, including the local handling, warehousing, or freight-forwarding of dangerous goods within Samoan territory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated if the goods are physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply and evidence of export is retained. Services, including logistics, agency, and freight-forwarding services for dangerous goods, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident customer who receives the benefit of the service outside Samoa, and the service is not supplied in connection with land or movable property located in Samoa. An International Company carrying on offshore DG forwarding must establish that the service is performed outside Samoa and that neither the service nor any directly related goods are consumed, delivered, or used inside Samoa; the entity must also obtain a written non-resident status declaration from the customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (S.43 – Customs Act)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Samoa
  • Signed non-resident customer declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit evidence showing the foreign-currency consideration received into a Samoan bank account, where repatriation to Samoa is used to support the zero-rating claim
  • Covered dangerous goods documentation: IMO Dangerous Goods Declaration, DG packing certificate, transport emergency card, and proof of compliance with the IMDG Code / IATA DGR

Samoa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based government e-invoicing, or digital stamping for VAGST invoices. Registered persons must issue a valid tax invoice within 28 days of supplier. For exports and zero-rated supplies, the tax invoice must show the customer's name, supplier TIN, description, quantity, consideration in WST or foreign currency, the date of supply, and the zero rate applied. The SRA may require periodic electronic filing via SRA ONLINE, and import-related VAGST is assessed through the Samoa Customs electronic Single Window, but no transaction-level clearance is imposed for export invoices.

Zero-rating claims are a high-priority audit area for the Samoa Revenue Authority. The exporter must substantiate the export chain and foreign-currency inflow. Failure to provide the required customs and banking evidence, or any false declaration as to the offshore destination, export of goods, or residency of the customer, results in a retrospective VAT clawback at 15%, a late-payment penalty of 20% of the unpaid tax, interest (currently 20% per annum), and statutory penalties of up to WST 100,000 for a body corporate; directors and officers are personally liable where the false zero-rating claim is made knowingly or without reasonable care. Fraudulent export claims may additionally be prosecuted under the Customs Act 2014 and the Penal Code 2016, with imprisonment of up to 14 years for serious dishonesty offences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a monitored convertibility regime for the Samoan Tala (WST), with full current-account convertibility through CBS-authorized dealers and prior-approval requirements for capital-account transactions. A Samoa International Company (IC) is classified as a non-resident entity under the International Companies Act 2012 and may hold foreign-currency accounts, receive export freight proceeds, and effect outward remittances without mandatory conversion into WST. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted for tax-exempt ICs, provided the remittance is supported by audited financial statements and standard AML/CBS anti-money-laundering clearance. The practical hurdle is not convertibility but the availability of a full documentary trail proving the lawful source of funds and the underlying commercial substance of dangerous-goods forwarding operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Samoa's Exchange Control Regulations 1977, as amended, resident exporters must repatriate the full export proceeds within 120 days of the date of shipment and surrender 100% of any WST-denominated receipts to an authorized dealer. However, a non-resident International Company engaged in cross-border DG forwarding is outside the resident exchange-control surrender framework: freight and logistics proceeds earned outside Samoa may be retained indefinitely in overseas foreign-currency accounts without surrender or conversion. If the IC voluntarily opens a WST account with a Samoan bank, any local-currency receipts will be subject to the standard resident surrender and repatriation rules.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) acting under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and the Exchange Control Act 1972

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore EMI platforms are not licensed by the Central Bank of Samoa and cannot clear WST transactions or provide local Samoan settlement. Their feasibility is therefore low as a primary treasury rail, and medium only as a supplementary collection vehicle for USD/EUR freight receipts. Legal constraints require that the EMI be fully regulated in its home jurisdiction, maintain strict FATF-compliant KYC/UBO files, and avoid any solicitation of Samoan-resident clients. A Samoa IC may use such an EMI for receipt of foreign invoices, but all onward settlement into the Samoan banking system must route through a CBS-authorized dealer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 using CBS-recognized inward remittance purpose code 'A02 – Capital Investment' or the corresponding 'Q171 – Capital Contribution to an International Company' designation. The receiving authorized dealer requires a board resolution, updated shareholder register, proof of foreign source of funds, and a confirmation from the Samoan licensed registered agent. For amounts exceeding WST 100,000, CBS Form FR-03 (Source of Funds) is mandatory; for DG forwarding, the bank may additionally request the underlying freight services contract and a certificate of dangerous-goods liability insurance to evidence legitimate commercial purpose.The primary clearing rails for outbound trade disbursements are SWIFT MT103 for operational invoices and fee settlements, and commercial letters of credit (MT700 / UCP600) for high-value cargo prepayments or carrier procurement. The IC should adopt a clearing matrix: invoices below USD 50,000 settle via MT103 with attached invoice and DG manifest; ocean-carrier freight settles by T/T quoting the carrier's SWIFT BIC; port and terminal charges settle through a Samoan paying agent when necessary; and chartered shipments exceeding USD 250,000 require a sight documentary credit. All SWIFT messages must contain the invoice reference, Incoterms, and a precise description of the hazardous goods compliant with cargo-classification requirements.Onshore deliverable FX forwards against WST/USD and WST/EUR executed with CBS-authorized commercial banks, tenor limited to 12 months.Offshore nondeliverable forwards (NDFs) on WST/USD used solely to hedge accounting mismatches between foreign-currency receivables and WST payables.Cross-currency swaps between USD and EUR for freight-billing portfolio hedging, with CBS notification when booked by an onshore dealer.Plain-vanilla FX options for hedging firm commitments on high-value DG freight contracts, subject to the onboarded bank's risk approval.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (general resident corporate rate); 0% effective rate for an International Company (IC) on foreign-source income under the International Companies Act 1987 (ICA 1987), s.219.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory 15% for dividends paid by a Samoa resident company to non-residents; 0% where the payer is an International Company distributing foreign-source profits to non-resident shareholders (ICA 1987, s.219); treaty-reduced rates apply where a Samoa resident company is the payer and the shareholder holds a Samoa Tax Residency Certificate (TRC).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Income tax return and final payment due within 6 months after the end of the tax year, i.e., 30 June for a 31 December year-end; provisional tax instalments payable half-yearly per the Commissioner's instalment schedule (typically 30 June and 31 December); extension of time may be granted by the Commissioner in writing before the due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory SAT-denominated threshold for mandatory Local File or Master File documentation has been gazetted in Samoa; the Income Tax Act 2015 requires all cross-border related-party dealings, including DG forwarding fees, intercompany demurrage, insurance reinsurance, and management charges, to be priced at arm's length, and the Commissioner of Inland Revenue may on request require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for any transaction size; best practice is to prepare a Local File where aggregate related-party turnover exceeds SAT 5 million or where the IC transacts with connected foreign parent operators.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Samoa's International Companies Act 1987, as amended in line with OECD/EU tax transparency standards, imposes an economic substance requirement on International Companies: an IC carrying on a relevant activity (including freight and logistics services physically performed in Samoa) must demonstrate adequate premises, employees, and direction and management in Samoa. A DG forwarding IC that merely invoices cargo movements from Samoa without physical substance loses its offshore tax exemption, becomes taxable on Samoan-source income at 27%, and risks deregistration; conversely, an IC whose operations, contracts, and decision-making are located in a foreign jurisdiction must evidence that foreign substance to MaF to sustain the s.219 exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed Dangerous Goods Transport

Statutory sanction: Revocation or suspension of the transport licence; fines of up to SAT 100,000 per breach; seizure and detention of cargo, vehicles, or vessels; prohibition of the director from holding a transport licence for a defined period; invalidated insurance cover for hazardous cargo incidents.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for third-party injury, environmental clean-up costs, and property damage where the company carried dangerous goods without a licence or valid insurance; criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Loss of the ICA 1987 tax exemption; recharacterisation of entire fee income as Samoan-source and assessment to tax at 27%; deregistration and strike-off of the IC; late filing penalties and reinstatement costs; adverse tax transparency listing by the OECD/EU.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for corporate tax arrears recovered by the Commissioner; travel bans pending settlement; disqualification from acting as directors of any Samoa company; personal penalties for knowingly permitting the company to operate as a shell.
Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall plus interest at the prescribed rate; criminal conviction carrying fines up to SAT 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years; default assessments issued on the Commissioner's information; publication of assessment and recovery proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and the financial officer personally liable for the fraud; criminal prosecution of natural persons; restitution orders for tax lost; director travel bans and asset freezing orders during investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing Violations

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Samoa's Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018 and AML/CFT regulations: fines up to SAT 1 million for the entity; suspension or revocation of the IC's licences; freezing and forfeiture orders over freight proceeds and related bank accounts; inclusion on the FIU watchlist disrupting correspondent banking relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the money laundering reporting officer (MLRO) face personal fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering offences; personal asset forfeiture; travel bans and extradition exposure for predicate offences committed abroad.
Improper Profit Repatriation / WHT Evasion

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the payment as a dividend and assessment to 15% withholding tax (or treaty rate if relief granted late); penalty of up to 100% of the WHT evaded plus interest; denial of deduction for the disguised fee payment; recovery proceedings against the payor company.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding tax; personal contempt sanctions for ignoring Commissioner's notices; travel restrictions and criminal prosecution for aiding deliberate WHT evasion.
