Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT Act s.6 zero-rates exported goods and services supplied to non-residents where the benefit is enjoyed wholly outside Vanuatu, provided the supplier is VAT-registered and holds evidence of the offshore supply. For VU_IC the analysis is superseded by statutory exemption: offshore ISIC 6201 software licensing, remote screen-share access and IoT telemetry service income is outside the VAT system entirely, with no registration right or refund claim, provided customers are non-resident and no Vanuatu taxable establishment or domestic end-user exists.

VAT returns are lodged electronically through the VRC E-Services portal; commercial invoices in PDF or ERP format are lawful. Fraudulent zero-rating and export claims attract VAT Act penalties: civil default assessment up to 200% of the VAT evaded, monthly late-payment accruals on unpaid VAT, fines up to VUV 500,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years for knowingly false declarations. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) operates under a managed arrangement pegged to a trade-weighted basket of major trading-partner currencies; current-account convertibility is unrestricted for residents executing through an Authorized Dealer with underlying commercial documentation, while capital-account movements require standard AD due diligence rather than discretionary central-bank approval. An International Company (IC) carries a statutory exchange-control exemption under the International Companies Act, enabling unrestricted inbound equity, unrestricted repatriation of profits and liquidation proceeds, and retention of 100% of export receipts in USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, or VUV accounts without conversion obligation. The material constraint is offshore deliverability: the VUV has no active offshore market, spot liquidity is shallow outside the Authorized Dealer network, and USD-VUV conversion costs widen outside ANZ/BSP; therefore the treasury baseline should be USD-denominated concentration with VUV usage limited to local statutory spend.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no fixed percentage surrender ratio for export receipts. The Exchange Control regime requires resident exporters to repatriate proceeds to a local Authorized Dealer account within six months of export and to either convert into VUV or credit a Foreign Currency Account in accordance with the RBV exchange-control operating manual. Non-resident International Companies are exempt from mandatory repatriation, surrender, and conversion; they may retain all software and SaaS service receipts in eligible foreign currency accounts indefinitely. Where VUV is required for on-island obligations, conversion is executed at the Authorized Dealer prevailing buying rate with the supporting IC corporate file and an underlying expense instruction.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act, acting as the sole monetary authority and exchange-control administrator under the Exchange Control Act, in coordination with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) as the regulator and registrar of International Companies. The practical gatekeepers are the four licensed Authorized Dealers: ANZ Bank (Vanuatu) Limited, Bank of South Pacific (Vanuatu) Limited, BRED Bank Vanuatu Limited, and National Bank of Vanuatu Limited, each empowered to operate SWIFT, administer Foreign Currency Accounts, and execute VUV conversion trades.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Vanuatu has no domestic EMI or payment-institution licensing regime analogous to PSD2; offshore fintech EMIs (United Kingdom, European Union, Singapore, or Hong Kong) generally classify Vanuatu-domiciled International Companies as jurisdictionally high-risk due to Vanuatu's current FATF increased-monitoring status and the absence of a local operating subsidiary. The legally compliant architecture is a principal tier-1 Vanuatu bank account paired with upstream merchant acquirers Stripe and Paddle, whose payout batches settle via SWIFT to the local bank; an EMI may be used only as a supplementary expense-card or disbursement wallet where the EMIs upstream correspondent is ANZ, BSP, or a reputable broker. Any EMI relationship must be disclosed in the bank's KYC refresh; undisclosed aggregator wallets constitute a material compliance breach and invite account restriction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital should be injected via SWIFT MT103 (using MT202 for two-legged cover where size warrants) from the parent to the IC's USD concentration account at ANZ or BSP Vanuatu, with the payment narrative quoting BPM6 purpose code 5212 (Direct Investment – Equity Capital) and the receiving compliance pack containing a board resolution, share subscription or capital contribution agreement, and the VFSC return of allotment evidencing issuance. The IC's exchange-control exemption removes central-bank pre-approval, but source-of-funds evidence and clean parent-company banking references must pre-date the transfer to satisfy the receiving bank. Stripe and Paddle merchant settlements constitute cross-border service-export receipts and should be verified through payout statements mapped to EBOPS category 1800 (Telecommunications, Computer and Information Services) and directed into the same USD concentration ledger, never converted into VUV at the gateway stage, to preserve base-currency matching and avoid double conversion costs.Outbound operating disbursements (hosting, software procurement, independent contractors, director fees, marketing, and legal or agent fees) should be executed as SWIFT MT103 messages on the GPI network through ANZ or BSP, with each payment tagged to a sub-ledger purpose code that matches the underlying agreement, and a board-approved payment ladder enforcing dual-approval controls. For physical procurement or hardware-associated trade, the acceptable clearing instrument is an irrevocable Letter of Credit issued under ICC UCP 600, or documentary collections where the transaction is low-value; however, the remote-screen-share SaaS model will predominantly rely on Paddle and Stripe upstream aggregation and subsequent SWIFT sweeps into the Vanuatu account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Vanuatu statutory threshold in VUV. Vanuatu imposes no income tax and has no active transfer-pricing documentation obligation for a VU IC. However, to defend against CFC or economic-substance challenges, the IC should maintain transfer-pricing documentation in Local File/Master File style once related-party transactions exceed USD 1 million, using OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as an administrative benchmark.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu implements international standards on harmful tax practices and can require substance for internationally mobile business models. A VU IC deriving revenue from remote screen-share software, IP licensing, or IOT telemetry should maintain real economic substance: a physical office, locally engaged management, board minutes in Vanuatu, operational staff performing core R&D/licensing functions, and adequate cash-flow and contract records. Absent such substance, foreign tax authorities may reallocate profits under CFC rules, while the IC risks being characterized as an abusive shell in the shareholder's home jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and VAT Fraud

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full VAT shortfall plus statutory penalties (commonly up to 100% of the tax), civil fines, and criminal prosecution. Offences can lead to imprisonment and seizure of payment intermediary balances.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for fraudulent participation, required to make restitution, subjected to asset-freezing orders, and, upon conviction, sentenced to imprisonment. A travel ban may be imposed pending extradition or criminal proceedings.
AML and Money-Laundering Infraction

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties under Vanuatu's Financial Transactions Reporting Act and anti-money-laundering rules; freezing of accounts by the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit; revocation of any VFSC registration; criminal fines and imprisonment on conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal liability for money laundering if they knew or suspected the unlawful origin of funds; they can be held jointly liable for disgorgement, prohibited from managing companies, and subjected to travel bans and extradition requests.
Abusive Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authorities may deny tax residency, disregard the Vanuatu entity, reassess the enterprise in the parent jurisdiction, apply conduit-avoidance rules, and impose penalties of up to 200% of the tax understatement. Vanuatu authorities may strike off the IC for non-maintenance of the registered office and agent.

Director personal liability: Courts can pierce the corporate veil where the shell is a sham or fraud. Directors may be held liable for civil conspiracy, unjust enrichment, and restitution; in egregious cases they face winding-up examinations, disqualification, travel restrictions, and custodial sentences.
Sanctions Evasion and Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Violations expose Stripe/Paddle accounts to termination, fund holds, and blacklisting; enforcement authorities may impose severe civil penalties, seize proceeds, and refer individuals for criminal prosecution. The VFSC cooperates through international information exchange and regulatory assistance.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate false invoicing face criminal fraud sanctions, personal financial penalties equal to the value laundered, property restraint orders, and travel bans. In serious cases, extradition to the affected sanctioning jurisdiction is possible.
