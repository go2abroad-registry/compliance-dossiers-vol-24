Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa — the central tax administration responsible for income tax, Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST), and stamp duty; official taxpayer portal: https://www.mfr.gov.ws (MfR Taxpayer Online Services).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987, the statutory TIN sequence is: (1) engage a SIFA-licensed registered agent to file the International Company incorporation application with the Registrar of International Companies; (2) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and register the registered office and registered agent particulars; (3) submit TIN registration Form TIN-01 to the Ministry for Revenue together with the Certificate of Incorporation, Articles of Association, registered agent consent, certified passport copies of directors and ultimate shareholders, and legal opinion as to non-resident offshore status; (4) receive the TIN certificate identifying the entity as a tax-exempt International Company; (5) apply for VAGST registration (Form VAGST-01) only if the IC makes taxable supplies in Samoa, which is ordinarily not applicable to offshore cosplay manufacturing sales; the foreign parent requires no separate Samoa TIN because the Samoan IC is the distinct legal taxpayer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — a Samoa International Company is statutorily exempt under section 154 of the International Companies Act 1987: it is deemed to carry on business exclusively outside Samoa, is non-resident for income tax purposes, and is exempt from Samoa income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, customs duty, and VAGST on offshore transactions; the entity is treated as corporate-level taxable only in its owner's jurisdiction, not in Samoa.

Available election forms

(1) Domestic Company Election — an IC may elect by special resolution to redomicile under the Companies Act 2006 and become a Samoan resident ordinary company, thereby abandoning the IC tax exemption and becoming subject to the 25% corporate income tax and VAGST on domestic supplies. (2) No United States-style check-the-box election exists; Samoa does not provide single-entity pass-through classification for ICs. (3) Hybrid instrument election — the IC's governing documents may elect the legal character of shareholder advances/instruments to optimize source-country tax classification, and Samoa claims no unilateral hybrid-mismatch carveout; any conflict is resolved by the foreign jurisdiction's rules. (4) Registration for VAGST is elective for turnover below WST 70,000, allowing an IC voluntarily to register to recover input tax where local procurement occurs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 70,000 (Samoan Tala and Sene) of annual taxable turnover in Samoa; mandatory registration applies where taxable supplies and imports exceed this threshold, while foreign suppliers without a Samoa establishment are instead subject to reverse-charge obligations unless the supply qualifies for zero-rating as an offshore/export supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods — including cosplay costumes, licensed theatrical robes, and synthetic wigs — are zero-rated under section 11 of the VAGST Act 2008 when the supplier retains proof that the goods were physically removed from Samoa and delivered to the foreign buyer within the customs-prescribed export period. Offshore services rendered to a non-resident outside Samoa likewise attract 0% VAGST. A Samoa International Company engaged in export-only cosplay manufacturing is outside the VAGST net: its export sales bear 0%, and any VAGST suffered on local ancillary inputs (freight, packing materials, negligible domestic services) may be recovered as input credit only if the supplier is VAGST-registered; otherwise it is a non-creditable cost. Goods exported duty-free may also qualify for a customs duty drawback on imported inputs used in manufacture under the Customs Act 2014.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World export entry) bearing the export entry number and date
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of the goods from Samoa
  • Commercial Invoice matching the customs valuation
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin (where required by the destination jurisdiction)
  • Foreign buyer purchase order and SWIFT payment confirmation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or licensed bank credit advice evidencing final conversion and crediting of export proceeds to the IC's bank account

No real-time national e-invoicing, fiscal digital stamping, or API-based continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance system is currently mandated in Samoa. Registered persons must issue sequential-numbered VAGST tax invoices, credit notes, and adjustment notes compliant with sections 15–17 of the VAGST Act 2008; returns and payments are filed electronically through the MfR online portal, and customs export data is captured in ASYCUDA World, but there is no government pre-approval or API clearance gate for individual invoices.

The MfR and Samoa Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims under the VAGST Act 2008 and Customs Act 2014. Where export documentation is deficient, goods are diverted, or proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the export is recharacterized as a domestic supply: VAGST becomes payable at 15% retroactively to the original invoice date with interest at 2% per month or part-month. Fraudulent misuse of zero-rating or a false export declaration is a criminal tax evasion offence punishable by a fine of up to WST 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 7 years under section 65 of the VAGST Act 2008; Customs penalties may be up to three times the evaded amount with forfeiture of goods. For an International Company, such abuse triggers revocation of IC status under the International Companies Act 1987 by the Registrar of International Companies, causing retrospective loss of the tax-exempt zone classification and a look-back assessment of up to 6 years for income tax, VAGST, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) operates under a managed float administered by the Central Bank of Samoa. Current account transactions (trade, services, interest, dividends below statutory thresholds) are generally convertible upon submission of supporting trade documentation. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment equity injections, intercompany debt, and remittance of liquidation proceeds, require prior exchange control permits from CBS. Samoa International Companies (ICs) that are non-resident owned and maintain no domestic business activity enjoy a statutory tax exemption; however, CBS still applies exchange control monitoring to any WST-denominated accounts. Profit remittance is permissible in the original currency of receipt without a mandatory conversion to WST, provided the payment is identified as a dividend from an IC and is made from a licensed external or foreign currency account. Full convertibility of the tala on the capital account is not available; case-by-case approval is required for capital outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBS Exchange Control regulations, all export receipts derived by a Samoa-resident entity must be repatriated to Samoa within 90 days of the later of shipment date or payment receipt. The full 100% of export proceeds must be sold to a licensed commercial bank for WST unless the exporter holds an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or External Account, in which case the surrender requirement is waived for the fund balance retained offshore. For International Companies that are non-resident and transact exclusively through an external account, the surrender rule does not apply; however, if any WST account is maintained, all foreign-currency inflows converted into WST must be declared to CBS on a monthly basis. Any conversion above USD 100,000 equivalent per single transaction requires prior CBS approval.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), also operating as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and Financial Institutions Act 1996.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Samoa does not currently issue electronic money institution (EMI) licenses or standalone fintech payment licenses. Payment services are restricted to banks and credit institutions licensed under the Financial Institutions Act 1996. Non-resident digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex may not establish a direct local settlement account in WST. They can be used by the Samoa IC only as offshore collection channels for retail card payments, subject to downstream settlement into a Samoa-licensed commercial bank account. No legal entity of the EMI may be registered as an authorized dealer or payment intermediary in Samoa.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Samoa International Company, all inbound capital injections must be received through a licensed commercial bank account in Samoa via SWIFT MT103/MT202. The wire instruction must use the beneficiary account name exactly matching the IC's Certificate of Incorporation. The payment purpose field must reference 'ISIC 3240 – Capital Contribution to International Company' and include the Samoa International Company registration number (e.g., IC-XXXX). For amounts exceeding USD 100,000 equivalent, the Samoa receiving bank will require a copy of the exchange control approval letter from CBS or a declaration that the funds represent a non-resident shareholder loan/equity qualifying for automatic approval under the Exchange Control (General) Exemption Notice. All inbound wire confirmations must be supported by a source-of-funds statement (bank reference from the remitting bank) and an undertaking that the capital is used solely for the company's legitimate foreign trade activities.Primary clearing rail for outbound trade settlements is the SWIFT network via Samoa-licensed correspondent banks. Payments to overseas suppliers are executed as SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with full reconciliation. For letters of credit (LCs), the Samoa bank will advise or confirm commercial LCs on behalf of the International Company under UCP 600. The company will typically open an LC from its external account funded by USD/EUR balances, with the counterparty presenting documents through their confirming bank. For low-value e-commerce refunds and chargebacks on cosplay apparel, the IC may use the Stripe and Shopify Payments settlement rails directly, but aggregated refund payouts must not create a WST deficit in the Samoa operational account. The bank will require monthly reconciliation of all outbound disbursements against purchase orders, bills of lading, and warehouse receipts. High-value trade disbursements above USD 50,000 equivalent must include a copy of the commercial invoice, packing list, and shipping proof to avoid exchange control penalties.Onshore forward currency contracts with a Samoa-licensed commercial bank, permitted for current account trade transactions with a maximum maturity of 12 months and genuine underlying export/import exposure.Cross-currency swaps between WST and USD/EUR approved on a case-by-case basis by the Central Bank of Samoa for capital account items or long-term supply contracts.FX swaps with a licensed bank for short-term liquidity management (up to 6 months), subject to daily CBS position reporting.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore; however, offshore non-resident counterparties may transact in WST-based NDFs outside Samoa without CBS regulatory approval, provided the Samoa IC does not directly book the NDF in its Samoa bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory SAT-denominated threshold is codified. Samoa has no mandatory Local File / Master File filing regime for International Companies. However, if the IC transacts with a Samoan resident or related party, transfer pricing documentation must be prepared based on OECD principles to substantiate arm's length pricing and protect 0% exemption status. Such records must be retained at the registered office for 5 years.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has not enacted a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime, but SIFSA and the Tax Administration impose a substance-over-form doctrine. An IC must demonstrate a physical registered office, a licensed agent, board decisions documented outside Samoa, and genuine commercial activity conducted outside Samoa. A cosplay prop and apparel manufacturing IC must show real foreign distribution contracts, procurement, and invoicing substance; otherwise it may be treated as a sham, subject to strike-off, or taxed as a Samoan resident.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misrepresentation of Offshore Income

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2016, evading tax attracts a penalty of 150% of the tax evaded, plus interest at 2% per month; criminal conviction carries a fine up to SAT 400,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a false declaration may be personally liable for the company's unpaid tax, subject to asset freeze orders, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.
Shell Company Abuse / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SIFSA may revoke the IC status, impose fines of SAT 10,000 per non-compliance, and strike the company off; foreign tax authorities may recharacterize the structure and impose withholding or anti-avoidance taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for company debts and tax liabilities; if fraud is proven, they face Director disqualification orders and travel bans under mutual assistance litigation.
AML/CFT and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's AML/CFT Act 2018, fines up to SAT 1,000,000 per offence; banks may close accounts, and suspicious transactions are reported to the FIU; criminal money laundering charges carry penalties of up to SAT 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Personal asset confiscation, seizure of digital payment proceeds, travel bans, and imprisonment for 10–14 years may apply.
Misuse of Tax Loss Carry-Forward or Improper Records

Statutory sanction: Fines of SAT 50,000 for failure to keep records, plus daily default penalty of SAT 500; the exemption may be backdated and revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable to pay penalties and may be sentenced to imprisonment for up to 3 years for failure to maintain proper books.
