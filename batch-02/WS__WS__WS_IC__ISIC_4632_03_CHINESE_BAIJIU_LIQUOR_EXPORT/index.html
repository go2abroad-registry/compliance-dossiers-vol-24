Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), established under the Samoa Revenue Authority Act 2022 as the unified revenue and customs administration (successor to the Ministry for Revenue and the Customs Division); self-service filing via the SRA Tax Administration System (TAS) portal at https://www.sra.gov.ws.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Samoa International Company (WS_IC): (1) the licensed registered agent files the incorporation application with the Registrar of International Companies at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL) under the International Companies Act 1987; (2) upon issue of the Certificate of Incorporation, the registered agent lodges SRA Form TIN-01 with the Samoa Revenue Authority, attaching the certified certificate of incorporation, the memorandum and articles of association and the agent's letter of good standing; (3) the SRA issues the company's unique Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) via the TAS portal within 5-10 business days; (4) because the IC is entirely offshore, no income-tax file and no VAGST registration is opened unless the company later elects to carry on a taxable activity in Samoa.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — For a foreign-owned Samoa International Company (WS_IC): (1) the licensed registered agent files the incorporation application with the Registrar of International Companies at the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL) under the International Companies Act 1987; (2) upon issue of the Certificate of Incorporation, the registered agent lodges SRA Form TIN-01 with the Samoa Revenue Authority, attaching the certified certificate of incorporation, the memorandum and articles of association and the agent's letter of good standing; (3) the SRA issues the company's unique Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) via the TAS portal within 5-10 business days; (4) because the IC is entirely offshore, no income-tax file and no VAGST registration is opened unless the company later elects to carry on a taxable activity in Samoa.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box election. The IC is automatically exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, distributions tax, stamp duty and all other Samoan taxes under the International Companies Act 1987, s. 186 (as amended). The only statutory election is an opt-in to the Income Tax Act 2015 regime: an IC that files notice with the SRA that it is carrying on business in Samoa becomes taxable on its Samoa-source income and forfeits the exemption. There is no hybrid-mismatch or disregarded-entity re-characterization election available to an exempt IC other than formal liquidation and re-incorporation as a local company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WS$ 100,000 (Samoa Tala) value of taxable supplies made in Samoa over any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is available where taxable supplies are between WS$ 50,000 and WS$ 100,000; a non-resident IC whose baijiu supply chain (PRC stock to non-resident distributors) never crosses into Samoa has no taxable activity in Samoa and is not registrable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAGST Act 2003, s. 28: supplies of goods physically exported from Samoa to a place outside Samoa, and long-distance international transport, are zero-rated where the exporter holds documentary proof of export within 30 days of the time of supply. A Samoa IC structured as an offshore spirits wholesaler that sources Chinese baijiu from bonded stock outside Samoa and invoices non-resident distributors by SWIFT wire or letter of credit has no Samoa taxable supply and therefore no VAGST payable; where goods are consolidated or trans-shipped through a Samoan port, the 0% rate applies and the IC may claim refundable input credits (local warehousing, handling and customs-related charges) through the VAGST-R1 refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDEC) lodged through ASYCUDA World; Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Samoa; Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the licensed commercial bank confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Original sales contract and commercial invoice matching description, volume (litres of pure alcohol) and unit price to the CusDEC
  • SWIFT wire or Letter of Credit documentation matching the remittance certificate and the bank's exchange-control filing

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no national clearance-API e-invoicing layer and no digital stamping. Samoa mandates electronic filing and payment of VAGST returns in the SRA TAS portal; domestic point-of-sale receipts must be issued through SRA-approved Electronic Fiscal Devices where applicable; export transactions are cleared through ASYCUDA World (Samoa Customs) and the CusDEC export entry, the validated bill of lading and the VAGST-R1 refund application are digitally cross-matched against the exporter's TAS account before any refund is remitted.

Export zero-rating refund claims are subject to mandatory SRA post-clearance audit and Customs verification. Where false declarations, fictitious exports, circular remittances or forged bills of lading are detected, the SRA claws back the refunded VAGST plus a shortfall penalty of 25%-150% of the tax shortfall (Tax Administration Act 2012), imposes interest at 20% per annum on the clawed-back amount, cancels the exporter's VAGST registration, and may prosecute directors and beneficial owners for tax evasion under TAA 2012, s. 33, with joint and several criminal and civil liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The WST is a partially convertible, administered currency without a deep offshore interbank market; conversion is available only through ADs at CBS-published indicative rates. Current-account transactions (trade in goods such as baijiu export, shipping, insurance, distributor commissions) are freely permitted for an International Company (IC) through an AD with underlying commercial documentation. Capital-account flows (initial equity injection, intercompany loans, profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders) require prior CBS exchange-control approval via the AD, or a documented AD exemption for non-resident ICs operating in the Tax-Exempt Zone. Profit remittance from the IC is generally unrestricted once audited financial statements, tax clearance (if any), board resolution, and source-of-funds evidence are filed; practical hurdle is WST liquidity, so USD-denominated accounts and USD settlement are mandatory for the Chinese baijiu trade.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory remittance rule: 100% of export proceeds must be repatriated through an AD in Samoa within a maximum of 90 days from the bill-of-lading date, with the export certificate/electronic customs declaration filed to CBS via the AD. Proceeds invoiced in a foreign currency (USD, and where accepted CNY) may be credited to and retained in an approved foreign-currency account held with the AD; mandatory conversion into WST applies only where the exporter elects WST settlement or operates without an approved foreign-currency account. For a tax-exempt IC, CBS generally permits USD retention to fund onward China supplier payments, provided no net WST conversion is sought; any extension of the 90-day window requires a written CBS exemption submitted by the AD at least 14 days before expiry.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) - Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa, exercising statutory exchange control powers under the Exchange Control Act 1976 (as amended) and the Exchange Control Regulations 1983. CBS operates exclusively through designated Authorized Dealers (ADs), which are the licensed commercial banks in Apia. The AD acts as the reporting and approval gateway for all inward/outward remittances, export proceeds verification, and any capital-account transaction involving the Samoan Tala (WST).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Samoa has no fintech EMI licensing regime; offshore digital banks and EMIs (e.g., UK FCA or Lithuanian-licensed) cannot clear WST, cannot operate local IBANs, and CBS exchange control requires all trade receipts to flow through a Samoan AD. An offshore EMI may only be used as a collection conduit if funds are swept immediately to the BSP/ANZ-class AD account and documented, but most Samoan compliance officers will reject such flows as opaque source-of-funds; therefore, reliance on EMIs is not commercially viable for a baijiu exporter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity injections must be executed as SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers from the shareholder's account to the IC's BSP USD operational account, using remittance purpose code 'EQUI/CAPI' (equity contribution) and unrestricted memo 'Subscription to WS International Company capital'. The AD will require CBS approval or exemption before crediting; funds must not be commingled with export trade receipts. Mandatory verification pack: share subscription agreement, board resolution, certified member register, identity/source-of-funds documents of the injecting shareholder, and the CBS application or AD waiver. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% resident corporate income tax rate; 0% effective rate for a compliant Samoa International Company (statutorily exempt on non-Samoan-source income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident Samoan company to non-residents; 0% on dividends and distributions paid by a tax-exempt International Company (no deduction at source because the IC has no Samoan tax liability).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No published statutory threshold in the Income Tax Act 2012; the MoR applies an indicative threshold of WST 5,000,000 aggregate annual related-party cross-border turnover above which contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation is required. A tax-exempt International Company sits outside the CIT net but must still document arm's-length export margins for its Economic Substance Declaration and to resist any recharacterization of income as Samoan-source.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations under the International Companies Act 2023 apply with full force. A baijiu export distribution IC is classified as a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity and must demonstrate: board meetings held in Samoa, physical premises in Samoa, qualified employees resident in Samoa, adequate local operating expenditure, and core income-generating activities performed in Samoa. An annual Economic Substance Declaration must be filed with SIFA; failure triggers penalties, deregistration, and recharacterization of the IC as a resident taxable company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Exemption Breach / Unauthorized Domestic Trading

Statutory sanction: Retroactive corporate income tax at 27% on all Samoan-source profits; penalty surcharge up to 100% of the tax evaded; interest at 2% per month compounding; assessment of 15% withholding tax on deemed distributions; strike-off by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax after strike-off; courts may impose travel bans to secure collection; knowing breach is a criminal offence punishable by fine up to WST 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance penalties up to WST 100,000 per contravention; deregistration of the IC; automatic loss of the tax exemption; adverse notification to the Samoan FIU and international regulatory partners.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines for knowingly certifying false substance declarations; disqualification from acting as a director for up to 5 years; cross-border asset-freeze orders in aid of foreign tax authorities.
AML/CFT and Financial Integrity Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act: fines up to WST 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 14 years; asset forfeiture; FIU freezing orders against bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecutable for willful blindness or consent to laundering; travel bans and passport surrender may be imposed pending investigation; civil confiscation of director assets traceable to tainted funds.
Fraudulent Trading and False Filings

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act 2023: criminal fine up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; restitution orders for funds extracted; voidability of fraudulent preferences.

Director personal liability: Directors are civilly liable for the company's debts where fraudulent trading is proved (piercing the corporate veil); disqualification; court-ordered travel bans; extradition cooperation for related foreign offences.
