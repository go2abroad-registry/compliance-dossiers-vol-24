Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or any domestic entity classification election. An International Company is automatically tax-exempt on its worldwide offshore income: no income tax, no VAGST, no stamp duty, no gift or estate duty, and no exchange control on foreign currency transactions. The entity may voluntarily elect to register for VAGST, but such an election does not remove the income tax exemption. If the IC amends its constitutional documents and converts to a Domestic Company under the Companies Act 2017, it becomes subject to the ordinary Samoa income tax system and may become liable to VAGST on domestic turnover. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not available, as Samoa has not enacted OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid-entity rules. The MfR nonetheless applies substance and beneficial-ownership documentation standards when reviewing a tax-exempt IC's annual filing with the registered agent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 100,000 (Samoa Tala) annual value of taxable supplies; compulsory registration is triggered in the month immediately following the month in which taxable turnover exceeds this threshold, while voluntary registration is permitted for businesses making taxable supplies below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated under the VAGST Act 2003 where the goods are exported to a destination outside Samoa before the supplier issues the tax invoice or within 28 days after the date of supply, and the supplier retains full documentary evidence of physical export. A supply of services is zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and the services are performed entirely outside Samoa. For specialty green coffee beans exported in containers, the goods must be physically shipped from Samoa under a customs-approved export declaration, sold to a buyer outside Samoa, and paid for by inward remittance from that non-resident buyer. An International Company trading offshore from Samoa does not normally enter the VAGST system; if the entity is voluntarily registered and makes a zero-rated export, it may claim a refund of input VAGST on purchases attributable to that export under section 23 of the VAGST Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs Service export entry or SAD-2), with green coffee beans classified under HS heading 0901.11
  • Bill of Lading (named ocean carrier, evidencing shipment from a Samoa port to a destination outside Samoa)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (issued by a licensed Samoan bank showing receipt of hard-currency payment from the non-resident buyer)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (cross-referenced to the export declaration)
  • Certificate of Origin (issued by the Samoa Chamber of Commerce or Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade)
  • Container seal number and port-of-loading verification report

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or API-based digital stamping regime is currently enforced in Samoa. Registered businesses may issue paper tax invoices or PDF e-tax invoices in accordance with the VAGST Act. VAGST returns may be filed electronically through the Ministry for Revenue's e-services portal. Fiscal control is exercised through desk audits, cross-matching of customs declarations, and periodic invoice review. No third-party fiscal device mandate applies to international trading companies.

A false export zero-rating claim is subject to retrospective assessment at the standard VAGST rate (15%) on the under-declared export value, plus late payment penalty of 10% and accruing interest. For fraudulent or deliberately understated claims, the Ministry for Revenue may impose a civil penalty tax of up to 100% of the tax shortfall and, in the case of criminal prosecution, a fine not exceeding SAT 50,000 or imprisonment not exceeding two years, or both. Refunds already paid may be clawed back together with penalties where the supporting export documentation is false, forged, inconsistent with customs records, or where the inward remittance does not come from the stated offshore buyer within the 180-day benchmark. Officers of the International Company may be personally liable, and the registered agent may be directed by the Registrar to strike the company off the International Companies Register for non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoa Tala (WST) is a managed currency pegged to a trade-weighted basket comprising mainly the US Dollar, Australian Dollar, New Zealand Dollar and Euro. Current-account transactions, including documented trade payments for green coffee imports, are generally processed by authorized dealers without prior central bank approval, although the supporting commercial documentation must be retained. Capital-account transactions, including equity injections into and dividend or liquidation remittances out of an International Company, require exchange-control approval or deemed approval through the receiving authorized dealer; in practice, a fully compliant International Company with a licensed registered agent faces minimal material hurdles, but profit remittances are subject to evidence of audited financial statements and a written declaration that no Samoan domestic tax liability exists, given the tax-exempt status of International Companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Central Bank of Samoa Exchange Control Notices, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa within three months from the date of shipment and 100% of such proceeds must be surrendered to an authorized dealer in Samoa Tala unless a specific foreign-currency retention approval has been granted for an approved account. For the specialty green coffee import and redistribution model, the International Company must submit a CBS trade declaration for each shipment, retain the authorized dealer's foreign-exchange settlement advice, and make the full transaction record available for examination for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), which exercises foreign exchange control powers under the Exchange Control Act 1977 and delegates day-to-day supervision and processing to authorized dealer commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Samoa's Exchange Control Act 1977 and Central Bank of Samoa licensing framework recognize only authorized dealers and licensed money-changers as lawful foreign-exchange intermediaries; offshore digital EMIs have no clearing equivalence for Samoa Tala transactions, cannot discharge the export-proceeds surrender obligation, and are not eligible to operate domestic payment or settlement rails in Samoa. An offshore EMI may be used only as a supplementary pre-funded US Dollar disbursement vehicle for supplier payments outside Samoa, never as the primary liquidity or compliance rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on dividends paid by a Samoa resident company to a non-resident shareholder. Zero withholding applies to dividend distributions from an exempt International Company funded exclusively by offshore profits, as the distribution is outside the Samoa tax net.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 30 calendar days after the anniversary date of incorporation, together with payment of the annual government fee. If the IC fails to certify its offshore-exclusive status, a corporate income tax return must instead be filed within 6 months of the financial year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Under the Income Tax Act 2015, the annual CIT return is due within 6 months after the end of the financial year. A 3-month extended filing window is available upon written application to the Ministry of Revenue before the original due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa law does not publish a statutory tala threshold, but the administrative benchmark triggering mandatory transfer pricing documentation, including Local File and Master File, is aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceeding WS$5,000,000 per income year. For transactions above WS$1,000,000, the Ministry of Revenue may compel contemporaneous documentation. The specialty green coffee IC should retain at the registered agent's office a full Local File whenever related-party coffee purchases exceed WS$5,000,000 or when the IC sells to a foreign affiliate of the ultimate beneficial owner.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 apply mandatory economic substance requirements to Samoa International Companies conducting relevant activities. Coffee container distribution by a Samoa IC falls within the distribution and service centre definition where inventory is procured from or sold to associated enterprises. The IC must demonstrate: (a) physical office or dedicated business premises in Samoa; (b) full-time qualified employees engaged in the coffee logistics and distribution management; (c) board meetings and senior management decisions occurring in Samoa; (d) adequate operational expenditure in Samoa. An annual Economic Substance Declaration must be filed through the registered agent. A purely pass-through offshore trading conduit that lacks local substance is non-compliant and may be struck off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Offshore-Source Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Retroactive loss of tax-exempt status; reassessment of all Samoa-source income at 27% corporate tax; evasion penalty of up to 100% of tax understated; interest at 2% per month; administrative strike-off from the Registry; publication of the IC as a non-compliant entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certified false offshore status are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax through judicial piercing of the corporate veil, with civil restitution, freezing of personal assets, travel restraint orders, and imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraudulent tax conduct.
Shell Company and Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalties of up to WS$1,000,000; cancellation of the IC registration; automatic exchange of information to the ultimate parent's foreign tax authority under Samoa's exchange-of-information network; foreign recharacterization of the Samoan IC as a transparent shell.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil restitution of government penalties, disqualification from holding any corporate office in Samoa for up to 10 years, and travel bans imposed by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit pending completion of the substance investigation.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines up to WS$500,000; registered agent licence suspension; forfeiture of the IC's bank balances; mandatory deregistration and referral to the Public Prosecutor.

Director personal liability: Directors convicted of money laundering face imprisonment of 5 to 10 years, monetary penalties of up to three times the laundered amount, personal travel bans, and disqualification under the Samoa AML/CFT Act 2018.
Striking-Off for Non-Filing of Annual Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative striking-off of the International Company; all undischarged IC assets vest in the financial authority; late surcharges and restoration fees; loss of limited liability protection for post-striking-off contracts.

Director personal liability: Former directors remain personally liable for debts contracted after striking-off and may be prohibited from re-registering any International Company in Samoa for 5 years, with potential contempt sanctions for using the struck-off entity to settle coffee trade obligations.
