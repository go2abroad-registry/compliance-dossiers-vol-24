Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of Samoa (MfR), Apia; online filing portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

International Companies under the International Companies Act 1987 are statutorily non-resident and exempt from Samoan income tax, stamp duty, and exchange control; they do not require a domestic TIN for normal offshore activities. The Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of International Companies serves as the statutory identity identifier. A foreign entity engaging in local VAGST registrable activities must submit the prescribed MfR registration forms (including Form VAGST-1 and TIN application) supported by the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director identification, and a local address or registered agent confirmation; the Ministry issues a nine-digit Inland Revenue taxpayer number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods physically exported from Samoa within the prescribed statutory period (goods must leave Samoa within 21 days of supply, or 60 days for specially manufactured goods, with satisfactory evidence). Services may be zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Samoa. For the die-cast scale automotive model business, each shipment must be an outright sale to a non-resident customer, with no local consumption, storage, installation, or further supply in Samoa before export.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged with the Samoa Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical removal of goods from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit record proving foreign-exchange repatriation
  • Original commercial invoice and signed packing list
  • Certificate of Origin (where required by destination jurisdiction)

None. Samoa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping. Registered suppliers must issue sequential tax invoices (paper or electronic PDF), retain them for at least six years, and file periodic VAGST returns (generally bi-monthly) through the Ministry for Revenue online portal or physical submission.

The Ministry for Revenue may reassess an export zero-rating claim as a taxable supply at 15% VAGST where export documentation is incomplete, goods are diverted into the Samoan market, or foreign-exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the prescribed period. Civil penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2012 include shortfall penalties based on conduct (from 20% to 100% of the unpaid tax), late-payment penalties, and interest. Fraudulent export zero-rating, false declarations, forged inward remittance evidence, or fictitious exports expose the company and its officers to criminal prosecution, substantial fines, and imprisonment; the VAGST refund is clawed back with interest from the date of the original claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a CBS-managed basket peg with limited offshore deliverability. Current-account payments are freely convertible upon documented underlying transactions. Capital-account transfers by resident persons require CBS exchange-control approval; however, an International Company (IC) maintaining an External Account is treated as non-resident, enabling unrestricted convertibility and profit remittance of WST/USD/EUR balances without a CBS permit. The only practical hurdle is the lack of an active offshore WST market, which compels USD or AUD as the functional treasury currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control (Export) Regulations, resident exporters must surrender export proceeds into Samoa within 3 months of shipment unless an External Account exemption is granted. A non-resident IC is not subject to the mandatory surrender ratio and may retain 100% of receipts in a designated foreign-currency proceeds account. Each inward credit must be matched against a commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading to satisfy CBS reporting and bank-level AML documentary requirements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) — Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa; Exchange Control Act 1976; Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Prohibition of Financing of Terrorism Act 2017; Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) for international-company licensing

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No formal Local File or Master File regime exists under Samoa domestic law for International Companies; the general arm's length principle applies to cross-border related-party dealings under the Income Tax Act 2014; prudent contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is recommended wherever annual related-party turnover exceeds WST 2,000,000 or where intercompany IP royalties exceed WST 500,000, and such documentation is mandatory for treaty relief applications and ESR evaluations

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as amended 2021) require any IC carrying on a relevant activity to demonstrate sufficient economic substance in Samoa. An IC that purchases zinc die-cast and tampo-printed 1:18 scale replicas from a connected Chinese manufacturer and sells them to global distributors is classified as a distribution and service centre, requiring core income-generating activities in Samoa, including strategic board-level management, contract negotiation, logistics and inventory control, and risk management. The IC must maintain a physical office in Samoa, employ adequate full-time staff, and incur adequate operating expenditure; a pure equity holding IC needs only registry compliance. Failure to meet the substance test attracts an administrative penalty of up to WST 500,000 and mandatory strike-off from the register, and the Registrar must notify the exchange of information partner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through abuse of International Company exemption

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of the tax avoided plus daily default penalties; forfeiture of the ICA exemption with retrospective assessment at 27% for all concealed income; freezing orders against bank accounts; deregistration of the IC

Director personal liability: Directors, registered agents, and beneficial owners who knowingly participate are personally and jointly liable for civil restitution of unpaid taxes; the Commissioner may issue departure prohibition orders imposing travel bans; criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to seven years
Shell company and sham economic substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty up to WST 500,000; compulsory strike-off and dissolution; disgorgement of profits derived during the period of non-compliance; referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit for coordinated AML action

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false substance declarations are personally liable for the penalty and restitution; the court may disqualify them from acting as directors for up to ten years; false declarations carry imprisonment of up to five years
Money laundering and unlicensed payment activity

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to WST 7,000,000 under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007; restraint and confiscation orders over all assets, including the entire share capital of the IC; deregistration and blacklisting of the registered agent

Director personal liability: Directors and signatories are criminally liable for acts of laundering committed with knowledge or reckless disregard; imprisonment of up to 14 years; personal assets may be confiscated; travel bans and extradition cooperation apply
Breach of shareholder and director UBO disclosure obligations

Statutory sanction: Fine not exceeding WST 500,000 against the company; daily penalty of WST 1,000 for each day the register remains defective; deregistration in the case of persistent breach

Director personal liability: Each director and the registered agent are separately prosecuted; personal fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to two years; directors remain personally liable to creditors if the company is struck off while liabilities are unpaid
