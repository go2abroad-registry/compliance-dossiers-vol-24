Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Apia; online filing portal: SRA e-Tax / Samoa Tax Online Services (STOS) at https://www.revenue.gov.ws. International company registration is administered separately by the Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL).

E-government filing portal

For a Samoa International Company (IC): (1) obtain Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of International Companies under the International Companies Act 2012, including registered agent and registered office in Samoa; (2) apply to SRA for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) using Form IRD-001 accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered agent consent, and director/shareholder due diligence documents; (3) SRA issues the TIN and places the IC on the taxpayer register. The TIN is used for bank account opening and customs/VAT administrative dealings. The IC's international business income remains statutorily exempt from Samoa income tax and stamp duty by virtue of Section 220 of the International Companies Act 2012.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: A Samoa International Company is treated as a non-resident exempt entity for Samoa tax purposes. It is entitled to a permanent exemption from Samoa income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty with respect to all property, business, operations, and transactions conducted internationally. Income is taxed at 0%. Any domestic-source business activity conducted within Samoa is outside the statutory exemption and would be prohibited without a special licence.

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a check-the-box regime. IC tax exemption is automatic upon incorporation as an International Company and does not require a fiscal election. Available elections include: (i) redomiciliation/election for a foreign company to continue as a Samoa IC under Part XVII of the International Companies Act 2012; (ii) election to convert an IC to a domestic company under the Companies Act 2012, which would subject it to normal Samoa income tax at the prevailing corporate rate; (iii) elections as to share capital structure, financial statements, and annual return filings under the IC Act. No hybrid mismatch elections exist in Samoa domestic law; hybrid arbitrage is addressed, where relevant, through general anti-avoidance rules and economic substance considerations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT$100,000 (Samoa Tala) of taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period for goods or services supplied in Samoa. A person carrying on a taxable activity that meets or exceeds this threshold must register for VAT with SRA. An International Company carrying on exclusively offshore business is not required to register; however, if it makes taxable domestic supplies (e.g., local sales or local input purchases attracting VAT credits), it should register to comply with VAT obligations and to claim input tax credits on exported enamel-pin production inputs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Samoa qualify for zero-rating under Section 15 of the Value Added Tax Act 2008 when the goods are physically removed from Samoa within the prescribed period (generally 60 days of supply) and supplied to a person outside Samoa under a genuine export transaction. Supplies of services to non-residents who are outside Samoa at the time of supply and where the services are used wholly outside Samoa are also zero-rated. A manufacturer of hard enamel lapel pins may claim 0% VAT on export sales and recover attributable input VAT on raw materials, die-struck labor, and cloisomé finishing services, subject to SRA substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged electronically with the Samoa Customs Service evidencing the outward shipment of the enamel pins
  • Commercially complete invoice and packing list describing the merchandise, ISIC/HS classification, quantities, and export value
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing the consignor (Samoa IC) and consignee outside Samoa, with proof of physical exportation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 credit advice demonstrating receipt of payment from the foreign buyer in convertible currency
  • SRA VAT export schedule and VAT refund claim form supported by source tax invoices for materials, processing, and freight services attributable to the export

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API clearance mandate is currently in force. VAT-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices in the prescribed form (paper or PDF) containing the particulars required under Schedule 5 of the Value Added Tax Act 2008, retain them for audit, and file VAT returns and payments electronically via the SRA STOS portal on a monthly basis. SRA may require digital record-keeping but does not operate a continuous e-invoicing clearance system similar to Latin American models.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict SRA audit scrutiny. A claim found to be fraudulent or unsupported triggers: (i) immediate assessment and clawback of the 15% VAT that should have been paid, computed on the full export value; (ii) statutory shortfall and evasion penalties, which may reach up to 100% of the evaded VAT, plus late-payment interest at 2% per month; (iii) possible criminal prosecution under the Value Added Tax Act 2008 and Tax Administration Act 2012, with fines up to SAT$50,000 and imprisonment for responsible officers; and (iv) for an International Company, a review of its Section 220 tax-exempt status, potential revocation or forfeiture of the IC certificate, and recharacterization of offshore transactions under general anti-avoidance rules, causing loss of the 0% effective rate and exposure to full Samoa corporate income tax on deemed domestic-source income.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a licensed exchange-control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1980 and the Foreign Exchange Regulations 1984, administered by the Central Bank of Samoa. The Samoan Tala (WST) is freely convertible for current international transactions, but capital-account remittances by resident persons are subject to CBS prior approval or authorized-dealer authorization. For the Samoa International Company (WS_IC), the core operations, export sales, and merchant settlement proceeds are non-resident transactions: the IC may lawfully maintain foreign-currency bank accounts, receive USD trade proceeds, and remit dividends abroad without WST conversion or CBS profit-remittance approval. If the IC were to incur domestic Samoan expenses in WST, the local bank would require a normal conversion entry and supporting invoice; no additional CBS approval is triggered for external, non-resident transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa does not impose any statutory mandatory conversion ratio or fixed surrender deadline for export receipts. Under standard CBS authorized-dealer practice, resident exporters are generally required to route proceeds through a licensed bank within 180 days of shipment and to retain supporting customs, transport, and invoicing documentation; the proceeds may then be held in the original foreign currency. A Samoa International Company is treated as an external entity for exchange-control purposes: its USD receipts from Etsy Direct Pay, Stripe-equivalent intermediaries, and direct wholesale buyers are not subject to any Samoan surrender requirement and can be retained, pooled, and disbursed in USD without converting into WST.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial equity and shareholder loans should be lawfully injected through a licensed authorized dealer using SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's or counsel's bank in Hong Kong, Singapore, Australia, or the United States. Beneficiary name must match the Samoa International Company exactly; field 70 should state "EQUITY INJECTION / WS_IC CAPITALIZATION"; the remitting bank should classify the purpose as direct-investment equity capital (equivalent to BPM6 Direct Investment equity). Because the WS_IC is non-resident, no CBS prior approval is required for an offshore-to-offshore capital injection; the receiving Samoa bank will request a certified board resolution allotting shares, register of allotment, and a source-of-funds statement for each beneficial owner. The capital should be held in a USD-denominated account to avoid any WST conversion risk.All outbound supplier payments for enamel-pin materials and subcontracted cloisonné polishing should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 direct-beneficiary transfers to the Chinese or Southeast Asian manufacturer, or, for larger production runs, through an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (SWIFT MT700). The clearing orchestration is a USD corridor: WS_IC's ANZ/BSP Samoa USD account is linked to a US correspondent bank, and each disbursement must be backed by purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or courier airway bill, and quality-inspection certificate to pass anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening. To use Etsy Direct Pay or USA-origin card rails, the practical non-prohibited structure is to use a USD bank account in a supported clearing jurisdiction, or a licensed payment facilitator under the IC's own name; otherwise the legal route is a USD account at ANZ Samoa or BSP Samoa with Payoneer as the intermediary settlement engine.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No monetary threshold in Samoa tala (WST) has been promulgated for International Companies because they are outside the income tax net. There is no mandatory Local File or Master File for an exempt IC. The relevant threshold is practical: where total annual worldwide related-party revenue from cross-border sales exceeds the scale at which foreign tax authorities require OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation, the IC should prepare a lightweight arm's-length analysis covering the purchase of enamel pin blanks, die-striking and cloisonné polish work, and sales commission paid to any related marketing entity.

Economic substance scope: Samoa applies an OECD/EU-aligned economic substance doctrine to International Companies. The IC must demonstrate adequate physical presence, staff, management and expenditure in Samoa, or engage a licensed Samoa international service provider that performs those functions. A pure shell company with no substance and no business purpose is exposed to deregistration and information-exchange referrals. For the subject activity (hard enamel lapel pin manufacture and e-commerce distribution), the IC may satisfy substance through contracting with third-party original-equipment manufacturers outside Samoa, provided the IC retains substantive decision-making, invoicing, quality-assurance, and bank-account control; if these functions are merely delegated to the non-resident UBO, the substance doctrine is violated.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Samoa International Companies Act 1987 and the Tax Administration Act 2012, false declarations to the Registrar are punishable by monetary penalties, cancellation of the IC's certificate, and compulsory registration under Samoa tax law if the activity is found to produce Samoa-source income. The Tax Administration Act authorises assessment of evaded tax, interest, and penalty.

Director personal liability: In civil recovery, the corporate veil is pierced where the IC is a façade: directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes, penalties, and third-party claims. A director may be subject to travel restrictions while an offshore integrity investigation is pending, and criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike the IC off, requiring an application for restoration with a penalty. The Samoa International Finance Authority may revoke the IC's tax-exempt licence and report the entity through the exchange of information mechanism to foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the company to be a shell are personally liable for restoration costs, government fees, and any Samoa taxes determined to be overdue; they also face disqualification from acting as directors of a licensed international entity.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with Samoa's Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007, as amended, triggers administrative pecuniary penalties, suspension of the international company registration, and regulatory impact on the licensed international service provider.

Director personal liability: A director who is knowingly involved in money laundering is personally subject to criminal fine and imprisonment; in serious cases, asset-freezing orders and travel bans are imposed, and the director's personal bank accounts may be reached.
Unlicensed Carrying On of Business in Samoa

Statutory sanction: The International Companies Act permits revocation of the IC licence; the company becomes an ordinary resident Samoa company across the board and is assessed on its worldwide income at the standard corporate rate, plus late-payment interest and penalties for unlicensed trade.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unlicensed-business penalty and any unpaid corporate income tax to the extent the company is treated as a bare partnership or the directors acted outside the company's capacity; the directors may also be enjoined from doing business within Samoa until the licence is perfected.
