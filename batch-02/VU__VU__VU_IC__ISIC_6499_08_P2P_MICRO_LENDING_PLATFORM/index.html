Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; administrative portal: Vanuatu Trade Information Portal and Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) e-Services

E-government filing portal

No national income tax TIN is issued to exempt International Companies. For indirect tax purposes, the entity must register for VAT with the Commissioner of VAT by filing the prescribed VAT registration form, annexing the VFSC International Company certificate, evidence of business licensing, beneficial ownership schedule, projected turnover statement, and address details. Non-resident-owned ICs may register exclusively through a licensed Vanuatu registered agent or tax representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — No national income tax TIN is issued to exempt International Companies. For indirect tax purposes, the entity must register for VAT with the Commissioner of VAT by filing the prescribed VAT registration form, annexing the VFSC International Company certificate, evidence of business licensing, beneficial ownership schedule, projected turnover statement, and address details. Non-resident-owned ICs may register exclusively through a licensed Vanuatu registered agent or tax representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available. An International Company may elect to remain fully tax-exempt under the International Companies Act, may elect to be VFSC-licensed as a restricted financial institution for micro-lending, and may voluntarily elect VAT registration if it makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu. No further pass-through or corporate entity-level election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 3,000,000 annual taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period, subject to ministerial gazette adjustment

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimable for: (i) goods physically exported from Vanuatu under a valid customs export declaration; (ii) direct international transport services; and (iii) services supplied to non-residents that are wholly performed outside Vanuatu and not enjoyed in Vanuatu. The P2P platform may zero-rate its offshore administration and lending platform services only if the service is provided to non-resident borrowers and no service element is consumed in Vanuatu.

  • Customs Export Declaration approved by Vanuatu Customs
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rating supporting schedule
  • Inward remittance certificate or foreign exchange receipt showing settlement offshore
  • Copy of VFSC International Company registration and VAT registration certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance, API stamping, or digital stamp mandate currently applies. VAT is self-assessed on standard prescribed invoices. The P2P micro-lending platform must maintain fully electronic transaction records, borrower agreements, invoice trails, and payment reconciliations accessible to DCIR and VFSC for AML and VAT audit purposes.

DCIR may reassess zero-rated supplies if documentary substantiation is incomplete or not produced within the applicable period, claw back any VAT refund received, impose interest from the original due date, and apply penalties of up to 200% of the evaded VAT. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may additionally trigger criminal prosecution, monetary penalties under the VAT Act, suspension or cancellation of the VFSC license, disqualification of directors, and anti-money-laundering enforcement under Vanuatu's Financial Transactions Reporting Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is fully convertible for current account transactions; no exchange control legislation or capital account restrictions are in force. Non-resident shareholders may freely receive dividends, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds through licensed banks. Profit remittance requires only standard commercial bank verification of source of funds and underlying transaction documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Exporters and service providers may retain export proceeds in any permitted foreign currency and may credit them to foreign-currency accounts maintained with licensed domestic banks without mandatory conversion into VUV.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Vanuatu does not operate a local electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime; the VFSC does not issue EMI licences. A Vanuatu International Company may not itself hold client funds or act as an EMI without a banking licence. However, the platform may engage a regulated offshore EMI or payment service provider (e.g., authorised under a reputable EU/UK/APAC regime) as a disbursement conduit, subject to RBV cross-border payment oversight and VFSC anti-money-laundering compliance requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold in Vanuatu (VUV 0): Vanuatu imposes no transfer pricing law, no Master File/Local File obligation and no contemporaneous documentation duty on ICs. Group prudential benchmark: maintain Local/Master File evidence where total intercompany transactions reach VUV 120,000,000 (approximately USD 1,000,000) to support foreign-parent OECD compliance and to rebut evasion allegations.

Economic substance scope: No domestic Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply in Vanuatu. However, statutory substance requirements operate through the IC Act: an IC must maintain a VFSC-licensed resident agent, a registered office in Vanuatu and accessible accounting records at the registered office or as the agent directs. An IC loses its exemption if it carries on business with Vanuatu residents, holds a beneficial interest in Vanuatu land, or does not hold the required license for banking, insurance, deposit-taking or trust business. P2P lending to non-residents through foreign payment rails (ACH/wallet payouts) is consistent with the exemption only if all origination and collection counterparties are non-residents.

AML-CFT Breach by Unlicensed Micro-Lending Platform

Statutory sanction: Bodies corporate: fines up to VUV 100,000,000 under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act (No. 16 of 2022), freezing and confiscation of platform assets, suspension or revocation of the IC registration, and disqualification of the resident agent.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are individually liable to imprisonment of up to 15 years, personal asset confiscation orders, travel bans and exit controls pending forfeiture proceedings, and civil restitution to defrauded lenders and borrowers; courts may pierce the corporate veil where the IC is a mere conduit.
Breach of International Company Exemption (Shell/Prohibited Domestic Activity)

Statutory sanction: Loss of the s.54 tax exemption; late-payment penalties of 10% per month up to the full fee; compulsory strike-off and deregistration; restoration only upon payment of arrears and VFSC restoration fees; contracts concluded after strike-off are unenforceable and income becomes taxable under residual Vanuatu tax laws, including VAT at 12.5% on local fees.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for debts incurred after strike-off or after prohibited domestic activity begins; courts may disregard limited liability under fraudulent trading principles; directors face disqualification orders, fines, and imprisonment up to 12 years for making false declarations to the VFSC.
Cross-Border Tax Evasion and CFC Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: false attestations to the VFSC attract fraud penalties, agent de-licensing, and registry strike-off; information is exchanged under TIEAs for criminal and civil tax matters. Foreign: reassessment of the full platform profit, denial of tax treaty protection, penalties up to 200% of the tax sought, and interest running from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal fraud prosecutions in the residence jurisdiction, worldwide restraining orders and extradition requests; civil discovery pierces nominee layers, and fraudulent trading rulings make each director personally liable for the entire group shortfall, including the lender losses and revenue authorities' costs.
