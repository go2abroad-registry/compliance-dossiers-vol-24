Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an International Company. Shares may be issued in any currency, and no escrow deposit or capital funding is required at formation. The declared issued capital need only be recorded in the register of members; beneficial ownership declarations remain subject to VFSC record-keeping rules.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Vanuatu Fire Services under the Fire Services Act for the server halls and generator rooms; structural load-certification from the Ministry of Public Works confirming raised-floor rack loadings; electrical installation clearance from the Electrician Licensing Board verifying UPS and stand-by generator circuits; and workplace occupational clearance under the Work Health and Safety Act, including rack-mount lift plans, fire-exit signage and confined-space procedures for cooling and cable risers.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department (VCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; the Commissioner of Inland Revenue administers the Value Added Tax Act No. 30 of 1998 (Cap 313) through the VAT Office in Port Vila / Luganville, with electronic filing via the Vanuatu Integrated Tax Administration System (VITAS) portal.

E-government filing portal

The International Company (IC) is exempt from income tax and stamp duty, so no income-tax TIN is issued. Where the IC carries on or intends to carry on a taxable activity in Vanuatu with actual or anticipated annual taxable turnover above VUV 5,000,000 (approximately USD 41,000), it must submit a VAT Registration Application via VITAS (or prescribed paper form) within 21 days of crossing the threshold or within 21 days of commencing activity. The application is supported by the VFSC certificate of incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, registered office address, data-centre lease or ownership evidence, director identity documents, and a statutory declaration of expected turnover. VCIR then issues a Certificate of Registration bearing a unique VAT registration number, which serves as the IC's statutory tax identity for indirect-tax purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — The International Company (IC) is exempt from income tax and stamp duty, so no income-tax TIN is issued. Where the IC carries on or intends to carry on a taxable activity in Vanuatu with actual or anticipated annual taxable turnover above VUV 5,000,000 (approximately USD 41,000), it must submit a VAT Registration Application via VITAS (or prescribed paper form) within 21 days of crossing the threshold or within 21 days of commencing activity. The application is supported by the VFSC certificate of incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, registered office address, data-centre lease or ownership evidence, director identity documents, and a statutory declaration of expected turnover. VCIR then issues a Certificate of Registration bearing a unique VAT registration number, which serves as the IC's statutory tax identity for indirect-tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No corporate income tax or check-the-box / hybrid mismatch election exists because Vanuatu does not levy CIT. Available elections under the VAT Act are: (i) voluntary VAT registration below the VUV 5,000,000 threshold; (ii) application for Commissioner approval to zero-rate qualifying exported services; and (iii) maintaining non-resident status without any Vanuatu physical data-centre presence so that no taxable activity arises. No other statutory tax-classification elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 (approximately USD 41,000) of annual taxable turnover; registration is mandatory within 21 days of crossing this threshold, and there is no territorial carve-out for ICs conducting in-Vanuatu data-centre colocation activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for 0% VAT when physically removed from Vanuatu, and exported services qualify where supplied to non-resident recipients and the Commissioner is satisfied that the services are performed outside Vanuatu or their value is in no way attributable to property situated in Vanuatu. Server rack space physically located in a Vanuatu data centre is presumed connected with local property and therefore standard-rated unless the IC obtains a binding VCIR ruling that the supply is an offshore bare-metal hosting / managed data-processing service performed for non-resident clients, with billing in foreign currency and no local attribution of value.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Vanuatu Customs SAD/ED Form) cleared through VITAS proving the physical export of eligible goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the movement of exported goods outside Vanuatu
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt from a Vanuatu licensed bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds
  • Executed international service agreement with the non-resident recipient and the zero-rated VAT invoice pack
  • Commissioner-approved zero-rating ruling or endorsement letter for offshore data-centre services where applicable

No real-time electronic-invoicing clearance, fiscal data stamping, or API integration mandate is currently enforced by VCIR. Standard-rated and zero-rated supplies are documented by self-issued VAT invoices with sequential numbering and mandatory content (supplier name and VAT ID, recipient details, invoice date, description, taxable value, VAT amount, and total). VAT returns and supporting schedules are filed electronically through the VITAS portal; invoices and underlying records must be retained for at least 7 years.

VCIR audits zero-rating claims by reconciling the export bundle, shipment logs, service contracts, and FX remittance evidence. Unsupported or fraudulent claims are clawed back as assessments of the underpaid 12.5% VAT, with an immediate late penalty of 5%, continued default interest of 1% per month (or SDR-linked statutory interest), an evasion penalty of up to 150% of the VAT liability, and criminal liability including fines and up to two years' imprisonment under the VAT Act. Repeated violations trigger cancellation of VAT registration, exclusion from the zero-rating regime, and suspension of refunds pending full documentary verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) is convertible for all current-account transactions with minimal administrative encumbrance, and the capital account is liberalised for non-resident investors; the International Company (IC) structure carries an express statutory exemption from domestic exchange-control restrictions, authorising the entity to conduct business and hold accounts in any convertible currency (USD, AUD, EUR, GBP, NZD). No RBV approval or exchange-control permit is required to invoice data-center colocation fees in USD, to hold USD treasury balances, or to remit lease income, affiliate fees, dividends and loan repayments abroad. The operative constraints are commercial rather than legal: VUV is a thin, basket-referenced currency quoted against AUD, USD, NZD, EUR and JPY, and global correspondent banks apply enhanced de-risking filters to Vanuatu-domiciled tax-exempt entities, so the treasury blueprint must keep USD as the dominant operating, invoicing and debt-service currency while channelling VUV only for local payroll, utilities and statutory obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no export-proceeds surrender or conversion regime: neither the Exchange Control Act, RBV delegated directions, nor dealer practice prescribes a statutory timeline for converting export or lease receipts into VUV nor any mandatory minimum conversion ratio. Lease and colocation service receipts may therefore be retained 100% in the original billing currency (USD) in a designated foreign-currency account. Authorised dealers complete routine balance-of-payments reporting to the RBV and file currency-transaction reports under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act (as amended) when statutory monetary thresholds are triggered; the reporting duty is statistical and suspicious-transaction-oriented, requiring no treasury approval action.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act 1980 (Cap. 125); statutory holder of the exchange-control mandate under the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 85), supervising authorised dealer banks, the VUV interbank market and the national RTGS payment system (Vanuatu National Payment System). Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) registers and supervises International Companies but exercises no FX licensing power.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A — Vanuatu has no transfer pricing legislation; no local file/master file monetization thresholds are prescribed.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu's Economic Substance Regulations (Cap 247) apply to tax-resident companies engaged in specified relevant activities. Data center colocation is relevant only if structured as a 'distribution and service centre' providing services to foreign group members. Third-party colocation leasing is generally outside ESR unless the company is a tax resident and the activity is deemed a service business. In-scope entities must maintain local management, physical premises, and adequate employees in Vanuatu.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Abroad / Misleading Foreign Tax Authority

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu itself imposes no income tax, so domestic tax evasion penalties do not arise; however, such conduct constitutes fraud under Vanuatu Penal Code, exposing the company to VFSC deregistration, and may activate foreign revenue penalties and exchange-of-information requests.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil authorization for foreign tax assessments, asset forfeiture, travel bans under mutual assistance channels, and imprisonment in the prosecuting foreign jurisdiction for criminal tax evasion.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: If the company is within the Economic Substance Regulations, failure to satisfy the substance test results in financial penalties, a possible direction to provide information, and ultimately strike-off. Outside ESR, persistent non-compliance with registered agent and annual renewal obligations leads to automatic removal from the VFSC register.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally disqualified from acting in Vanuatu, held jointly liable for VFSC fees and penalties, and subject to imprisonment for knowingly providing false declarations to the VFSC.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Financing of Terrorism

Statutory sanction: Under Vanuatu's AML/CFT Act, financial institutions and DNFBPs face criminal fines, forfeiture of assets, and revocation of any registration or license; the VFSC may also suspend corporate registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for criminal penalties, including imprisonment and civil monetary penalties, and may be subject to travel bans restricting ability to leave Vanuatu pending investigation.
Unauthorized Financial Services / Licensing Breach

Statutory sanction: Conducting financial services without a licence triggers cease-and-desist orders, administrative penalties, and a court-ordered winding-up if the business is deemed harmful to Vanuatu's regulatory regime.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability including substantial fines and imprisonment, as well as permanent disqualification from managing any company in Vanuatu; personal bank accounts may be subject to freezing orders.
