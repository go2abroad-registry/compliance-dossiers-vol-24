Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue, Government of Samoa – Taxpayer Services Division; online portal: RevEnue Online (https://www.revenue.gov.ws).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC): (1) submit Form TIN-01 'Application for Tax Identification Number' to the Ministry of Revenue along with certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation issued under the International Companies Act 1987, memorandum and articles of association, registered office certificate (by licensed registered agent), and a copy of the ultimate beneficial owner's passport; (2) the Ministry reviews and issues the TIN within 7–10 working days; (3) if the IC will engage in VAGST-registrable supplies (≥ WST 60,000 annual turnover), additionally file Form VAGST-01 for VAT registration and obtain a VAGST registration number; (4) for import/export activities, obtain customs client code from the Samoa Customs Service (Ministry of Revenue) using the same TIN. Foreign entities may appoint a local registered agent to file in person; no minimum capital or local shareholding is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — For a foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC): (1) submit Form TIN-01 'Application for Tax Identification Number' to the Ministry of Revenue along with certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation issued under the International Companies Act 1987, memorandum and articles of association, registered office certificate (by licensed registered agent), and a copy of the ultimate beneficial owner's passport; (2) the Ministry reviews and issues the TIN within 7–10 working days; (3) if the IC will engage in VAGST-registrable supplies (≥ WST 60,000 annual turnover), additionally file Form VAGST-01 for VAT registration and obtain a VAGST registration number; (4) for import/export activities, obtain customs client code from the Samoa Customs Service (Ministry of Revenue) using the same TIN. Foreign entities may appoint a local registered agent to file in person; no minimum capital or local shareholding is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not provide a check-the-box regime. However, an International Company may irrevocably elect under section 122 of the International Companies Act 1987 to be subject to Samoa income tax as if it were a local ordinary company (thereby losing statutory tax exemption). A VAGST registration election is available for voluntary registration even if turnover is below the WST 60,000 threshold. No hybrid mismatch elections are separately recognized for ICs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 annual taxable turnover of goods and services supplied in Samoa (for non-resident suppliers, nexus also arises if the supply is made to a Samoa resident and the recipient is not a VAGST-registered person; mandatory registration applies once threshold is met).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including surgical suture needles and assemblies) from Samoa to a destination outside Samoa are zero-rated under section 23 of the VAGST Act 2008 when the supplier retains documentation proving the goods are exported within 30 days of supply. Services that are 'exported' – supplied to a non-resident and not used in Samoa – are also zero-rated. The manufacturing operations of an International Company in Samoa, even if tax-exempt, must still charge VAGST at 0% on qualifying export sales, and may claim input VAGST credits/refunds for VAGST-bearing inputs used to produce the exported goods, subject to the standard refund process in the VAGST Administration Act 2012.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form CUS-EXP, lodged via ASYCUDA World)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill for air freight including all inland haulage)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from the Samoa commercial bank evidencing payment of the export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Certificate of Origin (if claiming tariff preference, but not mandatory for VAGST zero-rating)

Samoa does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, government-API stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing system. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue standard tax invoices containing the supplier's name/address/VAGST number, recipient details, date, description, consideration, tax amount, and total. Digital invoices via approved ERP are acceptable, but no government digital stamp is required. The Samoa Customs Service uses the ASYCUDA World for customs declarations, but this is not linked to VAGST real-time clearance.

Samoa Revenue enforces export zero-rating strictly. If the exporter fails to produce the export documentation within 60 days of the Minister's written demand, or if proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days from the date of export without a valid extension from the Central Bank, the zero-rating is reversed, and the VAGST becomes payable at 15% with interest. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract an additional shortfall penalty of 50% of the tax evaded, or up to 100% for deliberate concealment, plus a fine of up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 years under the Tax Administration Act 2012 (as amended). Retrospective clawback extends to all export shipments in the same audit period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is not a freely tradable currency. Samoa accepts the IMF Article VIII obligations for current account convertibility, but capital account transactions are subject to Central Bank of Samoa approval. A Samoa International Company (IC), if not carrying on business within Samoa, is generally treated as a non-resident for exchange control purposes and may maintain foreign-currency accounts with licensed authorized dealers. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders are permitted once audited accounts, tax status certificates, and Central Bank notifications are filed; however, for an IC operating purely outside Samoa, the statutory tax-exempt status means no in-country profit recognition and no local remittance hurdle. Business activity within Samoa, including the establishment of a physical manufacturing plant, would trigger resident status, capital importation requirements, and repatriation restriction constraints under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and associated Foreign Exchange Control Regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa's exchange control framework requires exporters to route export receipts through a Central Bank licensed authorized dealer in Samoa. Export proceeds must be converted to WST or credited to a Samoan foreign-currency account within a statutory period of 90 days from the date of shipment. There is a mandatory retention rule allowing up to 100% of export earnings to be retained in a foreign-currency account solely for approved trade and debt-service purposes, but any conversion to WST, or payment to a third party abroad, requires submission of the export invoice, customs export declaration, and clearing certificate to the remitting bank. An IC undertaking offshore manufacturing cannot rely on the local surrender regime unless the goods are physically exported from Samoa; in that case the exporter, including the IC, must comply with CBS reporting and conversion/retention thresholds or apply for a specific exemption on a shipment-by-shipment basis.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. The Central Bank of Samoa does not license offshore digital banks or electronic money institutions to take deposits or provide payment services in Samoa. Non-bank remittance providers fall under the Money Remittance Act 2017 and must be licensed by CBS, with a local physical office, directors, and onward bank settlement arrangements. A cross-border fintech EMI incorporated outside Samoa cannot directly hold a settlement account with a Samoan authorized dealer unless separately licensed and assessed; the practical limitation is that the IC will need a direct banking relationship with a Samoan bank or a recognized international bank with an Apia branch. Foreign EMIs are therefore unsuitable as the primary treasury vehicle for this IC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital injection into the Samoa IC is executed as a SWIFT MT103/TT wire from the ultimate parent's bank to the IC's foreign-currency account at a licensed Samoan authorized dealer. The wire must include a structured purpose code reflecting direct investment equity and the message must state 'Injection of paid-up capital into a Samoa International Company pursuant to the International Companies Act 2012'. Samoan banks require a cover letter from the IC's local registered agent referencing the entity ID WS_IC, a copy of the board resolution authorizing the allotment, and confirmation that the inward funds are external laundered funds, not derived from Samoan residents. The bank will credit the amount to the IC's capital escrow account and issue a foreign exchange transaction confirmation. No WST conversion is required if the account is maintained solely in foreign currency; however, if conversion is needed, the CBS gets transaction reporting automatically through the bank's daily exchange-control returns.Trade settlements for surgical suture component purchases are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the IC's USD/AUD/EUR account. For larger or recurring transactions, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit at sight is opened in favor of the supplier through the Samoan bank's correspondent banking network, usually with confirmation from a major international bank (e.g. ANZ, HSBC, DBS) to reduce country risk. Each outbound transfer is supported by the underlying import contract, invoice, transport document, and when required, a Customs export/import declaration. Payments to related parties require a copy of the group intercompany service agreement and a transfer pricing memorandum confirming arm's length terms. The Samoan bank will deduct no exchange-control levy if the outward payment is made from the IC's foreign-currency account; otherwise, WST conversion is processed at the bank's wholesale rate with CBS reporting.Onshore deliverable forward exchange contracts in USD/WST, AUD/WST, NZD/WST with a Samoan licensed authorized dealer, for firm commercial commitments up to a tenor approved by Central Bank of Samoa (typically under 12 months).Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on WST/USD or WST/AUD for hedging trade exposures, arranged by the parent treasury or the IC with a credit-rated non-resident bank, subject to CBS hedging policy requiring documentary evidence of the underlying exposure.Cross-currency swaps (XCCY) between two fully convertible foreign currencies (e.g. USD/AUD or USD/NZD) used to match the IC's borrowing or intercompany funding currency with supplier payment currency, provided no WST leg is entered into without prior CBS approval.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% standard corporate income tax rate for resident companies under the Income Tax Act 2015 (as amended); effective 0% for a qualifying Samoa International Company engaged exclusively in offshore international business within the tax-exempt International Company regime.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends paid by a resident Samoa company to non-resident shareholders; dividends paid by a qualifying tax-exempt International Company are outside the Samoan withholding regime and may be distributed free of dividend withholding tax and income tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return: within three months after the end of the company's accounting period; a calendar-year company must lodge by 31 March of the following year. Any final balance of tax is due on the same date. Companies are also obliged to pay provisional corporate tax in quarterly instalments due 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March when the Commissioner issues a provisional assessment.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1-3 (Identifier and period) Legal name, Samoa TIN, corporate registration number and accounting period start and end date. Complete using the International Company name as registered with the Samoa International Companies Registry. Non-resident ownership is permitted; the Samoan licensed registered agent must be identified in the Agent Particulars schedule. Late lodgement incurs a monetary penalty of up to 50 penalty units (each WST 100) plus a daily default penalty of one penalty unit for every day until filed; late-payment interest accrues at 10% per annum compounded; shortfall penalties range from 20% for unintentional understatement to 100% for intentional evasion; wilful non-filing is prosecutable, with fines up to 150 penalty units and imprisonment up to five years.
