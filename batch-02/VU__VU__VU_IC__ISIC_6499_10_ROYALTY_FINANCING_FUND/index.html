Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRCS), Department of Customs and Inland Revenue, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official national e-service portal administered at the VRCS central level.

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) is statutorily outside the domestic income tax and VAT net. No TIN is required solely for incorporation or offshore activity. If the IC makes taxable domestic supplies in Vanuatu (which is generally outside the permitted IC scope) or applies for voluntary VAT registration, it must submit the VAT registration application to VRCS with the certificate of incorporation, registered office details, corporate records, director and nominee shareholder particulars, and a statement of expected taxable turnover. VRCS will issue a VAT registration number/TIN effective from the date of taxable activity. For fully offshore non-resident ICs, no TIN procurement step is mandated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax. The IC's exempt status is automatic if no business is conducted within Vanuatu. A voluntary conversion from IC status to a domestic company under the Companies Act is the only statutory election that would subject the entity to local VAT and business licensing, but it is not a tax classification check-the-box mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 2,000,000 in taxable supplies during any consecutive 12-month period; compulsory registration applies once the threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration is available for lower-turnover businesses. ICs supplying services entirely to non-residents outside Vanuatu have no local VAT nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated supplies under the Vanuatu VAT Act include: (i) goods physically exported from Vanuatu under customs control; (ii) services directly connected with exported goods or foreign land; and (iii) services supplied to a non-resident where the benefit occurs outside Vanuatu. An IC providing revenue-based financing or royalty-financing services to non-resident digital brands is, if registered, outside the domestic supply net or eligible for zero-rating when the recipient is foreign and the funds or economic use occur offshore.

  • Customs Export Declaration in the approved VRCS format
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial Invoice and executed Royalty/Revenue-Financing Contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing settlement in foreign currency from the non-resident counterparty

Vanuatu does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, digital stamping, or transaction-level API reporting. Registered suppliers must issue serially numbered tax invoices showing the supplier's VAT registration number, date, description of supply, consideration, VAT amount, and must retain records for audit by VRCS on request.

There is no statutory FX repatriation deadline for a Vanuatu IC, as ICs are exempt from exchange control and may retain offshore export proceeds. However, VRCS retains audit rights to verify that exported goods or offshore services genuinely left Vanuatu or were supplied to non-residents. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims trigger a VAT reassessment at 12.5% with statutory interest, monetary penalties for false or misleading representations, criminal fines, and possible imprisonment under the Vanuatu VAT Act and Penal Code. Refunds previously paid are recoverable as tax debts with enforcement through the courts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is fully convertible for current account transactions. International Companies (ICs) owned by non-residents enjoy statutory exemption from exchange control restrictions on external borrowing, dividend and royalty remittances, and capital account transfers. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders attracts no withholding tax and is freely processed by authorized dealers upon submission of audited financials, board resolutions, and standard KYC documentation. Resident entities remain subject to RBV approval for selected capital account operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under RBV Exchange Control Notices, physical merchandise exporters must repatriate all export proceeds into a Vanuatu authorized dealer within six months of shipment date. Foreign currency receipts must be sold to an authorized dealer or credited to a foreign currency account; no fixed statutory conversion ratio applies, and conversion into VUV for local settlement occurs at prevailing interbank rates. This regime does not apply to non-exporting International Companies nor to cross-border royalty financing transactions, which are treated as invisible current payments.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) as exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 65]; Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) supervises non-bank financial services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Vanuatu does not maintain a dedicated Electronic Money Institution licensing regime. Offshore digital EMIs can be used for peripheral invoice collection and disbursement in USD/EUR/GBP, but they cannot serve as the primary treasury or escrow rail because RBV exchange control policy requires foreign currency receipts and payments to be routed through a licensed authorized dealer in Vanuatu. EMIs may operate only where the funds remain offshore and no Vanuatu-clearing connection is established.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — Vanuatu has no codified transfer pricing documentation rules, no Local File or Master File thresholds, and no thin capitalization limitation for tax purposes. An IC engaged in royalty financing is nonetheless subject to VFSC prudential oversight and must maintain contractual records supporting the arm's length nature of cross-border transactions.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations Act No. 37 of 2019 applies to ICs undertaking 'relevant activities', including financing and leasing, intellectual property holding, and fund management. A royalty financing fund must demonstrate in Vanuatu: (i) board meetings and strategic management held in-country, (ii) adequate physical office and qualified staff, and (iii) core income-generating activities performed in Vanuatu. Failure attract fines and deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Offshore Exemption Abuse)

Statutory sanction: Domestic: loss of the IC tax-exempt status, retroactive assessment at the corporate income tax headline rate applicable to non-exempt entities, derecognition of the company under s. 13 of the ICA, and deregistration by the VFSC. Foreign: countervailing tax reassessment, CFC attribution, and penalty regimes of the investor's home jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may be held personally liable for restitution of avoided taxes, subject to director disqualification orders, cross-border tax information exchange under the VFSC's exchange mandates, and travel-bank/criminal extradition risk where intentional evasion is shown.
Shell Company Abuse / Defective Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Civil and administrative penalty under the Economic Substance Regulations Act of up to VUV 5,000,000 for the first year of default, additional periodic penalties of up to VUV 1,000,000 per month for continuing default, and mandatory deregistration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or tolerate the substance gap are jointly and severally liable for the statutory penalties; the court may order sequestration of personal assets, director disqualification for a fixed term, and immigration restrictions preventing re-entry to Vanuatu.
AML/CFT Breach and Financial Intelligence Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act: fine of up to VUV 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 15 years for failure to report, tipping-off, or facilitating money laundering; in addition the VFSC can revoke the IC's registration and the registered agent's licence.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and controlling shareholders face criminal imprisonment, personal fines, confiscation of proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act, and travel bans imposed by the Vanuatu courts pending investigation; private civil suits by defrauded lenders may pierce the corporate veil on 'sham' grounds.
Unlicensed Financial Services / Deposit-Taking Outside IC Scope

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties against the IC including fine of up to VUV 10,000,000; forced winding-up; repayment of all unauthorised deposits; permanent loss of the IC exemption for breach of the domestic business prohibition in the International Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, imprisonment, and civil liability for all investor losses; the VFSC may issue a public warning, seek a freezing order over personal assets, and impose a travel ban while civil/substantive proceedings are pending.
