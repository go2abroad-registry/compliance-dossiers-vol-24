Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Authority (VRCA), established under the Revenue Administration Act No. 22 of 2017; national taxpayer portal: VRCA TAPS (Taxpayer Accounting and Processing System) at https://www.vanuatuvra.gov.vu/ operating a single national Taxpayer Identification Number regime.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that will make taxable supplies in Vanuatu must apply for a single Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration by lodging the prescribed application form with the VRCA Commissioner, together with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation or international company registration, memorandum and articles, directors' identification (passport and proof of address), registered-office or licensed-agent confirmation, and a written undertaking to maintain records. The application must be filed within 14 days after the entity becomes liable to register for VAT; the VRCA issues the TIN and the VAT registration certificate in accordance with its published service standards. An International Company carrying on no business in Vanuatu is exempt and is not required to obtain a TIN; however, an IC may voluntarily apply for VAT registration under section 9(3) of the VAT Act No. 12 of 1998 to issue zero-rated export invoices or recover input tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — non-resident International Company (International Companies Act, Cap 176) conducting wholly offshore business. Vanuatu imposes no income tax, corporate profits tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax, or withholding tax on any taxpayer, so the IC classification is exemption-driven and territorial in effect; the IC remains outside the domestic VAT net unless it elects to carry on a taxable activity within Vanuatu. — A foreign entity that will make taxable supplies in Vanuatu must apply for a single Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration by lodging the prescribed application form with the VRCA Commissioner, together with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation or international company registration, memorandum and articles, directors' identification (passport and proof of address), registered-office or licensed-agent confirmation, and a written undertaking to maintain records. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — non-resident International Company (International Companies Act, Cap 176) conducting wholly offshore business. Vanuatu imposes no income tax, corporate profits tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax, or withholding tax on any taxpayer, so the IC classification is exemption-driven and territorial in effect; the IC remains outside the domestic VAT net unless it elects to carry on a taxable activity within Vanuatu.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections exist under Vanuatu law because no income tax statute is in force. Available statutory elections: (1) an IC may apply to the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission Registrar to convert to or from domestic-company status under the International Companies Act; (2) an IC may elect voluntary VAT registration to recover input tax on acquisitions or to render VAT-compliant zero-rated export invoices; (3) an IC may elect to maintain its accounting records offshore without local audit, subject to the VRCA's statutory powers of access and record production.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 10,000,000 of annual taxable turnover in any 12-month period (VUV 2,000,000 for a business whose supplies are principally manufacturing); voluntary registration permitted below threshold; exports of goods and services are zero-rated, and an offshore International Company without local taxable supplies has no registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies under section 13(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the goods are exported out of Vanuatu and the supplier retains the customs export entry. Services: 0% applies under section 13(1)(b) where the services are supplied to a person outside Vanuatu and the benefit and use of the services occurs outside Vanuatu; offshore electronic medical record (EMR) software-as-a-service supplied by the IC to non-resident medical clinics qualifies as an exported service. Vanuatu has no exchange-control legislation; the 180-day inward-remittance window is applied as the compliance benchmark for evidenced foreign exchange repatriation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document, Form C1) certified by Vanuatu Customs at the port of exportation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods or, for pure e-services, a geo-IP server log and online service-delivery record of the subscription provided to the offshore clinic
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Vanuatu bank or, for e-services, SWIFT or Stripe settlement evidence identifying the non-resident payer and matching the invoice amount within the 180-day repatriation window
  • Final 0% VAT invoice cross-referenced to the export contract, the customs entry, and the payment receipt, retained for the statutory six-year record-keeping period

VRCA Electronic Time-Stamping System (ETSS): registered traders must use VRCA-certified invoicing or point-of-sale software that transmits each fiscal invoice to the VRCA in real time through an API interface, receives a digitally authenticated fiscal stamping token, and renders a coded invoice (QR or tamper-evident fiscal seal) before the supply is complete for VAT purposes. Monthly VAT returns (Form VAT-11) are lodged electronically through the TAPS portal, and payment is due no later than the 20th day of the month following the relevant taxable period; the ETSS mandate applies without turnover exemption to tourist-facing and other high-risk traders.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the IC and its officers to retrospective VAT assessment at 13% of the value of the supply, a late-payment penalty of 25% of the tax evaded, and monthly arrears interest at 2%; knowingly false declarations and forged supporting documentation constitute criminal offenses under the VAT Act and the Revenue Administration Act No. 22 of 2017, punishable by monetary fines and terms of imprisonment; customs export fraud additionally triggers seizure and forfeiture of the goods, cancellation of the supplier's VAT registration, and clawback of any refunds historically paid for the prior six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a fully convertible currency regime under IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no exchange control approvals required for capital account transactions, profit remittances, or trade-related settlements in VUV or foreign currencies. The Vatu is freely transferable within the Pacific regional settlement systems, and non-resident International Companies may maintain foreign currency accounts without permission. Remittance of dividends or repatriation of capital attributable to the EMR SaaS business is unrestricted, subject only to standard anti-money laundering and know-your-customer due diligence by the remitting bank. No statutory restriction applies to current account convertibility; all cross-border payments may be executed by commercial banks under the prevailing RBV and Financial Transactions Reporting Act compliance framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists under the Laws of Vanuatu for surrender of export proceeds. Export receipts derived from the licensed medical clinic EMR SaaS platform may be retained in foreign currency or converted to VUV at the discretion of the account holder. The RBV does not impose any mandatory forex repatriation, deposit, or conversion regime; the only applicable obligations are the anti-money laundering record-keeping requirements under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act, which require the underlying cross-border transaction documentation, including purpose codes and client identification, to be preserved for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low to Medium. Vanuatu does not have a dedicated Electronic Money Institution licensing category under the Financial Dealers Act or the RBV Act. Foreign fintech EMIs and offshore digital payment providers are not permitted to directly hold client electronic money or conduct payment services without entering into a formal banking partnership or acquiring a local dealer/exchange licence under the Financial Dealers (Licensing and Control) Act. The most viable compliant structure is a sponsored account arrangement with a tier-1 domestic commercial bank acting as the settlement and treasury gateway, while the EMI operates solely as a non-settlement software front-end. However, the RBV has issued cautionary guidance on unlicensed cross-border EMIs, making their use for the EMR SaaS's recurring medical clinic billing and treasury flows legally inadvisable unless a local custodial banking arrangement is approved.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol for the initial capital contribution is a SWIFT MT103 corporate credit transfer from the parent company or investor's bank to the Vanuatu International Company's designated operational or escrow account. The remittance must include the purpose code 'Capital injection to an International Company' (or the receiving bank's equivalent code) and a clear remittance narrative referencing the corporate file number and the EMR SaaS commercial contract. The prescribed reference is 'VU_IC / ISIC 6201 / EMR SAAS'. The bank may request a copy of the certificate of incorporation, the Registered Agent's certificate, and a shareholder resolution specifying the subscription amount. No Foreign Investment Act approval is required because an International Company is offshore-owned; the injection may be received in VUV, USD, EUR, or AUD. The funding bank must complete a confirmation of the source of funds form and, where the amount exceeds the threshold, an unusual-transaction report to the Financial Intelligence Unit must be prepared. The funds are immediately available for operational disbursement upon receipt and completion of post-arrival AML screening.Export disbursements from the treasury blueprint will execute across three rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for low-value recurring operational payments such as cloud hosting, staff payroll, software license fees, and telemedicine infrastructure vendor payments; (2) commercial documentary Letters of Credit (UCP600 compliant) issued by the VU bank on the account-to-account relationship for high-value medical hardware and software development procurement; and (3) USD-denominated automated clearing house facilities where the settlement bank maintains direct participation in USD clearing via correspondent banks in New York and Australia. For each disbursement the supporting dossier must contain the sales contract, purpose code reflecting 'software export' or 'license remittance', a copy of the healthcare customer's licence schedule, and the export invoice linked to a clinical workflow deployment. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax on International Companies (ICs); the Income Tax Act [Cap 174] prescribes no effective charging rate, and ICs are expressly exempted from all taxation under Section 2(1) of the International Companies Act [Cap 222].

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividend distributions by an International Company to its foreign shareholders, and no advance tax or distribution tax is levied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not Applicable — Vanuatu has no corporate income tax and therefore maintains no transfer pricing statute, no Local File or Master File filing obligation, no contemporaneous documentation threshold, and no OECD TP penalty regime. For a foreign-parent EMR SaaS group, OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation may nonetheless be required in the counterparty's fiscal jurisdiction where the IC is a related-party contractor or licensee.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not apply Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). No ESR filing, no annual substance declaration, and no economic-substance economic test is imposed on International Companies. The IC's legal nexus with Vanuatu is satisfied solely through the licensed registered agent and the registered office; there is no requirement for local board meetings, local payroll, or physical presence. However, the foreign beneficial owner's residence jurisdiction may apply its own CFC, LOB, or substance rules to disregard the Vanuatu IC.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Prohibited Domestic Business / Loss of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Contravention of the activity restrictions under Part 2 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222] renders the offending transactions voidable, subjects the IC to a fine imposed by VFSC, and strips the IC of its tax-exempt status, triggering retrospective exposure to the 12.5% domestic Value Added Tax and any future income tax charge; VFSC may additionally refuse annual return acceptance and deregister the company.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise or fail to prevent domestic business may be personally liable for civil restitution of the IC's losses, may be disqualified by VFSC from managing Vanuatu entities, and may face criminal fines under the International Companies Act.
Foreign Tax Evasion / Abusive Zero-Tax Shell

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu itself imposes no domestic tax-evasion penalty on an IC, but it will exchange information under its network of Tax Information Exchange Agreements (TIEAs) and OECD Global Forum commitments; the foreign residence jurisdiction may impose civil fraud penalties up to 200% of the unpaid tax, foreign CFC rules may attribute the IC's income to the parent, and the entire structure may be disregarded for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors and the ultimate beneficial owner face personal foreign tax assessments, asset-tracing orders, and restitution claims; courts will pierce the corporate veil where the IC is used as a mere facade, and a director could face extradition and imprisonment for tax fraud in the jurisdictions where the unreported revenue arises.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction for money laundering carries a fine up to VT 10,000,000 (circa USD 87,000) and/or imprisonment up to 15 years under the Vanuatu AML/CFT Act and the Proceeds of Crime Act; the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) may impose administrative penalties and freeze accounts, and VFSC may revoke the registered agent's licence.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the registered agent's AML officer are personally prosecutable for failure to report suspicious transactions; convictions trigger imprisonment, travel bans, and forfeiture of personal and corporate assets, and foreign counterparties may rely on veil-piercing to recover civil losses.
Misrepresentation to VFSC / Registered Agent

Statutory sanction: False declarations to VFSC constitute an offence under the International Companies Act and may attract fines up to VT 5,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 years; VFSC may strike the IC from the register without further notice, rendering the company incapable of suing or enforcing contracts.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the IC is found to be a sham; directors are personally liable for all debts and losses incurred by defrauded counterparties, face disqualification, travel bans, and criminal conviction, and may be compelled to make restitution of all improperly distributed dividends.
