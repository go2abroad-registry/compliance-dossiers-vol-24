Samoa International Companies ('ICs') incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 are exempt from Samoa income tax and do not receive an income tax TIN. Fraudulent or false export claims carry an additional 50% penalty, a fine up to WST 100,000, and imprisonment for up to five years. SRA may reopen assessments for up to five years from the filing date, or indefinitely in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoa tala (WST) is not a fully convertible international currency. Current account transactions generally receive unrestricted conversion through authorized dealers, but capital account transactions are subject to CBS exchange control approval or exemption. A Samoan International Company (IC) classified as a non-resident offshore entity may hold and operate foreign currency accounts in USD, AUD, NZD, EUR, or JPY at licensed commercial banks without mandatory conversion into WST. Dividend distributions and repatriated capital to non-resident shareholders are structurally smoother when the IC maintains its full treasury in USD or AUD because no WST conversion is required at the operating-bank level; profit remittance hurdles arise only if the IC converts offshore receipts into WST for local Apia operating costs. CBS approval is advisable before any large shareholder loan repayment, capital reduction, or liquidation distribution, and the underlying transaction evidence must demonstrate that the flow is a genuine trade or capital transaction rather than a disguised domestic financing arrangement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident Samoa exporters must bring export receipts through an authorized dealer and surrender for conversion into WST within 30 days of receipt unless a CBS exchange control exemption or foreign-currency retention account has been approved. For the WS_IC International Company structure, the mandatory surrender ratio is effectively zero where the entity is designated as non-resident and holds a CBS-approved foreign-currency account; all proceeds from export sales of vapor-deposited amorphous silicon cesium iodide DR detectors may be retained in USD or AUD. The IC should nevertheless maintain a documentary trail of export invoices, airway bills or bills of lading, customs declarations, and proof of payment to satisfy the six-month export proceeds realization rule, which requires that Samoan exports be paid for within 180 days from shipment unless an extended term has been approved by CBS.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) as the exchange control authority acting under the Exchange Control Act 1975, the Exchange Control Regulations 1976, and the Financial Institutions Act 2007

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium. Samoa has no dedicated electronic-money institution or digital-bank licensing category; an entity seeking to issue e-money or provide payment services must obtain a restricted or full licence from the Central Bank of Samoa under the Financial Institutions Act 2007, which effectively requires a physical Samoa presence and a substantial AML/CFT compliance function. Offshore global EMI providers such as Wise or Airwallex generally do not offer onboarded USD or WST accounts to Samoa International Companies due to de-risking by correspondent banks and limited Pacific routing capabilities. The only feasible structure is a local bank-backed white-label arrangement where a licensed Samoan commercial bank holds the underlying funds and the EMI merely provides a restricted cross-border interface. Legal risks include unlicensed money transmission, exchange control infringement, and repudiation by the AML/CFT supervisor; therefore, the IC should treat foreign digital EMIs as auxiliary payment rails only after obtaining a written legal opinion from a Samoa counsel confirming that the EMI does not engage in deposit taking or money transmission in Samoa.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The preferred compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 from the parent or subscribing shareholder's bank to the Samoa IC's foreign-currency capital escrow account held at ANZ Bank Samoa Limited or Bank of South Pacific (Samoa) Limited. The wire instruction must identify the beneficiary as the International Company with its full name and Samoa company registration number; the remittance information field should contain a precise purpose declaration such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION FOR SHARE SUBSCRIPTION — WS_IC ISIC 3250' or 'INVESTMENT IN EQUITY OF SAMOA INTERNATIONAL COMPANY' to satisfy CBS exchange-control reporting and the receiving bank's CDD. Funds should be transmitted in USD or AUD and held in the IC's offshore-class account without conversion into WST. The receiving bank should be supplied in advance with the certified board resolution, deposit ledger, and a funds-source questionnaire; upon receipt, a foreign-currency credit report is filed with the Central Bank of Samoa in the aggregate periodic returns submitted by the authorized dealer.For payment of component suppliers, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfer at sight, especially for high-turnover procurements of amorphous silicon substrates, cesium iodide scintillation layers, thin-film transistor panels, and detector read-out electronics. Each outbound instruction should include the underlying invoice number, purchase order, commodity description, HS tariff code, and purpose code for 'Import Payment for Medical Equipment Components'. For larger or repetitive transactions, the Samoa IC may open an irrevocable documentary letter of credit through ANZ or BSP, issued under SWIFT MT700, payable at sight against presenting bank's draft, commercial invoice, clean on board bill of lading, and inspection certificate. Confirming bank capacity in Singapore or Hong Kong is recommended to reduce documentary risk. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory transfer pricing documentation is imposed on tax-exempt International Companies. If an IC carries on a taxable onshore business in Samoa (a violation of IC status), the Income Tax Act 1974 applies the arm's length principle and TP documentation obligations are expected to follow OECD TP Guidelines; a Local File / Master File is triggered when annual turnover exceeds WST 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not maintain BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, an International Company must maintain a registered office in Samoa, appoint at least one licensed registered agent, keep adequate financial records at the registered office, and avoid deriving income from any Samoan-source activity. The board must hold meetings in person or by proxy at intervals set in the bye-laws, and major related-party transactions require written board approval.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 1974 and the International Companies Act 1987, the Commissioner may re-characterise the IC as a resident taxable company, impose corporate income tax at 25% on unreported Samoan-source profits, add monthly interest at 1%, and levy penalties up to 150% of the tax avoided. The company may be struck off and its assets frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties; the court may order civil recovery of diverted profits, impose travel bans, and impose imprisonment of up to 7 years for wilful tax evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike off and dissolve the company; the FIU may impose administrative fines of up to WST 100,000; assets may be restrained under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2004; the separate legal personality of the shell company may be disregarded.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold nominee directors personally liable for all company obligations; directors may be disqualified for up to 10 years, subjected to personal bankruptcy proceedings, and charged with criminal offences that may trigger extradition.
AML / CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2021, failure to comply attracts a fine of WST 500,000 for a corporate body and WST 100,000 for an individual; the company's registration or operating licence may be revoked; Samoa may be subjected to international countermeasures and blacklisting.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal liability of up to 5 years' imprisonment, personal financial penalties, disqualification, freezing of bank accounts by the Central Bank of Samoa, confiscation of proceeds, and travel bans pending investigation.
