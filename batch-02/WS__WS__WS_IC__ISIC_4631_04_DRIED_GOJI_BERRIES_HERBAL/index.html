Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA) – Government of Samoa; official e-services portal: sra.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) must obtain a Samoa Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by submitting an application to the Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA) via the online registration facility or in person at the SRA Revenue Centre. The application must include the certificate of incorporation, the International Company's registered agent details, certified copy of the charter or articles of association, director and shareholder particulars, and the anticipated date of first taxable activity. Where the IC does not derive income sourced in Samoa and engages exclusively in offshore export trade, the TIN is issued for customs and VAGST compliance purposes only; the International Companies Act 2009 provides no requirement to obtain a domestic business license. Registration for VAGST is mandatory if taxable supplies exceed SAT 100,000 in any 12-month period; however, a tax-exempt IC whose supplies are exclusively exported is still required to hold a TIN to effect customs procedures and to claim zero-rating.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists under Samoa tax law. An International Company is statutorily classified as a tax-exempt offshore entity under the International Companies Act 2009 and the Income Tax Act 2020, and is not subject to Samoa income tax on foreign-source income or on transactions conducted through a licensed IC. The IC may elect to register as a domestic company by surrendering its International Company status, which triggers ordinary Samoa entity-level taxation. Hybrid mismatch elections under the Income Tax Act 2020 are only relevant to domestic corporate groups and are not applicable to International Companies outside the domestic tax net.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 100,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; non-resident persons carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa are also liable to register if threshold is met

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods qualifies for zero-rating under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2009 where the supplier has sold the goods in the course of a taxable activity and the goods are either (i) exported by the supplier to a destination outside Samoa, or (ii) transported to an overseas principal, or (iii) removed from Samoa under customs control as provided under section 30A. For the International Company exporting dried goji berries and herbal botanicals, the following conditions must be met: a written export sales contract with a non-resident buyer, payment in a foreign currency from outside Samoa, physical departure of the goods from Samoa within 21 days after the invoice date or within a longer period approved by customs, and strict compliance with Samoa phytosanitary export licensing for plant-based commodities. Offshore services ancillary to the export (such as packaging and shipping) are zero-rated only if supplied directly to the overseas buyer of the goods; any services supplied to a Samoa resident remain subject to 15% VAGST.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration (CUSEXP) submitted in Asycuda World
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical shipment out of Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement proving receipt of foreign currency export proceeds

Samoa does not currently operate a real-time e-invoicing or mandatory fiscal clearance system. VAGST invoices are standard paper or electronic invoices specifying the supplier's TIN, invoice date, description, quantity, unit price, VAT amount, and total. Export clearances are processed through the Samoa Customs computer system (Asycuda World) via approved customs brokers; there is no API-based digital stamping requirement. Taxpayers with an annual turnover above the registration threshold must file VAGST returns electronically through the SRA e-services portal, but no transaction-level clearance or State-approved invoice stamping is required.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims or export documentation misstatements may expose the International Company to a VAGST shortfall penalty equal to 150% of the tax avoided, together with interest accrual from the original filing date. The Samoa Revenue Authority may also issue an amended assessment and immediate clawback of any VAGST refund improperly claimed. Deliberate falsification of customs declarations triggers penalties under the Customs Act 2014, including fines not exceeding 300 penalty units and imprisonment for up to seven years; directors and officers involved in export invoice fraud are jointly and severally liable. Continuing failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline may be treated as deemed taxable income derived in Samoa, subjecting the IC to income tax at 27% on the unremitted amount, plus late payment penalties and collection action.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a managed-float currency subject to exchange control. Current account payments (trade, services, income) are generally authorized through licensed commercial banks acting as authorized dealers upon documentary verification. Capital account transactions — inbound equity subscription, shareholder loans, outward profit remittance, and cross-border lending — require prior CBS approval or notification unless expressly exempted. International Companies (ICs) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 are statutorily exempt from the Exchange Control Act 1976 in respect of all non-resident, foreign-currency offshore transactions, meaning the WS_IC may freely receive equity, repatriate profits via dividends, and hold foreign currency accounts without CBS permits, provided no Resident of Samoa counter-party and no WST settlement is involved. Profit remittance from any residual WST onshore account remains approval-gated and requires audited financial statements, tax clearance from the Ministry of Revenue, and evidence of the underlying non-resident shareholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must repatriate proceeds to an authorized dealer within 90 days of bill of lading date and surrender to WST unless a Foreign Currency Account is pre-approved; 100% conversion is the statutory norm for resident export receipts. The WS_IC (non-resident, exchange-control-exempt) is not subject to mandatory surrender, provided: (1) invoice is payable in USD/HKD/SGD to a designated offshore account; (2) CBS-approved bank receives the related shipping/contract dossier; and (3) no portion of proceeds is settled in WST or credited to a resident account. Any incidental onshore credit must be converted immediately and flagged for CBS quarterly reporting by the bank as a non-resident transaction.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS / Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa) — Exchange Control Division and Financial Institutions Supervision Department; statutory regulators under the Exchange Control Act 1976, Financial Institutions Act 1996, Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007, and KYC/AML Rules 2008.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low-to-Medium. Samoa has no dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime; offshore fintech banking platforms (Wise-style, Airwallex, Mercury, Currenxie) generally decline Samoa-IC structures because the jurisdiction is not on their supported coverage, FATF mutual evaluation improvements notwithstanding. Legal constraint: foreign EMIs may lawfully onboard the IC as a non-resident client without Samoan licensing only if the EMI's home regulator permits cross-border Pacific clients; the IC may use such EMIs solely as supplementary settlement conduits, never as the primary correspondent rail, because EMIs cannot issue SWIFT MT103 directly and create an unregulated bridge that bank compliance teams will treat as a red flag.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a qualifying International Company (IC) on all income derived outside Samoa, pursuant to section 177 of the International Companies Act 1987, as amended; ordinary resident companies are otherwise taxed at a headline rate of 25% under the Income Tax Act 2015.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory final withholding tax on dividends is 15% for dividends paid by a Samoa-resident ordinary company (Income Tax Act 2015). Dividends distributed by a tax-exempt International Company out of income derived outside Samoa are not subject to Samoan withholding tax, provided the IC has maintained its exempt status and holds no Samoa-sourced income. Treaty-reduced rates of 5% on direct holdings of 10% or more and 15% otherwise apply under the Samoa-Australia, Samoa-New Zealand, and Samoa-China double taxation agreements.

Due on or before each anniversary of the date of incorporation; the annual government licence fee must accompany the declaration. A grace period is not statutorily granted, though the Registrar routinely allows a further 30 days before penalty accrual.

Deadline: · Frequency:

If the IC were to be assessed as an ordinary Samoa-resident company, the income year would end on 31 December and the return would be due on the following 30 June.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No specific monetary threshold in WST is prescribed by statute for Local File or Master File preparation under the Income Tax Act 2015 or Tax Administration Act 2015. The Commissioner of Inland Revenue may issue a notice requiring transfer pricing documentation for any cross-border transaction with a related party, regardless of amount. Prudent compliance practice is to maintain a functional analysis, a benchmarking study, and a local transfer pricing file where annual related-party turnover with non-residents exceeds WST 2,500,000, or where the aggregate book value of related-party intercompany balances exceeds WST 10,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Amendment Act 2021 and associated Rules apply economic substance requirements to International Companies conducting relevant activities. Wholesale distribution and export of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals constitutes a distribution and service centre relevant activity. The IC must satisfy: (i) conduct core income-generating activities in Samoa, including contract negotiation, procurement decisions, inventory risk management, and invoicing; (ii) direct and manage the business in Samoa through board meetings held in Samoa; (iii) maintain adequate physical premises in Samoa; (iv) employ a number of staff proportionate to the trading activity; and (v) incur local operating expenditure proportionate to the level of profits booked in Samoa. An annual economic substance declaration must be filed with the Registrar through the licensed agent, and underlying records must be retained for six years. Failure to meet the substance test results in financial penalties, deregistration, and reporting to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit and the Global Forum on Transparency and Exchange of Information.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 2015, civil shortfall penalty of 100% of the avoided tax plus monthly interest of 2%, regardless of the IC's nominal exempt status; criminal conviction for tax evasion carries a fine of up to WST 20,000 and imprisonment for up to three years.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the tax shortfall on grounds of dishonest assistance; the court may issue a travel ban and order surrender of passports; proof of fraudulent evasion extends the imprisonment term to five years and disqualifies the individual from serving as an officer of a Samoa international company.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with the International Companies (Economic Substance) Amendment Act 2021 attracts a penalty of WST 250,000 to WST 500,000; the Registrar may strike the IC off the register, seize the annual fee guarantee, and notify the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit, resulting in adverse cross-border tax transparency reports and possible blacklisting.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance declarations are personally liable for the regulatory penalty and for costs of the Registrar's investigation; upon strike-off, courts will pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally accountable for unsatisfied third-party trade creditors of the herbal wholesale business.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Act 2017, a corporate fine of up to WST 50,000 applies for register breaches; the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018 imposes fines of up to WST 300,000 and imprisonment for up to 14 years for laundering, and the FIU may issue freezing orders over bank accounts held by the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors who act with willful blindness to the source of funds are treated as accessories to money laundering and face personal fines of WST 50,000 and 14 years' imprisonment; the court may prohibit departure from Samoa, compel disclosure of foreign bank accounts, and order confiscation of personal assets.
Unlicensed Activity and Breach of IC Perimeter

Statutory sanction: Breach of the IC perimeter under section 181 of the International Companies Act 1987 invalidates the tax exemption and subjects the IC to ordinary corporate income tax; the Business Licences Act 1998 imposes a fine of WST 10,000 plus a continuing daily penalty of WST 100; unlicensed herbal export results in product forfeiture and a health regulatory fine of up to WST 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for all unpaid corporate fines and enforcement costs; the Registrar may revoke the IC's certificate of incorporation and the licensed agent's registration; courts may disregard the corporate entity and order personal restitution of profits derived from the illegal domestic trading activity.
