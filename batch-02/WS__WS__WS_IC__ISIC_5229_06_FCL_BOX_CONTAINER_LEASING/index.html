International Companies Act 1987 (Samoa), as amended by the International Companies Amendment Acts 2005 and 2008; key provisions: Part II (Incorporation, ss. 5-18), Part V (Shares and Capital), Part VI (Directors and Officers), and s. 112 (Registered Office and Agent).

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital requirement; share capital may be denominated in any currency and no escrow or capital deposit account is required under the International Companies Act 1987.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue, Government of Samoa (www.revenue.gov.ws)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities incorporating an International Company must apply under the International Companies Act 1987 through the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). The SIFA issues the Certificate of Incorporation and automatically registers the company with the Ministry of Revenue for a Tax Identification Number (TIN). No separate ad hoc tax registration filing is required for the International Company.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Foreign entities incorporating an International Company must apply under the International Companies Act 1987 through the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). The SIFA issues the Certificate of Incorporation and automatically registers the company with the Ministry of Revenue for a Tax Identification Number (TIN). No separate ad hoc tax registration filing is required for the International Company.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

International Companies are statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax and capital gains tax. There is no check-the-box election. An International Company may elect to be a non-resident company under the International Companies Act, but the tax exemption applies regardless of residence. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available under Samoan domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT$100,000 in annual taxable supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the VAGST Act 1998 to supplies of goods exported from Samoa and supplies of services supplied directly to non-residents for use wholly outside Samoa. Intermodal container leasing by an International Company to foreign lessees qualifies as an exported service when the place of supply is outside Samoa, evidenced by a written lease agreement and the foreign lessee's overseas location.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusEx)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Way Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Transfer Evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently mandated for VAGST invoices. Registered taxpayers must file VAGST returns electronically via the Ministry of Revenue online portal and retain invoices for audit. A valid VAGST invoice must contain the supplier's TIN, invoice number, date, description, and tax amount.

Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to immediate reassessment, interest at the statutory rate, and a penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded. Severe misconduct attracts criminal liability with fines not exceeding SAT$100,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years. Fraudulent claims carry no statutory limitation period for retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoa tala (WST) is convertible for current international trade and service transactions, including cross-border container lease rentals, subject to exchange control oversight. Capital account transactions, including shareholder loans, equity injections, and significant offshore transfers, require prior Exchange Control approval or notification to an authorized dealer. Profit remittance by an International Company is permitted without Samoan income tax or dividend withholding under the International Companies Act, but banks will require a tax clearance/status letter, audited or reviewed financial statements, source-of-funds evidence, and an underlying board resolution before executing the remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations, all export receipts earned by a Samoan resident or from a Samoan trade must be paid into an authorized dealer bank in Samoa within 30 days of receipt and offered for conversion into WST at a 100% mandatory conversion ratio, unless the Central Bank of Samoa grants a specific exemption. For a Samoa International Company whose container-leasing activity is booked offshore and invoiced in foreign currency, a general or specific exemption may be obtained to retain proceeds in an approved foreign-currency account, provided that the underlying lease contracts, shipping documents, and settlement advice are maintained for central-bank inspection.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), acting as the designated exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1976.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability Rating: Low. Samoa does not operate a licensed non-bank Electronic Money Institution (EMI) regime. Offshore digital EMIs cannot receive WST clearing, hold corporate funds in Samoa, or provide IBAN-like account services without a banking or financial-institution licence under the Financial Institutions Act 1999. Engaging an unlicensed offshore EMI as a primary treasury rail would be an AML red flag and would likely trigger account refusal by licensed Samoan banks. A licensed Samoan commercial bank is the mandatory clearing anchor; a regulated international EMI may be used only as a peripheral card-acquiring facilitator, never as the main settlement ledger.

Defense evidence: Compile the complete container lease portfolio, charterparty and master-lease agreements, maintenance and depot-service contracts, insurance certificates, container serial-list evidence, board resolutions confirming management decisions, and a substantive-presence statement explaining where fleet assets are managed and how revenues arise.

Defense evidence: Deliver the signed container-lease agreement, commercial invoice, payment schedule, underlying transport-chain evidence such as bills of lading and port terminal records, a reconciliation of each remitter to the contractual charterer, and an explanation of any factoring or collection-payment assignment.

Defense evidence: Upload the completed know-your-customer file for each charterer and depot operator, the vessel registration and flag certificate, P&I insurance certificate, sanctions-screening results, and the applicable charterparty terms containing compliance representations and warranties; prohibit cash and bearer-instrument payment terms.
High-velocity fund movements or round-tripping flows through the Samoa clearing account without documented treasury rationale

Root cause: High-velocity fund movements or round-tripping flows through the Samoa clearing account without documented treasury rationale

Defense evidence: Provide an annual treasury policy, cash-flow forecast, intercompany lease and funding agreements, independent tax advice, monthly bank reconciliation, and a narrative linking each material transfer to a genuine container-purchase, repositioning, maintenance, or dividend-remittance event.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's corporate bank account into the Samoa International Company's capital-escrow account, ordinarily held at ANZ Bank Samoa Limited or Bank of Samoa Limited. The remittance must carry a clear purpose code and narrative identifying it as shareholder capital injection or shareholder loan, referencing the board-resolution date and share-subscription amount. The bank will require the executed board resolution, share certificate, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth statements for the remitter, audited or certified financial information where applicable, and a SWIFT confirmation. No physical cash shipment is permitted. If the injection is in USD, the bank may hold it in an approved foreign-currency account or convert to WST under prevailing exchange-control rules.Cross-border outbound settlements should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for high-volume operational disbursements such as container depot fees, repositioning logistics, insurance premiums, and management fees. For the outright purchase of new or used containers from offshore suppliers, use Commercial Letters of Credit issued through a Samoa commercial bank (SWIFT MT700) or, where appropriate, a Bank Payment Obligation under uniform trade-finance rules. For interbank and funding transfers, SWIFT MT202 or MT202COV is used between the clearing bank and intermediary banks. For Samoa-domestic lease payments, the local automated clearing-house equivalent (ACH debit) may be applied only where the payee maintains an account at a licensed Samoan bank; all foreign-currency payables must be matched to a signed contract, invoice, and sanctions-screening certificate.Deliverable USD/WST forward contracts with an authorized dealer bank in Samoa, supported by underlying container-lease receivables or trade payables documentationUSD/WST non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts entered into with a licensed international bank in an offshore centre, permitted for hedges against forecast lease-rental revenue streams where Central Bank approval is obtainedUSD/WST cross-currency swaps with a licensed commercial bank, matching the International Company's USD-denominated lease assets to WST operational liabilities or shareholder loan repaymentsChinese-walls-compliant currency options (USD call / WST put) on a covered trade basis, executed only with an authorized dealer or permitted offshore counterparty after exchange-control clearance
