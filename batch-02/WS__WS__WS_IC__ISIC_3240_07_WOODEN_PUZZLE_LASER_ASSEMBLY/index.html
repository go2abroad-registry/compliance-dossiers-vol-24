Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue, Government of Samoa (formerly Inland Revenue Division) — central tax administration; official online portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws (TIN issuance, VAGST registration, income tax and VAGST e-filing). Customs functions are performed by the Samoa Customs Service under the same Ministry, operating the ASYCUDA World electronic single-window system for export declarations and clearance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 with the Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), through a licensed IC registered agent; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and IC registration number. Step 2 — Apply to the Ministry for Revenue (MfR) for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, IC registration extract, registered-office consent letter, notarized or certified passport copies of directors and shareholders, and a board resolution appointing the Samoa-resident agent as authorised representative. Step 3 — Register for VAGST (Form VAGST-REG) where annual taxable supplies in Samoa reach WST 100,000, or voluntarily where turnover exceeds WST 40,000, or where the IC intends to claim input-tax credits on export inputs destined for zero-rated export supplies. Step 4 — Obtain a Customs Exporter Code (ASYCUDA World client ID) from the Samoa Customs Service for electronic export declarations. Step 5 — File the annual return and economic substance declaration with the Registrar of International Companies (MCIL) to maintain IC tax-exempt status. TIN issuance is typically completed within 5–7 business days; VAGST registration certificate within 10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Samoa International Company is a separate limited-liability taxable entity; however, it is exempt from income tax on foreign-source income, foreign dividends, and foreign capital gains (International Companies Act 1987, s.208). Only Samoa-source income is subject to entity-level corporate income tax at the standard rate of 25% under the Income Tax Act 2015, meaning the entity operates under a territorial scope qualified by a statutory tax-exempt zone for offshore activities. No pass-through or flow-through treatment is permitted, and the IC must satisfy the economic substance requirements introduced by the 2019 amendments to preserve its exempt classification.

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' classification regime. Available statutory elections include: (1) election to establish the entity as an International Company (tax-exempt-zone treatment for foreign-source income) rather than as a domestic company under the Companies Act 2006; (2) voluntary VAGST registration election where turnover is below the WST 100,000 mandatory threshold but exceeds WST 40,000, or where the taxpayer seeks refunds of VAGST inputs attributable to zero-rated export supplies (VAGST Act 1992, s.3); (3) VAGST group registration election for related companies (VAGST Act 1992, s.16); (4) substituted accounting period / year-end election with prior approval of the CEO of the Ministry for Revenue (Income Tax Act 2015, s.96); (5) group-loss offset election between resident group members (Income Tax Act 2015, s.72–75); and (6) no hybrid-mismatch elections are available because Samoa imposes no domestic OECD hybrid-mismatch rules, and Samoa has no comprehensive double-tax treaties in force, only a limited network of Tax Information Exchange Agreements (TIEAs).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (Samoa Tala) of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period — mandatory registration; voluntary registration permitted for persons making taxable supplies exceeding WST 40,000 (VAGST Act 1992, s.3(1) and s.4).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports applies under the VAGST Act 1992 zero-rated supply provisions. For the CO2-laser wooden-puzzle manufacturing and assembly business, eligibility requires: (a) the finished wooden puzzles are physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of the time of supply, or within such extended period as the CEO of the Ministry for Revenue approves; (b) the exporter holds documentary evidence of export, comprising the customs export declaration, bill of lading or airway bill, commercial invoice, and packing list; (c) payment is received in foreign currency from the non-resident customer; (d) services physically performed entirely outside Samoa, or supplied to a non-resident located outside Samoa whose benefit is wholly consumed offshore, are also zero-rated (relevant to offshore design, logistics, and after-sales support); (e) the supplier is VAGST-registered and has issued a valid tax invoice; and (f) input tax attributable to zero-rated exports exceeds output tax, giving rise to a refundable credit which the MFR must process within the statutory refund cycle. Domestic retail sales of wooden puzzles within Samoa remain standard-rated at 15%.

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), government API certification, or digital stamping mandate is currently imposed in Samoa. The Ministry for Revenue operates an e-filing and e-payment portal (https://www.revenue.gov.ws) for periodic VAGST and income tax returns, while the Samoa Customs Service requires all export declarations to be filed electronically through ASYCUDA World. Exporters must retain paper or PDF invoices, customs declarations, and carrier documents in original or certified true copy form for audit inspection. A legislative roadmap toward mandatory structured e-invoicing is under review, but no binding e-invoicing clearance obligation exists as of the 2025 tax year.

Fraudulent export zero-rating and refund claims are subject to rigorous audit and retrospective enforcement. Penalty framework: (1) under VAGST Act 1992, s.38–40, submitting an incorrect return or claim to obtain an undue exemption or refund attracts a tax-shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the amount involved, plus late-payment interest at 2% per month (24% per annum), monetary fines up to WST 100,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; (2) under Income Tax Act 2015, s.138–140, false statements or evasion carry fines up to WST 50,000 and, for responsible officers, imprisonment up to 5 years; (3) the Ministry for Revenue may retrospectively reassess VAGST and income tax positions within 6 years of the end of the tax year, and without time limit where fraud or wilful evasion is proven, and may claw back refunds, disallow input credits, and apply penal interest; (4) under the Exchange Control Act 1972, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may trigger monetary penalties up to WST 10,000, forfeiture of the unrepatriated foreign currency, and denial of future export clearances; (5) under the Customs Act 2014, false export declarations attract fines up to WST 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years, together with seizure and forfeiture of the goods; and (6) an IC that fails the economic substance requirements of the 2019 International Companies amendments may lose its tax-exempt status, become retrospectively taxable on previously exempt foreign-source income, and incur penalty tax of up to 150% plus interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is a controlled currency with limited convertibility. Current-account payments are convertible through authorised dealers upon production of genuine trade or service documentation, while capital-account transfers, including non-resident equity injections, shareholder loans, and profit or dividend repatriation, require specific CBS exchange-control approval. An International Company (WS_IC) that maintains non-resident status and derives no local Samoan source income should, in practice, keep core treasury liquidity offshore to avoid unnecessary Samoan exchange-control approval frictions. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is legally permissible but is subject to central bank formality, tax clearance where applicable, and confirmation that no local Samoan tax liabilities are outstanding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Samoan-resident exporters, all export proceeds must be repatriated and surrendered to an authorised dealer in Samoa within 90 days from the date of export, unless CBS grants an explicit foreign-currency retention or offset exemption. The standard default conversion ratio is 100% into WST through an authorised dealer at prevailing rates. For a non-resident International Company such as WS_IC, export or trade proceeds earned outside Samoa are outside this surrender obligation, but any remittance landing in a Samoan bank account must be correctly declared, and any required local conversion must be executed only through a CBS-licensed authorised dealer.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), exercising exchange control oversight through the Exchange Control Act and delegated authority to authorised commercial banks under Samoa law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. An unlicensed offshore digital EMI or e-money institution has no direct independent access to Samoan clearing and cannot lawfully hold transaction accounts without a CBS-issued licence. Usage of non-resident digital EMIs is feasible only as a settlement pass-through from Stripe, Amazon Pay, or similar international payment providers, and must not interact with the Samoan domestic banking system in a manner that would constitute unlicensed e-money issuance. The primary operational account must remain with a licensed Samoan commercial bank if any WST clearing is required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection or shareholder loan proceeds should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the parent entity's bank in a low-risk, FATF-compliant jurisdiction directly into the WS_IC operational account at ANZ Bank (Samoa) Limited or BSP Samoa Limited. The remittance purpose must be classified as direct investment capital under the CBS exchange-control form; the bank will require the incorporated entity records, board resolution, shareholder register, source-of-funds verification, and CBS approval before crediting the account. WST conversion, if needed, must occur only through an authorised dealer at the prevailing interbank exchange rate. Crypto rails and unregulated digital tender are not accepted for inbound capital injection.Settlement to component suppliers and laser-tube manufacturers should be executed through irrevocable documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) for large bulk orders, with final settlement through SWIFT MT103 following presentation of compliant trade documents. For smaller working-capital disbursements, SWIFT MT103 with full trade invoice and custom declaration support is acceptable. Samoan banks do not currently provide a meaningful island-based high-value local clearing system; all interbank WST/USD clearing is run through offshore correspondent networks, principally in Australia, New Zealand, and the United States. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% standard Samoa corporate income tax rate (Income Tax Act 2012, s.8); effectively 0% for an International Company (ICA 1987, s.147 exempt status)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard dividend withholding tax on distributions by a resident Samoa company to non-residents (Income Tax Act 2012). International Company dividends: 0% withholding tax by statutory exemption (ICA 1987, s.147).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year ends 31 December; return due by 31 March of the following year (3 months after year-end). Not applicable to International Companies, which are exempt and not required to register for or file income tax returns.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return and annual government fee due on or before the anniversary of incorporation each year (ICA 1987, s.109). A company may elect an alternative due date; statutory grace period of 30 days before late penalty accrues.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File / Master File regime and no monetary threshold in Samoan law. The Income Tax Act 2012 (international transactions provisions) applies a general arm's length standard to cross-border related-party dealings; however, an exempt International Company is outside the income tax net and has no TP documentation obligation. Related-party transactions with the Samoa shareholder or connected entities must nevertheless be commercially rational and evidenced internally.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance (International Companies) Regulations 2021 requires all International Companies to file an annual economic substance declaration. Relevant activities subject to substantive requirements: banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service center, holding company, and IP business. Precision CO2 laser cutting and assembly of 3D mechanical wooden puzzles (ISIC 3240) constitutes manufacturing, which is not a designated relevant activity; the IC must therefore file a nil declaration confirming that it is not carrying on a relevant activity. The IC must nonetheless demonstrate adequate physical presence (registered office, agent, and board minutes) if it is managed or controlled from Samoa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abuse of IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: Forfeiture of the exemption; retrospective assessment to income tax at 27% for all exempt years; penalty tax up to 100% of tax evaded; criminal fine up to WST 100,000; strike-off and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for fraudulent trading debts; civil restitution orders; travel bans; imprisonment up to 7 years under the Income Tax Act 2012 fraud provisions.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Economic Substance (International Companies) Regulations 2021 up to WST 500,000 per offense; deregistration; automatic exchange of information with foreign tax authorities under the Samoa Exchange of Information Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are principal offenders for false substance declarations: fines up to WST 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; disqualification from managing any Samoa entity.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act 2018: monetary penalties up to WST 5,000,000 for regulated institutions; freezing orders; suspension or revocation of IC registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines up to WST 500,000; imprisonment up to 10 years for money laundering; travel bans imposed pending criminal investigation.
False Filing / Fraudulent Misrepresentation to Registrar

Statutory sanction: ICA 1987, s.152: conviction on indictment liable to a fine and imprisonment up to 5 years; deregistration of the company; revocation of the registered agent's license if complicit.

Director personal liability: Criminal personal liability; court-ordered restitution to defrauded counterparties; extradition risk; permanent director disqualification across Samoa and reciprocal Commonwealth jurisdictions.
