Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of Samoa, Independent State of Samoa, Apia (formerly Inland Revenue Division); official web portal: Samoa Ministry for Revenue Taxpayer Access Point Service (TAPS) at https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent or offshore International Company (IC) applies to the Ministry for Revenue using Form TIN-01 (Application for a Taxpayer Identification Number - Non-Resident Company), submitting: (i) Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) IC registration certificate; (ii) certificate of incorporation and memorandum/articles of association; (iii) board resolution appointing the registered agent; (iv) certified passport copies of directors and shareholders; and (v) registered office consent from the licensed agent. The TIN is issued within 5-10 business days. Where the IC makes taxable supplies connectable with Samoa (uncommon for pure offshore export brokerage), it separately files a VAGST registration application and obtains a VAGST registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - International Companies incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 are exempt from Samoan income tax, VAGST on offshore supplies, stamp duties, and withholding taxes on distributions to non-residents; the only periodic charge is the annual government licence fee (WST 100 to WST 1,200 depending on authorised share capital) payable to SIFA, and the IC must not carry on business with Samoan residents or derive Samoa-source income in order to retain this exempt classification.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective flow-through mechanism exists under Samoan tax law; the unitary regime taxes domestic corporations at entity level at 27% and exempts ICs territorially. The sole relevant election is one of corporate form at incorporation: a company may elect for International Company status under the International Companies Act 1987, which statutory election renders the entity a fully exempt offshore corporation. Hybrid mismatch elections and US-style entity classification elections are not recognised. An IC may further elect to be a Limited Life Company, an International Exempt Trust, or to register as a foreign-owned domestic company, each with distinct tax consequences. The IC Act also permits nomination of a resident director without changing the exempt tax character.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 50,000 per annum of taxable supplies in Samoa (approximately USD 18,500); for an International Company engaged purely in export brokerage of raw cotton bales, supplies are zero-rated, and registration is generally required only if the IC has Samoan domestic taxable turnover or chooses to register to recover input VAGST on local establishment costs. No threshold applies to importations of goods into Samoa, as VAGST is collected at the border regardless of importer turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of raw cotton bales is zero-rated for VAGST where: (i) the goods are physically exported from Samoa by the supplier within 28 days of supply (or such extended period as the Chief Executive Officer of the Ministry for Revenue approves); (ii) property in the goods passes to an overseas purchaser; (iii) payment is received in a convertible foreign currency; and (iv) the supplier retains the prescribed documentary evidence. International brokerage services in respect of raw cotton located outside Samoa, and logistics services performed offshore, are treated as offshore services and either zero-rated or outside the scope of VAGST. The IC must report the supply as zero-rated on its VAGST return (if registered) and must not issue a tax invoice charging 15% VAGST. The zero-rating claim attaches to the export, not the exporter's tax-exempt IC status, so documentary proof must be independently maintained.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World electronic e-declaration with Customs-assigned export reference number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Apia Wharf or Faleolo International Airport to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the customs declaration and payment terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or authenticated SWIFT transaction report evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds in foreign currency
  • SIFA Certificate of Good Standing for the International Company confirming valid tax-exempt status and current paid annual government licence fee

Samoa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping for exporters as of the 2025 tax year. Statutory VAGST returns and tax invoices are filed and retained via the Ministry for Revenue TAPS e-filing portal, and the Samoan Customs Service operates ASYCUDA World as the national single-window electronic customs clearance system, requiring the lodgement of export declarations and supporting documentation in electronic form. All VAGST records, including export substantiation, must be retained for a minimum of 7 years.

The Ministry for Revenue may audit zero-rated export claims for up to 5 years from the date of assessment. Under the Tax Administration Act 2015, shortfall penalties of 20% apply for failure to exercise reasonable care and 40% for fraudulent evasion, plus late payment interest of 2% per month compounded. The VAGST Act 2003 (as amended) provides additional monetary penalties and imprisonment up to 7 years for knowingly false export documentation or aiding tax evasion. Customs penalties may be imposed for misdeclared exports, and in extreme cases ASYCUDA export privileges may be suspended. For an International Company, engaging in undisclosed domestic Samoan trade or failure to maintain the offshore documentary chain triggers revocation of the exempt IC status under the International Companies Act 1987, exposing the entity retrospectively to 27% corporate income tax on Samoa-source income and 15% VAGST on infringing supplies, together with licence revocation and removal from the SIFA register.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a CBS-managed float tied to a trade-weighted currency basket. Current-account payments (trade settlement, commissions, and documented contract fees) are freely convertible through authorized dealers upon filing of a standard FX application; capital-account transfers involving Samoan residents require prior CBS approval. A Samoa International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 2012 qualifies as a non-resident entity: its foreign-currency treasury operations fall outside the domestic capital-control perimeter provided no business is conducted with Samoa residents, no WST source funds are used, and no Samoan-asset positions arise. Dividend and profit remittance from an IC is unrestricted when funded from a permitted foreign-currency account; no WST conversion or profit-remittance approval is engaged.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters of physical goods must declare exports to CBS through an authorized dealer, repatriate proceeds within the statutory period (generally six months from shipment), and surrender or offer for conversion any foreign-currency proceeds to an authorized dealer unless a CBS retention or foreign-currency-account exemption has been granted. The ISIC 4620 raw-cotton brokerage operates entirely offshore: legal title does not enter Samoa customs territory, and invoices flow from foreign gin mills directly to foreign spinning mills. Accordingly, no Samoan customs export declaration is triggered, no WST surrender obligation arises, and the IC may lawfully retain USD proceeds in a designated offshore-IC account.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Apia — statutory regulator under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and Exchange Control Act 1976 (as amended), responsible for exchange-control policy, authorized-dealer licensing, and administration of the Exchange Control Regulations 1980, in coordination with the Financial Intelligence Unit under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2017.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

FEASIBILITY: LOW. Samoa has no digital-EMI licensing category, and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2017 does not authorize foreign EMIs to conduct clearing or account-keeping for a Samoa-licensed IC. Global EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Dukascopy) generally decline Pacific offshore ICs owing to correspondent de-risking, restricted beneficiary-country coverage, and inability to verify Samoan IC registers in real time. Legal constraint: engaging an unregulated EMI may be treated as unauthorized banking business in Samoa. Recommended route: BSP SWIFT clearing coupled with a second-leg corporate account at a Singapore or Hong Kong licensed bank; no EMI dependency.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% standard corporate rate; 0% statutory exemption for International Companies (ICs)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% final withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident taxable company to non-residents; 0% on dividends and distributions paid by a compliant International Company pursuant to the IC Act 1987 exemption

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 aggregate related-party cross-border transactions per annum (administrative materiality threshold; the Income Tax Act 2015 imposes the arm's length standard without a statutory de minimis, and contemporaneous Master File/Local File documentation is mandatory above this level for taxable entities)

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2017 and Regulations require every IC to file an annual ES declaration and maintain genuine substance in Samoa for relevant activities, including distribution and service centres and holding companies. An IC brokering raw cotton bales must demonstrate: meetings and decisions of the board in Samoa, CIGAs undertaken in Samoa (negotiation, contracting, invoicing, treasury management), adequate local premises and qualified employees, and annual local operating expenditure proportionate to income. ICs without substance are treated as shell companies; the exemption is denied and information is exchanged automatically with treaty partners

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Wrongful Claim of IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of up to 6 years of concealed income at 27%; evasion penalty equal to 100%-200% of tax evaded; penal interest at 1.5% per month; criminal fine up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for fraudulent returns

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent may be personally assessed where the IC is a facade; courts may issue travel bans, asset preservation orders, and director disqualification orders
Economic Substance Failure (Shell Company)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WST 500,000 plus continuing daily penalties; mandatory strike-off of the IC; automatic exchange of information with tax authorities of the parent/shareholder jurisdiction, triggering follow-on assessments abroad

Director personal liability: Knowingly signing a false ES declaration is an offence; directors face personal fines, disqualification from acting as directors of any Samoa company, and loss of the corporate veil in subsequent recovery proceedings
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Anti-money laundering fines up to WST 2,000,000 for body corporates; confiscation of transaction proceeds; deregistration of the IC and freezing of Samoa bank accounts

Director personal liability: Directors and nominee shareholders face criminal liability for money laundering with imprisonment up to 14 years; travel bans and extradition cooperation apply for predicate trade fraud
Transfer Pricing and Trade Mis-invoicing

Statutory sanction: Arm's length re-adjustment, additional tax at 27%, accuracy-related penalties up to 100% of the adjustment, and penal interest; comparable documentation must be produced within 30 days of MoR notice

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false invoices or transfer pricing returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall; criminal sanctions apply for fraudulent trade mis-invoicing
