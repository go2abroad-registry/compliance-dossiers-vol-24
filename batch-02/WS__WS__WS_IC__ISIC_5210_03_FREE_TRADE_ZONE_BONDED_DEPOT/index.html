Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of Samoa (Office of the Commissioner of Revenue), Apia, Samoa; official taxpayer e-Services/e-Filing portal operated by the Ministry for Revenue (MRE e-Services). Samoa Customs Service, administered within the revenue portfolio, handles customs export clearance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 2012 through the Samoa Companies Registry (Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour), obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation and a Registered Agent Certificate. Step 2: Submit Form IR-CR/2 (Application for Tax Identification Number) to the Ministry for Revenue together with the certificate of incorporation, registered agent/office details, director and shareholder particulars, and beneficial owner identification. Step 3: If the IC will make taxable VAGST supplies exceeding the threshold in the next 12 months, file Form VAGST-R (VAGST Registration Application) with projected turnover, business description, and any free trade zone or bonded warehouse licence; the Commissioner will issue a VAGST registration number. Step 4: Obtain a Free Trade Zone licence under the Free Trade Zones Act 2010 and a bonded warehouse approval from Samoa Customs before conducting duty-suspended or zero-rated depot operations. Step 5: A foreign-owned IC transacting wholly offshore is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax and VAGST but must still procure a TIN for customs, administrative, and annual government fee compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Not applicable. Samoa does not administer a US-style check-the-box election, partnership classification, or hybrid-mismatch elective regime. A Samoa IC is classified by statute as a tax-exempt offshore entity under the International Companies Act 2012, and free trade zone/bonded depot operators benefit from the statutory exemption and zero-rating rules of the Free Trade Zones Act 2010 and the VAGST Act 2008. The General Anti-Avoidance Rule in the Income Tax Ordinance 2015 and the Tax Administration Act 2012 may recharacterize abusive classification or mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 50,000 (Samoan Tala) of taxable supplies in the preceding or prospective 12-month period; a person making or likely to make taxable supplies exceeding WST 50,000 must register for VAGST. Foreign entities and non-residents carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa, including bonded warehouse or free trade zone operations with a local supply nexus, are subject to the same threshold unless an express statutory exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAGST Act 2008, a 0% rate applies to (a) goods physically exported from Samoa by the supplier within a prescribed timeframe and substantiated by customs export documentation; (b) services supplied to non-residents or for consumption wholly outside Samoa, including bonded warehouse handling, storage, and logistics services performed for non-resident principals in respect of goods destined for export; and (c) supplies made between licensed free trade zone operators while goods remain in bond or customs suspension. The exporter must retain evidence of physical export, maintain valid VAGST registration, and ensure export proceeds are received into Samoa within the statutory FX repatriation deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs electronic export entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the receiving Samoan bank confirming export proceeds repatriation
  • VAGST export tax invoice showing the 0% rate or zero-rating statement with both supplier and recipient TINs
  • Bonded Warehouse Removal / Transit Entry evidencing customs-authorized transfer from the free trade zone depot to the export conveyance
  • Free Trade Zone Licence and warehouse goods in/out register supporting the bonded movement trail

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate is currently legislated in Samoa. VAGST tax invoices must include prescribed particulars (serial number, tax identification numbers, supplier and recipient details, date, and VAGST-exclusive/inclusive pricing) and be retained for seven years. The Ministry for Revenue continues to digitalize taxpayer e-filing but does not require government pre-clearance or API submission of invoices. Bonded depot operators must nevertheless maintain electronic customs-linked stock and transfer records evidencing goods movement into and out of the free trade zone.

The Commissioner of Revenue may audit zero-rated export claims and claw back VAGST at 15% plus statutory shortfall penalties where physical export, export documentation, or FX repatriation within 180 days is not substantiated. Fraudulent zero-rating, forged customs declarations, or false export certifications attract: (i) tax shortfall penalties of up to 150% for intentional or reckless non-compliance under the Tax Administration Act 2012; (ii) monthly late-payment penalties on assessed tax; (iii) criminal fines and imprisonment of up to three years for directors or officers involved in fraudulent evasion; and (iv) revocation of bonded warehouse and free trade zone licences together with deregistration of VAGST privilege. Retrospective assessments may extend beyond standard time limits where evasion or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST), managed by the CBS against a trade-weighted basket of partner currencies (USD, AUD, NZD, EUR, JPY), is not freely convertible. Current-account payments (imports, freight, insurance, agency fees) are approved routinely by authorised dealer banks on documentary evidence. Capital-account transactions — inbound equity capitalisation, shareholder loans, dividends, liquidation or branch remittances, and offshore loan repayments — require prior CBS exchange-control approval or an explicit dealer-bank delegated limit, plus, where applicable, a Foreign Investment Certificate under the Foreign Investment Act 2020 (unless a written legal exemption confirms that FTZ offshore warehousing falls outside the foreign-investment activity definition). For an International Company, dividend remittances are income-tax-exempt at source but operationally must clear CBS outward-remittance review supported by audited retained-earnings evidence. Practical hurdles: (i) correspondent-bank de-risking limiting USD/AUD clearing into Apia; (ii) CBS processing windows of 2–5 business days for non-automatic approvals; (iii) strict documentary back-up for every capital movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Exchange Control Act 1976 and CBS policy instruments require all export and re-export proceeds, including sales of bonded Free Trade Zone inventory, to be repatriated to Samoa within 90 days of shipment and sold (surrendered) to an authorised dealer bank in WST, unless the CBS has granted an exemption permitting retention in a Samoan foreign-currency account or an approved offshore account. The statutory surrender ratio for a standard bonded/reexport operator is 100% of the net foreign-currency proceeds. Compliance evidence includes the customs declaration, bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and bank credit advice, retained for at least five years for CBS/FIU audit; any deviation requires pre-approval through the CBS outward-remittance approval process, and non-compliant proceeds are recoverable by the CBS.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) — the national monetary authority and sole exchange-control administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018; all licensed commercial banks operate as CBS-licensed authorised foreign-exchange dealers, exercising delegated approval thresholds with CBS retaining jurisdiction over capital-account and large-value applications.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Samoa has no electronic-money-institution or digital-bank licensing category; the Banking Ordinance 2000 and CBS licensing rules do not recognise offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer) as authorised dealers. Such EMIs cannot hold WST clearing accounts, cannot issue Samoan clearing codes, and routing payments through them may breach the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018. Feasible workaround: maintain a Tier-1 Samoan dealer bank as the sole licensed settlement point and use an overseas EMI only for pre-settlement aggregation outside Samoa, forwarding funds to the Samoan account by SWIFT MT103.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity and shareholder-loan injections must flow by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank) to the IC's WST or USD account at a CBS-licensed authorised dealer. Use approved CBS SWIFT purpose codes mapped by the dealer bank — inward-investment / equity capitalisation, intercompany loan, and trade-goods codes — accompanied by the bank's inward-remittance advice. Supporting file: board resolution, share subscription or loan agreement, source-of-wealth pack, and Foreign Investment Certificate or legal exemption. Funds originating from China must reflect SAFE-approved outbound ODI equity or trade-in-goods remittance to avoid refusal; the receiving bank will escrow the credit pending CBS reconciliation of the inward-investment notification.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 payment orders for supplier settlements, with underlying invoice, packing list and transport document appended electronically. Commercial letters of credit are issued via SWIFT MT700 with a Samoan advising or negotiating bank (BSP or ANZ) and a confirming bank in the supplier's jurisdiction for high-value stock purchases. Domestic WST disbursements (FTZ rent, utilities, wages, customs bonds) clear through the CBS-overseen interbank clearing and RTGS in WST using standard local settlement instruments. Outward capital and high-value trade payments require CBS outward-remittance approval and the re-export certificate from Samoa Customs; the bank matches every outward payment against the surrender discipline on related export proceeds.Onshore deliverable forward contracts (USD/WST, AUD/WST) with a CBS-licensed authorised dealer, available up to 12 months against bona fide commercial trade exposure (bonded-stock purchases or re-export receivables) supported by underlying contracts.Plain-vanilla FX options (USD/WST) on a discretionary bank-offered basis where the option premium is embedded in the trade contract and the bank holds a CBS hedging mandate.Cross-currency swaps — technically permissible only with prior written CBS Exchange Control approval and only where linked to a specific financing or trade facility; in practice Samoan banks do not offer WST swaps to ICs, so the de facto hedge is the WST basket peg.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on WST — not permissible; there is no recognised offshore NDF market for the tala, and any such contract would be an unauthorised exchange-control transaction subject to CBS sanctions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Samoa-resident company to non-resident shareholders. Dividends of an offshore-exempt International Company are outside the WHT charge where the dividend is sourced entirely from FTZ/offshore income. Under the New Zealand-Samoa Double Taxation Agreement, the rate is 15% on portfolio dividends and 5% where the beneficial owner holds directly at least 25% of the paying company's capital.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final CIT return is due within six months after the income year end; for calendar-year taxpayers the deadline is 30 June of the following year. The Commissioner may grant a written extension of up to three months. Quarterly provisional tax instalments fall due on 31 March, 30 June, 30 September and 31 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A contemporaneous Samoa Local File is mandatory where aggregate connected-party transactions, including bonded storage fees, handling charges, group management fees and related-party interest, exceed WST 5,000,000 in an income year. A Master File is required where the overseas parent group has consolidated turnover exceeding WST 50,000,000 or where related-party cargo passing through the bonded depot exceeds WST 10,000,000. Below the threshold, a summary transfer pricing declaration (Form TP-IC) must still be filed for any IC with cross-border related-party transactions. All documentation must be in English and produced to the MoR within 30 days of demand.

Economic substance scope: The Samoa Economic Substance Requirements (2020) apply to every International Company, including the FTZ Bonded Depot activity class. The IC must demonstrate: (i) physical office premises in Samoa; (ii) at least two board meetings per year held in Samoa; (iii) a bank account in Samoa; (iv) adequate employees and core income-generating expenditure in Samoa proportionate to the warehousing logistics activity; and (v) books and records maintained in or accessible from Samoa. An annual Economic Substance Return (ESR-IC) is due within six months from year-end. Failure attracts financial penalties, potential loss of offshore tax-exempt status, deregistration, and automatic exchange of information to the jurisdiction of the ultimate beneficial owner under the multilateral exchange framework.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful False Statements

Statutory sanction: Civil assessment of the full evaded tax, an evader penalty equal to 200% of the tax shortfall where fraud is proved, and daily late-payment interest of 2% per month. Criminal prosecution may impose a fine up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who signed or caused the false return are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall. The court may issue a departure prohibition order (travel ban) pending settlement, order confiscation of personal assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act, and impose direct custodial sentences on individual wrongdoers.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of the income as Samoan-source, withdrawal of tax-exempt IC status, an ESR penalty of up to WST 50,000, compulsory deregistration, and reporting of the beneficial owner to foreign tax authorities under the Exchange of Information framework (CRS/MLI).

Director personal liability: Where the arrangement is a sham, the corporate veil is pierced. Directors are personally assessed on the re-characterised income, subject to a five-year director disqualification, asset freezing orders, and criminal conviction for defeating the Revenue where nominee or trustee structures conceal the true controller.
AML / Counter-Financing of Terrorism Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to WST 500,000 per violation under the Samoa Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018. The AML Supervisory Unit may suspend the statutory licence required for bonded depot operations, order immediate closure, and cause confiscation of funds connected to the suspicious transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and registered agents face personal fines up to WST 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years where they knowingly or with gross negligence permitted the unflagged transaction. The FIU may also seek personal restraint orders on director bank accounts pending investigation.
Transfer Pricing Misstatements

Statutory sanction: MoR adjustment to the arm's length price, a shortfall penalty of 100% of the additional tax, monthly compounding interest, and loss of treaty relief where the WHT-DTA-15 declaration was obtained on false premises. The adjustment may escalate to the 200% fraud penalty if the mispricing is deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed defective transfer pricing documentation are jointly liable for the tax adjustment and penalties. The Samoan courts may issue a charging order on personal property and a prohibition on leaving Samoa while the additional tax and penalties remain unpaid.
