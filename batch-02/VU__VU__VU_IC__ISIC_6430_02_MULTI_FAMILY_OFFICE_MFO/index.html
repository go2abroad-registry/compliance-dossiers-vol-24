Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Department of Customs and Inland Revenue, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; online portal www.customs.gov.vu; International Company incorporation and annual registration via Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) portal www.vfsc.vu

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned International Company, prepare notarized constitutional/imprint documents, register with VFSC under the International Companies Act [Cap 222], and obtain Certificate of Incorporation; this carries a registration number but not a revenue tax ID because ICs are tax-exempt. If the IC, exceptionally, makes in-Vanuatu taxable supplies or imports for local use, it must submit the Customs/Inland Revenue Form VAT-001 (Application for VAT Registration), provide the incorporation certificate, office address in Vanuatu, expected turnover, and evidence of local agent appointment; the department will issue a 10-digit VAT identification number. A physical domicile/registered office in Port Vila is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, inheritance tax, or stamp duty; the International Company is expressly exempt under its license from all Vanuatu taxes and duties on offshore/non-resident activities; entity is treated as a separate juridical person but tax classification is 'exempt association' rather than pass-through or entity-level. Local-source commercial activity remains constitutionally limited for ICs and would bring VAT compliance.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective pass-through classification exists under Vanuatu law. ICs automatically have entity-level legal personality with tax exemption; there are no hybrid mismatch elections, no CFC election, and no entity classification regimes comparable to US IRC Section 7701. The only 'election' available is voluntary VAT registration where the applicant anticipates making taxable supplies below the compulsory threshold (subject to IC business restrictions).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 5,000,000 in expected taxable turnover over any 12-month period (USD-equivalent approx. 41,000); compulsory registration also applies without threshold for large importers and providers of imported services subject to reverse charge

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) goods physically exported by the registered supplier and removed from Vanuatu within a prescribed period under customs control; (ii) services supplied wholly to a non-resident for use outside Vanuatu, including overseas portfolio advisory and discretionary asset-management services for offshore vehicles, where the benefit of the service is sustained wholly outside Vanuatu; (iii) international transport, insurance, and ancillary services. The supplier must retain documentation evidencing export and non-residence of the recipient; for MFO services, written service contract, investment mandate, and custodian records are required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form CED, lodged in ASYCUDA World)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and SWIFT/FX transfer confirmation evidencing foreign-currency payment
  • Service Agreement / Discretionary Portfolio Mandate for offshore services
  • VAT registration certificate and evidence of exemption for IC license holder
  • If applicable, Certificate of Origin or manufacturer's export records

No mandatory electronic-invoicing platform, no real-time fiscal clearance system, and no digital stamping regime exists in Vanuatu. Tax invoices must be issued in physical form and carry the supplier's name, VAT ID, date, description, consideration, and 12.5% VAT amount; electronic transmission and storage are permitted but not required to be cleared by the Revenue Authority.

Claims of export zero-rating are subject to review by Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue for at least 5 years from the date of supply. A knowingly false or fraudulent zero-rating return is punishable by a fine not exceeding VT 2,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years, plus repayment of any VAT refunded and restoration of the VAT liability at 12.5% with 50% surcharge; deliberate omission of export documentation triggers retrospective clawback of all related input VAT credits and disqualifies the exporter from claiming refunds for a further compliance period; serious organized fraud may lead to criminal prosecution under the Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Act for predicate VAT fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is convertible for bona fide current-account payments through RBV-licensed authorized dealers; capital-account flows by residents require prior approval, but an International Company is statutorily treated as non-resident, permitting free foreign-currency operation, unrestricted offshore settlement, and no requirement to convert foreign receipts into VUV. Practical hurdles: the VUV interbank market is thin and offshore VUV liquidity is scarce, so large conversions are executed by authorized dealers at negotiated AUD/USD cross rates with T+1 to T+3 settlement; profit, dividend, management-fee, and liquidation remittances to non-resident MFO principals face no exchange-control block and no Vanuatu withholding tax, subject to presentation of audited financial statements and the IC tax-exemption certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation window for an IC; all receipts from discretionary portfolio advisory, custody services, and family-office management are treated as non-export foreign-exchange earnings and may be retained indefinitely in any authorized-dealer foreign-currency account. Filing obligations are limited to statistical balance-of-payments declaration via the bank and FIU cash/wire reporting above the USD 10,000 (approx. VUV 1,000,000) threshold; no RBV conversion form is triggered for IC treasury flows.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), acting under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 112] and the Exchange Control Regulations, is the central bank and foreign-exchange authority; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) separately licenses the International Company (IC) and supervises its multi-family office conduct, while the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) directs AML/CFT surveillance. The Minister of Finance retains residual approval powers for exceptional exchange-control transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Vanuatu has no domestic e-money institution (EMI) licensing regime; foreign-regulated EMIs (for example FCA, BaFin, or MAS-licensed platforms) may lawfully hold the IC's non-resident liquidity but cannot serve as the licensed custodian or settlement bank required by the VFSC for discretionary MFO client assets. Legal constraints: EMIs are not RBV authorized dealers, so any VUV conversion must be routed through a licensed Vanuatu bank; VFSC client-money rules require trust-account custody at a regulated bank; and EMI wallets used to distribute funds to Vanuatu residents could trigger financial-customer licensing exposure under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act and Banking Act. Recommended use: low-value outbound administrative fees and short-term liquidity, not settlement-grade rails.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The VFSC annual return is due within 28 calendar days following each incorporation anniversary. Failure to file by the due date enters the company into the late renewal regime; the exact late period is calculated from the anniversary date.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
N/A (no tax form) No income tax schedules, balance-sheet-to-tax adjustments, transfer-pricing disclosures, or tax reconciliation are required. The VFSC annual return identifies the company name, registration number, registered agent, registered office in Vanuatu, directors, shareholders, and any amendments to constitutional documents or principal officers. Non-resident shareholders and directors have no local tax filing obligation. Foreign-resident principals must instead report any Vanuatu-sourced income under their own domestic tax rules; Vanuatu imposes no duty on the IC to withhold, report, or transmit tax on foreign investor distributions. Late filing of the VFSC annual return triggers a late penalty assessed by the VFSC, scaled to the period elapsed after the anniversary. Persistent non-compliance can lead to risk-based inspection, monetary penalties, and striking-off or dissolution of the International Company. Directors and the registered agent may be held responsible for the default.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A (no statutory threshold in VUV; there is no mandatory Local File or Master File requirement under Vanuatu law for International Companies).

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not currently impose a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime equivalent to BVI, Cayman, or Jersey. However, ICs must maintain a registered office and registered agent in Vanuatu. The VFSC treats substance, effective management, and commercial rationale as material indicators when evaluating licensing, AML/CFT risk, and good-standing applications. For a licensed multi-family office, maintaining substantive portfolio management, board oversight, client due-diligence records, and staff in a reputable jurisdiction is commercially advisable to prevent classification as a shell entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Offshore Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Although Vanuatu levies no local income tax, such conduct is a criminal offense under applicable Vanuatu penal, proceeds-of-crime, and anti-money-laundering legislation. Consequences may include VFSC deregistration, confiscation of assets, civil penalties, and criminal fines. Cooperation with foreign tax agencies under exchange-of-information instruments can also expose the entity to downwards reporting and administrative sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors, nominee officers, and beneficial owners may have the corporate veil pierced where the IC is used for fraud or evasion. Personal civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment are possible. Under proceeds-of-crime powers, the court may order confiscation of property derived from unlawful conduct.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The VFSC may refuse the annual renewal, issue adverse findings, or strike the company off the International Companies Register. Financial penalties and license revocation can apply to licensed activity. International standard-setting bodies may identify the vehicle as a high-risk shell subject to enhanced due diligence.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for continued trading or licensed activity without substance or proper authorization. In addition to deregistration, civil liability to creditors and regulatory fines may attach. Personal travel restrictions or bans on holding directorship positions in Vanuatu may be imposed.
AML/CFT Breach – Beneficial Ownership, Client Due Diligence, and Suspicious Transactions

Statutory sanction: Stringent fines and penalties are payable under Vanuatu's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act and Financial Intelligence Unit directives. The VFSC can suspend or revoke the multi-family-office license, freeze accounts, and impose monetary administrative penalties against the company and its controllers.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal liability for deliberate or reckless AML failures. Sanctions include imprisonment, fines, and debarment from management duties. Civil confiscation of director and beneficial owner assets is possible where those assets derive from money laundering or terrorist financing.
False Filings / Misleading VFSC and Licensing Representations

Statutory sanction: The VFSC may void or correct the company registration, impose financial penalties, revoke the license, and report the matter to the Public Prosecutor. Fraudulent filing carries criminal-law exposure under the International Companies Act and general criminal statutes, including monetary fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false declarations are personally accountable. They can be ordered to pay restitution, removed and disqualified, subjected to travel bans, and imprisoned. Regulatory authorities may also require the registered agent to resign, thereby triggering automatic default and potential compulsory striking-off.
