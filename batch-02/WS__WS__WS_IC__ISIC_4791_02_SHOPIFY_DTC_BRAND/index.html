Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), successor to the Ministry for Revenue; official e-services portal: https://sra.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign or offshore entity applies to the SRA for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) on the prescribed Form TR-1 (Application for Registration of Taxpayer), accompanied by a certified certificate of incorporation (for an International Company, the Certificate of Registration issued by the Registrar of International Companies, MCIL), memorandum and articles, proof of registered office in Samoa, and identification of directors/shareholders. The SRA issues the TIN within 3-5 business days; a non-resident IC with no Samoan business activity and no VAGST/customs/employer obligations is not required to hold a TIN for its offshore DTC e-commerce activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign or offshore entity applies to the SRA for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) on the prescribed Form TR-1 (Application for Registration of Taxpayer), accompanied by a certified certificate of incorporation (for an International Company, the Certificate of Registration issued by the Registrar of International Companies, MCIL), memorandum and articles, proof of registered office in Samoa, and identification of directors/shareholders. The SRA issues the TIN within 3-5 business days; a non-resident IC with no Samoan business activity and no VAGST/customs/employer obligations is not required to hold a TIN for its offshore DTC e-commerce activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Samoa. An International Company is automatically exempt from Samoan income tax and is non-resident unless it carries on business in Samoa; if it transacts in Samoa it loses the exemption and must convert/re-register as an ordinary company under the Companies Act 2001 (triggering the 27% corporate income tax and VAGST). A VAGST group registration election is available for affiliated companies, but group consolidation for income tax is not available to ICs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (voluntary registration permitted below threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Samoa to a non-resident, to services supplied directly to a non-resident outside Samoa ('exported services'), and to supplies of internationally traded digital/remote services where the DTC customer is a non-resident. A VAGST-registered IC may claim 0% where the supply contract, delivery, performance, and payment evidence establish that supply is made to a non-resident; offshore income of an International Company is entirely outside the Samoan tax base and therefore not subject to VAGST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice and VAGST tax invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit evidence showing foreign-source payment
  • Delivery/performance evidence (courier proof, non-resident billing and IP-country address) for DTC digital/remote supplies
  • Copy of the International Company registration certificate supporting the exempt-entity file

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API mandate exists in Samoa; the SRA e-Services portal receives VAGST returns and invoice-level data on a post-transaction filing basis. Registered suppliers must issue serially numbered VAGST tax invoices showing the supplier's TIN, date, description, taxable value in Samoan Tālā, and 15% output tax, and all supporting records must be retained for 7 years.

SRA may raise an assessment within 3 years (7 years in cases of evasion), reclassify domestic supplies at 15%, and claw back input tax refunds with statutory interest at 2% per month on late amounts. Civil shortfall penalties run up to 20% (or 150% for intentional evasion); criminal liability for fraudulent export claims and false customs declarations attracts fines of up to WST 100,000 (or up to three times the tax evaded) and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tālā (WST) is a managed-float currency with limited offshore convertibility. Samoa maintains exchange controls distinguishing current-account transactions (generally liberalised and delegated to authorized commercial banks) from capital-account transactions (dividend repatriation, shareholder loan repayments, equity injections) that require prior CBS approval or a no-objection certificate. For the non-resident International Company ('IC') engaged in DTC e-commerce outside Samoa, convertibility is effectively unrestricted if all receipts and disbursements are held in designated foreign-currency accounts; remittances of sales revenue from overseas payment processors to WST accounts are freely exchangeable to USD within commercial bank dealing limits. Profit remittance hurdles are moderate: audited financial statements, tax clearance, CBS exchange-control approval, and confirmation that the IC does not derive Samoan-source income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBS exchange-control regulations, export receipts from physical goods shipped from Samoa must be repatriated within 90 days of shipment; a percentage may be required to be converted into WST at a licensed bank if the exporter is a Samoan resident. A Samoan International Company whose Shopify-branded e-commerce sales originate outside Samoa and are settled through PayPal, Stripe, or Shopify Payments is not treated as a Samoan exporter for surrender purposes; the IC may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in an approved offshore USD/EUR/GBP clearing account, provided the bank classifies the account as a non-resident or offshore IC account and the CBS grants a standing exemption.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank Act 1984, which administers the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility. Samoa has no dedicated electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing regime, and CBS does not regulate offshore digital payment providers. Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut cannot open settlement accounts at Samoan banks and will not provide multi-currency IBANs to a Samoan IC unless the IC first obtains a bank account in an accepted jurisdiction; even then, such providers typically reject countries appearing on FATF grey lists. The primary permissible fintech rails are the payment gateways themselves (Shopify Payments, PayPal, Stripe), which operate as card acquirers and remit funds in USD/EUR via SWIFT/ACH; they are not substitutes for Samoan banking relationships.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share-capital or shareholder-loan funds must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from a qualifying bank to the IC's USD or EUR operational account at an authorised Samoan commercial bank. The wire must use standard purpose code G010 (equity injection) or G101 (intercompany loan) and be supported by the board resolution and a CBS exchange-control approval for non-resident foreign investment. The originating bank must be an approved financial institution with a valid AML/CFT relationship; funds may be sequestered until the IC provides a notarised share certificate, a director confirmation letter, and the CBS mandatory disclosure of the ultimate beneficial owner. If the remittance is made to a WST account, the bank will automatically convert to WST at the prevailing CBS reference rate and issue an exchange-control clearance slip to enable future profit repatriation.Clearing rails for supplier and fulfilment payments are primarily SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for single transactions under US$100,000, and commercial letters of credit (MT700) for high-value inventory procurement. The bank will require the underlying sales invoice, packing list, pro-forma invoice, and a signed trade-purpose declaration. Payments to Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe settlement entities (e.g., Stripe Treasury accounts) must be recorded as 'intercompany settlement' with purpose code G105; to avoid delays, the end destination must not be a high-risk jurisdiction or a private virtual-asset service provider. For supplier disbursements in EU and GBP, IC can request a multi-currency clearing account at the same Samoan bank, or use the bank's currency conversion desk for MT103 settlements.FX forward contracts up to 12 months for USD/EUR and GBP/USD, underlying a confirmed sales or procurement flow, executed through a CBS-licensed commercial bank in Samoa.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/WST transacted with an offshore investment-grade bank, provided the hedge is recorded in the IC's internal treasury policy and is not speculative.Cross-currency interest-free swaps between USD and EUR (principal exchange only) under a bilateral ISDA or CSA, subject to CBS exchange-control notification and only if the counterparty is a Schedule A bank in an OECD jurisdiction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by a Samoan International Company to non-resident shareholders; the statutory 15% dividend withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 2015 attaches only to distributions by resident companies and does not apply to exempt IC distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: SAT 1,000,000 (approximately USD 37,000) of aggregated cross-border related-party transactions in an income year triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation; dedicated thresholds of SAT 500,000 apply to intercompany management fees and IP royalties, and the SRA may compel documentation below these thresholds in audit risk cases.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations apply to ICs deriving relevant income from banking, insurance, shipping, headquarters, holding, distribution, and service centre activities. A Shopify DTC brand routed through a Samoan IC must evidence: a physical registered-office presence in Samoa, at least one board meeting held in Samoa, adequate employees and expenditure commensurate with the functions performed, and an annual substance certification filed with the Registrar. The substance requirement fails where revenue is booked in Samoa but all decision-making, inventory control, and digital operations occur entirely outside the jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Fraudulent IC Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the tax shortfall plus an evasion penalty of up to 150%, prosecution under section 73 of the Tax Administration Act 2015, imprisonment for up to 5 years, and publication of the taxpayer's name in the official list of tax defaulters.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the shortfall and penalties where they signed false returns; the SRA may impose travel bans via Departure Prohibition Orders, freeze bank accounts, and seize assets; directors face disqualification and criminal conviction where culpable knowledge is proven.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance penalties up to SAT 200,000, compulsory strike-off of the IC, revocation of exempt status, and back-assessment of corporate income tax at 27% on up to 5 years of previously exempt income.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for back-taxes and penalty amounts; directors may be disqualified from acting as Samoan company officers for up to 10 years; the entity is flagged on international exchange-of-information registers, exposing shareholders to global enforcement.
AML / Payment-Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to SAT 5 million and imprisonment of up to 14 years under the Samoa Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (as amended), mandatory forfeiture of proceeds, freezing of merchant accounts, and suspension of payment-rail settlement privileges.

Director personal liability: Individual directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal conviction, asset forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition risk; the corporate veil is pierced where the IC is shown to be an instrument of fraud or money laundering.
Withholding Tax Evasion via Mischaracterized Distributions

Statutory sanction: SRA deemed-dividend adjustments, penalty of 100% of the undeclared amount, monthly interest surcharge of 2%, and, in treaty contexts, denial of relief with additional fines of up to SAT 100,000 under the International Companies Act 1987.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for conduit-company abuse, including repayment orders and recovery from personal estates; prohibition orders bar them from acting as officers of any Samoan company, and continued abuse risks criminal prosecution for tax fraud.
