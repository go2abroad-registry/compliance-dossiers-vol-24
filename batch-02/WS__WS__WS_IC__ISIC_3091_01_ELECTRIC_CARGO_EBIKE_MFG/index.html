Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Ministry of Revenue (MoR), Apia, Samoa – official taxpayer portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws; customs administration: Samoa Customs Service (Ministry for Revenue) operating ASYCUDA World.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) formed under the International Companies Act 2016 must first be incorporated through a licensed SIFA (Samoa International Finance Authority) registration agent. Following incorporation, the IC files a Ministry of Revenue 'Non-Resident Taxpayer Registration / TIN Application' (Form TIN-NR-01) accompanied by: (1) Certificate of Incorporation; (2) Registered Office Certificate; (3) Licensed Agent Consent; (4) Beneficial Ownership Declaration; and (5) Tax Exemption Certificate application. The MoR issues the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) within 5 business days. The Tax Exemption Certificate is issued by MoR concurrently with the IC annual license renewal, confirming exemption from income tax, withholding tax, stamp duty, capital gains tax, and exchange control on all offshore business activities. No minimum capital or physical Samoan office is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone – An International Company under the International Companies Act 2016 is statutorily treated as a non-resident corporation and is exempt from all Samoan income tax, capital gains tax, dividend/interest/royalty withholding tax, gift and estate duties, and stamp duty on its worldwide offshore income and transactions. Tax exemption is automatic upon issuance of the Tax Exemption Certificate; the IC becomes taxable only if it carries on prescribed domestic trading activity in Samoa with residents, in which case the Income Tax Act 2015 applies to Samoan-source income only. Manufacturing and export of electric cargo e-bikes to non-resident customers falls wholly within the tax-exempt zone and incurs 0% corporate income tax. — A foreign-owned International Company (IC) formed under the International Companies Act 2016 must first be incorporated through a licensed SIFA (Samoa International Finance Authority) registration agent. Following incorporation, the IC files a Ministry of Revenue 'Non-Resident Taxpayer Registration / TIN Application' (Form TIN-NR-01) accompanied by: (1) Certificate of Incorporation; (2) Registered Office Certificate; (3) Licensed Agent Consent; (4) Beneficial Ownership Declaration; and (5) Tax Exemption Certificate application. The MoR issues the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) within 5 business days. No minimum capital or physical Samoan office is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – An International Company under the International Companies Act 2016 is statutorily treated as a non-resident corporation and is exempt from all Samoan income tax, capital gains tax, dividend/interest/royalty withholding tax, gift and estate duties, and stamp duty on its worldwide offshore income and transactions. Tax exemption is automatic upon issuance of the Tax Exemption Certificate; the IC becomes taxable only if it carries on prescribed domestic trading activity in Samoa with residents, in which case the Income Tax Act 2015 applies to Samoan-source income only. Manufacturing and export of electric cargo e-bikes to non-resident customers falls wholly within the tax-exempt zone and incurs 0% corporate income tax.

Available election forms

No check-the-box transparent entity election exists under Samoan law. However, an IC may file with the MoR a 'Hybrid Entity Consistency Declaration' under the Income Tax Act 2015 to align Samoa's opaque entity classification with a counterpart jurisdiction's check-the-box transparent treatment, thereby addressing hybrid mismatch outcomes under BEPS Actions 2. An IC may also elect to apply for a 'Domestic Activity Permit' from SIFA/MoR to carry on an approved local support service (e.g., warehousing, after-sales servicing) with a Samoan subsidiary; such an election subjects only the domestic activity to VAGST and income tax while preserving the exempt offshore manufacturing and export status. Any election is valid for the tax year and must be filed at least 30 days before year-end.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT$ 100,000 (Samoan Tālā) cumulative annual taxable turnover threshold for mandatory VAGST registration; voluntary registration is permitted where annual turnover exceeds SAT$ 60,000. An exporting IC that exclusively zero-rates its e-bike export turnover may nevertheless be required to register and file monthly VAGST returns solely to claim input credits on domestic raw materials, components, logistics, utilities, and agency fees.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the physical export of goods (including electric cargo e-bikes, spare parts, and accessory kits) provided that: (a) the goods are exported by a VAGST-registered person within 60 days of the tax invoice date; (b) title in the goods passes to a non-resident buyer; (c) the goods leave Samoa and are delivered to a destination outside Samoa; (d) the exporter retains prescribed export evidence; and (e) the exporter holds a valid Tax Exemption Certificate (for ICs). Direct services ancillary to the exported goods (design, integration, OEM after-sales remote diagnostics) that are performed in Samoa and enjoyed outside Samoa are likewise zero-rated; support services consumed in Samoa are taxable at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs CED-01, lodged through ASYCUDA World)
  • Commercial invoice stamped with the MoR Digital Fiscal Clearance Reference (DFCR)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the goods departed Samoa and delivered to the non-resident buyer
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (Samoa Chamber of Commerce)
  • Vendor/OEM sales contract or purchase order identifying the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed Samoan commercial bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment
  • Certificate of non-residency of the buyer (supporting evidence such as foreign TIN or certificate of incorporation)
  • Freight forwarding agent's export certificate of receipt

VAGST-registered exporters must issue sequentially numbered electronic tax invoices through the MoR Taxpayer E-Services Portal (e-Invoicing module). Each export invoice above SAT$ 100 must be submitted digitally at the time of issuance; the MoR system performs near-real-time validation of the TIN, invoice sequence, tax category (zero-rated export), and buyer residency, returning an 18-digit Digital Fiscal Clearance Reference (DFCR) that must be printed on the final invoice and customs export declaration. No physical fiscal stamping machine is required, but the exporter's ERP/accounting system must be capable of API-based invoice transmission to the MoR gateway (SOAP/REST), and all invoice data must be hosted in or accessible to Samoa for 7 years. Failure to obtain a DFCR renders the invoice non-deductible for the buyer and disqualifies the corresponding input credit claim for the exporter.

The Samoa Ministry of Revenue may reopen a zero-rated supply within 4 years of the relevant VAGST return. If export documentation or the DFCR cannot be substantiated, the full 15% VAGST is assessed retrospectively together with monthly default interest of 2% on the unpaid tax. Deliberate or fraudulent zero-rating claims attract a civil penalty of 200% of the evaded tax, a criminal fine not exceeding SAT$100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years under section 78 of the VAGST Act 2008. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline constitutes a breach of Samoa's Exchange Control Regulations 2005, rendering the exporter liable to forfeiture of up to 50% of the unrepatriated amount and suspension of VAGST refund privileges; however, a Certified International Company holding a valid Tax Exemption Certificate is wholly exempt from exchange control and may retain or repatriate proceeds offshore at its discretion. Exporters are also exposed to clawback of all input VAGST credits previously allowed on materials attributable to the non-exported or mischaracterized supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a managed float for the Samoan Tala (WST) with broad convertibility on the current account, including trade transactions and normal remittances, subject to submission of supporting documentation to authorized dealer banks. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment inflows, profit repatriation, and offshore loan disbursements, require CBS general or specific approval under the Exchange Control Act. Profit remittance by non-resident shareholders of an International Company is generally permitted once the company is tax-exempt and provides a certificate of compliance from the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), but the remittance is subject to a final withholding tax of 10% on dividends unless an applicable tax treaty reduces this. Practical hurdles include the need to present audited financial statements, board resolutions declaring the dividend, and confirmation that no domestic tax liabilities are outstanding. The CBS imposes real-time reporting thresholds on all cross-border wire transfers, and funds may be temporarily held pending verification of economic substance or beneficial ownership information.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa within 90 calendar days of shipment date. Once received in an authorized dealer bank account, the proceeds must be converted to WST at the prevailing market rate within one business day unless the exporter obtains a specific CBS exemption to retain the balance in foreign currency for verified commercial working capital. Exemptions are granted only for recurring export receivables in the same foreign currency and require quarterly reporting to CBS. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% for non-exempt export receipts; no partial retention is permitted for ordinary export sales without prior written approval.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) — Falefa o Tupe Tutotonu o Samoa, operating under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and the Exchange Control Act 1978

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium for offshore digital EMIs. Samoan law does not license foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) to hold WST-denominated accounts or to provide payment services to residents or registered international companies. An offshore EMI, such as a UK FCA-authorised e-money institution, may be used as a non-resident treasury vehicle for receiving pre-shipment deposits or invoicing in foreign currency, but it cannot clear funds into the local banking system without a corresponding Samoan dealer bank relationship. The CBS maintains strict anti-money laundering rules requiring all in-country payment services to be rendered by licensed commercial banks or licensed money remitters. Therefore, the offshore EMI route is legally permissible only for pre-funding of foreign currency raw material purchases, provided no WST conversion occurs, and all transactions are reported to the CBS via the beneficiary bank. Any attempt to use an EMI for recurring operational disbursements to Samoan suppliers would be non-compliant.

Root cause: Mixed or opaque beneficial ownership structure involving a trust or nominee shareholder

Defense evidence: A formal trust deed or nominee declaration, a complete chain of ownership diagram showing all natural persons as ultimate beneficial owners, certified copies of passports for all UBOs, a letter of comfort from the Samoan trustee or licensed nominee service provider, and a statement of source of wealth and source of funds signed by each UBO.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for inbound capital injection: (1) The shareholder or ultimate beneficial owner wires funds from a personally held bank account in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction to the International Company's capital escrow or operational account at a licensed Samoan commercial bank. (2) The wire must contain a precise purpose code using the SWIFT MT103 field 70: 'INVCAP' (capital injection) or 'INVESTMENT EQUITY', and must reference the company registration number. (3) The Samoan bank will request a board resolution authorizing the issuance of shares or recording the shareholder loan, a statement of account from the remitting bank showing the source of funds, and a certified copy of the share register reflecting the increase in paid-up capital. (4) The bank is required to verify the identity of the remitter and the ultimate beneficiary; any split transfers below reporting thresholds to evade scrutiny are prohibited. (5) For capital injections exceeding USD 50,000 or equivalent, the Samoan bank must submit an ExPost report to the CBS within five business days; therefore, the documentation bundle must be assembled in advance. (6) If the funds are for a shareholder loan rather than equity, a loan agreement with commercial terms must be lodged, and the CBS may require prior approval for any non-resident borrowing exceeding WST 1,000,000.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) For supplier payments of raw materials and components, use SWIFT MT103 direct transfers from the Samoan operational account to the supplier's bank, with good instructions including the supplier's IBAN or account numbers, SWIFT/BIC codes, and precise purpose codes (e.g., 'TRADEPAY' for trade payment, 'PREPAY' for advance payment, or 'COMMLC' for documentary credit settlement). (2) For bulk or high-value settlements, a commercial letter of credit (L/C) issued by the Samoan bank in favor of the offshore supplier is acceptable; the L/C is typically irrevocable and confirmed by a prime international bank. Samoan banks require a margin or full collateralization for the L/C amount. (3) Disbursements for freight, insurance, and inspection fees can be settled via MT103 with supporting transport documents. (4) For larger transactions exceeding WST 200,000, the bank must verify the underlying trade document (invoice, packing list, and proof of shipment) before releasing funds, in order to comply with CBS exchange control rules. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% under the Income Tax Act 2012; International Companies (ICs) classified in the Tax_Exempt_Zone pay 0% on qualifying offshore (international) income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory non-resident dividend withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 2012; reduced to 0% for distributions made by a Samoa International Company to non-resident shareholders under the International Companies Act 1987, provided the IC acts only as an IC and does not carry on domestic Samoan business.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Samoa-specific threshold mandating Local File or Master File preparation is prescribed in the Income Tax Act 2012. The arm's length principle applies to all cross-border related-party transactions. Prudent compliance benchmark: maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where annual cross-border related-party turnover exceeds WST 1,000,000 (approximately USD 370,000), covering purchases of e-bike components, royalties, management fees, and intercompany financing.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not maintain a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to BVI or Cayman. Nevertheless, the International Companies Act 1987 imposes mandatory presence requirements: a licensed registered agent and registered office in Samoa, a board of directors, and books and records maintained at the registered office. The IC tax-exempt zone status is contingent on deriving exclusively 'international income'; local manufacturing, local warehousing, or local sales activity in Samoa taints the exemption and converts the IC into an ordinary taxable resident company at 25%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Exemption Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 2012: full reassessment of the tax shortfall, a 100% evasion penalty, 2% monthly late payment interest, and criminal fines up to WST 100,000 upon conviction; the Registrar may additionally revoke the IC status.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly file or approve false returns are personally liable for the unpaid tax on a joint and several basis; prosecution may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years, asset freezes, and travel bans preventing departure from Samoa pending final assessment.
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the International Companies Register, forfeiture of the tax-exempt status, retroactive assessment of 25% corporate income tax for the full period of non-compliance, and monetary penalties under the International Companies Act 1987.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be disqualified from holding Samoan corporate offices, prohibited from leaving Samoa, and held personally liable for the re-assessed corporate tax, interest, and penalty amounts where the sham structure was knowingly maintained.
Anti-Money Laundering / Trade-Based Finance

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007: fines up to WST 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years upon conviction; the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit must receive Suspicious Transaction Reports for any irregular trade-payment pattern; the underlying assets may be confiscated.

Director personal liability: Senior officers who knowingly direct trade-based money laundering face personal prosecution, imprisonment, personal fines, confiscation of personal assets, and travel restrictions; prior conviction does not preclude separate civil recovery proceedings.
Withholding Tax / Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full 15% withholding tax on the gross payment, 2% monthly late payment interest, late filing penalties, and a 100% false return penalty where the exemption was claimed without supporting documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as withholding agents are personally liable for the unremitted WHT if they authorize distributions while aware of the non-compliance; criminal conviction carries fines and imprisonment of up to 3 years, with personal travel bans available to secure recovery.
