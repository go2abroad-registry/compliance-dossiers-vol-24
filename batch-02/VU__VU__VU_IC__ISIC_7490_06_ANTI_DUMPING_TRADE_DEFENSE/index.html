Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Authority (VRCA), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official portal: https://www.vrca.gov.vu. Company registration and International Company (IC) licensing: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the International Company (IC) with VFSC and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and a licensed registered agent; the VFSC registration number is the entity's primary statutory identifier because Vanuatu levies no income tax. Step 2 - If the IC makes (or voluntarily elects to make) taxable supplies in Vanuatu, submit the VAT Registration Application (Form VAT-01) to VRCA with certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles, registered agent consent, director and beneficial-owner identification, and expected turnover estimate; VRCA issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT Registration Certificate, typically within 10 business days. Step 3 - If the IC imports or exports physical goods, obtain an Importer/Exporter Code from the VRCA Customs Division (ASYCUDA World platform). Step 4 - For the pure offshore consulting model (ISIC 7490 trade-remedy defense), no VAT registration or TIN is required because the IC is statutorily tax-exempt and its export services are zero-rated; the entity must maintain the VFSC annual return and pay the flat annual government fee to remain in good standing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - International Companies are exempt from all Vanuatu taxes (income, corporate, withholding, capital gains, stamp duty, and estate taxes) under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222], as amended. The IC sits outside the VAT net when it carries on no taxable activity inside Vanuatu; export services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated if registration is required or elected.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no direct taxation, so no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections exist. Available statutory elections: (1) voluntary VAT registration under Section 7 of the VAT Act No. 12 of 1998 for persons below the VUV 5,000,000 threshold; (2) continuation or redomiciliation of a foreign company as an International Company, or conversion of an IC into a domestic company under the Companies Act, by directors' or members' resolution lodged with VFSC; (3) the Minister of Finance may grant additional tax exemption certificates to VFSC-licensed offshore entities by order. No CFC, dividend, interest, or royalty elections apply because Vanuatu does not tax such flows.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 (five million vatu) annual taxable turnover; registration is mandatory once actual or expected turnover in any 12-month period exceeds the threshold and voluntary below it; ICs supplying services entirely offshore are not carrying on a taxable activity and are not registrable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Section 9 of the VAT Act, goods qualify for zero-rating when exported and delivered outside Vanuatu within 28 days; services qualify when supplied to a recipient who is not in Vanuatu and the services are performed (or the benefit is enjoyed) wholly outside Vanuatu. ISIC 7490 anti-dumping and trade-remedy defense consulting qualifies when the foreign client is the recipient, the defense work is executed offshore, and the output is used exclusively in foreign administrative or judicial proceedings. The supplier must hold contemporaneous service, delivery, and remittance evidence. Vanuatu has no exchange-control repatriation statute; the 180-day inward-remittance evidence window is the standardized bank and AML substantiation benchmark used in the zero-rating refund file.

  • Customs Export Declaration (goods) or Export Service Contract and engagement letter (services)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill (goods) or service delivery and utilization report with foreign proceeding reference (services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - SWIFT MT103 credit advice (services) or foreign currency bank statement

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate exists in Vanuatu as of the current regime. VAT-registered suppliers must issue a prescribed tax invoice within 28 days of supply and retain records for 5 years. VRCA operates electronic return filing and the ASYCUDA World customs platform, but individual invoices are not cleared or stamped by the revenue authority.

VRCA may deny or claw back zero-rating, issue a default assessment at 12.5% on the full supply value, and recover interest at 10% per annum where export, service, and remittance evidence is missing or false. Fixed monetary penalties apply per offence for invoicing, registration, and return failures; wilful evasion or fraudulent refund claims are criminal offences punishable by significant fines and/or imprisonment. Retrospective assessment periods generally extend up to 5 years, and up to 7 years in cases of fraud or wilful misstatement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is freely convertible on both current and capital account. Vanuatu maintains a liberal exchange control regime; non-residents and International Companies may hold VUV and foreign currency accounts without prior RBV approval. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan servicing, and capital repatriation are not subject to exchange control licensing or ad-hoc administrative barriers. Payments for imports, professional services, and trade settlements are unrestricted. The only practical frictions arise from correspondent bank compliance and the limited depth of the onshore FX interbank market, which is predominantly quoted in VUV/USD, VUV/AUD, VUV/NZD, and VUV/EUR.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory surrender requirement on export receipts. There are no mandatory conversion ratios, no designated time limits for repatriation, and no RBV requirement to convert foreign currency earnings into vatu. Exporters and consulting firms may retain overseas currency in licensed dealer bank accounts indefinitely, subject to standard AML/CFT record-keeping obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Vanuatu has no domestic Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing framework; only banks and financial institutions licensed under the Banking Act may accept deposits or provide payment services in vatu. Offshore digital EMIs are not recognized as local deposit-taking institutions and cannot directly clear VUV. They may be used as auxiliary FX conversion or wallet providers for USD/Euro flows, but the core cross-border treasury operation must anchor to a licensed Vanuatu dealer bank to maintain legal payment rails and correspondent settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the originating counsel or client trust account to the VU_IC operational account at a licensed Vanuatu dealer bank. The beneficiary account must exactly match the International Company registered name. Recommended ISO 20022 purpose codes are LEGA (legal services) or ARPU (accounts receivable) for anti-dumping defense fees, and CENL (capital in enterprise) for shareholder capital injections. The sending bank must include the invoice or engagement letter reference in the payment narrative to satisfy the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit and the receiving bank's AML review. RBV requires no prior approval, but the bank must record the economic purpose.Outbound trade disbursements for settlements and litigation-related payments are executed through SWIFT MT103 for low-value remittances and via irrevocable commercial letters of credit subject to UCP600 for high-value disbursements with documentary proof. Vanuatu dealer banks rely on ANZ and BSP correspondent networks in Australia, New Zealand, and the United States; direct CHIPS or Fedwire membership is not available. For anti-dumping defense fees payable to foreign legal teams, MT103 wires with purpose code LEGA are the standard clearing rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in Vanuatu: no corporate income tax, no transfer pricing statute, and no Local File/Master File thresholds exist in vatu (VUV). If the IC belongs to a foreign group, the group's parent jurisdiction rules (commonly OECD Action 13 standards, e.g., a USD 1,000,000-plus related-party turnover threshold) determine whether a Master/Local File must be prepared abroad; the IC itself has no domestic filing obligation.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations have been enacted for International Companies; however, the International Companies Act requires a registered office, licensed registered agent and an annual return, and commercial substance expectations are imposed indirectly by VFSC AML supervision and by foreign CFC/economic-substance rules. For ISIC 7490 anti-dumping defense consulting, the IC must demonstrably manage, staff and execute the consulting engagements from a genuine base (typically the foreign client markets) and avoid being characterized as a letterbox holding or sham conduit in foreign tax or trade-remedy investigations. Vanuatu remains subject to ongoing OECD Global Forum and EU good-governance monitoring, which strengthens the need for documentary evidence of real activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Value-Added Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Value Added Tax Act combined with the Vanuatu Criminal Code, confiscation of proceeds, a stop-trading order, revocation of the IC's business license and strike-off from the International Company Registry.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false VAT or activity declarations are personally liable for evaded duty and penalties; the Supreme Court may issue travel bans, asset-freeze orders and disqualification orders preventing management of VFSC-registered entities, with custodial sentences attached to fraudulent evasion.
Abuse of International Company Tax-Exempt Regime / Shell Company Conduct

Statutory sanction: Deregistration and loss of limited liability under the International Companies Act; registry monetary penalties; in importing jurisdictions, civil false-certification penalties, customs trade-remedy evasion penalties and retroactive duty assessments attach to the foreign group entities.

Director personal liability: Vanuatu Courts pierce the corporate veil where the IC is a facade; directors face civil restitution for registry costs and third-party losses, personal liability for foreign anti-dumping evasion judgments, plus extradition and travel-ban exposure in the jurisdiction conducting the trade-remedy investigation.
Unlicensed Legal Practice / Business License Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Strict-liability fines under the Legal Practitioners Act and Business License Act, closure of Vanuatu operations, nullification of fee recovery for unlicensed engagements, and revocation of the registered agent's capability to service the IC.

Director personal liability: The principal consultant and each directing mind face personal fines, disqualification from holding regulatory approvals, adverse immigration consequences, and personal judgments for refund of client fees paid into the unlicensed practice.
Anti-Money Laundering / Countering Financing of Terrorism Breach

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act penalties: substantial corporate fines, personal fines for directors and the registered agent, imprisonment up to 15 years, forced deregistration, and freezing of the IC's bank accounts; the agent's license is suspended or revoked.

Director personal liability: Nominated and shadow directors face criminal liability, personal fines, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans pending investigation, and permanent disqualification from any financial or trustee role in Vanuatu.
False Filings and Misrepresentation to the Registrar

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment and/or fine under the International Companies Act, mandatory deregistration, and loss of the benefits of limited liability for all transactions entered into during the period of concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors and the authorized signatory are personally liable for the fine and for civil restitution to defrauded counterparties; the Registrar may apply for a travel ban and for judicial orders requiring personal re-registration costs before any new Vanuatu vehicle may be incorporated.
