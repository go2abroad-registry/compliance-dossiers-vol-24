Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR) of the Independent State of Samoa — the Commissioner for Revenue administers income tax, VAGST, customs, and taxpayer registration through the unified e-services portal at www.mfr.gov.ws. International Companies are incorporated and regulated by the Registrar of International Companies within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 through a licensed IC service provider; no minimum paid-up capital is required. Step 2: Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and IC registration number from the Registrar of International Companies. Step 3: For an IC transacting exclusively offshore, no domestic Samoan TIN is required because the statutory IC tax-exemption regime applies automatically from incorporation and covers foreign-source income. Step 4 (optional, and necessary only to validate VAGST zero-rating on physical exports from Samoa): Submit the standard Taxpayer Registration Application to the Ministry for Revenue, attaching certified copies of the Certificate of Incorporation, the IC Constitution, passports of directors and shareholders, and proof of the licensed registered office in Samoa. Step 5: MfR issues a unique Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and, if applied, a VAGST registration certificate within an administrative processing period of approximately 5-10 working days; applications may be lodged electronically through the licensed service provider.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Samoa International Company holds statutory exemption from all Samoan income taxes, capital gains taxes, estate and gift taxes, and stamp duties for 20 years from the date of incorporation. This exempt status extends to heparin sodium extraction, fractionation, and export revenue derived from non-resident customers; a fixed annual government license fee is paid to the Registrar of International Companies in lieu of tax.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Samoa domestic law. The available statutory mechanisms are limited to: (i) an election at incorporation to register under the International Companies Act 1987 rather than the Companies Act 2001; (ii) a subsequent resolution to redomicile or migrate the IC into or out of Samoa; and (iii) an election by an IC to register for VAGST under the VAGST Act 2007 solely to support zero-rated export claims. None of these elections create an Entity_Level_Taxation or Pass_Through classification for Samoan tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (approximately USD 37,000) annual taxable turnover in the current or immediately preceding 12-month period. Registration below the threshold is voluntary, but a registered supplier must remain registered for at least two years. An International Company engaged in the physical export of heparin sodium must obtain VAGST registration to claim input credits and to zero-rate export supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the zero-rating provisions of the VAGST Act 2007, exported goods — including pharmaceutical extractives and low-molecular-weight heparin sodium (HS 3001.90) — are zero-rated if the supplier exports the goods within 28 days after the day of supply (or within an extended period approved by the Commissioner for Revenue), retains prescribed evidence of physical export, and the customer and place of enjoyment are both outside Samoa. Services, such as contract fractionation or quality-control services supplied to a non-resident pharmaceutical principal, are zero-rated when provided to a recipient who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and the services are not consumed in Samoa. Zero-rating is inapplicable to any supply consumed, enjoyed, or exploited in Samoa.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged in the ASYCUDA World customs management system administered by the Ministry for Revenue
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical departure of the heparin sodium consignment from Samoa
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Certificate of Origin and analytical/pharmaceutical certificate for low-molecular-weight heparin
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Credit advice or bank SWIFT/Letter of Credit settlement confirmation evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is in force in Samoa. Registered VAGST suppliers must issue statutory tax invoices containing the supplier's name, TIN, invoice date, description of supply, and VAGST amount, and must file VAGST returns electronically with the Ministry for Revenue on a monthly or quarterly cycle. Physical or PDF tax invoices satisfy the prescribed invoice standard. Export declarations and customs clearance are processed through the ASYCUDA World electronic customs management system, which acts as the sole clearance gateway for goods leaving Samoa.

The Commissioner for Revenue may audit zero-rated export claims for the statutory assessment period, and the burden of proof rests entirely on the International Company to evidence physical export and receipt by a foreign customer. Where the documentation bundle is deficient, inconsistent, or circular, MfR will re-assess the supply at 15% VAGST, recover any input credits refunded, and impose late-payment interest and shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2012. Knowingly false export declarations, forged bills of lading, and fictitious inward remittance certificates attract criminal penalties including fines and imprisonment, customs fraud sanctions, revocation of VAGST registration, and exchange-control penalties where export proceeds remain unrepatriated beyond the 180-day deadline set by the Central Bank of Samoa.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is a managed-float currency. Current-account transactions, including trade and service payments, are broadly permissible through authorized dealer banks with documentary support. Capital-account transactions, including the injection of equity capital into a Samoa International Company and repatriation of funds, require prior Central Bank of Samoa approval or an applicable standing exemption. As a non-resident International Company conducting business wholly outside Samoa, the entity is generally outside the domestic WST convertibility regime; however, any WST-denominated clearing or settlement with Samoa-resident counterparties will trigger CBS scrutiny. Profit remittance by an International Company is permitted provided the company is solvent, the transaction is backed by audited financial statements or a director's solvency certificate, and the remittance is routed through a CBS-licensed commercial bank with correct SWIFT and purpose codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa's Exchange Control Regulations require a resident exporter to surrender all foreign-currency export proceeds to an authorized dealer bank within 30 calendar days of receipt, and the mandatory conversion ratio to WST is 100% unless a specific exporter is granted a CBS exemption. For the subject International Company (WS_IC), because it operates under the International Companies Act offshore regime and derives its export revenue from heparin-sodium active pharmaceutical ingredients sold to foreign buyers, the export surrender obligation does not apply automatically; a formal exemption confirmation or a non-resident account designation from CBS should be obtained in the corporate bank account opening process to ensure that foreign-currency trade receipts may be retained without conversion.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), acting as the Foreign Exchange Administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1970 and Exchange Control Regulations 1976

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility, with strict legal constraint: Samoa does not authorize unlicensed offshore Electronic Money Institutions or non-bank EMIs to provide deposit-taking, multi-currency settlement or SWIFT access to International Companies. Only licensed commercial banks and licensed money remitters operating under the Central Bank of Samoa's licensing framework may hold client funds. Fintech EMI solutions such as offshore neobanks or digital payment intermediaries may be used only for sub-limits, reimbursable expense disbursements, or third-party payment facilitation, provided they are regulated in a FATF-equivalent jurisdiction and do not operate a Samoa-resident branch; principal trade settlement and heparin-sodium related fiduciary cash flows must remain in a CBS-supervised bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Companies under the International Companies Act 1987 (full statutory tax exemption evidenced by a Certificate of Tax Exemption); Samoa resident corporate income tax headline rate: 25% (Income Tax Act 2015).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for International Companies (dividends paid by an IC to any shareholder, resident or non-resident, are exempt from Samoan withholding tax under the International Companies Act 1987); the domestic statutory rate for resident companies is 15% under the Income Tax Act 2015.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on each anniversary of incorporation; payment of the annual SIFA government fee is required within 30 days of the anniversary date, after which statutory late penalties accrue; continued default beyond 90 days triggers registry strike-off.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable to tax-exempt International Companies because they have no assessable income in Samoa. Samoa transfer pricing rules (Income Tax Act 2015, Part VIII) apply to resident taxpayers and non-residents with a Samoan permanent establishment; no monetary threshold is published, and documentation is demand-based, requiring a contemporaneous transfer pricing file to be produced within 30 days of a Commissioner's request.

Economic substance scope: No dedicated Economic Substance Regulations statute is in force for Samoan International Companies, and this gap underpins Samoa's inclusion on the EU list of non-cooperative jurisdictions for tax purposes. OECD BEPS 'No or Only Nominal Tax Jurisdiction' standards and Global Forum peer reviews nevertheless demand verifiable economic activity. For ISIC 3250 heparin sodium fractionation, the commercial substance must reside at the actual manufacturing plant; the Samoan IC must not passively hold IP, supply contracts, or bank accounts without locally demonstrable staff, premises, and decision-making control.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2015, conviction for tax evasion carries a fine of up to WST 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; the shortfall tax is recoverable with a penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus monthly statutory interest; the general anti-avoidance provision empowers the Commissioner to disregard the IC and reallocate profits to the real economic operator.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and officers knowingly concerned in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt and fines; upon conviction they are disqualified from managing companies in Samoa, are subject to asset restraint and travel bans pending settlement, and may be imprisoned.
Unauthorized Domestic Business (Shell Company Violation)

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act 1987, the company is liable to a fine of up to WST 50,000; the tax exemption is automatically revoked, and the IC is assessed as a resident company on its worldwide income from the date of the prohibited activity; SIFA may also strike the company off the register.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or participated in the prohibited domestic business are personally liable for the company's penalties and unpaid tax assessments; courts may issue civil restitution orders and travel bans to secure the company's assets during recovery.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance (Enhanced Medium-Risk Monitoring)

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's AML/CFT Act 2018, a body corporate is liable on conviction to a fine of up to WST 100,000 per offense; the bank account is subject to freezing orders, and the transaction proceeds are liable to restraint and forfeiture as proceeds of crime; reputational consequences include FATF counter-measures against Samoan corporate service providers.

Director personal liability: Directors with actual knowledge or intent face imprisonment of up to 5 years; investigation orders may impose travel bans and asset restraint on directors personally; directors who facilitate fraudulent invoice or product declarations are also exposed to civil claims by defrauded counterparties and banks.
Sham Company / Absence of Economic Substance (BEPS)

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the corporate structure under the general anti-avoidance rule and allocate all profits to the actual operator; Samoa's EU-blacklisted status subjects the structure to enhanced automatic exchange of information and country-by-country scrutiny; the Certificate of Tax Exemption may be revoked, and the company is exposed to struck-off restoration proceedings.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil when the IC is a mere facade; directors may be ordered to repay diverted profits to the operating company, face disqualification orders, be subject to travel bans during registry or tax investigations, and may incur personal liability for debts of a sham company.
