Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), the national tax administration agency; portal: SRA eServices (https://www.sra.gov.ws). Company registration is filed separately with the International Companies Office (ICO), Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour, Samoa.

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is, without more, outside the Samoan income tax net and is not required to obtain a Samoan TIN for offshore business. If the IC seeks GST/VAT registration in Samoa (e.g., to acquire zero-rating/refund capacity or to make Samoan taxable supplies), it must apply to SRA on the prescribed GST registration form, providing: (i) International Company certificate of incorporation and memorandum/articles; (ii) identification of directors and shareholders; (iii) Samoan registered office and appointed agent details; (iv) description of supplies and expected annual turnover; and (v) bank account or receipt evidence. SRA verifies the application and issues a TIN/GST registration number. Non-resident foreign corporations with no Samoan place of business generally do not need a TIN unless an obligation such as withholding tax, GST registration, or physical economic activity arises.

Available election forms

Samoa has no US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. An International Company may elect to be taxed as an ordinary Samoa resident company by re-registering under the Companies Act 2001 (thereby surrendering IC tax-exempt status), and may elect to voluntarily register for GST if it makes or intends to make taxable supplies. SRA may also issue binding rulings concerning GST treatment of offshore or export arrangements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to a GST-registered supplier's export of movable goods where the goods are physically exported from Samoa and shipped to a non-resident within the time specified in the GST Act, and to international services directly related to exported goods or physically performed outside Samoa for a non-resident. The supplier must retain documentary evidence proving movement, the non-resident customer, and the receivable. An offshore e-commerce IC whose goods are never imported into or exported from Samoa will not meet Samoa export zero-rating conditions; it generally remains outside the Samoan GST net rather than claiming zero-rating.

  • Samoa Customs export declaration / exit manifest
  • Ocean bill of lading or air waybill showing Samoa port or airport of departure
  • Commercial invoice and sale contract stating export terms (e.g., FOB/EXW)
  • Packing list / container stuffing records where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit evidence from the Samoan banking system
  • Certificate or other evidence of the non-resident status of the buyer or offshore service recipient

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API filing, or digital stamping regime is in force for Samoa. GST-registered persons must issue prescribed tax invoices and credit notes, retain records (generally seven years), and file periodic GST returns through the SRA eServices portal or paper returns. SRA is developing digital services but no continuous transaction control mandate currently exists.

SRA scrutinizes zero-rating claims by cross-checking customs, banking and returns data. Goods or services that were not actually exported or that lack the prescribed evidence within the statutory period are treated as taxable at 15%; the resulting GST, default assessment and late-payment penalties are immediately clawed back. Filing false export declarations or using artificial arrangements to claim refunds may attract criminal sanctions, personal liability of directors, and substantial civil penalties, in addition to interest and loss of refund offsets.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a managed exchange-control regime centered on the Samoan Tala (WST), which is not freely convertible offshore. Current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, interest, royalties, and ordinary profit remittances) have been substantially liberalized: authorized dealer banks may process such payments without case-by-case CBS approval provided full underlying documentation is lodged. Capital-account transactions — including equity injections, shareholder loans, and repatriation of invested capital — remain subject to prior CBS approval, although payments of profits, dividends, and liquidation proceeds by tax-exempt International Companies (ICs) registered under the International Companies Act 2019 (including the WS_IC entity) are normally approved without substantive tax review given the IC operates in a tax-exempt zone. In practice, the WS_IC's e-commerce treasury is structured to collect USD revenue offshore through payment aggregators (Stripe and PayPal) and to retain those proceeds in foreign-currency accounts; conversion into WST is required only where the entity maintains onshore WST operational balances. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal for ICs but banks must nevertheless verify source of funds, payment-processor settlement statements, and underlying sales contracts before executing cross-border credit transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1984 and current CBS policy, exporters of goods or services must collect payment no later than three (3) months after the date of export/supply, remit the full proceeds into Samoa through a licensed authorized dealer, and surrender 100% of the foreign currency into Tala (WST) within thirty (30) days of receipt unless a specific CBS exemption has been granted. The Central Bank may, upon written application, permit a foreign-currency retention account or partial deferral of conversion where the exporter demonstrates recurring foreign-currency liabilities (e.g., USD-denominated supplier settlements for hydroponics equipment procurement), but such approval must be secured in advance and is reviewed annually. E-commerce sellers settling through Stripe/PayPal are treated as exporters of services/goods for exchange-control reporting purposes; the WS_IC must therefore furnish periodic proof of payment-processor settlement and, absent an exemption, route net proceeds to its Samoa bank account within the mandated timeline.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) — statutory monetary and exchange control authority established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; foreign exchange administration is additionally governed by the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1984, together with periodic CBS Exchange Control Notices issued to all licensed authorized dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability. Samoa does not maintain a stand-alone licensing regime for electronic money institutions (EMIs) or digital-only cross-border banks; the Financial Institutions Act 2019 does not recognize offshore EMI charters as eligible for onshore banking licenses. As such, foreign fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, or licensed EU/UK EMI entities) cannot obtain a Samoa banking license or direct settlement access at CBS. Their functionality is limited to acting as non-bank payment intermediaries supported by correspondent banks; accordingly, for WS_IC treasury purposes, the use of such platforms is legally permissible only as payment-collection or payment-disbursement conduits, not as the primary regulated bank account holder. The registered commercial banks in Samoa remain the sole onshore deposit-taking and FX-conversion counterparties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections — whether shareholder loans or equity subscription funds — must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COVER where an intermediary bank is used) from the parent/shareholder's home-country bank to the WS_IC's Samoa operational account. The wire instruction must state the purpose of payment as 'Share Capital Contribution — WS International Company' or 'Shareholder Loan Disbursement', using the Balance of Payments classification codes applicable under CBS reporting: Direct Investment — Equity Capital for share subscriptions, and Other Investment — Loans for shareholder advances. The receiving authorized dealer is required to verify the underlying resolution of the board of directors, the share allotment/loan agreement, and the UBO identification evidence before crediting the account. CBS prior approval is required when the initial injection exceeds the standard authorized-dealer threshold; in such case, a CBS Exchange Control application must accompany the bank, including audited financial projections evidencing the e-commerce working-capital requirement.Trade disbursements for hydroponics-equipment procurement from overseas suppliers are settled primarily by SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for invoice payments under USD purchase orders. For larger or recurring shipments where the supplier demands documentary security, a commercial letter of credit (MT700) may be opened through Bank of Samoa or Samoa Commercial Bank, with documents drawn including the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill, and certificate of origin. Settlement cycles must align with the CBS reporting calendar: the bank requires underlying contracts, proforma invoices, and supplier KYC on file so that each disbursement is tagged with the appropriate purpose code (General Merchandise — Imports) and submitted for CBS balance-of-payments aggregation. Where the WS_IC retains USD in a foreign-currency retention account, supplier wires may be effected directly in USD without WST conversion, thereby reducing FX friction; payroll, rent, and domestic Samoan obligations would be settled from a WST sub-account funded by the 30-day conversion rule applicable to export receipts.Onshore USD/WST spot transactions executed through authorized dealer banks with full CBS reporting.Forward foreign-exchange contracts (deliverable USD/WST forwards) booked with a licensed Samoa commercial bank, permitted for trade-related exposures and requiring an underlying commercial invoice or purchase order.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) offshore against the WST, permissible only where the counterparty is a reputable international bank and the WS_IC either holds an offshore treasury account or maintains adequate credit support; such NDF transactions are generally outside CBS onshore regulatory perimeter but must be disclosed to the onshore bank for consolidated reporting.Cross-currency swaps (USD/WST) with authorized dealers for the purpose of refinancing shareholder loans or hedging committed e-commerce treasury flows, subject to CBS case-by-case approval when the swap term exceeds 12 months.Natural hedging via foreign-currency retention accounts denominated in USD, allowing matched inflows from Stripe/PayPal settlement and outflows to overseas suppliers, thereby minimizing the need for explicit derivative instruments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for an International Company on offshore-sourced income; 27% standard corporate income tax rate applies if the IC generates Samoan-source income or is deemed resident due to domestic business activity.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends and other distributions paid by a Samoan International Company to non-resident shareholders. By contrast, a domestic resident company is subject to a standard 15% non-resident dividend withholding tax, unless reduced under an applicable Double Tax Agreement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: International Companies in the wholly tax-exempt regime have no mandatory transfer pricing documentation obligation because they are outside the income tax net. If an IC loses exemption or elects to pay Samoan tax on domestic-source business, Samoa's Income Tax Act 2015 requires a Transfer Pricing Local File where related-party transactions exceed WST 2,000,000 (approximately USD 740,000), or related-party debt exceeds WST 1,000,000. A Master File threshold of WST 50,000,000 in group revenue applies for multinational groups entering the Samoan documentation framework.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not impose BVI/Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) on International Companies. However, the IC must maintain a licensed registered agent and a registered office in Samoa. If the IC seeks to use an agreement tied to Samoa tax residence or claims a DTA benefit, Samoa Revenue may apply a general anti-avoidance principal-purpose test and require factual substance: local premises, employees, and real decision-making. Without such substance, the IC may be deemed a shell and denied treaty benefits, and the corporate status may be reconstructed as a local resident company on Samoan-source income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Annual Declaration

Statutory sanction: An additional tax assessment at 27% CIT on all undeclared Samoan-source income, plus interest at 10% per annum and an evasion penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded. Criminal prosecution may result in a court fine of up to WST 250,000 for the company and mandatory disclosure of the IC's full tax affairs.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew or ought to have known of the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for all corporation tax, interest, and evasion penalties. On conviction, a director may face imprisonment for up to 7 years, a personal fine of up to WST 50,000, and a Ministry of Revenue departure ban prohibiting exit from Samoa until the liability is settled.
Shell Company Abuse and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits in the source country; Samoa Ministry of Revenue may cancel tax residency certificates, assess penalties of up to WST 50,000 per year of abuse, and retroactively classify the IC as an ordinary domestic resident company on its worldwide income. Foreign tax authorities may also impose secondary transfer pricing adjustments.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierceable where the IC is a pure instrumentality. Directors and controlling shareholders may be personally liable for the difference in withholding taxes in the source jurisdiction, plus all Samoan penalties. Where abuse is aggravated, directors face criminal anti-conduit charges with civil asset restitution and a travel ban pending adjudication.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Money Laundering Prevention Act and AML/CFT Rules, the IC and its registered agent face fines up to WST 100,000, suspension of the registered agent's license, freezing of the IC's Samoa and correspondent bank accounts, and forced deregistration. Non-compliance may also result in the IC being blacklisted by correspondent banks and excluded from payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer are individually criminally liable if they knowingly permit AML failures. Penalties include fines up to WST 50,000, imprisonment for up to 5 years, civil forfeiture of all proceeds linked to the unregistered e-commerce activity, and a personal travel prohibition in serious cases.
Unauthorised Local Business and Stamp Duty Evasion

Statutory sanction: The International Companies Act exemption is invalidated; the IC is retrospectively assessed as a resident company at 27% tax on all Samoan-source revenue, with an additional unauthorized business penalty of WST 30,000. Samoa Customs and Revenue may also impose duty and VAT (VAGST) on the hydroponics equipment sold or warehoused, plus stop-sale orders against the online portal for Samoa IP addresses.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate taxes, duties, penalties, and interest. If the unpaid amounts are fraudulently concealed, directors face criminal sanctions of imprisonment up to 5 years, personal civil restitution, and an asset freeze across Samoa. The court can order a travel ban until all tax liabilities are settled.
