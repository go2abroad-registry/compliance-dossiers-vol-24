Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment applies to supplies of goods and services exported from Samoa, including the charter of sea-going vessels used in international or offshore trade, provided the charterer is a non-resident and the vessel is not consumed or used within Samoa. For offshore supply vessel leasing, the charter must be substantiated as employment exclusively in offshore support activities outside Samoan territorial waters, and consideration must be received in foreign currency.

No mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API-based reporting, or digital stamping regime is currently operational in Samoa. Taxpayers must retain manual or electronic invoices in a form acceptable to the Ministry for Revenue. Customs export declarations are processed through the ASYCUDA World system, which requires electronic manifest data; no additional e-invoicing clearance is imposed.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective clawback of the zero rate, disallowance of associated input tax credits, assessment of underpaid VAGST with interest at the statutory rate, civil penalties of up to 150% of the avoided tax under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992, and criminal prosecution with fines up to WST 20,000 and/or imprisonment for up to three years. The Ministry for Revenue may reopen assessments for up to eight years where fraud or wilful default is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a managed float, with current account convertibility generally liberalized subject to prior notification to an authorized dealer for transactions exceeding prescribed thresholds. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment injections, offshore loans, guarantees, and issuance of equity to non-residents, require exchange control approval or documented notification via the prescribed Central Bank of Samoa forms. Profit remittances by an International Company (IC) are permissible once the distributing entity demonstrates compliance with the Foreign Investment Act, provides audited financial statements or a solvency certificate, and obtains tax clearance; however, dividend remittances to non-residents attract enhanced review where the IC is tax-exempt and the underlying capital was sourced from related-party cross-border financing. Dividends and charter hire remittances are treated as current transfers and are not subject to capital gains withholding, but remissions require the bank to record the transaction under the CBS Foreign Exchange Reporting Framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Charter hire earned by the offshore supply vessel leasing entity is classified as invisible export earnings under the Exchange Control Act 1976. Such foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa through an authorized dealer within 30 days of the contractual invoice due date where the vessel operates on cross-border voyages, and in all cases no later than 90 days from the date of invoice issuance. The statutory default is 100% mandatory surrender of collected foreign currency into WST at the prevailing authorized dealer rate, unless the Central Bank of Samoa has granted a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) facility permitting retention of proceeds for working capital, loan servicing, or vessel operating expenditure; any FCA retention is capped at the approved limit and subject to quarterly reporting. For vessels on bareboat charter to non-resident operators, the CBC Class statutory timeline is extended to 180 days but the full proceeds must be credited to a Samoan licensed bank account prior to any discretionary reinvestment or repayment of shareholder advances.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Central Bank Act 1984, in coordination with the Ministry of Finance and the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) for offshore company related approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium for operational collection purposes and Not Viable for capital injection or statutory escrow custody. Legal constraints: Samoa has no licensing framework for foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs), and the Central Bank of Samoa does not recognize offshore EMI licenses as equivalent to an authorized dealer status. Consequently, the WS International Company may use an offshore EMI (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud, or MultiPass) exclusively as a vetting and collection aggregation layer for charter hire paid by counterparties in restricted remittance corridors, provided all funds are swept within 48 hours into a CBS-licensed commercial bank account. The EMI cannot clear capital escrow obligations, cannot provide an FX hedging execution venue for CBS-reportable exposures, and must submit a no-objection letter from the FIU where aggregate monthly throughput exceeds USD 50,000 equivalent.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate under Samoa's Income Tax Act 2012; effectively 0% for an International Company (IC) licensed under the International Companies Act 1987, which is statutorily exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, and business license taxes.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% final withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a resident company under the Income Tax Act 2012, but dividends and other distributions paid by a licensed International Company to non-resident shareholders are statutorily exempt from all Samoan taxes and are remitted gross without any withholding obligation.

IC Annual Return: within 30 days after each anniversary of incorporation, accompanied by payment of the annual government fee. ESR-1 Economic Substance Return: within 12 months of the financial year end. No provisional or quarterly CIT installments apply to an IC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa's Income Tax Act 2012 formally embeds the arm's length principle but prescribes no published Local File/Master File monetary threshold. The Samoan Revenue applies a de facto administrative review focus on cross-border related-party turnover of approximately WST 10 million or more per annum. A tax-exempt IC is not statutorily required to prepare transfer pricing documentation, but must maintain contemporaneous charter-hire rate cards, independent marine-charter benchmark reports, commercial contracts, and operating cost records at the registered agent's offices in Samoa to substantiate arm's length charter rates should the exemption be challenged.

Economic substance scope: The Samoa International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 and Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply to any IC carrying on a relevant activity. Offshore supply vessel leasing falls within 'shipping' (the definition covers vessel chartering and hire); where bareboat leasing without crew or operational control is adopted, the activity may be characterized instead as 'financing and leasing'. Both classifications are designated relevant activities. The IC must therefore prove economic substance in Samoa: (1) management and control exercised in Samoa through board meetings and strategic charter decisions, (2) physical premises and a substantive registered office, (3) adequate Samoan employees proportionate to the chartering administrative workload, (4) minimum Samoan operating expenditure, and (5) performance of core income-generating activities in Samoa, namely vessel scheduling, charter negotiation, crew management, invoicing, and maintenance procurement. Compliance is documented in the annual ESR-1 return filed with SIFA; failure to satisfy the test triggers escalating penalties, forfeiture of tax-exempt status, and deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Exploitation of IC Tax Exemption

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all diverted charter profits at 25% corporate income tax plus a 20% late payment penalty, an evasion penalty of up to 200% of the tax avoided, and running interest; criminal prosecution before the Supreme Court of Samoa with fines up to approximately WST 200,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act 2012.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false annual returns or intentionally directed the sham are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax via anti-avoidance and veil-piercing orders; courts may impose personal asset freezes, travel bans restricting departure from Samoa, and custodial sentences on de facto controlling shareholders.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Escalating civil penalties under the Economic Substance Regulations, including a daily penalty for continuing non-compliance and cumulative fines approaching WST 130,000; sustained failure leads to suspension of the IC certificate, compulsory strike-off, retroactive revocation of tax-exempt status, and mandatory notification to the Samoan Revenue Authority for tax recovery.

Director personal liability: Directors face up to 2 years' disqualification from managing any Samoa-registered entity, personal financial penalties, and court-ordered winding-up with asset restitution to creditors; the registered agent may lawfully resign, and resigning agents must notify the Registrar, exposing the company to immediate deregistration risk.
AML/CFT Violation in Maritime Charter Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018 and AML/CFT Amendment Act 2018, money laundering offences expose a body corporate to fines up to approximately WST 1,000,000 and individuals to fines up to WST 100,000 or imprisonment up to 10 years, or both; the Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze the IC's Samoan bank accounts; the court may confiscate charter income equal to the full value of the laundered proceeds.

Director personal liability: AML obligations extend to directors and beneficial owners: approving charter payments without KYC/UBO verification, authorizing escrow splits designed to route cash into exempt channels, or concealing sanctions-linked counterparties triggers personal criminal liability, travel bans and departure restriction orders, asset freezing, and extradition coordination with the vessel's flag state.
Prohibited Local Trading and Derogation of IC Status

Statutory sanction: The prohibited transaction is void and the IC loses its exempt character: the entire charter hire is assessed at 25% corporate income tax with back assessments covering up to 5 prior years, plus late payment penalties and interest; unlicensed domestic trade is an offense carrying fines of up to approximately WST 50,000 plus a daily continuing fine, and the Registrar may dissolve the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors who sanction local trading without converting the entity bear joint and several personal liability under Samoa's Companies Act 2001; courts may grant civil restitution orders, issue travel bans preventing directors from leaving Samoa until the assessed taxes are secured, and convict directors as principals for the IC's unlicensed maritime operations.
