Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. Under the International Companies Act 1987 and related revenue statutes, a Samoa International Company conducting offshore business is generally outside Samoa income tax and exchange-control restrictions; activities sourced in Samoa remain taxable.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available. The exemption is automatic for an International Company that complies with the offshore business limitation in its constitutional documents. A foreign entity may alternatively operate through a Samoa branch of a non-resident company, which requires separate branch registration and does not preclude taxation on Samoa-source income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAGST registration applies when the value of taxable supplies made in Samoa exceeds WST 60,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted for persons who make exclusively zero-rated supplies, including exporters of dairy commodities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimable for exports of raw whole and skimmed milk powder where: (i) the goods are physically exported to a destination outside Samoa; (ii) the export is made under a contract with a non-resident buyer; (iii) shipment occurs within a reasonable period, normally not exceeding 28 days after sale; and (iv) the consideration is paid in foreign currency to a Samoa licensed bank. The exporter must retain contemporaneous evidence evidencing the actual movement of goods and receipt of proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec) endorsed by the Samoa Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the foreign destination and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed bank confirming foreign-currency repatriation
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed packing list
  • Original sale contract, purchase order or letter of credit evidencing the export transaction

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based transaction reporting, or digital stamping requirement is currently in force. VAGST tax invoices must be issued within 28 days of supply and retained for seven years. Returns are filed through the Ministry for Revenue e-services portal on a monthly or bi-monthly filing cycle; no pre-clearance of invoices is required.

The Ministry for Revenue audits export zero-rating claims and may require production of the full documentary bundle plus bank statements and agent reports. If export documentation is insufficient or the goods never leave Samoa, the zero-rating will be reversed and VAGST at 15% assessed on the consideration, together with late payment penalties, default interest, and civil fines. Deliberate false or fraudulent zero-rating claims may attract enhanced financial penalties, clawback of any previously refunded VAGST, and criminal exposure for the directors and registered agent under the VAGST Act and the Criminal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is freely convertible for all current international transactions; capital-account transactions require CBS approval for domestic resident entities. Samoan International Companies (ICs) operate as non-resident, tax-exempt entities under the International Companies Act 1987 and are effectively outside the CBS exchange-control net. They may maintain and settle entirely in foreign currency, with no statutory requirement to convert WST for trade payments. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation from an IC are unrestricted; however, any dividend derived from a Samoa-source domestic trading presence would be subject to the applicable Samoa tax treaty and CBS capital-transfer notification. A de facto no-approval regime applies to ICs when funds are routed through licensed commercial banks in foreign currency and are not converted into local WST.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Samoa International Companies, mandatory export-proceeds surrender rules are disapplied because the entity is non-resident and holds an Exchange Control Exemption Certificate under the International Companies regime. Export receipts in USD, EUR, AUD, or NZD may be retained offshore and utilized directly for trade settlements. Should the IC maintain a local WST account or convert any export receipt into tala through an authorised dealer, the transaction is treated as an ordinary spot conversion under the IC's global foreign-currency license and no surrender ratio applies. A domestic-resident trading entity engaging in milk-powder shipments into Samoa would technically fall under the CBS Exchange Control Regulations, requiring repatriation of export proceeds within the prescribed statutory window and conversion through an authorised dealer, but the WS_IC structure is structured to avoid this exposure.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Apia, Samoa

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs). The Central Bank of Samoa does not grant full banking licenses to foreign fintechs, and no offshore EMI possesses a Samoa financial institution licence. A Samoa International Company can use an EMI domiciled in the UK, EU, or Australia solely as a non-bank payment processor for outbound trade transactions, but this is not a substitute for a licensed corporate bank account. The ECB/Financial Conduct Authority-licensed EMI may provide EUR/USD IBANs and MT103-equivalent clearing, but any touchpoint with WST or a Samoa domestic clearing rail is prohibited without a CBS license. Legal constraints include inability to accept inbound capital injection via an EMI without exposing the IC to de-risking, and most tier-one banks treat EMIs as a trigger for enhanced due diligence. Therefore, the IC's primary treasury must be a licensed commercial bank, with EMIs used only as auxiliary collection points for small counterparty prepayments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection is executed by a SWIFT MT103 transfer from the beneficial owner's or corporate parent's bank account to the IC's capital-escrow account at a licensed Samoan bank. The wire must be denominated in USD, EUR, AUD, or NZD, and the purpose code must clearly state 'Share Capital Subscription' or 'Paid-up Capital for International Company' so the receiving bank can classify the flow as equity and not as a loan or trade payment. The remitting bank must include the IC's full name, Samoa registered agent address, and the specific escrow account number. The IC must not receive capital by cash, digital-asset transfer, or from an unrelated third-party payer without a notarized declaration of donor and source of funds. After verification of the source of funds, the bank will release the escrowed capital into the operational account; no CBS prior approval is required for the IC because it holds a foreign-currency exemption.Outbound trade disbursements for raw milk powder and dairy commodity settlement must be executed through SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers and through a commercial letter of credit opened via the Samoan bank's trade desk. For LC-based settlements, the standard rail is SWIFT MT700 followed by MT710/MT720 as applicable, with the IC's bank acting as issuing bank and the seller's bank in New Zealand, Australia, or the EU as confirming bank. Wires for actual trade payments must include an accurate purpose code such as 'Trade Settlement - Import/Export of Dairy Commodity' and must match the underlying invoices and customs declarations. For transactions within Samoa, the domestic clearing house operated by the CBS handles same-day WST clearing; however, the IC's natural clearing rails are offshore, and all payments should be made in the currency of the underlying contract. It is critical that the IC never allows payment reversal or refunds to be made from the same operational account without a corresponding board-approved commercial resolution and a new compliant SWIFT message referencing the original transaction.Plain vanilla forward contracts for USD/NZD and USD/WST with an authorised Samoan commercial bank or an APAC offshore counterparty, where the underlying exposure is a committed milk-powder trade invoice.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/WST booked offshore through a licensed international bank, settling in USD, since the tala is only lightly traded onshore and physical deliverability of WST is not available to non-resident ICs.Cross-currency swaps between USD and AUD or NZD for matching medium-term supply contracts, provided the swap is documented under ISDA 2002 and the bank has performed a derivatives suitability assessment.Synthetic hedging through dual-currency deposits or time deposits in the contracted payment currency to lock in cash-flow values without requiring a listed exchange instrument.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for resident companies (Income Tax Act 2015); 0% for International Companies on foreign-source trading income pursuant to the exemption provisions of the International Companies Act 1987 (ICA 1987).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory rate on dividends paid by resident Samoan trading companies; 0% for a WS_IC because the ICA 1987 exempts International Company distributions to non-resident shareholders from all Samoan income tax.

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has not codified a statutory local-file threshold; SRA applies the arm's-length principle via general anti-avoidance rules. Prudent benchmark: prepare and retain a Local File and Master File where annual related-party turnover from milk powder trading exceeds WST 5,000,000 (approximately USD 1,800,000), with documentation retained for six years.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not administer a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime; however, OECD/EU good-governance standards and Samoa's exchange-of-information obligations require the WS_IC to demonstrate genuine economic substance for its dairy trade (physical or contractual arrangement, trade finance decisions, retained risk, and adequate capacity), failing which the IC may be recharacterized as a shell and its exemption denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of up to 100% of tax evaded plus 2% monthly interest; on conviction, fines up to SAT 100,000 and imprisonment up to seven (7) years; deregistration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are personally liable for civil restitution, subject to travel bans, and liable to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of profits as Samoan-source taxable income at 27%; penalty of 150% to 200% of the shortfall; strike-off by SIFA; exchange of confidential information with treaty partners.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil: directors and the registered agent face joint civil liability, disqualification, and departure restrictions.
AML / Counter-Terrorism Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to SAT 1,000,000 per breach under Samoa's anti-money laundering legislation; asset freezing and confiscation orders; imprisonment up to ten (10) years.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners bear personal criminal liability, travel restrictions, and confiscation of proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / False Documentation

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment with interest; penalties of 50% to 200% of underpaid tax; invalidation of the ICA 1987 exemption for the affected year.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or sign false trade documentation are personally liable for the adjustment, disgorgement of dividends, and entry bans of up to five (5) years.
