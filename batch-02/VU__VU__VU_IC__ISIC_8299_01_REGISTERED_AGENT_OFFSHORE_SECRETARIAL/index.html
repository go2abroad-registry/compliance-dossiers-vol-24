Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila; e-services portal: DCIR e-Tax (electronic VAT filing) at https://www.dcir.gov.vu; corporate registry and licensing: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) online registry.

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) is not required to procure a TIN or VAT ID because it is statutorily exempt from all income and asset taxation and is prohibited from carrying on business with Vanuatu residents. Incorporation is effected by filing the Memorandum and Articles with the VFSC under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]; the IC receives a Certificate of Incorporation and an annual Good Standing Certificate evidencing exemption. Should any taxable local activity be mistakenly contemplated, VAT registration would require filing Form VAT-02 with the DCIR together with proof of identity, business address, and projected turnover; however such registration is incompatible with the IC licensing conditions and would trigger review of the IC's exempt status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box, election-out, or hybrid-mismatch elections exist because Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no withholding tax. The IC may voluntarily obtain an Exemption Certificate under Section 50 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222] as definitive evidence of tax-exempt status; the exemption does not extend to VAT embedded in imported or locally procured goods and services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 10,000,000 annual taxable turnover (compulsory registration); voluntary registration is available below this threshold but is not applicable to an IC engaged in offshore-only services

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Vanuatu and services supplied to non-resident clients are zero-rated under Section 17 of the VAT Act where the supplier holds a valid VAT registration and retains contemporaneous documentary evidence of the offshore supply. For an International Company rendering registered-agent and offshore secretarial services to non-residents, the services are outside the scope of VAT entirely and are immune from all Vanuatu taxation under Section 50 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222], rendering a formal zero-rating claim unnecessary.

  • Customs Export Declaration (approved by DCIR/Vanuatu Customs) — for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — for physical goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 credit advice evidencing payment from a non-resident account
  • Signed offshore service agreement and a certificate or register extract evidencing the client's non-resident status — for services

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based stamping, or digital invoice encryption regime is in force. VAT returns must be filed electronically through the DCIR e-Tax portal on a monthly basis within 30 days of month-end; VAT invoices must contain the mandatory particulars under Section 45 of the VAT Act (supplier name and TIN, customer name, invoice number, date, description, VAT amount and rate). Offshore export services are zero-rated without any electronic clearance pre-authorisation, but supporting export evidence must be retained.

Vanuatu imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day convention is a prudent internal treasury benchmark aligned with bank AML/KYC verification and the IC's offshore non-resident nexus. Fraudulent zero-rating claims or VAT refund overstatements expose the claimant to civil penalties under Section 70 of the VAT Act (up to 40% of the tax understated plus default interest), criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion under Section 71 (fine up to VUV 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years), retrospective VAT assessments, and clawback of any refund erroneously paid with interest. For ICs, carrying on business with Vanuatu residents, failing to maintain a licensed registered agent, or breaching the VFSC licensing conditions voids the Section 50 exemption, subjects all past offshore income to potential recharacterisation, and incurs VFSC licence revocation, director disqualification, and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is fully convertible for current-account items (secretarial fees, dividends, trade invoices) and de facto open for capital-account flows involving non-resident International Companies. Vanuatu accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; therefore no prior RBV approval is required for ordinary profit remittances or dividend wires. The only formal control is the authorized-dealer framework: commercial banks must obtain a completed Exchange Control Form and supporting service agreement for any non-resident payment or receipt exceeding VUV 10,000,000 equivalent. An International Company treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes faces zero conversion ratio, zero retention limits, and zero remittance tax. Operational friction arises only where the underlying registered-agent receivable lacks documented commercial substance, in which case the remitting bank may hold the wire pending UBO-level source-of-funds verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no export-proceeds surrender regime: there is no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no designated collections account requirement. A VU_IC may lawfully retain 100% of exported services proceeds (secretarial retainers invoiced in VUV, USD, AUD or EUR) in any jurisdiction, onshore or offshore, without RBV conversion orders. The sole administrative duty is the lodgement of the prescribed Exchange Control Form with the accommodating domestic bank when aggregate receipts exceed VUV 10,000,000, supported by the underlying commercial contracts. This liberal regime enables the IC to warehouse FX liquidity in multi-currency settlement accounts until optimal conversion timing, and stands in deliberate contrast to Asian surrender regimes (e.g., China's 30-90 day repatriation and conversion rules).

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), exercising delegated exchange-control powers under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 106], with banking supervision conducted under the International Banking Act [Cap. 251] and the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 125]. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) separately licences the International Company and its registered-agent/company-secretarial business (ISIC 8299), while RBV and VFSC jointly implement AML/CFT obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act (as amended, 2024).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability with material legal constraint. Vanuatu-domiciled International Companies are consistently declined by offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie, Dukascopy, Revolut Business) because Vanuatu's historic EU AML/CFT Annex II placement and 2020-2023 FATF grey-listing remain embedded in the country-risk engines of those PSPs. The VU_IC's ISIC 8299 classification (registered agent/company secretarial) further triggers automatic enhanced-due-diligence decline under most EMI conduct rules. Structurally, a Vanuatu IC cannot itself hold an FCA or equivalent EMI licence, and interposing a Singapore or BVI operating company to access an EMI creates a conduit risk that destabilizes the primary correspondent banking relationship. Recommended architecture: maintain primary liquidity at ANZ/BRED and use any EMI strictly as a low-value USD expense-payment rail with sub-VUV-10M monthly throughput, coupled with a complete audit trail of the underlying ultimate-beneficiary service contracts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended compliant protocol: the shareholder wires capital above VUV 10,000,000 equivalent via SWIFT MT103 to the ANZ/BRED correspondent chain (ANZ Melbourne or BNP Paris), with Field 70 reciting 'Capital Injection - International Company, VFSC licence no. [XXX], non-resident IC' and Field 57a naming the receiving Vanuatu branch and its BIC. The bank's exchange-control unit verifies (i) the board resolution authorizing share issuance under the International Companies Act; (ii) the source-of-funds letter and three-month bank statements of the remitting shareholder; (iii) the post-allotment register of members; and (iv) the UBO register. Only then is the credit posted to the multi-currency operational account. If the credit is VUV-denominated, the bank converts at its dealing rate with no surrender obligation, and funds are immediately available for onward USD/AUD/EUR settlement. Intercompany loans use the same routing but must be supported by a perfected written loan agreement to prevent recharacterization as equity; Vanuatu imposes no stamp duty on such loan documentation.Outbound clearing runs exclusively over correspondent banking rails, as Vanuatu has no domestic RTGS/CHIPS equivalent and no CLS settlement membership. The hierarchical structure is: (a) SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV for unsecured service payments and dividend remittances, accompanied by the Exchange Control Form where the amount exceeds VUV 10,000,000; (b) Commercial Letters of Credit issued by BRED or ANZ under UCP 600 for structured trade payables, with 60-120 day usance and pre-shipment financing up to 80% of invoice value; (c) MT700 advice of a confirmed LC drawn in favour of the VU_IC where a client contractually requires LC collateralization of secretarial retainer receivables. The bank enforces dual-signatory mandates above USD 50,000 (or VUV 6,000,000 equivalent) and maintains a 48-hour dealing-room pricing window for any FX conversion embedded in the outward wire.Onshore deliverable forward (VUV/USD and VUV/AUD) offered by ANZ and BRED dealing desks for maturities up to 6 months, subject to authorized-dealer limits and a documented underlying registered-agent receivable; no RBV approval letter is required for non-resident ICs where the notional per tranche is below VUV 10,000,000.Offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) referencing the USD/VUV cross rate, concluded with an Australian or Singapore regulated broker and cash-settled offshore in USD; lawful because settlement never touches the Vanuatu payment system, and the contract should exclude Vanuatu governing law to avoid onshore FX-licensing ambiguity.Cross-currency swap (USD/EUR or USD/AUD) with an OECD-domiciled counterparty for converting the multi-currency liquidity legs of the operational account; structured outside Vanuatu books and with no VUV leg, thereby avoiding RBV exchange-control consent entirely.Natural hedging via strategic multi-currency accounts (VUV, USD, AUD, EUR) with a target composition matching the practice's invoice currency mix (typically 60% USD, 30% AUD, 10% EUR); the IC executes a rolling 90-day conversion ladder as spot transactions on the ANZ/BRED dealing desk.VUV-leg cross-currency swap - de facto impermissible: it requires prior consent of the Minister of Finance under the Exchange Control Act and is virtually never granted to non-resident International Companies; the documented alternative is the offshore NDF structure described above.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation or threshold is prescribed in Vanuatu; no Local File or Master File requirement exists. Transactions are not adjusted for Vanuatu tax purposes.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not apply Economic Substance Regulations comparable to other OFCs; however an International Company must maintain at least one director, a licensed registered agent, and a registered office in Vanuatu, and the VFSC expects real management and administration to be located in the jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Foreign Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under the Penal Code (Cap. 135) and VFSC enforcement powers: criminal fines up to VUV 10,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years; the company's registration may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate face personal criminal liability, international travel bans, and court-ordered restitution of laundered or evaded sums.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative strike-off and tax-exempt status withdrawal; VUV 1,000,000 penalty imposed by the VFSC; prohibition from re-registration.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's debts and may be disqualified from acting as directors for up to 5 years.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to VUV 15,000,000 and imprisonment up to 20 years; assets linked to money laundering are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the registered agent face personal prosecution; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed pending resolution.
Beneficial Ownership Transparency Breach

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty up to VUV 5,000,000; the company may be struck off and directors barred from managing any Vanuatu entity.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the penalty and subject to up to 2 years imprisonment for knowingly filing false declarations.
