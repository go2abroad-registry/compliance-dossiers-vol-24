Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue, Government of Samoa; official e-services portal: Samoa Ministry of Revenue E-Filing system at https://www.samoatax.gov.ws / https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by filing the prescribed TIN Application Form with the Ministry of Revenue, accompanied by a certificate of incorporation, international company status evidence, memorandum and articles of association, beneficial-owner identification, and registered address. For an International Company conducting only offshore business, no Samoa TIN is mandated solely for income tax, but VAGST registration is required before making taxable supplies in Samoa or importing taxable goods, and the same TIN is then used for VAGST, excise, and customs purposes. Non-resident applicants must appoint a Samoa-based tax agent or local contact person.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not provide the U.S. check-the-box or similar hybrid-mismatch election. An International Company may elect to convert to a Samoa domestic company by special resolution under the International Companies Act 1987 and notification to the Registrar of International Companies, thereby relinquishing its offshore tax-exempt status and becoming subject to ordinary Samoa income tax and VAGST rules. No further deemed classification election is available; anti-avoidance provisions apply to artificial hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 2,000,000 (Samoa tala) annual taxable turnover; compulsory registration also applies to any business importing taxable goods into Samoa for commercial purposes, regardless of turnover, and to non-resident suppliers mandated to collect VAGST on prescribed digital and local supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of goods that are exported by the registered person from Samoa to a destination outside Samoa within the statutory export time frame, to goods supplied under duty-free/excise-approved arrangements, and to services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa if the services are wholly received and enjoyed outside Samoa. The exporter must maintain evidence that the goods left Samoa and the supply was made in the course of a taxable business; otherwise the standard 15% VAGST rate applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoan Customs Service approved clearance)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Samoa
  • Commercial invoice and supplier's VAGST tax invoice
  • Packing list and cargo manifest
  • Sales contract or purchase order by overseas buyer
  • Certificate of origin where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence from overseas counterparty

Samoa currently does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or government-API invoice validation. Registered suppliers must issue a valid VAGST tax invoice showing supplier name, TIN/VAGST number, invoice serial number, date, description of goods/services, taxable amount exclusive of VAGST, the VAGST amount and the total. VAGST returns are filed electronically or manually with the Ministry of Revenue and remittance is due within the statutory return period. The Ministry is gradually implementing integrated e-tools, but no API-based clearance is yet legally mandated.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to post-clearance documentary audit by the Ministry of Revenue and Samoa Customs. If the exporter cannot substantiate physical export, approved foreign-receipt status, or the absence of on-island consumption, the VAGST is clawed back at the standard 15% rate, plus late payment interest, late filing penalties, and a civil penalty typically not exceeding 200% of the evaded tax. Wilful fraudulent export zero-rating or false inward remittance documentation may lead to criminal prosecution, fines, directors' personal liability, and deregistration from the VAGST register.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is convertible for current account transactions (trade payments, services, dividends, interest) subject to standard documentary verification and tax clearance. Capital account transactions, including direct investment capital injections, portfolio investments, and offshore loans, require prior approval by the Central Bank of Samoa unless conducted by a licensed International Company with a specific exemption. Profit remittance (dividends) is facilitated via authorized dealer banks upon submission of audited financial statements, BOARD resolution, and tax clearance certificates; there are no withholding tax hurdles for International Companies, but foreign remittance fees and CBS transaction reporting apply. The practical convertibility risk is low for retail volumes, but the market is thin, and large one-off transfers may be settled on a T+2 basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa’s Exchange Control Act requires all export proceeds to be received through an authorized dealer in Samoa within 30 days from the date of shipment and to be surrendered or credited to a Samoan bank account in WST unless an exemption or retention of foreign currency account is approved by the Central Bank of Samoa. For an International Company engaged in re-export or wholesale of imported goods, conversion of export receipts into WST is mandatory for the fulcrum of the sale, with the standard ratio being 100% conversion within the statutory timeline; however, CBS may permit offshore-retained export earnings if the company demonstrates operational necessity and prior written consent is obtained.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) – the unified monetary authority and exchange control administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1987 and the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low – Offshore digital EMI/FINTECH service providers are not licensed or recognized by the Central Bank of Samoa for local merchant settlement or treasury operations. The Central Bank does not currently maintain a sandbox for inbound cross-border EMI acceptance; any non-bank payment service provider must obtain a Payment Services Provider license under the Financial Institutions Act 2011. No regulated offshore EMI connectivity (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) can provide Samoan WST clearing or direct local POS/Interac gateway access. Such services may be used only for ancillary remote supplier payments, subject to individual bank approval and not as a primary treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Samoa International Company may be injected via authorized dealer banks using SWIFT MT103 with payment purpose code 'EC' (Equity Capital) or 'DE' (Direct Investment Loan). The routing protocol is: (i) the company's non-resident parent/beneficial owner initiates a wire from a regulated financial institution (e.g., Singapore, Hong Kong, Australia) to the Samoa operational account at ANZ or BSP in WST or USD; (ii) the recipient bank will require a Foreign Investment Certificate (FIC) from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour, a certified board resolution authorizing the capital injection, and a worksheet evidencing the source of funds; (iii) if the transfer exceeds WST 50,000 equivalent, CBS prior approval is required unless the company holds a blanket exemption from the exchange control regulations; (iv) an 'A2' remittance form or electronic declaration must be completed, and the bank will issue a credit advice with the unique FX identification number.Outbound trade settlements for imported Asian condiments and vegetables must be executed exclusively through authorized dealer banks using the SWIFT MT103 (direct credit transfer) or MT202 (for interbank cover). For large, recurring supplier transactions exceeding USD 250,000 per month, a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) with deferred payment terms is the indemnified clearing rail. All outward remittances must be supported by the supplier proforma invoice, customs import permits, and an exchange control form TR1. The clearing timeline is typically T+1 for WST/USD conversions via Samoa's Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) domestic leg, with an international next-day processing window.Outright forward foreign exchange contract with a maturity not exceeding 12 months, approved on a case-by-case basis by the Central Bank of Samoa and executed with a licensed commercial bank (ANZ, BSP, SCB) on a non-speculative, underlying-transaction basis.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) offered offshore by major global banks referencing WST, but subject to domestic disallowance – formal CBS approval is required before the Samoan entity may enter, and the instrument must be settled only in USD via a wholly offshore corridor with no domestic WST conversion.Cross-currency swap (WST/USD or WST/AUD) with a licensed bank, provided the swap is fully covered by a defined trade receivable or payable stream, approved by CBS, and documented under ISDA 2002 with the Samoan entity as a rated or collateral-backed counterparty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory Local File/Master File is triggered for a tax-exempt International Company; where an IC nevertheless engages in related-party cross-border trade, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation should be retained once annual related-party transaction value equals or exceeds WST 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has adopted Economic Substance Regulations applicable to international companies and offshore companies carrying on geographically mobile activities (banking, insurance, shipping, pure holding, IP, and headquarter services). Physical grocery retail distribution operating in Samoa is a local substantive activity and is not classified as a pure foreign-sourced IP business; nevertheless, all ICs must file an annual economic substance declaration with the Registrar where any deemed activity occurs, demonstrating real management, physical premises, local staff, and operating expenditure in Samoa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Sham Transactions

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 200% of tax evaded, a fine not exceeding WST 500,000, and imprisonment up to 7 years under the Income Tax Administration Act 2012

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for undeclared tax and penalties; travel bans and asset freezing orders may be imposed pending assessment.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Registrar cancellation and striking off of the IC, daily penalty of WST 200 until removal, and restoration fee of WST 5,000 plus all arrears

Director personal liability: Continued trading after striking off renders directors personally liable for corporate debts; directors may be subject to a 5-year disqualification from managing any Samoan company.
AML/CFT and Unlicensed Retail Banking

Statutory sanction: Under the Samoan AML/CFT Act 2007, penalties may reach WST 1,000,000 plus civil forfeiture of proceeds; unauthorised financial business may also trigger liquidation proceedings

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced where the company is found to be a mere cloak for individual wrongdoing; responsible directors personally face fines, restitution orders, travel restrictions, and imprisonment up to 10 years.
Failing the Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of up to WST 50,000 per year per infraction under Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations, plus reassessment of income tax at the full 27% rate and applicable late payment surcharge

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the additional tax if they signed erroneous substance declarations or directed concealment; a history of repeated violations exposes directors to disqualification and removal from the Samoan directors register.
