Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa — tax administration and online services portal (MfR e-Services / Lafeta)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not otherwise carrying on a taxable Samoa activity is not required to hold a Samoan TIN. However, a non-resident supplier that meets the VAGST registration threshold must apply to the Ministry for Revenue using Form TIN/VAGST Registration, submit its certificate of incorporation, the foreign entity's constitutional and registry documents, and a letter of intent. The Ministry assigns a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and, if applicable, a VAGST registration number. A Samoa International Company that remains within the statutory tax-exempt regime does not file an income tax return and does not undertake VAGST registration unless it makes a taxable supply in Samoa; where exemption is desired in documentary form, an application for a Certificate of Tax Exemption may be made under the International Companies Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate an elective check-the-box classification for foreign purposes. A Samoa International Company is tax-exempt by statutory default under the International Companies Act, and no hybrid mismatch or entity classification election is available before Samoa's Ministry for Revenue. If the International Company elects to carry on business within Samoa or to obtain Samoan-source income outside the exempt perimeter, it thereby self-assumes ordinary company tax treatment and becomes registrable for VAGST and income tax; no other elective mechanism exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available in Samoa for goods that are exported from Samoa to a destination outside Samoa, provided the goods are removed from Samoa in accordance with the Customs Act and an approved export entry is lodged. Services supplied by a Samoan entity are zero-rated when they are supplied to a recipient outside Samoa and are physically performed outside Samoa, or when the services are directly related to exported goods under the VAGST rules. An offshore 3PL and order-fulfillment hub operated by a Samoa International Company may zero-rate its paid services to non-resident e-commerce sellers if the service recipient is non-resident, the services relate to goods located outside Samoa, or the services are provided solely to the non-resident party with no local consumption or benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory real-time electronic-invoicing clearance, API filing, or fiscal digital stamping regime applies as of the current Samoa tax administration. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue a tax invoice containing the prescribed particulars under the Tax Administration Act and keep the underlying records accessible to the Ministry for Revenue. VAGST returns are filed electronically through the MfR online portal.

Samoa Customs and the Ministry for Revenue may audit zero-rated export claims retrospectively. If an export zero-rating is unsupported by the prescribed export documentation or the sales proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the claim is clawed back in full with VAGST assessed at 15%, plus late payment interest and a shortfall penalty under the Tax Administration Act. Fraudulent zero-rating claims carry criminal penalties including monetary fines and, for bodies corporate, fines on the entity and on each director or officer; in serious cases the court may impose a custodial sentence on responsible officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a managed float. Samoa has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, so current-account transactions (trade in goods/services, operational income, business profits, dividends) may be settled through authorized dealers without discriminatory currency restrictions, subject to standard supporting documentation. However, WST is thinly traded and non-deliverable offshore: there is no unrestricted offshore interbank WST market, and all physical WST conversion occurs within Samoa or via limited correspondent lines. Capital-account transactions (shareholder equity injections above threshold, intercompany loans from non-residents, portfolio and direct investments, and repatriation of capital) require prior Central Bank approval or a 'no objection' confirmation from an authorized dealer under delegated authority. For a Samoa International Company (IC) operating exclusively offshore under the International Companies Act 1987, the Central Bank has granted exchange-control exemptions for external accounts: the WS_IC may hold, receive, and disburse USD and other foreign currencies without WST conversion restrictions, provided no transactions occur with Samoa residents, no business is conducted inside Samoa, and no liability is denominated in WST. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation from the IC are not subject to Samoan withholding tax or exchange-control encumbrances, but the underlying transfer must be documented as a legitimate current-account payment through an authorized dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For onshore Samoan exporters, the Exchange Control Regulations 1976 require export proceeds to be remitted to an authorized dealer in Samoa within 31 days of shipment or the due date of payment, and the proceeds must generally be converted to WST unless the dealer holds a standing foreign-currency account approval for export retention. These surrender and mandatory conversion rules do NOT apply to the WS_IC because it is classified as an external/international company under the International Companies Act 1987: all export-style receipts from e-commerce platform settlements and cross-border trade may be retained in USD without WST conversion and without surrender deadlines. If any incidental WST credit arises on the IC account (e.g., refund from a local utility or erroneous inbound transfer), the funds must be converted to USD without delay and a written explanation provided to the authorized dealer.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 2002; foreign exchange administration is delegated under the Exchange Control Act 1975, the Exchange Control Regulations 1976, and the Central Bank's Exchange Control Guidelines and Authorized Dealer Notices

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability as a primary on-island banking relationship. Airwallex, Wise, OFX, and similar offshore digital EMIs do not hold any Samoa banking or e-money license and cannot directly clear into the Samoa Interbank Network System (SINS) or maintain a settlement account with the Central Bank of Samoa. The WS_IC may use Airwallex as a supplementary cross-border payout rail for e-commerce disbursements, provided that (i) funds are staged in a tier-1 Samoan bank account, (ii) the EMI holds a valid licence and demonstrates a clean regulatory record in its home jurisdiction, (iii) the EMI onboarding accepts the Samoa IC's beneficial ownership documentation, and (iv) the Central Bank's authorized-dealer touchpoint rule is satisfied: every cross-border movement of funds must trace back to an authorized Samoan bank transfer. Crypto-backed or unlicensed digital EMIs, and any EMI that cannot produce a full audit trail of source of funds, are prohibited.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity through nominee shareholders, bearer shares, or an offshore family trust holding the Samoa IC; banks face downgrade pressure from APG (Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering) and correspondent bank de-risking

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity through nominee shareholders, bearer shares, or an offshore family trust holding the Samoa IC; banks face downgrade pressure from APG (Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering) and correspondent bank de-risking

Defense evidence: Notarized beneficial ownership register extract or CBS-compliant statutory declaration identifying every ultimate beneficial owner (full legal name, date of birth, nationality, residential address); full trust deed, deed of amendment, and trustee resolution if the shareholder is a trust; registered-agent confirmation letter under the International Companies Act 1987; bank reference letters from the jurisdiction of incorporation of the parent; and a written economic substance narrative explaining the cross-border 3PL/fullfilment business, with client contracts, warehouse lease agreements, and inventory records demonstrating real commercial activity outside Samoa.
High-volume, small-ticket payment flows from e-commerce platforms (e.g., Amazon, Shopify, Temu, TikTok Shop) structured in a manner resembling an unlicensed money service business, remittance agent, or payment aggregator

Root cause: High-volume, small-ticket payment flows from e-commerce platforms (e.g., Amazon, Shopify, Temu, TikTok Shop) structured in a manner resembling an unlicensed money service business, remittance agent, or payment aggregator

Defense evidence: Executed marketplace or fulfilment-services agreements; payout statements from the platform showing order-level reference data; a value-chain description (buyer -> platform -> WS_IC -> logistics providers, freight forwarders, and last-mile carriers); management accounts and bank statements showing that inflows match fulfilment fees and outbound disbursements; and a written undertaking that the IC is not a 'money or value transfer service' under the Samoa AML/CFT Act 2007, but a warehouse operator and order-fulfilment agent classified under ISIC 5210/NAICS 493110.
Third-party and split payments from Chinese, HK, US, or EU counterparties where invoice payee differs from the beneficial recipient, generating sanctions-evasion and fraudulent-transaction red flags

Root cause: Third-party and split payments from Chinese, HK, US, or EU counterparties where invoice payee differs from the beneficial recipient, generating sanctions-evasion and fraudulent-transaction red flags

Defense evidence: Complete supplier and counterparty registry with corporate KYC documents (certificate of incorporation, tax ID, director ID, licence); commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and customs entry for each disbursement; a third-party payment authorization letter ('letter of permission') signed by both the contracting party and the payee; internal sanctions-screening report run against OFAC, UN, EU, and Samoa Consolidated List; and transaction rationale memoranda maintained for 7 years for examiner review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent or shareholder capital injection is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the originating tax-advantaged jurisdiction through a tier-1 Samoan authorized dealer. Samoa does not use standardized transaction purpose codes (e.g., Chinese-style SAFE codes); the wire narrative functions as the purpose declaration and must state clearly: 'INTERNATIONAL COMPANY EQUITY INJECTION – ISIC 5210 CROSS-DOCK FULFILMENT HUB WORKING CAPITAL'. The authorized dealer requires: certificate of incorporation and registration; board resolution authorizing the bank filing and the share allotment; share subscription/allotment documents; a source-of-funds statement (6-month bank statements or capital statement from the remitting entity); and confirmation that the injection is not derived from a restricted jurisdiction. If the capital originates from a PRC-related group entity, the remitting party must complete all SAFE outbound investment filings in China before submission to the Samoan bank. CBS prior approval is not required for an external IC account, but the bank must complete its 24-hour monitoring report as a 'high-value inward transfer'.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for high-value, time-critical trade settlements, using ANZ Bank (Samoa)'s Australian correspondent line or Bank of South Pacific (Samoa)'s regional Pacific/NZ lines with same-day or next-day value dating. For heavy procurement and cross-dock capital expenditure, the secondary rail is the Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (UCP600), issued by ANZ/BSP Samoa in USD, payable at sight to suppliers and logistics vendors; standby LCs may be used to secure warehouse lease obligations. Local_ACH (SINS) is available only for Samoan-resident counterparties, which is generally irrelevant to this offshore 3PL structure. Bulk recurring payouts to multiple overseas subcontractors should be batched through bank-hosted host-to-host payment files or via an approved EMI (Airwallex) layered on top of the authorized-dealer account. The WS_IC should maintain multiple USD accounts at two tier-1 banks to ensure clearing redundancy and avoid single-counterparty liquidity risk.Outright FX Forwards: Deliverable USD/WST forward contracts transacted with ANZ Bank (Samoa) or BSP Samoa only, supported by a documented underlying trade exposure; authorized-dealer approval is delegated for trade-backed forwards up to 12 months tenor; used exclusively to hedge any incidental Samoan-dollar operating costs or local regulatory payments.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): Offshore USD/WST NDFs booked through top-tier international banks (Australia/NZ based) referencing the Samoan Tala as a restricted-delivery currency; permitted for the WS_IC where the hedge is cash-settled offshore and no WST account conversion occurs; requires a written hedge rationale and matching of the USD exposure under the IC's treasury policy.Cross-Currency Swaps and Plain Vanilla FX Swaps: USD/AUD, USD/NZD, or USD/EUR cross-currency swaps and rolling FX swaps are permissible for intercompany funding and temporary liquidity management through the authorized dealer, subject to CBS prior authorization for intercompany-loan hedging; WST-denominated cross-currency swaps are not viable in the absence of an offshore deliverable WST market and should be avoided.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% standard CIT for resident companies; International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from Samoan CIT on all income other than Samoan-source income derived from a Samoan business

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% under the Income Tax Act 2015 on dividends paid by a resident company to a non-resident; however, dividends paid by an exempt IC to non-resident shareholders are not subject to Samoan WHT because the IC is not liable to Samoan income tax and the dividend is treated as foreign-source income

Filing and annual fee payment due no later than 30 June annually, or within 30 days of the anniversary of incorporation, whichever is earlier per the International Companies Act and SIFA fee schedule

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File or Master File threshold is prescribed under Samoan law; Transfer Pricing Rules under the Income Tax Act 2015 apply the OECD arm's length principle to all cross-border related-party transactions, including management fees, software licensing, and logistics sub-contracting. Prudent practice requires contemporaneous documentation when annual related-party turnover exceeds WST 1,000,000 equivalent.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not impose Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the International Companies Act 1987 requires each IC to maintain a licensed registered agent and a physical registered office in Samoa, and to keep adequate accounting records reflecting economic activities. The OECD Global Forum reviews Samoa's transparency standards; ICs must demonstrate substantive business presence in Samoa to avoid being characterised as a shell company and to preserve the CIT exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (False Declaration of Foreign-Source Income)

Statutory sanction: Shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded; late payment interest at 2% per month; fine up to WST 100,000; imprisonment up to 7 years under the Tax Administration Act 2015; forfeiture of IC exempt status and retroactive CIT assessment at 27%

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the ICA, WHT, and interest; civil restitution orders; travel bans; disqualification as director for 10 years; criminal imprisonment up to 7 years
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SIFA strike-off and dissolution; restoration fee up to WST 1,000; reinstatement by court order; automatic loss of limited liability for all debts incurred after strike-off; Samoan Ministry for Revenue may disregard corporate personality and assess the beneficial owner personally

Director personal liability: Directors and officers personally liable for company debts if the IC is struck off with no solvent winding up; statutory restitution of misappropriated assets; prohibition against holding a Samoan registered agent licence
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WST 2,000,000 for the corporate entity under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter- Financing of Terrorism Act 2018; asset freezing; cancellation of the registered agent's licence; criminal penalty of imprisonment up to 10 years for money laundering

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for fines up to WST 500,000; criminal imprisonment up to 10 years; confiscation of personal assets; exclusion from banking services in Samoa and partner jurisdictions
Withholding Tax Failure (Resident IC with Samoan-Source Income)

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax recovery from the IC; penalty of 50% of the unpaid WHT; interest on unpaid amounts at 2% monthly; refusal of deduction for the underlying expense; customs and border enforcement of tax debts

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unremitted WHT if they knowingly directed the payment without withholding; criminal fine up to WST 50,000; disqualification from serving on any Samoa-registered company board
Transfer Pricing Manipulation (Related-Party Cross-Border E-commerce Fees)

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of assessable income by Samoa Revenue; secondary adjustment deemed distribution; penalty of 40% of the transfer pricing adjustment; interest accrual; publication of the taxpayer's name on a non-compliance register

Director personal liability: Directors who approved inflated related-party payments may be assessed for reckless tax conduct; civil penalty of 20% of the adjusted amount; travel restriction pending full payment; personal liability extends to foreign directors with no Samoan physical presence
