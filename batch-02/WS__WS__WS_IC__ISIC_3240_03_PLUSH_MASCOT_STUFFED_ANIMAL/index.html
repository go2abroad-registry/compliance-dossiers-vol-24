Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MINR), Government of Samoa; official taxpayer portal: MINR e-Services (http://www.minr.gov.ws)

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned International Companies (ICs) must obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from MINR and, if VAGST-registrable, a separate VAGST registration number. Procedure: (1) File the certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation issued under the International Companies Act 1987, the constitutional documents, and the registered agent appointment letter; (2) Submit the TIN application form with director and shareholder particulars, registered office address in Samoa (via the licensed registered agent), and a statement confirming non-resident status; (3) MINR verifies the application against the Samoa International Financial Services Authority (SIFSA) registry and issues the TIN within 5 to 10 business days; (4) If annual taxable turnover reaches or is likely to exceed WST 100,000, file mandatory VAGST registration under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2012 within 21 days; (5) Obtain a Customs client code with the Samoa Customs Service (administered by MINR) for lodging export declarations for the plush mascot goods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box style entity classification election exists in Samoan law. IC status is elective at incorporation under the International Companies Act 1987; electing and maintaining IC status statutorily exempts the entity from Samoa income tax, VAGST, and stamp duty on offshore business activities. Hybrid mismatch elections under OECD BEPS Action 2 have not been domesticated, and no statutory hybrid or deemed-dividend elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover (compulsory); voluntary registration is available but uncommon for ICs whose offshore turnover is statutorily exempt

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 12 of the VAGST Act 2012 to: (a) goods physically exported by the supplier from Samoa within the prescribed period (generally 28 days from the date of supply) and retained outside Samoa; (b) services performed outside Samoa or wholly consumed outside Samoa; and (c) supplies to non-resident operators of vessels or aircraft engaged in international transport. A plush toy OEM exporting finished mascots to overseas wholesale buyers qualifies for the 0% rate on export supply provided the supplier retains documentary proof of physical export, transfer of ownership, and receipt of consideration from the non-resident buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SED/EDN) lodged via Samoa Customs Service ASYCUDA World
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (dated, endorsed, and consigned to the overseas buyer)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Purchase order, sales contract, and INCOTERMS delivery terms supporting export title transfer

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is in force in Samoa. Taxpayers may issue paper or standard electronic invoices; VAGST returns and payments are filed electronically through the MINR e-Services portal. Export transactions must be supported by standard customs declarations through the Samoa Customs Service (ASYCUDA World), but no fiscal device certification or digital stamping of invoices is required.

MINR conducts desk and field audits of zero-rated export claims, cross-matching customs export data, shipping documents, and FX inflow records. Where zero-rating is claimed without actual physical export or with false or forged documentation, MINR issues a default assessment of VAGST at 15% on the supply value, plus a late payment penalty of 10% of the unpaid tax and statutory interest accruing under the VAGST Act 2012; intentional or reckless conduct attracts an additional 50% shortfall penalty. Criminal prosecution may result in fines up to WST 50,000 and imprisonment. For ICs, fraudulent use of the exemption regime can trigger revocation of the International Company certificate and reclassification as a domestic company by SIFSA, with retrospective income tax and VAGST exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is not freely convertible offshore; the CBS manages the exchange rate against a trade-weighted basket of major partner currencies. Current-account payments for goods and services are processed through authorized dealers upon submission of supporting trade documents, while capital-account transactions require CBS approval unless a statutory exemption applies. A qualifying International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is exempt from Exchange Control approval for foreign-currency transactions with non-residents and is prohibited from domestic WST-denominated operations; profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are exempt from Samoan withholding tax provided the IC does not carry on business with Samoan residents and all banking is executed in foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods physically exported from Samoa, the exporter must file an export declaration with an authorized dealer before shipment, repatriate full proceeds to a licensed Samoan bank within 3 months from the date of export, and offer the foreign currency for conversion to WST; the CBS may order mandatory conversion of up to 100% of proceeds only in exceptional reserve emergencies. An IC in the offshore plush-toy OEM model (goods procured in China and shipped directly to overseas buyers, never entering Samoan customs territory) falls outside the territorial surrender obligation, but the licensed Samoan bank will still route all receipts through foreign-currency accounts and apply CBS reporting codes to prevent misclassification as a domestic export.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Exchange Control Division, Apia; governing statutes are the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1976, supplemented by the Foreign Investment Act 2000 (administered by the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour) and the Samoa International Financial Authority Act 2017.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Samoa has no dedicated EMI or digital-bank licensing class; the CBS licenses banks and non-bank financial institutions under the Financial Institutions Act 1996 and does not recognize electronic money issuance as a standalone licensed activity. Major global EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) generally do not onboard Samoa-registered International Companies due to correspondent-bank de-risking and lack any Samoa clearing membership; they may be used only as a secondary inbound collection conduit where the EMI accepts Samoan corporate clients and settles onward via SWIFT to the IC's USD account at a CBS-licensed bank. Legal constraint: no EMI may clear WST-denominated payments or hold Samoan client deposits without a CBS license.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity capital via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's account (typically Hong Kong or Singapore) to the IC's USD foreign-currency account at the licensed Samoan bank; the remittance must carry the exchange-control purpose code for inward investment/capital injection and a structurally clear memo referencing the IC registration number and 'working capital for OEM trade'. A Foreign Investment Certificate under the Foreign Investment Act 2000 is not required because the IC conducts no business with Samoan residents, but the bank will require a board resolution and share-allotment evidence, and for amounts above approximately USD 100,000 it will also require a legal opinion and beneficial-ownership declaration; the licensed bank completes CBS exchange-control reporting on behalf of the IC.Settle trade payables to overseas plush-toy manufacturers via SWIFT MT103 priority telegraphic transfer using the trade purpose code 'Payment for Imported Goods', or via a sight Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Samoan bank through SWIFT MT700 and confirmed by a first-class Asian confirming bank to mitigate exporter risk. Open-account settlements through Platform_Pay rails are acceptable only where the platform remits directly to the IC's BSP or SCB USD corporate account and the payment is supported by matching invoices and shipment documentation; Samoa has no domestic cross-border clearing system, so all settlements settle through the banks' USD correspondent networks in the United States or the Asia-Pacific region.Spot foreign-exchange contracts (delivery within T+2) with the licensed authorized dealer for converting any incidental WST balances into USD or other required trade currencies.Deliverable forward FX contracts with a licensed Samoan commercial bank for maturities up to 12 months, permitted only where the underlying import/export exposure is evidenced by contracts and invoices.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in WST executed by the IC directly with a non-resident bank and settled in USD outside Samoa; no domestic NDF market exists, but offshore execution is legally permissible and does not require CBS approval under the IC exchange-control exemption.Bilateral cross-currency swaps between the IC and a non-resident counterparty for hedging USD/WST or USD/AUD exposures, documented and booked outside Samoa, with CBS notification to the licensed Samoan bank where settlement cash flows transit the Samoan account.Natural hedging strategy (recommended primary practice): denominate both supplier payments and customer receipts in USD to eliminate WST conversion risk entirely in the plush-toy OEM trade cycle.

0% on dividends paid by a Samoa International Company to non-resident shareholders, including distributions of previously exempt trading profits; domestic non-international companies may face Samoan final dividend withholding tax at the applicable rate (31 December 2017 regime), but an IC is contractually and statutorily excluded from that regime.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Annual return and annual fee are statutorily due no later than 60 days after the anniversary date of incorporation; no extension beyond six months is ordinarily granted and a longer delay may trigger administrative strike-off

Not applicable unless income taxable under Samoa's Income Tax Act 2012 is unexpectedly derived; a domestic corporate taxpayer must file within six months after the end of the accounting period

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has no detailed local-file/master-file threshold enacted for domestic taxpayers; the Income Tax Act 2012 applies a general arm's length rule to cross-border related-party transactions. For an exempt International Company, no mandatory transfer pricing documentation is statutorily required, but prudent risk-based policy requires a Country-by-Country Report threshold benchmark of WST 550 million consolidated group revenue when the ultimate parent group is obliged under the OECD Multilateral Instrument or Samoa's CbC rules; in all events, the IC should retain a one-page TP policy, cost contribution summary, and margin analysis in WST below a notional WST 10 million transaction threshold.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's International Companies framework applies economic substance principles derived from the International Companies Act 1987, latest amendments, and Samoa's international tax standards: an IC must have a registered agent and registered office in Samoa, maintain books and records reflecting transactions, and cannot carry on business with Samoan residents. For a plush mascot OEM, the IC must demonstrate commercial substance in the buyer-supply chain through genuine contracts, purchase orders, and correspondence evidencing that it performs design, procurement, and logistics functions; no separate Samoan 'Economic Substance Regulations' akin to UAE ESR apply, but the Samoa Tax Administration will respect the IC's exemption only if the IC is not a shell and does not produce Samoan-source income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Revocation of tax-exempt status retroactively, assessment of corporate income tax at 27% on all relevant profits, understatement penalties up to 100% of the tax due, refusal of treaty benefits, and criminal liability under the Tax Administration Act 2012 including fines up to WST 300,000 and imprisonment up to seven years for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made personally liable for tax collected but not remitted, ordered to pay civil restitution equal to the unpaid tax and penalties, barred by court order from leaving Samoa pending criminal tax proceedings, and during a criminal conviction subjected to imprisonment of up to seven years in addition to disgorgement of hidden profits.
Shell Company Misuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Compulsory strike-off after failure to file annual returns, negative shareholder and director disqualification, refusal of tax residence/benefit documentation by Samoa's Ministry of Revenue, and potential reclassification of profits under the general anti-avoidance rule, resulting in a 27% corporate tax assessment plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors can be disqualified from holding office in any Samoa company for up to five years, ordered to reimburse the company for any loss caused by failure to maintain records, issued travel bans where a criminal investigation commences, and sentenced to imprisonment for up to one year for delictual filing without substance.
AML / Counter-Targeted Financial Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty up to WST 500,000 for a corporate body, freezing of bank accounts under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2007, seizure of the plush OEM manufacturing assets, and criminal sanction of up to fourteen years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and other senior officers are personally liable for knowingly causing the IC to breach AML/CFT obligations; the court may order civil forfeiture of the profit share, an international travel ban to prevent abscondment, and a term of imprisonment of up to fourteen years, with no right to rely on corporate limited liability.
Improper Profit Repatriation / Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unrepaid withholding tax at the full statutory rate of 20% on the redirected payment, add-on penalty of 10% per month up to 100% of the tax, interest at commercial rate, and potential criminal fine of up to WST 240,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise the false classification of a payment are jointly and severally liable for the withholding shortfall when Samoa tax recovery is impossible from the corporate funds, may be barred from emigration pending an exit-permit receipt or court order, and may be charged with fraudulent return preparation punishable by up to seven years' imprisonment.
Improper Use of Samoa IC as a Domestic Trading Entity

Statutory sanction: Loss of the IC's tax exempt status; the Samoa Ministry of Revenue will assess corporate tax at the 27% headline rate on all local-source income; a separate business license fee becomes due; and the Registrar of International Companies may issue a compliance order and impose a monetary penalty not exceeding WST 100,000.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly permits the IC to carry on domestic business may be personally assessed for the resulting tax, held liable for all employment taxes and social security contributions, subjected to a court injunction against further local operations, and in egregious cases sentenced to up to five years' imprisonment for conducting unlicensed commercial activity through an exempt vehicle.
