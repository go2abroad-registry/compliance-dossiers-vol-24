Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the International Companies Act 1987 and no escrow or capital release account requirement for a Samoa International Company. The authorised share capital stated in the constitution governs issued shares; government fees and registered agent fees are payable separately.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Evacuation and Safety Certificate issued by the Samoa Fire and Emergency Services Authority (FESA) covering the registered warehouse and office premises, including certification of fire extinguishing equipment, emergency egress and storage racking stability. Occupational safety and workplace clearance issued by the Labour and Employment Relations Division of MCIL under the Labour and Employment Relations Act 2013, encompassing safe manual handling procedures, racking load ratings, first aid provisions, incident reporting, and the posting of safety notices in English.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the International Companies Act 1987 and no escrow or capital release account requirement for a Samoa International Company. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue, Samoa (Inland Revenue Division); official portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company is tax-exempt on offshore income and is not required to obtain an income tax ID solely by reason of incorporation. A TIN and VAGST registration number may be obtained by filing the Ministry for Revenue TIN application form with a certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation or International Company licence, constitutional documents, director and shareholder identification, and a registered office certificate. The application can be lodged in person or through the registered local agent; the Ministry issues the TIN certificate and, where taxable annual turnover reaches the VAGST threshold or voluntary registration is approved, a VAGST registration certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election is available under Samoan domestic law. An International Company may elect to become a licensed international company under the International Companies Act 1987 and may voluntarily register for VAGST, payroll, or excise obligations where it transacts locally. Treaty-based hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act 2015 apply only to cross-border arrangements with treaty or structured-party exposure; otherwise entity-level exemption remains the default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 per annum of taxable supplies; voluntary registration is permitted for businesses below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of commercial aircraft parts where the goods are physically exported from Samoa and title passes to a non-resident buyer. For offshore services, zero-rating applies where the recipient is a non-resident outside Samoa and the benefit of the service is consumed outside Samoa, subject to supporting contracts and payment evidence. An International Company held under a valid international company licence remains exempt from Samoan income tax on the resulting export margin.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged through Samoa Customs Electronic Single Window
  • Commercial invoice issued by the International Company to the non-resident buyer
  • Packing list describing the surplus commercial aircraft parts and part numbers
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Samoa
  • Signed board resolution of the International Company authorizing the disposition of surplus inventory
  • Proof of payment or inward remittance certificate issued by a licensed Samoan commercial bank
  • ICA licence and certificate of good standing for the International Company

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is currently in force. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue compliant tax invoices, whether paper or electronic, showing the tax identification number, invoice serial number, date, description, amount, and VAGST charged. Electronic invoices are acceptable if retained in a format accessible to the Ministry for Revenue during audit. No API-based clearance is required.

The Ministry for Revenue may conduct deep audits of export zero-rating documentation. A false or unsupported zero-rating claim gives rise to an immediate VAGST assessment at 15%, plus a late payment penalty of up to 100% of the tax, monthly interest, and a negligence penalty of 200% of the tax evaded. Knowingly fraudulent documentation attracts criminal prosecution, fines up to WST 50,000, and imprisonment. The Commissioner has retrospective clawback authority for six years from the date of assessment, and the relevant aircraft parts may be subject to seizure at export if declaration discrepancies are detected.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The WST is only partially convertible. For resident entities, current-account transactions are substantially liberalized, but capital-account movements involving WST conversion or a resident counterparty (dividend remittances, loan repayments, liquidation distributions, share transfers) require Central Bank exchange-control approval. The Samoan International Company, by contrast, is a non-resident offshore person for exchange-control purposes: it may maintain and deploy unrestricted USD foreign-currency accounts, settle cross-border aircraft-part sales, remit dividends to non-resident shareholders, and repay shareholder loans without Central Bank consent and without triggering WST conversion. Profit-remittance hurdles are therefore institutional rather than regulatory: the receiving bank will require proof of economic substance, certified UBO records, current registered-agent and government licence certificates, and a documentary trail substantiating the aviation-parts trade. Once satisfied, dividends and capital distributions flow offshore freely with no exchange-control approval, no withholding tax, and no convertibility constraint. The sole capital-account boundary is the International Companies Act prohibition on conducting business with persons resident in Samoa; any such transaction would pull the affected proceeds into the resident exchange-control regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1978, resident Samoan exporters must surrender the full value of export proceeds to an authorized dealer within the statutory period following export clearance (practically within 30 days), supported by declared export documentation; shortfalls and late remittances are reported by the authorized dealer to the Central Bank. This mandatory surrender and conversion regime does not apply to the WS_IC International Company. As a non-resident offshore legal person trading surplus certified civil aircraft rotables and avionics exclusively with foreign airlines, MRO providers and redistributors, the IC retains 100 percent of its USD proceeds in its offshore operating account with a zero statutory conversion ratio and no obligation to sell foreign exchange to the Central Bank or an authorized dealer. The only compliance duty is statistical: the receiving bank flags each inflow as a non-resident transactor entry in Samoa's balance-of-payments reporting. Should the IC ever derive Samoan-source receipts (a prohibited activity for an International Company), those specific proceeds would immediately become subject to the full surrender, conversion and reporting rules.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Falepuna, Apia, Independent State of Samoa) - the statutory monetary authority and exchange-control administrator acting under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Exchange Control Regulations 1978, and the anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing framework supervised by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit. The Central Bank licenses authorized dealers (commercial banks), administers Samoan Tala (WST) convertibility, compiles balance-of-payments statistics, and exercises prior-approval powers over resident-entity transactions; non-resident offshore International Companies registered under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) fall outside this prior-approval perimeter when operating exclusively in foreign currency.
