Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (Samoa) — governing the Samoa Revenue Authority; official web portal: www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) is incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 through a licensed registered agent (typically via the Samoa International Finance Authority facilitation). The IC then registers with the Ministry for Revenue to obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and a Certificate of Registration. Because the IC is statutorily exempt from income tax, no active income-tax return filing is required; however, annual government fees payable to the Ministry for Revenue keep the entity in good standing. If the IC voluntarily registers for VAGST (uncommon for passive trading companies), a separate VAGST registration and electronic filing activation are required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Samoa International Companies are exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, estate duty, and exchange control, subject only to an annual government fee under the International Companies Act 1987.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' or US-style pass-through election exists under Samoa law; the IC is an entity-level tax-exempt corporate vehicle by statutory operation. No hybrid mismatch election is available, and Samoa has not enacted general anti-hybrid/pillar-two rules applicable to exempt ICs. An IC may elect in its constitution to maintain or waive limited liability attributes, but such elections do not alter the tax-exempt classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 (or WST 15,000 over any three consecutive months) of taxable supplies per annum; voluntary registration permitted for exporters. An exempt IC with only passive electronic inventory distribution outside Samoa generally has no VAGST registration obligation, but zero-rated export claims require registration if the entity chooses to recover input tax on local costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% VAGST rate where the exporter is registered, holds a valid Customs Export Declaration, and ships the goods out of Samoa physically within the statutory time frame (generally within 28 days of invoicing or collection). Offshore services supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Samoa may also be zero-rated where the benefit is not received in Samoa. A passive electronic components distributor (ISIC 4652) selling to foreign buyers on a Delivered Duty Paid or FOB basis can claim zero-rating on the sale of inventory, but must retain transport evidence proving the goods left Samoa. An untaxed International Company engaging only in offshore trading outside Samoa is effectively outside the VAGST net; zero-rated export registration is optional and only useful for recovering VAGST on local overheads.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD Form / Electronic Export Lodgement printout), duly cleared by Samoa Customs
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) showing exporter/shipper and overseas consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or Foreign Exchange Transaction Record from a licensed Samoan commercial bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the shipped electronic component inventory (e.g., multilayer ceramic capacitors, resistors)

Samoa mandates the use of Ministry for Revenue-approved Electronic Fiscal Devices (EFD) / fiscal invoicing software for all VAGST-registered businesses. Certified software transmits invoice data to the Government of Samoa's fiscal clearance portal via API in near-real time, receives a digitally signed fiscal stamp (fiscal code) for each document, and requires printed or PDF copies bearing the unique fiscal reference. ERP integration is permitted through certified middleware; failure to use certified e-invoicing software invalidates input tax credits and attracts penalties.

The VAGST Act 1993 and Customs Act 1977 impose robust enforcement: any false zero-rating claim is a tax offense subject to retrospective assessment for up to six years, a penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded, and late-payment interest currently set at 15% per annum. Where export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline without prior exchange-control approval, the zero-rating is revoked and the exported goods are treated as taxable local sales with full VAGST, penalties, and interest due. Criminal liability may also attach for knowingly false declarations, with fines up to WST 10,000 and imprisonment under the Customs Act; the Ministry for Revenue may demand bank records, shipping manifests, and customer purchase orders during audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a managed-float currency that is not freely convertible offshore. Samoa operates a dual-tier exchange-control framework: Samoan-resident entities and individuals are subject to CBS approval requirements for capital-account transactions, though current-account payments are liberally approved through delegated authorized dealers (licensed commercial banks). The WS_IC, as an International Company incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended), is statutorily treated as a non-resident entity for exchange-control purposes and is expressly exempted from the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977. Accordingly, the WS_IC may open and operate foreign-currency accounts, receive export proceeds, and remit dividends, management fees, and other profits offshore without CBS conversion or approval requirements. No profit-repatriation hurdle exists as a matter of exchange-control law; the only practical impediments are bank-level AML/KYC verification and correspondent-bank routing standards applicable to USD-denominated flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident Samoan exporters, the CBS exchange-control regime requires export proceeds to be repatriated to Samoa within six months of the date of shipment, and any foreign currency received must be surrendered to an authorized dealer and converted into WST within 30 days of receipt. Netting or offset arrangements require prior CBS approval. The WS_IC is categorically outside this regime: as an International Company it conducts all permitted business in foreign currency with non-residents, and its export receipts from the offshore distribution of electronic components are exempt from mandatory surrender and conversion. If the WS_IC were to receive WST from a Samoan-resident counterparty, the surrender and conversion rules would re-attach to that discrete transaction, and CBS approval would be required for the IC to retain funds in foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Falefa, Apia, Samoa; statutory authority derived from the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, the Exchange Control Act 1976, and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977; CBS is also the prudential regulator and licensing authority for commercial banks under the Financial Institutions Act 1996.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Samoa is not a supported incorporation jurisdiction for the principal cross-border corporate EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer), and no Samoa electronic-money-institution licensing regime exists under the Financial Institutions Act 1996. An offshore EMI providing payment services in or from Samoa would constitute unlicensed financial-institution business under Samoan law, and global EMIs generally decline Samoa-domiciled International Companies for sanctions-screening and correspondent-banking de-risking reasons. The only feasible use of an offshore EMI is ancillary — e.g., receivables collection through an FCA-or-HKMA-regulated EMI maintained by the group treasury — while the WS_IC’s primary operating account remains with a CBS-licensed Samoan bank. Such an ancillary structure is operationally viable but is not a substitute for the primary SWIFT wire and credit-line banking rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital injections to the WS_IC's USD settlement account must be routed as plain SWIFT MT103 customer transfers with a verified remittance narrative stating 'Share capital contribution to WS_IC International Company' or 'Subordinated shareholder loan to WS_IC'; ISO 20022 purpose code 'INV' (Investment) or 'LOAN' (Loan) must be applied by the remitting bank. The receiving Samoan bank will apply standard CBS balance-of-payments reporting and may require: (i) board resolution approving the capital injection; (ii) share-allotment form filed at MCIL (for equity); (iii) executed loan agreement with repayment schedule (for debt); (iv) source-of-funds evidence for the contributing shareholder (bank statements, tax returns, or audited wealth certification); and (v) certified register-of-shareholders extract. No CBS exchange-control approval is required for an International Company, but the bank's compliance unit must receive written confirmation that the contributing shareholder is a non-resident and that the funds remain outside the Samoan domestic exchange-control perimeter.Trade settlements for the purchase of multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors from suppliers in PRC, Taiwan, Malaysia, and Hong Kong will be executed through the Samoan bank's USD correspondent network using three permitted clearing rails: (i) direct SWIFT MT103 customer transfers to the supplier's nominated bank account for open-account transactions; (ii) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV bank-to-bank transfers where the supplier bank is not a direct correspondent; and (iii) commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) issued or confirmed by a major US or Asian bank (e.g., Citi, HSBC, MUFG) for large-value or first-trade consignments. Documentary collections (SWIFT MT400) with DP/DA terms may be used for mid-value transactions. USD clearing will flow across the New York correspondent network (Fedwire/CHIPS); the WS_IC must provide its bank's trade operations unit at least 48 hours' notice before each disbursement, together with the relevant trade file, and must maintain a statistical register for CBS balance-of-payments transparency. The credit-line rail will be structured as a pre-export finance or supplier-invoice-discounting facility secured against confirmed purchase orders and warehouse receipts, with the Samoan bank taking a floating charge over the WS_IC's inventory receivables.Outright FX forwards (USD/WST) with tenors up to 12 months, executed with a licensed Samoan authorized dealer (BSP, ANZ, or SCB) under an ISDA-style confirmation, used solely to cover the WS_IC's limited WST operating-cost exposure; subject to bank credit-line approval and CBS transaction reporting.Cross-currency swaps (USD/AUD, USD/NZD, USD/HKD) via the bank's offshore treasury desk for revenue-cost matching where suppliers invoice in non-USD Asia-Pacific currencies; permitted under the International Company exchange-control exemption.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/WST — legally permissible for non-residents under the WS_IC's statutory exchange-control exemption but commercially illiquid; available only through large offshore banks with no active Samoa onshore market.Natural-hedge policy (full currency matching of trade payables and receivables in USD) as the primary risk-management instrument; derivative instruments are authorized exclusively for hedging booked exposures and speculative trading is expressly prohibited by the WS_IC board treasury mandate.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Samoa is not a supported incorporation jurisdiction for the principal cross-border corporate EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer), and no Samoa electronic-money-institution licensing regime exists under the Financial Institutions Act 1996. An offshore EMI providing payment services in or from Samoa would constitute unlicensed financial-institution business under Samoan law, and global EMIs generally decline Samoa-domiciled International Companies for sanctions-screening and correspondent-banking de-risking reasons. The only feasible use of an offshore EMI is ancillary — e.g., receivables collection through an FCA-or-HKMA-regulated EMI maintained by the group treasury — while the WS_IC’s primary operating account remains with a CBS-licensed Samoan bank. Such an ancillary structure is operationally viable but is not a substitute for the primary SWIFT wire and credit-line banking rails.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% standard corporate income tax for resident companies and branches; 0% effective rate for a qualifying Samoan International Company (IC) whose income is derived outside Samoa under the International Companies Act 2012 statutory exemption.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% final withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident Samoan company to non-resident shareholders. Dividends or distributions paid by an International Company to its non-resident shareholders are statutorily exempt from any Samoan withholding tax, producing a 0% effective rate for the WS_IC.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within six months after the end of the income year. The standard Samoan income year is 1 July to 30 June; the return is therefore due by 31 December of the following calendar year, unless the Commissioner approves a substituted accounting period and extends the statutory filing date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing and payment of the annual licence fee are due within 30 days following the company's incorporation anniversary, or such anniversary date as endorsed on the certificate of incorporation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa prescribes no legally binding Local File or Master File threshold in tala (WST). The Income Tax Act 2015 contains a general arm's-length principle for related-party transactions; contemporaneous pricing documentation should nonetheless be maintained where annual related-party inventory or financing transactions exceed the contemporaneous documentation file standard of WST 500,000 (approximately USD 180,000), and the group should prepare a benchmarking memo under the CUP method because distribution of interchangeable capacitors and resistors is a commodity-type activity.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations similar to Caribbean ESR apply in Samoa. Samoa is nonetheless a member of the OECD Inclusive Framework and subject to OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices monitoring for no-or-only-nominal-tax jurisdictions. To avoid being characterized as a shell: the IC must maintain its licensed agent, a physical registered office in Samoa, statutory registers and accounting records maintained there, board resolutions uploaded to the agent, and genuine strategic direction of the excess-inventory distribution conducted from Samoa, while all commercial counterparties remain outside Samoa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2015: civil penalty of up to 200% of the short-paid tax plus late-payment interest; on conviction, a fine of up to WST 50,000, or imprisonment for up to five years, or both.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers and the Samoan agent are personally liable for the underpaid tax and penalty where they directed, consented to or negligently permitted the evasion; a court may additionally issue travel bans, restitution orders attaching personal and foreign assets, disqualification from directorships, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act 2012: compounding default fines, suspension, and strike-off from the International Companies Register; a struck-off IC loses its statutory tax exemption retroactively where the Registry determines it was never properly constituted, rendering Samoan-source amounts taxable at 27% with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally ordered to bear Registry and receivership costs, unpaid annual fees, and restitution to third-party creditors because the limitation-of-liability clauses in the articles are void in cases of fraud; adverse classification of the IC as a shell by the OECD or EU triggers CFC, PFIC and transparency consequences for the shareholders in their residence jurisdictions.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Prevention Act and subordinate AML/CFT rules: fines per infringement against the company and the licensed agent, suspension of the IC's registration, forfeiture of connected property, and the FIU may publish reporting orders; in aggravated cases, criminal fines of up to WST 200,000 for continuing non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent face joint and several monetary penalties, travel bans, and asset freeze orders during investigation; the beneficial owners are disclosed through the Registry's automatic exchange-of-information request pathway to the residence tax authority, exposing undisclosed offshore inventory distribution income.
