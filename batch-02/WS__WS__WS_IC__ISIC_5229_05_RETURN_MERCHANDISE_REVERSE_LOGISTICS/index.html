Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an International Company; capital may be unpaid at incorporation. No escrow, capital deposit or paid-up certificate is required. Authorised capital up to USD 1,000,000 attracts the US$150 fee; higher bands are separately rated and shares may be denominated in any convertible currency.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Samoa Fire and Emergency Services Authority (SFESA) for any warehouse or grading facility exceeding 200 m2; building approval and structural load certification under the Building Act 2014 issued by the Ministry of Works, Transport and Infrastructure (MWTI); workplace registration, hazard management plan and first-aid inspections under the Occupational Safety and Health Act 2002 (MCIL); expatriate managers require MCIL Labour Division work permits and Samoa Immigration entry permits.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue of the Independent State of Samoa (MfR); official taxpayer portal and e-services: revenue.gov.ws (Samoa Tax e-Services, including TIN registration, VAGST registration, and electronic return filing).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company first incorporates under the International Companies Act 1987 through a licensed registered agent in Samoa. The registered agent then files the MfR taxpayer application on the company's behalf, submitting the certificate of incorporation, IC registration certificate, registered office and agent details, and shareholder/director identification documents. The MfR issues a TIN that functions as the single administrative identifier for all tax interactions. Because the IC is a Tax_Exempt_Zone entity, the TIN supports VAGST filing, economic substance reporting, and Bank of Samoa exchange-control declarations rather than income tax assessment. If the IC separately makes taxable supplies into the Samoa domestic economy above the VAGST threshold, the agent or local representative must complete a supplementary VAGST registration through the MfR portal; otherwise, the IC remains administratively registered but not liable to domestic income tax or VAGST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity-classification election is available under Samoa law. A Samoa International Company is automatically and mandatorily classified as an offshore tax-exempt company under the International Companies Act 1987 and does not elect its classification. The only statutory elections are (i) voluntary VAGST registration to recover input credits where the IC carries on minor taxable activity, and (ii) an election to be treated as a non-resident IC for territorial and treaty purposes, subject to MfR approval. Samoa does not recognise hybrid-mismatch or disregarded-entity elections; reliance on foreign pass-through characterisation must be substantiated by a Samoa tax ruling from the Ministry of Revenue.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (i.e., SAT 100,000) of taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period; compulsory registration for exceeding suppliers and voluntary registration permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2003, goods qualify for 0% output tax only where they are physically exported from Samoa, normally within 28 days of supply, under a Customs export declaration, and are not supplied for local consumption in Samoa. Services qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is a non-resident located outside Samoa at the time of supply and the services are not performed for local persons or directly connected with real property in Samoa. Reverse-logistics services (inspection, quality grading, light repair, repackaging, and warehousing of returned merchandise) are zero-rated only when performed for an offshore principal under a documented return-merchandise chain, paid for offshore, and evidenced by export documentation; any local release or resale of remediated goods in Samoa is a taxable supply at 15% VAGST.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs Service) or electronic export permit evidencing physical departure of goods from Samoa
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the ocean/air carrier confirming export and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Central Bank of Samoa or licensed commercial bank) evidencing timely repatriation of the offshore sale proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and freight and insurance documents matching the export declaration
  • Return-Merchandise Authorization (RMA) and service contract evidencing the offshore principal's ownership of the returned goods and the reverse-logistics service chain
  • Warehouse inventory, grading, and disposal records linking each unit of goods to the zero-rated supply

No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous-control mandate is in force in Samoa. Samoa has not implemented CTC e-invoicing, digital stamping, SAF-T, or API-based transactional clearance. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue prescribed tax invoices and simplified retail receipts, file periodic VAGST returns via the MfR online e-Services portal, and retain all electronic and paper records for the statutory seven-year period. Large taxpayers are required to file electronically under the Tax Administration Act 2019, but no third-party invoice clearance or digital stamping is required.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to post-export audit by the Ministry of Revenue against the customs and FX documentation bundle. If the export is fictitious, the goods are diverted into the domestic market, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day window, the MfR will claw back the refunded VAGST with statutory interest, impose tax shortfall penalties up to 150% of the avoided tax for intentional concealment or fraud, and may initiate criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment for fraudulent VAGST refund claims. For a Samoa International Company, a failed export or exemption audit also triggers the Samoa International Financial Authority economic-substance review and may result in revocation of the IC's Tax_Exempt_Zone status and retrospective reassessment as a domestic taxable entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is convertible for current account transactions, including trade-related payments, dividends, and service fees, under a liberalised exchange control regime administered by the Central Bank of Samoa. Capital account transactions require case-by-case approval, and profit remittances are permitted subject to submission of audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and evidence of foreign direct investment registration. Non-resident International Companies enjoy statutory tax exemption but remain subject to exchange control reporting thresholds for inward and outward transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa within 180 days from the date of shipment or export. International Companies operating under the International Companies Act are not generally subject to mandatory conversion of export receipts into WST; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in designated external accounts, provided that the Central Bank of Samoa is notified and documentary evidence of the underlying export transaction is maintained. Any conversion to WST for local disbursement must be executed through an authorised dealer bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Airwallex and similar offshore fintech/EMI platforms are permitted for cross-border receipt and disbursement routing, but they are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions in Samoa. International Companies may use such platforms for trade settlement and reverse logistics disbursements, provided that (i) the underlying funds are not sourced from prohibited activity, (ii) the platform maintains an anti-money-laundering (AML) framework acceptable to the Central Bank of Samoa, and (iii) the Samoan entity maintains a local operational account for statutory reporting and tax clearance interactions. The use of EMIs is legally constrained to non-resident business accounts and cannot be used as a substitute for a Samoan bank account where exchange control declaration is required for inbound capital injections or profit remittances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident shareholders or related group entities must be routed through the Samoan banking system via SWIFT MT103, MT202, or MT210 messages, using the appropriate Harmonised System / balance-of-payments purpose codes. For International Companies, the recommended inbound purpose code is 'Other External Assets / Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Investment' for share capital injections and 'Cross-border Trade-related Working Capital' for intercompany loans. The Central Bank of Samoa imposes a notification obligation on authorised dealer banks for all inbound foreign currency transfers exceeding WST 100,000, and the receiving bank must verify the corporate registry excerpt, director resolution, and underlying business plan before crediting a Capital Escrow Account. Proceeds of capital injections may be credited to a foreign currency denominated external account, converted to WST only through an authorised dealer, or held in escrow pending issuance of the International Company certificate from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL).Outbound trade disbursements for returned goods quality grading, refurbishment, carrier settlement, and refunds shall be cleared through the following rails: (i) local and regional supplier payments via Samoa ACH / FAST Settlement network where the beneficiary holds a Samoan account; (ii) cross-border supplier and logistics payments via SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers, MT202 cover payments for interbank reimbursements, and MT199 ancillary messages for reconciliation; (iii) commercial letters of credit using ICC UCP 600 rules where the counterparty requires documentary assurance; and (iv) bulk merchandising refunds and e-commerce payouts via Airwallex or equivalent licensed fintech credits routed to overseas bank accounts. 0% (Samoa International Company holds a statutory Tax Exemption Certificate; no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or gift duty is imposed on exempt international companies under the International Companies Act 1987, s.208, as amended)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa does not prescribe a statutory currency threshold for Local File or Master File documentation for exempt International Companies; as an OECD Inclusive Framework member, Samoa follows the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines 2022. A Local File is recommended where connected-party cross-border transactions of the reverse logistics company exceed WST 1,000,000 per annum; a Master File is recommended where the group global turnover exceeds WST 5,000,000 and group-wide related-party transactions are present; the Tax Exemption Certificate does not exempt the entity from contemporaneous documentation obligations in the reverse logistics value chain

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not apply the European Union-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the International Companies Act 1987 ss.10, 25 and 213 impose a functional substance requirement: the company must maintain a registered office in Samoa, engage a licensed resident agent, and keep accounting records and registers at the agent's premises. For return merchandise and reverse logistics activities, the Samoa Ministry of Revenue and SIFA require evidence of real economic presence commensurate with the digital or physical flow of goods, including documented contracts, employee or director activity, third-party logistics agreements, and revenue records showing that the company is not a bare conduit; failure to demonstrate substance to the satisfaction of the Commissioner permits reclassification of the entity as a standard resident taxpayer and withdrawal of the Tax Exemption Certificate

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 14 years under the Income Tax Act 2012 ss.110-112; reassessment of all profits at the standard corporate income tax rate of 27%; revocation of the Tax Exemption Certificate and a civil penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax, penalties, and interest; the Commissioner may obtain a personal recovery order, impose a travel ban, and seek disqualification of directors from managing any Samoan or international company for up to 10 years; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and forfeiture of personal assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act
Shell Company Abuse and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the Register, cancellation of the Certificate of Compliance, and a default assessment equal to 25% of the gross remittances passing through the entity; the Tax Exemption Certificate is annulled retroactively and all protected years become taxable

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the entity is found to be a sham; directors and shadow directors are personally assessed for the corporate tax debt, compelled to make civil restitution of diverted profits, and may be subjected to asset freezing orders and travel bans pending full settlement
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to WST 250,000 per contravention under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act 2018; revocation of the registered agent's licence; the company's bank accounts with local ACH or Airwallex payment providers may be frozen; criminal prosecution carries fines of WST 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal imposition of the monetary penalties where the AML breach was reckless; a personal travel ban and confiscation of assets can be ordered; directors of a licensed trust or company management provider are subject to permanent disqualification from the Samoa financial services industry
Late Filing and Unauthorised Trading After Strike-Off

Statutory sanction: Automatic dissolution and forfeiture of all company assets to the Samoan Crown; the company is treated as a standard resident taxpayer and assessed at 27% on all income earned during the default period; late filing penalties accrue at WST 100 per month and a WST 200 flat penalty per unfiled return

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for debts incurred after the company was struck off; the court may order the directors to restore the company at their personal cost, pay the tax assessment from personal funds, and impose a prohibition on leaving Samoa until the outstanding penalties are settled
