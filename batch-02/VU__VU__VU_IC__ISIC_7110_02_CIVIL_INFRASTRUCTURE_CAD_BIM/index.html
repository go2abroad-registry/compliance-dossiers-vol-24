Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance and Economic Management (MFEM); the Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), operating as the integrated Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), administers VAT, customs duties and the prospective income tax regime; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) is the competent body for incorporation and company registration; filings are effected through the VRC electronic services portal and the VFSC online registry.

E-government filing portal

For a Vanuatu International Company (IC): (1) appoint a VFSC-licensed Registered Agent; (2) file the Articles of Association and statutory declarations with the VFSC and pay the fixed registration fee; (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation bearing the unique IC company number, which serves as the identification reference for all tax purposes; (4) by operation of section 194 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222], the IC automatically enjoys a 20-year statutory exemption from all Vanuatu taxes, duties, fees and levies, so no separate TIN is procured unless a domestic taxable activity arises; (5) if the IC creates domestic VAT nexus (e.g., local payroll, property, or supplies to Vanuatu residents), file Form VAT-01 with DCIR/VRC to obtain a VAT registration number; (6) for pure offshore CAD/BIM export service delivery, the IC files no customs declaration and no VAT return but must maintain the incorporation certificate, client contracts, invoices, and inward remittance evidence.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — For a Vanuatu International Company (IC): (1) appoint a VFSC-licensed Registered Agent; (2) file the Articles of Association and statutory declarations with the VFSC and pay the fixed registration fee; (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation bearing the unique IC company number, which serves as the identification reference for all tax purposes; (4) by operation of section 194 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222], the IC automatically enjoys a 20-year statutory exemption from all Vanuatu taxes, duties, fees and levies, so no separate TIN is procured unless a domestic taxable activity arises; (5) if the IC creates domestic VAT nexus (e.g., local payroll, property, or supplies to Vanuatu residents), file Form VAT-01 with DCIR/VRC to obtain a VAT registration number; (6) for pure offshore CAD/BIM export service delivery, the IC files no customs declaration and no VAT return but must maintain the incorporation certificate, client contracts, invoices, and inward remittance evidence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None. Vanuatu has no check-the-box, pass-through, hybrid mismatch or entity classification election. An International Company is classified by statute as a tax-exempt offshore entity for 20 years; an ordinary domestic company cannot elect IC status, and an IC cannot elect to become taxable. Re-domiciliation into or out of the IC regime is available through VFSC administrative filings, not through any tax election. The Income Tax Act No. 27 of 2023 (residency-based personal income tax, deferred implementation) does not alter the IC's statutory exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 5,000,000 (approximately USD 42,000) of taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period triggers compulsory VAT registration; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold; exports of goods and qualifying services to non-residents are zero-rated and excluded from the domestic threshold calculation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated where exported by the supplier within the statutory export timeframe and supported by evidence of physical removal from Vanuatu. Services: zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply and the service is performed wholly outside Vanuatu or the benefit is enjoyed outside Vanuatu; electronically delivered offshore BIM, structural drafting and engineering CAD services to overseas clients fall squarely within this exported-service zero-rating. The claim requires the IC's exempt status, a written contract with the foreign counterparty, delivery records, and inward remittance evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World) with export entry number for physical goods; not applicable to pure digitally delivered CAD/BIM services
  • Commercial invoice and detailed service contract identifying the non-resident recipient, service description, currency and consideration
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice from a Vanuatu-licensed bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment
  • Proof of non-resident status of the overseas client (e.g., tax residence certificate or registry extract) and evidence the service was not performed in Vanuatu
  • Electronic delivery and project records (transmission logs, BIM model metadata, milestone sign-offs) substantiating that the service was provided and accepted offshore

No national e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate is in force; paper or PDF invoices issued in vatu are acceptable. Customs import and export declarations are mandatory and processed electronically through the ASYCUDA World single-window system. Offshore CAD/BIM service exports attract no e-invoicing API or fiscal data reporting obligation.

DCIR/VRC audits export zero-rating and refund claims strictly; failure to substantiate export within 180 days or the use of fabricated export documentation exposes the taxpayer to full VAT clawback (15%) plus penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall, compound monthly interest on unpaid VAT, and criminal fines up to VT 5,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years for tax fraud. Directors, registered agents and signatories may incur personal liability, and the IC may lose its 20-year statutory exemption and VFSC good-standing status for transacting with Vanuatu residents or breaching the International Companies Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is convertible on the current account without routine RBV pre-approval for trade-in-services payments, including offshore engineering design and CAD/BIM outsourcing fees, provided the underlying contract and invoice are retained by the authorized dealer. Capital account transactions remain administratively controlled, but an International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [CAP 222] is treated as a non-resident entity for exchange-control purposes; accordingly, the IC may lawfully hold multi-currency external accounts (USD, AUD, EUR, NZD) and effect capital remittances without mandatory conversion into VUV. Profit repatriation and intercompany settlement face no statutory repatriation tax or withholding, but the RBV requires that all payments be routed through an authorized dealer or licensed external channel so that balance-of-payments statistics are recorded. The genuine practical barrier is bank compliance appetite and correspondent-bank due diligence, not the legal convertibility ceiling.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters, the Exchange Control Act [CAP 69] compels surrender of foreign-currency export proceeds to an authorized dealer within the prescribed timeline (administratively applied as up to 90 days from receipt) and permits the dealer to require conversion to VUV under standard exchange-control notices. A non-resident International Company registered under the IC Act [CAP 222] is excluded from the surrender and conversion mandate; therefore, all CAD/BIM service export receipts may be retained in the original contract currency, provided documentary evidence of the underlying service export is made available to the settling bank. In every instance, proceeds must pass through the banking system via an authorized dealer or an approved external account (not cash or unregulated crypto) to satisfy the RBV's statistical and anti-money-laundering reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [CAP 125], exercising exchange-control supervision pursuant to the Exchange Control Act [CAP 69] and delegated Exchange Control Notices issued to authorized dealer banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Vanuatu does not operate a mature e-money institution licensing regime; no RBV-licensed foreign digital EMI can on-board VU resident clients as a principal, and unlicensed offshore EMI accounts create AML/CTF Act No. 44 of 2014 violations for the IC's directors. An offshore IC may practically use third-country EMIs (e.g., UK-FCA, Singapore-MAS, or AUSTRAC-regulated providers) as a secondary collection rail for small-value client invoices, subject to the EMI's own jurisdiction rules, but the mandatory primary treasury and capital-injection rail must be a SWIFT-connected commercial bank account in Vanuatu or an accepted regional hub (e.g., Hong Kong, Singapore, Australia). Structural recommendation: hold the sanctioned bank account at ANZ or BRED as the anchor, and use a licensed EMI only for low-risk receivables under USD 10,000 per transaction, with automatic monthly sweeps back to the anchor account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity or intercompany loan injections must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's regulated bank account to the IC's external USD/AUD account at ANZ or BRED. For equity, the bank requires: (i) the IC's board resolution approving the allotment and account credit; (ii) the shareholder subscription agreement; and (iii) book-entry confirmation on the IC's register of members. For intercompany loans, a signed facility agreement with arm's-length interest (no Vanuatu withholding tax applies) and clear repayment schedule is required. The MT103 must state in Field 70/77B a plain-language purpose, e.g., 'Share capital contribution to Vanuatu International Company - BIM/CAD services' for equity, or 'Performance-based intercompany loan for working capital'; Vanuatu has no nationally standardized ISO 20022 purpose-code registry, so authorized dealers typically map the narrative to 'Trade in Services – Computer and Information Services' for BOP reporting. Amounts above VUV 1,000,000 (approx. USD 8,300) trigger automatic AML/CTF reporting; amounts above USD 100,000 will normally require the underlying contract and invoice to be provided to the bank before credit confirmation.Primary rail: direct SWIFT MT103 full-value transfers from the IC's multi-currency account at ANZ/BRED, with USD cleared via the bank's New York correspondent (typically JPMorgan/HSBC) and AUD via the Sydney network; intra-region supplier payments in Hong Kong or Singapore settle via the bank's Asian correspondent grid. The dominant operating model for the ISIC 7110 outsourcing business is invoice-based payables for subcontract drafting, cloud BIM licensing, and professional indemnity premiums; these are executed as plain MT103 'B2B' payments supported by the supplier's commercial invoice. For large subcontract awards exceeding USD 250,000, a Commercial Letter of Credit is available from ANZ or BRED but is unusual in services; an SBLC issued in favor of the principal client is the more relevant instrument, with the IC's own bank as advising bank. No RBV pre-approval is required for outbound service payments from a non-resident IC external account, but the bank will demand the invoice, the signed service agreement segment, and confirmation that the recipient is not on any sanctions list. Settlement timing is typically T+1 for USD/AUD/EUR; VUV clearing for any residual domestic expenses is executed through the same bank's local clearing system.Onshore FX Forwards (USD/AUD/EUR vs VUV and major crosses) written by authorized dealers (ANZ, BRED) for genuine commercial exposure up to 12 months; prohibited for speculative positions under Exchange Control Act [CAP 69].Offshore Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) in VUV executed by the IC's international counterparties (Singapore/Hong Kong desks) when hedging VUV-denominated residual receipts; Vanuatu banks may act as advisers but do not maintain a liquid onshore NDF market.Cross-Currency Swaps between USD, AUD, and EUR for balance-sheet hedging of multi-currency receivables and contractor payables, permitted upon demonstration of underlying service-trade exposure and recorded by the authorized dealer.Plain Vanilla FX Options on liquid pairs (USD/AUD, USD/EUR) available on a request-for-quote basis from ANZ/BRED treasury desks for large single exposures above USD 500,000; premium payments are freely remittable as financial-services expenses.

N/A — non-tax return The Form 2 filing contains IC name, registration number, registered office address, registered agent name, directors, shareholders and a declaration that no Vanuatu resident business was transacted. No income, loss, deduction or tax computation boxes are provided because no CIT applies. A non-resident IC shareholder must not enter Vanuatu tax particulars. The registered agent obtains beneficial-owner declarations, files the return and maintains the entity's tax-exempt status. Late filing penalty: VT 5,000 per month or part thereof, capped at VT 50,000. Continued failure may result in VFSC strike-off and loss of the zero-tax exemption.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No threshold (N/A) — Vanuatu has no transfer pricing legislation and no local file/master file requirement. If the IC belongs to a multinational group, foreign parent-tax rules may nevertheless compel group-level documentation.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu's Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply only to companies carrying on certain mobile activities. A pure offshore CAD/BIM provider with no Vanuatu staff, no Vanuatu office and no management in Vanuatu is outside the ESR scope. If it ever claims domestic service-centre substance in Vanuatu, it must satisfy annual substance reporting: adequate premises, full-time employees and adequate expenditure proportionate to income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / abuse of zero-tax regime

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act, the company may be fined up to VT 2,000,000; VFSC may strike off the company, revoke the exempt status and render the entity liable to VAT at 12% on any domestic supplies.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate may face joint and several civil liability, restitution orders, travel bans during investigation, and imprisonment up to 2 years.
Shell company / sham substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the International Companies Register; administrative penalty up to VT 500,000; contracts concluded after strike-off are void and any foreign court may disregard the separate legal personality.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for debts incurred after strike-off; Supreme Court may issue claw-back orders over dividends and director fees, disqualification for up to 5 years, and travel bans in asset-recovery proceedings.
AML/CFT failure and sanctioned payment rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act, a corporate penalty of up to VT 10,000,000; VFSC can freeze bank accounts, suspend the registered agent, and contest any Certificate of Good Standing.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal fines up to VT 5,000,000, imprisonment up to 10 years, confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act, and travel bans pending trial.
