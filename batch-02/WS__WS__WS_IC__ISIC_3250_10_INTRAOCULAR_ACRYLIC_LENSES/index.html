Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa; Self Access Revenue System (SARS) portal at www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) is incorporated by a licensed registered agent with the Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended). Within 30 days of incorporation, the registered agent must lodge the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office consent, director and shareholder particulars, and specimen signature with the Ministry for Revenue (MfR) to obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). For an IC that does not carry on business in Samoa, the MfR issues a TIN together with a Certificate of Tax Exemption confirming that the company is exempt from Samoan income tax, VAGST, stamp duty, and withholding tax. If the IC later elects to carry on business in Samoa, it must notify the MfR and obtain an amended tax registration as a resident commercial company.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election akin to US Subchapter S or Treasury check-the-box rules. An International Company is statutorily exempt from all Samoan taxes while it does not transact business with residents of Samoa. The IC may elect in its constitution to prohibit bearer shares, may elect to merge or migrate into and out of Samoa under the International Companies Act, and may voluntarily elect to be treated as a resident trading company, in which case the standard 27% corporate income tax and VAGST regime apply. No CFC or hybrid mismatch election is available under domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, including foldable hydrophobic intraocular acrylic lenses falling under ISIC 3250 / HS Chapter 90, may be zero-rated at 0% VAGST under Section 7 of the VAGST Act 2009 where the supplier exports the goods, directly or indirectly, in the course of a taxable business and the goods leave Samoa within 28 days of supply. The exporter must retain documentary proof that the goods were physically exported and sold to a non-resident customer; input VAT credits on attributable manufacturing inputs remain refundable. Offshore services supplied to non-residents who are outside Samoa at the time of supply are separately zero-rated where the territorial export test is satisfied.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration (CUSDEC) bearing customs endorsement
  • Commercial Invoice describing goods and stating 0% VAGST export treatment
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Samoa
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (Form A or non-preferential certificate)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

No national real-time e-invoicing, e-clearance, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force. Taxable persons must issue a valid VAGST invoice for every taxable supply and retain supporting records for seven years. VAT returns and payments are filed electronically through the MfR Self Access Revenue System (SARS), and customs export declarations are lodged electronically through the Samoa Customs electronic clearance system.

The Ministry for Revenue may audit any zero-rated export claim and refund within a seven-year limitation period and recover any refund or input tax credit erroneously claimed together with default interest of 1.5% per month, a late payment penalty of 10% of the outstanding tax, and an additional penalty equal to up to 100% of the tax shortfall for evasion or intentional misstatement. Fraudulent zero-rating and refund claims are criminal offences punishable by fines of up to WST 10,000, imprisonment of up to five years, disqualification of the registered agent, revocation of the exporter's status, and a retrospective clawback of all benefits claimed with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is partially convertible: current-account payments for trade, services, and interest are liberally processed through authorized dealers, but all capital-account transactions require prior CBS exchange-control approval. Profit and dividend remittances by the WS_IC International Company are permitted only with a valid CBS permit, audited after-tax profit evidence, a tax-clearance certificate from the Samoa Ministry of Revenue, and confirmation that all exchange-control reporting obligations are current. The WS_IC is tax-exempt but must nevertheless demonstrate genuine commercial substance in the intraocular-lens manufacturing chain; remittances above the declared threshold are reviewed on a per-transaction basis by the authorized dealer and CBS.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate all export receipts through an authorized dealer within 3 months from the date of export, with 100% mandatory conversion into WST unless the CBS grants a Foreign Currency Retention Account exemption (typically restricted to funding trade inputs within 180 days). For the cataract-lens production line, each inward credit must be matched against the relevant customs export declaration; any shortfall, delay, or discrepancy must be explained in writing to the authorized dealer and is reportable to CBS on the monthly exchange-control return.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), also known as Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa, exercising delegated exchange-control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1976 (as amended), with ultimate statutory oversight retained by the Minister of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low / not independently viable. Samoa does not license digital EMI entities, and offshore EMIs cannot provide WST-denominated accounts, domestic Samoan clearing, CBS reporting, or authorized-dealer FX functions. Offshore EMIs may serve only as secondary liquidity rails (e.g., USD pooled settlement accounts) after the primary local authorized-dealer relationship is fully established; any direct use of an EMI for Samoan FX transactions would violate the Exchange Control Regulations 1976.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard resident company rate under the Income Tax Act 2012, effective for income years commencing on or after 1 January 2024; previously 27%); effective rate for a validly registered International Company is 0% by virtue of section 198 of the International Companies Act 1987.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for an International Company: section 198 of the International Companies Act 1987 exempts the IC from all Samoan taxation, so dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders are not subject to Samoan dividend withholding tax. The general non-resident dividend withholding rate of 15% under Schedule 1 of the Income Tax Act 2012 is displaced for ICs.

Must be signed by a director; a false declaration triggers shortfall penalties and criminal liability. Late filing or non-payment of the annual fee attracts a late penalty of WST 200 plus interest at 10% per annum; default continuing beyond 3 months leads to administrative strike-off, vesting of assets in the Registrar, and a restoration fee of WST 1,000 together with all arrears.
Item 8 - Economic Substance Declaration Statement of whether the company carries on a relevant activity under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Rules 2021 (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, holding company, intellectual property, distribution and service centre). For a pure lens manufacturer with no IP-licensing or distribution function, declare 'none of the above'; retain evidence of board meetings, premises and employee presence in Samoa. Late filing or non-payment of the annual fee attracts a late penalty of WST 200 plus interest at 10% per annum; default continuing beyond 3 months leads to administrative strike-off, vesting of assets in the Registrar, and a restoration fee of WST 1,000 together with all arrears.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa Transfer Pricing Rules under the Income Tax Act 2012 and Tax Administration Act 2015: a Local File is mandatory where cross-border related-party turnover exceeds WST 10 million per income year; a Master File is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds WST 200 million. Below these thresholds, the IC must still retain a TP self-review matrix covering the lens-manufacturing intercompany margin and any IP or distribution functions.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Amendment Act 2021 and the International Companies (Economic Substance) Rules 2021 impose an economic substance requirement on ICs carrying on relevant activities. Manufacturing of intraocular acrylic lenses (ISIC 3250) is not a listed relevant activity per se; however, if the IC licenses lens-design IP, holds an IP box, or performs distribution and service centre functions tied to the Samoan registration, ESR reporting applies and the IC must demonstrate: (i) a physical office in Samoa; (ii) at least one full-time employee or managing director in Samoa; (iii) board meetings held in Samoa; and (iv) proportionate local operating expenditure. A pure letterbox company is conclusively presumed to lack substance, and the tax exemption may be reversed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Exemption and Shortfall

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 2015: a tax shortfall penalty of 150% of the avoided tax plus a fine of up to WST 100,000; criminal tax evasion carries imprisonment of up to 7 years on summary conviction or 10 years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the shortfall, penalties and civil restitution; the Commissioner may issue a departure prohibition order (travel ban) and freeze personal assets; conviction triggers director disqualification for up to 10 years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty of WST 25,000 plus WST 2,500 per month of continuing default; the Registrar may strike the IC off the register and treat the tax exemption as void for the income year in question.

Director personal liability: Where the IC traded while insolvent or the shell was used to misrepresent beneficial ownership, directors are personally liable for the company's unpaid fees and penalties, are subject to disqualification, and may be imprisoned for up to 3 years for fraudulent trading.
AML and Special-License Medical Device Trade

Statutory sanction: AML penalties of up to WST 500,000 per contravention; money-laundering predicate imprisonment of up to 14 years; revocation of the special medical-device manufacturing license and seizure of non-conforming products at the border.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face asset seizure, personal fines equal to twice the value of the laundered proceeds, travel bans, mutual-assistance extradition and imprisonment for the predicate AML offence; compliance officers with knowledge are individually accountable.
Unlicensed Regulated Manufacture and Export

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines of up to WST 50,000 per unlicensed batch; seizure and destruction of non-conforming devices; suspension of the IC's annual registration and automatic loss of tax-exempt status for the affected income year.

Director personal liability: The technical director and signatory directors are personally liable for fines, product recall costs and third-party compensation claims; criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 2 years and debarment from acting as a director in Samoa.
