Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

International Companies (ICs) are exempt from Samoa income tax under the International Companies Act 1987; there is no general check-the-box election. An IC may elect to become a tax resident in rare circumstances, but such election negates the offshore-exempt status and is generally inadvisable for non-resident owners. No applicable hybrid-mismatch election exists for ICs.

Zero-rating applies to the supply of goods physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply, dental/medical goods supplied overseas, and certain internationally connected services. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is managed under a controlled float against a trade-weighted basket dominated by USD, AUD and NZD. Current-account payments for goods and services flow freely through CBS-licensed authorized dealers, while capital-account transfers require documented CBS exchange-control approval. Resident companies face profit-remittance hurdles including WST surrender obligations and tax-clearance certificates. The International Company is statutorily non-resident, is prohibited from carrying on business with Samoan residents, and must transact exclusively in foreign currency (normally USD). Consequently, USD dividend and capital remittances to non-resident shareholders are freely executable after a CBS exchange-control notification, subject only to the IC's AML/KYC standing with its licensed bank and the absence of any WST-denominated local funding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must repatriate export proceeds to a licensed Samoan bank and offer the WST equivalent to an authorized dealer; CBS administrative guidance requires surrender within 90 days of shipment for goods on the Samoan export schedule. The International Company is exempt from the surrender regime: 100% of USD export receipts may be retained in the IC's designated USD clearing account at a CBS-licensed bank or held offshore, provided no conversion into WST occurs and no Samoan resident is a counterparty to the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Falefa, Apia, acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977; the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) co-supervises the International Company (IC) registry and economic-substance filings.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium restricted supporting role. Samoa maintains no fintech EMI or virtual-banking license; CBS does not recognize foreign EMI authorizations for holding WST or accessing local clearing. Global digital payment providers such as Airwallex, Currenxie or Wise may be used solely as non-routed pass-through vehicles for small supplier prepayments backed by a correspondent relationship with a CBS-licensed bank. Such EMIs cannot issue, advise, confirm or settle commercial letters of credit, cannot open Samoan resident accounts, and are not accepted as a primary clearing rail for Class III MedTech trade settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (statutory CIT rate for non-trading/passive-holding companies in Samoa; reduced to 25% for active trading and manufacturing companies under the Income Tax Act 1974 as amended). A Samoa International Company ('IC') registered under the International Companies Act 1987 is excluded from the CIT base: taxable income is deemed nil, producing a 0% effective rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% final non-resident withholding tax on dividends paid by a Samoan domestic company. Dividends and distributions declared by a Samoan International Company are wholly exempt from Samoan tax: the IC regime imposes no dividend withholding, and the effective repatriation rate is 0% in Samoa (foreign tax applies exclusively in the shareholder's residence jurisdiction).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return must be filed within six months after the end of the company's tax year; for a 31 December year-end, filing is due by 30 June of the following year. The IC Annual Return to the Registrar is due by the anniversary date of registration (or the 31 December of the year, as directed), failing which annual fees are doubled.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by the registration anniversary date (typically 31 December for calendar-year renewals); a grace period is available only when the territory is under a declared force majeure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has not enacted a formal Local File/Master File statutory threshold in its Income Tax Act; the MoR may, however, apply the arm's-length standard and adjust any associated-enterprise transaction under the general anti-avoidance and recharacterization provisions. Prudent compliance threshold for the IC: prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (master file, local file, and country-by-country indicators where applicable) whenever cross-border related-party flows attributable to the Samoa IC exceed WST 1,000,000 (approximately USD 380,000) per annum, or when the IC holds material intangible property (stent patents, trademarks, or regulatory approvals). Where the group is headed by or includes entities in jurisdictions applying BEPS Action 13 thresholds (e.g., EUR 750 million consolidated revenue for CbCR; EUR 50 million for Master File; EUR 3 million related-party sales for Local File), those thresholds bind the group and the Samoa entity must be covered.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Samoa applies Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) to International Companies carrying on 'relevant activities', including banking, financing, leasing, headquarters, shipping, holding-pure-equity, and intellectual property holding. A Samoa IC holding coronary drug-eluting stent IP (patents and regulatory dossiers) and receiving royalties or service income must demonstrate all of the following in Samoa: (a) direction and management (board meetings physically held in Samoa, minutes kept at the registered office); (b) adequate premises and equipment; (c) adequate qualified full-time employees in Samoa; (d) adequate annual operating expenditure in Samoa proportionate to income; and (e) core income-generating activities (DEMPE functions: development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, and exploitation of the IP) performed in Samoa. A pure IP-holding company is subject to enhanced substance scrutiny and a rebuttable presumption that its income requires substantial local DEMPE activity; failure leads to information exchange with the beneficial owner's tax administration, penalties, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion — Abuse of IC Tax-Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Retrospective withdrawal of the tax exemption; reassessment of CIT at 25%/27% on all income since exemption commencement; late-payment interest at 1.5% per month and penalties up to 100% of the under-assessed tax; mandatory automatic exchange of information (Global Forum, CRS/AEOI) leading to foreign assessments; deregistration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors and the licensed registered agent are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid taxes where the IC is struck off while liabilities remain; personal fines up to WST 100,000; imprisonment up to 5 years for knowingly authorizing false nil returns; travel bans and director disqualification orders available under Samoan company and tax administration law.
Economic Substance Failure — Shell Company / Harmful IP Regime

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of up to WST 150,000 per relevant activity for false ESR declarations; deregistration by the Registrar; adverse EU/OECD listing consequential countermeasures (refusals of deductions, withholding tax sanctions in EU jurisdictions); exchange of information with the beneficial owner's jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false ESR declarations face criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years; disqualification from managing any company in Samoa; the registered agent loses its licence and may be sued in negligence by affected shareholders; foreign courts may impose personal liability for the foreign tax consequences triggered by the sham substance.
AML/CFT Breach — High-Risk Class III Medical Trade Corridor

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines up to WST 1,000,000; asset freezing orders; revocation of the registered agent's licence; mandatory suspicious transaction reporting to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit; blacklisting of the IC under the high-risk medical-device trade typology; debanking by correspondent banks.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally indictable with imprisonment up to 14 years; personal confiscation of proceeds of crime; travel bans and extradition exposure under mutual legal assistance treaties; statutory duty of the registered agent to report directors who tolerate anonymous beneficiaries.
Misrepresentation to Revenue Authorities — False Exemption Certification

Statutory sanction: MoR audit and revocation of the exemption certificate; penalty equal to 200% of avoided tax; criminal fine up to WST 500,000; notification of the foreign tax authority through exchange of information; cancellation of the IC registration and the registered agent's consent to continue.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal restitution orders for taxes evaded; imprisonment up to 10 years for false certification; travel bans; in cross-border insolvency, a court may pierce the corporate veil and hold the ultimate controlling shareholders personally liable for misrepresentations to tax authorities.
Regulatory Fatal Red Flag — Unauthorized Class III Medical Device Marketing

Statutory sanction: Seizure and destruction of product at ports of entry; criminal product-liability sanctions in the destination jurisdiction; revocation of the Samoan IC and its registered agent; loss of DTA/exemption benefits if the regulating authority reports the fraud to the MoR or the Samoa FIU.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to personal product-liability judgments, extradition requests, and imprisonment in the destination jurisdiction; personal fines for export trade fraud; disqualification from future medical device trades; the corporate veil is pierced in most common-law destination jurisdictions for personal injury caused by a fake or mislabeled life-saving device.
