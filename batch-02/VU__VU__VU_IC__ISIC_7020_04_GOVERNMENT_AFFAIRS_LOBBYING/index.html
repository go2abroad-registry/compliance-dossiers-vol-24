Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Commission (VCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila; corporate/offshore-sector regulator and company registry: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC); primary revenue portal: https://customsinlandrevenue.gov.vu (VAT registration and filing); corporate registry portal: https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] holds no domestic income-tax registration and is not issued a TIN because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax and the IC is a statutory Tax_Exempt_Zone entity (exemption at s.112, IC Act [Cap. 222]). SOP: (1) obtain the IC certificate of incorporation and 'International Company' status from VFSC through a licensed registered agent; (2) use the VFSC company/registration number as the statutory identifier for all banking, contracting and KYC purposes; (3) VAT registration with VCIR is required only if the entity makes taxable supplies within Vanuatu — which an IC is statutorily prohibited from doing (no business with Vanuatu residents, no local landholding); consequently no VAT ID is procured for a pure offshore government-affairs advisory/lobbying operation; (4) should anomalous local revenue activity arise, file VCIR VAT registration Form 1 within 14 days of crossing the registration threshold and obtain a VAT number; (5) no customs, business-licence or social-security registrations apply to an offshore-only IC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the IC is exempt from all present and future Vanuatu taxes, duties, levies and imposts on income, profits, capital gains, distributions and branch results (IC Act [Cap. 222], s.112); the exemption is worldwide in scope but contingent on refraining from domestic Vanuatu-resident business; ICs are also outside the scope of the turnover-based VAT given the prohibition on local taxable supplies, preserving the IC as an offshore tax-exempt platform.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no income tax law; therefore no check-the-box, fiscal-transparent, or hybrid-mismatch elections exist for ICs, and entity-level tax exemption is automatic and non-elective. Available elections of consequence: (1) voluntary VAT registration under the VAT Act for a person below the compulsory turnover threshold (practically unavailable to an IC because of the prohibition on local taxable supplies); (2) election to convert the IC into a domestic company under the Companies Act [Cap. 191] — a one-way decision that terminates tax-exempt status and subjects the business to future local tax, VAT registration and business-licence obligations; (3) no CFC, thin-capitalisation, or transfer-pricing elections apply because no income tax or withholding tax is levied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 4,000,000 (approx. USD 34,000) of taxable turnover in any 12-month period for compulsory registration (VAT Act s.6); threshold and rate are fixed by national law and confirmed annually in the Appropriation Act — verify the prevailing figure with VCIR at onboarding; supplies made by a non-resident to Vanuatu recipients do not by themselves trigger registration unless the non-resident maintains a local fixed establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (i) exports of goods that physically leave Vanuatu; and (ii) 'exported services' — services supplied to a person who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply, the benefit of which is enjoyed wholly outside Vanuatu, including international consulting, cross-border trade-policy analysis, and foreign-stakeholder lobbying or advocacy services for non-resident principals. For the ISIC 7020 Government_Affairs_Lobbying entity, the standard criteria are: the principal is a non-resident foreign stakeholder; the engagement letter specifies offshore performance and foreign-law advisory; the invoice is addressed abroad in foreign currency; performance occurs and benefits are enjoyed outside Vanuatu; and no advocacy or related benefit is delivered in Vanuatu. Because an IC is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes and is barred from domestic business, it does not register for VAT; its offshore supplies are outside the VAT net, so there is no VAT to zero-rate and no refundable input VAT to claim. A domestic (non-IC) advisory entity would become VAT-registered only on crossing the VUV 4,000,000 threshold and would then zero-rate qualifying service exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World electronic goods declaration) — for any goods movements
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / freight receipt evidencing physical export from Vanuatu
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export or lobbying-services contract naming the non-resident principal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103/credit advice evidencing foreign-currency receipt of the export consideration
  • Recipient non-residency evidence (foreign incorporation certificate, foreign tax ID, or overseas address/official ID) substantiating offshore consumption

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, digital stamping or clearance e-invoicing regime applies in Vanuatu. VCIR accepts paper and PDF tax invoices. Registered persons must issue full tax invoices for taxable supplies (simplified/receipt invoices permitted for transactions under VUV 5,000), retain sufficient transactional records for audit, and file VAT returns on a monthly basis. A national digitalisation programme (ASYCUDA World) is being phased in for customs declarations, but there is currently no machine-readable continuous transaction-control obligation for services.

VCIR may audit zero-rated claims post-export and retrospectively claw back wrongly zero-rated output VAT with interest. VAT evasion and false-declaration offences (VAT Act s.76) carry a fine of up to VUV 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years; the Commissioner may additionally impose shortfall penalties and interest, and adjust input credits within the statutory assessment window. Vanuatu maintains no exchange controls and imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure in this dossier reflects prevailing banking/AML best practice for documenting inward remittances rather than a statutory clawback trigger. Fraudulent zero-rating documentation may concurrently expose the IC to VFSC deregistration, disqualification of its registered agent, and reporting or sanctions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2014.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is freely convertible on both current and capital account operations for International Companies. Vanuatu maintains no exchange control legislation. Profit remittance, dividend repatriation, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred offshore to non-resident shareholders without prior central bank approval. The practical hurdle is not regulatory restriction but correspondent banking due diligence and clearing-house reputation risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no mandatory surrender timeline or conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in any convertible foreign currency or repatriated to the International Company's settlement account with any licensed bank in Vanuatu. For an ISIC 7020 lobbying advisory entity, cross-border service fees and retainer payments are classified as service exports and are entirely exempt from surrender and conversion requirements. The only compliance obligation is statistical reporting under the RBV's Balance of Payments regulations for transactions exceeding VUV 5,000,000 per calendar year.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) – the statutory monetary authority and foreign exchange administrator under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap 125] and the International Companies Act [Cap 222].

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for regulated offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed in reputable fintech jurisdictions (e.g., United Kingdom FCA, Lithuania, Gibraltar, or Singapore) to support multi-currency treasury operations. Legal constraint: an offshore EMI has no direct clearing access to the RBV's local settlement systems; any VUV-denominated disbursement must ultimately route through a licensed Vanuatu commercial bank. Vanuatu currently has no domestic EMI licensing class, so the IC may engage an offshore EMI as a payment agent, provided the EMI's KYC and AML framework is at least equivalent to FATF standards.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Politically Exposed Person (PEP) status of lobbying principals or client legal beneficiaries

Root cause: Politically Exposed Person (PEP) status of lobbying principals or client legal beneficiaries

Defense evidence: Audited entity accounts for the preceding two years, executed client engagement contracts, a certified extract from the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) corporate registry, and an independent PEP screening certificate from a recognized provider showing no adverse matches or confirmations of legitimate non-executive advisory roles.
High-risk cross-border wire patterns to or from opaque jurisdictions or secrecy-haven territories

Root cause: High-risk cross-border wire patterns to or from opaque jurisdictions or secrecy-haven territories

Defense evidence: A source-of-wealth declaration signed by the beneficial owner, 12 months of original bank statements showing origin of funds, a board resolution approving each recurring wire with commercial rationale, and a treasury narrative memorandum demonstrating that the cross-border pattern is ordinary for government-relations retainer business.
Complex beneficial ownership structure utilizing nominee shareholders or offshore trusts

Root cause: Complex beneficial ownership structure utilizing nominee shareholders or offshore trusts

Defense evidence: Complete trust deed or nominee shareholder agreement, individual identification of settlors and all identifiable beneficiaries, an organizational ownership chart from the IC through to natural persons, and a certificate of current incumbency issued by the registered agent in Vanuatu evidencing economic substance and compliance with the International Companies Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 priority wire transfers. The acquiring Vanuatu bank mandates the inclusion of the IC's VFSC registration number and the purpose code 'EQUI' for share capital or 'LOAN' for shareholder subordinated debt. Inward remittance vouchers must carry the phrase 'International Company – Non-Resident Account' to preserve tax-exempt status. Verification comprises the bank's automated AML screening, a VU beneficiary ownership declaration form, and a real-time check against the RBV financial intelligence unit sanctions list. No central bank approval is required for capital infusion.The primary clearing rail for outbound retainer and advisory disbursements is SWIFT MT103 with SWIFT gpi (Global Payments Innovation) tracking for real-time confirmation. For large international policy-research procurement contracts, a Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP 600 may be issued through ANZ Vanuatu or BSP Vanuatu, with confirmation by a top-tier correspondent bank (e.g., Citi, HSBC, or Standard Chartered). Domestic disbursements inside Vanuatu settle via the local Vanuatu Automated Transfer System (VATS), which clears VUV in T+0. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual renewal is due on or before each anniversary of incorporation. A VAT return is required quarterly, due within 30 days after the end of each quarter, if the IC exceeds the VAT registration threshold.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local transfer pricing documentation threshold exists under Vanuatu law, and no Local File or Master File is required. For multinational groups, the common reporting threshold of EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue applies only under foreign CbC rules; Vanuatu does not mandate or exchange CbC reports.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu International Companies are subject to the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019. ESR applies to relevant activities: banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, holding company, intellectual property holding, and distribution/service centres. Government affairs lobbying and management consultancy (ISIC 7020) is not itself a listed relevant activity; however, an IC that also operates as a distribution and service centre or IP holding vehicle must satisfy the full economic substance test, including being directed and managed in Vanuatu, maintaining physical premises in Vanuatu, having adequate staff and expenditure in Vanuatu, and performing core income-generating activities in Vanuatu.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Foreign revenue authorities may recharacterise the IC as a sham and impose underpayment penalties of 100% to 200% of the tax avoided, plus interest. In Vanuatu, a false declaration to the VFSC may trigger a fine of up to VUV 1,000,000 and deregistration.

Director personal liability: Nominee and shadow directors who knowingly enable the sham can be held personally liable in the foreign jurisdiction for unpaid corporate taxes, subjected to civil asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to seven years.
Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: VFSC may levy a financial penalty of up to VUV 1,000,000, order rectification, or strike the company off the register; repeat violations may attract criminal prosecution and higher fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure compliance with the ESR may be personally fined, disqualified from managing any Vanuatu company for up to five years, and face imprisonment of up to two years if contempt is proven.
Anti-Money Laundering / Corruption

Statutory sanction: Under Vanuatu AML/CFT laws, a company may face civil penalties up to VUV 10,000,000, revocation of its registered agent or financial license, freezing of bank accounts, and forfeiture of illegal proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be subject to personal fines of up to VUV 25,000,000 and imprisonment up to 15 years for money laundering, bribery conspiracy, or wilful blindness to corruption.
Shell Company Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: The International Companies Act imposes fines of up to VUV 1,000,000 per false declaration and permits the Registrar to cancel the company's registration, effectively dissolving it and preventing the company from enforcing contracts.

Director personal liability: Signatories of false statutory declarations accept personal criminal liability and can be barred from serving as directors in Vanuatu for a cumulative period of five years; foreign courts may pierce the corporate veil and render the foreign principal and nominal directors jointly and severally liable.
