Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of the Independent State of Samoa (MfR), formerly the Department of Inland Revenue — Tax Online Services (TOS) Portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) is incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 and, upon issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation, the Registered Officer or licensed Nominee applies to the Commissioner of Revenue through the Tax Online Services (TOS) portal for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the standard TIN application form accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation, Registered Office Certificate, and Memorandum & Articles of Association. The MfR registers the IC as a non-resident, tax-exempt entity and may issue a Tax Exemption Certificate upon payment of the annual government fee; no separate VAGST registration is required where the IC's turnover derives exclusively from offshore towage and salvage services supplied to non-residents.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) is incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 and, upon issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation, the Registered Officer or licensed Nominee applies to the Commissioner of Revenue through the Tax Online Services (TOS) portal for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the standard TIN application form accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation, Registered Office Certificate, and Memorandum & Articles of Association. The MfR registers the IC as a non-resident, tax-exempt entity and may issue a Tax Exemption Certificate upon payment of the annual government fee; no separate VAGST registration is required where the IC's turnover derives exclusively from offshore towage and salvage services supplied to non-residents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended), an IC may statutorily elect under section 12 to opt in to the Companies Act 2001 (becoming subject to general Samoan company law) or may apply to redomicile; no US-style check-the-box election exists under Samoan tax law, but the Income Tax Ordinance 2015 applies OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules governing offshore hybrid entities, and the IC may voluntarily register for VAGST solely to recover input tax (subject to MfR approval).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 150,000 annual taxable turnover of goods or services supplied in Samoa; an International Company supplying only offshore deep-sea towage and maritime salvage services to non-residents falls outside the territorial nexus and is not required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the VAGST Act 2007 applies to: (a) goods physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply; (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply, including deep-sea tugboat towage and emergency maritime salvage contracts performed entirely outside Samoan territorial waters under Lloyd's Open Form or SCOPIC; and (c) supplies to holders of a valid International Company certificate where the supply is used exclusively in the offshore business. The supplier must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence of export performance and foreign-currency settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World CusDec)
  • Bill of Lading or Computer-Generated Sea Waybill evidencing offshore towage/salvage performance
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT/telex credited advice issued by a Samoa commercial bank) evidencing receipt of foreign currency

No real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is imposed. Taxable persons must issue full tax invoices in the prescribed VAGST format, retain electronic records, and lodge returns via the Tax Online Services (TOS) portal on monthly or quarterly cycles; the MfR operates automated risk-based matching of ASYCUDA World customs export declarations against zero-rating claims.

Under the Tax Administration Act 2012 and VAGST Act 2007, fraudulent export zero-rating or refund claims attract a tax shortfall penalty of up to 200% of the underpaid tax, late-payment interest at 2% per month, fines of up to WST 25,000 per offence, and imprisonment of up to 7 years on conviction; refunds are retrospectively clawed back and subject to set-off where supporting export documentation is not produced within the statutory 180-day repatriation window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The WST is convertible on current-account grounds for bona fide trade, service, and shipping transactions through authorised dealer banks, while capital-account transactions require individual CBS approval. Non-resident International Companies are exempt from the Exchange Control Act 1976 by section 208 of the International Companies Act 1987 (unless CBS directs otherwise), so the IC may invoice, accumulate, and remit offshore towage and salvage awards (LOF 2020, SCOPIC) in USD, EUR, or AUD without conversion into Tala. Practical hurdles are: (i) authorised dealers require a CBS exemption affirmation and clean IC corporate records before crediting foreign-currency proceeds; (ii) wires entered without the s.208 exemption narrative trigger a 2-5 business day CBS compliance review; and (iii) any WST-denominated local disbursement invokes exchange-control reporting of the underlying salvage or towage service.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident export traders under the Exchange Control Regulations 1977 must collect proceeds within 6 months of shipment and surrender 100% to an authorised dealer in WST unless CBS grants a retention licence. Where the Samoa International Company (WS_IC) remains non-resident, the s.208 ICA exemption supersedes the surrender and conversion regime for ISIC 5222 proceeds; salvage awards, SCOPIC uplifts, and charter hire may be accumulated abroad and remitted offshore without conversion. The bank requires an annual CBS notification certificate, the LOF/GTCon contract trail, and audited accounts demonstrating that no Tala conversion has occurred in order to maintain the exemption architecture.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa, established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; the CBS administers the Exchange Control Act 1976 and Exchange Control Regulations 1977, the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (as amended to 2020), and the Financial Intelligence Unit regime; it is the sole statutory curator of the Samoa Tala (WST).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Samoa has no fintech or electronic-money-institution licensing regime, and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (as amended) treats unlicensed payment providers as unlawful financial institutions; foreign digital EMIs uniformly decline Samoa-IC onboarding for maritime-salvage business and no offshore Tala NDF or EMI bridge exists. The viable treasury stack remains a CBS-licensed commercial bank account for operating liquidity plus a London solicitors' Lloyds Standard Form escrow for casualty security and award settlement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a licensed Samoa International Company holding a valid tax exemption certificate under the International Companies Act 1987; 27% statutory rate for ordinary Samoa-resident companies under the Income Tax Act 2015 (as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for an International Company while the tax exemption certificate is in force; the general Samoa statutory dividend withholding tax rate applicable to ordinary resident companies is 15% under the Income Tax Act 2015.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has not published a statutory monetary threshold for transfer pricing documentation; the Income Tax Act 2015 imposes an arm's-length standard on all cross-border related-party dealings; in practice, an International Company engaged in offshore towage and salvage should prepare and retain a Local File and Master File when annual related-party transactions exceed WST 1,000,000, or when related-party operating expenses exceed WST 500,000 in an exempt IC structure; contemporaneous documentation must be produced within 30 days of a SIRD request.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act and associated Regulations require Samoa International Companies engaged in geographically mobile activities including shipping, towage, and maritime transport support to satisfy a Samoa economic substance test: (i) the IC must be managed and directed from Samoa, with a majority of board meetings held in Samoa; (ii) core income-generating activities such as strategic chartering, fleet deployment, salvage project approval, and risk management must occur in Samoa; (iii) the IC must maintain adequate qualified employees, physical premises, and annual operating expenditure in Samoa proportionate to its activity; and (iv) an annual Economic Substance Return must be filed with the Registrar; failure to comply results in penalties, information exchange with the OECD Inclusive Framework, and potential loss of the IC tax exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 150% of the tax underpaid; criminal fine of up to WST 1,000,000; automatic cancellation of the 20-year tax exemption certificate; strike-off from the International Companies Register; adverse findings reported to foreign tax authorities under the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance and bilateral Tax Information Exchange Agreements.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the restitution of evaded tax and penalties; deliberate falsification attracts personal imprisonment of up to 7 years; on-exit travel bans and asset freezing orders may be imposed against directors and the beneficial owner.
Shell Company and Sham Directorship

Statutory sanction: Deregistration of the IC and nullification of the tax exemption certificate with retrospective effect; imposition of a 100% penalty on undeclared income; designation as a shell company in the jurisdiction's risk rating, triggering automatic exchange of information with the ultimate shareholder's residence state; denial of treaty benefits under limitation-on-benefits clauses.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face disqualification from acting as directors and substantial regulatory fines; the foreign beneficial owner is treated as a shadow director and becomes personally liable for the company's debts and tax penalties if the IC traded while insolvent or without economic substance.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Violation in Maritime Payments

Statutory sanction: Corporate fine of up to WST 500,000; freezing of the IC's bank accounts; revocation of the IC registration; cancellation of the maritime towage licence; seizure and confiscation of the towage vessels under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and beneficial owners face imprisonment up to 5 years, travel bans, confiscation of personal assets, and exclusion from future maritime licensing; directors who knowingly permitted the movement of proceeds of crime are personally liable for civil restitution to the state.
Improper Profit Repatriation and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the distribution as assessable income of the IC with a 20% penalty tax; recovery of unpaid withholding tax at 15% plus interest; penalty of WST 100 per day for each incomplete statutory record; deregistration for continued non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted withholding tax and associated penalties if they authorised unlawful distributions; the Ministry of Revenue may impose travel restrictions and garnish director bank accounts to recover the shortfall.
