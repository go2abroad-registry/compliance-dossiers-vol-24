Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue (MOR), Samoa - central tax administration headed by the Commissioner of Revenue; official eServices portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws (business TIN registration, VAGST returns, payroll returns, tax clearance certificates). Customs export formalities are processed by Samoa Customs Service through the ASYCUDA World national clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 through a licensed registered agent at the Registrar of International and Foreign Companies (Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour); Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing issue in 3-5 working days with no minimum capital requirement. Step 2 - The IC is not required to obtain a revenue TIN for its exempt offshore business; it maintains tax-exempt status through annual Registrar renewal and compliance with the non-resident business restriction. Step 3 - Obtain a 9-digit MOR Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) at the first point of contact with the Samoan tax system, namely voluntary VAGST registration, employer PAYE/WHT registration, customs import transactions, or any taxable activity in Samoa; the licensed agent lodges Form TIN-01 with certified incorporation documents, director and beneficial ownership declarations (AML/CFT Act 2018), and the registered office/agent certificate; TIN issuance takes 5-10 business days. Step 4 - Quote the TIN on all tax returns, customs declarations and Revenue correspondence; a VAGST registration number is the same TIN with a 'VG' suffix.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - under sections 198-202 of the International Companies Act 1987, the IC enjoys a statutory territorial exemption from all Samoan taxes, including income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, gift and estate duties, and is free from exchange control. The exemption applies only where the IC does not carry on business with Samoa residents, has no Samoan-source income, and has no permanent establishment in Samoa as recharacterized under the Income Tax Act 2019 general anti-avoidance rules.

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box entity classification election. Available statutory elections relevant to an IC: (1) voluntary VAGST registration under section 10 of the VAGST Act 2003 to recover Samoan input tax on local overheads; (2) employer registration election for PAYE/withholding tax once Samoan employees are hired; (3) re-domiciliation or migration election converting the IC into an ordinary company under the Companies Act 2006, which surrenders the section 198 exemption; and (4) elections are not available for consolidated or exempt filing with MOR - an IC files annual returns solely with the Registrar of International and Foreign Companies. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized by the Income Tax Act 2019 general anti-avoidance and substance-over-form provisions; no elective hybrid mismatch or consolidated tax regime exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 100,000 (Samoan Tala, approx. USD 37,000) of annual taxable supplies; registration is mandatory once actual or reasonably expected turnover meets or exceeds SAT 100,000, with voluntary registration available for exporters and suppliers of zero-rated goods and services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) goods physically exported from Samoa under a valid customs export declaration and (b) services supplied to a non-resident where the benefit of the service is received wholly outside Samoa and is not consumed or enjoyed in Samoa, including B2B marketplace platform and cross-border digital wholesale services. An International Company whose marketplace revenue is derived exclusively from offshore counterparties is outside the scope of VAGST entirely (no output tax liability); if the IC voluntarily registers, the same offshore services qualify for zero-rating. Substantiation requires an export contract, commercial invoice, customs export declaration (goods), transport documents, bank credit evidence, and, for digital services, non-resident evidence and platform usage logs proving offshore consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World) - mandatory exit record evidencing physical removal of goods from Samoa
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / electronic waybill - through-transport document evidencing export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice - evidencing receipt of the foreign sales consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, API, or government pre-approval e-invoicing mandate exists in Samoa. Suppliers must issue invoices containing prescribed particulars (supplier and recipient identity, VAGST registration number, nature of supply, tax-exclusive and inclusive amounts, and VAGST amount). VAGST returns and customs declarations are lodged electronically through the MOR eServices portal and the ASYCUDA World customs clearance system, which interoperate for import and export control, but individual invoices are not cleared or stamped in real time.

The Commissioner of Revenue may deny, claw back, and assess any zero-rated refund or exemption where export documentation is false, incomplete, or non-genuine (VAGST Act 2003 ss. 53-62 and Tax Administration Act 2019). Civil exposure includes repayment of the refunded tax, shortfall penalties of 20% for failure to take reasonable care and up to 100% for intentional or evasive false statements, plus statutory late-payment interest of approximately 2% per month or part thereof. Criminal exposure includes fines and imprisonment for fraudulent export declarations and tax evasion under the Customs Act 1977 and Tax Administration Act 2019. For International Companies, misuse of the section 198 exemption to channel Samoan-source activity triggers immediate de-exemption with full income tax assessment from inception, and the licensed registered agent may be jointly penalized for facilitating the non-compliant structure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains a regulated but effectively convertible exchange control regime for current account transactions. Current account payments, including trade settlement, service fees, dividends, and management fees, may be processed through an authorized dealer in Samoa upon presentation of authentic underlying commercial documentation. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity subscription, shareholder loans, M&A proceeds, and capital repatriation, require either CBS approval or an exemption certificate where available. An International Company (IC) organized under the Samoa International Companies Act 1987 is treated as non-resident for exchange control purposes and is permitted to maintain fully foreign-currency-denominated accounts without mandatory conversion into Samoan Talā (WST). Profit remittance by an IC is subject to minimal friction provided the IC does not carry on business with Samoa residents, does not hold Samoan land or trust assets requiring licensing, and maintains transparent beneficial ownership records. The principal practical hurdles are anti-money laundering and know-your-customer compliance rather than exchange control exhaustion; unsubstantiated cross-border settlements or transactions with prohibited jurisdictions will be rejected by the correspondent banking layer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident commercial enterprises in Samoa, CBS Exchange Control Regulations require export proceeds to be repatriated to Samoa through an authorized dealer within 90 days from the date of shipment, with mandatory conversion into WST where the exporter trades locally in WST. A resident exporter must retain documentary evidence of the export contract, customs export declaration, and payment receipt for audit by CBS upon demand. For a non-resident Samoa International Company engaged in cross-border B2B marketplace facilitation, no export-proceeds surrender is mandated: the IC must be listed as the exporter or principal on the relevant commercial documents only when goods are sold into Samoa. In the typical offshore IC distribution model where the platform controls sales from third-country warehouses to overseas buyers, the IC's foreign-currency receipts are retained freely in the offshore USD/EUR settlement account, provided the IC maintains a written non-resident declaration with its registered agent and files an annual compliance certificate with CBS if requested.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), acting under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, the Exchange Control Act 1976, and the Exchange Control Regulations 1979, as the sole monetary authority and exchange control administrator for the Independent State of Samoa.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for secondary settlement flows, Low for primary treasury custody. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or Revolut Business may be used as payment aggregation rails for B2B marketplace receipts and disbursements, but they are not licensed by the Central Bank of Samoa and cannot substitute for a commercial bank relationship. Legal constraints include: (i) the IC must execute a cross-border payment processing agreement in writing with the EMI, and the EMI must maintain settlement funds in segregated accounts at a top-tier OECD bank; (ii) all funds landing in Samoa must ultimately be routed to a CBS-licensed bank for clearing; (iii) the EMI must be established in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction and must provide full transaction-level reporting to the Samoan bank upon request; and (iv) use of an EMI may trigger enhanced due diligence if the aggregate monthly throughput exceeds USD 100,000, in which case a treasury reconciliation pack, signed auditor confirmation, and up-to-date sanctions screening must be maintained.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

High-velocity cross-border electronic settlements via Alibaba/Pay, Payoneer, or other platform rails without a demonstrable goods-and-services trail.

Root cause: High-velocity cross-border electronic settlements via Alibaba/Pay, Payoneer, or other platform rails without a demonstrable goods-and-services trail.

Defense evidence: A complete order-level reconciliation bundle containing (i) the platform's settlement statement mentioning every transaction ID and invoice number; (ii) commercial invoices and packing lists for each sales order; (iii) third-party logistics shipment tracking and delivery confirmation; (iv) customs declarations or electronic export notifications; and (v) an auditor-prepared treasury report showing that the settlement amounts match the invoiced receivable ledger.
Beneficial ownership opacity through bearer shares, nominee shareholders, or a chain of discretionary trust holding companies.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity through bearer shares, nominee shareholders, or a chain of discretionary trust holding companies.

Defense evidence: A current Register of Members issued by the Samoa registered agent; a declaration that no bearer shares are outstanding or, if originally issued, evidence that bearer share certificates have been cancelled and placed with a licensed custodian; full CHART of beneficial ownership up to natural persons; and a certified copy of the constitutional documentation confirming that the IC is validly registered under the International Companies Act 1987.
Unusually high capital injection or shareholder loan from a third-party jurisdiction with inadequate source-of-wealth proof.

Root cause: Unusually high capital injection or shareholder loan from a third-party jurisdiction with inadequate source-of-wealth proof.

Defense evidence: Bank statements evidencing the accumulation of funds over at least 12 months; a notarised sworn declaration of source of wealth; a copy of the board resolution approving the injection; an updated investor register entry; and, where the capital is provided through an intermediate holding vehicle, the holding vehicle's memorandum of association and audited financial statements demonstrating lawful origin.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscriptions and shareholder loan advances should be wired from the investing entity's own bank account to the IC's beneficiary foreign-currency account at a CBS-licensed Samoan commercial bank using SWIFT MT103 for single payments under USD 50,000 and MT202 COV for larger-value transfers. The SWIFT payment fields must contain: beneficiary name matching the IC certificate exactly; the IC's registered account number; remitter name and full address; and the purpose-of-payment code corresponding to 'equity injection' under the Balance of Payments classification, typically 'FA-DIRECT INVESTMENT - EQUITY' (or Samoa's BOP code 3131 if required by the receiving bank). The remittance narrative field should include the phrase 'CAPITAL INJECTION TO SAMOA IC - NON-RESIDENT ACCOUNT' plus the registered agent's reference. To prevent freezing at the beneficiary bank, the IC must pre-clear the injection by submitting board resolution, updated register of members, and a capital verification letter from the Samoa registered agent confirming the non-resident status; the bank may require source-of-funds certification for injections exceeding USD 100,000 in any 12-month period.For trade settlements of the B2B marketplace, principal clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 bank-to-bank wire transfers for supplier payments, quoting 'INV-[invoice number]' and the relevant Incoterms in the payment narrative; (b) Commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C) issued by the Samoan bank in favour of the supplier for procurement above USD 50,000, allowing trade settlement against shipping documents; and (c) local WST clearing through Samoa's automated interbank payment system for any settlement to domestic Samoan logistics or warehouse service providers. E-commerce platform settlement receipts, e.g., from Alibaba/Pay and similar aggregators, may arrive as a single consolidated batch; the treasury management system must match the batch to individual order IDs before an internal clearing instruction is released. The IC's bank will require that all payments are made exclusively to account beneficiaries identified on the board-approved ACL and that outgoing wires include standard financial crime prevention messages consistent with the Wolfsberg correspondent banking transparency principles.Outright deliverable foreign exchange forward contracts on USD/WST, EUR/USD, and USD/GBP with a CBS-licensed commercial bank, hedged against documented commercial exposure such as supplier invoices, platform settlement currencies, and receivables conversion, with a maturity not exceeding 12 months.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on WST/USD or USD/CNH transacted through a non-bank financial institution outside Samoa, permissible where the underlying exposure is a genuine cross-border B2B flow and the hedge is excluded from the onshore WST deliverable market to avoid regulatory conflict with CBS exchange control.Cross-currency swaps between USD and EUR or USD and AUD for matching mismatched procurement and settlement cashflows, available only with a licensed international bank counterparty and subject to the IC's board-approved treasury policy and CBS notification where the swap creates a foreign currency liability of the IC.Currency options (call/put) on USD/EUR or USD/WST purchased on a premium-paid basis for protection against adverse platform-currency movements, permitted when the notional amount does not exceed 150% of forecast settlement receipts for the corresponding period and when the option is fully documented in the treasury management file.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for secondary settlement flows, Low for primary treasury custody. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or Revolut Business may be used as payment aggregation rails for B2B marketplace receipts and disbursements, but they are not licensed by the Central Bank of Samoa and cannot substitute for a commercial bank relationship. Legal constraints include: (i) the IC must execute a cross-border payment processing agreement in writing with the EMI, and the EMI must maintain settlement funds in segregated accounts at a top-tier OECD bank; (ii) all funds landing in Samoa must ultimately be routed to a CBS-licensed bank for clearing; (iii) the EMI must be established in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction and must provide full transaction-level reporting to the Samoan bank upon request; and (iv) use of an EMI may trigger enhanced due diligence if the aggregate monthly throughput exceeds USD 100,000, in which case a treasury reconciliation pack, signed auditor confirmation, and up-to-date sanctions screening must be maintained.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% effective for an International Company (IC) under the tax-exempt zone regime; the standard Samoa corporate income tax headline rate for taxable resident companies is 27% (Income Tax Ordinance 2015).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions paid by an exempt Samoan International Company to non-resident shareholders (statutory IC exemption overrides the general 15% non-resident dividend withholding tax under the Income Tax Ordinance 2015). Profit repatriation may be executed by board resolution without Samoan WHT or exchange control approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has no published Local File/Master File thresholds; the arm's-length principle applies where a non-exempt IC has related-party cross-border transactions. For the tax-exempt B2B platform entity, the Samoa Revenue may require contemporaneous documentation where related-party platform fees or intercompany payments exceed WST 250,000 per annum. Prudent practice: prepare a thin Local File for annual intercompany service flows above WST 500,000, even though the IC is exempt.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not apply OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) to International Companies. However, the International Companies Act 2008 mandates a licensed resident agent, a registered physical office, and maintenance of proper accounting records. The IC exemption is conditional: no Samoa-source income, no transactions with Samoa residents, and management activities consistent with bona fide international business. Non-compliant entities are deemed shell vehicles, subject to strike-off and domestic taxation at 27%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Assessed tax at 27% on the undeclared worldwide income; penalty equal to 200% of the tax evaded (or WST 100,000, whichever is greater); interest at 15% per annum; criminal fine of up to WST 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years under the Income Tax Ordinance 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax where they knew or ought reasonably to have known of the concealment. Courts may order civil restitution of misappropriated funds, issue travel bans preventing departure from Samoa, and impose disqualification orders of up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the International Companies Register under section 199 of the International Companies Act 2008; declaration of the IC as a dissolved and escheated company; fines on the agent up to WST 50,000; registration in the International Tax Bulletin as a 'non-compliant/transparent' entity.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil under the Companies Act 2006 fraudulent trading provisions, holding directors personally accountable for the company's debts up to WST 500,000; a personal asset freeze and confiscation order may extend to beneficiaries who received hidden distributions.
Anti-Money Laundering Breach (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 2,000,000 against the IC and WST 500,000 against each officer under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2017; license revocation of the resident agent; suspension of the IC's right to accept wire payments; inclusion on the Samoa AML watchlist.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines of WST 250,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years for wilful blindness to money laundering. Travel bans are mandatory for accused persons; the court may order disgorgement of all platform fees earned during the breach period and prohibit re-entry to Samoa for up to 5 years.
False Declaration / Fraud on the Registrar

Statutory sanction: Fine of WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the International Companies Act 2008; automatic forfeiture of the IC exemption; retroactive CIT assessment for 5 years with the 200% evasion penalty.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and the resident agent are jointly liable to the Revenue for the recalculated tax; the court may order a recovery judgment against personal estates, impose a travel ban, and issue a 15-year corporate management disqualification. Willful fraud is an extraditable offence under Samoa's Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act.
Withholding Tax Failure on Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment at 27% on the gross payment; withholding tax of 15% imposed on the gross amount; penalty of 150% of the unpaid WHT; disallowance of the interest/royalty deduction for the payer.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid WHT where they authorized the payment while knowing the payer was non-exempt; the court may order restitution equivalent to the tax plus penalties, freeze director bank accounts, and impose imprisonment of up to 3 years for deliberate non-remittance.
