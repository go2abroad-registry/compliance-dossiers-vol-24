Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (Maota o Lafoga), Government of Samoa — successor to the Samoa Revenue Authority and the former Inland Revenue Division of the Ministry of Finance; official online services portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws (e-Tax registration, e-filing, GST returns and customs declarations). International Company licensing is administered separately by the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity first files a Business and Taxpayer Registration application with the Ministry for Revenue under the Tax Administration Act 2015, providing certified charter/constitutive documents, registered office address in Samoa, director and beneficial-owner particulars, and (where relevant) a GST registration application under the Goods and Services Tax Act 2007. For an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987: (1) the company is incorporated/licensed through SIFA and the Samoa Registrar of International Companies; (2) a TIN/Tax ID is issued by the Ministry for Revenue upon registration of the IC's offshore status; (3) a Tax Exemption Certificate is obtained confirming that the IC is outside the Samoan tax charge for income tax, GST and stamp duty on offshore transactions; (4) GST registration is only obligatory if the IC commences taxable activities in Samoa with turnover exceeding WST 100,000.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign entity first files a Business and Taxpayer Registration application with the Ministry for Revenue under the Tax Administration Act 2015, providing certified charter/constitutive documents, registered office address in Samoa, director and beneficial-owner particulars, and (where relevant) a GST registration application under the Goods and Services Tax Act 2007. For an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987: (1) the company is incorporated/licensed through SIFA and the Samoa Registrar of International Companies; (2) a TIN/Tax ID is issued by the Ministry for Revenue upon registration of the IC's offshore status; (3) a Tax Exemption Certificate is obtained confirming that the IC is outside the Samoan tax charge for income tax, GST and stamp duty on offshore transactions; (4) GST registration is only obligatory if the IC commences taxable activities in Samoa with turnover exceeding WST 100,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows the International Companies Act 1987 and Income Tax Act 2015. Available statutory elections: IC tax-exempt election via SIFA (Certificate of Exemption for offshore-only International Companies, including offshore stevedoring/terminal revenues); voluntary GST registration election (s.8 GST Act 2007) for supply of domestic taxable goods/services; option to maintain a separate resident subsidiary/branch for on-island terminal operations which is taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act 2015; no domestic hybrid-mismatch election rules are enacted, and hybrid arrangements are policed through the general anti-avoidance provisions of the Tax Administration Act 2015.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (compulsory registration where taxable supplies in Samoa exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed WST 100,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration available with the Commissioner's approval). Non-resident suppliers are registrable only if they carry on a taxable activity in Samoa; offshore-only International Companies are outside the GST charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply qualifies for the 0% GST rate if it is an export of goods (including bulk cargo loaded at a marine stevedoring terminal) with physical departure from Samoa within the statutory window; a supply of services to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply where the benefit is consumed offshore; international carriage of cargo or passengers; or ancillary supplies such as stevedoring, stevedoring supervision, dock handling and wharf services rendered under contract to a non-resident shipowner/carrier in connection with ocean-going export vessels or cargo moving between Samoa and foreign ports. The exporter/service supplier must retain conclusive evidence of the export supply, issue an export '0% GST' tax invoice, and separately disclose zero-rated supplies in the GST return to substantiate input tax credits/refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs / Ministry for Revenue export entry via ASYCUDA World showing ISIC 5224 stevedoring cargo, port of loading and vessel)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or equivalent combined-transport document endorsed by the carrier for the bulk cargo movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Samoan commercial bank confirming receipt of the export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Stevedoring service order / terminal handling agreement evidencing the supply to the non-resident customer at the 0% export rate
  • Samoa Ports Authority wharfage and harbour-dues invoice together with the cargo manifest reconciliation report

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal-clearance, API stamping or digital-clearance regime is currently operational in Samoa. GST-registered businesses must issue compliant tax invoices (supplier name, TIN, invoice serial number, date of issue, description, GST-exclusive and GST-inclusive amounts or the 0% export statement) and file GST returns monthly or quarterly through the Ministry for Revenue e-services portal. The authority performs risk-based e-audits and data-matching against ASYCUDA World Customs export entries, bank FX flows and CRS/CbC datasets; invoice-level clearance is not yet mandated.

Under the Tax Administration Act 2015, the Commissioner may reopen zero-rated export claims within 36 months of the end of the return period in cases of fraud, wilful misrepresentation or evasive intent (generally 24 months for ordinary negligence). False zero-rating of a domestic supply is treated as GST evasion: shortfall penalty of up to 200% of the tax undercharged, daily late-payment interest and penalties, monetary fines of up to WST 20,000 under the GST Act 2007, imprisonment of up to 2 years, retrospective clawback of every input tax credit claimed on the false export invoice, denial/withholding of refunds until the complete customs and FX documentation bundle is produced, and referral to the Attorney General for criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is a non-internationally convertible currency and is not freely deliverable offshore; Samoan banking settlements in foreign currency are conducted in USD, EUR, AUD or NZD through the licensed bank's correspondent network. Current-account payments for genuine stevedoring, wharfage, and cargo-handling services are generally allowed if supported by commercial invoices, customs entries, and the relevant vessel documentation. Capital-account inflows and outflows, including the initial equity injection, related-party loans, repayment of shareholder debt, and periodic dividend/profit remittance, require either prior CBS exchange-control approval or a post-transaction reporting compliance process. Profit remittance to a foreign shareholder is lawful only after (i) audited statutory financial statements are available, (ii) the dividend or profit distribution satisfies Samoa's solvency and company-law requirements, (iii) any applicable dividend withholding tax is paid or certified as exempt, and (iv) the Ministry for Revenue issues a tax-clearance status on the remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the WS_IC port-stevedoring terminal, all foreign-currency receipts arising from international vessels and cargo services are treated as export-like earnings and must be repatriated to a licensed commercial bank in Samoa within 180 days from the invoice date or the date of loading/discharge, unless a CBS-approved foreign-currency retention arrangement is obtained. There is no mandatory WST conversion percentage under Samoa's current exchange-control practice; consequently the surrender ratio is 0% of the foreign-currency receipt. Once repatriated, the stevedoring company may either retain the proceeds in a CBS-approved interest-bearing foreign-currency account or sell them to the licensed bank at the prevailing CBS reference rate to fund WST payroll, port dues, customs duties, and local tax liabilities. Any request to extend the 180-day period must be supported by the terminal service contract, an ageing schedule of receivables, and a treasury cash-flow projection.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank Act 1984, is the national monetary authority and the administrator of exchange control, bank licensing, and the Samoa Interbank Settlement System. Its Exchange Control Division and Financial Intelligence Unit approve capital remittance transactions and enforce AML/CFT obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Restricted/Low. No passported EU or UK EMI licence is automatically recognised in Samoa; an offshore digital EMI may not alone be used for the WS_IC treasury because Samoan money-transfer and banking regulations require an onshore licensed bank or a licensed remitter to report transactions to the Central Bank and to clear WST obligations. A CBS-licensed money remitter may be added as an ancillary rail for small-value pre-funded beneficiary payments, but the core inbound capital and outbound trade disbursements must flow through a licensed commercial bank. The WS_IC may use an EMI only for temporary pre-funding accounts in USD/EUR where the final settlement leg is a SWIFT MT103 to the licensed bank in Samoa and no local-currency (WST) balances are held offshore.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) in USD, EUR, AUD, or NZD to the Samoan bank's correspondent account (never as WST outside Samoa), using the bank's prescribed inward-remittance purpose code for direct equity investment and adding 'EQUITY CAPITAL INJECTION / WS_IC / FOREIGN INVESTMENT APPROVAL REF / CBS REF' in Field 70. The Capital Escrow Account may only be disbursed after the Samoan bank has verified: (i) the IC's current registration and good standing with the Registrar of International Companies, (ii) the foreign-investment certificate or exemption, (iii) a certified board/shareholder resolution showing the issuance of shares and contribution amount, (iv) the CBS inward-investment notification, and (v) the source-of-funds dossier for the sending trust/bank. All supporting documents must be delivered in English or official Samoan translation, and the receiving bank will file the transaction in the CBS exchange-control and balance-of-payments statistical return.Major import contracts for port cranes, conveyors, spare parts, and marine fuel should be settled by an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Samoan bank, or by SWIFT MT103 T/T for smaller disbursements. Each outward remittance must clearly state the direct-investment or trade purpose classification, quote the Samoa Ministry for Revenue tax clearance for remittances, and attach the underlying term-sale contract, supplier pro-forma invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, and for LC utilizations the transport document and port receipt. Local port-authority charges, stevedoring wages, and Samoan withholding taxes are paid in WST through the onshore clearing system using the bank's electronic platform, with the resulting same-day RTGS/ACH settlement; any USD-denominated disbursement above the bank's transaction threshold must be pre-registered in the CBS reporting system.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracts between WS_IC and a licensed commercial bank in Samoa for WST/USD, WST/EUR, and WST/AUD tenors up to 12 months, supported by a rolling foreign-exchange exposure schedule.Offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) referencing the Samoan tala, transacted with an internationally licensed bank/correspondent for hedging import payments, with USD settlement and no WST physical delivery.Cross-currency swap (USD/WST or USD/EUR) executed with a licensed Samoan bank or its offshore parent to match the financing currency of port equipment loans with the terminal's revenue currency.FX zero-cost collar or bought call/put options over USD/EUR/WST for the port tariff receivables, permissible when transacted through a licensed bank and reported to the CBS treasury surveillance desk for derivative exposures.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (onshore resident companies under the Income Tax Act 2015); 0% effective Samoan corporate income tax for a Samoa International Company (IC) under the statutory total tax exemption.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for an International Company distributing dividends by virtue of the IC total tax exemption; 15% final withholding tax on dividends distributed by an onshore Samoan resident company to both resident and non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Administrative risk-based thresholds: WST 1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions triggering mandatory Local File; WST 100,000,000 group-wide gross revenue triggering Master File; Samoa transfer pricing legislation applies the arm's length standard to all associated-party dealings without a statutory monetary de minimis.

Economic substance scope: Samoa applies Economic Substance Regulations to International Companies in alignment with OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices standards. A bulk-port stevedoring IC (ISIC 5224) must maintain in Samoa physical operational assets such as berth equipment or credible subcontracted terminal presence, Samoan-resident management and employees, board meetings held in Samoa, and locally stored accounting records. Absent such substance the IC is treated as a shell, the tax exemption may be displaced, and exchange of information notifications to the foreign jurisdiction will follow.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False IC Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Loss of IC exemption; retrospective reassessment of Samoan corporate income tax at 27% on all Samoan-source revenue for the full period; tax shortfall penalty up to 100% of the evaded tax plus prescribed interest; criminal fines and strike-off under the Tax Administration Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false declaration are personally assessable for the evaded tax where the corporate form is a sham; imprisonment up to five years, disqualification from directorship, travel bans during investigation, and civil restitution of diverted profits to the Crown.
Economic Substance / Shell Company Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty plus daily accrual under the Samoa Economic Substance Regulations; publication of the non-compliant IC; mandatory automatic exchange of information with the foreign parent jurisdiction; revocation of the IC licence; reallocation of profits to the operational jurisdiction for tax assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and the licensed registered agent may be fined and imprisoned for knowingly lodging false substance declarations; travel restrictions apply while the inquiry is pending; directors owe fiduciary restitution to the company and creditors where assets were stripped.
AML / Money Laundering Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's AML/CFT framework and Financial Intelligence Unit enforcement: freezing and forfeiture of vessels and terminal assets, fines up to WST 1,000,000 or three times the transaction value, and revocation of the payment agent licence.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability extends to directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners involved in structuring or false identification; penalties include imprisonment up to fourteen years, passport seizure and travel bans, and personal asset confiscation.
Fraudulent Trading and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the International Companies Act 2012 for false statements; cancellation of registration; corporate veil lifted for the entire stevedoring business; all transactions restated as onshore taxable activity subject to 27% corporate tax, penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Personal liability for the full tax shortfall, legal costs and creditor claims; imprisonment for false declaration; automatic disqualification from managing any company in Samoa for ten years.
