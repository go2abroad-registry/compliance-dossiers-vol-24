Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department (VCIR) of the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Government of Vanuatu — statutory administrator of the Value Added Tax Act [Cap. 245], Customs Act [Cap. 68] and Stamp Duties Act [Cap. 138]; official portal: https://www.customs.gov.vu. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC; https://www.vfsc.vu) acts as the company registry and offshore regulator issuing International Company certificates and tax-exemption certificates; the national e-registry is operated through VFSC's online platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Vanuatu International Company (IC) with VFSC under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and registry company number; no income-tax TIN is issued because Vanuatu levies no direct taxation. Step 2: For VAT, file the VCIR VAT registration application (Form VAT-01) comprising the certificate of incorporation, registered-office consent, details of the offshore litigation/arbitration activity, and identity of the tax representative; non-resident entities and IOLTA trust structures must appoint a Vanuatu fiscal representative. Step 3: VCIR issues a nine-digit VAT number and the IC may alternatively apply to VFSC for a Tax Exemption Certificate confirming statutory exemption for counterparties. Step 4: Standard annual returns to VFSC (filing fee) and zero monthly/bi-monthly VAT returns where no local taxable supplies occur.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — the International Company is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes (ICPA [Cap. 222], s.256: income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, insurance premium tax, stamp duty), is a non-resident/offshore person for VAT purposes, and is outside the scope of entity-level direct taxation; supplies are treated as offshore exports. The exemption is conditional on the IC not carrying on business with Vanuatu residents and on maintaining valid registration with VFSC. — Step 1: Incorporate the Vanuatu International Company (IC) with VFSC under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and registry company number; no income-tax TIN is issued because Vanuatu levies no direct taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the International Company is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes (ICPA [Cap. 222], s.256: income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, insurance premium tax, stamp duty), is a non-resident/offshore person for VAT purposes, and is outside the scope of entity-level direct taxation; supplies are treated as offshore exports. The exemption is conditional on the IC not carrying on business with Vanuatu residents and on maintaining valid registration with VFSC.

Available election forms

Because Vanuatu has no corporate income tax and no income-tax treaty network, US-style check-the-box and hybrid-mismatch elections are not applicable. Available elections under the VAT Act [Cap. 245]: (1) voluntary VAT registration for any taxable activity below the VT 5,000,000 threshold (s.8(6)); (2) election to zero-rate supplies made to an IC upon substantiation by the supplier and the IC's exemption certificate; (3) election for the IC to obtain an International Business Licence from VFSC for regulated cross-border services, preserving exemption; (4) conversion/redomiciliation election to a local Companies Act [Cap. 191] company, which brings the entity within the (still tax-free) local regime and is tax-neutral; (5) for VAT group/consolidated filing — not available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT Act [Cap. 245], s.8: compulsory registration of a person carrying on a taxable activity in Vanuatu where annual taxable turnover exceeds VT 5,000,000 (12-month look-back); voluntary registration is available below that threshold. Offshore International Companies whose supplies are exclusively exported/zero-rated are normally outside compulsory registration absent any local taxable consumption; non-resident service providers without a Vanuatu establishment have no registration obligation unless making local taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (or treatment as outside the territorial scope of VAT) applies to the IC's cross-border commercial litigation, arbitration, and asset-tracing services where: (a) the recipient/client is a non-resident of Vanuatu and the benefit of the services is enjoyed wholly outside Vanuatu; (b) the supply is made to or by an International Company in the course of its offshore business (ICPA [Cap. 222], s.256 and VAT Act [Cap. 245], Sch.2 zero-rating of supplies to ICs); (c) no Vanuatu-situs arbitration hearing or court forum is used, and no Vanuatu resident defendant is charged costs; (d) fees are remitted into a non-resident IOLTA/trust account or foreign bank account; and (e) contemporaneous evidence of the non-resident recipient status, engagement contract, invoice annotation, and inbound FX remittance is retained. Local legal services supplied to Vanuatu residents or connected with Vanuatu real property/litigation remain taxable at 12.5%.

  • Engagement letter / arbitration mandate and fee agreement with the non-resident client
  • Zero-rated export tax invoice annotated 'ZERO-RATED — EXPORT' under VAT Act [Cap. 245], Sch.2
  • Foreign client tax-residence certificate (or passport/ID copies for natural persons)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice evidencing fees received from outside Vanuatu
  • IOLTA Trust Account ledger and reconciliation report for the matter
  • Customs Export Declaration / ASYCUDA goods declaration (required only where physical exhibits, court bundles, or due-diligence files are shipped abroad)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for physical export of case files or evidence)
  • Board resolution of the IC approving the offshore engagement and confirming non-resident status

No real-time electronic tax-invoice clearance ('continuous transaction controls') or fiscal digital stamping regime is in force in Vanuatu; VCIR relies on post-issuance invoice audits. Registered suppliers must issue manual or PDF tax invoices containing the statutory particulars (supplier name/address, VAT registration number, sequential invoice number, date, description, VAT-exclusive consideration, VAT at 12.5%) and must annotate export invoices 'ZERO-RATED — EXPORT' under VAT Act [Cap. 245], s.22 and Sch.2. Customs declarations are processed electronically through ASYCUDA World for physical exports, but this is not a tax-invoice clearance mandate. There is no API-integrated e-invoicing or CTC obligation for the IC.

VCIR audits zero-rating claims using risk-based post-clearance review; unsubstantiated export treatment is recharacterised as a local taxable supply, triggering a default assessment for the unpaid VAT plus a 200% evasion penalty and 20% per annum late-payment interest (VAT Act [Cap. 245], ss.30, 48, 65). Fraudulent zero-rating or refund fraud is a criminal offence punishable on conviction by a fine of up to VT 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years for responsible officers. The VFSC may retroactively revoke the IC's tax-exemption certificate, strike the company off, and impose civil fines for breach of the no-local-business condition (ICPA [Cap. 222]), and the IC's asset-tracing remedies (fraudulent preference, recovery of dissipated trust funds from IOLTA accounts) are available to the liquidator/Commissioner to claw back wrongly refrained amounts.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains an open currency regime; the Vatu (VUV) is fully convertible for current and capital transactions on the domestic market. International Companies incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] are expressly exempt from exchange control restrictions and may receive, hold, remit, and disburse funds in any currency designated in their constitution. Capital account convertibility is broad for International Companies, and profit or dividend remittance requires no RBV approval. Banking practice, however, requires a cleared onboarding file, source-of-funds evidence, and beneficial-ownership declarations before any outward dividend or fee payment is processed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation for Vanuatu entities. Export receipts, including cross-border legal service fees and arbitration recoveries, may be retained in the currency of receipt, with 0% mandatory conversion into VUV and no prescribed repatriation period. The legal-services export activity of a Vanuatu International Company is exempt under the Exchange Control Act because the company is non-resident for exchange-control purposes; banks nevertheless require underlying commercial contracts and invoices for each inbound credit.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) administering company licensing, AML/CFT supervision, and Virtual Asset oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail. Vanuatu has no electronic-money-institution licensing category; foreign digital EMIs are not authorized deposit-takers in Vanuatu. An unlicensed offshore EMI may not legally hold client-trust monies for a Vanuatu International Company, and such use would breach the AML/CFT Act and create sub-custody and co-mingling risks. A VFSC-licensed money remitter or money changer may be used only for auxiliary expenses, never as the safeguarded account for commercial-litigation client funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022 pacs.008 when via a clearing house) into the International Company's VUV or foreign-currency account. No RBV exchange-control approval is required. The wire must state a correct purpose code under the receiving bank's transaction classification, normally 'subscription for share capital' or 'equity contribution', and must cite the company's registry number and constitution clause authorizing the issuance. Verification files must include the capital subscription resolution, ledger entry, share-register extract, source-of-funds narrative, and a confirmation from the registered agent that the IC is a tax-exempt offshore entity.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for court filing fees, witness compensation, expert fees, adverse-cost settlements, and disbursements to co-counsel in foreign jurisdictions. Commercial Letters of Credit are not the natural rail for legal services; if a client requires conditional security for a costs order, the correct instrument is a demand guarantee or standby Letter of Credit issued by the client's bank in favor of the IC's account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VUV 0 — no statutory Local File or Master File requirements apply to ICs because their income falls outside the Vanuatu tax net. Nevertheless, VFSC may require an arms-length justification for cross-border related-party legal service fees when the IC acts as a group 'service centre'. A practical threshold of VUV 30 million in annual revenue is commonly used by local advisers to determine whether bespoke documentation should be maintained.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu's Economic Substance (International Companies) Regulation 2019 imposes a substance-over-form test on ICs engaged in 'relevant activities' (banking, insurance, shipping, IP holding, financing/leasing, headquarters or distribution/service centres). A cross-border arbitration and asset-tracing chamber is not automatically a 'relevant activity' if legal services are performed overseas and the IC merely receives settlement proceeds from clients. The IC will be treated as a 'service centre' when it performs group administrative functions. In that case, adequate substance must be demonstrated in Vanuatu, including a physical office (or shared agent premise), at least one resident director (unless expressly exempted), adequate employee records, and evidence that significant management decisions are made in-country. Non-compliance attracts penalties up to VUV 1,000,000 per offence, potential compulsory winding-up, and a statutory basis for piercing the corporate veil.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Sham IOLTA Structures

Statutory sanction: In Vanuatu: striking-off under s.168 of the International Companies Act and a VFSC fine up to VUV 1,000,000. In the foreign jurisdiction: the corporate veil is disregarded, retained settlement funds are treated as taxable profits of the de facto beneficial owner, and surcharges up to 150% of the tax avoided may apply.

Director personal liability: The foreign litigator who exercises de facto control is treated as a shadow director; the court will pierce the veil for commingling IOLTA funds and misrepresenting the IC's substance. Directors are jointly and severally liable for foreign tax, penalties, interest, and costs; a travel ban may be issued; imprisonment penalties of up to 5 years for perjury and up to 7 years for laundering the concealed proceeds may be imposed.
Shell Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: VFSC may order compulsory striking-off with an annotation stating 'No Economic Substance Filed'. The ESR Enforcement Committee may impose an administrative fine of VUV 1,000,000, and under global exchange of information mechanisms the negative substance determination will be shared automatically with foreign competent authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false substance declaration are personally liable for the VUV 1,000,000 fine plus costs; the liability cannot be discharged in bankruptcy; directors may be disqualified from acting as directors for 10 years; they are placed on the Crown 'Cancelled Directors' list and excluded from holding or managing any financial licence in the South Pacific region.
Anti-Money Laundering and AML-KYC Failure on Client-Fund Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Vanuatu Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism Act 2015, failure to conduct customer due diligence attracts fines up to VUV 500,000 for a first offence and up to VUV 2,000,000 plus 5 years imprisonment for money laundering. International FATF review may add enhanced vigilance measures against the institution handling the rails.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in authorising the transfers are treated as designated reporting officers under the AMLCFT Act; the court may order disgorgement of all fee income, restitution to the defrauded counterparty, freezing of the directors' personal Vanuatu bank accounts, and a civil confiscation order over assets procured through laundered funds. A travel ban may remain in force until full repatriation is achieved.
Fraudulent or Corrupt Cross-Border Litigation

Statutory sanction: The IC's business licence becomes voidable and VFSC may cancel it under the Business Licence Act 1998 for conduct unbecoming a licensee. Local anti-corruption offences carry imprisonment up to 7 years and fines up to VUV 10,000,000. The award will be denied recognition under the New York Convention where the arbitration process was tainted by bad faith or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who issued instructions or signed forged documents are jointly and severally liable. Criminal sanctions include fraud, false sworn affidavit, and conspiracy. The court may order restitution of all fees paid to the IC plus punitive damages, professional disbarment, and a lifetime prohibition from any legal-services licence in Vanuatu.
