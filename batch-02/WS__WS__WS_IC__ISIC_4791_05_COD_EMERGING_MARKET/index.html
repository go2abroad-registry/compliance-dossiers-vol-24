Competent corporate registry

Registrar of International Companies, commercial registry functions administered by the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) and the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), Apia, Samoa

Governing statute

International Companies Act 1987 (No. 17 of 1987) as amended by the International Companies (Amendment) Act 2017, Part II (Incorporation and Powers), Part III (Memorandum and Articles), Part V (Directors and Officers); read with the International Companies Regulations 1988 and the SIFA AML/CFT Beneficial Ownership Rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form IC-1 Application for Registration of an International Company, submitted with the executed Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no capital escrow or bank deposit requirement; the Memorandum must state an authorised share capital, which may be denominated in any currency, and shares may be par value, no par value, issued or partly paid.

Funding & unlock path: Not mandatory for office-based e-commerce. If a distribution warehouse is occupied, obtain a Fire Safety Certificate of Compliance from the Samoa National Fire and Emergency Services Authority, a structural occupancy clearance for the premises, and comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act 2011 for workplace conditions.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no capital escrow or bank deposit requirement; the Memorandum must state an authorised share capital, which may be denominated in any currency, and shares may be par value, no par value, issued or partly paid.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Ministry of Finance, Apia; operating the SRA Taxpayer Central Accounting System (TCAS) e-filing portal and the ASYCUDA World customs clearance platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity incorporating as an International Company files through a licensed registered agent with the Samoa International Companies Registrar within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour. Upon issue of the Certificate of Incorporation under the International Companies Act 1987, the registered agent submits an SRA Taxpayer Registration application (prescribed T1 form in TCAS) attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, Articles of Association, registered agent consent, director and shareholder identification documents, and evidence of the registered office in Samoa. The SRA issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) to the International Company, which is required for payment of the annual government fee and for any limited administrative filings. A separate VAGST registration is required only when the International Company makes taxable supplies in or into Samoa; an International Company whose operations are exclusively offshore mail-order retail with cash-on-delivery settlement is not obligated to register for VAGST. The TIN is also practically required to enable the repatriation of foreign exchange through a Samoan commercial bank and to substantiate the 20-year tax exemption certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box, fiscal unity, or hybrid mismatch election in its tax code. The International Company may elect under s.85 of the International Companies Act 1987 to domesticate or convert into a local company, thereby revoking its tax-exempt-zone status and subjecting future worldwide income to the standard Samoan corporate income tax rate of 27%. Without such election, the entity remains a non-resident, tax-exempt International Company. For VAGST purposes, a registrable International Company may elect a one-month, two-month, or six-month taxable cycle and may apply to the Commissioner for approval to use the cash (payments) accounting basis rather than the standard invoice basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 in taxable turnover within any 12-month period (GST Act 1992, s.10). Where an International Company's supplies are exclusively exported goods and offshore COD retail services, the entity is not required to register for VAGST because such supplies are zero-rated or outside the scope of Samoan tax. Non-resident suppliers of remote or digital services to consumers located in Samoa may be subject to separate offshore supplier VAGST registration rules, but those rules do not capture physical goods delivered outside Samoa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated where the goods are supplied to a non-resident, are physically exported from Samoa, and documentary proof of export is retained. Services directly in connection with exported goods, including freight, insurance, and logistics, are likewise zero-rated. The International Company must not take delivery or make local supply in Samoa; title to the goods must pass outside Samoa for the zero-rating to apply. A VAGST refund may be claimed only by a registered International Company carrying excess input credits; an unregistered International Company does not charge Samoan VAGST on its foreign COD sales and therefore has no local refund entitlement. Breach of the International Companies Act operational restrictions, such as carrying on local business within Samoa, would jeopardize the 20-year tax exemption and the zero-rating position.

  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the COD consignment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 from the receiving Samoan bank, or Airwallex settlement statement
  • COD settlement reconciliation report issued by the local logistics remittance agent

No real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in Samoa. VAGST tax invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992, including supplier name and TIN, buyer name, description of supply, date, tax-exclusive consideration, and VAGST amount. Export consignments are cleared electronically through the ASYCUDA World customs system, requiring lodgment of an export declaration (CusEx) subject to risk-based inspection. No SRA API or digital stamping obligation is imposed on an International Company's invoices issued to overseas COD customers.

The Samoa Revenue Authority may audit zero-rated export claims for up to five years from the end of the relevant taxable period. Where export documentation is defective or goods are not physically exported, zero-rating is reversed, VAGST is assessed at the standard 15% rate, and any refunded input credits are clawed back with interest. The Tax Administration Act 2015 applies tiered shortfall penalties scaled by culpability, ranging from failure to take reasonable care up to intentional disregard, in addition to overdue-tax interest. The Goods and Services Tax Act 1992 separately criminalizes false or misleading export claims with monetary fines and imprisonment. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days exposes the International Company to Central Bank of Samoa exchange-control penalties, suspension of the exporter's ASYCUDA clearance facility, and refusal of future zero-rating declarations. Given the COD payment rails of Airwallex and local logistics remittance, coordinated anti-money-laundering scrutiny may apply to settlement flows, with potential referral to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tālā (WST) is classified as a managed-float currency with controlled convertibility. Current account transactions are broadly free from restriction, but all payments to non-residents require authorised dealer approval, supporting invoices, and a designated foreign exchange purpose code. Capital account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment, loans, guarantees, and outward remittances of profit or capital, are subject to CBS licensing or notification, with repatriation of proceeds from an International Company generally permitted only after a tax/exempt status declaration. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders of an International Company is allowed under the International Companies Act 1987 but must be routed through a licensed Samoan bank and supported by audited financial statements or a certified solvency/round-trip declaration. Hidden hurdles include a 0.5% foreign exchange levy on certain outward remittances and a historical suspicion of capital flight that triggers enhanced documentary review for transfers to high-risk jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa imposes a general exchange control obligation requiring all export proceeds to be repatriated to Samoa within 180 days of shipment. However, an International Company (IC) that qualifies as a non-resident controlled offshore entity under the International Companies Act 1987 is normally exempted from the mandatory conversion to WST, provided the proceeds are maintained in a foreign-currency account designated as an IC Foreign Currency Account. For resident trading entities, the mandatory surrender ratio is 0% (full retention of hard currency in an approved foreign-currency account), but the exporter must provide the CBS with quarterly proof of receipt. If the IC engages in any domestic retail or cash-on-delivery collection activity within Samoa, the exchange control treatment deems those collections as domestic income, requiring conversion to WST and standard surrender/commercial banking compliance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) acting as the sole monetary authority and exchange control administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; the CBS approves all foreign exchange transactions through licensed commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary local bank account, Medium as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail. Samoa does not license or regulate offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise, Revolut) as banks; they cannot hold WST client funds or issue local IBANs. However, an IC with a foreign-currency account at an authorised dealer can use EMI platforms for inbound cash flows from emerging-market COD customers and for outbound logistics payments only if the EMI is the correspondent/collector of a Samoan bank or operates under a reciprocal arrangement. Legal constraints include the CBS prohibition on unlicensed cross-border money service businesses, the anti-money-laundering requirement that all value transfers must pass through a licensed bank, and the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit’s requirement to report any non-bank payment intermediary exceeding WST 20,000. Recommended structure: use the local bank account as the principal clearing node and the EMI as a virtual sub-account for low-value COD receipts in soft currencies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions to the International Company’s Samoan bank account must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages with a clear funds-purpose narrative. The approved routing is: (1) foreign shareholder sends funds to the IC’s foreign-currency account at a licensed Samoan bank, using purpose code 'C1' (Capital Investment) or 'F1' (Foreign Direct Investment) as defined by CBS exchange-control circulars; (2) the bank verifies the source-of-funds document (shareholder resolution, subscription agreement, bank statement) and the IC’s certificate of good standing; (3) if the capital is in a currency other than WST, the bank will require a conversion order only if the IC wishes to hold Samoan tālā; otherwise the amount is retained in the original currency in a designated IC foreign-currency account. For capital above WST 500,000, a prior CBS General Permission application must be filed with a certified board resolution and a subscribed capital statement. No inward funds may be received in cash; all injections must be authenticated by an electronic SWIFT message or a certified correspondent bank instruction. The bank will issue a 'Certificate of Inward Remittance' to the IC for future foreign-exchange hedging and repatriation purposes.For trade settlements to offshore suppliers, logistics carriers, and telecommunication/COD platforms, the IC may use three clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct bank-to-bank payments exceeding USD 10,000, requiring an invoice, customs declaration, and a CBS export/import purpose code (e.g., code M2 for general imports, code C2 for trade payments); (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) at sight or usance for high-value purchases, issued via ANZ or BSP with the underlying sales contract and airway bill; (c) local payment platforms connected to the corresponding EMTL provider (e.g., Airwallex, local logistics remittance solutions) for low-value recurring COD disbursements, but every aggregated settlement must be reported to the bank monthly. Outbound remittances below WST 20,000 may be executed under a bank’s delegated authority; above that threshold, a CBS exchange-control form must be completed. The disbursement corridor to Samoa’s emerging-market target countries (e.g., Pacific Island states, Southeast Asia, or African COD markets) must be pre-approved by the bank’s AML unit, and the bank will require a trip-wire code 'T1' if the beneficiary is a high-risk jurisdiction. Standby L/Cs may be used to guarantee logistics escrow accounts, but the IC must hold identical collateral or a cash margin at the issuing bank.Forward foreign exchange contracts with authorised Samoan banks up to 12 months tenors, backed by a declaration of underlying commercial exposure (e.g., USD/WST, AUD/WST, SGD/WST).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for non-WST currency pairs quoted by offshore banks, such as USD/CNY or USD/EUR, because the WST is not a deliverable offshore currency; the Samoan bank may act as a pass-through arranger under a CBS notification.Cross-currency swaps involving WST and a major convertible currency are permissible for a licensed Samoan bank counterparty, but only with a documented trade or capital-flow obligation and CBS approval for tenors exceeding 12 months.Vanilla FX options (call/put on WST) may be permitted for hedging of confirmed trade receivables, but only where the bank obtains a copy of the underlying receivable and the option is booked onshore.Interest-rate/FX combination swaps are generally not available to non-resident ICs; a more efficient hedging vehicle is parallel lending via the Samoan bank's international branch network.In-kind hedging through multi-currency accounts (holding USD, AUD, EUR, NZD, CNY) is expressly permitted as an internal treasury operation, but the CBS must receive quarterly returns of the aggregate open position.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for qualifying International Companies under the International Companies Act 1987; standard domestic corporate rate is 25% for non-IC entities

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for International Companies distributing dividends to non-residents; domestic companies face a final withholding tax rate of 15%

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa does not maintain mandatory Local File or Master File rules for International Companies; however, transactions above WST 200,000 in a fiscal year require contemporaneous documentation for DTA and substance purposes

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2021 requires an International Company with relevant banking, distribution, or intellectual property activities to maintain economic substance in Samoa, including adequate physical presence, qualified employees, and core income-generating activities in Samoa; a mail-order retail with cash-on-delivery settlement must file an annual Economic Substance Statement and assess the presence of distribution services revenue

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A fine up to SAT 500,000 and a period of imprisonment not exceeding three years under the Tax Administration Act 2004

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and fines; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed pending investigation
Shell Company Misuse

Statutory sanction: Deregistration of the International Company, immediate revocation of tax-exempt status, and a retrospective levy of the 25% domestic corporate income tax on accumulated profits

Director personal liability: Directors face personal restitution orders for the underpaid tax and may be disqualified from holding offshore directorships for up to ten years
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to SAT 500,000 per breach under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act 2018, plus forfeiture of any unattributed funds

Director personal liability: Directors or compliance officers demonstrating gross negligence are personally liable for the penalty amount, with a potential custodial sentence of up to 12 months
