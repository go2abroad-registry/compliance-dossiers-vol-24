Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue, Samoa (MfR) — Taxpayer Administration System (TAS) portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

International Companies (ICs) under the International Companies Act 1987 are exempt from Samoan direct taxation and are NOT required to obtain a domestic TIN for offshore trading. The foreign parent/brokerage registers the IC with the Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL). A VAGST TIN is required only if the IC makes domestic taxable supplies in Samoa; application is filed via Form VAGST-1 on the TAS portal attaching the IC's certificate of incorporation, memorandum & articles, board resolution appointing a local process agent, and a registered Samoan address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (IC: exempt from income tax, capital gains, dividend/distribution taxes, stamp duty, estate duty, gift duty, and foreign-exchange control; 20-year statutory exemption from commencement of business, renewable in practice)

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists in Samoa. VAGST registration is compulsory once taxable turnover reaches WST 100,000 and is elective below that threshold; no hybrid mismatch elections apply (no hybrid rules enacted); ICs may elect in their constitutional documents to operate as exempt or limited companies under the IC Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual value of taxable supplies (VAGST Act 1994, s.7); registration effective within 21 days of the obligation arising

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAGST applies to: (1) goods physically exported out of Samoa within 28 days of supply; (2) services supplied to a non-resident where the benefit is wholly offshore; (3) marine fuel and bunker supplies to ships engaged in international voyages / foreign-going vessels; (4) international air/sea transport. The IC's offshore brokerage of crude oil and refined products is structured either outside the Samoan VAGST net (non-resident counterparties, title passing offshore) or zero-rated if a Samoan supplier is contractually used.

  • Samoa Customs export declaration (CusDEC) with electronic customs clearance receipt
  • Original negotiable Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill evidencing offshore discharge port
  • Final commercial invoice and packing list
  • Signed sales contract (FOB/CIF/CFR terms) identifying buyer and physical delivery point
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing foreign currency settlement
  • Bunker delivery receipt (BDN) and independent quantity surveyor report for marine fuel parcels

No real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime in force. Registered suppliers must issue a valid VAGST invoice (supplier TIN, recipient name/TIN, date, description, VAGST amount) within 5 working days of supply; tax returns and refund claims are filed electronically via the TAS portal.

MfR audits all zero-rated refund claims and may offset refunds against any outstanding tax liability. Shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2012 scale at 25% (failure to take reasonable care), 50% (recklessness) and 75% (intentional disregard); fraudulent export claims trigger full retrospective clawback of refunds plus monthly late-payment interest (2% per month or part-month) and criminal prosecution of directors/officers, with deregistration and revocation of the IC's tax exemption as collateral sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoa Tala (WST) is a non-internationally traded, managed-float currency. Current-account convertibility for bona fide trade and service payments is available through CBS-authorised dealers subject to transactional documentation; capital-account conversions and WST-denominated cross-border transfers require prior CBS exchange-control approval. Profit remittance for an International Company (IC) is substantially unhindered where the entity maintains fully foreign-currency (USD) accounts and funds are repatriated without conversion into WST. Dividends, return of capital, and liquidation proceeds may be freely wired to non-resident shareholders under the IC tax-exempt regime, supported by audited financial statements, a board resolution, and confirmation of good standing; no Samoan withholding tax applies to IC distributions. The residual hurdle arises only where funds are converted into or cleared through the domestic WST system, at which point an authorised dealer will require underlying transaction evidence and CBS forms for capital-account remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters are subject to CBS surrender requirements obligating repatriation of foreign-currency receipts through an authorised dealer within the statutory timeline (practically 30 to 90 days from shipment date) and conversion into WST absent an express retention quota or exemption granted by the CBS. The mandatory conversion ratio is applied at 100% of qualifying export receipts unless a waiver is secured. However, a Samoa International Company in the crude-oil and refined-products wholesale scope is treated as an offshore-exempt foreign-currency structure: export invoices booked offshore, denominated in USD, and settled into the IC's authorised-dealer foreign-currency account are outside the mandatory surrender regime, and no WST conversion is imposed. The exemption operates only while funds remain in the foreign-currency clearing circuit and the registered agent maintains the IC's exempt-status certificate.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), constituted under the Central Bank Act 2016, exercising delegated exchange-control powers through the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Samoa has no locally regulated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime, and offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK/FCA, EU/EMD passport holders) are not recognised as CBS-authorised dealers and cannot issue locally actionable Standby Letters of Credit or access the Samoan clearing infrastructure. Their practical utility is confined to ancillary FX conversion or pre-paid disbursement, which is lawfully insufficient for high-risk marine-fuel wholesale settlement. Use of an unlicensed EMI for core trade proceeds would breach Samoa's AML/CFT obligations attaching to IC registered agents and would materially damage the high-risk banking profile with any downstream correspondent bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has not enacted a statutory Local File or Master File threshold in WST. As a prudential audit and commercial-banking benchmark for the petroleum trading sector, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation aligned with OECD Chapter V is expected where annual related-party commodity turnover exceeds WST 1,000,000 (approximately USD 375,000). The SRA can invoke general anti-avoidance and arm's-length powers in the Income Tax Act 2012 to demand comparability studies, pricing models and board documentation during audit.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2020 apply to ICs carrying on relevant activities, including a distribution and service centre, trading, holding-company and headquarter functions. A marine fuel brokerage IC must demonstrate in Samoa: (i) full-time qualified employees proportionate to the trading activity, (ii) physical premises and registered office, (iii) adequate operating expenditure, and (iv) core income-generating activities performed in Samoa, namely negotiation, contracting, fuel procurement, risk management and operational control of the SBLC/SWIFT payment cycle. An annual economic substance declaration must be filed with SIFA. Pure equity holding companies enjoy reduced substance requirements only.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abuse of IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: SRA reassessment at 27% on the entire undeclared income, plus a 25% penalty tax on the deficiency, monthly interest at 2%, and deregistration of the company by the Registrar of International Companies. Criminal conviction carries fines up to 100 penalty units (WST 100 each) and imprisonment up to 12 months.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false returns or directed domestic trading are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalty and interest; the corporate veil is pierced for tax debts where the company is used as a sham. Directors face travel prohibition orders, disqualification from acting as directors in Samoa for up to 10 years, and imprisonment for tax fraud.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Strike-off and cancellation of IC registration under ICA 1987; refusal by SIFA to issue a Certificate of Good Standing; automatic exchange of information with OECD and treaty partners exposing the arrangement to foreign tax assessments. The Ministry of Industry and MCIL may impose regulatory fines of up to WST 50,000 for failure to maintain the registered agent requirement.

Director personal liability: Directors and the licensed agent may be held personally liable for restoration costs and outstanding annual fees; the corporate veil is pierced to attach personal liability for obligations incurred through the sham, and directors may be named in a Samoa international tax information exchange notice leading to travel bans and asset freezing in foreign jurisdictions.
AML / High-Risk Petroleum Trading

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (as amended), the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) may impose fines up to WST 1,000,000 per offence, suspend or revoke the statutory commodities trading licence, and refer the matter to the Samoa Law Society for professional sanction of the compliance officer, with parallel forfeiture proceedings over all related commission and held property.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal fines up to WST 500,000, confiscation of trading proceeds, and imprisonment up to 14 years; conviction results in automatic disqualification from holding directorships in Samoan international companies for a decade, and travel bans may be imposed while an FIU money-laundering or terrorist-financing investigation is pending.
Economic Substance Regulation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2020, SIFA may impose a financial penalty of up to WST 250,000, require supplementary disclosure, strike the company off the register, and exchange the full tax-file with the foreign revenue authority of the beneficial owners, triggering imposition of foreign CFC or anti-avoidance levies.

Director personal liability: Each director who signed the false economic substance declaration is personally liable for the regulatory penalty and may be prosecuted criminally with imprisonment up to 3 years; directors become personally exposed to foreign tax-recovery claims under tax collection-assistance treaties, and the IC is reclassified as a fully taxable resident in the blacklisted or offshore-audit jurisdiction, subject to double taxation and 100% penalty loading.
