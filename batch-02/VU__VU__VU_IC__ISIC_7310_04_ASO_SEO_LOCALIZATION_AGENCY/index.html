Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Department (VRCD), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; e-services portal: https://www.customs.gov.vu. International Company registration and supervision: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC).

E-government filing portal

An offshore foreign entity forming a Vanuatu International Company (IC) obtains a Certificate of Incorporation from the VFSC through a licensed registered agent. No statutory tax identification number (TIN) is required unless the IC carries on a taxable activity in Vanuatu. If a TIN or VAT registration is needed, the entity must submit Form VAT-1 to the VRCD with the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, registered office/registered agent evidence, director and shareholder details, and annual turnover projections. The VRCD issues a VAT/TIN certificate. An IC conducting only offshore ASO/SEO digital services holds the VFSC registration number as its primary corporate identity and has no Vanuatu direct tax filing obligation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no corporate income tax, no check-the-box regime, and no hybrid mismatch election under domestic law. The IC is statutorily exempt from income tax, VAT on offshore activities, and exchange controls. Voluntary VAT registration is available for persons making taxable supplies in Vanuatu, but an IC’s offshore ISIC 7310 digital services are outside the Vanuatu VAT net. Foreign tax authorities may independently classify the IC for their own tax purposes; such classifications do not alter Vanuatu tax status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; non-residents supplying taxable services in Vanuatu may be required to register without regard to threshold. An International Company performing only offshore services has no VAT registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Vanuatu within the statutory timeframe and services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Vanuatu qualify for the 0% VAT rate. For ISIC 7310 ASO/SEO localization services, the supplier must establish that the recipient is a non-resident, the service is performed or delivered outside Vanuatu, and the consideration is paid from outside Vanuatu. Export services are substantiated by contracts, invoices, delivery evidence, and non-resident declarations.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice
  • Executed service agreement and delivery/performance evidence for offshore digital services
  • VAT export invoice and customer non-resident declaration

No mandatory continuous transaction control or real-time fiscal clearance regime exists. VAT invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form and must contain the prescribed particulars. There is no government API invoice clearance, no digital fiscal stamping, and no prior online approval of invoices.

VRCD may audit zero-rated export claims retrospectively. Deficient substantiation or fraudulent export zero-rating triggers clawback of refunded VAT, penalty tax up to double the amount, interest, deregistration, and disqualification from future refunds. Criminal liability may include fines up to VUV 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment. Directors and registered agents may be held jointly and severally liable. False customs declarations attract separate forfeiture and penalty provisions under the Customs Act.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Vanuatu operates a fully liberalised foreign-exchange regime. The Vatu (VUV) is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange-control permit requirements, no statutory limits on the purchase or sale of foreign currency, and no restrictions on profit remission, dividend distribution, repayment of shareholder loans, or capital repatriation. The RBV exercises prudential oversight to ensure anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-financing-of-terrorism (CFT) compliance, but it does not impose economic or quantitative controls. For an International Company (IC) structured as a tax-exempt non-resident status entity, the practical retainment of USD, EUR, AUD, or other major currencies is unrestricted. Profit remittance is effected through customary corporate bank wire transfers subject to standard customer due diligence and source-of-funds documentation, rather than sovereign approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists in Vanuatu for the mandatory surrender of export proceeds or for the conversion of foreign-currency receipts into Vatu. An International Company may retain export and service revenues indefinitely in foreign currency accounts with RBV-licensed commercial banks or transfer such proceeds offshore at will. There is no mandatory timeline, no prescribed conversion ratio, and no central bank accounting of individual trade receipts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) as the central bank and monetary authority with supervisory oversight over licensed banks and payment systems; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) exercises supplementary regulatory supervision over non-bank financial institutions and offshore service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business and Payoneer are the contracted target payment rails for this ASO/SEO localization agency. Neither is licensed as a deposit-taking institution in Vanuatu; therefore they cannot provide VUV-denominated accounts or benefit from RBV depositor protection. The legal constraint is that these EMIs operate under foreign money-transmitter and e-money licences, and they may internally flag Vanuatu-incorporated ICs as tax-haven vehicles requiring heightened review. The practical mitigation is to open multi-currency accounts with the EMI as a non-Vanuatu regulated entity, use those accounts for affiliate disbursements, client collections in USD/EUR/GBP, and cross-border vendor payments, while maintaining a primary operational account with an RBV-licensed bank for VUV settlement, legal invoicing, and higher-value counterparty verification.

Frequent high-volume cross-border payments originating from multiple unrelated jurisdictions to the Vanuatu IC's account.

Root cause: Frequent high-volume cross-border payments originating from multiple unrelated jurisdictions to the Vanuatu IC's account.

Defense evidence: Master service agreements and per-client contracts; beneficial-owner identification data of all major counterparties; payment schedule with reference numbers linking each inbound wire to a specific invoice; reconciliation statements prepared in accordance with the International Company's internal records; a notarised declaration confirming that no physical goods transit Vanuatu; and a board-approved AML controls manual, including a payment-screening policy.
Immediate injection of equity capital or shareholder loan soon after incorporation without an operational revenue history.

Root cause: Immediate injection of equity capital or shareholder loan soon after incorporation without an operational revenue history.

Defense evidence: Notarised board resolution approving share allotment and bank account mandate; shareholder loan agreement or subscription agreement; certified source-of-funds bank statements covering at least the preceding six months; identity proof of the transferor; an independent legal opinion confirming the capital transaction is lawful under the Vanuatu International Companies Act and the Foreign Exchange and Banking Act; and a statement from the registered agent confirming good standing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing for inbound capital injection into the Vanuatu International Company is a SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfer initiated by the beneficial owner or lending institution to the company's designated corporate account at an RBV-licensed bank. The wire instruction must clearly reference the purpose code corresponding to 'Share Capital Injection' or 'Intercompany Shareholder Loan', and the sub-category should align with the IMF Balance of Payments direct-investment or other-investment classification. The remitting bank must provide the full legal name, address, and account number of both sender and recipient. The receiving RBV-licensed bank will verify the board resolution appointing the account signatories, the beneficial-owner declaration, and the source-of-funds details if the remittance exceeds USD 10,000. No RBV exchange-control permit is required, but the transfer must pass international sanctions screening, particularly the UNSC and OFAC lists, substantiated by the bank's internal controls.Outbound settlement for service-based trade from this ASO/SEO localization agency is executed primarily through the SWIFT MT103 electronic credit transfer for vendor payments, contractor settlements, and licensing fees. For high-frequency, low-value payments, the designated offshore EMI rails (Wise Business and Payoneer) are used for automated batch disbursements without substantial documentary friction. Commercial Letters of Credit are not used because digital service transactions do not generate shipping documents; however, for contingent strategic acquisitions or a substantial service milestone, a documentary standby credit may be structured under UCP600 with a Port Vila branch of a domestic commercial bank. All outbound payments must reference the underlying invoice number, be screened for OFAC/UN/EU sanctions, and be supported by retained contracts and vendor AML profiles.FX spot contracts (VUV/USD, VUV/EUR, VUV/AUD, and other major cross pairs) executed through an RBV-licensed bank.Outright FX forwards with tenors of up to 12 months for major currency pairs, documented under ISDA or local bank master agreements.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on the VUV (permissible, though counterparty appetite is limited given the absence of exchange controls).Cross-currency interest rate swaps available for matched principal flows in USD, EUR, AUD, and JPY, executed through offshore ISDA counterparties.Vanilla FX options for hedging long-term revenue streams and protecting profit conversion margins.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No formal transfer pricing documentation threshold exists in Vanuatu under the International Companies Act. Because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax, the domestic TP regime is effectively inapplicable. In practice, intercompany transactions with foreign related parties should still be commercially documented to substantiate the entity's economic substance and to withstand scrutiny in the source jurisdiction under that jurisdiction's TP rules.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 applies to International Companies carrying on prescribed 'relevant activities': banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters operations, holding company activities, intellectual property holding, and distribution and service centres. An ASO/SEO localization agency engaged purely in online marketing services does not ordinarily fall within these categories. However, if it operates a physical distribution or service centre in Vanuatu, it must satisfy the economic substance requirement (i.e., prudent management, adequate physical presence, full-time employees, and sufficient local expenditure) and file an annual Economic Substance Return with the VFSC. Non-IP service activities outside these categories are exempt from substance reporting.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through False Exemption Claims

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 12 years under the Vanuatu International Companies Act for fraud; mandatory deregistration of the company by VFSC; confiscation of corporate records; administrative fine up to VUV 2,000,000. Additional exposure arises under foreign anti-avoidance rules, including economic substance legislation in OECD member states.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be personally liable for the underlying foreign taxes and penalties through 'piercing the corporate veil'. Courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders on personal assets, and a personal liability order for all corporate debts. Any director found to have knowingly made false declarations may be barred from acting as a director in any Vanuatu company for up to five years and may face imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act, fines range from VUV 500,000 to VUV 5,000,000 for substance mismatches; the registrar may also strike off the company and notify the Ministry of Finance, triggering foreign exchange restrictions. Shareholders may be directed to surrender all assets derived from the shell arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution orders for the value of benefits received, personal exposure to VFSC enforcement costs, and a statutory ban of up to 10 years on managing any international company. If the shell is used to commit fraud, directors are jointly and severally liable to creditors and may be extradited under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Anti-Money Laundering Violation

Statutory sanction: The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission imposes administrative penalties of VUV 1,000,000 to VUV 10,000,000; criminally, the Anti-Money Laundering Act 2011 provides imprisonment up to 15 years and/or a fine up to VUV 20,000,000. The company's bank accounts and PSP relationships are subject to freeze orders by the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are criminally liable where they knew or ought to have known of tainted funds. Civil consequences include personal confiscation of proceeds, jointly and severally with other perpetrators, and prohibition from holding bank mandates or trustee licenses. Travel bans and extradition to the requesting state are routinely sought.
Unauthorized Regulated Activity

Statutory sanction: An International Company carrying on a licensed activity without a license is liable to a fine not exceeding VUV 5,000,000 and forfeiture of all income derived from the unlawful activity. VFSC may also impose an additional disciplinary penalty of VUV 50,000 per day for continuing breach and may deregister any intermediary holder.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for restitution to affected clients and for any penalties imposed by VFSC. The court may impose a custodial sentence of up to 7 years for knowingly operating a financial business without a license; in practice, directors' passports are impounded pending resolution of the proceedings.
Non-Filing and Annual Fee Default

Statutory sanction: Automatic late penalty of VUV 25,000 plus VUV 1,000 per day of delay; after 90 days, VFSC may strike the company off the International Companies Register. Struck-off companies lose legal capacity, assets may be forfeited to the Government, and a restoration fee of VUV 150,000 plus all arrears is due before reinstatement.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow continued default are personally liable for the company's VFSC fees and penalties. When the company is struck off, its bank accounts freeze and PSP funds are trapped; directors can be sued by creditors and counterparties in civil proceedings for fraudulent conveyance, effectively piercing the corporate veil.
