Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA) — the integrated tax and customs administration for Samoa; taxpayer services accessible through the SRA e-Tax online portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign person incorporating a Samoa International Company (IC) must appoint a licensed registered agent. The agent submits the incorporation documents to the Samoa International Companies Office. Following incorporation, the agent files an application with the SRA for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) on behalf of the IC, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office address, and details of directors/shareholders. The IC is tax-exempt and is not required to obtain a VAGST registration unless it undertakes local taxable supplies, but a TIN is issued for administrative and financial purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods from Samoa, and the supply of services to a recipient outside Samoa, qualify for the 0% VAGST rate where the supplier is a registered VAGST person and retains the required documentary evidence. An International Company conducting offshore trade is outside the Samoan tax net; any incidental local supplies remain taxable unless zero-rated for export.

Samoa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-linked e-invoicing, or government digital stamping. Registered taxpayers may file VAGST returns electronically through the SRA e-Tax portal, but there is no automated clearance gateway for invoices or customs validation.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims and false export documentation may be disallowed by the SRA, resulting in retrospective VAT assessments, penalties, and interest at statutory rates. Criminal liability may arise under the Customs Act and VAGST legislation, with fines up to WST 1,000,000, imprisonment of directors, and forfeiture of goods or benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a managed float for the Samoan Tala (WST) under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984. The capital account is restricted: all non-resident direct and portfolio investments, and all external borrowings, require prior Exchange Control approval from the Central Bank of Samoa. Current account transactions, including trade-related payments and profit remittances, are generally permitted upon submission of documentary evidence, but repatriation of dividends, branch profits, and liquidation proceeds requires certification of source of funds, tax clearance from the Ministry for Revenue, and a valid Overseas Investment approval. The International Company (IC) regime, governed by the International Companies Act 1987, provides statutory exchange control exemptions for international companies holding a valid Tax Exempt Zone certificate and conducting business exclusively outside Samoa; such entities may operate foreign-currency-denominated accounts and remit profits freely without Central Bank approval. However, any physical conversion into WST, local disbursement, or establishment of a Samoan-dollar account triggers standard convertibility controls. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal for pure offshore IC treasury operations, but onshore subsidiaries or entities with Samoan-source revenue face a refundable withholding tax regime and must substantiate arm's length charges and audited financial statements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Exchange Control Act 1976 imposes a statutory obligation on residents to offer export proceeds for conversion to WST within 90 days of receipt, or such period as the Central Bank of Samoa may approve. For non-resident-owned International Companies operating under the International Companies Act 1987, the surrender and conversion requirement does not apply, provided the IC is not a 'resident' under the Exchange Control Act and the proceeds relate exclusively to offshore activities. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% for resident exporters and 0% for qualified ICs with non-resident ownership and offshore conduct. Documentary requirements for non-applicability include an Exchange Control exemption certificate, tax exemption certificate, and audited confirmations that sales were concluded outside Samoa and settled in foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High for non-WST operating currencies, Low for WST-denominated accounts. Samoa does not have a domestic EMI licensing framework; therefore offshore digital EMIs (e.g., BaaS platforms in Hong Kong, Singapore, or the UK) may be used by the Samoan International Company as foreign-currency treasury and payment settlement rails only if: (1) the EMI does not hold itself out as a Samoan bank, (2) the IC maintains its offshore tax-exempt status and non-resident ownership, (3) no Samoan-source receipts flow into the EMI account, and (4) the Central Bank of Samoa has not issued a marketing or solicitation prohibition in respect of that EMI. The 1987 International Companies Act is permissive as to opening accounts outside Samoa; however, banks and EMIs will typically require a certificate of good standing, register of directors, share register, and compliance with their own AML/KYC rules. Viability is reduced for EMIs that require a local office, directorship KYC interviews, or a Samoan physical nexus. No PSD2-style open-banking or local EMI passport exists in Samoa.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Samoan International Company should route exclusively through the international banking network in USD, HKD, or SGD using MT103 wire transfers with bank reference 'BEPS' (Beneficiary is the Samoan IC). Permissible purpose codes: EGR (acquisition/sale of shares), EIA (investment income), EBP (operating expenses), or P01 (trade-related advance) depending on the sending jurisdiction; for the USA use FFIEC BSA purpose code 'DF' / 'C11' with narrative 'share capital contribution to offshore IC'; for Hong Kong use TMA 'ID' (intercompany funding); for Singapore use MAS 'EC' (equity capital). Verification requirements: inward remittance advice, commercial invoice, share subscription agreement, board resolution, and a certificate of non-resident status. The Central Bank of Samoa does not require pre-approval for IC foreign currency accounts, but the receiving bank will require a copy of the IC's tax exemption certificate and confirmation that the funds do not represent proceeds of Samoan domestic sales. A minimum of USD 1 or equivalent is accepted; no minimum capital requirement exists under the International Companies Act 1987. Internal loan capital routed via a shareholder loan must be evidenced by a written loan agreement and may be repatriated under the same MT103 channel with purpose code 'IRP' (intra-group repayment).Outbound trade disbursements to manufacturers, licensors, logistic providers, and resellers must be settled via SWIFT MT103 standard wire transfers from the IC's offshore foreign-currency account, using beneficiary bank details in China, Hong Kong, or Singapore. Purpose codes: 'TCR' (trade payable), 'ROY' (royalty/license fee), 'COM' (commission), or 'ADD' (advertising and distribution). For larger purchase orders exceeding USD 50,000, a clean Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C at sight) may be issued by the Samoan IC's bank through a correspondent bank; however, most designer toy transactions are effectively settled through MT103 because intermediary banks and Chinese beneficiaries prefer telegraphic transfer due to low transaction amount and rapid clearance. The use of the Samoan IC as an offshore merchant intermediary does not require Central Bank approval if no WST conversion occurs. All outbound wires must append the Samoan IC name as legal entity, tax identification number or certified IC registration number, and the purpose code. Invoices must reference the purchase order number and the ISIC 3240 description. Clearing rails available: SWIFT MT103 direct, MT202 COVER for high-value settlement through a nostro account, Commercial L/C for documentary trade, and digital wallet/EMI bulk payout (e.g., Payoneer, Airwallex, or Wise Business) for payments below USD 10,000 to suppliers/creators; note that domestic POS rails in Samoa (local EFT/POS) are irrelevant to the offshore IC treasury architecture.Outright forwards on USD/WST, HKD/USD, SGD/USD, and CNY/USD through an internationally licensed counterparty, only where the IC has a demonstrable short-term WST exposure (unlikely for pure offshore operations).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing WST (offshore USD/WST NDF market is exceptionally thin) or CNY (onshore/offshore CNH deliverable forwards, not WST NDF), permitted for ICs hedging expected Chinese RMB settlement obligations.Cross-currency swaps between USD and HKD or USD and SGD, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with a global bank, permitted without Central Bank of Samoa approval, provided the swap proceeds settle outside Samoa.Vanilla FX options (call/put on USD/CNY or USD/HKD) for hedging purchase order payable obligations; speculative or uncovered options are prohibited by most international banks and subject to negative KYC treatment.Currency swaps between WST and USD, only under Central Bank of Samoa Exchange Control approval, with documentary proof of underlying trade exposure; this is generally not recommended for an offshore IC.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (Samoa International Company tax-exempt status); standard domestic headline corporate rate is 27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by a Samoa International Company to non-resident shareholders. Dividends paid by a standard Samoa domestic company to a non-resident are subject to a statutory withholding tax of 20%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No threshold is imposed on a tax-exempt Samoa International Company; it is outside the transfer pricing documentation net. A taxable Samoan enterprise, by contrast, must prepare contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation in accordance with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines when aggregate annual related-party turnover exceeds WST 15,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Samoa International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2018 impose economic substance obligations only where the IC carries on a 'relevant activity' specifically defined as: banking, insurance, financing, leasing, fund management, distribution and service centres, shipping, and intellectual property holding. A designer blind-box toys entity engaged in genuine manufacturing, online merchandising, and direct-to-consumer sales through Shopify Payments, Stripe, or Local POS is not ordinarily engaged in a 'distribution and service centre' activity because the latter targets related-party distribution arrangements. However, the IC must maintain a real physical presence, adequate employees, accurate accounting records, and board control in Samoa if it claims to be a participant in such activities; otherwise the exemption may be disregarded and the IC recharacterised as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A fine of up to WST 1,000,000 or 100% of the tax evaded, whichever is greater, plus default interest and reinstatement of all unpaid taxes for the period during which the exemption was improperly claimed. Criminal conviction under Samoa's Tax Administration Act 2012 carries imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the IC is proven to be an agent, alter ego, or sham vehicle of the director. Directors become personally liable for all unpaid tax, civil penalties, and restitution of diverted profits. The court may impose travel bans, asset freezing orders, and disqualification from managing any company for up to 10 years; imprisonment is automatic upon conviction for willful evasion.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike off the IC, revoke its certificate of incorporation, and impose administrative penalties under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations. The Ministry of Revenue may recharacterise the entire income stream in the hands of the ultimate beneficial owner and levy the standard 27% corporate rate plus 20% withholding tax, together with a 100% civil penalty for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and directors who execute false declarations are jointly and severally liable for all taxes, penalties, and restitution. Courts may issue a 'Continental Indemnity' style asset tracing order against personal bank accounts, enforce personal guarantees on merchant processing agreements, and order passport surrender pending full payment.
AML/CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act and the AML/CFT Regulations, the enforcement agency may impose a penalty of up to WST 750,000 per violation on the company, confiscate the designer-toy inventory and bank balances, and impose imprisonment of up to 7 years on the compliance officer for willful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated Money Laundering Reporting Officer face criminal prosecution, confiscation of personal property, and bankruptcy orders. Where the director knowingly allows the IC to be used for laundering, the court will pierce the veil and hold the director personally liable for the full value of laundered funds, with potential extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Corporate Fraud and False Statutory Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Crimes Act, conviction for false corporate filing or forgery attracts a fine of up to WST 200,000, restitution orders, and imprisonment of up to 5 years. Civil courts may order fraudulent trading compensation to creditors and compulsory winding-up of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for all debts incurred during fraudulent trading. A court may also declare the director personally liable to repay all amounts wrongfully extracted through the IC, prohibit the director from incorporating any new company in Samoa, and issue a bench warrant or travel ban for non-appearance at regulatory hearings.
