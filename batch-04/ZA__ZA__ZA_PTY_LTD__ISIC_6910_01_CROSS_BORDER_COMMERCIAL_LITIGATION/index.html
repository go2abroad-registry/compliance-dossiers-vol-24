Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a private company. The MOI may authorise a nominal share capital of one or more shares with negligible par value; paid-up capital may be zero, and the board may call unpaid share capital by resolution if operating or trust-account capital is required. No escrow account or capital-holding bank account is mandated for registration.

Funding & unlock path: Compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993 and the National Building Regulations SANS 10400, including fire extinguisher inspection, emergency evacuation planning, structural load safety, electrical certificate of compliance, and appointment of a health and safety officer; a municipal fire safety inspection certificate or landlord fire-risk certificate must be retained at the registered office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official eFiling portal at https://efiling.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned or domestic (Pty) Ltd must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). CIPC electronically transmits registration particulars to SARS, which automatically allocates an income tax reference number. For VAT, the entity must apply via SARS eFiling using form VAT 101 after meeting the compulsory registration threshold; a non-resident company or foreign person that engages in taxable activities in South Africa must appoint a SARS representative and register as a vendor. Required supporting documentation includes certified incorporation documents, identity documents of directors/members, proof of address, and, where applicable, a power of attorney for a local representative. All registration filings must be completed through eFiling or a SARS branch before the vendor can issue valid VAT invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned or domestic (Pty) Ltd must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). CIPC electronically transmits registration particulars to SARS, which automatically allocates an income tax reference number. For VAT, the entity must apply via SARS eFiling using form VAT 101 after meeting the compulsory registration threshold; a non-resident company or foreign person that engages in taxable activities in South Africa must appoint a SARS representative and register as a vendor. Required supporting documentation includes certified incorporation documents, identity documents of directors/members, proof of address, and, where applicable, a power of attorney for a local representative. All registration filings must be completed through eFiling or a SARS branch before the vendor can issue valid VAT invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

There is no South African equivalent to a check-the-box election; a resident (Pty) Ltd is automatically a separate taxable person subject to entity-level income tax on worldwide receipts and accruals. Statutes do, however, permit certain elective mechanisms, including the 'headquarter company' status under section 9I of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 for qualifying resident companies substantially holding foreign subsidiaries, corporate rule/rollover elections (sections 41-47), loss-transfer rules for group companies, and the application of the hybrid mismatch rules under section 25N, which are mandatory rather than elective. These elections affect reliefs and deductions but do not alter the entity's classification as a non-transparent corporate taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration is triggered when total taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period. Voluntary registration is available to a person whose taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12 months. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services are subject to the same ZAR 1,000,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may be zero-rated when they are exported from South Africa, provided the supplier retains proof that the goods were removed from the country and have left the Republic within the prescribed period. Services, including cross-border commercial litigation and legal consultancy services, may qualify for zero-rating under section 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act where the service is supplied to a non-resident that is not in South Africa at the time the service is rendered, the services are not connected with land or moveable property physically in South Africa, and the services are not purchased by a person who exports or uses the services directly in a taxable enterprise carried on in South Africa. The supplier must obtain and preserve documentary proof of the non-resident status of the recipient, the nature and purpose of the service, and evidence that payment in foreign currency or Rand was received from outside South Africa.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD500) submitted to SARS Customs
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical export from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an authorised dealer confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial contract, invoice, and bank payment records identifying the foreign client and jurisdiction of payment
  • Proof of non-resident status of the foreign client (e.g., foreign VAT registration, passport copy, or foreign corporate registry extract)
  • Retained zero-rating supporting declarations and correspondence in accordance with SARS schedule 11 requirements

South Africa does not currently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval, or digital stamping mechanism for commercial tax invoices. Electronic tax invoices issued in PDF, XML, EDI or a comparable, integrity-preserving format are accepted for VAT deduction and zero-rating support, provided they satisfy the substance and format requirements of section 20 of the VAT Act, including vendor VAT number, invoice number, value, output tax, and date of issue. SARS requires that electronic records be retained in an unaltered form and be made available during audit; the eFiling platform is used for submission of VAT returns and data extraction. No API-based real-time clearance regime applies as of this stage.

SARS may disallow the zero-rating and assess output VAT at the standard rate of 15% where export documentation is incomplete, missing, or inconsistent. In addition to the tax amount, SARS may impose a 20% understatement penalty for substantial understatement, increasing to 50%, 100%, or 150% depending on the degree of negligence or intentional tax evasion. Interest is charged from the due date of the return. A fraudulent claim for zero-rating or a refund constitutes an offence under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 and may lead to criminal sanctions, including imprisonment, as well as public disclosure under the name-and-shame regime. SARS retains a retrospective audit period of at least five years, and unlimited periods in cases of fraud or non-disclosure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is a partially convertible currency. Current-account items, including cross-border legal fees, arbitration disbursements, litigation settlement payments, and profit remittances (dividends), are freely convertible through an Authorized Dealer without prior SARB approval, subject to FICA verification, sanctions screening, and SARS tax compliance. Capital-account transactions, such as foreign equity subscriptions and inbound shareholder or third-party litigation-funding loans, are permissible but must be routed through and reported by an Authorized Dealer under SARB's electronic reporting framework; dividend remittances require audited financial statements or an auditor's certificate demonstrating distributable reserves and must be processed with the applicable South African dividend tax withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exported goods and services, including cross-border commercial litigation services rendered by a South African (Pty) Ltd, the full invoiced proceeds must ordinarily be received in South Africa through an Authorized Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment or service completion; extended terms require prior FinSurv approval. There is no current fixed percentage mandatory conversion into rand, but 100% of export receipts must be repatriated into a South African domestic bank account or a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with a licensed Authorized Dealer, and any disposal or hedging of such FCA balances must conform to the Exchange Control Regulations. Authorized Dealers must evidence receipt via the EMI (Electronic Message Interface) reporting system to preserve a clean exchange-control audit trail.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act No. 9 of 1932 and the Exchange Control Regulations (Government Notice R1111 of 1963), as amended; administration is delegated to Authorized Dealers (licensed commercial banks) with the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) overseeing AML/CFT obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary banking rail. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital banks have no South African banking license and cannot hold statutory attorneys' trust money or provide ZAR clearing through the state payment system. Such EMIs may serve only as an auxiliary, low-value disbursement corridor for overseas witnesses or arbitral institutions, but usage triggers FIC scrutiny, FX conversion leakage, and the absence of Legal Practice Council trust-account protections; they are not viable for escrow, IOLTA-equivalent, or settlement-grade treasury functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections by non-resident shareholders must be routed through an Authorized Dealer using a standard SWIFT MT103 with a precise purpose narrative (e.g., 'Share subscription — South African corporate registration number'); the AD records the capital flow in the SARB EMI reporting file and assigns the applicable capital transfer purpose code. Third-party litigation-funding loans are treated as inward loans and must be reported to the AD with a loan agreement setting out amount, interest, and term, together with source-of-funds documentation; ordinary FDI equity does not require prior FinSurv approval, but the transaction must be reflected in the SARB reporting records and may be swept into a domestic ZAR account for operating or escrow purposes.Outbound trade and litigation disbursements are cleared primarily through the Authorized Dealer's SWIFT infrastructure using MT103 for individual wire payments and MT202 for cross-border nostro covers, with legal-services purpose codes and underlying invoice/engagement documentation; ZAR domestic leg is settled through the SAMOS interbank system under BankservAfrica clearing rules. Commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) and standby LCs (ISP98) are permissible for cross-border settlement of trade and arbitration security costs, but for litigation awards, direct SWIFT transfers with a covering legal opinion and arbitral award order are the standard clearing rail; payments above internal thresholds require enhanced FICA verification and sanctions-list confirmation.Deliverable outright forward contracts (proximate cover only) for confirmed foreign-currency receivables and payables arising from litigation funding, legal fees, or awardsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/ZAR with a licensed foreign banking counterparty, used where the underlying exposure is marked to the offshore leg of a dispute structureCross-currency interest rate and principal swaps (CCS) booked through an Authorized Dealer to convert foreign-denominated litigation funding liabilities into ZAREuropean-style FX options (and collars) with an Authorized Dealer or approved offshore bank, provided the notional does not exceed the underlying approved current or capital transaction exposureZero-premium participating or ratio forward structures approved by FinSurv where they hedge a specific verified export invoice or litigation settlement flow

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5,000,000 aggregate value of all affected cross-border transactions concluded in a year of assessment generally triggers the requirement to prepare and maintain a SARS-aligned Local File. A Master File is expected for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding ZAR 10,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no general Economic Substance Regulations statute applicable to all Pty Ltd companies. However, SARS applies substance-over-form doctrine, the general anti-avoidance rules in sections 80A to 80L of the Income Tax Act, and the controlled foreign company provisions in section 9D. A shell company with no real office, no employees and no substantial economic activity may be disregarded, have its transactions re-characterised, or be denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return Submission

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 10% for standard cases, 50% for substantial non-compliance, 75% for intentional or negligent conduct, and 150% for concealment. Criminal conviction for tax evasion under the Tax Administration Act is punishable by a fine or imprisonment, with full additional tax and prescribed-rate interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who sign or control false returns may be personally liable for the company's tax debt under the reckless-trading provisions. Civil restitution can attach to personal assets; SARS may issue a travel ban, prohibit the director from leaving South Africa until the debt is finalised, and criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Misstatement

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the consideration to an arm's-length amount under section 31, disallow excessive interest deductions, impose deemed interest on unpaid amounts, and apply the understatement penalty table of 10% to 150%. Interest runs from the original assessment date.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise or sign transfer pricing returns without obtaining contemporaneous documentation can be personally assessed for the understatement. If the arrangement is shown to be a sham, corporate veil piercing allows SARS to recover the additional tax from director bank accounts and personal property, and directors may face travel restrictions and criminal prosecution.
Shell Company Structure / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the entity, apply the general anti-avoidance rules, or attribute the income to the real economic owner. Tax benefits are clawed back and understatement penalties up to 150% may be imposed. Controlled foreign company rules under section 9D may attribute foreign passive income to South African shareholders.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who allow the company to operate without substance can be held personally liable for the resulting tax debt and penalties. Civil restitution orders can trace hidden fees back to directors, travel bans may be imposed, and imprisonment is possible where the structure is used to evade tax or launder litigation proceeds.
Repatriation Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The unpaid withholding tax becomes a tax debt of the company as withholding agent. SARS imposes a 10% additional tax, prescribed-rate interest, and understatement penalties from 10% to 150%. Failure to withhold is a criminal offence under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise dividend or interest payments without ensuring withholding taxes are withheld may be held personally liable for the unpaid tax. SARS can issue a personal liability notice, attach personal assets, and prevent the director from travelling abroad until the debt is satisfied.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Intelligence Centre Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Centre Act administrative penalties can reach ZAR 10 million per contravention. Criminal conviction can result in a fine or imprisonment. The Legal Practice Council may also suspend or cancel the practice's licence, and proceeds of unlawful activity are subject to confiscation under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners who knowingly participate in money laundering or fail to exercise oversight of FICA obligations are personally liable. The corporate veil may be pierced to recover the value of laundered funds, personal travel may be restricted, and imprisonment remains a real consequence for egregious non-compliance.
