Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a (Pty) Ltd; minimum capital and escrow-account requirements were abolished by the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Shares may be par-value or no-par-value, and the initial subscription is fixed solely by the MOI.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), eFiling portal (www.sars.gov.za / secure.efiling.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A South African-incorporated Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd is automatically registered for income tax upon incorporation through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) under the integrated company registration system; SARS issues a Tax Reference Number (TRN) via eFiling Notice of Registration, which the company must confirm within 30 days. For VAT, a separate application is mandatory: file form VAT101/VAT101V electronically, supported by CIPC registration documents, directors' particulars, and a SARS-appointed representative or public officer. A foreign-incorporated entity without local incorporation may obtain a TRN for income tax, dividend withholding tax, VAT, or PAYE obligations through SARS's non-resident registration process, requiring appointment of a representative taxpayer in South Africa and the filing of the appropriate registration schedules.

For VAT, a separate application is mandatory: file form VAT101/VAT101V electronically, supported by CIPC registration documents, directors' particulars, and a SARS-appointed representative or public officer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 include: (1) the section 9I headquarter company election for a qualifying South African resident company whose foreign shareholders hold at least 80% of equity and voting rights, yielding concessions on foreign dividends, interest, royalties, and controlled foreign company (CFC) income; (2) section 41 group restructuring elections for tax-neutral intra-group asset transfers; (3) section 45 and section 47 corporate amalgamation and unbundling elections. Hybrid mismatch electivity is not permitted; sections 8E and 8EA deny deductions for hybrid equity and hybrid debt instruments regardless of taxpayer election, and anti-hybrid measures under the CFC rules override foreign entity classification mismatches. A Pty Ltd resident in South Africa is taxed on worldwide income at headline CGT-inclusive corporate rates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration: actual or expected taxable supplies exceeding R1 million in any 12-month rolling period (s23); voluntary registration: taxable supplies exceeding R50,000 in any 12-month rolling period (s25), subject to SARS's discretion. The R1 million compulsory threshold also applies to foreign vendors making taxable supplies in South Africa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, supplies of movable goods consigned or delivered to a destination outside South Africa qualify for the 0% rate if the goods are exported within the prescribed period (90 days from the time of supply in standard SARS VAT 404 guidance, extendable by SARS on application) and the vendor retains complete and verifiable proof of export. Services qualify for 0% under section 11(2)(l) where the supply is to a non-resident who is not present in South Africa at the time of supply and the services are used wholly for a taxable purpose in a foreign country; financial advisory and private equity buyout services rendered offshore are eligible where the non-resident nexus and foreign-use criteria are demonstrably satisfied. Goods supplied to a free-trade or special economic zone are not automatically zero-rated unless exported in accordance with the express provisions of the VAT Act and relevant regulations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged via SARS Customs / Acardia portal
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Airway Bill (air freight) evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank statement evidencing foreign-currency payment received in South Africa
  • Original VAT invoice issued under section 20 of the VAT Act
  • Commercial invoice and packing list corroborating the declared goods
  • Certificate of Origin where a preferential tariff or trade protocol is invoked

No mandatory real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance, digital fiscal stamping, or government-authorised clearance API is currently in force for domestic VAT invoices in South Africa. VAT invoices must comply with section 20 requirements, be issued within 21 days of the time of supply, and be sequentially numbered; eFiling and the SARS Customs single administrative document (SAD500) pathway operate as the electronic lodgement platforms. SARS has published an e-invoicing modernisation roadmap and consultations since 2023, signalling future adoption of interoperable e-invoicing standards, but as of the 2024/2025 fiscal year no statutory real-time invoice clearance API mandate exists.

SARS may disallow export zero-rating and claw back output VAT at 15% where proof of export, payment receipts, or timeous foreign-currency repatriation is defective. Statutory consequences under the VAT Act include additional tax of up to 100% (section 59) and up to 200% for intentional non-declaration or evasion (section 60), plus interest levied under section 39 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (TAA). SARS may also impose understatement penalties under sections 222-223 TAA ranging from 10% to 150% depending on conduct, and criminal liability with fines or imprisonment may arise under sections 234-235 TAA and the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 for false registration, false declarations, or fraudulent export claims. Exchange control contraventions for failing to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days (or extended period granted by the Financial Surveillance Department of the South African Reserve Bank) attract administrative penalties and potential debarment from the authorised dealer system.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is comprehensively convertible for current-account items (trade, services, fees, dividends, interest) without prior SARB approval, subject to documentary verification and a SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) pin where outbound. Capital-account items (inward equity/debt into the PE SPV, outbound acquisition payments, onward lending, disinvestment) are regulated but liberalised for bona fide commercial transactions: inward FDI requires only Authorised Dealer (bank) capture of the Balance of Payments (BOP) report and creation of the inward-investment file; outward acquisition payments by the resident SPV require proof of the underlying transaction, appropriate BOP purpose coding, and, where financing is by non-resident debt, compliance with market-linked interest-rate requirements. ZAR is a non-internationalised currency: cross-border payments must be settled in foreign currency through a SARB-authorised dealer; offshore ZAR clearing is prohibited. Within the Common Monetary Area (Lesotho, Namibia, Eswatini), ZAR transfers are unrestricted. Profit remittance: a (Pty) Ltd SPV may remit dividends and redeemable-preference returns to non-resident shareholders on a current-account basis if (i) the inward investment was reported at inflow, (ii) audited/signed financials evidence distributable reserves under the Companies Act 71 of 2008, (iii) the board passed the solvency-and-liquidity test (sections 4/46), and (iv) dividend tax at 20% (or treaty relief) is withheld.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not directly applicable to an ISIC 6499 PE buyout SPV with no export trade. For any trading affiliate, SARB Exchange Control Rules require full repatriation of export proceeds into South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days of shipment. The historic mandatory ZAR conversion (100% surrender) was abolished with effect from 1 January 2021 under the SARB exchange control modernisation; exporters may retain the full amount of export proceeds in Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs). Effective surrender-to-ZAR ratio: 0%; mandatory repatriation-to-house-bank: 100%. For the SPV, all inbound acquisition-related funds must similarly be received into the designated authorised-dealer account and be BOP-coded to establish the inward-investment record.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting as delegated agent of the National Treasury under the Currency and Exchanges Act, Act 9 of 1933, as amended, together with the Exchange Control Regulations (1961) and the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual (as updated).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW for capital-account deal flows. Legal constraints: (i) only SARB-authorised dealers (licensed banks) may execute FX conversion and settlement of ZAR against foreign currency; (ii) non-resident EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, MultiPass, Payoneer) hold no South African banking licence and cannot open ZAR accounts with SWIFT/ZAR capacity or act as accountable institutions under FICA for BOP filing; (iii) the FSCA/IFWG sandbox allows innovation testing but does not authorise EMI-class cross-border FX or capital-account intermediation for acquisition SPVs; (iv) an EMI would fail the source-of-funds and traceability requirements of a leveraged buyout. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident sponsor/investors remit equity and/or loan finance to the SPV's designated ZAR or FCA account at the chosen authorised dealer. Routing protocol: (1) Payment is sent via SWIFT MT103 (for amounts up to large-value thresholds) or MT202/COV for interbank cover; no pre-approval is required for equity injection, but the bank must open an inward-investment file; (2) Purpose code: the SWIFT beneficiary line and remittance advice must reference the SARB Balance of Payments (BOP) transaction purpose code corresponding to 'inward direct investment in shares of a South African private company' (BOP 1610-series as mapped by the authorised dealer) or, for debt, the appropriate 'other investment / loan' liability code; incorrect coding creates an unfiled currency reference that blocks later repatriation; (3) the authorised dealer records the BOP/IIP report with SARB FinSurv and issues a unique investment reference / forex file number, which must be maintained on the treasury ledger as the single source of truth for all future outward transfers and disinvestment repatriations; (4) for loan capital, interest terms must be market-linked (within the SARB benchmark band); otherwise a prior SARB approval application is mandatory; (5) verification: the bank retains evidence of the lender/investor source of funds (audited accounts, fund statements), conducts AML screening of both remitter and beneficiary, and may require a SARS TCS pin for the SPV before outbound flows are activated.The SPV's principal outbound flows are: (i) acquisition consideration to selling shareholders; (ii) repayment and interest on acquisition debt; (iii) professional fees (legal, audit, transaction advisory); and (iv) sponsor/management fees. All are routed via SWIFT (MT103 for customer-initiated payments; MT202 COV for interbank cover) or, in select structures, via documentary instruments where warranties and escrow mechanics apply: (a) Commercial Letters of Credit are uncommon for share acquisitions but can secure deferred consideration using a standby L/C issued by the South African acquiring bank; (b) escrow accounts at the authorised dealer or a conveyancer's trust account settle by MT103 value-dated to the completion date; (c) the BOP code for each outbound must match the underlying transaction (e.g., 'outward direct investment / purchase of non-resident shares' for the target acquisition; 'repayment of foreign loan' for debt service), and SARS TCS pins are mandatory for pecuniary payments to non-residents; (d) same-day domestic ZAR clearing for local pre-acquisition funding runs over SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement), the SARB-operated RTGS system; Legal constraints: (i) only SARB-authorised dealers (licensed banks) may execute FX conversion and settlement of ZAR against foreign currency; (ii) non-resident EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, MultiPass, Payoneer) hold no South African banking licence and cannot open ZAR accounts with SWIFT/ZAR capacity or act as accountable institutions under FICA for BOP filing; (iii) the FSCA/IFWG sandbox allows innovation testing but does not authorise EMI-class cross-border FX or capital-account intermediation for acquisition SPVs; (iv) an EMI would fail the source-of-funds and traceability requirements of a leveraged buyout. Domestic digital banks (TymeBank, Discovery Bank) are similarly unsuitable, lacking corporate FCA, BOP and FinSurv filing mechanics. Acceptable limited use: payment of small operating expenses of the SPV (software, regulatory fees) from sponsor funds; never for acquisition consideration, loan disbursement, or repatriation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for companies with years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (previously 28%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on dividends paid by a South African resident company (s64E, Income Tax Act). A unilateral reduction to 5% applies under s64FA where the beneficial owner holds at least 10% of the equity shares and the dividend is paid on or after 1 April 2012, regardless of a tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

IRP6: first payment by end of six months from commencement; second payment by the last day of the year of assessment (for companies with year-end on the last day of a month).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation must be prepared and retained for cross-border connected-party transactions exceeding ZAR 5 million in aggregate per year of assessment. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 1 billion. Country-by-Country Report is due for groups with consolidated revenue above ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa applies a full corporate tax system; zero-tax Economic Substance Regulations (as in UAE/Bermuda) are not directly applicable. However, the SPV must demonstrate genuine commercial substance locally to support tax residence (place of effective management test), escape the general anti-avoidance rule (s103(2)), and sustain pricing of intra-group loans, management fees, and acquisition services. The fixed-ratio rule under s23N specifically limits interest deductions on acquisition debt to the 'official rate' (repo rate + 100 basis points) and spreads the deduction over a period, effectively imposing a substance-based backstop on leveraged buy-out structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall (or 150% for a reportable avoidance arrangement) under s223 of the Tax Administration Act; plus 10% late-payment penalty and interest. Criminal conviction under s239 TAA: fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Public Officer are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts if the default was attributable to their fraud, negligence, or personal fault (s159–160 TAA). Directors face criminal prosecution, High Court travel bans through departure-prohibition orders (s189 TAA), disqualification under the Companies Act, and civil restitution in SARS debt-collection proceedings.
Thin Capitalization and Excessive Cross-Border Interest

Statutory sanction: Denial of excess interest deductions under s23N and s31(3); re-characterization of the excess return as a dividend subject to DWT at 20%/5%; reassessments with interest and understatement penalties of 75–100% for gross negligence or intentional evasion; possible withdrawal of tax clearance certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign misleading IRP6/ITR14 filings in respect of acquisition debt become personally liable for the assessed shortfall; failure to take reasonable care in verifying arm's-length pricing exposes directors to s159 TAA personal liability, including attachment of personal assets and travel restrictions.
Shell Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS invokes s103(2) GAAR or substance-over-form doctrine to disregard the SPV and tax the non-resident investors as if the entity did not exist; understatement penalties of 100–150%; refusal of treaty relief; denial of assessed loss utilization; deregistration or strike-off action by the CIPC.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and promoters face personal assessments as de facto trustees of the arrangement; fraudulent-trading liability under s77(3)(d) of the Companies Act; criminal sanctions for aiding tax avoidance; travel bans and freezing orders pending SARS investigations.
AML and Financial Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Offences under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA): fine up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment up to 15 years (s64); administrative sanctions up to ZAR 10 million imposed by the FIC; asset forfeiture under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act (POCA); prohibition of outbound transfers and de-risking by the payment bank.

Director personal liability: Directors can be indicted for money-laundering and financing of terrorism, with personal assets frozen, travel bans, and extradition exposure; disqualification from acting as a director for up to 10 years under s69 of the Companies Act; joint and several liability for the SPV's AML fines where the director failed to prevent the breach.
