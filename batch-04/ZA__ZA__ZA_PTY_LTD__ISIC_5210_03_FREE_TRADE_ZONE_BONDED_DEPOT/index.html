Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://efiling.sars.gov.za; corporate registration via Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd is first incorporated through CIPC. Upon incorporation, SARS registers the company for income tax; South African tax reference number is issued, after which VAT vendor registration is separately effected through SARS eFiling using Form VAT 101 where taxable supplies meet the relevant threshold. Foreign principals must appoint a SARS-recognised representative taxpayer and provide founding documents, proof of registered address, identity particulars of responsible persons, and CIPC registration certificate.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd is first incorporated through CIPC. Upon incorporation, SARS registers the company for income tax; South African tax reference number is issued, after which VAT vendor registration is separately effected through SARS eFiling using Form VAT 101 where taxable supplies meet the relevant threshold. Foreign principals must appoint a SARS-recognised representative taxpayer and provide founding documents, proof of registered address, identity particulars of responsible persons, and CIPC registration certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not employ an elective pass-through check-the-box system for Pty Ltd entities. Classification is fixed by legal form under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Elections are available for headquarter company status under section 9I, for certain controlled foreign company inclusions, and for dealings in hybrid instruments; hybrid mismatch rules impose mandatory adjustments rather than elective elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies per twelve-month period (or reasonable expectation); voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 in taxable supplies per twelve-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from South Africa to a destination outside SACU, and removed from the bonded depot under customs supervision, qualify for zero-rated VAT under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 provided the supplier retains prescribed documentary proof that the goods have left South Africa within the allowed period. Certain categories of services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under section 11(2)(l), but the claim must be supported by export documentation as specified in SARS VAT 411.

  • Valid SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 / eCustoms bill of entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing destination outside South Africa
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (or bank SWIFT receipt)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the bonded depot
  • Customs-protected removal record from the bonded warehouse

SARS requires a valid tax invoice to support output tax; electronic tax invoices are permitted in a durable, machine-readable format. As of the current regime there is no national real-time fiscal stamping or continuous clearance mandate, but SARS accepts digital data submissions through eFiling and e@syFile, and customs export entries are filed electronically (SAD 500/E-Customs). SARS is progressively introducing mandatory e-invoicing for registered vendors, with phased implementation and data-reporting requirements expected for designated classes of suppliers.

SARS may audit export zero-rating claims within five years of assessment; insufficient export proof reverses the zero-rating, output VAT becomes due, and refunds are clawed back with interest from the date paid. Deliberate or grossly negligent conduct triggers the Tax Administration Act understatement penalty of 10% to 75%, with additional SARS assessment of up to 200% for tax evasion; fraudulent export declarations additionally expose directors and representatives to criminal prosecution under the Customs and Excise Act, including imprisonment up to 10 years and financial fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible on the current account for trade in goods and services, provided the mandated supporting documentation is presented to an authorised dealer (licensed commercial bank) and the transaction is correctly disclosed on the balance-of-payments (BoP) reporting system. The capital account operates under a managed-convertibility regime: non-resident foreign direct investment into a South African (Pty) Ltd is liberalised and does not require prior FinSurv approval, but the inward funds must be received through an authorised dealer, credited to a domestic ZAR account and reported as an inward investment. Profit remittance: dividends may be remitted offshore after declaration from after-tax profits, payment of South African dividend withholding tax to SARS, and presentation of a valid SARS tax clearance certificate together with evidence that the underlying capital entered the country lawfully; interest on shareholder loans is remittable only where the external loan agreement was reported or approved as required and the rate is arm's-length. Practical hurdles for a bonded-depot treasury: the capital escrow remains frozen until bank KYC is cleared; every later current-account outflow must be matched to import/export documents (SAD500, EX-1, invoices, bills of lading); and any rapid in-and-out movement of capital or triangulation to a third country triggers enhanced scrutiny by the authorised dealer and FinSurv.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 5(1) and the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, export proceeds must be received in full by the exporter within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless FinSurv grants a longer period. Upon receipt, the foreign currency must be surrendered to an authorised dealer and converted to ZAR at the prevailing spot rate, with 30 days being the standard surrender window applied by South African banks. An exporter holding an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) may retain up to 100% of the proceeds in foreign currency for permissible working-capital uses; without FCA approval, mandatory conversion to ZAR on receipt is required. The exporter must file the customs export declaration (EX-1) and align it with the bank's BoP report; any shortfall or excess between goods shipped and proceeds received must be explained through the authorised dealer export-verification procedure, and all proceeds from bonded warehouse sales must pass through the licensed entity's own bank account to preserve clean realisation evidence.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting as the delegated exchange-control authority under the Exchange Control Regulations issued pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

LOW-MEDIUM as the primary treasury rail, acceptable only as a supplementary front-end. Under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, only registered banks may accept deposits or hold customer funds in South Africa; under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998, ZAR clearing and settlement through SAMOS is restricted to licensed settlement participants; and under the Exchange Control Regulations, trade receipts and payments must pass through an authorised dealer for BoP reporting. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) cannot directly hold ZAR settlement balances, cannot satisfy SARS and SARB trade-reporting requirements, and their use for bulk trade remittances would be treated as third-party payments attracting enhanced AML review. The feasible structure is to run all regulated liquidity through a Tier-1 domestic bank using SWIFT and local clearing rails, and to use an offshore fintech platform only for small incidental cross-border disbursements and FX visibility, with full OFAC, UN, EU and UK sanctions screening integrated.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) pass board and shareholder resolutions authorising the equity subscription for bonded-warehouse operations in the Free Trade Zone; (2) instruct the offshore parent or investor to send a SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the accepting South African authorised dealer's nostro account, with beneficiary shown as the entity's registered name and account number and the remittance field stating 'Share subscription / Inward FDI / ISIC 5210 Bonded Depot / CIPC registration number', using the bank's inward investment purpose code for direct-investment equity; (3) deliver the inward-transfer notification packet within 24 hours of value date containing corporate resolutions, FICA documents, source-of-funds evidence, upstream SWIFT confirmation and an undertaking not to deploy funds until clearance; (4) the bank credits the ZAR Capital Escrow Account at the ruling spot rate; (5) the authorised dealer files the inward transfer and BoP report with SARB FinSurv in accordance with the Currency and Exchanges Manual - no prior FinSurv approval is required for pure equity FDI; (6) after KYC, SARS and sanctions clearance, the bank issues the capitalisation release and the funds may be applied to depot development and working capital; no in-and-out triangulation to a third country within 90 days is permitted. If funding is by shareholder loan instead of equity, the external loan agreement must be approved or reported through the authorised dealer before disbursement.Clearing rails for the bonded-depot treasury: (1) direct foreign supplier payments via SWIFT MT103 with full invoice, packing list and customs-entry identifiers, supported by MT202 bank-to-bank cover where nostro funding is required; (2) commercial documentary Letters of Credit issued by the South African bank via MT700, with amendments via MT707, for high-value imports, requiring presentation of compliant documents at the confirming bank; (3) documentary collections (MT400 series, D/P and D/A) for open-account trade; (4) domestic EFT and SAMOS clearing for ZAR settlement between the depot and local tenants or SACU counterparties; (5) outward remittances of dividends, interest and royalties require an approved FCA debit, SARS tax clearance and an outbound BoP income or service code. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (28% for prior years of assessment)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax (Income Tax Act s64E) imposed on the beneficial shareholder at the time dividend is paid by the resident company; dividends paid to another South African-resident company that qualifies for the 'exempt shareholder' provisions are exempt from dividends tax; dividends to non-resident shareholders are subject to 20% unless reduced by a double tax agreement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 must be submitted within 12 months after the end of the company's year of assessment (Tax Administration Act No. 28 of 2011, s33); for a typical February year-end, the statutory eFiling deadline is 28 February of the following year. Provisional tax payments are due on the last business day of the sixth month and the last business day of the year of assessment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where the aggregate value of cross-border connected-person transactions exceeds ZAR 100 million in the year of assessment, or where a single transaction category exceeds ZAR 50 million; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion (Public Notice GG 46955, effective for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022); a de minimis threshold of ZAR 1 million applies below which s31 arm's length provisions do not apply to a transaction class.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation separate from income tax; substance requirements are enforced through the Income Tax Act general anti-avoidance rules (ss80A-80L), controlled foreign company provisions (s9D), the 15% SEZ preferential rate requiring genuine operations within a designated zone, and the Customs and Excise Act bonded warehouse licensing regime which demands the physical licence holder, on-site inventory management, segregation of bonded goods, auditable stock records, and a registered premises. A free trade zone bonded depot that operates as a shell - without employees, premises, management decisions, or economic risk in South Africa - will be disregarded for tax purposes, the SEZ privileges revoked, and all prior preferential concessions recaptured.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall for intentional tax evasion (TAA Table 3); interest at the prescribed rate compounded; criminal conviction on a charge of tax evasion carries imprisonment of up to 10 years (TAA s234); SARS may also issue a tax certificate as an impediment to the company's customs licence renewal.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the false return may be jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall, interest and penalties under s241(2) TAA; a director may additionally face personal criminal liability, travel bans (SARS emigration banishment order under s159 TAA), seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment on conviction for fraud or theft of revenue.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The SEZ tax dispensation is revoked with retrospective effect; the full 27% rate plus interest and a 50% to 200% understatement penalty is assessed due to the absence of reasonable grounds; SARS may apply the GAAR (s80A-80L) to void the arrangement and re-characterise the income; the customs licence may be suspended under the Customs and Excise Act for failure to maintain the depot's statutory presence.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell company risk a civil claim by SARS for the unrecouped tax liability, a ban on holding directorships under s69 of the Companies Act, cross-border travel bans, and a criminal charge for the fraudulent claim of preferential tax status; the company's bank accounts may be frozen and payments from foreign principals intercepted.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: SARS primary adjustment re-determining taxable income to the arm's length amount, secondary adjustment treating the underpayment as a loan-bearing interest (deemed dividend or deemed loan under s31(2)), and an understatement penalty in the range of 50% to 200% if the taxpayer fails to show that it applied reasonable care or where there is gross negligence; interest accrues from the original assessment date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing documentation that is knowingly fraudulent may be personally liable for the understatement penalties; revenue authorities may hold directors liable for the company's tax debt in voluntary liquidation scenarios; repeated mispricing triggers criminal referral and SARS enforcement actions including attachment of foreign bank accounts.
Dividends and Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The company is liable for the full unpaid dividends tax over and above the shareholder's liability, plus prescribed interest and a 10% late payment penalty; royalty/interest withholding failures attract the same recovery, interest, and penalties; SARS may also treat the royalty payment as a deduction disallowed if the tax was not withheld.

Director personal liability: Under s179 TAA, directors are jointly and severally liable for all withholding taxes (dividends, royalties, interest) that the company failed to collect and pay over; SARS may issue a director liability assessment, attach personal assets, obtain a court order, and prohibit directors from leaving South Africa until the debt is settled.
Customs Bonded Depot Violations

Statutory sanction: Seizure and forfeiture of the goods, an administrative penalty of up to three times the duties evaded (Customs and Excise Act s99), cancellation or suspension of the customs bonded warehouse licence, and prosecution with fines of up to R50,000 or imprisonment up to five years for each act of unauthorised removal.

Director personal liability: The licence holder - typically the (Pty) Ltd - is strictly liable, and directors who are the licensed operator's controlling minds may be criminally prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act; SARS Customs may institute a revenue claim against directors' personal estates, and a criminal conviction can attract a custodial sentence.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance (FICA)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) of up to R10 million for accountable institutions; the General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering) Amendment Act adds criminal sanctions where the breach is repeated or coupled with concealment; directors may be fined up to R100,000 and imprisoned for up to five years for intentional failure to report.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge of or directing the non-reporting are personally criminally liable; the Financial Intelligence Centre may impose against a director the same administrative penalty as the company, and customs/banking access is revoked, effectively terminating the bonded depot licence and incurring civil claims for the group's losses.
