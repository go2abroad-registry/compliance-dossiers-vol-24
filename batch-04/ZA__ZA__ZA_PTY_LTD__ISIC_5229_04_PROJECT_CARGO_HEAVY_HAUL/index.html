Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to a private company; the MOI may authorize shares with or without par value. At least one share must be subscribed by an incorporator on incorporation. There is no escrow bank account requirement; however, inward foreign equity contributions must be channelled through a SARB Authorised Dealer (bank) under the Financial Surveillance Regulations, with capital import reporting where applicable.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the local municipal fire authority under the National Building Regulations (SANS 10400-T) with Certificate of Occupancy; structural floor-load verification by a registered professional engineer for any warehouse or depot; Department of Employment and Labour workplace compliance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, including a valid COIDA Letter of Good Standing, emergency response plan, and dangerous goods handling approvals where out-of-gauge cargo is accompanied by hazardous materials.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — portal: SARS eFiling at www.sars.gov.za, including Customs processing via the SARS Customs division

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishes the South African Proprietary Limited company through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), obtaining a CIPC registration number and Memorandum of Incorporation. The company then registers with SARS via eFiling or through a registered tax practitioner using the IT14 income tax registration form, the VAT101 VAT vendor application (where the compulsory threshold is met), and a PAYE registration where employees are engaged. Non-resident directors/shareholders must submit certified identity documents, board resolutions, and proof of registered office. SARS issues the Income Tax Reference Number and VAT registration number upon verification; registration must be completed within 21 business days of commencement of business or incorporation. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services, where applicable, must separately register under section 54(7) of the VAT Act.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign parent establishes the South African Proprietary Limited company through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), obtaining a CIPC registration number and Memorandum of Incorporation. The company then registers with SARS via eFiling or through a registered tax practitioner using the IT14 income tax registration form, the VAT101 VAT vendor application (where the compulsory threshold is met), and a PAYE registration where employees are engaged. Non-resident directors/shareholders must submit certified identity documents, board resolutions, and proof of registered office. SARS issues the Income Tax Reference Number and VAT registration number upon verification; registration must be completed within 21 business days of commencement of business or incorporation. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services, where applicable, must separately register under section 54(7) of the VAT Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not offer a check-the-box election equivalent. The (Pty) Ltd is automatically an entity-level taxpayer resident in South Africa under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, taxed on worldwide income at the corporate rate of 27%. Relevant statutory elections include: section 24J interest accrual elections (for hybrid or milestone-based project funding), section 8E/8EA anti-hybrid disallowance elections, section 9D controlled foreign company high-tax exemption elections where the foreign effective rate exceeds 81.25% of the South African rate, and company distribution/withholding tax elections under sections 64B–64G. Transfer pricing and thin capitalisation adjustments apply mandatorily under section 31, and hybrid mismatch arrangements with treaty jurisdictions are counteracted under section 25D and the OECD hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (compulsory VAT vendor registration); voluntary registration is permitted from ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies, and a non-resident carrying on an enterprise with a South African branch or main head office is deemed to be a vendor. A ZAR 2,000,000 turnover concession applies to the payment basis of accounting for small vendors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act applies to the export of goods where South Africa is the origin of export and the exporter retains documentary proof of removal to a destination outside South Africa within the prescribed period. Export-related services, including heavy-haul and out-of-gauge project cargo freight, are zero-rated under section 11(1)(g) where the services are supplied directly in connection with exported goods or the international transport of such goods, the recipient is a non-resident, and the supplier remains liable for the risk of loss. Ancillary services must be separately substantiated and connected to a genuine physical export. The zero-rating claim is invalid where the goods are subsequently returned to South Africa without proper customs entry. Vendors must account for zero-rated supplies on the VAT return and file supporting schedules to the SARS vendor desk.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged via SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill (Air Waybill for air freight) evidencing through carriage to a final foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank SWIFT confirmation of foreign payment
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and freight-forwarding contract for the project cargo
  • Signed Proof of Delivery / delivery receipt from the consignee at the foreign project site
  • VAT 263 refund application or offset election annexed to the periodic VAT return

SARS mandates VAT tax invoices under section 20(4) of the VAT Act for all taxable B2B supplies, with de minimis simplified-invoice concessions for small-value supplies. SARS is progressively introducing a mandatory national e-invoicing framework starting with the largest VAT vendors: invoices must be generated in the SARS-compliant XML format, digitally signed, transmitted through SARS-approved interoperability service providers, and reported on a near-real-time continuous transaction control basis. Full real-time fiscal clearance and government digital stamping are not yet universal for the logistics sector but are anticipated in phases; large freight-forwarding vendors should ensure e-archiving capability, API-based submission with SARS eFiling, retention of invoice integrity records for five years, and VAT 263 refund lodgement via eFiling.

SARS audits export zero-rating by cross-matching the SAD500 customs declaration, carrier records, bank statements, and e-invoicing data. Where the vendor fails to substantiate the export, the supply is re-characterised as standard-rated: output VAT at 15% is levied under section 10(6) of the VAT Act, and a 10% additional VAT penalty is imposed for undeclared tax. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties of up to 150% apply for intentional tax evasion or gross negligence; criminal convictions can attract fines of up to ZAR 10,000,000 or imprisonment of up to 10 years. Retrospective clawbacks arise where export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, triggering exchange-control penalties and, in severe cases, forfeiture of goods or denial of the input VAT deduction connected to the export supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current-account trade and services. The capital account is embedded in SARB exchange control: inward equity, shareholder loans, and external borrowing require compliance with the Currency and Exchanges Manual (CM) and reporting through an authorized dealer. Profit remittance/dividend outflow to non-resident shareholders is permitted without FinSurv prior approval if the entity is tax-compliant with SARS, has no outstanding tax debt, has recorded the inward equity on the Non-Resident Equity register, and the remittance is routed via an authorized dealer on Form MP336(a). Loans from non-residents must be registered in the SARB Foreign Loan register; interest payments require the added withholding-tax resolution step (15% domestic WHT, subject to treaty relief).

Export proceeds surrender rules

The CM (Section E) obligates the exporter to receive full export proceeds in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment. The default surrender rule requires conversion of hard-currency receipts into ZAR upon collection. An approved exporter may obtain a SARB Foreign Currency Account (FCA) status that allows 100% retention in the original hard currency for working-capital purposes, effectively reducing the mandatory surrender ratio to 0% for that approved class; otherwise, the mandatory conversion ratio is 100%, with all receipts reported through the authorized dealer and reconciled to SARS Customs and the Single Export Document.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act No. 9 of 1933 (as amended) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (Government Notice R.1111). Authorized dealers are domestic banks licensed by FinSurv to implement exchange control policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A compliant capital-injection routing is: (1) the company passes a shareholder allotment resolution before the inflow date; (2) the remitter initiates a SWIFT MT103/MT202 with narrative referencing 'Share Subscription / MP337'; (3) the receiving South African authorized dealer executes Form MP337 and credits the local account; (4) the bank records the inflow under balance-of-payments purpose code 4102 (ordinary share capital) or 4105 (shareholder loan); (5) the bank issues an inward-funds confirmation and balance certificate; (6) the company files the CIPC allotment and beneficial-ownership particulars within 20 business days; and (7) any intercompany loan is registered in the SARB Foreign Loan Register. No mandatory ZAR conversion applies to the inward capital; conversion to ZAR occurs at market rate when operational funding is deployed.For milestone wires: SWIFT MT103 with narrative bearing the freight or service invoice number and the SARB balance-of-payments purpose code aligned to the service (e.g., 2197 sea freight, 2195/2196 road and air freight, 2270 auxiliary transport services). Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% final Dividend Withholding Tax (DWT) on dividends paid by South African resident companies

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return due within 12 months after financial year-end; provisional tax payments due 6 months after year-end with top-up by 6 months after year-end if required.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed R50 million per annum; Master File for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least R10 billion. SARS may request documentation at lower thresholds under s31 ITA / s25 TAA.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no formal ESR regime; however, CIT residency flows from incorporation/effective management, and GAAR (s80A-L) applies to disregard shell structures lacking commercial substance. A ZA (Pty) Ltd is tax-resident and taxed on worldwide income unless termination of residence or treaty tie-breaker applies.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Transfer Pricing Understatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty up to 200% of shortfall, interest at statutory rate, criminal prosecution under s235 TAA; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors participating in fraud may incur criminal liability; SARS may invoke s159 TAA to hold directors personally liable for unremitted PAYE/VAT/UIF; civil restitution and cost orders apply.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR (s80A-L) allows SARS to disregard the arrangement, re-characterise transactions, and impose up to 75% penalty for impermissible avoidance; deductions may be denied.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability under the Companies Act for reckless trading; SARS can apply exit control orders or travel bans where a director attempts to move assets or flee the jurisdiction.
AML / FIC Compliance Red Flags

Statutory sanction: FIC administrative penalties up to R10 million per contravention; POCA money-laundering offences may result in imprisonment up to 15 years; CIPC penalties for non-filing of beneficial ownership returns.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to supervise AML obligations can be held personally liable for fines; imprisonment for money laundering; travel bans and freezing orders may be issued.
