Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Companies Act 71 of 2008, covering s1 definitions, s13 power to incorporate, s14 registration and certificate, s19 legal personality, s66 board of directors, and s69 directors; together with the Companies Regulations, 2011, including prescribed Forms CoR 14.1 and CoR 14.1B.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notice of Incorporation (CIPC Form CoR 14.1), filed together with the Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI) via CIPC eServices.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is imposed under the Companies Act 71 of 2008, and no escrow or blocked capital account is required. Shares may be issued for cash, property, or services as approved by the board; any unpaid or deferred payment arrangement must be recorded in the share register and board minutes.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the applicable municipal fire department, compliance with Part T of the National Building Regulations (SANS 10400) and the Fire Brigade Services Act; structural load inspection and certificate from a registered professional engineer confirming the warehouse floor and racking capacity; and occupational clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, including electrical installation certificate, hazardous chemical agent assessment where relevant, Compensation Fund letter of good standing, and workplace compliance inspection.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); taxpayer portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs and excise division: SARS Customs via SAD500/EDI (https://www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned South African (Pty) Ltd, SARS registration is separate from CIPC incorporation. Steps: (1) incorporate the company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain the CIPC company registration certificate; (2) register the company on SARS eFiling as an income-tax taxpayer, using the CIPC registration details, and obtain a SARS income tax reference number; (3) if the value of taxable supplies exceeds R1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period, submit SARS form VAT101 (Application for Registration as a VAT Vendor), or apply voluntarily once taxable supplies exceed R50,000; (4) if the entity is a non-resident external company, register as an external company with CIPC, register with SARS for income tax on South African-source income, and appoint a local representative taxpayer; (5) for VAT, a non-resident carrying on an enterprise through a representative vendor in South Africa must appoint that representative vendor to act for it; (6) submit the required eFiling records: CIPC registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, director/shareholder identification and passport copies, registered office proof, confirmation of a South African bank account, and signed authority for any appointed representative; (7) SARS will issue the income tax reference number and, where applicable, a separate VAT vendor number through eFiling; the company must maintain a valid Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN for customs clearance, banking, and cross-border transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned South African (Pty) Ltd, SARS registration is separate from CIPC incorporation. Steps: (1) incorporate the company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain the CIPC company registration certificate; (2) register the company on SARS eFiling as an income-tax taxpayer, using the CIPC registration details, and obtain a SARS income tax reference number; (3) if the value of taxable supplies exceeds R1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period, submit SARS form VAT101 (Application for Registration as a VAT Vendor), or apply voluntarily once taxable supplies exceed R50,000; (4) if the entity is a non-resident external company, register as an external company with CIPC, register with SARS for income tax on South African-source income, and appoint a local representative taxpayer; (5) for VAT, a non-resident carrying on an enterprise through a representative vendor in South Africa must appoint that representative vendor to act for it; (6) submit the required eFiling records: CIPC registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, director/shareholder identification and passport copies, registered office proof, confirmation of a South African bank account, and signed authority for any appointed representative; (7) SARS will issue the income tax reference number and, where applicable, a separate VAT vendor number through eFiling; the company must maintain a valid Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN for customs clearance, banking, and cross-border transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election and no general pass-through election for a (Pty) Ltd; the company is automatically a separate entity-level taxpayer. Elections and statutory options relevant to a cross-border express courier operator include: (i) voluntary VAT registration under s.23 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991; (ii) VAT group registration under s.50 VAT Act for connected companies to be treated as a single vendor; (iii) micro business turnover tax election for qualifying companies with annual turnover not exceeding R1,000,000; (iv) small business corporation concessional income-tax rates, where company qualifications are satisfied; (v) CFC high-tax exemption under s.9D(2A) of the Income Tax Act; and (vi) hybrid mismatch rules, which apply automatically under the statutory anti-hybrid provisions and are not subject to elective disapplication.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration in South African Rand (ZAR) when the value of taxable supplies exceeds R1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months; voluntary registration is available once taxable supplies exceed R50,000. A non-resident supplier carrying on an enterprise in South Africa must register and may be required to appoint a South African representative vendor.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered South African VAT vendor may zero-rate a supply of movable goods exported from South Africa under s.11(1)(a) VAT Act, and may zero-rate international transport, freight forwarding, and courier services supplied in respect of exported goods or supplied to a non-resident, provided the goods are physically removed from South Africa under a valid customs declaration and the supplier obtains and retains acceptable proof of export. The onus is on the vendor to prove entitlement. For cross-border express courier operations, the service must be separate from any domestic leg and the zero-rated consideration must be identifiable on the tax invoice. Input VAT attributable to the zero-rated supply may be deducted, and any excess credit is refundable through the VAT201 refund system. If export proof is not retained or the goods are not removed as recorded, the supply is reclassified as standard-rated.

  • SARS Customs export declaration (SAD500 Bill of Entry) with customs acknowledgment or release reference
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing the physical departure of the goods from South Africa
  • Commercial invoice and VAT tax invoice issued under s.20 of the Value-Added Tax Act
  • Proof of export such as signed proof of delivery, route documents, foreign customs import evidence, or carrier release documents
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT credit evidence showing that export proceeds were received in South Africa

South Africa does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, mandatory government pre-clearance, or digital stamping of ordinary VAT invoices. SARS eFiling is the mandatory channel for filing VAT returns, and export declarations are filed digitally as SAD500 Bills of Entry through SARS Customs EDI or Remote Processing. VAT tax invoices may be issued electronically or on paper under s.20 VAT Act, provided they include all prescribed particulars and are issued within 21 days of the supply. The SAD500 customs release, not a digital stamp, is the operative export clearance record.

SARS audits export zero-rating through VAT refund reviews and the Customs Export Verification unit. Where a vendor cannot substantiate the actual export, output VAT is retrospectively clawed back to the original VAT return period, with statutory interest and penalties. The taxpayer bears the burden of proof under s.102 of the Tax Administration Act. Understatement penalties range from 20% to 200% of the assessed shortfall depending on the conduct involved, and fraudulent zero-rating may result in criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment of up to 10 years under the Tax Administration Act and the Customs and Excise Act. No prescription period applies to additional assessments when fraud or misrepresentation is proven; otherwise SARS generally has a three-year prescription window. Export documentation and all tax records must be retained for at least five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa is an IMF Article VIII current-account convertible regime. Current-account transactions supporting the courier business - cross-border trade settlement, carriage and freight fees, airway bill payments, agent commissions, and remittance of dividends out of genuinely realised profits - are freely convertible through an Authorised Dealer against documentary proof and SARB Balance of Payments (BOP) reporting. Capital-account flows require FinSurv authorisation unless covered by a standing general allowance; a 100% foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd is permitted, but inward equity and shareholder loans must be declared through the Authorised Dealer under the prescribed FDI notification procedure and booked under the 'N' non-resident reserve code. Profit repatriation hurdles are primarily documentary: a current SARS Tax Clearance Status/TCC is required for dividend and interest remittances, dividends are subject to 20% dividends tax at source (with treaty-based exemptions available on submission of declaration and undertaking), and the company must confirm that dividends are paid from available distributable profits audited in accordance with the Companies Act, No. 71 of 2008.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export earnings, including foreign-currency revenues for cross-border express courier services, must be fully repatriated and received by an Authorised Dealer in South Africa within 180 days of the date of export or service rendition (SAD500 or commercial invoice date). There is no mandatory minimum conversion ratio into ZAR; the exporter may retain 100% of foreign receipts in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with an Authorised Dealer or convert at the onshore market rate provided the funds are deployed for transactional business expenses. Compliance is monitored through the FinSurv EMCS and monthly BOP declarations; late receipt, shortfall, or unauthorised use of offshore funds must be reported via the Authorised Dealer and may trigger VAT input disallowance, penal interest, and withdrawal of the exporter's forex accommodation.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933, as amended, and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; day-to-day administration delegated to Authorised Dealers (Category I commercial banks) through the Electronic Monitoring and Control System (EMCS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium. Under the National Payment System Act, No. 78 of 1998, SARB retains exclusive authorisation over e-money issuance and settlement memberships; non-resident EMIs such as Airwallex, Wise, and Revolut Business cannot directly issue ZAR e-money, access the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system, or clear ACH Direct batches. Their viable role is limited to offshore collection of inbound courier fees in USD/EUR/GBP and funding of outbound partner payments outside South Africa, provided all ZAR legs settle through a licensed local bank. Any offshore balance remains subject to the 180-day repatriation rule; therefore an EMI cannot replace a Category I Authorised Dealer as the entity's principal settlement bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders inject equity or shareholder loans via SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) into the entity's Capital Escrow Account at an Authorised Dealer, using the SARB BOP purpose-of-payment code applicable to direct investment (FDI) or intercompany lending as prescribed by the Authorised Dealer's system. The remitter must cite the company's registered name and VAT registration; the Authorised Dealer completes the inward FDI declaration and assigns the 'N' non-resident reserve code within 30 days. Funds may then be converted to ZAR at the onshore market rate or credited to an FCA for operating needs; subsequent disbursement requires supporting invoices, board approval minutes, and current VAT/PAYE compliance status. For capital raising exceeding general allowance limits, the FinSurv prior approval letter must be presented before the inward remittance is credited to the account.Settlement to foreign partner couriers, airlines, and last-mile agents is executed by SWIFT MT103 from the ZAR or FCA leg with BOP purpose codes applicable to freight and logistics services. High-value seasonal charter or capitalised freight purchases are supported by Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) with documents against payment. Domestic subcontractors and suppliers are settled through the Automated Clearing Bureau (ACB) ACH Direct batch via SAMOS; Card POS receipts are aggregated by the acquiring bank through BankservAfrica and settled in ZAR T+1. All foreign disbursements require an Authorised Dealer-approved underlying invoice, exchange-control declaration for amounts exceeding applicable thresholds, and current SARS tax clearance to avoid payment delays.Onshore deliverable USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR, and GBP/ZAR outright forwards transacted with an Authorised Dealer under an ISDA or local master agreement, strictly limited to the courier's identified transactional trade cycle covering fuel, aircraft charters, and cross-border agent settlements.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) USD/ZAR executed offshore through a non-resident counterparty, available only where a deliverable onshore hedge is contractually or operationally impossible; a resident (Pty) Ltd is restricted by FinSurv from using NDFs without a hard underlying exposure, and any unwind must be evidenced to the Authorised Dealer.USD/ZAR or EUR/ZAR cross-currency swap with matched principal exchange concluded through the Authorised Dealer for duration-alignment of foreign shareholder loans or bridge financing, subject to FinSurv loan approvals and annual rollover certification.
