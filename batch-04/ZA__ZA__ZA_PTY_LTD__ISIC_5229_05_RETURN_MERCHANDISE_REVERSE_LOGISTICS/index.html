Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), national tax and customs authority; eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; SARS Customs division receives SAD 500 export declarations via the electronic Customs lodgement system.

E-government filing portal

Upon CIPC incorporation, a domestic (Pty) Ltd is auto-registered for income tax with SARS and issued a 10-digit income tax reference number via eFiling; any deficiency is rectified by filing the SARS IT77 company registration form. A foreign company must first register as an external company with CIPC (s 23, Companies Act 71 of 2008), then apply for a SARS income tax reference number via form IT77 and separately apply for VAT vendor registration via form VAT101 (ss 23-24 VAT Act 89 of 1991). SARS requires certified proof of incorporation, physical business address, company resolution and resident director details, and issues a distinct VAT vendor number upon approval. Where the foreign applicant has no South African fixed place of business, a SARS-appointed representative taxpayer is required under s 157 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident private company (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person subject to South African normal tax at a headline rate of 27% on worldwide income for tax years ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through or flow-through treatment applies. Non-resident shareholders remain exposed to SA-source withholding taxes on dividends and interest, but the operating entity is taxed at entity level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident private company (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person subject to South African normal tax at a headline rate of 27% on worldwide income for tax years ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through or flow-through treatment applies. Non-resident shareholders remain exposed to SA-source withholding taxes on dividends and interest, but the operating entity is taxed at entity level. If the operating entity were a foreign-incorporated non-resident company, only its SA-source income would be taxable (Territorial Scope).

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification election exists; classification is statutory under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 read with the Companies Act. Available elections relevant to a logistics/trading (Pty) Ltd include: (i) headquarter company election (s 9I ITA) exempting foreign dividends, interest, royalties and CGT gains subject to 80% foreign-service income and expenditure thresholds; (ii) CFC high-tax exemption election (s 9D(9A) ITA) for outbound CFC participation; (iii) VAT group registration election (s 50 VAT Act) enabling two or more connected resident companies to be treated as a single vendor; (iv) corporate reorganisation roll-over elections (ss 42-45 ITA) for intra-group asset transfers; and (v) special-resolution public company election affecting interest deductibility under s 23M. Hybrid mismatch rules (ss 23M-23N and related) apply mandatorily and are not elective, denying deductions or recharacterising income where payments are made to hybrid entities or under hybrid financial instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period (s 23(1) VAT Act); voluntary registration permitted when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12 months (s 23(3)). The threshold applies irrespective of residence, provided the person carries on an 'enterprise' in South Africa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods export (s 11(1)(a) VAT Act): the vendor supplies movable goods to a non-resident, receives payment, and the goods are exported from South Africa within 90 days of payment (or longer as SARS permits). Services export (s 11(2)(l)): services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa when the services are rendered, the services are not directly connected with movable or immovable property located in South Africa, and the services are not used or consumed in South Africa. Caution for this reverse-logistics scope: sorting, grading and handling of returned goods physically located in South Africa constitutes a supply 'directly connected with movable property in South Africa' and is standard-rated at 15%. Zero-rating is claimable only where the export of the returned goods itself qualifies under s 11(1)(a) and the grading services are integral to that export supply, or where the services relate to goods located outside South Africa. SARS applies a strict documentary-supremacy test per its VAT Guide on Exported Services (VAT 411).

  • SAD 500 (Single Administrative Document) customs export declaration lodged electronically with SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from South Africa
  • Full tax invoice marked 'zero-rated' identifying the vendor, non-resident customer, goods description, and invoice date
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT/bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency payment from the non-resident customer
  • Signed supply contract / purchase order and Return Merchandise Authorisation (RMA) records confirming non-resident customer status and reverse-logistics service scope
  • Warehouse and quality-grading records (goods-in/out, inspection reports, disposal certificates) substantiating the export or the services rendered

South Africa has not yet implemented a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or API-based tax document authentication regime. SARS is progressively introducing voluntary e-invoicing under a national Peppol-aligned e-Invoicing framework (phased voluntary rollout from 2023-2025). Until mandated, both paper and electronic tax invoices are valid. Registered vendors must issue a full tax invoice with the mandatory descriptors under s 20 VAT Act within 21 days of supply or delivery, issue credit/debit notes within 21 days (s 21), and retain all records for five years (s 55 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011).

SARS verifies export zero-rating and refund claims on a documentary basis before releasing VAT refunds; refunds may be withheld or set off under s 44 VAT Act pending audit completion. Where goods are not exported within 90 days or the documentary bundle is incomplete, zero-rating collapses and output VAT at 15% is assessed retrospectively on the full consideration plus statutory interest under s 187 TAA. Misstatements trigger the understatement penalty regime (ss 222-223 TAA): 10% of the shortfall for the first substantial understatement, escalating to 25%, 50% and up to 100% where there are no reasonable grounds, and up to 200% for intentional tax evasion. Criminal sanctions under ss 234-235 TAA provide fines of up to ZAR 50,000 and/or imprisonment of up to five years for deliberately false statements or fraudulent zero-rating claims; parallel liability may arise under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 for false export declarations. SARS may reopen assessments within five years, or without time limit in cases of fraud (s 99 TAA), resulting in retrospective clawback of refunded VAT, penalties, interest and enhanced AML/reputational risk under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is a freely floating, fully convertible currency for current account transactions, which are liberalised and require no SARB approval subject to underlying trade documentation and the reporting obligations imposed on Authorised Dealers. Capital account transactions are subject to a dual-track regime: inward foreign direct investment into the Republic is unrestricted for most sectors and requires only notification via the Electronic Exchange Control Reporting (EER) system through an Authorised Dealer, whereas outward investments and certain inward loans above ZAR 100 million per lender require prior FinSurv approval. Profit remittances are permitted without exchange-control approval once the dividend is validly declared under the Companies Act 71 of 2008, but the remitting institution must verify SARS Tax Directives, corporate compliance standing, and the shareholder register; statutory Dividends Withholding Tax of 20% applies unless reduced by an applicable double-taxation treaty. The practical hurdles to profit repatriation are not exchange-control blocking but documentary: board resolution, solvency and liquidity test, audited financial statements, and proof of beneficial ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exported from South Africa must be fully paid for within 180 days of the date of shipment; an extension up to 365 days may be granted by an Authorised Dealer on application to FinSurv where trade cycles require it. All export receipts must be repatriated to South Africa through an Authorised Dealer and credited to an exporter's domestic ZAR account unless the exporter operates an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA). In the absence of an approved FCA, the mandatory conversion ratio into ZAR is effectively 100% of the invoiced value; where an FCA is held, the proceeds may be retained in the FCA and deployed for approved foreign expenditure, meaning the conversion ratio is nil for that portion. VAT and customs documentation, including the SARS Customs SAD500 bill of entry and commercial invoices, must be retained to evidence the underlying trade and to satisfy the EER reporting requirements for each export collection.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), together with the National Treasury, under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. South Africa does not have a statutory 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licence category; non-bank cross-border payment providers must instead operate as Financial Services Providers (FSPs) under the FAIS Act or as payment facilitators under the National Payment System Act (NPS Act) through a sponsoring/partner bank. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex and Wise may lawfully support the ZAR-PTY LTD's cross-border receipts and disbursements by pairing with licensed South African banking partners, but exchange-control obligations — EER reporting, Authorised Dealer verification, and SARB/FinSurv custody of foreign-currency flows — cannot be discharged by the EMI itself and must remain with a South African Authorised Dealer. The entity may therefore use Airwallex as a rapid collection/payout rail for low-value reverse-logistics settlements, but the statutory ledger and capital escrow functions should be maintained at a Tier 1 commercial bank to guarantee proper FICA, EER, and SARS compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from non-resident shareholders must be received via SWIFT into the company's account at an Authorised Dealer, referencing the share-subscription purpose code (e.g., inward foreign direct investment / equity capital) and accompanied by a board resolution, updated shareholder register, share certificates, and a statement of inward investment. No prior FinSurv approval is required for share capital, but the Authorised Dealer must capture and transmit the transaction in the Electronic Exchange Control Reporting (EER) system and retain FICA-compliant evidence of the payee's identity and underlying documentation. For shareholder loans, any single inward loan exceeding ZAR 100 million from a non-resident lender requires prior FinSurv approval, and all related interest and capital repayments must be routed through the same Authorised Dealer with a SARS tax compliance directive where applicable. The company should instruct senders to avoid payment-context ambiguities by using the issuing bank's ISO 20022 purpose code and including the company's name and reference in both the SWIFT field 50 and field 59.Trade settlements to foreign suppliers and logistics counterparties are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 single-credit transfers routed via an Authorised Dealer with full purpose-of-payment annotation, or through commercial documentary Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued via MT700 where the supplier requires bank-guaranteed payment against shipping-document inspection. Domestic refunds and merchant payouts in ZAR will be cleared through the Payments Association of South Africa (PASA) automated clearing house rails, namely the ACB credit services and the Spark (FIS) real-time payment network, with same-day settlement available under the Rapid Payments Programme; for larger ZAR liquidity movements, the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) interbank real-time gross settlement system provides final settlement in central-bank money.
