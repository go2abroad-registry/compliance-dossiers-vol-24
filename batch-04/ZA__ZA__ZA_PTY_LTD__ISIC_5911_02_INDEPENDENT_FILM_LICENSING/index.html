Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd companies must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). For income tax, register for a SARS income tax reference number via eFiling or a SARS branch using the CIPC registration certificate, signed registration forms, and identity documents of directors. VAT registration is a separate application using SARS form VAT 101 (via eFiling or branch) once taxable turnover exceeds the compulsory threshold; a VAT vendor number is then issued and a VAT registration certificate is provided.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election. A (Pty) Ltd is conclusively a taxpayer subject to South African corporate income tax. Foreign-incorporated entities can become tax residents if place of effective management is in South Africa, but this is not an elective regime. Hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act apply to deny benefits from certain arrangements. VAT grouping under the VAT Act is available as a limited statutory election for cash/tax invoice treatment, and s8D–s8L rules operate to counteract cross-border mismatch outcomes.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (1) goods physically exported from South Africa within the statutory export period to non-resident purchasers, and (2) services supplied to non-resident persons where the services are not consumed by any resident in South Africa and the rights/output are used wholly outside South Africa. For independent film licensing, a South African licensor may zero-rate royalties and licence fees for the supply of copyright/usage rights to a non-resident distributor if the licensing agreement demonstrates the exploitation outside South Africa, and adequate commercial records support the foreign destination. The supplier must retain evidence of the export, non-resident status of the recipient, and actual receipt of foreign consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 for goods) or SARS-recognised supporting evidence for services, such as VAT 265 or an interface export slip
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / electronic customs release (for goods); for licensing services: executed Licence Agreement and exploitation evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident recipient

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or tax-authority API stamping regime is in force. Taxpayers must issue valid tax invoices in either paper or electronic form; electronic invoicing is permitted provided the invoice meets SARS VAT invoice requirements and is reliably stored/retrievable. VAT returns and payments are filed periodically through SARS eFiling. SARS is exploring enhanced digital invoice reporting, but it has not been legislated as a real-time clearance requirement.

SARS may disallow zero-rating and assess VAT at 15% on the full supply value if documentary proof is inadequate or the supply is later found to be consumed in South Africa. Underdeclaration penalties typically range from 10% to 20% of the tax evaded, rising to 150%–200% for intentional or fraudulent misconduct. Interest runs from the original due date. In addition to tax, civil and criminal sanctions apply under the Tax Administration Act, including fines and imprisonment for knowingly issuing false export/invalid zero-rate claims. Retrospective clawbacks can extend to prior tax periods and trigger full audit reviews of the taxpayer's indirect and direct tax positions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand is broadly convertible on the current account; current-account payments such as royalties, film licensing fees, dividends, interest and service payments are permitted through an authorised dealer without SARB approval, subject to submission of commercial documentation and applicable tax clearances. Capital-account transactions are not fully liberalised: inward FDI and non-resident shareholding are generally permitted and must be reported/monitored through an authorised dealer, while outward capital transfers and certain derivative/loan exposures require exchange control approval or compliance with the SARB capital flow management framework. Dividend and profit remittances to non-residents are freely remittable after the entity proves available earnings, produces a board resolution, and attests tax compliance; branch-profit remittances require an approved tax clearance and are subject to South African withholding taxes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SARB Exchange Control Regulations (Regulation 11 read with the Currency and Exchanges Manual), proceeds from exports, including intangible royalty/licensing proceeds earned by a South African film licensing company, must be repatriated and received through an authorised dealer in South Africa within 180 days (or SARB-approved longer period) from date of shipment or completion of the licensed services. On receipt, the proceeds must be surrendered/converted to ZAR through the authorised dealer within 30 days. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the export proceeds unless FinSurv has granted an approval to retain foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA); any FCA retention is a risk-mitigation exception, not an expatriation of proceeds.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, with banking supervision by the Prudential Authority and Registrar of Banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the principal treasury clearing rail. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) do not hold an SARB authorised-dealer licence, are not banks under the Banks Act, 1990, and are not registered as payment system operators under the National Payment System Act, 1998. They cannot issue SWIFT MT103/MT202 payment instructions on the company's behalf for regulated export proceeds or obtain SAMOS ZAR clearing rights. An offshore EMI may at most serve as a supporting disbursement/collection node for foreign-currency receipts, but all film licensing proceeds must flow to a domestic authorised dealer and be surrendered under the exchange-control timeline. Maintaining licensing proceeds offshore in an unlicensed EMI account would constitute a breach of the 100% repatriation/surrender obligation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for share capital/loan funding from non-resident shareholders requires a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 if bank-to-bank) directed to the SA (Pty) Ltd's authorised-dealer account. The local bank will apply the applicable Balance of Payments/purpose-code classification (e.g. inward direct investment/equity capital) and the entity must provide the corporate authorisation (board resolution), share certificate/loan agreement, CIPC share register and beneficial ownership data. On the South African leg, the dealer will convert foreign currency to ZAR at the prevailing market rate and, where applicable, issue an inward declaration/acknowledgment. Any delayed or split funding must be documented to avoid misstatement of capital duties and to support future dividend/profit remittances.Outbound film-licensing/trade payments should be initiated through domestic authorised-dealer channels: SWIFT MT103 for conventional royalty/distribution fees and LC payments for physical-media or film-delivery instruments such as a Commercial Letter of Credit. The company's payment instructions must reference the relevant contract/invoice, be supported by a valid underlying economic transaction, and be submitted with SARS tax clearance/good-standing and, if needed, proof that the inward amount has been surrendered/converted.
