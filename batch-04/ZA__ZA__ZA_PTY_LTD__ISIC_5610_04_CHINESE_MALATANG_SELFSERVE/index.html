Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A resident (Pty) Ltd is subject to national income tax at the entity level at 27% (from 2023) on worldwide income, with an 80% capital-gain inclusion rate, 20% dividends withholding tax, and no pass-through regime. A qualifying entity with gross income up to ZAR 20 million may access Small Business Corporation progressive rates (0%/7%/21%). Controlled foreign company passive income may be imputed to the resident shareholder under s 9D.

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election; classification is statutory. Available elections: (i) Small Business Corporation status under s 12E claimed on assessment; (ii) Headquarter Company election under s 9I for companies held at least 80% by non-residents (tax-neutral foreign dividends, interest, royalties and CFC inclusions); (iii) VAT grouping election for associated companies under s 50 VAT Act; (iv) CFC elections under s 9D, including the election to treat a CFC as resident in limited instances; (v) s 23N election allocating the interest-limitation formula in cross-border financing. Hybrid mismatch rules (ss 8F, 8FA, 23N, 25D) are mandatory, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected in the next 12 months (s 23(1)). Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12 months (s 24). Foreign suppliers of electronic services register at a ZAR 50,000 annual threshold (s 23A).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: s 11(1)(a) VAT Act where goods are consigned to a non-resident and physically removed from South Africa within 90 days of supply. Services: s 11(2)(l) where services are supplied to a non-resident recipient and are not directly connected with land or movable property in South Africa; franchise and royalty fees from non-resident master franchisees are zero-rated when the licensor's services are physically performed outside South Africa or the benefit is consumed offshore. The vendor bears the burden of proof and must retain prescribed documentary evidence within the statutory period under s 11(3); input VAT on procurement may be refunded against export supplies on the VAT return.

  • SARS SAD500 Customs Export Declaration (bill of entry)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical removal from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming receipt of export or franchise proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated VAT invoice (s 20 VAT Act compliant)
  • Foreign master-franchise or royalty agreement (for services zero-rating)
  • Bank statement and SARB exchange-control records
  • Packing list, consignment note and weight certificate (where applicable)

SARS phased e-invoicing mandate (Draft Rules 2024): voluntary from 1 July 2025; mandatory for VAT vendors with turnover above ZAR 10 million from 1 September 2025; mandatory for all other VAT vendors from 1 September 2026. Invoices must be issued in the SARS-approved structured XML format (UBL 2.1-aligned) and transmitted digitally via the SARS eInvoicing gateway or a certified service provider, with digital signature and unique invoice identifier; there is no pre-issuance fiscal clearance approval, but s 20 VAT Act tax-invoice and s 55 record-keeping rules apply.

SARS audits zero-rating retrospectively: failure to obtain export proof or physical removal within 90 days triggers output VAT clawback under s 16(2)(e) VAT Act with interest, plus additional tax under s 60 VAT Act of up to 200% of the tax evaded. The Tax Administration Act s 223 understatement penalties apply at 10% (substantial understatement), 25% (lack of reasonable care), 50% (no reasonable grounds) and 100% (intentional tax evasion), with interest under s 187. Non-repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days leads to SARB exchange-control enquiries, suspension of tax clearance and potential criminal exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is freely tradable offshore but remains subject to a regulated exchange-control framework administered by FinSurv and the Authorised Dealers. Current-account transactions, including settlement of genuine trade import invoices, service fees, after-tax dividends to non-resident shareholders, and approved royalty/licence fees, are permitted without individual FinSurv approval provided supporting documentation is presented and the transaction is recorded through an Authorised Dealer for BOP purposes. Capital-account transactions are liberalised for inward foreign direct investment: a non-resident shareholder may subscribe for shares in a South African Pty Ltd without prior SARB approval, but the funds must enter through an Authorised Dealer as an inward transfer, and the foreign shareholder must not be part of a prohibited loop structure. Profit-remittance hurdles are administrative rather than prohibitive: a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN is required for any individual remittance exceeding ZAR 10 million, dividends must be declared in accordance with the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and paid timeously after finalisation of the annual financial statements, and the original share capital must have been duly recorded on the BOP reporting system. The master franchise entity in scope is primarily a ZAR-generating domestic business with optional inbound USD/CNY capital and outbound royalty/trade flows; no convertibility blockage is present for lawful commercial flows, but non-resident financing routed through opaque conduits will attract enhanced domestic and correspondent-bank scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers (Section B) and the FinSurv directives applicable from 2021, exporters must repatriate foreign-currency export proceeds to South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment; extensions to 365 days require Authorised Dealer approval, and longer periods require FinSurv consent. The statutory mandatory conversion or surrender requirement was abolished in January 2021, so the mandatory conversion ratio is nil (0%) and exporters may elect to retain proceeds in approved Foreign Currency Accounts or convert at commercial market rates. Exporters must nevertheless present proof of receipt, usually the SWIFT MT103 credit advice matching the export invoice, the SARS Customs Bill of Entry, the underlying sale contract, and a reconciliation of all receipts; failure to repatriate within the statutory window is a criminal exchange-control offence. For export of services, such as sub-franchise advisory or supply-chain management fees invoiced to non-resident franchisees, payment must be received within 180 days of invoice or completion of the service, with the same repatriation obligation. For the inbound direction, payments to a foreign master licensor of franchise royalties or licence fees require a recognised exchange-control-approved licence agreement and full withholding-tax compliance before the outbound wire is approved.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations of 1961, and the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA). Day-to-day administration is delegated to Category I Authorised Dealers, being the licensed South African commercial banks, which execute all inward and outward foreign-exchange transactions and balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting.
