Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up or issued capital applies to a Proprietary Limited Company. Shares have no par value; the subscriber must deliver the agreed consideration under section 38/40 of the Companies Act, and no escrow account or statutory deposit is prescribed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local Fire and Rescue Service, structural load inspection by a registered professional engineer, approved building plans, and workplace clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, including an electrical certificate of compliance for leased premises, are required before occupying a warehouse, showroom, or office facility.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rating applies under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act in respect of supplies of movable goods that are supplied in the course of an export consignment where the goods are delivered to an address in a foreign export country or are removed from South Africa under an approved customs customs manifest, and the supplier retains the prescribed documentary proof. For services: zero-rating applies under section 11(2)(l) and 11(2)(m) where the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient, the recipient is resident outside South Africa, and the services are not in respect of land or movable goods situated in South Africa at the time of supply, and certain labour or intellectual property conditions are satisfied.

SARS has implemented an electronic e-invoicing framework (SAPICS-compliant tax invoice standards) but real-time fiscal clearance, API-based digital stamping, and block-chain anchored clearance are not currently mandatory for all VAT vendors. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current-account purposes (merchandise trade, services, dividends, interest) subject to authorised-dealer documentary verification; the capital account remains administratively controlled, meaning every cross-border capital movement must be executed through a SARB-licensed authorised dealer and non-standard structures require FinSurv pre-approval. Profit remittance by the foreign investor of ZA_PTY_LTD is treated as a current-account payment and may be paid abroad without FinSurv pre-approval once the following conditions are satisfied: (i) the 20% Dividends Tax on the gross dividend has been withheld and remitted to the South African Revenue Service (SARS); (ii) the board resolution satisfies the Companies Act 71 of 2008 solvency-and-liquidity test; (iii) the company is in good standing at the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); and (iv) the bank has obtained a SARS tax-compliance acknowledgement plus the original inward foreign-investment declaration evidencing the non-resident shareholding. Practical hurdles: mandatory FX-dealer intermediation for every conversion; bank rejection of transactions appearing to constitute round-tripping, speculative ZAR carry-trade or unverified beneficial-ownership structures; and the administrative burden of maintaining a complete foreign-liability register. Overall assessment: current account — convertible as of right with documentary compliance; capital account — convertible with moderate administrative friction and a clean audit trail.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: the full export value of goods must be received in the Republic within 180 days from the date of shipment under Regulation 7 of the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 and the FinSurv Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers (Export Chapter); any extension requires prior authorisation from the authorised dealer or FinSurv. Mandatory conversion: all foreign currency received for exports must be surrendered to an authorised dealer (exchanged into ZAR) unless the exporter has obtained an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA)/retention facility; no general percentage retention allowance applies today, so the de facto requirement is 100% conversion on repatriation, with an FCA available only for approved liquidity-management purposes. Supporting documentation: SARS customs bill of entry (SAD500), commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, proof of payment, and bank settlement records must be lodged with the authorised dealer. Failure to receive proceeds within the 180-day window exposes the company to SARS customs penalties, FinSurv administrative sanctions, potential blacklisting of future export facilities, and compulsory write-back of the unrecovered amount.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which exercises delegated powers of the Minister of Finance under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961. Commercial banks operate as SARB-licensed authorised dealers for day-to-day exchange-control approvals; FinSurv retains jurisdiction over capital-account transactions requiring prior approval. The National Payment System Department of SARB additionally supervises clearing and settlement infrastructure under the National Payment System Act, 1998 (Act No. 78 of 1998), while the Prudential Authority and the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) regulate banking conduct and non-bank payment service providers respectively.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per year for the requirement to prepare a Local File; ZAR 250 million for the Master File. For a company with annual turnover below ZAR 100 million and cross-border related-party transactions below ZAR 100 million, only limited contemporaneous documentation is required. SARS requires the documentation to be contemporaneous and submitted within 21 days of a request. Section 31 of the Income Tax Act empowers SARS to adjust taxable income where connected-person transacting is not at arm's length. Secondary adjustment for transfer pricing recharacterization also applies. The full Local File and Master File forms (SARS TP-1 and TP-2) are aligned with OECD BEPS Action 13 recommendations.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE or Mauritius. However, the Income Tax Act incorporates the 'substance over form' doctrine, the general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) under section 80A-L, controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution rules under section 9D, and the headquarter company regime under section 9I as economic substance tests. A company must demonstrate commercial substance, staff, operational premises, and business risks when claiming treaty benefits or headquarter company relief. SARS requires economic substance for treaty shopping protection: a conduit company with no real business purpose can be disregarded under the limitation of benefits provisions now included in the MLI and in South Africa's updated DTAs. Cross-border e-commerce retail of oversized furniture with third-party logistics providers must prove that warehousing, order processing, logistics management, and procurement functions are actually performed in South Africa to avoid CFC attribution and GAAR challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act section 234 and the Income Tax Act: imprisonment up to 5 years, and a fine of up to ZAR 30,000 per charge. SARS may impose a 200% understatement penalty under section 223, plus daily interest at the prescribed rate. Assets and bank accounts may be seized through civil judgment. In addition, a 20% VAT penalty and additional VAT and penalties apply under the Value-Added Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and members of the board are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts under section 18 of the Tax Administration Act if the company failed to pay employee taxes (PAYE), VAT, or withholding taxes. For general corporate income tax debts, the personal liability regime requires proof that the director knowingly assisted in the evasion, was negligent, or authorized the conduct, allowing SARS to pierce the corporate veil under common law and section 20 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Severe cases trigger orders for civil restitution, freezing of personal assets, travel bans under the Prevention of Corruption Act and the Extradition Act, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rules under sections 80A-L allow SARS to disregard the transaction, recharacterize the structure, deny treaty benefits, and impose a 200% understatement penalty. The CFC rules under section 9D attribute undistributed profits to South African residents. The courts have confirmed in cases such as STC v Colonial and SARS v NWK Ltd (though NWK was later qualified by SARS) that transactions without commercial substance are invalid. Administrative penalties of up to ZAR 5 million may apply for failure to file TP documentation. SARS may also invoke the limitation of benefits clause and deny all treaty relief. The Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) exposes the entity to monetary penalties for inaccurate beneficial ownership records.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the tax liability if the company was used as a facade or vehicle for the evasion or for improper profit diversion. Under the Companies Act, a director who knowingly carries on business recklessly, with gross negligence, or for a fraudulent purpose is liable for the company's debts. Personal restraint orders, asset seizure orders, and travel bans may be imposed; directors may additionally be banned from serving as directors for up to 10 years under section 162 of the Companies Act.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income to reflect an arm's length price under section 31, leading to additional tax of up to 27% (or 28% for years before FY2023) plus interest. An understatement penalty under section 223 of the Tax Administration Act of 50% to 200% applies for the substantial understatement. Where the transaction is found to have a mainly tax avoidance purpose, the GAAR penalties apply. Secondary transfer pricing adjustment may result in a deemed loan and dividend withholding tax (DWT) at 20% on the notional dividend where the secondary adjustment is treated as a distribution. Criminal prosecution applies where false documentation is submitted, with penalties of up to ZAR 25,000 or imprisonment of up to 3 years per false statement.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign off on falsified transfer pricing file, or who cause the company to provide false information to SARS, incur personal liability under section 234 and section 235 of the Tax Administration Act. They are subject to civil restitution for taxes owed, punitive damages, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years. Where the transfer pricing manipulation forms part of money laundering, the Prevention of Organised Crime Act allows SARS and the National Prosecuting Authority to seize personal assets and freeze bank accounts.
Money Laundering / AML Infraction

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, as amended by the General Laws Amendment Act 2022, prescribes administrative penalties up to ZAR 50 million for accountable institutions. Criminal sanctions include fines up to ZAR 100 million and imprisonment up to 30 years. The Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998 provides for asset forfeiture and proceeds of crime recovery without conviction. Contempt due to failure to file beneficial ownership returns under the Companies Act can result in a fine of up to ZAR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 3 months.

Director personal liability: Directors constitute part of management; a director who knowingly conducts business with proceeds of unlawful activity commits a statutory offence and is liable to imprisonment up to 15 years. Directors may face personal asset forfeiture, curatorship orders over their property, and travel bans of indefinite duration. Directors who knowingly failed to prevent money laundering while holding supervisory responsibility are personally liable under section 42 of FICA.
Failure to Withhold Dividend or Non-Resident Taxes

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is jointly and severally liable for the tax. The penalty is 20% (DWT) of the dividend amount plus interest from the date the dividend was paid. For royalties or interest, the tax authority imposes the 15% tax plus understatement penalties up to 200% and interest at the prescribed rate. Criminal prosecution may follow under section 235 of the Tax Administration Act for deliberate failure, leading to imprisonment of up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise a dividend declaration knowing that withholding tax has not been paid and who are personally vested with the responsibility to make payments on behalf of the company are jointly and severally liable. SARS may assess directors directly under section 18 of the Tax Administration Act; the liability extends to unpaid withholding taxes, interest, and penalties. Directors' personal assets may be attached under a tax judgment; travel bans may be issued where outstanding tax exceeds ZAR 50,000.
