Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — national tax, customs and excise authority; online portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs declarations administered through SARS Customs and Excise using the Automated Customs System and electronic SAD500 lodgement.

E-government filing portal

Domestic (Pty) Ltd: (1) obtain CIPC registration number under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; (2) register for income tax via SARS eFiling using the CIPC number and company particulars to obtain the income-tax reference number; (3) apply for VAT registration on Form VAT101 once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months (compulsory) or voluntarily from ZAR 50,000; (4) apply for the customs-broker licence under s 64J of the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 and enrol as an electronic data interchange (EDI) user with SARS Customs; (5) appoint a public officer (and, for offshore-controlled entities, a resident representative) under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the equity; foreign-incorporated equivalents register through the same eFiling channel using foreign registration particulars and a South African representative address.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the 27% corporate rate (income years ending on or after 31 March 2023); no pass-through; dividends distributed are subject to 20% dividends tax; net capital gains are included at the 80% inclusion rate for companies. — Domestic (Pty) Ltd: (1) obtain CIPC registration number under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; (2) register for income tax via SARS eFiling using the CIPC number and company particulars to obtain the income-tax reference number; (3) apply for VAT registration on Form VAT101 once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months (compulsory) or voluntarily from ZAR 50,000; (4) apply for the customs-broker licence under s 64J of the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 and enrol as an electronic data interchange (EDI) user with SARS Customs; (5) appoint a public officer (and, for offshore-controlled entities, a resident representative) under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the equity; foreign-incorporated equivalents register through the same eFiling channel using foreign registration particulars and a South African representative address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the 27% corporate rate (income years ending on or after 31 March 2023); no pass-through; dividends distributed are subject to 20% dividends tax; net capital gains are included at the 80% inclusion rate for companies.

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows the Companies Act form and the statutory residence tests (incorporation or effective management). Available elections of practical relevance: (1) VAT group-of-companies election under s 50(1) VAT Act 89 of 1991 for connected companies to consolidate VAT accounting; (2) headquarter-company designation election under s 9I Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 for qualifying inbound holding operations; (3) micro-business turnover-tax election under the Sixth Schedule (generally unavailable to a licensed customs brokerage); (4) functional-currency election under s 25D for predominantly foreign-currency trade; (5) the OECD-aligned hybrid-mismatch rules (effective 1 January 2024) are mandatory anti-avoidance rules, not elective, with primary-rule ordering at the entity level.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a dual-tier convertibility model: current account transactions are generally unrestricted but must be routed through an Authorised Dealer with reasonable documentary support, while capital account inflows and outflows remain regulated, although inward foreign direct investment into a ZA (Pty) Ltd requires no prior SARB approval if financed by recognised equity or loan capital and reported through the bank's Balance of Payments reporting. Profit remittance: a dividend or branch remittance to a non-resident is exchange-control permissible only if the entity has no unpaid local tax obligations, the dividends tax at 20% (subject to treaty relief) has been accounted for, the board has satisfied the Companies Act 71 of 2008 solvency and liquidity test, and the foreign capital originally received is evidenced by inward transfer documentation or a Foreign Investment Registration certificate issued on disinvestment. Main hurdles are administrative: obtaining SARS tax clearance/PIN, auditing the source of funds, confirming no outstanding exchange control penalties, and refraining from 'round-tripping' local source funds as foreign capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For tangible exports by a resident customs broker's underlying client: SARB Exchange Control Manual requires the full export proceeds to be received within 180 days from the date of shipment, declared on Form SAD500 in the SARS customs export system, and either credited to a domestic account with an Authorised Dealer or retained in a permitted Foreign Currency Account at an Authorised Dealer. There is no fixed official percentage mandatorily convertible into ZAR; the proceeds may be held in foreign currency only where the exporter demonstrates a liquidity need, subject to bank limits and FinSurv oversight. If the proceeds have not been received within 180 days, the exporter must apply to the Authorised Dealer for an extension supported by debt-collection or legal-exemption status. For the customs broker's own commissions and service fees earned cross-border, the rule does not apply as capital surrender, but the broker must ensure that invoiced amounts are repatriated through normal banking channels and reported for income tax.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administers exchange control under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and delegated Authorised Dealer rules. SARS Customs and Excise interacts only on duties and customs compliance; the prudential regulator is SARB's Prudential Authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury account, Medium as an ancillary payment channel. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer and Mercury are not licensed as South African banks under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, cannot accept deposits from the public, and are not Authorised Dealers under SARB exchange control. They may be used only to execute inbound/outbound payment instructions for rapid cross-border clearing, but they cannot finance client fiduciary balances, issue SARB-compliant export-import reporting, or hold the company's segregated operating funds as a qualified account. Any flow between an EMI and a ZA bank must be evidenced by the underlying trade invoice and reported to FinSurv where required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: the non-resident shareholder remits SWIFT MT103 in the original foreign currency to the ZA company's Capital Escrow/Operational Account designated by the Authorised Dealer bank. The bank screens sanctions, records the transaction under SARB FinSurv Balance of Payments reporting with a narrative purpose code describing 'inward equity investment, FDI in a resident private company', and issues an inward transfer confirmation / foreign investment certificate. The AD will convert the foreign currency into ZAR at the ruling spot rate if the company requires ZAR liquidity. The company must allocate the inbound funds exclusively to the declared business purpose, maintain records of the MT103 against the share issue, and register the non-resident shareholder in the CIPC beneficial ownership register to prevent an unlawful disinvestment later.Clearing rails: (1) Domestic SARS customs duties and excise payments are made from the operational account via SARS eFiling or through the bank's automated customs payment channel using the unique customs payment reference; these are ZAR-only and are not subject to FX regulation. (2) Payments to foreign service providers, sub-agents and freight partners are executed by SWIFT MT103 (single-customer credit transfer) or MT202 for subsequent settlement to overseas or SA banking accounts. (3) For higher-value merchandise, the broker may arrange a Commercial Letter of Credit for its client using SWIFT MT700 / MT707 with documents presented through negotiated banks; MT7xx messages require underlying trade documentation and customs proof. (4) In-country clearing of third-party duties uses the Automated Broker Interface ACH, corporate internet banking batch files, and the South African Multiple Settlement Option system, with real-time reconciliation against SARS receipts.Forward exchange contracts (FECs) with an Authorised Dealer, capped to the value and maturity of documented current-account exposures such as fees, commissions and freight liabilities, and not speculative.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in currencies other than ZAR where no liquid onshore forward market exists, executed through an Authorised Dealer and supported by an underlying real commercial exposure.Cross-currency swaps for long-dated foreign-currency loan or working-capital facilities, subject to FinSurv approval where the swap transforms a non-resident loan into a local-currency exposure.FX spot transactions for immediate drawdown and settlement of duties and service obligations.Foreign-currency options only where specifically authorised by the Authorised Dealer's risk committee and FinSurv as a proven hedge of a firm, non-speculative trade exposure; otherwise exchange control compliance requires a plain FEC.
