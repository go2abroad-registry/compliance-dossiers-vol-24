Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), National Head Office, Lehae la SARS, 299 Bronkhorst Street, Brooklyn, Pretoria; taxpayer portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd registers through SARS eFiling or a SARS branch as follows: (1) appoint a public officer ordinarily resident in South Africa per s.246 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; (2) file the consolidated business registration application (RA01) together with the VAT 101 vendor form, attaching the certificate of incorporation, Memorandum of Incorporation, board resolution appointing the public officer, certified powers of attorney, and proof of the registered place of business; (3) SARS issues a 10-digit income tax reference number and a linked VAT vendor number under a single taxpayer profile which also covers PAYE, UIF, SDL, customs, and excise accounts.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a (Pty) Ltd is a South African-resident company taxed at 27% corporate income tax on worldwide income; distributions are subject to 20% dividends tax withheld at shareholder level (s.64D); capital gains are taxed at an effective rate of 21.6% for companies (80% inclusion rate x 27%). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a (Pty) Ltd is a South African-resident company taxed at 27% corporate income tax on worldwide income; distributions are subject to 20% dividends tax withheld at shareholder level (s.64D); capital gains are taxed at an effective rate of 21.6% for companies (80% inclusion rate x 27%). No pass-through treatment is available for this legal form.

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box or elective hybrid classification regime; entity taxation follows legal form under the Companies Act and Income Tax Act. Available elections for the bitumen-trading (Pty) Ltd: (1) small business corporation (SBC) status under s.12E for qualifying resident companies with turnover below ZAR 20 million; (2) micro-business turnover tax election under the Seventh Schedule for turnover not exceeding ZAR 1 million, mutually exclusive with SBC status; (3) functional currency election under s.25D where accounts are prepared in a foreign currency; (4) VAT group registration election under s.50(1) of the VAT Act for companies under common control. Hybrid mismatch rules (s.23N) and earnings-stripping interest limitation (s.23M) apply mandatorily without election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration where taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (s.23(1) VAT Act); voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies per annum subject to SARS discretion and a minimum registration period of 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under s.11(1)(a) of the VAT Act applies to the sale of containerized bitumen and asphalt in drums or bags where the goods are physically exported by the supplier or its agent, leave South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply, and the prescribed export evidence is obtained and retained. Related logistics and trading services to offshore counterparties may qualify for zero-rating under s.11(2)(l) where supplied to a non-resident for consumption outside South Africa and not directly connected to land or movable property physically situated in South Africa at the time of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form SAD500) with SARS Customs release and unique declaration reference evidencing export from a South African seaport
  • Bill of Lading (or multimodal transport document) showing the South African port of loading, foreign port of discharge, vending customer, and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit advice evidencing receipt and conversion of export proceeds, matching SARB BOP&A exchange-control records

South Africa currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping for individual invoices. Electronic filing and payment of VAT 201 returns via SARS eFiling is mandatory for all registered vendors. SARS is progressively implementing a voluntary e-invoicing framework (2023/24 program) with certified service providers and machine-readable tax invoice standards; all tax invoices must satisfy the s.20 particulars and be retained for five years. SARS performs automated cross-matching of invoice data against customs declarations (SAD500), shipping manifests, banking remittances, and port data.

If goods are not exported within the 90-day statutory window or documentary proof of export is deficient, the supply retroactively attracts VAT at 15% on the full consideration under s.11(3) of the VAT Act, with interest at the SARS prescribed rate (s.89(2)/s.89bis) and a potential understatement penalty ranging from 10% to 200% of the shortfall under s.222 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, escalating for gross negligence or intentional tax evasion, and possible criminal prosecution for fraud. SARS Customs and the SARB exchange-control division cross-match SAD500 data, shipping manifests, and bank remittance confirmations; assessments may be raised for up to five years, with no prescription period where fraud or misrepresentation is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is a managed floating currency with full current-account convertibility for trade in goods and services, subject always to Authorised Dealer screening and Form A declaration. Capital-account convertibility is restricted: equity and loan inflows require an inward listing and applicable FinSurv approvals. Non-resident shareholders may remit ordinary dividends and liquidating distributions after the underlying inward funding evidence, a properly maintained share register, audited financial statements, and a SARB tax clearance certificate are produced. The ZA (Pty) Ltd structure is treated as an onshore tax-resident entity; profit remittance is legal but must not violate exchange-control measures on external liabilities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from date of shipment unless FinSurv approves a longer period. On receipt, the exporter must surrender the foreign currency to an Authorised Dealer or credit it to an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or Export Foreign Exchange Earners Account (EFEA). There is no fixed percentage compulsory conversion to rand, but the proceeds must be either converted into ZAR or retained in a local bank-approved foreign currency facility; speculative retention offshore is prohibited. Banks monitor EFEA balances and require justification for retention beyond the trade cycle.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Exchange Control Regulations of 1961 and the Currency and Exchanges Guidelines for South Africa

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (E-Money Institutions) are not recognised as Authorised Dealers under the South African Reserve Bank's National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 and cannot provide the full Treasury or trade-clearing rail for a ZA trading company. An EMI may be used only as a supplementary collection/payment tool if it partners with a licensed South African bank and does not create unapproved foreign-currency balances or circumvent exchange-control reporting. Primary clearing and settlement must remain with SARB-authorised banking institutions.
