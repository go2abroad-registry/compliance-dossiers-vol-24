Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or share capital is prescribed for a private company under South African law. The company may issue one (1) no-par value ordinary share for nominal or in-kind consideration as determined by the board in terms of s. 38 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008. No escrow account or government-supervised capital deposit account is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the local municipal Fire & Rescue Division confirming compliance with SANS 10400 Part T and National Building Regulations fire-protection provisions, including emergency exits, detection and suppression systems; structural load inspection certificate for the floor plates housing the GPU/server rooms and full-height racking, confirmed by a registered structural engineer; electrical certificate of compliance (SANS 10142) for all installations including UPS systems; Department of Employment and Labour occupational clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993 covering ergonomic workstation assessments for annotation staff, artificial lighting and glare control, ventilation and air-conditioning performance per SANS 10400 Part O, and a COIDA registration confirmation evidencing occupational-injury insurance cover.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or share capital is prescribed for a private company under South African law. The company may issue one (1) no-par value ordinary share for nominal or in-kind consideration as determined by the board in terms of s. 38 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008. No escrow account or government-supervised capital deposit account is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities (non-resident companies) register via SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch by completing the applicable registration forms (VAT 101 for VAT; income tax registration for the non-resident company). Submit certified copies of the certificate of incorporation / Memorandum of Incorporation, a power of attorney or SARS resolution authorising the responsible person, proof of identity of the responsible person, and details of the local address or representative in South Africa where applicable. SARS then issues a 10-digit income tax reference number and a separate 10-digit VAT reference number. VAT registration is compulsory from the date an enterprise's taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration applies from ZAR 50,000 per annum. Non-residents making taxable supplies in South Africa or deriving South African source income fall within SARS's registration net.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Pty Ltd is a separate corporate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; resident companies are taxed on worldwide income while non-resident companies are taxed only on South African source income; no pass-through regime applies to a standard (Pty) Ltd) — Foreign entities (non-resident companies) register via SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch by completing the applicable registration forms (VAT 101 for VAT; income tax registration for the non-resident company). Submit certified copies of the certificate of incorporation / Memorandum of Incorporation, a power of attorney or SARS resolution authorising the responsible person, proof of identity of the responsible person, and details of the local address or representative in South Africa where applicable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Pty Ltd is a separate corporate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; resident companies are taxed on worldwide income while non-resident companies are taxed only on South African source income; no pass-through regime applies to a standard (Pty) Ltd)

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box rules. Elective regimes include: (1) Headquarter Company election under section 9Ihers (exempting qualifying foreign dividends, foreign branch income, interest, royalties, and foreign service fees from South African tax); (2) Micro Business turnover election under the Sixth Schedule (turnover not exceeding ZAR 1 million); (3) VAT group registration election under section 50 of the VAT Act (two or more 100%-owned group companies may be registered as a single vendor); (4) tax-neutral intra-group asset transfer / amalgamation / unbundling elections under sections 41 to 47; (5) hybrid interest / hybrid dividend elections under sections 8E, 8F, and the section 25H hybrid mismatch rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 (R1 million) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (section 23 VAT Act). Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 per annum (section 23A VAT Act).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods export: zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods are supplied to a non-resident and are removed from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply, with proof of export retained. Services export (including AI data labelling and BPO services): zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act where the services are supplied to a non-resident and are not physically rendered in South Africa and are not directly connected with land or movable property situated in South Africa; the contract, invoices, and evidence of the recipient's non-resident status must be retained to substantiate the zero rating.

  • For goods: Customs Export Declaration (SAD500), Bill of Lading or Airway Bill, commercial invoice, packing list, and proof of export.
  • For AI data-labelling / BPO services: signed contract with the non-resident client, detailed tax invoice, confirmation of non-resident status of the client, foreign-currency remittance evidence via a SARB authorised dealer, bank statement showing receipt of funds, and transfer pricing documentation where applicable.

No universal real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently mandated. Electronic VAT return (VAT 201) filing via SARS eFiling is mandatory for registered vendors. B2G e-invoicing compliant with the South African PEPPOL e-invoicing framework is required for government transactions. Tax invoices must satisfy the requirements of sections 20 and 20A of the VAT Act. SARS is phasing in broader e-invoicing and anticipates future real-time transaction reporting for large vendors.

SARS may reverse an export zero-rating and assess VAT at 15% where documentary substantiation is deficient. Retrospective assessment periods are 3 years generally, 5 years where a return was not fully disclosed, and unlimited in cases of fraud or non-registration. The Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 imposes understatement penalties ranging from 10% to 75% of the shortfall depending on the culprit behavior (substantial understatement, no reasonable grounds, gross negligence, or intentional evasion), together with interest under section 223. Criminal prosecution for deliberate zero-rating fraud may lead to fines and/or imprisonment of up to five years, in addition to the clawback of refunds with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including cross-border service fees, dividends declared out of taxed profits, interest, and royalties, without prior SARB approval, provided the processing Authorized Dealer verifies the underlying commercial documentation and, for dividend remittance, the company's SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN. Capital-account inflows, such as non-resident share subscription or shareholder loans, are freely permitted in respect of inward foreign direct investment but must be reported by the receiving bank via the relevant SARB balance-of-payments codes and endorsed as non-resident obligations on the company's records. Structural convertibility constraint: urgent remittance can be slowed by documentary due-diligence bottlenecks at commercial banks; no approval is required for bona fide service payments, but payments exceeding the Form A threshold for imports of services require a SARS No-Objection Directive before the bank releases funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Chapter 8 of the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, a South African resident exporter of services must procure that the full export proceeds are paid into South Africa within 90 days of the invoice date or the date the services are rendered, and any foreign currency received must be sold to an Authorized Dealer within 30 days of receipt unless retained in an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with an Authorized Dealer. There is no fixed percentage mandatory conversion ratio; the operative duty is 100% repatriation within the statutory time bar, with the Authorized Dealer monitoring overdue receipts, and non-compliance exposes the company to administrative penalties and exchange-control breach proceedings under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, together with the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) for AML/CFT oversight under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW for offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) as primary banking counterparties. Legal constraints: an offshore EMI cannot hold a ZAR current account, issue a local South African IBAN, or on-board a South African corporate client as a principal without a banking license under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 and without FSCA licensing for financial services rendered in South Africa. Practical blueprint: use an EMI only as a collection gateway for foreign-currency client payments (e.g., Wise Business USD/EUR account) followed by SWIFT or local-rail repatriation into the ZAR operational account at an Authorized Dealer bank, ensuring the inward leg carries the correct SARB balance-of-payments purpose code for services exports; EMIs cannot provide SARB-compliant Forms A, non-resident endorsements, or SARS directives.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders transfer the capital subscription via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas remitting bank to the ZA_PTY_LTD operational account at the South African Authorized Dealer, quoting the SARB-approved balance-of-payments purpose code for non-resident equity investment (share subscription) and referencing the company registration number; the beneficiary bank will ring-fence the receipt as a non-resident capital inflow, issue a Non-Resident Endorsement on the company's banking records, and report the transaction in the quarterly Exchanges Control reporting. For shareholder loans, the company must submit a Form BDI through the bank to register the foreign liability before drawdown, and the loan agreement must specify the interest rate, term, and repayment mechanism. All inward capital must be recorded in the company's Financial Surveillance annual return and, upon winding-up or capital reduction, the non-resident capital and realised profits are remittable only after the bank verifies the SARS Tax Compliance Status and the non-resident endorsement remains intact.For cross-border trade-related disbursements, the company instructs the Authorized Dealer via electronic banking to issue SWIFT MT103 for third-party supplier payments (e.g., overseas GPU hosting, annotation tooling licences, and subcontractor micro-payouts) with the correctly mapped SARB purpose code for imports of services, completing the Form A declaration as required and producing the SARS No-Objection Directive or eFiling Service Import directive where the payment exceeds the Form A threshold. Where goods-based trade occurs (e.g., import of computing hardware), the company may additionally rely on SWIFT MT700 documentary letters of credit with full-set bills of lading or on MT202 COVER for nostro-cleared foreign-currency settlements; all outbound payments are debited against the ZAR account at the bank's published dealing rate unless a forward cover was previously contracted, and the bank will verify the counterparty and the nature of the contract to prevent use for capital flight.Deliverable forward exchange contracts (FECs) concluded with an Authorized Dealer in respect of committed cross-border services receivables/payables, covering a maximum of 12 months of committed transactions and requiring documentary support for the underlying exposureCross-currency basis swaps and vanilla currency swaps between ZAR and USD/EUR for medium-term balance-sheet hedging of non-resident shareholder loansCurrency options and zero-premium collars to hedge repatriation-driven FX volatility on USD/EUR invoiced revenuesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) transacted only through non-resident counterparties for offshore hedging of ZAR exposure where the non-resident is unable to take physical deliveryImmediate and spot-rate contracts ('tom-next' roll swaps) managed by the Authorized Dealer for daily treasury cash-flow optimisation within the approved hedging limit

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual ITR14 must be submitted within 12 months after the end of the company's financial year / year of assessment. First provisional tax payment is due within six months after the beginning of the year of assessment; second payment is due before the last business day of the company's financial year; the optional top-up payment is due within six months after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5 million aggregate affected-transaction threshold for preparation of a South African Transfer Pricing Local File; Master File documentation is generally required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above ZAR 1 billion; a SARS Country-by-Country Report is mandatory where consolidated group revenue is at least ZAR 10 billion. SARS may still request transfer-pricing records for affected transactions below these thresholds based on risk.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations as a standalone regime, but requires real economic substance through the common-law substance-over-form doctrine, the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rules in s80A–s80L of the Income Tax Act, and specific anti-avoidance provisions for interest deductions and controlled foreign companies. A South African BPO entity must demonstrate actual staff, management decisions, operational premises, and risk-bearing in South Africa; a paper-only booking entity that funnels profits through connected-party service fees is vulnerable to recharacterization and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% to 200% for intentional tax evasion; criminal fine of up to ZAR 50,000 or ten times the tax shortfall; imprisonment up to five years under the Tax Administration Act; and accumulation of prescribed interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause, participate in, or knowingly benefit from fraudulent returns may be jointly and severally liable for company tax debts, face criminal prosecution, be subjected to SARS departure bans, and be disqualified from serving as directors.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS invalidation of the avoidance arrangement under the GAAR, reassessment at 27% corporate tax, disallowance of connected-party deductions, understatement penalties of up to 200%, and possible deregistration by CIPC for inactivity.

Director personal liability: Directors of an inactive or insolvent shell may be personally liable under the Companies Act for reckless trading or knowingly carrying on business with the intention to defraud SARS; civil restitution, asset attachment, travel restrictions, and imprisonment are available remedies.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: SARS adjustment to arm's-length consideration, additional tax at 27%, understatement penalties ranging from 10% to 200%, interest, and administrative non-compliance penalties for failure to prepare or produce the required Local File or Master File.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the intercompany pricing, sign the related-party agreements, or execute the loan structures may be found personally liable if the adjustment is caused by gross negligence, willful blindness, or intentional tax evasion; asset attachment and departure prohibition orders may be imposed.
AML/CFT Payment-Rail Violations

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act include administrative fines up to ZAR 10 million, criminal prosecution for money laundering, forfeiture of proceeds, and SARS assessment on undeclared funds as taxable income. Businesses that intentionally avoid FIC registration as an accountable institution face escalated penalties and possible closure orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly enable or conceal suspicious payment flows may be prosecuted as principals or accessories to money laundering, held personally liable for civil compensation, subjected to freezing orders and departure bans, and imprisoned; corporate veil-piercing applies where the company is a mere conduit.
