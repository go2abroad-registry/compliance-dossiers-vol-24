Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sars.gov.za; customs and VAT administered centrally with no sub-national tax distinction

E-government filing portal

Foreign-incorporated entity must first register as an external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) under s. 23 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 within 20 business days of commencing business. Thereafter, apply for a SARS Income Tax Reference Number via eFiling using the ITREG/ITA registration process, normally within 21 business days of incorporation or commencement. A Pty Ltd must appoint a public officer (resident in South Africa) under s. 246 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. For VAT, register under s. 23(1) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 within 21 days of exceeding the compulsory threshold; voluntary registration under s. 23(2) requires a R50,000 turnover. A foreign entity may obtain a SARS tax number for withholding/VAT purposes without an SA incorporated subsidiary if it makes taxable supplies in the Republic. SARS will issue separate VAT vendor number and customs importer/exporter code (unique 8-digit code) linked to the single taxpayer reference.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Pty Ltd is a separate legal person; corporate income tax at 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; dividends from SA-resident companies exempt under s. 64FA; no SA pass-through regime for Pty Ltd) — Foreign-incorporated entity must first register as an external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) under s. 23 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 within 20 business days of commencing business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Pty Ltd is a separate legal person; corporate income tax at 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; dividends from SA-resident companies exempt under s. 64FA; no SA pass-through regime for Pty Ltd)

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in South Africa. Statutory elections available: (i) VAT group registration under s. 50 of the VAT Act allowing entities under common control to file one VAT return and be jointly and severally liable; (ii) group relief elections under s. 41(1) and s. 41(2) of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 for intra-group asset transfers at tax value or the 3-year rollover of assessed losses; (iii) hybrid interest characterisation elections under s. 8E and s. 8EA for instruments where debt/equity substitution is disputed; (iv) election to calculate foreign exchange gains/losses on the translation method versus deferred method under s. 24I, subject to SARS approval. Hybrid mismatch rules (Part IIB of the Income Tax Act) apply mandatory recharacterisation of double deductions and deduction/non-inclusion scenarios; no opt-out election available for transactions between connected persons in reportable jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 taxable turnover in any 12-month period (compulsory registration, s. 23(1) VAT Act); voluntary registration permitted from ZAR 50,000 turnover under s. 23(2); once registered, all taxable supplies including zero-rated exports are reported on the VAT201 return. Foreign suppliers of electronic and imported services must register based on a R1,000,000 threshold of supplies to SA recipients under s. 23(1A).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Offshore towage and salvage services qualify for 0% VAT under s. 11(2)(k) of the VAT Act where the services are physically rendered outside South Africa, i.e., the towage/salvage operation occurs beyond the territorial waters of the Republic (12 nautical miles from the baseline). Alternatively, s. 11(2)(l) zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident recipient (who is not a SA vendor, or if a vendor, the services do not relate to an enterprise carried on in SA) where the services are not consumed or used in South Africa. Key conditions: (i) contract must be with a non-resident principal (flag state, shipowner, P&I club, or insurers); (ii) the vessel being towed or salved must be outside SA waters at the time of performance, or if within SA waters, the services must be supplied to a non-resident and demonstrably consumed in the international commercial context (s. 11(2)(l) read with s. 54 place-of-supply deeming rules); (iii) the supply must not be directly connected with land or movable property situated in South Africa - for salvage equipment physically deployed from SA but performing offshore, the equipment export must be separately evidenced; (iv) the supplier must keep documentary proof that the physical performance occurred outside the Republic, typically the towage/salvage service report, AIS/vessel tracking data, and contract specification. Services performed in SA territorial waters or in aid of a vessel in a SA port are taxable at 15%.

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration (DA550) lodged electronically via SARS Customs or an authorised clearing agent, covering exported goods or temporary export of salvage/towage equipment, with the correct tariff classification and ISIC 5222 reference
  • Bill of Lading or sea waybill (or equivalent towage contract bill) evidencing delivery of the towed/salved vessel to the non-resident owner and the place of performance outside SA territorial waters
  • Signed towage/salvage contract (e.g., BIMCO TOWHIRE, LOF 2020, or Lloyd's Open Form Salvage Agreement) stating the geographic scope, currency, and non-resident counter-party details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorised dealer (bank) under SARB Exchange Control Regulations evidencing receipt of foreign currency in the prescribed form, or Swift MT103/MT202 with the original remitter and reference to the specific vessel and contract number
  • Salvage master's report, AIS track record, and logbook extract proving the vessel was located outside South African waters during the service period; where an escrow is used (Lloyd's Standard Form), the escrow release instruction and proof of final award settlement must be retained

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of individual invoices is in force. SARS requires tax invoices issued by VAT vendors to contain all statutory fields listed in s. 20(4) of the VAT Act (seller and buyer identity, VAT number, invoice date, description, consideration, tax amounts); invoices may be issued in paper or electronic HTML/PDF form. SARS is implementing a phased e-invoicing mandate under its VAT modernisation roadmap: from 2025, high-value vendors (turnover above ZAR 10 billion) and listed categories must issue structured e-invoices (XML/UBL) transmitted via SARS eFiling API or certified service provider, with broader rollout expected through 2027. Until the full mandate, PDF invoices remain acceptable. All rental, export, and mixed-use invoices must be retained electronically for 5 years, and SARS may require digital file extraction (s. 30/31 TAA) in an audit. The e-invoice must pass SARS schema validation before transmission, but there is no pre-clearance or authorisation blocking of invoice issuance.

SARS may audit VAT and zero-rating claims for a 3-year general period, extended to 5 years in cases of non-disclosure or material understatement. If zero-rating is incorrectly applied, SARS assesses output VAT at 15% on the full invoice amount from the date of supply, plus interest at the prescribed rate under s. 187 TAA (calculated on a daily compounding basis). The understatement penalty under ss. 222-223 of the Tax Administration Act ranges from 10% of the shortfall for minor omissions, 25% for substantial understatement, 50% for reasonable care not taken, to 150% for intentional tax evasion; behaviour classification is determined by SARS at audit. Where export documents are false or forged, criminal prosecution under s. 234 TAA and s. 15(6) VAT Act applies, with fines up to ZAR 10 million and imprisonment up to 10 years. SARS also has power to make a 100% additional assessment for fraud under s. 95 TAA, to collect the tax despite the 3/5-year prescription period where fraud or misrepresentation is proven, and to suspend VAT refunds pending verification (s. 40 VAT Act). Retrospective clawback includes the reversal of any input VAT credits claimed on the transaction and holds the director or public officer personally liable in certain cases of deliberate failure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current-account transactions, including trade in maritime services, towage fees, salvage awards, and crew and port disbursements, provided the transaction is genuinely documented and routed through an authorised dealer. Capital-account transactions, including non-resident equity subscription, intra-group shareholder loans, and outward direct investment, are free to be concluded if effected via an authorised dealer and reported to FinSurv, subject to anti-money-laundering verification, tax clearance where applicable, and withholding tax on dividends or interest. Profit remittance from a ZA (Pty) Ltd to non-resident shareholders is permitted after a valid dividend declaration under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and after the company has satisfied the solvency and liquidity test; the remittance is handled through an authorised dealer, with South African dividend withholding tax at 20% deducted from the gross dividend. The ZAR does not have layered capital controls blocking current account flows, but no surplus may be retained offshore without express exchange-control approval; any capital imported must be recorded as a non-resident liability on the company's books.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exports of goods and services, including offshore towage and marine salvage operations, the export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment or completion of the service, unless FinSurv approval extends the period. Once foreign currency is received into the authorised dealer, it must be sold or converted to rand and surrendered to the authorised dealer within 30 days of receipt; no portion of the foreign currency may be retained, transferred to an offshore account, or used for offshore settlement without FinSurv permission. The towage or salvage invoice must be raised in foreign currency, the underlying contract and all supporting documents must be kept by the bank, and any disbursement run-off outside South Africa must be separately declared to the authorised dealer.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the National Treasury delegated authority; the Prudential Authority regulates authorised banks and foreign exchange dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low standalone viability. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment fintechs are not authorised dealers under South African exchange control and cannot lawfully process ZAR settlement or act as the clearing agent for inbound export receipts or outbound capital flows. An unlicensed offshore EMI is viable only as a supplementary front-end channel if it operates behind a South African-licensed authorised dealer, with all cross-border wire transfers ultimately settled by the local bank. Any use of a foreign EMI for director disbursements or trade settlement will fail the FinSurv required authorised-dealer route and may trigger adverse findings under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 and the Exchange Control Regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders should inject equity by wire transfer in a freely convertible hard currency to the company's designated capital escrow account at the authorised dealer bank. The wire must state the foreign-reference purpose code consistent with an inward direct investment into a South African private company, for example 'share subscription in a South African resident company', and must be accompanied by the completed authorised-dealer capital transaction form, the signed share subscription agreement, board approval to allot shares, and the SARS income-tax reference number of the investing entity. The payment must arrive as an MT103 to the bank with clear beneficiary account details; same-day credits are acceptable only where the funds are not subject to offshore clearing restrictions. The company must ensure the inward transfer is not labelled as a loan or ambiguous 'general remittance' because such mis-labelling can delay credit, trigger FinSurv enquiries, or cause the funds to be classified as temporary external debt. For loan funding above the company threshold, the loan agreement, interest rate, repayment schedule, and tax-compliance position must be submitted for department-of-finance review if not covered by an authorised-dealer permission. All amounts received must be booked to the non-resident liability or share-issue memorandum, and the authorised dealer must file the transaction with FinSurv under the prescribed balance-of-payments and capital-transaction reporting systems.Trade disbursements for offshore towage and salvage operations shall be executed by the South African bank through SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers for low-to-medium value port disbursements, bunker fuel purchases, crew pay, and insurance premiums, and through MT202/MT202COV where pooling or netting is required by a correspondent bank. Large or contractual trade payments to overseas sub-contractors may be structured under commercial documentary letters of credit (at sight or usance) with SWIFT MT700 messages, particularly where the beneficiary requires evidence of settlement before releasing the vessel or salvage equipment. Where the counterparty insists on an escrow holder, the South African company should only use a fully-licensed escrow agent that routes cleared funds through the authorised dealer and can issue an escrow agreement that does not create an offshore liability; the escrow agent must not hold ZAR outside South Africa. All outward remittances require a load-shedding purpose declaration through the bank's electronic banking platform, supported by the commercial invoice, bill of lading, towage work order, salvage award certificate, port agency invoice, or the underlying policy certificate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low standalone viability. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment fintechs are not authorised dealers under South African exchange control and cannot lawfully process ZAR settlement or act as the clearing agent for inbound export receipts or outbound capital flows. An unlicensed offshore EMI is viable only as a supplementary front-end channel if it operates behind a South African-licensed authorised dealer, with all cross-border wire transfers ultimately settled by the local bank. Any use of a foreign EMI for director disbursements or trade settlement will fail the FinSurv required authorised-dealer route and may trigger adverse findings under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 and the Exchange Control Regulations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual total cross-border related-party transactions equal to or exceeding ZAR 100 million; a Local File and Master File are required for transactions above ZAR 100 million, while a CIR (Country-by-Country Report) is required for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding ZAR 10 billion. Smaller transactions require contemporaneous comparability documentation upon SARS enquiry.

Economic substance scope: South Africa applies the general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) under s 80A–80L of the Income Tax Act, which nullify any transaction lacking commercial substance and whose sole or main purpose is to obtain a tax benefit. Additionally, for South African tax residency purposes, the 'management and control' test requires that substantive decision-making and central management of the (Pty) Ltd be located in South Africa; offshore towage and salvage operations must demonstrate real operational substance, including crew, vessels, contracts, invoicing, and risk management in South Africa (or in the situs of operation) to avoid PE exposure and GAAR recharacterisation. South Africa does not apply separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to the UAE; instead, the GAAR, controlled foreign company (CFC) rules under s 9D, and transfer pricing rules under s 31 collectively enforce economic substance. A South African resident company using a low-tax offshore subsidiary must ensure the subsidiary has adequate substance to avoid CFC attribution of passive income and to justify a lower effective tax rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s 234 and s 235 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; conviction attracts a fine not exceeding ZAR 500,000 or imprisonment not exceeding 10 years, or both; in addition, SARS may impose a 75% understatement penalty under s 223 and levy interest plus the outstanding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable under s 159 of the Tax Administration Act where the company fails to deduct or pay any withholding tax (including dividend tax); for fraudulent evasion, the corporate veil may be pierced under s 20(9) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 if the company is used to defraud creditors or evade tax; personal liability extends to civil restitution of diverted funds, compensation to SARS for wasted costs, travel bans under s 166 of the Tax Administration Act, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke GAAR (s 80A–80L) to disregard the company or recharacterise transactions, disallow deductions, assess the company on recalculated taxable income, and impose a 75% penalty; registration may be deregistered under the Companies Act and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) may issue administrative fines under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 for non-compliance with beneficial ownership reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the company to participate in a sham arrangement are jointly and severally liable for resulting tax deficiencies under s 159; a director may be declared delinquent under s 162 of the Companies Act and disqualified from holding office; prosecution for fraud or theft of corporate assets may result in imprisonment of up to 15 years.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines up to ZAR 100 million for the company under s 49 of the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (as amended by the General Laws Amendment Act 22 of 2022); employees and directors may face fines up to ZAR 10 million and imprisonment up to 15 years for wilful non-compliance; SARS may also invoke the cross-border tax information exchange and disclose to the FIC; a conviction of money laundering under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998 attracts a fine or imprisonment up to 30 years.

Director personal liability: Directors personally accountable for failure to file risk and compliance returns, establish an AML compliance programme, and report suspicious transactions; personal assets are at risk through civil forfeiture proceedings; travel bans are commonly imposed; imprisonment for money laundering offences under s 6 of POCA (up to 15 years) and for racketeering under s 2 (up to 30 years).
Transfer Pricing Adjustment / Cross-Border Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: A transfer pricing adjustment under s 31 increases taxable income; the understatement penalty under s 223 can reach 75% of the shortfall; interest runs from the date the tax should have been paid; a CbC report failure triggers a penalty under s 54 of the Tax Administration Act of ZAR 500 per day per jurisdiction up to ZAR 10,000 per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure contemporaneous documentation or who knowingly approve artificial pricing may be held personally liable for the additional tax, penalties, and interest as a responsible person under s 159; criminal liability may attach under s 235 for aiding and abetting tax evasion; director disqualification under the Companies Act may follow for gross negligence.
