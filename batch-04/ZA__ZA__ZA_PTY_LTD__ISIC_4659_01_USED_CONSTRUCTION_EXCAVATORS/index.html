Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), established under the South African Revenue Service Act 34 of 1997. Primary transactional portals: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za) for income tax and VAT registration/returns; SARS Customs & Excise module for import/export declarations and excise registrations.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd: (i) incorporate through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the company registration number; (ii) appoint a resident public officer as required by s. 252 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; (iii) register for income tax on SARS eFiling, obtaining the income tax reference number (the company TIN); (iv) submit a VAT101 application to register as a VAT vendor within 21 days after taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period (voluntary registration available from ZAR 50,000); (v) obtain an importer/exporter customs code through the SARS Customs registration process (DA185) prior to physical exports; (vi) register for PAYE, SDL, and UIF when employees are engaged. Non-resident owners may hold 100% of the shares, but the South African legal entity is the registered taxpayer; no separate foreign-entity TIN is issued for a branch unless a permanent establishment of a non-resident company is registered.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a company on its worldwide income at 27% corporate income tax for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through eligibility. Territorial scope: residence-based worldwide taxation. Tax-exempt zones: no general tax holiday applies; special economic zone (SEZ) concessions (e.g., reduced CIT and s. 12I allowance) are limited to SEZ-designated qualifying operators, not to general wholesale traders of used machinery. — For a foreign-owned Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd: (i) incorporate through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the company registration number; (ii) appoint a resident public officer as required by s. 252 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; (iii) register for income tax on SARS eFiling, obtaining the income tax reference number (the company TIN); (iv) submit a VAT101 application to register as a VAT vendor within 21 days after taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period (voluntary registration available from ZAR 50,000); (v) obtain an importer/exporter customs code through the SARS Customs registration process (DA185) prior to physical exports; (vi) register for PAYE, SDL, and UIF when employees are engaged. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a company on its worldwide income at 27% corporate income tax for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through eligibility. Territorial scope: residence-based worldwide taxation. Tax-exempt zones: no general tax holiday applies; special economic zone (SEZ) concessions (e.g., reduced CIT and s. 12I allowance) are limited to SEZ-designated qualifying operators, not to general wholesale traders of used machinery.

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT group registration election under s. 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991 for connected/related vendors; (ii) election to account for VAT on the payments basis under s. 15 of the VAT Act where the vendor meets qualifying turnover criteria; (iii) small business corporation tax treatment under s. 12E of the Income Tax Act if statutory ownership and turnover thresholds are satisfied; (iv) foreign tax credit election under s. 6quat of the Income Tax Act for foreign taxes suffered on export or foreign-source income; (v) hybrid mismatch rules (ss. 8F, 8FA, 23M, and 25H of the Income Tax Act) are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions, not elective, and impose deemed interest or deduction denials that must be disclosed on the ITR14 return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory VAT registration when taxable supplies exceed this amount in any consecutive 12-month period); ZAR 50,000 (voluntary registration threshold). Non-resident suppliers of imported services to South African recipients may be subject to the reverse-charge mechanism under s. 7(1)(c) of the VAT Act even without local registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act zero-rates the supply of movable goods, including secondhand hydraulic earthmovers and rollers, where the vendor physically exports the goods (or delivers them to an export enterprise) within 90 days of the date of supply, under customs supervision, and retains documentary proof of export. The 0% rate applies to the export sale; input VAT on the purchase of used equipment is creditable under s. 16(3) of the VAT Act provided valid tax invoices are held and the goods are not deemed supplied or apportioned for mixed use. The goods must permanently leave South Africa; re-importation generally invalidates the zero-rating. Offshore services incidental to the export (e.g., international freight) are not within s. 11(1)(a) but may be considered separately under s. 11(2)(k) if the service is physically rendered and consumed outside South Africa; for this goods-trading model, s. 11(1)(a) is the operative zero-rating ground.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 / DA 500) electronically lodged with and cleared by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill (air freight), or CMR consignment note (road freight) showing South Africa as origin and a foreign consignee/destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the SAD 500, with unit serial numbers, model designations, and declared customs values
  • SWIFT MT103 or bank inward remittance certificate (IRC) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a South African bank account
  • Exchange control settlement record (EXM form / authorised dealer certification) where required by the South African Reserve Bank
  • Proof of physical departure (terminal gate pass, outturn report, or border customs stamp) for the relevant transport mode

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory tax-authority API clearance regime is currently in force. SARS accepts paper and electronic VAT invoices that satisfy s. 20 of the VAT Act (vendor name, address, VAT number, invoice description, quantity, price, tax amount, and 'tax invoice' legend). VAT returns are filed electronically via eFiling (VAT201), and customs export declarations are lodged through the SARS Customs EDI platform. SARS has published a draft national e-invoicing framework with a phased mandate expected in future years; until legislated, no authorization token or real-time clearance code is required, but all original electronic invoices must be retained in unaltered form for audit.

SARS reconciles export zero-ratings against customs SAD 500 clearances, bills of lading, freight-forwarder files, and bank settlement records. If goods are not exported within 90 days of supply, the transaction is re-characterised as standard-rated at 15%, triggering retrospective output VAT, interest, and penalties under the VAT Act. Invalid or missing export evidence causes reversal of the zero-rating and clawback of all input VAT claimed on the equipment. Under s. 59 of the VAT Act, additional tax may be imposed at rates from 10% to 200% of the shortfall depending on conduct. Understatement penalties under s. 222 of the Tax Administration Act range from 25% to 200% of the tax shortfall for undisclosed or incorrect positions. Fraudulent zero-rating is a serious tax crime under s. 234 of the Tax Administration Act, carrying a fine of up to ZAR 7,500,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 10 years. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days additionally breaches South African Reserve Bank exchange control rules, exposing the exporter to financial penalties and potential suspension of customs trading privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa is an IMF Article VIII member: current-account flows (trade settlement, service fees, royalties, dividends) are fully convertible without prior SARB approval and with no quantitative limit. The rand (ZAR) is a fully deliverable onshore currency. Capital-account flows are partially liberalised: inbound foreign direct investment (FDI) requires no prior exchange-control approval, only ex-post declaration to FinSurv through the receiving AD, whereas outbound investment, offshore guarantees and cross-border lending by residents remain subject to CEM thresholds and allowances. Dividend/profit remittance is unrestricted once the authorised dealer verifies the source of profit and the company files a dividend declaration; 20% dividends withholding tax applies to non-resident shareholders. Practical hurdles for a used-machinery trader are not convertibility constraints but bank de-risking of counterparties in Southern African high-risk corridors and the documentary proof of no unrepatriated export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CEM Section B (Exports) requires that full export proceeds be received in South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 calendar days of the shipment date (bill of lading/air waybill date). The exporter must submit the customs SAD500 export declaration and lodge a bank-copy export form before or at shipment. Mandatory conversion ratio is nil: the historical 80%/10% surrender obligations were abolished, so proceeds may be retained in a SARB-compliant Foreign Currency Account (FCA), converted to ZAR, or applied to approved foreign trade uses. Delays beyond 180 days, partial non-receipt, or exports to sanctioned destinations require FinSurv approval via the bank; non-repatriation is a criminal offence under the Currency and Exchanges Act. For re-exported secondhand excavators, the AD will cross-match the SAD500 with procurement invoices to mitigate trade-based under/over-invoicing.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) is the statutory exchange-control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, supported by the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual (CEM). Banking supervision is performed by the SARB Prudential Authority under the Banks Act 94 of 1990. All cross-border foreign-exchange transactions must be executed through a SARB-licensed Authorised Dealer in Foreign Exchange (AD), being the tier-1 commercial banks and their registered branches.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW-MEDIUM - supplementary rail only; not a permitted primary treasury gateway. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, dLocal) do not hold SARB Authorised Dealer status and may not settle export proceeds or import payments for a South African resident without routing through a licensed AD. Channeling sale or supplier funds via an unlicensed EMI would breach the Currency and Exchanges Act and the FIC Act because cross-border transaction reports flow only through AD bank channels. A South African-licensed payment fintech (third-party payment provider under the National Payment System Act, 1998) may handle domestic ZAR collection via PASA rails, and foreign EMIs may cover incidental low-value disbursements (corporate cards, SaaS fees), but all FX conversion, trade settlement, LC issuance and escrow operations for used-machinery deals must remain inside a tier-1 authorised bank. Recommended architecture: local regulated EMI for small domestic receipts, tier-1 AD bank for every USD/EUR flow above USD 10,000.

Defense evidence: Share subscription agreement and SARB inward-FDI declaration filed through the bank (IMF BPM6 classification: Direct Investment - Equity Capital); foreign bank statements showing the named shareholder as originator and the corresponding interbank trail; remitting bank's licence and bank reference letter; board resolution confirming the capital purpose; funds held in escrow until the source-of-funds file is approved.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW-MEDIUM - supplementary rail only; not a permitted primary treasury gateway. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, dLocal) do not hold SARB Authorised Dealer status and may not settle export proceeds or import payments for a South African resident without routing through a licensed AD. Channeling sale or supplier funds via an unlicensed EMI would breach the Currency and Exchanges Act and the FIC Act because cross-border transaction reports flow only through AD bank channels. A South African-licensed payment fintech (third-party payment provider under the National Payment System Act, 1998) may handle domestic ZAR collection via PASA rails, and foreign EMIs may cover incidental low-value disbursements (corporate cards, SaaS fees), but all FX conversion, trade settlement, LC issuance and escrow operations for used-machinery deals must remain inside a tier-1 authorised bank. Recommended architecture: local regulated EMI for small domestic receipts, tier-1 AD bank for every USD/EUR flow above USD 10,000.
