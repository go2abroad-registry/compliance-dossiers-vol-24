Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at www.sarsefiling.co.za; head office at 299 Bronkhorst Street, Brooklyn, Pretoria. SARS is the sole national tax authority; no provincial/state-level tax registration exists in South Africa.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the enterprise registration number. 2) Register the company as a taxpayer via SARS eFiling to secure an income tax reference number; appoint a Public Officer (s24 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011) within one month of incorporation. 3) Apply for VAT registration through Form VAT 101 where taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory, s23(1) VAT Act 89 of 1991) or ZAR 50,000 (voluntary, s23(3)); foreign-owned local subsidiaries follow the same process and require a local director and SARS-mandated banking details in South Africa. 4) Register for Customs Code if physical import/export of goods occurs (not required for pure digital Steam/PlayStation publishing).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — tax-resident South African company taxed at 27% corporate income tax on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; no pass-through to shareholders; distributions subject to 20% dividends tax (s64D) unless a shareholder exemption applies; branch profits of foreign operations are subject to CFC attribution rules (s9D); no territorial election is available.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in South African law. Available elections relevant to digital publishing: (i) VAT group registration election under s50 VAT Act for groups of connected persons with ≥50% holding; (ii) s45 intra-group corporate restructure roll-over election; (iii) s23M hybrid mismatch deneturalization/adjustment elections for cross-border instruments; (iv) s9D CFC foreign business establishment exclusion election and high-taxed CFC election; (v) s24I forex realization elections where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected in the next 12 months (s23(1) VAT Act). Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 (s23(3)). Once registered, VAT 201 returns are filed on a monthly or bi-monthly basis; standard 15% rate, 0% zero-rated exports, and exempt supplies under s12 of the VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act for goods physically exported, and under s11(2)(b) for services supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time of supply, provided the services are not directly in connection with land or movable property in South Africa and are not consumed or used in South Africa. For PC/console game publishing via Steam/PlayStation: (i) license/supply of digital game rights to Valve Corporation (Steam, non-resident) or Sony Interactive Entertainment (non-resident) qualifies as an exported service at 0% where the recipient is outside South Africa and the platform-sourced revenue is earned outside South Africa; (ii) direct digital sales to South African end-users are subject to 15% VAT; (iii) the vendor must retain documentary proof of the non-resident recipient before applying zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) — required only where physical goods are exported; not applicable to pure digital downloads
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill — required only for physical goods; not applicable to digital Steam/PlayStation publishing
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT foreign-currency credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Signed license/distribution agreement with Steam (Valve Corp) or PlayStation Store (Sony) evidencing non-resident recipient and place of supply
  • Platform settlement reports (monthly payout statements) reconciling gross sales, platform fees, and net remittance in USD/EUR
  • Non-resident certificate or jurisdiction-of-establishment evidence for the platform counterparty
  • VAT export register (s11(2)) with invoice numbers, dates, currency, exchange rates, and proof of payment

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is currently in force. SARS eFiling is the mandatory electronic channel for VAT 201 returns and tax invoices must comply with s20 VAT Act (full tax invoice for supplies exceeding ZAR 5,000; abridged for ZAR 5,000 or less; recipient-created tax invoices require prior SARS approval). SARS published an OECD-aligned e-invoicing policy framework in 2023 with phased voluntary adoption through certified PEPPOL/SDI service providers; mandatory clearance is not yet legislated. For digital platform payouts (Steam/PlayStation), the taxpayer must retain platform settlement reports and reconcile them to VAT return outputs.

SARS and the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 require export proceeds to be declared and repatriated to South Africa within 180 days of export/supply; failure triggers exchange-control penalties and administrative enforcement. Incorrect zero-rating claims are clawed back: VAT at 15% becomes payable with interest (s187 TAA); additional tax of up to 200% of the unpaid VAT may be imposed under s60(1) of the VAT Act; understatement penalties under Schedule 1 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 range from 10% (substantial non-compliance) to 200% (intentional tax evasion). Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating constitutes a criminal offense under s234–s235 TAA and the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964, carrying fines, imprisonment, and retrospective disgorgement; the burden of proof to substantiate zero-rating rests on the vendor (s102 TAA).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a dual-tier exchange control regime. Non-resident rand accounts and inward capital transfers are freely convertible for current account transactions, while capital account transactions by residents require specific FinSurv approval. Profit remittance for foreign investors is permissible after satisfying tax clearance and exchange control documentation: the payment must be made via an authorised dealer using an inward transfer reference and the beneficiary must be the non-resident shareholder. Resident companies may freely convert ZAR for genuine current trade, but outbound investment and asset transfers require FinSurv approval. The rand is a controlled currency; technological gaming revenue from Steam/PlayStation is classified as exported services and qualifies for full conversion upon surrender, but capacity to retain foreign currency earnings is capped.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All exports of goods or services (including digital gaming licences and royalties) must have proceeds received in the Republic through an authorised dealer within 30 days of the earlier of shipment or date of invoice, and the full foreign currency amount must be offered for immediate sale (surrendered) to the authorised dealer. Under the Exporters' Retention Protocol, a South African resident company may retain up to 5% of eligible foreign currency earnings in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) for operational needs, provided the remaining 95% is converted to ZAR in the interbank spot market; retention above 5% requires prior FinSurv approval.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low – offshore digital EMIs (electronic money institutions) are not authorised dealers under the South African Exchange Control Regulations. South African resident entities are required to route all foreign exchange receipts and payments through authorised dealing banks. While EMI accounts may receive funds without formal approval if they operate outside the SARB authorisation perimeter, export surrender obligations and the inability of EMIs to supply transfer evidence can trigger exchange control penalties. Offshore EMI usage is viable only for incidental low-value collections subject to prompt repatriation through an authorised dealer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of all affected transactions (cross-border connected-person transactions) exceeds ZAR 100 million in the year. Master File is required for a South African entity that is part of an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion; Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the same ZAR 10 billion group-revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but substance is legally enforced through the place of effective management test, CFC provisions (s 9D), transfer pricing connected-person rules, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (s 80A-L). A company without employees, premises, or commercial function in South Africa will be disregarded or re-characterised for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under TAA s 234: fine of up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment up to five years. SARS concurrently imposes an understatement penalty under TAA s 222 up to 200% of the tax shortfall, plus interest and the 10% late-payment penalty.

Director personal liability: SARS may hold a director personally and jointly liable for the company's unpaid tax where the director knowingly participated in the evasion; the director faces a travel ban, civil restitution orders, disqualification from management, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / Impermissible Tax Avoidance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS applies GAAR (s 80A-L) to disregard the shell, re-characterise income, and deny all tax benefits; understatement penalty of 100%–200% of the assessed shortfall and criminal referral under TAA.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable as parties to an impermissible avoidance arrangement and may be sued for restitution; asset-preservation orders and travel bans are common; criminal proceedings may lead to imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the consideration to arm's length under s 31 of the Income Tax Act, imposes consequential withholding tax on adjusted amounts where appropriate, and applies understatement penalties of up to 200% for deliberate or negligent failures; failure to keep records is a separate offence.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement the mispricing may become jointly subject to the assessed liability, civil restitution, travel bans, and, on criminal conviction, imprisonment.
AML / Money Laundering / FICA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FICA s 51C administrative fine up to ZAR 10 million per contravention (plus continuing daily penalties); POCA s 4 conviction: fine up to ZAR 100 million and/or imprisonment up to 30 years; asset forfeiture available under POCA Chapter 6.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly assist in money laundering face arrest, travel bans, asset freezes, personal civil forfeiture, imprisonment, and disqualification from the board.
