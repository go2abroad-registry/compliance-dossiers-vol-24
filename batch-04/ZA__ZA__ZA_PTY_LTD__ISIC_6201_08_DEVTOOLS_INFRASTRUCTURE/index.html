Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; official website at https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity must first complete company registration through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). The company then registers for income tax via SARS eFiling, and SARS issues an income tax reference number. For VAT, the taxpayer submits Form VAT101 through SARS eFiling within 21 days after the VAT registration threshold is exceeded. Non-resident or foreign-owned entities must file through an appointed SARS representative or registered tax practitioner with a power of attorney, and provide CIPC registration certificate (CoR 14.3), Memorandum of Incorporation, director identification, proof of address and banking details as requested by SARS. SARS then issues a VAT vendor number upon approval.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned entity must first complete company registration through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). The company then registers for income tax via SARS eFiling, and SARS issues an income tax reference number. For VAT, the taxpayer submits Form VAT101 through SARS eFiling within 21 days after the VAT registration threshold is exceeded. Non-resident or foreign-owned entities must file through an appointed SARS representative or registered tax practitioner with a power of attorney, and provide CIPC registration certificate (CoR 14.3), Memorandum of Incorporation, director identification, proof of address and banking details as requested by SARS. SARS then issues a VAT vendor number upon approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box entity classification regime. A (Pty) Ltd incorporated or effectively managed in South Africa is a resident company subject to entity-level tax on worldwide income. Available statutory elections include VAT group vendor registration under s50 of the VAT Act, CFC high-tax exemption elections under s9D(9) of the Income Tax Act, and certain corporate restructuring rollover elections. Hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules apply compulsorily and cannot be opted out through unilateral classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period. Voluntary registration: permitted where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period. Foreign suppliers of electronic services have a mandatory registration threshold of ZAR 50,000 in South African- sourced turnover under s23A of the VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act if supplied to a non-resident, physically exported from South Africa through the customs export process, and exported within the prescribed period after the tax invoice is issued (generally 90 days), with retained documentary evidence. Services are zero-rated under s11(2) when supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not used or consumed in South Africa. Cloud infrastructure, database hosting, API monitoring and DevOps tools supplied to offshore customers are eligible if the direct recipient is a non-resident and the services are not consumed in South Africa.

  • Valid SARS customs export declaration (SAD500)
  • Commercial invoice / VAT tax invoice showing 0% VAT
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical export
  • Proof of physical delivery and export acceptance at the South African customs point
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank advice evidencing foreign exchange receipt from the non-resident purchaser
  • Written supply or sale contract stating the export terms (FOB / CFR / CIF)
  • Export packing list and relevant cargo or transport documentation

SARS has not implemented a continuous transaction control or real-time fiscal clearance mandate. Electronic tax invoices are valid if they contain the prescribed VAT particulars and preserve data integrity and authenticity under the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002. No mandatory API approval or digital stamping is currently required; VAT returns and electronic schedules are filed periodically through SARS eFiling, and SARS may require supporting XML or electronic records during audit.

SARS may retrospectively reassess a zero-rated export if the supporting evidence is deficient, irregular or fraudulent. The exporter is then liable for output VAT at 15%, interest under s187 of the Tax Administration Act, and understatement penalties under s222 of the Tax Administration Act. Intentional false zero-rating or fraudulent refund claims may result in criminal prosecution, fines and imprisonment. SARS retains an unlimited prescription period in cases of fraud or evasion, and failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may trigger separate South African Reserve Bank exchange-control penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a partially convertible currency regime with formal distinction between current and capital account transactions. Current account payments, including trade and service-related cross-border settlements, are generally permitted through an Authorised Dealer without SARB FinSurv approval, subject to documentary verification. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, acquisition of local assets by non-residents, and restructuring, require Authorised Dealer sign-off within delegated authority or SARB FinSurv prior approval. Profit remittances by a non-resident-owned South African (Pty) Ltd are permissible but subject to compliance with the Investment Reconciliation Form, annual financial statements demonstrating distributable reserves, a valid SARS tax clearance, and formal bank reporting. Dividends may be repatriated through an Authorised Dealer, but the company must ensure no inconsistent 'loop structure' exists and that the original inward capital was properly declared and recorded with the SARB. Branch profits similarly require audited accounts and tax compliance. Capital convertibility is therefore not absolute: each transfer is subject to documentary validity, anti-money-laundering checks, and exchange control reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of export. The exporter must surrender the full export proceeds to an Authorised Dealer, meaning the foreign currency proceeds must be converted into ZAR unless a foreign currency account or retention arrangement was specifically approved by SARB FinSurv. The mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts is 100% where no approved foreign currency account is held. For services exports, which include cloud-based DevTools infrastructure, the carrier or service provider must receive payment through the South African banking channel within 180 days of the invoice date or service completion date. The exporter must submit an export declaration, invoice, proof of receipt, and any supporting contracts to the Authorised Dealer for exchange control purposes. Non-compliance can lead to penalties, suspension from operating a foreign currency account, and criminal exposure under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the SARB Exchange Control Rulings and Currency and Exchanges Manual.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium as a primary clearing rail and Medium as a supplementary settlement tool. Foreign digital EMIs such as Mercury and Stripe Treasury are not licensed as South African Authorised Dealers, cannot process ZAR-denominated exchange control reporting, and cannot independently satisfy SARB FinSurv requirements for capital entries or trade payments. They may be used for segregated offshore collection of USD/EUR/GBP subscription revenues from international customers, provided the funds are subsequently swept into the South African operating account through a SARB-approved banking channel. Any EMI receiving funds from South African customers or making payments into South Africa without an appropriate authorised-dealer partnership may constitute unauthorised cross-border money transmission. Therefore, the primary corporate banking relationship must be with a Tier-1 South African commercial bank; the EMI can serve only as a treasury sub-ledger for offshore SaaS billing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended routing is a verified SWIFT MT103 transfer from the non-resident shareholder's offshore bank to the South African (Pty) Ltd's designated capital or operational account at a Tier-1 SARB-authorised commercial bank. The foreign currency transfer should use an accepted inward payment purpose code corresponding to 'Foreign direct investment in South Africa' or 'Capital increase by non-resident'. The bank must receive the board resolution, updated Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of share allotment, and a completed foreign investment declaration (Form FA1 or equivalent). The Authorised Dealer will enter the inward capital onto the South African Reserve Bank reporting system and issue an inward payment reference for use in future dividend remittances. The funds must be recorded on an Investment Reconciliation Form, which becomes the baseline for profit repatriation. No ZAR conversion or offshore account should be used without explicit SARB approval; all foreign capital inflows should remain identifiable and traceable to the corporate banking ledger.Outbound payments for trade-related cloud infrastructure, data-centre bandwidth, software licences, and subcontracting services should be settled through SWIFT MT103 or commercial letters of credit. For straightforward supplier payments below Authorised Dealer thresholds, corporate internet banking with an approved foreign-exchange deal slip is acceptable; larger or regular payments require Form A.1 and supporting contracts/invoices. Recommended SARB payment purpose codes include '3610 – Computer and processing services', '3620 – Cloud computing services', '3501 – Data processing, storage and hosting services', and '3205 – Computer software licensing fees', as appropriate. Domestic ZAR legs should settle through the South African clearing system, while cross-border legs will use SWIFT.
