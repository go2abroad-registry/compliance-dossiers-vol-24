Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide a check-the-box election. A (Pty) Ltd is a resident company taxed at entity level on worldwide income unless exempt under section 10(1)(e) of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; non-resident owner is taxed on South African-source income only. Partnerships and ordinary trusts may qualify for pass-through treatment but corporate entities cannot elect pass-through. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act operate automatically to deny deductions for certain hybrid financial instruments and financing arrangements, and no elective relief is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of goods: Section 11(1)(a) of the Value-Added Tax Act will apply where movable goods are supplied to a non-resident, the goods are removed from the Republic during a period not exceeding 60 days from date of supply or within such further period as SARS allows, and documentary proof is supplied. Zero-rating of services: Services physically performed outside South Africa, or supplied to a non-resident where the effective enjoyment does not occur in South Africa, or certain export-related services under Section 11(2)(k) to (p) are zero-rated if no negative adjustment applies. The entity must maintain irrefutable evidence connecting payment, export, and the VAT invoice to the actual physical movement or offshore use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DA185)
  • Bill of Lading (sea/air waybill) or equivalent shipping document
  • Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange confirmation from an Authorised Dealer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of payment and bank statement evidencing the receipt of foreign currency proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate in South Africa. VAT invoices must meet the requirements of section 20 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991 (supplier name, address, VAT including, analysis when applicable). SARS has progressively implemented e-Filing-based data submission, third-party data matching (banks, customs, company registry), continuous risk profiling, and a standard digital turnover report. SARS is developing a phased e-invoicing framework aligned to the Peppol standard but as of the current statutory regime it is not universally mandatory for all taxpayers. Taxpayers must retain VAT invoices and relevant export documentation for at least five years.

SARS may deny zero-rating or claw back input VAT deductions where export documentation is insufficient to prove physical export or offshore service delivery. An understatement penalty will apply, based on the substantial understatement percentage (0%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75% or 150% of a shortfall in the presence of intentional disregard, gross negligence or fraud) per Chapter 16 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, in addition to interest at the prescribed rate. Criminal prosecution is possible under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 and the Tax Administration Act where there is fraudulent export zero-rating or false documentation. SARS may retrospectively assess for a longer period and disallow input tax credits, with protective collection measures and public officer or director personal liability in applicable cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for bona fide current-account transactions, including trade settlement, service payments, royalties, dividends, interest, and profit remittances, provided each transaction is supported by genuine underlying documentation and filed with the correct SARB Balance-of-Payments purpose code through an Authorized Dealer. Non-residents benefit from the non-resident dispensation: no prior SARB approval is required to repatriate qualifying investment returns (dividends, interest, royalties, liquidation proceeds) after the inward investment has been registered on Form F (equity) or Form D (loan) and all SARS withholding taxes (Dividends Tax, Interest WHT, Royalty WHT) have been settled. Capital-account transactions by South African residents, such as outward equity investments, overseas guarantees, or offshore treasury placements, remain subject to FinSurv approval, regulatory allowances, or the Authorized Dealer's delegated per-case limits. Profit remittance hurdles are therefore documentary rather than discretionary: audited financial evidence, board resolutions validating the distribution, tax clearance status (SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN), and exchange-control forms are sufficient for SARB-compliant outward flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 7, an exporter must take reasonable steps to obtain payment within 90 days from the date of export, and the full foreign-currency consideration must be repatriated into South Africa within 180 days of shipment (a 60-day window applies to SACU-bound exports, and extension beyond 180 days is available only via Authorized Dealer or FinSurv approval based on proven trade-cycle requirements). There is no partial conversion ratio: 100% of the export proceeds must be sold to or deposited with a South African Authorized Dealer, either into a domestic ZAR account or into an approved foreign-currency account held with a licensed local bank. Offshore retention of export receipts is prohibited absent an individual FinSurv permit, which is granted only for demonstrated operational funding needs and not for treasury deferral.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), with delegated enforcement under the Currency and Exchanges Act No. 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations of 2021, SARB Exchange Control Circulars, and the FIC Act via commercial banks operating as Authorized Dealers in foreign exchange.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary clearing rail; viable only as a SUPPLEMENTARY payout-orchestration layer. Offshore digital EMI/PSP platforms (Wise, Revolut Business, Currencycloud, BC Group) hold no South African banking license under the Banks Act No. 94 of 1990 and cannot originate ZAR Direct Debit ACH collections from domestic bank accounts or act as the settlement bank for revenue-based financing collections. They may lawfully engage a Tier-1 South African bank as the licensed settlement counterparty, meaning all ZAR liquidity must still traverse an Authorized Dealer. The EMI may therefore handle FX conversion, sub-ledger accounting, and payout instruction routing, but it cannot replace the domestic bank account and would introduce unlicensed custody risk if client funds were held outside the regulated South African banking perimeter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol: SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident investor or lending institution to the SA Authorized Dealer with field 70 (Remittance Information) citing the inward investment registration reference — Form F (equity subscription) for shareholder capital injections, or Form D (loan registration) for intercompany/third-party financed capital. The Authorized Dealer must file the relevant SARB inward declaration within 30 days; supporting evidence includes the signed financing or royalty-funding agreement, board resolution approving the facility, final share register or loan schedule, UBO declarations, and SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN. Debt funding must respect arm's-length transfer pricing on interest and is registered to permit corresponding offshore repayment streams as collections arise. No prior SARB approval is needed for genuine inward FDI or shareholder loans, but the loss of Form F/D registration will trigger mandatory outbound remittance blocking.Disbursements to foreign digital-brand merchants are cleared via SWIFT MT103 through the Authorized Dealer's correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire for USD legs) with Form A filed where the outbound transfer exceeds the dealer's exemption threshold; for secured merchandise-backed disbursements, UCP600-compliant commercial Letters of Credit are the senior clearing rail. The outbound packet must contain the unsigned drawdown notice, underlying revenue-financing agreement, pro-forma or commercial invoice, and the correct SARB BOP purpose code (royalties/use of intangible rights for pure revenue-share flows, trade-finance code for LC-supported shipments). Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividend withholding tax on dividends paid by a South African resident company to non-resident shareholders, levied under section 64G of the Income Tax Act; no secondary tax on companies (STC) remains.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 12 months of the company's financial year-end, unless the company's assessment is issued earlier. SARS may impose a provisional tax cycle with two six-monthly provisional payments (first half-year, second half-year) and an optional top-up payment; final CIT return is due within 12 months after year-end. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South African transfer pricing rules apply where cross-border transactions between connected persons are not at arm's length. Mandatory documentation is required to be prepared before submission of the ITR14 if the transaction value exceeds: ZAR 5 million for transactions involving goods or services (including financing arrangements) in a single year, or ZAR 1 million for financial transactions (interest, guarantees, royalties, and other cross-border financial assistance). The Regulations to section 31 require contemporaneous TP documentation, including a Master File, Local File, and Country-by-Country Report. The CbCR threshold is consolidated group revenue of ZAR 10 billion (approximately). The SARS TP return (CRA-TP) must be completed in eFiling for qualifying transactions; failure to prepare documentation may result in penalties including a 'reportable arrangement' penalty and 20%-200% adjustment of taxable income plus interest.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute in the Caribbean/BEPS-style form; however, economic substance is enforced through: (1) the general anti-avoidance rules under sections 80A-80L, which invalidate 'impermissible avoidance arrangements' lacking commercial substance; (2) the anti-hybrid rules under section 25D and the Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) regime under sections 9D, which attribute tainted income (including passive royalties, interest, and financing income) of CFCs to South African residents; (3) the transfer pricing secondary adjustment under section 31, which deems a loan or contribution where primary adjustments are made, triggering imputed interest; (4) the 'residency' tie-breaker under DTA rules and the place of effective management test. A royalty-financing fund with minimal substance and significant intra-group financing or royalty flows must demonstrate actual decision-making, operational capacity, and risk-bearing in South Africa, or face recharacterization of transactions, denial of deductions, and punitive interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act, No. 28 of 2011, 'serious tax crime' penalties: a fine up to R750,000 or imprisonment up to 10 years for tax evasion under section 235; for fraud, a fine up to R750,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years under the common law or the Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act. SARS may impose an understatement penalty under section 223 from 10% to 200% of the shortfall depending on behaviour (substantial understatement, intentional, or gross negligence).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable under the Tax Administration Act section 51 (carrying on business while tax debts outstanding) and under the Companies Act section 77 for failure to file or paying dividends while insolvent. Directors and officers may be personally prosecuted for 'intentional tax evasion' even where the company is a legal person, leading to imprisonment, travel restrictions, asset attachment, and disqualification from acting as directors. Civil restitution includes the full tax arrears, penal interest, and costs of investigation.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance rules (sections 80A-80L) allow SARS to disregard, recharacterize, or annul the arrangement, deny deductions, and impose an understatement penalty of 75% (intentional) or 150% (gross negligence) of the shortfall; section 31 transfer pricing secondary adjustment deems a loan to the shareholder in the amount of the primary adjustment, subject to 15% interest withholding tax on imputed interest; CFC rules tax tainted 'South African source' passive income at 27% plus potentially dividend WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in a sham arrangement may be personally liable for the company's tax debts under section 51 of the Tax Administration Act and may be interdicted from leaving South Africa under the Alien Control Act or Income Tax Act enforcement procedures; criminal liability under the Companies Act section 213 for recklessness or gross negligence includes fines and imprisonment up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing and Cross-Border Mispricing

Statutory sanction: SARS may make a primary transfer pricing adjustment, disallow excess deductions, and impose the secondary adjustment (deemed loan) with 15% interest WHT. Understatement penalty: 25% if 'substantial understatement' (shortfall > 5% of taxable income and exceeding R1 million), 75% if 'intentional', 150% for gross negligence. Interest at prescribed rate compounded daily. Late documentation penalties of up to R2,000 per month remain applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in setting intercompany pricing without supporting documentation can be held personally liable for the 75%/150% understatement penalties in egregious cases; SARS may issue an 'inability to pay' notice and pursue directors under section 51; professional negligence exposure to shareholders for breach of fiduciary duty under the Companies Act.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) administrative penalties: up to R10 million per contravention for accountable institutions (or up to R50 million for repeat violations); criminal penalties for failure to report suspicious transactions under FICA schedule 1: fines up to R100 million or imprisonment up to 15 years; SARS and the FIC can impose compliance orders. Banks may freeze accounts; enforcement agencies may issue asset forfeiture orders under POCA.

Director personal liability: Directors of the (Pty) Ltd are personally liable for FICA penalties if the entity acts without procedures and the director was knowingly involved. Directors may face criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 15 years, travel bans under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act, and confiscation of personal assets. The Financial Intelligence Centre may also issue a 'director disqualification' notice under certain conditions.
Failure to Withhold Dividend, Interest, or Royalty WHT

Statutory sanction: The company is liable for the unpaid dividend WHT, interest, and administrative penalties. Under section 64K and section 46B(4), the payer who fails to withhold must pay the tax plus 10% penalty and interest, and is denied a deduction for the unwithheld amount. SARS may impose understatement penalties of 75%-150% for intentional non-withholding. Directors may be ordered to pay the tax personally if the company is wound up.

Director personal liability: Joint and several liability under section 51 of the Tax Administration Act for tax debts of the company; criminal liability for deliberate failure to withhold under section 235 (R750,000 fine or 10 years imprisonment); civil liability to the beneficial owner for any tax improperly deducted and not remitted.
