Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
South African Revenue Service (SARS); primary eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; customs and excise module: SARS Customs Portal, accessible through https://www.sars.gov.za/customs/.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign investor must first incorporate or register the South African Proprietary Limited company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and file the necessary incorporation documents. The company then obtains its income tax reference number by registering as a South African resident company with SARS, ordinarily through the SARS Business Registration process on eFiling or at a SARS branch. A foreign company without local incorporation that carries on a South African trade must register as a non-resident company for income tax using the SARS non-resident company registration procedure, including appointing a local public officer where required and providing constitutional documentation, resolutions, and proof of place of effective management. Once the value of taxable supplies reaches or is likely to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a 12-month period, the entity must apply for VAT registration via the VAT101/eFiling VAT module; a foreign supplier of electronic services is required to register if its South African taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in 12 months. A customs importer/exporter code is obtained separately through SARS Customs registration for physical goods export. The operative national tax identifier is the SARS income tax reference number; there is no separate tax identification card.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
South Africa does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election. A Proprietary Limited company is a separate legal person and a resident taxpayer taxed on entity-level income tax principles; pass-through treatment is not available. The relevant elections concern income tax accounting periods, trading stock valuation, certain VAT joint-venture or group arrangements where statutory conditions are satisfied, and hybrid mismatch primary adjustments under sections 31A to 31M of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. No election exists to disregard a local subsidiary or to treat a Pty Ltd as transparent for domestic income tax purposes.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
15%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Compulsory registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration may apply at ZAR 50,000 for ordinary vendors; a foreign supplier of 'electronic services' must register if its South African taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a 12-month period.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods are zero-rated if supplied under a sale and physically exported from South Africa within the prescribed period, or supplied to an export clearing agent for export. Services are zero-rated as 'exported services' under section 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act where they are supplied to a non-resident and are not used or consumed in South Africa. Digital social-casino or virtual-slots services settled through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing may qualify for zero-rating only if the supplier can substantiate that the recipient is a non-resident and that the service is not enjoyed in South Africa. In app-store distribution, the legal analysis must identify whether the Pty Ltd is supplying the end-user consumer directly or supplying digital rights/licences to the platform operator; if the latter, the supply to the non-resident platform may be zero-rated as an exported service, while Apple or Google may be the VAT vendor for the electronic service supplied to South African consumers. Zero-rating also requires the supplier to be a registered VAT vendor, to issue a valid tax invoice, and to receive the export consideration within the applicable foreign-exchange time limits.

South Africa does not currently mandate a real-time government fiscal stamping or continuous tax clearance system for standard e-commerce vendors. Registered VAT vendors must submit VAT returns electronically via SARS eFiling or permitted SARS API channels and retain all electronic invoices and records in a manner that ensures authenticity, integrity, and readability. Tax invoices, including electronic tax invoices, must contain the prescribed particulars under section 20 of the VAT Act. SARS is progressively modernising data exchange through its eFiling web-service API and may require digital audit-file submissions, but there is no mandatory country-wide real-time fiscal integration or digital stamping obligation applicable to social casino mobile app receipts.

      
SARS may audit export documentation, foreign-exchange receipts, and electronic service records before allowing any refund. If zero-rating is disallowed or substantiation fails, output VAT at 15% is assessed on the full consideration, together with interest and late-payment penalties. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, an understatement penalty may be imposed up to 150% of the understated tax depending on conduct; deliberate VAT evasion or refund fraud can attract criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment, and the VAT Act authorises additional penalties of up to twice the amount of unpaid tax in certain fraud cases. Zero-rated export refund claims are subject to enhanced pre-refund audit and may be clawed back retrospectively if any required export or service-supply document is missing or inaccurate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The South African rand is freely convertible for current account transactions, including most service royalties, licence fees and trade-related inbound and outbound settled funds, provided supporting documents and tax clearances are lodged with an Authorised Dealer. Capital account movements are subject to exchange control: inward equity subscriptions by non-residents are permitted through the inward transfer route, while outward capital flows, shareholder loans, offshore treasury facilities and certain capital distributions require Authorised Dealer oversight or FinSurv approval above stated thresholds. Profit remittance by way of dividends is permitted if the (Pty) Ltd satisfies solvency and liquidity requirements under the Companies Act 2008, has complied with South African dividends tax obligations, and undertakes the payment via an Authorised Dealer on a standard outward remittance file. A reduction of share capital or winding-up distribution to a non-resident shareholder is treated as a capital outward remittance and attracts additional FinSurv exchange-control documentation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
All export proceeds, including export of software services and digital content via app stores, must be repatriated to a South African bank through an Authorised Dealer. The standard statutory timeline is receipt of payment no later than 180 days from date of shipment or provision of service unless an extension is granted by the Authorised Dealer. There is no partial surrender ratio; 100% of foreign-currency export proceeds must be credited to the exporter's South African account and, unless the exporter holds an approved Foreign Currency Account in terms of the Currency and Exchanges Manual, the foreign currency must be converted into ZAR at the dealing bank's spot rate. Longer or prepayment terms require documentary substantiation and exchange-control approval/reporting to FinSurv.

      

      
Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), with routine administration delegated to Authorised Dealers (South African commercial banks) under the Currency and Exchanges Manual and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Offshore digital EMIs and electronic money institutions are viable only as complementary collection or payout gateways, not as a primary SARB-compliant treasury rail. A South African (Pty) Ltd must maintain its principal ZAR operating account and its inward capital/export receipts through a South African Authorised Dealer. An offshore EMI cannot issue a domestic South African bank account or provide exchange-control records acceptable to FinSurv for capital injections or export surrender. Where the EMI is used for non-resident sales settlement, funds must be swept to the South African bank via SWIFT MT103, and the EMI's KYC/onboarding documentation must be furnished to the Authorised Dealer as part of enhanced due diligence.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Non-resident beneficial ownership chain with nominee directors, indirect trust holdings, or incomplete CIPC beneficial ownership disclosure.
        
Root cause: Non-resident beneficial ownership chain with nominee directors, indirect trust holdings, or incomplete CIPC beneficial ownership disclosure.

        
Defense evidence: Completed Companies and Intellectual Property Commission beneficial ownership filing, formal group structure chart, trust deeds and founder resolutions, certified passports or national identity documents of all natural-person UBOs, OECD tax residency certificates, negative media and sanctions screening, and an attestation from the company secretary that no nominee or bearer shares exist.

      

      
      

        Inward capital subscription from a non-resident shareholder without clear source of wealth, particularly a newly formed entity seeking a large equity or shareholder-loan injection.
        
Root cause: Inward capital subscription from a non-resident shareholder without clear source of wealth, particularly a newly formed entity seeking a large equity or shareholder-loan injection.

        
Defense evidence: Sworn source-of-wealth declaration, antecedent 12-month bank statements of the investor, sale agreements or liquidation reports supporting the funds, corporate registration and existence certificate of the investor, signed board resolutions approving the subscription or loan, and written confirmation that the payment originates from the investor's own non-South African financial institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        All inbound capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's external bank directly into the South African corporate operational or escrow account held at an Authorised Dealer. The remittance narrative should state 'Capital injection for equity subscription, non-resident direct investment, ZA_PTY_LTD'. The Authorised Dealer will assign the appropriate balance-of-payments purpose code for inward direct investment equity, verify that the shareholder is the true remitter, and document the transfer as an inward foreign investment for exchange-control purposes. No prior SARB approval is required for an ordinary equity subscription where the funds come from a non-resident's own account and do not involve a foreign loan that requires FinSurv approval. For shareholder loan funding, the entity must execute a written loan agreement, provide the Authorised Dealer with the loan schedule and board resolution, and comply with any applicable FinSurv notification thresholds when the loan is subsequently serviced or repaid. The bank will convert the foreign currency to ZAR at the prevailing ruling rate unless an approved linked Foreign Currency Account exists.Outward commercial settlement for software licensing, cloud infrastructure, advertising technology, service contractors, and consulting fees must be processed through an Authorised Dealer using SWIFT MT103 with supporting invoices, underlying contracts, tax invoices, and proof of actual service delivery. Physical-goods purchases, if triggered by printed merchandising or hardware distribution, are settled through a commercial documentary letter of credit or SWIFT MT103 with bill-of-lading and insurance documents attached. All outbound payments must name the actual beneficiary receiving the goods or services; no third-party payment instructions are acceptable unless accompanied by true commercial justification and an assignment or collection letter. App-store proceeds from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are repatriated to the South African bank account through the platform's scheduled payout rails, either as foreign-currency wire transfers converted to ZAR or, if platform-local settlement is enabled, as cleared ZAR ACH deposits in South Africa; the entity must reconcile each settlement batch to its export-proceeds surrender records and provide documentary evidence to the Authorised Dealer for annual exchange-control audit purposes.Plain-vanilla Forward Exchange Contracts with an Authorised Dealer for USD/ZAR and EUR/ZAR trade exposures arising from recognised app-store receipts, licensing agreements, and cross-border vendor payments; maturities generally run from 1 to 24 months and must be supported by an underlying commercial contract or forecast cash-flow exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in ZAR pairs are ordinarily not available to South African resident companies because the ZAR is a deliverable currency and an offshore NDF would constitute unauthorised offshore foreign-exchange exposure; if an NDF is proposed, it must be structured only through an Authorised Dealer with FinSurv approval and a documented commercial or investment hedge rationale.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between USD or EUR and ZAR are permissible to hedge principal and interest cash flows of approved foreign-currency funding such as shareholder loans or offshore debt, subject to Authorised Dealer credit approval, FinSurv reporting thresholds, and documentation of the underlying funding instrument.FX swaps may be used for temporary rolling of settlement liquidity between ZAR and foreign currency, provided each leg is closed through the same Authorised Dealer and the economic purpose is treasury liquidity management rather than speculation.FX options, including plain-vanilla calls and puts, are permissible for hedging firm or highly probable contracted foreign-currency receipts and payments when booked through an Authorised Dealer with clearance from the dealer's derivatives desk; exotic structures and uncovered options are not permitted for a resident operating company without FinSurv approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is required where the aggregate gross value of cross-border connected-person transactions exceeds ZAR 100 million in a year of assessment. Master File documentation is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion; Country-by-Country reporting is required where consolidated group revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion.

      
Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation code, but the Income Tax Act contains general anti-avoidance rules (s 80A–80L), controlled foreign company attribution rules, and substance-over-form doctrine. A social casino publisher seeking treaty benefits or deducting royalties to offshore connected entities must demonstrate real functions, employees, decision-making, risk assumption, and financial capacity in South Africa. A shell or mailbox company lacking economic substance will face re-characterisation of transactions, denial of treaty relief, and reattribution of profits to the South African operating entity.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misrepresentation
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, a false or misleading return triggers an understatement penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall, plus fixed administrative penalties up to ZAR 50,000 per month for non-filing. Conviction for tax evasion carries a fine or imprisonment for up to five years, or both, under section 234 of the Tax Administration Act.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently cause the company to submit false returns can be held jointly and severally liable for company tax debts under section 155 of the Tax Administration Act; SARS may also obtain travel bans, restraint orders, and civil restitution of diverted corporate profits.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Abusive IP Royalties
        
Statutory sanction: SARS is empowered under section 31 of the Income Tax Act to adjust the taxable income to the arm's length price, impose secondary adjustments creating a deemed dividend subject to 20% dividends withholding tax, and apply an understatement penalty of 75%–150% of the adjusted shortfall.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation may be pursued personally for the adjusted tax where they acted with gross negligence; personal liability, travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution arise where the documentation is created with intent to mislead SARS.

      

      
      

        Dividend Withholding Tax / Profit Repatriation Failure
        
Statutory sanction: The company as withholding agent is liable for the entire uncollected 20% dividends withholding tax, together with prescribed interest and an understatement penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall; SARS may recover the amount from any person who knowingly assisted in the non-compliance.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable as responsible persons under sections 158–160 of the Tax Administration Act if they fail to take reasonable steps to ensure the company withholds and pays the dividends tax; personal civil liability, travel bans and criminal sanctions may follow.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure / Treaty Shopping
        
Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rules in sections 80A–80L of the Income Tax Act allow SARS to disregard the entire structure, deny treaty benefits, treat the foreign recipient as having a South African permanent establishment, and impose a 150% understatement penalty where the arrangement lacks commercial substance.

        
Director personal liability: Nominee directors and shadow controllers of such shell structures may be held jointly liable for the company's tax liabilities, personal fines, and imprisonment for fraudulent concealment where they knowingly participate in the artificial arrangement.

      

      
      

        AML / KYC Breach in Digital Payment Rails
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, accountable institutions face administrative fines up to ZAR 100 million for contraventions; from a tax perspective, unreported digital receipts may be treated as undeclared income, attracting understatement penalties of up to 150% and interest.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve inadequate AML systems while managing digital payment flows can be held personally liable for penalties arising under FICA and may face imprisonment for up to 15 years where records are falsified to conceal the true origin of virtual slot revenues.
