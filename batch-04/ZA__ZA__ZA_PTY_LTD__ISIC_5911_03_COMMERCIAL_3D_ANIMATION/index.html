Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the local fire authority must issue a fire compliance certificate or occupancy certificate for the building used as a studio under the National Building Regulations and SANS 10400 structural loading standards. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); electronic filing portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs filings via SARS Customs 'Trade, Ports and Borders' e-Clearance.

E-government filing portal

Incorporation of the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) automatically creates the income tax registration and income tax reference number with SARS; a resident public officer must be appointed within one month of incorporation to act for the company. VAT registration is a separate filing: Form VAT101 must be submitted through SARS eFiling or a SARS branch as soon as taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period (voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000 via Form VAT102). Foreign non-resident entities carrying on an enterprise in South Africa apply for an income tax number through the eFiling non-resident registration path (with apostilled constitutional documents, director identifications and a SARS-authorised representative), and must similarly file VAT101 where the enterprise is conducted in South Africa; the 'electronic services' regime (from 1 April 2019) requires foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African recipients to register for VAT where annual taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000. SARS issues separate income tax and VAT registration numbers; returns are subsequently filed on eFiling (ITR14 income tax, VAT201 VAT).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at 27% (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2024; 28% before), with an effective capital gains rate of 18.6% and Dividends Tax at 20% on distributions; a non-resident company carrying on business through a branch or permanent establishment in South Africa is taxed at 27% on South African-source branch income only. Pass-through treatment is not available to companies; the only departure is the Sixth Schedule turnover tax election available to qualifying micro businesses with annual turnover at or below ZAR 1,000,000. For VAT purposes the entity is a standard vendor taxed at 15% separately from income tax. — Incorporation of the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) automatically creates the income tax registration and income tax reference number with SARS; a resident public officer must be appointed within one month of incorporation to act for the company. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at 27% (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2024; 28% before), with an effective capital gains rate of 18.6% and Dividends Tax at 20% on distributions; a non-resident company carrying on business through a branch or permanent establishment in South Africa is taxed at 27% on South African-source branch income only. Pass-through treatment is not available to companies; the only departure is the Sixth Schedule turnover tax election available to qualifying micro businesses with annual turnover at or below ZAR 1,000,000. For VAT purposes the entity is a standard vendor taxed at 15% separately from income tax.

Available election forms

South Africa does not offer a US-style check-the-box entity election; tax classification follows the Companies Act 71 of 2008 legal form. Elective mechanisms available in the domestic code are: (i) the Sixth Schedule turnover tax election for micro businesses (annual turnover ≤ ZAR 1,000,000); (ii) the VAT group registration election under s.50 VAT Act for connected persons; (iii) the headquarter company election under s.9I Income Tax Act where 80% or more of the company's business consists of services/reliefs conferred on foreign shareholders; and (iv) small business corporation (s.12E) status, which applies automatically without election when the qualifying criteria are met. Hybrid entity outcomes are not elective: the hybrid mismatch rules in ss.25D–25I Income Tax Act, effective 1 January 2024, operate mandatorily (OECD BEPS Action 2 ordering, primary rule before defensive rule, with a structured-arrangement carve-out) and override the domestic classification where a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch arises.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration at ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in the past or projected next 12 months; voluntary registration at ZAR 50,000 per annum; foreign suppliers of 'electronic services' to South African recipients register for VAT when annual taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: s.11(1)(a) VAT Act zero-rates a supply where the goods are exported by the supplier, by the recipient, or on behalf of either, within the prescribed period (generally 90 days from the time of supply; SARS may allow an extension) and documentary proof of export is retained. Services: s.11(2)(l) VAT Act zero-rates services supplied by a South African-resident vendor to a non-resident who is not a South African VAT vendor, unless those services are supplied directly in connection with immovable property or movable property situated in South Africa at the time of supply, or are used or consumed in South Africa. Commercial 3D animation / CGI production delivered as digital files and intellectual property licences to non-resident studios, broadcasters or platforms qualifies where the deliverables are exploited and consumed outside South Africa; the vendor bears the onus of proving non-resident status of the recipient (foreign VAT registration or tax residence certificate) and non-South-African consumption.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD500) filed electronically through the SARS Customs 'Trade, Ports and Borders' system (applicable to physical goods; not required for pure digital service deliverables)
  • Bill of Lading or Airwaybill (or equivalent transport document) evidencing export of goods within the prescribed period
  • South African tax/commercial invoice meeting the VAT Act s.20 particulars and citing the applicable zero-rated supply reason (s.11(1)(a) or s.11(2)(l))
  • Packing list, purchase order/sales contract, and intellectual property licence or service delivery schedule covering the animation/VFX deliverables
  • Bank-endorsed export payment proof / Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate issued by an Authorised Dealer evidencing repatriation of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • For services: non-resident status certificate (foreign tax residence or foreign VAT registration certificate) and written recipient confirmation that the services are not used or consumed in South Africa

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping applies. SARS eFiling/e@syFile is the statutory channel for electronic submission of the VAT201 return; SARS Customs 'Trade, Ports and Borders' requires electronic filing of customs SAD500 declarations, and a voluntary 'SARS eInvoicing' pilot based on the Peppol standard was launched in 2024. Tax invoices and credit notes must contain the s.20(4)/(6) VAT Act particulars and be issued within 21 days after the supply date; zero-rated export invoices should cite the zero-rating provision, and vendors must maintain an auditable trail of customs records, foreign exchange receipts and contracts to satisfy SARS's automated matching engines before refunds are paid.

SARS audit and refund-matching engines reconcile zero-rated claims across VAT201 returns, customs declarations and bank foreign-exchange records; where the export proof, non-resident status or non-consumption test fails, the zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back as 15% output VAT, with interest at the SARS prescribed rate under s.187 Tax Administration Act ('TAA') until payment. Under ss.222–223 TAA, understatement penalties of 20% (standard), 50% (failure to take reasonable care or substantial understatement), 75% (unreasonable tax position) or 150% (intentional tax evasion) may be imposed on the shortfall; additional assessments remain open for 5 years, or 10 years in fraud cases. Fraudulent zero-rating also attracts criminal prosecution under s.58 VAT Act and s.234 TAA (fine or imprisonment of up to five years), joint liability of directors in appropriate cases, and an adverse SARS tax-compliance status that can interrupt licensed payment rails and bank relationships.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current-account purposes: service fees, licensing royalties, and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders may be processed by an Authorized Dealer (AD) without prior SARB approval, subject to documentary support (commercial contracts, invoices, tax clearance, and audited evidence of distributable reserves). Capital-account convertibility is controlled: inward equity subscription and any foreign borrowings must be recorded with the AD on the company's Foreign Liability Register within 30 days of inflow to secure future capital-repatriation rights. Because the special-purpose entity is intended to be 100% non-resident owned and to have no local borrowings, the company should apply for 'Foreign Status' via the AD once operational; this designation liberates the balance sheet from routine exchange-control approval for offshore asset purchases, intercompany settlements, and dividend flows, materially reducing profit-remittance friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All proceeds from exported 3D-animation services must be received in convertible currency within 180 days from the date of invoice or service completion, and the full foreign-currency amount must be surrendered to an Authorized Dealer within 30 days of receipt, converting to ZAR at market rates; the mandatory conversion ratio is therefore 100% unless the AD approves a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) for matching offshore expenditure, in which case only the net surplus must be converted within the 30-day window. Failure to repatriate within 180 days requires written explanation and possible FinSurv approval, exposing the company to administrative penalties and loss of favorable exchange-control status.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorized Dealers

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - legally viable as an auxiliary rail, not as a primary treasury anchor. Wise (Wise SA Pty Ltd, FSP 51650) and Payoneer may be used to receive USD/EUR/GBP client settlements and to disburse small-value international contractor payments; however, they cannot register inward FDI debt/equity as a Foreign Liability, cannot perform SARB AD surrender obligations, and do not provide rand clearing through the South African national payment system. The 3D-animation entity should therefore maintain a Tier-1 ZAR current account as the regulatory anchor for exchange-control reporting and treat EMI balances solely as pass-through settlement, mapping each inbound EMI credit to the corresponding commercial invoice to maintain a complete FIC Act audit trail.

Inbound equity injection must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder to the company's designated capital escrow or operational account at the Authorized Dealer, referencing the company registration number and the SARB balance-of-payments purpose code for inward direct investment (equity share subscription). The AD will issue an inward-transfer advice, and the company must complete the Foreign Liability Registration within 30 days, supported by the issued share certificate, updated CIPC share register, board resolution approving the allotment, and the bank's inward-declaration form. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (corporate income tax for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% Dividends Tax, imposed as a final withholding tax on dividends paid by South African resident companies; treaty rates may reduce the rate (commonly 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on shareholding and DTA terms).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Provisional tax: first payment due on or before the last day of the sixth month of the year of assessment; second payment due on or before the last day of the year of assessment; optional third payment due within six months after year-end if the taxpayer's assessed liability exceeds provisional payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local and Master File) is required for cross-border transactions with connected persons where the aggregate annual value of those transactions exceeds ZAR 5 million, unless a specific SARS practice note or rate-based exemption applies. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of ZAR 10 billion or more. Taxpayers must be able to demonstrate arm's-length pricing for intercompany management fees, royalties, interest, and service charges.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute as applied in the UAE. Substance is nonetheless enforced through tax residence rules (incorporation and place of effective management), Section 9D controlled foreign company rules, Section 31 transfer pricing adjustments, and the general anti-avoidance rules in Sections 80A–80L of the Income Tax Act. A shell company with no physical office, no employees, and no genuine commercial activity will not be recognised as the beneficial owner of IP or dividends and may be denied DTA relief, subjected to the full 20% Dividends Tax and 15% royalty WHT, and have its transactions recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of up to 75% of the tax shortfall plus prescribed interest; criminal conviction under Section 235 of the Tax Administration Act may result in a fine of up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years. SARS may also issue an assessment based on its estimated taxable income and attach the company's assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or direct the company to conceal income are personal wrongdoers and can be ordered to pay civil restitution. Under South African law, a court may pierce the corporate veil if the company is used to commit fraud, impose a director's interdict preventing departure from South Africa, and sentence the director to imprisonment in a criminal case.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: SARS may redetermine the taxable income under Section 31 of the Income Tax Act, disallow the deduction, assess the full 27% corporate tax, add prescribed interest, and apply an understatement penalty of up to 75% for gross negligence or intentional tax evasion. A comparable adjustment under the transactional net margin method is common.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's length pricing and signed the tax returns may be personally liable for the company's unpaid tax under Section 77 of the Companies Act and common law negligence. A director can be banned from serving as a director for up to 10 years, subjected to a civil damages order, and prohibited from leaving South Africa if the court issues a travel interdict.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS can refuse treaty benefits for lack of beneficial ownership, invoke the general anti-avoidance rules in Sections 80A–80L, apply the Section 9D CFC attribution regime, and assess full 20% Dividends Tax and 15% royalty/interest WHT. The arrangement may be annulled with retrospective effect and penalties imposed from the original due date.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil where the company is an alter ego used for improper tax advantage. Directors can be held personally liable for misused assets or unpaid tax, restrained by interdict from administering other companies, and in egregious tax fraud cases, prosecuted and sentenced to imprisonment.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Evasion of SARS Documentation

Statutory sanction: Failure to file returns attracts administrative penalties of up to ZAR 250 per day. Knowingly failing to provide documents to SARS is a criminal offence carrying a fine or imprisonment of up to 2 years. Beneficial ownership non-compliance under the Companies Act and FIC Act can attract separate fines and custodial sentences; SARB/AML enforcement may also impose monetary penalties and compliance orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure statutory filings and document production expose themselves to personal liability for penalties, interest, and legal costs. In a court-directed enforcement, directors may be listed as non-compliant persons, placed under travel bans, and, for deliberate concealment or money laundering, imprisoned for up to 5 years under the FIC Act.
