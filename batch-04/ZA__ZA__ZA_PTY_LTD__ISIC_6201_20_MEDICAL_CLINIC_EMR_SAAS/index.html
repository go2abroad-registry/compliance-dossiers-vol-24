Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS). Corporate entity registration is effected through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and linked to SARS via the SARS eFiling portal (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za). SARS head office: 299 Bronkhorst Street, Nieuw Muckleneuk, Pretoria.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd through CIPC (https://www.cipc.co.za) to obtain a Companies Register enterprise number, with a Memorandum of Incorporation and Notice of Incorporation. 2. Register the company for Income Tax with SARS via eFiling (all companies are automatic taxpayers; SARS issues an income tax reference number). Appoint a SARS-registered natural person representative. 3. Register for VAT using SARS form VAT 101 (online via eFiling) once taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (compulsory under s23(1) VAT Act) or from R50,000 (voluntary under s23(3)(b)). 4. If the entity is a non-resident supplier of 'electronic services' to South African residents (as defined by SARS Notice 54 of 2014), apply for simplified VAT registration under the foreign-supplier e-services regime (same R1,000,000 threshold). 5. Clear customs code (client code) with SARS only if goods are imported/exported; not required for pure SaaS export. 7. All registrations and determinations are processed through eFiling; the company must maintain a valid Public Officer or SARS liaison representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A ZA-incorporated (Pty) Ltd is a resident of South Africa (s1 Income Tax Act 58 of 1962) and is taxed at 27% corporate income tax (from years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022). No pass-through treatment is available for private companies; dividends are subject to a 20% Dividends Tax at declaration. Territorial scope: worldwide income for resident companies, with a foreign-tax credit under s6quat; non-resident suppliers are taxed on a source basis (branch profits / PE attribution). The entity is not in a tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box election. Mandatory statutory deeming determines classification. Relevant elections/mechanisms: (i) Deemed non-resident election under s1(3)(b) Income Tax Act for a ZA-incorporated company whose place of effective management and all business operations are in a treaty jurisdiction (requires SARS-directed application and foreign residency certificate). (ii) VAT group election under s50 VAT Act permitting connected companies to be treated as a single VAT vendor (compliant with EU-style grouping; useful for multi-entity SaaS groups). (iii) Small Business Corporation (SBC) election (progressive 0%/7%/21%/27% brackets) if turnover is below R20 million and all shareholders are natural persons. (iv) Hybrid debt/equity recharacterisation rules (ss 8E, 8EA, 8F, 8FA and the anti-arbitrage interest limitation in s23M) operate automatically; no elective filing, but taxpayers can file for a VAT interpretation note / binding ruling (s76–s80 TAA) to confirm hybrid instrument treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 compulsory VAT registration (taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period); voluntary registration from R50,000. For non-resident suppliers of electronic services (SaaS/EMR software) to South African residents: compulsory registration at R1,000,000 (no local physical presence required; simplified e-services registration).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) exported movable goods (s11(1)(a)) and (ii) services supplied to non-residents (s11(2)(l)) if the recipient is a non-resident not present in South Africa at the time of supply and the service is not directly connected with movable or immovable property in South Africa. For the EMR SaaS product sold to a foreign medical clinic: zero-rating is available where the subscription is delivered to the non-resident customer's offshore servers/territory and the customer does not consume the service in South Africa. The supplier must preserve documentary evidence that the customer is a non-resident (foreign tax registration, overseas registered address, contract counterparty details) and that the service was rendered offshore. Caution: 'electronic services' supplied by the South African vendor to South African residents attract 15% VAT; and if the foreign clinic is merely a resident of South Africa (e.g., a local branch), zero-rating fails. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export supplies is creditable, and excess input VAT is refundable via the monthly/bi-monthly VAT 201 with SARS refund verification.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is in force in South Africa. SARS eFiling is the mandatory electronic channel for VAT 201 returns and payments. SARS has published voluntary 'Guidance on e-Invoicing' and is incrementally introducing a structured e-invoicing framework (currently voluntary; not yet a B2B mandate). VAT vendors must issue a full tax invoice containing s20 VAT Act particulars (vendor name, VAT number, serial number, description, consideration, VAT amount) within 21 days of the supply; an abridged invoice is permitted for consideration up to R5,000. Zero-rated export invoices may carry abbreviated details but must evidence the export/foreign-recipient status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a partially convertible exchange control regime. Current account payments are generally freely permitted without approval to the extent evidenced by genuine trade or service transactions, while capital account related transfers, direct foreign investment by residents, and certain offshore treasury operations remain subject to the SARB Exchange Control Manual, the Currency and Exchanges Act, and FinSurv approval thresholds. Profit remittances are permissible through an Authorised Dealer upon submission of the audited or reviewed financial statements proving distributable reserves, payment of the applicable dividends tax or withholding tax, and completion of a Form A.2 or the electronic Foreign Exchange (Forex) system record. Non-resident investors' remittance of the sale or liquidation proceeds is permitted under the 'asset swap' or indirect investment rules, but requires confirmation of no outstanding local liabilities, a tax clearance certificate from the South African Revenue Service (SARS), and the underlying transaction records. Dividends and profits may be transferred in the foreign currency of origin or converted into a freely usable currency by an Authorised Dealer. The ultimate restriction is not absolute inconvertibility, but rather the combination of documentary substantiation, SARS certification, and prudential limits imposed on residents' foreign exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Exchange Control Regulations, outward export proceeds generated by a South African resident must be received in an effective foreign or rand equivalent within 180 days from the date of shipment. Once received in a foreign currency, the exporter must offer the proceeds for conversion or surrender to an Authorised Dealer within 30 days of receipt, unless the exporter has been approved to retain the funds in a designated Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or a foreign asset facility under a specific FinSurv approval. Effective January 1, 2021, the Exchange Control Regulations were amended to remove the so-called 'surrender' obligation for current account receipts in certain formalised scenarios, and the permissibility of retaining export proceeds is dependent upon the exporter's classification as a 'juristic person' with a qualifying FCA, the nature of the underlying export, and the approval terms granted by an Authorised Dealer. The mandatory documented rule as maintained for standard exporters is the 180-day receipt period and the 30-day surrender/conversion requirement from the date of receipt of the export proceeds, subject to the Exporter's Letter of Approval. Software exports and electronic services, including EMR SaaS licenses, are classified as 'invisibles' (services) rather than physical merchandise; the same receipt timeline applies, but the exporter may apply to retain service export receipts in an approved FCA.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), together with the National Treasury under the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated under Section 9 of the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium with substantial legal constraints. South African regulation requires deposit-taking to be performed by a bank licensed under the Banks Act, while an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence does not exist in the South African financial regulatory framework. Offshore digital EMIs and payment service providers (e.g., payment gateways, treasury management platforms, currency brokers) may operate as 'Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange with limited authority' only if licensed by the SARB under the Exchange Control Regulations, or as 'payment institutions' under the Protection of Personal Information Act and the National Payment System Act if they have obtained the relevant registration from the South African Reserve Bank's National Payment System Department. Offshore FinTech treasury platforms generally require a partnership with a licensed South African bank or a licensed authorised dealer to execute currency conversions, cross-border payment instructions, and in-country beneficiary clearings. Unlicensed third-party EMI accounts opened by a non-resident service provider are not permitted to hold funds on behalf of a South African company or to provide in-country account-equivalent services without SARB approval. The practical viability is therefore 'low' for an offshore EMI acting as the principal settlement bank, and 'medium' for a regulated FinTech that uses a local tier-1 bank as the settlement sponsor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inward cross-border capital injections must be transmitted through an Authorised Dealer (a licensed South African bank) by means of an international SWIFT wire instruction. The remitter must clearly designate the beneficiary as 'ZA_PTY_LTD' with the company's local account number, include the purpose code or narrative aligned with SARB Balance of Payments (BOP) classification, commonly BOP code 3320 'Direct Investment: Shares and other capital contributions' or code 3350 'Investment in South African resident enterprise by non-resident investor' where applicable, and provide the inward remittance reference. The bank will require the investor's share subscription agreement, a Resolution of the Board authorising issuance of shares, and the completion of the ISO 20022 purpose coding or the legacy ForeX electronic notification. For funds transmitted as shareholder loans rather than share capital, the purpose narrative must reference 'Non-resident shareholder loan to resident entity' and must comply with the SARB loan-lending reporting requirements. The recipient must not treat offshore funds as revenue or service fees unless the underlying SaaS contracts and SARS-generated tax invoice supports that classification. Verification obligations include obtaining a SWIFT MT103 copy, an Inward Foreign Investment (IFI) notification where applicable, a tax clearance certificate to be submitted at the time of capital account repatriation, and confirmation that the funds are held in a Capital Escrow Account opened with the Authorised Dealer pending allocation to the actual operating account.Outbound treasury settlements for software license fees, supplier subscriptions, cloud infrastructure costs, and vendor payments are cleared through the domestic SWIFT network via an Authorised Dealer. Standard payment rails primarily include regular SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers denominated in the settlement currency, or, where commercial goods or defined trade transactions occur, an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit issued by the domestic bank and advised through a correspondent bank abroad. For operational expenses, the company will use the Authorised Dealer's electronic banking platform to generate MT103 messages with the appropriate settlement purpose code, such as business service payments, royalties, or software license fees. Payment processing must be supported by an underlying invoice, a signed contract, VAT and withholding tax certifications where relevant, and, for all foreign payments an FX approval routing step in the bank's exchange control portal where required. Settlement dates should align with the counterparty's expected clearing time; Standard settlement for MT103 USD transfers is generally one to two business days; ZAR payments are settled on a same-day basis through the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system.
