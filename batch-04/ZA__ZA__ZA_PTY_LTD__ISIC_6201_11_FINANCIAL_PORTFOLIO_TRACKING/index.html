Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must apply for a Statutory Income Tax Reference Number via SARS eFiling, submitting the company registration certificate, completed IT77/IT14 application, proof of physical address, and, where applicable, a SARS-recognized tax representative appointment. Non-resident entities making taxable supplies must separately apply for VAT vendor registration on Form VAT101 (or VAT703 for foreign electronic services).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income as a separate taxpayer; non-resident entities are taxed only on South African-source income; no tax-exempt zone designation applies to ordinary fintech operations. — Foreign entities must apply for a Statutory Income Tax Reference Number via SARS eFiling, submitting the company registration certificate, completed IT77/IT14 application, proof of physical address, and, where applicable, a SARS-recognized tax representative appointment. Non-resident entities making taxable supplies must separately apply for VAT vendor registration on Form VAT101 (or VAT703 for foreign electronic services).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income as a separate taxpayer; non-resident entities are taxed only on South African-source income; no tax-exempt zone designation applies to ordinary fintech operations.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available elections include: (i) CFC high-tax exemption election under s9D; (ii) corporate reorganisation rollover elections under s45/s46; (iii) hybrid mismatch ordering/elective carve-outs under s25L and s23H; (iv) group loss-transfer elections under s45; and (v) doubtful-debt deduction elections under s11A.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 actual or expected annual taxable turnover for compulsory registration; R50,000 voluntary registration threshold; separate R1,000,000 threshold for non-resident suppliers of electronic services under s23A.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under s11(1)(a) for goods physically exported from South Africa by the vendor or on its behalf within 90 days of supply, and under s11(2)(k)/l for services physically rendered outside South Africa to non-residents, including cloud-based financial portfolio tracking and related software-as-a-service, provided documentary proof of export or offshore service delivery is retained.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of lading / airway waybill or motor transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank proof of foreign-currency receipt
  • Commercial invoice and signed contract/purchase order
  • SARS-issued export certificate or vendor's own export record where applicable

SARS does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of invoices. VAT vendors must issue and retain authentic electronic invoices that satisfy audit-trail and control requirements. SARS has issued a draft e-invoicing framework and accepts e-invoicing via eFiling/secure data interchange, but there is no API-based live clearance mandate in force.

SARS audits export documentation, physical movement, and the 180-day foreign-currency repatriation requirement. Unsupported zero-rating is reversed with additional VAT, interest, and understatement penalties up to 125% of the shortfall under s223 of the Tax Administration Act; fraudulent export claims attract penalties up to 150%, potential criminal prosecution, and possible deregistration of VAT vendor status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand is freely convertible for current-account transactions, including trade settlement, service fees, royalties, dividends, and interest. Capital-account flows remain subject to exchange-control oversight administered by SARB FinSurv. Non-resident direct equity investment may be credited to a domestic ZAR account as foreign direct investment once reported through an authorised dealer; however, outbound loan redemptions, guarantees, share buy-backs, capital repatriations, and upward revaluation of non-resident loan accounts are restricted and require approval. Profit remittance by dividend is permitted from distributable reserves but must comply with dividends tax and SARS tax-clearance protocols, and must not constitute a prohibited loan to shareholders or related parties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export and foreign-earnings proceeds must be received in South Africa within 30 calendar days of the relevant shipment or service-rendering date under the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual. There is no partial conversion quota; the requirement is full repatriation. If an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) is not held, 100% of the proceeds must be converted to ZAR through an authorised dealer immediately on receipt. Any FCA retention must be approved by FinSurv and supported by export documentation (customs SAD500 for goods, valid commercial/service invoices for software and fintech subscription income).

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-low. Offshore digital EMIs are viable only as secondary inbound collection/settlement gateways, not as primary treasury accounts. Any entity accepting deposits or holding customer funds in South Africa requires a licence under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 or an exemption under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998. An offshore EMI cannot provide a local ZAR clearing account directly; proceeds must be settled onward into a domestic account maintained at a SARB authorised dealer. Legal constraints include SARB National Payment System approval, Payments Association of South Africa clearing rules, and the prohibition on unauthorised deposit-taking. The intended Stripe and Apple IAP rails should be licensed PSP channels feeding a domestic ZAR collection account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital must be routed through a SARB authorised dealer using a SWIFT MT103 wire. The beneficiary field must state the exact legal name of the (Pty) Ltd, the domestic ZAR account number, the receiving bank BIC, and the clear narrative: 'Inward FDI equity subscription — issue of ordinary shares'. The sending bank must be the same institution holding the shareholder‘s account to preserve the audit trail. The authorised dealer will settle the transfer into ZAR and report the transaction under the SARB Balance-of-Payments classification for foreign direct investment equity. Supporting documentation must include the board resolution, share subscription agreement, source-of-funds bank statements, share certificates, CIPC return, and a FinSurv-compliant inward FDI notification where required. Do not split the transfer or use ambiguous purpose narratives such as 'family remittance' or 'consulting fee'.Outbound treasury settlements for infrastructure, licensing, and partner pay-outs use the local electronic clearing system for ZAR legacy payments and SWIFT MT103 for foreign-currency disbursements. A local ZAR vendor payment proceeds through authenticated online banking with an underlying invoice and SARS tax clearance where a non-resident is paid. For actual foreign-currency settlement, the company submits a SWIFT MT103 instruction to the authorised dealer with the commercial invoice, proof of service delivery, and the correct SARB BOP purpose code. Payment must be made from an approved FCA or by spot purchase of the currency. For physical-goods trade only, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit may be used, stating Incoterms 2020 and matching the underlying shipment documents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 10 million for Local File per cross-border related-party transaction category (goods, services, intellectual property, financing); Master File required where consolidated group revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion or total cross-border related-party transactions exceed ZAR 100 million.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in the UAE. However, the Income Tax Act, CFC rules, and the common-law substance-over-form doctrine require real commercial activity, decision-making, and operational substance for tax residence, treaty eligibility, and transfer pricing compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s234 of the Tax Administration Act: fine up to ZAR 2 million, imprisonment up to 10 years, or both. Additionally, an understatement penalty up to 120% of the tax shortfall plus interest on late payment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate, assist, or benefit from evasion may be held jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt under s163, face SARS-imposed travel bans, and be personally prosecuted.
Shell Company / Reportable Tax Structure

Statutory sanction: SARS may recharacterise the entity as lacking substance; deny treaty benefits, impose CFC imputation on beneficial owners, and levy understatement penalties up to 100% of the shortfall plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in establishing a non-substantive structure without reporting it as a reportable arrangement may incur personal penalties under s167 TAA, be prohibited from leaving South Africa, and be subject to clawback claims from unpaid tax.
Money Laundering / AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, the accountable institution may face a fine up to ZAR 10 million per contravention; repeat non-compliance can lead to licence suspension and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors may be fined up to ZAR 10 million or imprisoned for up to 5 years for repeated violations; courts may order personal restitution of profits and issue interdicts preventing asset disposal.
