Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at www.sarsefiling.co.za; combined administration of income tax, VAT, customs and excise under the Tax Administration Act No. 28 of 2011.

E-government filing portal

A standard (Pty) Ltd first incorporates with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and receives its company registration number. CIPC transmits incorporation data to SARS; within 21 business days after incorporation the entity must complete income tax registration via SARS eFiling to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number. VAT registration (Form VAT 101) must be lodged within 21 days after exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in annual taxable supplies; voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000. The ODC must also appoint a SARS-approved public officer (resident individual), register for PAYE/UIF/COIDA for South African employees, and file the VAT 201 returns electronically through eFiling.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A standard (Pty) Ltd first incorporates with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and receives its company registration number. CIPC transmits incorporation data to SARS; within 21 business days after incorporation the entity must complete income tax registration via SARS eFiling to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number. VAT registration (Form VAT 101) must be lodged within 21 days after exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in annual taxable supplies; voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000. The ODC must also appoint a SARS-approved public officer (resident individual), register for PAYE/UIF/COIDA for South African employees, and file the VAT 201 returns electronically through eFiling.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in South African law. The (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act No. 58 of 1962 at 27% for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2023. Hybrid mismatch rules effective 1 January 2024 apply mandatorily where the offshore parent treats the South African entity as transparent or where deductible payments create mismatches; these rules operate by statutory overriding rather than elective election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration at ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies per 12-month period; voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000; registration must occur within 21 days of crossing the compulsory threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under s11(1)(a) where the supplier exports the goods and holds documentary proof that the goods were exported within 90 days of the supply; a longer 12-month window is available with ad hoc output VAT accounting adjustments. Services: zero-rated under s11(2)(l) where the services (including offshore software development and maintenance) are supplied to a non-resident who is not present in South Africa at the time the services are rendered, the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or movable property situated in South Africa, and the non-resident does not acquire the services for the benefit of a South African resident. For the ODC, the contract must clearly evidence a direct taxable supply to the foreign parent entity and be supported by contemporaneous service delivery documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged with SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in South Africa
  • Service agreement and delivery evidence confirming the non-resident recipient (for software services)

South Africa does not operate a real-time invoice-level fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate comparable to Latin American regimes. SARS eFiling is the statutory channel for VAT 201 returns and customs declarations. An e-invoicing framework is being phased in through SARS interoperability and API specifications for accredited e-invoicing software providers; invoices must still satisfy all particulars required under s20 of the VAT Act (supplier name and VAT number, invoice number, date, description of services, consideration). Records must be retained for 5 years and produced electronically for SARS audits.

SARS may disallow the zero rating and assess output VAT at 15% where the documentary proof bundle is incomplete, the 90-day export timing is missed, or the service recipient is not validly a non-resident. Interest is levied on underpayments under s187 of the Tax Administration Act, and understatement penalties under s223 range from 10% for substantial understatement up to 200% of the shortfall in repeat cases of intentional tax evasion; fraudulent export claims may also attract criminal prosecution and director liability. The ODC should retain complete export files, FX receipts, and service contracts for the statutory 5-year retention period to withstand a VAT audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa classifies the rand as a non-internationalised but fully convertible currency for current account purposes. Current account transactions, including software service exports and dividend remittances, may be settled through SARB Authorised Dealers without prior SARB approval, subject to documentary compliance. Capital account transactions, specifically foreign direct investment inward equity injections, are permitted without individual approval when routed through an Authorised Dealer, but involve formal reporting (BI115 FDI declaration). Profit remittance is permitted provided the company is tax-compliant, the dividend is declared from distributable reserves, and SARS withholding tax on dividends is paid. Non-resident lending and equity repatriation triggers stricter scrutiny and requires Authorised Dealer approval unless covered by a general permission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Exchange Control Manual and Currency and Exchanges Act, export proceeds in respect of services (including software engineering and maintenance fees) must be received in South Africa within 30 days from the date of performance or completion of the service. Upon receipt, the company must offer, without delay, the foreign currency to an Authorised Dealer for conversion into rand within 30 days of receipt; holding earned foreign currency beyond that period is prohibited without an explicit FinSurv exemption. No mandatory conversion ratio is prescribed because the surrender obligation is effectively 100% for export receipts held in foreign currency accounts, unless the Authorised Dealer has granted a specific approval to retain funds offshore or in a foreign currency account for defined working capital purposes.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the designated South African Exchange Control Authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a primary settlement or clearing entity, Medium as an auxiliary payment collection rail. Wise B2B and Payoneer may be used to receive foreign-currency client payments and to pay contractors offshore, but they are not licensed Authorised Dealers under the South African Bank Act and cannot lawfully maintain ZAR settlement accounts or perform exchange control duties. SARB and FinSurv require all ZAR-denominated resident clearing, statutory capital injection, and tax payments to flow through a domestic Authorised Dealer bank account. An ODC entity should use EMIs solely for cross-border income aggregation and invoice matching, with same-day or scheduled sweeps into the primary ZAR bank account to avoid non-resident money transmission regulatory exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inward capital injection must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for aggregate settlement) from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the South African company's account at a SARB Authorised Dealer. The payment must bear a purpose code covering 'Foreign Direct Investment / Equity Subscription' and should be accompanied by the company's acceptance of the BI115 inward investment reporting form. The Authorised Dealer will verify the non-resident source of the funds, confirm the corporate resolution of directors authorising share issuance, and issue an inward transfer record. The company must not credit shareholder current account or loan proceeds without prior Authorised Dealer approval. The same channel is used for subsequent capital increases; non-resident funding in kind or intellectual property contributions require additional SARB FinSurv authorisation and are outside the standard MT103 road.Outbound clearing for trade settlements is executed primarily by SWIFT MT103 for service payments and for but not limited to licensing, subcontracting, and repatriation of retained earnings. Commercial letters of credit are not relevant for ODC service exports but may apply to any bundled hardware sales; otherwise the standard clearing rail is cross-border electronic transfer against a service invoice and execution proof. Dividend outbound remittance proceeds via the Authorised Dealer using a Form A or the bank's authenticated internet banking channel, supported by an ordinary resolution, a solvency and liquidity test statement under the Companies Act, and proof of payment of dividend withholding tax to SARS (SARS electronic payment reference). Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional payment is due on the last day of the sixth month of the year of assessment; the second provisional payment is due on the last day of the year of assessment; the optional third top-up payment is due on the last day of the month immediately following the end of the year of assessment to avoid or reduce interest.

Deadline: · Frequency:

If no express notice is issued, the return must be filed within 12 months after the end of the year of assessment; where SARS issues a notice under Section 66(14) of the Income Tax Act, the return must be filed within 60 days of the date of the notice. SARS eFiling assigns an annual filing due date based on the company's registered financial year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Part A - Taxpayer Identification SARS tax reference number, entity name, and year of assessment for the provisional period. Non-resident companies with a South African permanent establishment must use the branch tax reference number and identify the South African registered business address. Underpayment of provisional tax triggers interest at the prescribed rate (currently 10.5% per annum) and, in flagrant cases, an administrative non-compliance penalty of up to 25% of the shortfall.
Part B - Estimated Taxable Income Reasonable estimate of taxable income for the full year of assessment based upon information available at the provisional payment date. Only income attributable to the South African permanent establishment should be included; treaty profit attribution principles apply. Underpayment of provisional tax triggers interest at the prescribed rate (currently 10.5% per annum) and, in flagrant cases, an administrative non-compliance penalty of up to 25% of the shortfall.
Part C - Provisional Tax Credits Prior provisional payments made for the same year of assessment and any withholding tax credits that are claimable. Foreign tax credits cannot be claimed on IRP6 unless they relate to taxes withheld on South African-source income attributable to the permanent establishment. Underpayment of provisional tax triggers interest at the prescribed rate (currently 10.5% per annum) and, in flagrant cases, an administrative non-compliance penalty of up to 25% of the shortfall.
Section A - Company and Registration Details Legal name, registration number, SARS tax reference number, public officer details, address, and principal trading activity (ISIC code). Non-resident companies identify themselves as foreign incorporated entities carrying on business through a South African branch; include the SA branch SARS number and foreign tax identification number. Late filing attracts an administrative non-compliance penalty equivalent to 25% of assessed tax for material defaults, or a monthly flat penalty up to ZAR 16,000 where no tax is payable; interest runs on unpaid tax from the effective due date until settlement.
Section B - Income Gross income, exempt income, gross profit, and other sundry amounts subject to income tax. Only South African-source amounts attributable to the permanent establishment are included; foreign-source passive income is not returned unless effectively connected to the SA trade. Late filing attracts an administrative non-compliance penalty equivalent to 25% of assessed tax for material defaults, or a monthly flat penalty up to ZAR 16,000 where no tax is payable; interest runs on unpaid tax from the effective due date until settlement.
Section C - Deductions and Allowances Expenditure incurred in the production of taxable income, capital allowances, R&D deductions, and bad debts. Deductions must be allocated to the permanent establishment using the arm's length 'separate enterprise' approach; head-office intra-group charges must be supported by a transfer pricing file. Late filing attracts an administrative non-compliance penalty equivalent to 25% of assessed tax for material defaults, or a monthly flat penalty up to ZAR 16,000 where no tax is payable; interest runs on unpaid tax from the effective due date until settlement.
Section D - Assessed Loss Opening assessed loss and current-year loss to be carried forward; apply the 80% annual utilisation cap where relevant. A non-resident branch may utilise assessed losses only against its South African permanent establishment taxable income; losses from foreign operations are not deductible. Late filing attracts an administrative non-compliance penalty equivalent to 25% of assessed tax for material defaults, or a monthly flat penalty up to ZAR 16,000 where no tax is payable; interest runs on unpaid tax from the effective due date until settlement.
Section E - Tax Calculation Taxable income multiplied by 27%, with credits for provisional tax paid, withholding taxes, and foreign tax credits. Provisional credits paid under the IRP6 are applied; dividend tax credits are refundable only to the company as shareholder, not to the foreign principal. Late filing attracts an administrative non-compliance penalty equivalent to 25% of assessed tax for material defaults, or a monthly flat penalty up to ZAR 16,000 where no tax is payable; interest runs on unpaid tax from the effective due date until settlement.
Section F - Additional Schedules Transfer pricing disclosure information, cross-border payments, and any elections or treaty positions. Complete the transfer pricing schedule for all cross-border connected transactions; attach a Country-by-Country notification where group thresholds are met. Late filing attracts an administrative non-compliance penalty equivalent to 25% of assessed tax for material defaults, or a monthly flat penalty up to ZAR 16,000 where no tax is payable; interest runs on unpaid tax from the effective due date until settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South African transfer pricing law does not provide a general de-minimis threshold for controlled cross-border transactions. SARS expects contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation for all material connected-person transactions; a strict administrative threshold of ZAR 5,000,000 per transaction class is applied for low-risk service arrangements. Mandatory Country-by-Country reporting filing applies to MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute of the Gulf-Cooperation-Council type, but substance is a decisive factor for transfer pricing and permanent establishment attribution. A pure offshore development centre lacking board control, risk management, core business functions, employees, and premises may be re-characterised as a dependent-agent permanent establishment of the foreign principal under the Income Tax Act and the applicable DTA, with profits attributed to the foreign head office and deductions disallowed. The general anti-avoidance provisions (sections 80A-80T) and common-law substance-over-form also disregard artificial booking arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Adjustment and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts taxable income to an arm's length benchmark under Section 31 of the Income Tax Act, imposes an understatement penalty of up to 125% of the adjusted shortfall (or 75% for gross negligence and 50% for substantial misstatement), and charges interest at the prescribed rate on the additional assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the preparation of false transfer pricing documentation may be personally assessed for the tax debt under Section 185 of the Tax Administration Act; the corporate veil may be pierced on grounds of fraudulent trading, exposing directors to restitution of the underpaid tax, travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution resulting in imprisonment of up to three years.
Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to double the amount evaded under Section 104 of the Income Tax Act; criminal conviction for fraud and tax evasion may result in a fine of up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment for up to ten years under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act read with the Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Every director and the public officer may be charged where the offence is attributed to the company; courts may impose joint and several civil liability for the evaded tax, confiscation orders, and a direct personal imprisonment sentence. SARS may obtain a foreign-departure prohibition order preventing directors from leaving South Africa pending settlement.
Shell Conduit / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS disregards the legal form under the general anti-avoidance rules, deems a permanent establishment of the foreign principal to exist in South Africa, denies all intra-group deductions, and assesses the foreign principal's SA-source profits at 27%. Penalties include administrative non-compliance penalties of up to ZAR 1 million for failure to keep adequate records and understatement penalties on tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham company may be treated as alter egos; courts may pierce the veil and order personal indemnification for company tax liabilities. Directors who sign false solvency or corporate authority documents face disqualification under the Companies Act and criminal liability for fraudulent trading.
Dividend Tax / Withholding Tax Compliance

Statutory sanction: The company is held liable for the full 20% unpaid dividend tax, together with interest, and a potential administrative penalty of up to 25% of the unpaid amount where the failure is attributable to gross negligence or wilful non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The public officer and responsible directors may be held jointly and severally liable for dividend tax not withheld, if it is proved they participated in the decision to pay the dividend without compliance; criminal enforcement may lead to financial penalties and imprisonment of up to five years.
