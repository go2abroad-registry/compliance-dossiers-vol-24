Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; principal website https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A South African (Pty) Ltd is registered with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), and SARS automatically links the CIPC registration to obtain an income tax reference number. Foreign branch or non-resident taxpayers must register with SARS via eFiling, providing incorporation/foreign registration documents, an appointed local representative, and a physical South African address. VAT registration is obtained separately through SARS eFiling using the VAT 101 (vendors) or applicable non-resident electronic services registration channel; the compulsory threshold is taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in a consecutive 12-month period, with voluntary registration available where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in the previous 12 months.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A South African registered (Pty) Ltd is a tax resident and a separate taxable 'person'; no pass-through is available. It is taxed at the corporate rate on worldwide income. A foreign-owned subsidiary similarly has no pass-through status; a foreign company branch is taxed on South African-source income. — A South African (Pty) Ltd is registered with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), and SARS automatically links the CIPC registration to obtain an income tax reference number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A South African registered (Pty) Ltd is a tax resident and a separate taxable 'person'; no pass-through is available. It is taxed at the corporate rate on worldwide income. A foreign-owned subsidiary similarly has no pass-through status; a foreign company branch is taxed on South African-source income.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists under South African law. Entity classification is fully statutory. Available elections include VAT grouping for closely connected companies under Section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act and certain filing exception elections; hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory and cannot be bypassed by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory threshold over any consecutive 12-month period; ZAR 50,000 voluntary registration threshold for resident vendors; non-resident electronic services suppliers are subject to separate SARS registration requirements under the VAT Act electronic services provisions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export goods zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident and removed from South Africa within the prescribed timeframe, with proper documentary proof of export. Export services zero-rated under Section 11(2) of the VAT Act where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time of supply, provided the services are not directly connected with South African land or moveable property retained in South Africa; the services must be performed outside South Africa or supplied for export use, subject to SARS conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) or digital SARS Customs declaration evidencing physical export clearance
  • Commercial invoice and export/import contract (if applicable), including IRS or client-specific service agreement for zero-rated services
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / carrier or freight-forwarder proof of removal from South Africa for goods; for services, signed affidavit or service performance evidence outside South Africa
  • Bank or authorised-dealer Inward/Outward Remittance Certificate, foreign currency receipt proof, or SWIFT/electronic transfer evidence showing overseas customer payment in foreign currency

South Africa does not currently operate a real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate. VAT-compliant invoices must meet SARS section 20 requirements, including full vendor name, VAT registration number, invoice date, description, quantity, unit price, and VAT amount. Invoices may be issued electronically, and VAT returns are filed online through SARS eFiling, with electronic data submissions; no separate government clearance API or fiscal stamp is required.

SARS may deny zero-rating retroactively if the statutory proof-of-export or service-export requirements are not fully maintained. Assessments can be reopened for up to five years (and longer in cases of fraud or non-disclosure). Understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 range from 10% to 200% of the shortfall, with additional interest charges. Wilful fraudulent zero-rating claims attract criminal liability, fines, possible imprisonment, and statutory additional tax including full repayment of VAT previously credited or refunded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a free-floating, fully convertible currency for current-account transactions, including export of services and profit/dividend remittances by non-resident shareholders, subject to normal authorised-dealer reporting and SARS tax-clearance verification. Capital-account transactions such as inbound equity subscriptions and shareholder loans require SARB FinSurv compliance via an authorised dealer, but no broad convertibility blockage exists for a legitimate services exporter. Profit remittance is permitted on proof of taxed profits, withholding tax compliance, and standard Anti-Money Laundering/Counter-Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

100% of service export proceeds (including ASO/SEO localization fees) must be repatriated to South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days after the service completion date or invoice date. No fixed statutory conversion ratio into ZAR is imposed; the exporter may credit proceeds to a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) maintained with an authorised dealer, and any conversion to ZAR must occur at the prevailing arm's-length market rate. Late repatriation or failure to surrender constitutes an exchange-control offence.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933, and the Exchange Control Rulings.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a supplementary collection rail, but not as primary treasury infrastructure. Wise B2B and Payoneer are non-bank payment facilitators and are not SARB-licensed authorised dealers. They may be used for low-value onboarding of clients via card/ACH, but all export proceeds must ultimately be routed into a South African authorised-dealer banking relationship within the 180-day surrender window. Holding funds in such EMIs for extended periods could be treated as unauthorised offshore accounts; therefore, a weekly or bi-monthly sweep to the local operational account is mandatory under a prudent treasury policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity capital must be introduced via a SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's own banking relationship, referencing the share subscription agreement and using the bank's prescribed inward investment purpose code (typically 'EQUITY CAPITAL' or 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION'). The local authorised dealer will log the inward transfer, issue a foreign-exchange conversion certificate where the funds are converted to ZAR, and create an SARB-control reference number for the facility. All inward remittance documents, including the bank's 'Inward Notification' letter and the capital-account schedule, must be retained for compliance reviews and future dividend-repatriation justification.Service-related disbursements to foreign subcontractors, freelancers, and technology vendors should be executed by SWIFT MT103 wires routed through the company's authorised dealer, supported by the underlying invoice, approved purchase order, and internal treasury authorisation in accordance with the four-eyes principle. For large, time-certain transactions, the company may utilise a commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) through its trade-finance desk; however, given the intangible-services nature of ASO/SEO operations, MT103s with full supporting documentation are the primary clearing rail. All foreign-currency purchases must be transacted on a deliverable basis via the local bank, and no payments may be made to sanctioned jurisdictions without explicit FinSurv and OFAC-comparative screening.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) with a SARB-authorized dealer for hedging fixed ZAR/USD or ZAR/EUR receivable/payable flows arising from service contracts.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for economically hedging the interest and principal on long-term shareholder loans or intercompany funding.Plain-vanilla FX options, including zero-cost collars, used to protect profit margins on non-ZAR invoice cash flows.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for onshore resident entities without an express FinSurv approval; therefore, treasury policy should rely exclusively on deliverable instruments transacted with local authorised dealers.
