Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS). Central portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs and excise through the SARS Customs Division; company co-registration interface with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A (Pty) Ltd is a South African tax resident by incorporation (s 1, Income Tax Act 58 of 1962) and is taxed on worldwide income at the corporate rate of 27% (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023). No pass-through status is available; dividends are subject to 20% dividends tax and interest to a foreign lender to 15% interest withholding tax (treaty-reduced). Territorial scope: worldwide taxation applies, but qualifying international shipping income of a South African shipowner, charterer or operator may be exempt under s 8B or taxed on notional tonnage under the s 8(6A) tonnage-tax election for qualifying ships (generally exceeding 100 gross tons).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; a resident company is mandatorily taxed as a taxable person. Relevant elective regimes: (i) s 8(6A) tonnage-tax election for qualifying international shipping/charter operations, irrevocable for ten years; (ii) s 12E small-business corporation election where all shareholders are natural persons and gross income limits are met; (iii) group-of-companies rollover elections under ss 41-47, including s 45 intra-group asset rollover relief; (iv) CFC high-tax exclusion election under s 9D(9) for foreign subsidiaries; and (v) mandatory rather than elective hybrid mismatch rules under ss 8F, 8FA, and 23N.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (s 23(1) VAT Act). Voluntary registration: taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12 months (s 24(1)(b) VAT Act). Non-resident suppliers of electronic services register without a threshold once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 (s 23A VAT Act).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: s 11(1)(a) VAT Act zero-rates goods supplied to a non-resident and exported within 90 days from the time of supply (12 months if the supplier proves export). Leased/offshore vessels: s 11(1)(l) zero-rates the lease of movable property (bareboat charter) where the lessee is a non-resident and the vessel is used exclusively outside South Africa, provided the vessel is exported within the statutory period. Transport services: s 11(1)(c) zero-rates the supply of services for the transport of goods to a destination outside South Africa, covering time charters and affreightment of offshore supply vessels performing international voyages; ancillary services rendered to non-residents and effectively used outside South Africa may zero-rate under s 11(2)(e)/(k). The vendor must hold documentary proof of non-resident status of the charterer, exclusive foreign use or export of the vessel, and receipt of the foreign-currency consideration.

  • SAD 500 customs export declaration (SARS Customs)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a South African Authorised Dealer
  • Signed charter-party or bareboat lease agreement
  • Commercial invoice or pro-forma invoice
  • VAT 201 return with the zero-rated supplies box and reconciliation evidence
  • Voyage log and AIS/GPS position records demonstrating use beyond South African territorial waters

No real-time fiscal-clearance or mandatory digital-stamping regime comparable to Latin American e-invoicing. Tax invoices must contain the s 20 VAT Act particulars, be issued within 21 days of the time of supply, and be retained in an electronic format acceptable to SARS for five years. SARS provides the voluntary SARS eInvoicing platform using an XML-based e-invoice schema, with large vendors progressively on-boarded through the Electronic Filing and Data Management System (EFDMS); export zero-ratings are reconciled electronically via the VAT 201 return and the customs SAD 500 declaration.

SARS matches VAT 201 zero-rated outputs against SAD 500 export data and may suspend refunds under s 44(3A) VAT Act for verification. If zero-rating is disallowed, VAT and statutory interest are clawed back; s 39 VAT Act additionally permits 'additional tax' of up to 200% of the tax evaded. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties scale from 10% (substantial understatement) to 150% (intentional tax evasion), plus s 187 interest; criminal prosecution under the TAA may result in a fine of up to ZAR 2,000,000 and/or five years' imprisonment. Exchange-control non-compliance (failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days) triggers separate financial surveillance penalties imposed by the SARB through the Authorised Dealer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is a managed-float currency with positive convertibility on the current account for bona fide trade, service, and dividend remittances, subject to documentary verification. Capital-account convertibility is conditional: inward direct investment is freely permitted via Authorised Dealers without prior SARB approval for equity participation up to 100% of a private company; outward portfolio and other capital outflows require FinSurv clearance. Profit remittance (dividends to non-resident shareholders) is permitted without prior approval provided it is declared as a bona fide dividend out of trading profits, reported on the CMF, and subjected to 20% dividends withholding tax (or a reduced treaty rate). The main practical hurdle is evidentiary rather than statutory: the remitter must file a SARS declaration, the tax clearance status of the entity, and the board resolution evidencing the dividend declaration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Charter-hire proceeds for offshore marine services rendered to non-residents constitute an export of services. Such foreign currency receipts must be brought to rest in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the invoice date or the date of completion of the charter service, whichever is earlier, unless FinSurv grants an extension. No mandatory conversion ratio applies: exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds in a designated Foreign Currency Account (FCA) and convert to rand only as liquidity needs require; however, full traceability of the holding in the FCA and proof of the underlying charter party must be maintained for FinSurv audit. Failure to repatriate within the statutory period exposes the entity to exchange-control penalties and adverse Authorised Dealer treatment.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), exercising exchange control through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations issued thereunder, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual. Day-to-day administration is delegated to Authorised Dealers (commercial banks) and Authorised Dealers with Limited Authority; all cross-border capital transactions and export/import proceeds reporting are executed through the Authorised Dealer channel and lodged on the prescribed Cross-border Mandate Form (CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium for use as the primary treasury vehicle; Medium as an ancillary collection instrument. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer) are not recognised as Authorised Dealers under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, cannot hold SARB-designated Foreign Currency Accounts, cannot file CMF declarations, and cannot provide exchange-control custody. A fintech EMI may lawfully be used only to receive charter-hire remittances in USD/EUR where the receivable is subsequently swept to the South African Authorised Dealer bank within the 180-day repatriation window; the EMI must have a valid South African payment partnership and the funds must ultimately pass through the domestic banking system to satisfy export-proceeds rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI equity capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas shareholder to the Authorised Dealer's nostro account, accompanied by a fully completed CMF declaring the nature of payment as inward direct investment / subscription to share capital in a private South African company. The Authorised Dealer verifies the CIPC certificate, share register, and board resolution; no prior FinSurv approval is required for equity participation up to 100% in a private company. Upon credits, the bank issues a Certificate of Receipt evidencing the exchange-control inward investment for subsequent outward guarantees or dividend remittances. For statutory escrow tranches, the funds are parked in a ring-fenced USD Capital Escrow Account and released to the operating account only upon completion of the SAMSA licence verification and the closing conditions of the charter contract.Charter-hire settlements to non-resident vessel owners, subcontractors, and equipment lessors are executed via SWIFT MT103 from the USD FCA or ZAR holding account using the applicable maritime nature-of-payment code for vessel charter hire, supported by the signed charter party, hire settlement statement, and voyage invoice. Domestic operating expenditure (crew payroll, port charges, fuel procurement within South Africa) clears through the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system using ZAR electronic funds transfers. Cross-border procurement of bunker fuel, lubricants, and offshore equipment may be structured either on open account with SWIFT settlement, or through Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued by the Authorised Dealer backed by the charter-party receivables; standby LCs are used to collateralise performance obligations under the vessel management agreements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5 million in covered 'affected transactions' (cross-border connected-party dealings) triggers Local File documentation; a Master File is generally expected where the multinational group has consolidated turnover above ZAR 1 billion or affected transactions exceed ZAR 50 million; Country-by-Country Reporting applies when consolidated group revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the SARS exercises substantial economic-substance review through the 'place of effective management' test, substance-over-form principles, the general anti-avoidance rule in section 103(1), controlled foreign company attribution in section 9D, and OECD BEPS anti-circumvention. The vessel owner/lessor must demonstrate real SA decision-making, personnel, maintenance capability and retained risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declarations

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of up to 200%, prescribed-rate interest, administrative penalties for late filing/non-registration, and criminal indictment leading to fines and custodial sentences where a return is false under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: SARS may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors jointly and severally liable for tax debts where they knowingly used the Pty Ltd as a shield; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment, director disqualification, travel bans and attachment of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Non-Documented Intercompany Rents

Statutory sanction: SARS makes an arm's length primary adjustment, may impute secondary adjustments, disallow deductions, and apply understatement penalties ranging from 10% to 200% plus interest; documents may be subpoenaed and contemporaneous evidence required.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false intercompany leases and TP reports may be pursued in civil recovery; where their conduct is grossly negligent, they can be made personally responsible for the company's shortfall and face criminal sanction.
Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: The arrangement may be recharacterised or disregarded under section 103(1) GAAR, tax benefits reversed, CFC thin capitalisation rules applied, and penalties imposed for absence of substance / failure to report beneficial ownership to the FIC.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners face personal liability for reckless trading, coercive SARS debt recovery, travel restrictions, anti-money laundering penalties and possible sequestration or imprisonment.
AML/CFT and Cross-Border Payment Violations

Statutory sanction: FIC administrative penalties up to ZAR 10 million, criminal money-laundering prosecution, SARS asset restraint orders, and forfeiture of profits; failure to report suspicious transactions can constitute a separate offence.

Director personal liability: Individuals can be personally fined and imprisoned, placed under travel bans, and have their personal assets restrained under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act where they were knowingly concerned in a laundering transaction.
