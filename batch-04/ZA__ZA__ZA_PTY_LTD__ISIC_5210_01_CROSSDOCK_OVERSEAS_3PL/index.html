Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), an autonomous agency established under the South African Revenue Service Act 34 of 1997, operating eFiling at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za and the corporate portal at https://www.sars.gov.za; customs and excise declarations are lodged through the SARS Customs Trader Portal.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic (Pty) Ltd: (1) incorporate via CIPC under the Companies Act 71 of 2008, which issues the company registration/enterprise number; (2) rely on the automatic CIPC–SARS data transfer: SARS pre-registers the company for Income Tax, VAT (where turnover triggers registration), PAYE, UIF, and SDL and issues a 10-digit income-tax reference number; (3) activate eFiling and complete the electronic Reg01/SAR01 record; (4) where the R1,000,000 VAT threshold is exceeded or reasonably expected, file VAT101 (and VAT102 bank-details form) through eFiling within 21 days of the trigger date to obtain a separate VAT vendor number; (5) prior to exporting or importing through the cross-dock, register on the SARS Customs Trader Portal (Trader Registration module) to obtain importer/exporter codes required for SAD500 export declarations. Non-resident owners do not require a local director or SA resident shareholder, but the Pty Ltd itself must appoint an auditor-or-accounting officer as required by the Companies Act and retain a SARS-verified public officer for tax correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the (Pty) Ltd is a resident 'company' as defined in s.1 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, subject to tax on worldwide income at 27% corporate income tax (tax years ending on or after 31 March 2023), with 80% capital-gain inclusion (21.6% effective CGT rate) and a further 20% dividends tax withheld at distribution; territorial scope is modified residence-based—non-resident shareholders are outside the SA net unless the income has a SA source, while the local subsidiary loses the 'foreign' classification entirely.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box (classification election) regime; the entity's classification follows the Companies Act legal form and statutory definition. Relevant elections: (1) CFC high-tax exemption opt-out or opt-in election under s.9D(9A) of the Income Tax Act for foreign disregarded/similar entities owned by the Pty Ltd; (2) corporate restructuring roll-over elections under ss.42, 44, 45 and 46 (including s.45 intra-group transactions); (3) VAT group registration election under s.59A of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 to treat related companies as a single vendor; (4) voluntary VAT registration election under s.23(3) at a ZAR 50,000 turnover (unless supplies will be wholly zero-rated); (5) s.9D(9A) high-tax exemption election and s.11D R&D incentive pre-approval. Hybrid mismatch rules (IT Act ss.8F–8Y) apply mandatorily, not electively, and can recharacterize payments/entities notwithstanding the chosen legal form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 — compulsory vendor registration within 21 days once total taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months exceeded R1 million or are reasonably expected to exceed R1 million in the next 12 months (s.23(1)(a) VAT Act); voluntary registration available from ZAR 50,000 (s.23(3)). Foreign suppliers of electronic services: threshold temporarily eliminated (zero) from 1 April 2022 to 31 March 2025; reinstated at ZAR 1,000,000 with effect from 1 April 2025 under the Rates and Monetary Amounts and Amendment of Revenue Laws Act, 2024. There is no separate sub-national (provincial) VAT or sales tax in South Africa; the national VAT is the sole indirect tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies under s.11(1)(a) of the VAT Act to movable goods supplied in the ordinary course of an enterprise and delivered to a recipient in an 'export country', provided the supplier obtains documentary proof in the prescribed form and the goods are physically exported within 90 days of the date of supply; s.11(1)(d) also zero-rates sales of goods in foreign currency to a non-resident where payment is received and goods are exported within 90 days. Services: offshore services to non-residents (s.11(2)(k)) and international transport of goods from SA (s.11(2)(b)) qualify for 0% where the service is not supplied directly in connection with land in SA or movable property situated in SA; cross-dock storage and handling performed physically in SA remains standard-rated, so the local 3PL operator must contractually separate the qualifying export-transport element from domestic logistics services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 series lodged on the SARS Customs Trader Portal, with the electronic declaration reference and date-stamp) - primary proof of physical removal from South Africa
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original or electronic negotiable transport document issued by the line/airline and endorsed to the overseas consignee) evidencing cross-border carriage to the export destination
  • Inward Foreign Currency Remittance Certificate / Authorised Dealer bank receipt (SWIFT MT103 or equivalent SARB export-proceeds report) showing external payment in convertible foreign currency received in the ordinary course

South Africa does not currently operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance/stamping regime for domestic invoices. Vendors may issue tax invoices electronically only where the system satisfies s.20(6) of the VAT Act — SARS-approved controls for authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility — and must retain the audit trail; paper tax invoices, with 15% VAT evidenced, remain lawful. SARS has announced a phased mandatory e-invoicing reform using the PEPPOL interoperability framework (2024–2026 discussion and pilot phases, with mandatory participation for high-turnover vendors expected in subsequent years); no central government digital-seal or unique invoice code is yet required. For exports, however, the customs clearance mandate is already real-time: every consignment requires an SAD500 customs export declaration lodged electronically on the SARS Customs Trader Portal, subject to risk-based screening and potential physical inspection before the goods are removed from the cross-dock facility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is fully convertible for bona fide current-account transactions (trade settlements, operating payments, service fees) without SARB prior approval, provided the mandated authorised dealer retains supporting documentation. Capital-account transactions remain administratively controlled: inbound foreign direct investment into a standard Pty Ltd is permitted without pre-approval and requires only an inward investment notification via the authorised dealer, whereas outbound capital transfers, shareholder loans, and certain cross-border funding arrangements require FinSurv approval or delegated-authority verification. Profit remittance is permitted without SARB approval for dividends paid from after-tax distributable reserves, subject to a valid SARS tax compliance status, a formal board dividend resolution, and supporting financial evidence held at the authorised dealer. Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are exposed to dividends withholding tax at 20% unless a treaty rate reduction and/or an approved exemption affidavit is available.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 10 and SARB FinSurv rulings applicable to exporters, proceeds of exported goods must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment and must be surrendered to an authorised dealer (converted to ZAR) within 30 days of receipt. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the foreign-currency export proceeds unless a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) retention facility has been granted to the exporter for working-capital or import-matching requirements. Each shipment must be backed by a SARS customs Bill of Entry export declaration; the authorised dealer performs value matching and may require the invoice, waybill, and payment evidence before releasing funds for company operations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), as amended, and the SARB Exchange Control Manual

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium commercially viable but legally constrained. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise) are not authorised dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations; they may collect e-commerce proceeds offshore but cannot bring those proceeds onshore except through a SARB-authorised commercial bank. An offshore EMI is therefore permissible as a marketplace collection and reconciliation rail, provided funds are swept periodically into the South African tier-1 ZAR account and the 180/30-day export realisation rules are satisfied from the underlying buyer settlement date. E-money licensing by the SARB Prudential Authority and FSCA does not confer exchange-control dealing powers, so the EMI cannot act as the treasury domicile for ZAR-denominated obligations. Legal constraint: indefinite offshore retention of export receivables without a FinSurv FCA approval or a SARS controlled-foreign-company attribution analysis must be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: (i) the Proprietary Limited company passes a board resolution authorising the issue of ordinary shares to the non-resident investor; (ii) the investor initiates a SWIFT MT103 from its bank with the strict purpose reference 'share capital subscription - ZA_PTY_LTD - inward FDI' and remits to the dedicated ZAR capital escrow account at the tier-1 authorised dealer; (iii) the authorised dealer matches the remittance against the share subscription agreement, completes the inward investment notification/Form A statistical declaration to SARB FinSurv, and confirms that no prior FinSurv approval is required for a standard equity injection into an unlisted Pty Ltd; (iv) funds settle through the SAMOS RTGS system in ZAR, and the dealer verifies that no securities transfer tax applies to an issue of new unlisted shares; (v) funds are released to the operational account only upon CIPC share-issue filing and SARS tax-status confirmation. Any material shareholder loan or hybrid funding must be segregated from pure equity remittances because such debt instruments attract FinSurv prior-approval assessment.Primary clearing rails: (i) local vendor and subcontracted 3PL payments are settled in ZAR through BankservAfrica EFT and Real-Time Clearing, with SARS VAT invoices retained; (ii) cross-border trade settlements to overseas warehouses, freight forwarders, and marketplace logistics providers are executed by the authorised dealer as spot or forward FX transactions, with the foreign-currency leg delivered by SWIFT MT103 from the ZAR operational account against documented underlying invoices, packing lists, bills of lading or airway bills, and customs entries; (iii) commercial letters of credit (MT700) with compliant documentary presentation are used for high-value inventory build, warehouse equipment, or capital purchases; (iv) collections from overseas marketplaces are routed via approved EMI sweeps into the FCA/ZAR account with full buyer-to-EMI-to-dealer reconciliation; (v) all payments to non-resident service providers require the dealer to evidence the actual service contract, the beneficial recipient, and the SARS transfer-pricing documentation so that the transaction cannot be characterised as round-tripping or thin capitalisation.Onshore outright FX forward (domestic forward cover) executed with the authorised dealer against documentary trade exposure, physically settled in ZAROffshore-led non-deliverable USD/ZAR forward (NDF) executed through the authorised dealer's foreign affiliate, permitted only where a genuine underlying FX exposure exists and where no exchange-control arbitrage is intendedEuropean-style FX options bought from the authorised dealer as premium-funded protection over identified trade settlement flowsCross-currency interest rate swap (CCIRS) hedging non-ZAR denominated funding, lease, or warehouse financing liabilities, subject to FinSurv-approved foreign-liability coverFX swap (spot-forward combination) for intra-group liquidity conversion between the Foreign Currency Account and the ZAR operational account within the authorised dealer's delegated limits

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium commercially viable but legally constrained. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise) are not authorised dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations; they may collect e-commerce proceeds offshore but cannot bring those proceeds onshore except through a SARB-authorised commercial bank. An offshore EMI is therefore permissible as a marketplace collection and reconciliation rail, provided funds are swept periodically into the South African tier-1 ZAR account and the 180/30-day export realisation rules are satisfied from the underlying buyer settlement date. E-money licensing by the SARB Prudential Authority and FSCA does not confer exchange-control dealing powers, so the EMI cannot act as the treasury domicile for ZAR-denominated obligations. Legal constraint: indefinite offshore retention of export receivables without a FinSurv FCA approval or a SARS controlled-foreign-company attribution analysis must be avoided.
