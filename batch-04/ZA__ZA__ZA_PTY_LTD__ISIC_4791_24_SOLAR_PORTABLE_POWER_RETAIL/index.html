Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official taxpayer interface: SARS eFiling at https://efiling.sars.gov.za. Legal-entity incorporation is administered by the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) through the BizPortal at https://b-eregistration.cipc.co.za.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned local (Pty) Ltd: incorporate through CIPC (Form CoR14.1 and Memorandum of Incorporation), appoint a South African-resident public officer, activate the eFiling profile, and obtain the company income tax reference number through the SARS-CIPC registration integration. Apply for VAT vendor registration using VAT101 on eFiling as soon as taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period, or elect voluntary VAT registration when taxable supplies have reached at least ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12 months. For a non-resident company making taxable electronic or digital supplies to South African customers without a local branch, register as a foreign supplier through the SARS eFiling foreign supplier onboarding module, provide certified proof of foreign incorporation and beneficial ownership, appoint a representative vendor or representative taxpayer in South Africa, and obtain a VAT vendor number. Supporting documents must include certified identification documents, a registered or physical business address, a South African bank account for refunds, and a power of attorney if a local agent acts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A proprietary limited company is an opaque resident corporate taxpayer and is not a pass-through entity. It is taxed on worldwide income while resident in South Africa, capital gains are subject to CGT, and distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to Dividends Tax at 20% (subject to applicable treaty relief). No special economic zone or tax-exempt-zone classification applies merely because the company is engaged in e-commerce.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in South Africa. A (Pty) Ltd cannot elect transparent tax treatment. Relevant statutory elections include voluntary VAT registration at the ZAR 50,000 threshold, VAT group registration under section 15 of the VAT Act for connected resident companies, and headquarter company status under section 9I of the Income Tax Act where the qualifying criteria are met. Hybrid mismatch rules contained in the Income Tax Act are mandatory and cannot be elected out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT vendor registration: taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period. Voluntary VAT registration: available if taxable supplies of at least ZAR 50,000 were made in the preceding 12 months. A (Pty) Ltd must register for income tax from incorporation regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, movable goods are chargeable at 0% where a vendor supplies the goods to a non-resident customer in the course of a sale, rental or consignment agreement, the goods are physically removed from South Africa, and the goods are exported within the statutory window (normally 90 days from the date of supply, or such extended period as SARS allows). For direct online retail of solar portable power systems and solar panels, zero-rating is claimable where shipping documents show delivery to a foreign destination and contemporaneous customs export evidence is obtained. Services may be zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) only where the services are supplied to a non-resident and are physically used and consumed outside South Africa. The refund mechanism is the VAT201 return; when a zero-rated supply is properly evidenced, no output VAT is declared on the export sale and excess input VAT on export-related costs is refundable by SARS. The burden of proof is on the vendor; inadequate or late export evidence will cause the supply to be reclassified at 15%.

  • Commercial and tax invoice showing the South African seller, overseas buyer/consignee, HS classification description for solar portable power generators or photovoltaic panels, unit prices, and a clear statement of zero-rated export under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act.
  • SARS customs export declaration (SAD500/DA550 or approved electronic manifest) reflecting the registered exporter, consignee, transport details, and final foreign destination.
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or road/rail freight manifest proving physical removal of the goods from South Africa and delivery to an address outside South Africa.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorised foreign-exchange dealer in South Africa, supported by bank statements and payment-rail settlement evidence, confirming receipt of the export proceeds in the prescribed manner.

South Africa does not currently mandate a national real-time fiscal clearance, QR stamp, or digital stamping system for all VAT vendors. Vendors must issue valid tax invoices or reduced tax invoices under section 20 of the VAT Act and retain electronic records for at least five years. Export goods are cleared electronically through the SARS Customs channel using SAD500/DA550 or an approved electronic manifest, and exports may be released only after customs clearance. SARS eFiling provides API-integrated submission of customs declarations, VAT201 returns and status inquiries. SARS is progressively modernising standardised e-invoicing, but digital stamping is not presently required.

SARS matches zero-rated export claims against customs cargo declarations, courier and freight transport data, payments received through payment rails such as Stripe and Affirm BNPL, and bank foreign-exchange records. If the required documents are absent, incomplete or materially false, the zero-rating is reversed and VAT at 15% is assessed from the original date of supply, with interest at the prescribed rate. The Tax Administration Act permits conduct-based understatement penalties on the shortfall, together with additional tax under the VAT Act for false export documentation; in cases involving fraud, the additional tax may reach 200% of the VAT shortfall. SARS may also issue a jeopardy assessment, claw back refunds, suspend zero-rated export privileges, and impose criminal liability on directors, the public officer or the representative vendor.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is a floating but non-internationalized currency with residual exchange control. Current-account payments (trade, dividends, interest, royalties) are freely permitted through an AD subject to documentation and the two-leg rule (the ZAR leg must be matched by a foreign-currency leg transiting the same AD). Capital-account convertibility for inward direct investment is open: non-residents may subscribe for 100% of the shares of a South African (Pty) Ltd using foreign currency routed through an AD, with no minimum capital and no prior SARB approval; the AD files a Foreign Investment Declaration (FID; formerly Form F) which becomes the necessary certificate for future remittance of dividends and repatriation of capital. Profit remittance hurdles are tax rather than exchange-control: dividends attract 20% Dividend Withholding Tax (DWT) if not reduced under a Double Taxation Agreement, and the FID-stamped bank record must evidence the non-resident shareholding; interest on shareholder loans is subject to a 15% withholding tax absent treaty relief. Outward capital-account transactions by residents remain restricted (e.g., individuals' R11 million discretionary offshore allowance; corporate outbound investment above prescribed limits requires FinSurv approval).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exports from South Africa require a SARS SAD500 export declaration lodged before goods leave the customs territory. Export proceeds must be received by the exporter within 180 days from the date of shipment (longer maturity is available with AD approval for capital goods); once received in foreign currency, the proceeds must be sold (surrendered) to an AD within 30 days unless credited to an Authorized Dealer-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) where the proceeds may be retained and deployed offshore without conversion. The statutory surrender ratio is effectively 0% if the FCA route is used, and 100% if the exporter has no FCA, because the 30-day rule requires full conversion of the export receipt. E-commerce exports to the Common Monetary Area (Namibia, Lesotho, Eswatini) settle in ZAR freely without FX surrender, as the ZAR is legal tender in the CMA, whereas SADC and other cross-border sales invoiced in USD or EUR are captured by the 180-day/30-day regime.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, administered through Authorized Dealers (ADs) which are commercial banks approved under the regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium - supplementary rail only, not a legal substitute for a South African Authorized Dealer. South Africa does not issue an EU-style EMI licence; cross-border fintech platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud) operate under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 by partnering with licensed commercial banks as payment aggregators or payment initiation providers and, where applicable, as FSCA-licensed FSPs. They are practical for disbursing USD to overseas suppliers and for collecting card revenue, but they cannot host SARB-reportable FDI flows, cannot issue an FID, cannot clear ZAR through SAMOS, and must not receive the equity injection because the two-leg rule requires the foreign-currency and ZAR legs to pass through the same AD. Affirm is not a licensed BNPL in South Africa; local BNPL players (Payflex, PayJustNow, Mobicred) clear via the domestic bank rails. Recommended architecture: AD bank as sole FX/FDI treasury backbone; fintech EMIs used only for satellite trade disbursements.

Turnover: A full independent audit is mandatory under section 30 of the Companies Act where the Public Interest Score is 350 or more; where the score is at least 100 and the company has 500 or more shareholders, holds securities in the public interest, or is a regulated entity. Companies scoring 100 to 349 generally require an independent review, while companies below 100 may elect self-review with unanimous shareholder consent unless classified as a regulated company. Public Interest Score components: 1 point per employee, 1 point per ZAR 1 million of turnover, 1 point per ZAR 1 million of third-party debt, and 1 point per 100 individual shareholders.
