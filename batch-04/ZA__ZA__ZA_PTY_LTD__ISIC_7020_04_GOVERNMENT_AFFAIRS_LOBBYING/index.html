Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
South African Revenue Service (SARS); principal portal is the SARS eFiling platform at https://efiling.sars.gov.za with public guidance at https://www.sars.gov.za.

        
E-government filing portal
A local (Pty) Ltd is first incorporated through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) e-services portal; SARS automatically activates the company's Income Tax reference number through the integrated CIPC/SARS registration interface. VAT registration is a separate step executed on SARS eFiling using the VAT101 application: compulsory registration is due within 21 days after the end of the month in which cumulative taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is available once taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in the preceding 12 months or are reasonably expected to exceed R50,000 in the next 12 months. A foreign-owned applicant must appoint and register a SARS-approved public officer and a South African registered auditor or accounting officer, file certified constitutional documents (Memorandum of Incorporation), proof of physical registered address in South Africa, identification and residential details of all directors, banking confirmation, and a power of attorney before SARS issues the Income Tax number and, upon approval of the VAT101, the separate VAT vendor number. A non-resident company without a South African permanent establishment that supplies only cross-border services into South Africa may register solely as a non-resident VAT vendor where the applicable electronic-services or imported-services threshold is crossed; no SARS income tax TIN is required absent a South African source or deemed-source taxable income.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a resident ZA (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person with no automatic pass-through eligibility; taxable on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate (years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022); the capital gains inclusion rate of 80% yields an effective 21.6% rate on capital gains; no territorial exemption for foreign business income, subject to foreign tax credits and controlled foreign company attribution under s 9D; not located in a tax-exempt or special economic zone by default. — A local (Pty) Ltd is first incorporated through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) e-services portal; SARS automatically activates the company's Income Tax reference number through the integrated CIPC/SARS registration interface. VAT registration is a separate step executed on SARS eFiling using the VAT101 application: compulsory registration is due within 21 days after the end of the month in which cumulative taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is available once taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in the preceding 12 months or are reasonably expected to exceed R50,000 in the next 12 months. A foreign-owned applicant must appoint and register a SARS-approved public officer and a South African registered auditor or accounting officer, file certified constitutional documents (Memorandum of Incorporation), proof of physical registered address in South Africa, identification and residential details of all directors, banking confirmation, and a power of attorney before SARS issues the Income Tax number and, upon approval of the VAT101, the separate VAT vendor number. A non-resident company without a South African permanent establishment that supplies only cross-border services into South Africa may register solely as a non-resident VAT vendor where the applicable electronic-services or imported-services threshold is crossed; no SARS income tax TIN is required absent a South African source or deemed-source taxable income.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation — a resident ZA (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person with no automatic pass-through eligibility; taxable on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate (years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022); the capital gains inclusion rate of 80% yields an effective 21.6% rate on capital gains; no territorial exemption for foreign business income, subject to foreign tax credits and controlled foreign company attribution under s 9D; not located in a tax-exempt or special economic zone by default.

        
Available election forms
South Africa does not provide an IRS-style check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the legal form of the entity. Elective and self-assessed mechanisms include: (i) the s 25H(2) Income Tax Act election, by which a qualifying foreign hybrid entity is treated as a South African company where all members are South African residents and unanimous consent is filed; (ii) voluntary VAT registration at the R50,000 threshold under s 23(2) of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991; (iii) corporate rollover elections under ss 42, 44, 45, 46 and 47 of the Income Tax Act, including the s 45 intra-group transaction election; (iv) self-assessed CFC classifications under s 9D, namely the foreign business establishment exclusion under s 9D(2)(b), the high-taxed foreign company exemption under s 9D(2A), and the s 9D(9) net-income limitation; (v) the s 12E Small Business Corporation elective tax rate schedule for qualifying resident companies with turnover up to R20 million per annum, subject to exclusion of personal-service companies whose income consists mainly of services rendered by connected shareholders; and (vi) SARS binding private rulings under ss 76–79 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 to confirm entity classification and rate treatment in advance. Hybrid interest and hybrid entity mismatch rules under ss 8F, 8FA and 25G–25M operate automatically and are not electable.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
15%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Compulsory: R1,000,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding rolling 12-month period or a reasonable expectation of exceeding R1,000,000 in the following 12 months; voluntary: R50,000 of taxable supplies in the past 12 months or reasonably expected future supplies exceeding R50,000. A non-resident supplier of electronic services to South African residents is also required to register for VAT where taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 per annum. Imported services, including foreign software licences, data feeds or offshore advisory inputs acquired by a resident vendor making taxable supplies, are subject to reverse-charge VAT at 15% under s 14(2)(d) of the VAT Act.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods exports are zero-rated under s 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the goods are physically removed from South Africa within 90 days after the time of supply and the vendor retains proof of export. Services exports are zero-rated under s 11(2)(k) where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time the services are rendered, the services are not connected with land, immovable property or movable property situated in South Africa, and the services are not supplied directly to the non-resident's South African permanent establishment; s 11(2)(q) additionally zero-rates services physically rendered outside South Africa. Cross-border trade policy analysis, government affairs advice, stakeholder mapping and lobbying services supplied to a foreign principal outside South Africa will ordinarily qualify under s 11(2)(k) provided the engagement is structured as an offshore service, invoiced in foreign currency, and the recipient has no South African establishment to which the services are attributable. Lobbying activity aimed at South African government organs remains zero-rated if the contracting recipient is non-resident; however, where the supply is connected with a South African permanent establishment or is provided to a resident intermediary, 15% VAT applies.

No legislation currently mandates real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or API-connected electronic fiscal devices for VAT invoices. SARS has published a voluntary e-invoicing framework and is piloting structured electronic tax invoices (XML/UBL formats) through the eFiling and customs LEDS interface; full mandatory e-invoicing remains on the SARS roadmap but is not yet in force. Vendors must nonetheless issue paper or electronic tax invoices within 21 days of the date of supply containing the s 20(4) VAT Act particulars, namely vendor name and VAT number, invoice date, description, quantity, consideration and VAT amount, and SARS may require transactional-level data through IT14D requests, audit information letters and data analytics on VAT201 returns.

      
SARS applies a risk-scored audit overlay to zero-rated export supplies, prioritising intangible services, related-party cross-border fees, and recurring VAT refunds. The exporter must preserve the customs export declaration, transport documents and inward foreign remittance evidence for at least five years under s 55 of the VAT Act read with s 29 of the Tax Administration Act; failure to do so triggers administrative non-compliance penalties of up to R6,000 per month per item. Where zero-rating is reversed, SARS raises an output VAT assessment at 15% plus interest at the prescribed rate (repo rate plus 3.5 percentage points). Understatement penalties under s 223 of the Tax Administration Act range from 20% for a standard case to 150% for intentional tax evasion, with 50% for a tax position without reasonable grounds and 75% for gross negligence. Section 59(3) of the VAT Act further authorises additional tax not exceeding double the disputed amount for fraudulent refund or export claims. Criminal referral under ss 234–235 of the Tax Administration Act, or common-law fraud prosecution, carries fines and imprisonment of up to five years. The five-year prescription period does not apply where SARS proves misrepresentation or intentional evasion, permitting retrospective clawback and assessment for periods substantially beyond five years.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Africa maintains a dual-tier exchange control system: current account transactions for services, retainers, and trade-related payments are generally free and may be processed by Authorised Dealers without prior SARB approval, subject to documentary verification. Capital account transactions remain restricted: inward foreign direct investment into a (Pty) Ltd requires exchange control reporting via the Authorised Dealer (Form F) but does not require prior SARB approval for equity subscription; outward capital flows, including profit repatriation and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders, require before-sight tax clearance from SARS (Form A) and a demonstrable compliance record with the South African exchange control framework. Non-resident profit remittance is effectively convertible once corporate income tax and dividends tax obligations are settled, but the remittance may be blocked or delayed by unresolved SARS tax clearance, unreported beneficial ownership, or failure to account for inward share subscriptions under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export proceeds arising from South African exports must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days of the date of shipment or service completion. Upon receipt, the exporter is required to offer surrender the full amount of foreign currency to an Authorised Dealer within 30 days, resulting in an effective 100% conversion into South African rand, unless the exporter operates a duly approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or benefits from a FinSurv exemption or retention facility. For a government affairs advisory and lobbying firm, any cross-border retainer receipt of foreign origin must be routed through an Authorised Dealer, with the corresponding Form F or Exchange Control 'F' reporting completed where such receipts are regarded as export earnings from non-resident clients.

      

      
Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) of the National Treasury, in conjunction with Authorised Dealers (primarily the major commercial banks licensed under the Banks Act 94 of 1990).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs and e-money institutions are not Authorised Dealers under the South African Exchange Control Regulations and are not integrated into the South African National Payment System (SAMOS). Consequently, they cannot perform SARB FinSurv reporting, surrender foreign currency into ZAR, or hold regulatory-compliant Capital Escrow Accounts for a domestic (Pty) Ltd. They may be used only as ancillary payment collection instruments for small incidental expenses; the primary corporate banking relationship must sit with a licensed Authorised Dealer with direct SAMOS clearing access.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        PEP status of clients and principals involved in government affairs and lobbying engagements
        
Root cause: PEP status of clients and principals involved in government affairs and lobbying engagements

        
Defense evidence: A comprehensive PEP risk assessment and independent advisory charter; corporate organogram demonstrating an arms-length relationship from all Government stakeholders; contracts containing explicit anti-bribery and anti-illicit-influence clauses; certified client source-of-funds declarations; individual sanctions and PEP screening results; board minutes approving the retainer engagement; and a monitored transaction threshold policy requiring escalated approval for politically-exposed counterparties.

      

      
      

        Non-resident shareholding and ambiguous source of inbound capital for the Capital Escrow Account
        
Root cause: Non-resident shareholding and ambiguous source of inbound capital for the Capital Escrow Account

        
Defense evidence: CIPC company registration certificate and full share register; notarised share subscription agreements and transfer documents; overseas bank statements in the remitting shareholder's name covering at least six months; a bank reference from the remitting institution; source-of-wealth declaration and affidavit; SARS tax clearance for the South African entity; the Authorised Dealer-completed SARB Form F; and a board-approved capital introduction memorandum describing the intended use of proceeds.

      

      
      

        Cross-border retainer wire at enhanced risk with only invoice-based service proof
        
Root cause: Cross-border retainer wire at enhanced risk with only invoice-based service proof

        
Defense evidence: Executed professional services agreement and detailed scope of work; monthly activity and time reports evidencing actual policy analysis and lobbying services; itemised VAT-capable invoices; a copy of the incoming SWIFT MT103 referencing the retainer purpose code; SARS certificates and tax compliance status; the company's physical substance documentation such as an office lease, payroll records, and utilities; and a sanctions screening record for each remitting counterparty.

      

      
      

        Statutory licence requirement and potential B-BBEE and beneficial ownership non-compliance
        
Root cause: Statutory licence requirement and potential B-BBEE and beneficial ownership non-compliance

        
Defense evidence: Valid lobbying/Government Affairs licensing or Public Affairs registration, where applicable, together with the PAIA manual and regulatory undertaking; CIPC beneficial ownership register; B-BBEE certificate or sworn affidavit; an implemented AML/CFT institutional risk framework; evidence that the entity is a going concern; and a signed statement of compliance with the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, the Prevention of Organised Crime Act, and the Protection of Constitutional Democracy against Related Activities Act.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound foreign capital injection must be routed through a SARB Authorised Dealer by instructing an SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the non-resident shareholder to the dedicated Capital Escrow Account of the South African (Pty) Ltd. The remitter must record the remittance as Foreign Direct Investment / Equity Subscription using the SARB exchange control purpose code prescribed by the receiving bank, and the receiving Authorised Dealer must submit the relevant Form F to FinSurv. The settlement is cleared via SAMOS and immediately converted into South African rand at the prevailing market rate. To ensure compliance, the following documents must be presented at the receiving bank: the CIPC registration certificate, board resolution authorising share issuance, notarised share subscription agreement, source-of-funds evidence from the overseas remitter, and the company's SARS tax clearance. No prior SARB approval is required for equity-only inward subscription, but the Form F statistical reporting is mandatory before the escrowed funds are released to the operational account.Outbound disbursements for retainer payments to foreign policy partners, research sub-contractors, or international advisory firms will be settled via SWIFT MT103 instructions initiated through the authorised dealer bank. Current account outward payments for professional services may be processed without prior SARB approval provided the bank is furnished with the final invoice, an executed consultancy agreement, proof of service delivery, a certified statement of withholding tax or dividends tax where payment is dividend-related, and an SARS clearance certificate. The transaction must be allocated to the appropriate SARB payment purpose code for advisory/professional services. The ZAR-to-FX conversion is executed at the authorised dealer's rate and settled via SAMOS for same-day or value-day disbursement. A Commercial Letter of Credit is not applicable to intangible lobbying services; where advance payments are required, the counterparty may require a standby letter of credit or bank guarantee, but ordinary retainer settlement is documentary SWIFT MT103 only.Onshore forward exchange contracts (FECs) with a SARB Authorised Dealer, restricted to firmly committed and ascertainable foreign currency receivables or payables arising from retainer or consultancy contracts, with typical maturities not exceeding 12 months.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) used to hedge long-term non-ZAR liabilities, shareholder loans, or material currency mismatches on the balance sheet, subject to FinSurv's transaction purpose test and full documentary substantiation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible for South African residents when an onshore deliverable ZAR forward market exists; in exceptional circumstances involving illiquid or restricted African-blocked currencies, an NDF may be authorised by FinSurv on a case-by-case basis with a clear commercial hedge rationale.Spot foreign exchange transactions conducted through the authorised dealing desk for immediate conversion of retainer inflows and disbursement of outbound service fees are allowable; all hedging instruments must be expressly non-speculative and supported by underlying board-approved commercial exposure.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs and e-money institutions are not Authorised Dealers under the South African Exchange Control Regulations and are not integrated into the South African National Payment System (SAMOS). Consequently, they cannot perform SARB FinSurv reporting, surrender foreign currency into ZAR, or hold regulatory-compliant Capital Escrow Accounts for a domestic (Pty) Ltd. They may be used only as ancillary payment collection instruments for small incidental expenses; the primary corporate banking relationship must sit with a licensed Authorised Dealer with direct SAMOS clearing access.
