Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za).

E-government filing portal

A South African Proprietary Limited company is incorporated via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) with a CIPC registration number. SARS automatically creates the income tax reference number (TRN) through verified CIPC data integration; if not generated, the company registers on SARS eFiling with the CIPC certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation and directors' identification. The company must register for VAT as a vendor under VAT Act No. 89 of 1991 via form VAT101 on eFiling once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period, or voluntarily from ZAR 50,000. Foreign entities contracting with the SA company without a local branch appoint a registered SARS practitioner/representative and file the non-resident registration pack (certified founding documents, foreign tax residency certificate, board resolutions) to obtain a non-resident TRN and, if required, a VAT vendor number. Separate customs registration (importer/exporter code with SAD500/SASEC electronic access) is completed through the SARS Customs Modernisation system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide a check-the-box classification election; a resident (Pty) Ltd is automatically taxed at entity level at 27% corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act No. 58 of 1962. Available statutory elections include: VAT group registration under s.23K of the VAT Act; corporate restructuring rollover elections under ss.44-46 of the Income Tax Act; dividend tax exemption election under s.10(1)(k)(i); and the hybrid mismatch integrity rules in ss.23M-23T of the Income Tax Act, which restrict deduction-conferring classification elections in cross-border app-store and licensor structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period (compulsory); voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000; foreign suppliers of electronic services use the same ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a vendor may zero-rate exported goods under s.11(1)(d) of the VAT Act where the sale is to a recipient outside South Africa and the goods are physically removed from South Africa within the period and under the documentary conditions prescribed by s.11(3). Services: zero-rating under s.11(2)(l) of the VAT Act for services rendered in South Africa to a non-resident who is not in the Republic at the time of supply, and under s.11(2)(k) for services supplied to a non-resident for business purposes outside the Republic; exclusions apply where services relate to South African immovable or movable property. For vertical micro-drama streaming sold through Apple/Google app stores, zero-rating is supportable where the end-user supply chain is documented as non-resident, the app-store counterparty is the billing agent resident outside South Africa, and South African consumer demand is standard-rated at 15%.

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration / SASEC electronic export declaration (physical goods only)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical exportation (where applicable)
  • Commercial invoice and signed digital distribution agreement with the non-resident app-store operator (Apple/Google) or non-resident customer
  • App-store settlement report/sales statement showing platform fees, territorial sales, device residency and non-resident attribution
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or authorised dealer bank statement evidencing that foreign currency sales proceeds were repatriated to the South African bank account

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or per-invoice pre-approval regime is currently in force in South Africa. SARS is phasing in PEPPOL-based electronic invoicing on a voluntary, pre-approved basis. VAT invoices must comply with s.20 of the VAT Act (full prescribed particulars, including the SARS 15-digit VAT registration number, supply date, consideration, VAT amount and zero-rated indication) and be issued within the statutory time frame. The entity must reconcile Apple IAP/Google Play/Stripe settlement reports to its VAT201 declarations; SARS may require submission of e-commerce transaction-level data via eFiling APIs during audit.

SARS audits will review the s.11(3) evidence bundle and territorial app-store data; on failure, zero-rating is reversed and the supply attracts 15% VAT with statutory interest. Additional tax under s.60 of the VAT Act may be imposed up to 100% of the tax (up to 200% where non-disclosure/false declaration was made with intent to evade tax). Understatement penalties under s.223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 may reach 150% of the shortfall, with monthly administrative non-compliance penalties in addition. Fraudulent export invoicing or forged IRC/customs documentation constitutes criminal tax evasion under s.234 TAA (fine and/or imprisonment up to 2 years per count), triggers retrospective refund clawback, and may be reported to the South African Reserve Bank for exchange-control violations (non-repatriation within 180 days).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR operates under a managed-float regime. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted for service exports; digital-content and vertical micro-drama streaming receipts are freely convertible to ZAR through an Authorised Dealer without prior SARB consent. Capital-account inflows (non-resident equity subscription) require no SARB pre-approval for a non-affected (Pty) Ltd, but must be recorded via Form A at the receiving bank and will be subject to source-of-funds KYC under FICA. Dividend and profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted upon: (i) issuance of a SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN; (ii) proof of the original inward capital in the bank's Foreign Liability/Form A records; and (iii) compliance with annual Exchange Control reporting. The main practical hurdle is not foreign-exchange restriction but the SARS TCS gate, which forces full corporate tax and VAT compliance before any distribution is effected offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory repatriation timeline for exported services is 180 days from the date of invoicing/rendering. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the foreign-currency export proceeds contractually due; partial conversion or deferred conversion requires FinSurv/Authorised Dealer consent granted via an approved Foreign Currency Account (rare for services). Where Apple, Google, and Stripe net their fees before remittance, the export proceeds are the net credited amounts evidenced by platform settlement statements; the bank will expect that every net remittance is converted to ZAR through the Authorised Dealer and reconciled to the corresponding platform report. A 180-day breach is an Exchange Control contravention carrying administrative penalties and, in persistent cases, referral to the National Prosecuting Authority, plus quarantine of the foreign account by the Authorised Dealer.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, and delegated powers to Authorised Dealers (licensed commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Medium with binding legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud) may be used as short-cycle collection accounts for Apple/Google/Stripe settlements, but cannot replace an Authorised Dealer: they are not licensed by SARB/FinSurv to convert ZAR, issue Form A records, or evidence 180-day surrender compliance. A lawful structure is: (i) collect foreign-currency app-store settlements into the EMI; (ii) sweep accumulated balances via SWIFT MT103 to the SA Authorised Dealer when the balance exceeds a minimum FX threshold; (iii) present EMI/platform statements to the bank for export-proceeds reconciliation. Any direct ZAR conversion by an unlicensed EMI would breach Exchange Control Regulations and trigger FICA reporting obligations. Reliance on a sole EMI as primary bank account is therefore inadvisable and materially increases AML underwriting risk.
