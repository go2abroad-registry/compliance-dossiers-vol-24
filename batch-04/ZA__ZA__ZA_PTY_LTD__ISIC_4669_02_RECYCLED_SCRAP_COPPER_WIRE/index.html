Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — statutory tax and customs authority; principal taxpayer portal is SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); corporate registry interface is CIPC (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission) via https://www.cipc.co.za; customs and export declarations are lodged electronically through the SARS Customs EDAN/SAD500 e-filing systems.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Pty Ltd: (1) incorporate the private company under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 at CIPC to obtain the unique company registration number, using the CIPC-SARS one-stop registration data channel; (2) complete SARS income tax registration through eFiling or at a SARS branch to obtain the income tax reference number; (3) file VAT registration via eFiling (Form VAT 101) with a declaration of current and projected annual taxable turnover; compulsory VAT registration applies when taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed R 1,000,000 per annum, and SARS will issue a VAT vendor number; (4) apply to SARS Customs for an importer/exporter customs code before moving goods; (5) file beneficial ownership information with CIPC and complete SARS activation requirements. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Pty Ltd is a legal person and company taxed as a separate taxable entity under s 1 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 and the Companies Act 71 of 2008; resident companies are taxed on worldwide income at the 27% normal corporate income tax rate for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (28% in prior years); there is no look-through or pass-through taxation of shareholders for corporate income tax; distributions are separately subject to dividends tax at 20% (subject to treaty relief and the s 64FA exemptions). Export profit from scrap copper sales is ordinary taxable income in South Africa, and foreign exchange gains are taxable (or losses deductible) when accounted for under the functional currency rules.

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box regime, but the following statutory elections are relevant to a cross-border scrap exporter: (1) headquarter company election under s 9I of the Income Tax Act, giving conduit treatment for qualifying foreign dividends, interest, royalties and CFC proceeds; (2) controlled foreign company elections under s 9D, including the high-tax exemption election (foreign effective tax at least 90% of South African normal tax), the voluntary inclusion election under s 9D(9A), and the foreign business establishment active-income exclusion; (3) conduit arrangement election under s 12B for foreign entities or partnerships substantially similar to South African partnerships, enabling transparent taxation of members; (4) VAT group registration election under s 50(1) of the VAT Act; (5) corporate reorganisation rollover elections under ss 45-46 of the Income Tax Act for tax-neutral asset-for-share and amalgamation transactions in metals supply chains; and (6) trading-stock valuation elections under s 22(1) for scrap inventory employing base stock, FIFO, weighted average or specific identification methods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa applies a controlled convertibility regime rather than full free convertibility. Current-account trade transactions, including payment of bona fide import/export obligations and trade-related expenses, are normally permissible through an authorised dealer without prior SARB approval once supporting contracts and SARS customs documentation are presented. Capital-account inflows, such as foreign direct investment and shareholder loans, are operationally welcome but must be reported and registered with the authorised dealer; capital-account outflows by residents, offshore investments by residents, and certain non-resident credit/guarantee structures remain exchange-control restricted. Profit remittances are permissible where there is evidence of after-tax distributable profits, audited or management financial statements, a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status/PIN, settlement of dividends tax at 20% or reduced treaty rate, and, if the capital was originally imported, proof of the inward transfer through the authorised dealer. The ZAR is not a fully externally convertible currency, but an active offshore NDF market exists and authorised dealers may offer appropriate hedging to residents with a documented underlying trade exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in full within 180 days from the date of shipment unless the authorised dealer has approved alternative payment terms. The exporter must submit the relevant SARS export declaration (SAD500) and shipping documents, and all foreign currency proceeds received by the South African exporting entity must be surrendered to an authorised dealer for conversion into rand unless a SARB/FinSurv-approved foreign-currency retention account exists. If no such account is approved, surrender is required within 30 days of receipt, and the default statutory position is 100% conversion of export proceeds into ZAR. Overdue export proceeds are reportable by the authorised dealer to FinSurv, and any write-off, reinvoicing, discount, or non-receipt of proceeds requires full documented justification and, where applicable, prior approval or tolerance from the authorised dealer.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, as amended. Exchange control is operationally delegated to Authorised Dealers, being the South African commercial banks approved by SARB.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Payoneer are not authorised dealers under the South African Exchange Control Regulations and do not hold a South African banking licence. They cannot serve as the primary clearing or surrender rail for export proceeds because those proceeds must be received through an authorised dealer and surrendered to the local banking system. Such EMIs may be used only as an ancillary collection or payment interface provided the final, regulated settlement remains in a SARB-licensed authorised dealer account. Holding funds offshore in an EMI without FinSurv approval, or routing export proceeds to an EMI instead of the authorised dealer, would constitute an exchange-control contravention and may trigger account closure.
