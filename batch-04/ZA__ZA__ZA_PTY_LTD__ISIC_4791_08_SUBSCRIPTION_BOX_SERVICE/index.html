Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permissible with material caveats. CIPC, under s 23(3) of the Companies Act, requires every company to maintain a physical registered office in South Africa capable of receiving service of process; a virtual-office arrangement that provides a genuine physical street address and mail-handling service satisfies the literal statutory mandate for this non-licensed, low-risk subscription-box business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a resident proprietary limited company is taxed on worldwide income at 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; territorial scope is residence-based, with non-residents taxed only on South African-sourced income; no pass-through treatment; dividends subject to 20% dividends tax and the CGT inclusion rate for companies is 80%; qualifying small business corporations may use progressive 0%/7%/21% rates on turnover up to R20,000,000).

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a US-style check-the-box election; a Pty Ltd is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Available mechanisms include: (i) VAT group registration under section 50 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 allowing group companies to be treated as a single vendor; (ii) small business corporation progressive rate election made via the annual income tax return; (iii) export services zero-rating election under section 11(2)(l) for services supplied to non-residents; (iv) headquarter company election under section 9I of the Income Tax Act for qualifying regional offices; and (v) hybrid mismatch rules (sections 8E, 8EA, 8F and 23M) which operate automatically and cannot be overridden by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 compulsory registration where the value of taxable supplies exceeded R1,000,000 in any preceding consecutive 12-month period or is reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in the following 12-month period; voluntary registration is available once taxable supplies exceed R50,000 per annum; foreign suppliers of electronic services are required to register regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act when goods are sold to a non-resident and physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply (extension available at SARS discretion). Services exports: section 11(2)(l) zero-rates services supplied to non-residents where the services are not consumed in South Africa and are not supplied to a non-resident carrying on a South African enterprise; this covers offshore subscription and digital/electronic services supplied by a resident vendor to non-resident recipients located outside South Africa. The vendor must obtain and retain documentary proof of export before claiming a refund of input VAT; failure to substantiate converts the supply to 15% output tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SARS SAD500 Bill of Entry for exports or electronic export declaration)
  • Commercial invoice and/or pro-forma invoice matching declared export value
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill (air freight), or CMR/road manifest (road or rail freight)
  • Inward remittance certificate or authorised-dealer foreign exchange confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in South Africa
  • Packing list and weight certificate
  • Customer purchase order and contract evidencing the non-resident buyer and export sale terms
  • Export permit or controlled-product licence where applicable

South Africa has adopted a decentralised certified e-invoicing model with no real-time central fiscal clearance or digital stamping. SARS-certified e-invoicing solutions transmit invoice data to SARS via APIs on a near-real-time basis. The phased mandate issued under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 requires vendors with annual turnover above R10,000,000 to use a SARS-certified e-invoicing system from the 2025/2026 tax period, with gradual extension to all vendors by the 2026/2027 period. Until mandatory, conventional paper or electronic invoices remain valid if compliant with section 20 of the VAT Act.

SARS applies intensive risk-based audit scrutiny to export zero-rating and refund claims; the vendor must retain full supporting documentation for at least 5 years. If the documentary bundle is incomplete or non-compliant, SARS denies the zero-rating, re-assesses the supply at 15% VAT, and claims the additional output tax retrospectively with interest under the Tax Administration Act. Additional penalties include VAT Act additional tax of up to 10%, understatement penalties of 10% to 75% depending on conduct (sections 222-223 of the Tax Administration Act), and criminal prosecution under section 234 TAA or sections 58-59 VAT Act for fraudulent refund claims, with imprisonment on conviction. Exchange-control contraventions for failure to timeously repatriate export proceeds attract penalties under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 of up to R25,000 per contravention or twice the amount involved, and may trigger recharacterisation of amounts as dividends or taxable loans for income tax purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, and royalties, subject to documentary verification and applicable reporting thresholds. Capital account transactions require exchange control approval or compliance with the SARB's General Compliance Ranges. Profit remittance by non-resident shareholders of a South African (Pty) Ltd is permitted without prior SARB approval provided that the remittance is supported by an external auditor's report confirming that profits arise from arm's-length trading and that all South African tax obligations have been settled. Dividends must be declared from retained earnings; contributions from non-residents must be reported via the BOP760 form and retained on a capital account unless a formal capital reduction is approved. The subscription box merchandising business (ISIC 4791) is a current-account-intensive activity, so subscription receivables, platform fees, and international payment processing fees are eligible for unrestricted conversion; however, any cross-border acquisition of intangible assets or equity instruments by the resident entity would require approval under capital flow management measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa does not impose a general export proceeds surrender requirement mandating compulsory conversion into ZAR at a SARB-set rate. However, all export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days of the date of shipment, invoiced in the contract currency, and credited to an Authorised Dealer account. The exporter must complete a Currency and Exchange (C&X) declaration and retain source documentation (bill of lading, customs SAD500, invoice). Receipts must be reported through the Authorised Dealer via the BOP760 return. No mandatory conversion ratio applies, but the full export value must be accounted for; any shortfall must be explained or compensated under an Exporter's Undertaking. Receipts may be held in a foreign currency account with the Authorised Dealer, subject to exchange control holding limits and utilisation rules.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), with delegated administration by Authorised Dealers (commercial banks with exchange control approval)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Foreign digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) cannot serve as the primary operating bank account for a South African resident (Pty) Ltd because the SARB requires resident companies to channel cross-border current account receipts and payments through Authorised Dealers for exchange control reporting. A foreign EMI that is not an Authorised Dealer cannot provide BOP760 submissions, C&X declaration handling, or exchange control cover for export proceeds. Foreign fintechs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are viable only as auxiliary payment collection rails, provided that funds are ultimately settled into a South African Authorised Dealer account. Legal constraints include the Banks Act 94 of 1990 (deposit-taking prohibition), the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998, and SARB Exchange Control Rulings restricting offshore netting and unauthorised foreign currency accounts. The subscription box business may use an Authorised Dealer's foreign currency account infrastructure for aggregated disbursements, but the primary corporate treasury account must be held with a SARB-regulated bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or loan capital must be received through an Authorised Dealer. Standard routing: foreign bank wire (SWIFT MT103) to the Authorised Dealer's USD/ZAR correspondent account, with beneficiary identified as the company name and account number, followed by conversion into ZAR. The remitter must include a payment reference indicating 'Share capital subscription' or 'Shareholder loan'. The Authorised Dealer completes BOP760 reporting to SARB FinSurv. There is no statutory minimum capital for a (Pty) Ltd, but contributed amounts must be consistent with the issued share register. If contribution exceeds ZAR 10 million or involves restricted foreign equity, the bank may require approval under the General Compliance Range or specific FinSurv approval. Documentary requirements include board resolution authorising allotment, foreign shareholder's bank reference, and source-of-wealth declaration. Verification is performed through SWIFT cover payment (MT202 COV) and standard financial crime checks.Outbound disbursements for physical inventory and logistics are settled via an Authorised Dealer using SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers; larger or documentary transactions may use an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) under UCP 600, confirmed or advised through a corresponding overseas bank. Payments for imported goods require purchase order, commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, and customs clearance evidence (SAD500) where applicable. Service-related disbursements (e-commerce platform fees, cross-border shipping, warehouse costs) require invoice and contract documentation; services payments above monitoring thresholds may require evidence of actual service performance and proof that South African withholding tax obligations have been addressed. ZAR-denominated settlement is effected through the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system; foreign currency settlement is effected through the Authorised Dealer's correspondent network. Foreign digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) cannot serve as the primary operating bank account for a South African resident (Pty) Ltd because the SARB requires resident companies to channel cross-border current account receipts and payments through Authorised Dealers for exchange control reporting. A foreign EMI that is not an Authorised Dealer cannot provide BOP760 submissions, C&X declaration handling, or exchange control cover for export proceeds. Foreign fintechs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are viable only as auxiliary payment collection rails, provided that funds are ultimately settled into a South African Authorised Dealer account. Legal constraints include the Banks Act 94 of 1990 (deposit-taking prohibition), the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998, and SARB Exchange Control Rulings restricting offshore netting and unauthorised foreign currency accounts. The subscription box business may use an Authorised Dealer's foreign currency account infrastructure for aggregated disbursements, but the primary corporate treasury account must be held with a SARB-regulated bank.
