Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); primary digital portal: SARS eFiling at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za/; customs matters administered through the SARS Customs division using eFiling and Customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

The (Pty) Ltd company is first incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtains a CIPC registration number. The company must appoint a Public Officer within 14 days after commencing business and register with SARS for income tax, VAT, PAYE, and Customs as applicable. Registration is effected by the Public Officer through SARS eFiling using the application forms: IT77 (income tax), VAT101 (VAT), and Customs registration profile. A tax reference number is issued as the company's SARS TIN; a separate vendor number is issued for VAT purposes; a customs client registration code is issued for import/export declarations. Non-resident shareholders are permitted and do not block registration, but foreign entities that remit dividends, interest, royalties, or payments to non-residents should also register for applicable withholding taxes and appoint a SARS representative in South Africa.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident Pty Ltd is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; subject to South African corporate income tax on worldwide income unless a special dispensation applies; no pass-through treatment). — The (Pty) Ltd company is first incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtains a CIPC registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident Pty Ltd is a separate taxable person under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; subject to South African corporate income tax on worldwide income unless a special dispensation applies; no pass-through treatment).

Available election forms

There is no 'check-the-box' election in South African tax law. A South African incorporated private company is automatically taxed at entity level. Available statutory elections include voluntary VAT registration under VAT Act section 23(1) where turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000; Small Business Corporation (SBC) concessional tax rate election for qualifying companies owned solely by natural persons with gross income below thresholds; and the worldwide employee share plan or section 8E anti-avoidance elections are not relevant. Hybrid mismatch rules follow the OECD/BEPS recommendations and are applied through the Income Tax Act; there is no elective hybrid-mismatch classification available to a standard Pty Ltd.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 in actual or expected taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period. Voluntary VAT registration threshold: ZAR 50,000 in taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months (with SARS agreement for persons not otherwise required to register).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, a supply of goods is zero-rated if the supplier delivers or ships the goods to an export destination outside South Africa and, in practice, the goods are exported within 90 days of the time of supply. The exporter must hold documentary proof of export and proof that the goods’ beneficial owner is outside South Africa. Services may be zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act where they are physically consumed outside South Africa by a non-resident recipient or non-resident person not carrying on business in South Africa, subject to the anti-avoidance rules preventing zero-rating where the services are supplied to a connected person resident in South Africa. Zero-rating is not automatic for local cold-chain logistics services rendered to foreign principals; each supply must meet the statutory export and supporting-document conditions. SARS may also permit zero-rating for deemed exports under customs controlled procedures and for supplies to approved export enterprises where special arrangements have been granted, but such arrangements require prior SARS approval.

  • Customs export declaration (SARS Customs SAD500) reflecting the exported goods and customs clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the VAT invoice and customs declaration
  • Proof of delivery and warehousing movement, including cold-chain temperature monitoring records where perishable pharma goods are involved
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment receipt showing receipt of foreign currency from the foreign buyer
  • Export permit or certificate of origin for controlled goods, if applicable

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls through a government e-invoicing clearance platform. A vendor must issue a full VAT invoice within 21 days of the supply; the invoice must contain prescribed particulars including seller name and VAT number, buyer name and address, description, consideration, tax fraction, and date of supply. SARS supports e-invoicing and electronic data interchange (EDI) where the invoice satisfies the legal requirements. VAT returns must be submitted through SARS eFiling, and SARS may require periodic electronic data reporting. No tax clearance stamp is required for invoices to be legally valid, but export documentation must be retained in auditable electronic or physical form.

SARS may audit export zero-rating claims on a post-verification basis using customs manifest data, bank payments, and container movement records. If the exporter fails to produce the prescribed documentation or does not receive the export proceeds within the 180-day exchange-control deadline without SARS approval, the zero-rating is reversed and VAT is assessed at 15% on the supply amount. The understatement penalty under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2014 may apply at rates from 10% up to 200% depending on the degree of behavior: substantial understatement, no reasonable grounds, or intentional tax evasion/stunning. SARS may also charge interest at the prescribed rate from the original payment date, demand refund repayment with interest, set off collections against other refunds, and refer fraudulent export schemes for criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is convertible for current-account transactions (trade, services, dividends, interest, royalties) through Authorised Dealers without prior SARB approval, subject to the furnishing of underlying documentary evidence. Capital-account transactions, including non-resident equity subscription, foreign loans, disposal of investments, and portfolio outflows, remain under exchange control oversight; equity capital injections are free of prior approval but must be reported by the Authorised Dealer, whereas non-resident loan funding above delegated bank limits requires FinSurv authorisation. Profit and dividend remittance to non-residents is permitted once a valid dividend declaration exists and the 20% dividends tax has been withheld and remitted to SARS; South Africa maintains a domestic currency restriction such that ZAR is not freely internationalised, and offshore banks can only transact in ZAR through a licensed local settlement participant or a sponsored account in SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory regime: for exported goods, the full invoice value (effectively 100% of proceeds) must be received in South Africa within 30 days from the date of shipment, and must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer by crediting a domestic ZAR account or, with bank approval, a Foreign Currency Account (FCA). No mandatory fixed percentage conversion ratio is separately imposed beyond full repatriation; proceeds retained in an FCA must be converted to ZAR or utilised for approved offshore payments within 12 months unless a longer retention limit is granted. Exporters may apply for negotiated periods or netting/exposure-compensation arrangements for multi-currency trade flows. Services export proceeds must be repatriated within 30 days from date of invoice or performance. Late receipt requires a penalty-or-notification proceeding through the bank, and non-submission of Form F (export payment evidence) is a criminal-offence risk.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) — Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), operating under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (as amended), assisted by the SARB Prudential Authority and the National Payment System Department.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium: purely offshore digital EMIs (for example Wise, Airwallex, or other non-resident electronic money institutions) cannot hold or clear ZAR independently in the South African National Payment System and are not Authorised Dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations. Such EMIs may only enter South Africa through sponsorship by a licensed domestic bank or a registered OSP (including the SARB-restricted payment-systems licence under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998). An offshore EMI cannot open a non-resident ZAR transaction account nor operate an FCA; proceeds routed through an EMI are not accepted by SARB as proof of export surrender or inward investment. Recommended use is restricted to low-value operational ancillaries; all treasury-critical inbound capital and outbound trade flows must pass through a tier-1 South African Authorised Dealer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) complete FICA onboarding and CDD at the chosen tier-1 Authorised Dealer; (2) non-resident investor executes and delivers the share-subscription agreement, CIPC allotment resolution, and any applicable SARB declaration; (3) the overseas bank remits ZAR or foreign currency via SWIFT MT103 to the company's designated ZAR beneficiary account at the Authorised Dealer, using the payment purpose code 'Equity Capital / Inward Direct Investment' and reference 'ZA_PTY_LTD share subscription'; (4) the Authorised Dealer verifies the beneficiary name and matches the credit to the company's inward-investment file, then files an Inward Investment Notification/Declaration with SARB FinSurv and issues a Certificate of Inward Investment evidencing the security; (5) funds are released to the operational account and are freely usable for capex and working capital. Larger equity subscriptions are permissible without prior FinSurv approval; however, non-resident loan funding, hybrid instruments, or deferred-payment structures that exceed the bank's delegated discretion require a formal FinSurv application and approval prior to disbursement. The full credit trail must be retained for five years.Clearing-rail hierarchy for ZA_PTY_LTD: (1) domestic supplier invoices, utility, payroll and freight charges are settled in ZAR via ACH/EFT through the ACB-SAMOS clearing system without FX involvement; (2) cross-border payments for imported refrigeration equipment, spare parts, and cold-chain consumables are executed by SWIFT MT103 outward remittance through the Authorised Dealer, supported by pro-forma invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, customs import entry (SAD500), and the appropriate foreign-payment purpose code; Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is generally required when the aggregate value of affected cross-border connected-person transactions exceeds ZAR 100 million in a year of assessment. A Master File becomes relevant where group annual consolidated turnover exceeds ZAR 1 billion or where SARS issues a formal documentation notice. Country-by-Country reporting follows the OECD-aligned threshold of consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million and is effected through SARS notification.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but it applies functionally equivalent substance controls through the place of effective management (POEM) test, controlled foreign company attribution under section 9D, section 31 transfer-pricing recharacterisation, section 103 general anti-avoidance rules, and beneficial-ownership and limitation-on-benefits clauses in tax treaties. A cold storage entity with no local premises, no permanent staff, no inventory risk, and no board activity in South Africa can be treated as a conduit or shell, with profits attributed to the true economic owner and treaty relief denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Filing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under the TAA carries a fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years; SARS must additionally collect the full tax shortfall plus interest and may impose an understatement penalty of up to 150% of the deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors who know of or approve false returns can be personally prosecuted. SARS may invoke representative liability under sections 159 and 162 TAA to recover unpaid tax from directors personally, supported by civil restitution, attachment orders, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard or recharacterise the arrangement under section 103 GAAR and section 31, recompute taxable income, apply a secondary adjustment, and impose an understatement penalty ranging from 50% to 150%; the arrangement may also be void under the common-law sham doctrine.

Director personal liability: Once the corporate veil is pierced, shareholders and directors are personally liable for corporate tax debts and civil restitution. Courts may issue director disqualification, travel bans, and asset preservation orders, and criminal penalties may follow under the TAA.
Transfer Pricing / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: SARS will make a primary transfer-pricing adjustment to the arm’s-length amount, impose a secondary adjustment for recharacterised dividends or loans, assess interest, and levy an understatement penalty of up to 150% of the shortfall; CFC income may be attributed under section 9D.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unsubstantiated intercompany pricing or sign false TP declarations may be held jointly liable for the additional tax as representatives. Civil restitution, travel bans, director disqualification, and imprisonment for fraud are available.
Anti-Money Laundering / FICA Breach

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) allows administrative penalties of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention and, in money-laundering cases, criminal prosecution under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act (POCA) with severe custodial sentences and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally prosecuted where they knew or ought to have known of laundering activity; individuals risk civil restitution, fines, travel bans, prohibition orders, confiscation of personal assets, and imprisonment.
Failure to Register and Withholding Default

Statutory sanction: SARS may impose non-compliance penalties, default assessments, daily interest, and penalties for late payments; withheld taxes are trust money, and deliberate non-payment is treated as criminal theft by SARS, with imprisonment of up to five years on conviction.

Director personal liability: Section 162 TAA makes directors and persons responsible for managing the company jointly and severally liable for unpaid withheld taxes and VAT. Piercing the corporate veil, travel bans, civil recovery, disqualification, and custodial sentences apply to directors who knowingly allow the default.
