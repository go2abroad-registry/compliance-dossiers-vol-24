Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – National Taxation and Customs Authority; eFiling portal: https://efiling.sars.gov.za; official website: https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

For a South African-incorporated (Pty) Ltd: register the company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the company registration number, then apply to SARS for an income tax reference number via the RA01 registration form (or via eFiling workflow). For VAT, submit VAT101 application through eFiling when taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months; voluntary VAT registration may be possible if taxable supplies exceed R50,000. A foreign-incorporated trucking operator with no local incorporation but with a South African permanent establishment or taxable supplies must register as a 'non-resident taxpayer' using SARS non-resident registration procedures, provide proof of foreign corporate existence, appoint a domestic tax representative where required, and register for VAT under the same threshold rules if the enterprise has a fixed establishment in South Africa or qualifies for the foreign supplier e-services regime. Supporting documents include the certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation, proof of bank account, and SARS registration documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available relevant elections include: (1) voluntary VAT vendor election under s23(2) of the VAT Act if taxable supplies exceed R50,000 but are below the compulsory R1,000,000 threshold; (2) election for the appointment of a tax representative for non-residents under the Tax Administration Act; (3) for inbound cross-border transport arrangements, election of an agent or TIR carnet holder as customs representative under the Customs and Excise Act; (4) in specific circumstances, election to apply proportionate VAT input apportionment under s17(1) of the VAT Act, and (5) hybrid instrument elections under the Income Tax Act for certain cross-border finance arrangements (applicable only if trucking operations involve hybrid debt or hybrid equity instruments). No check-the-box election is available for treating the (Pty) Ltd as flow-through.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (one million South African Rand) in taxable supplies over the preceding 12-month period for compulsory VAT vendor registration; voluntary registration may be granted where taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in the preceding 12 months

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from South Africa are zero-rated under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act if the goods are movable and are exported to a non-resident recipient within the statutory export period (normally within 90 days of supply, or such extended period as SARS allows), with documentary proof of actual removal from South Africa. For transport services, s11(1)(k) zero-rates international transport for the carriage of goods or passengers to a destination outside South Africa or from outside South Africa into South Africa. In the TIR cross-border trucking context, the supply of road freight services from a South African origin to a foreign destination is zero-rated, provided the transport is performed for export and the supplier retains a complete export evidence chain. This includes a valid customs export declaration, CMR waybill, TIR carnet, commercial invoice, and proof that the goods physically left South Africa. Where services are rendered to non-resident customers but no physical export of goods is involved, general offshore services zero-rating under s11(2)(l) may apply if the services are supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time, and the services are not used in South Africa. A VAT refund may be claimed for excess input VAT attributable to such zero-rated supplies, subject to normal vendor verification and audit controls.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / Ex electronic lodgement via SARS Customs)
  • TIR Carnet (issued by the South African TIR issuing association; used as customs transit guarantee and discharge record)
  • CMR Waybill (international consignment note for road freight)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent air/sea document where multimodal transport is used
  • Commercial Invoice (export VAT invoice containing full s20 VAT particulars)
  • Packing List and weight/axle certificate (where applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Currency Banking Receipt (proof of payment in convertible currency)
  • Proof of Export Delivery (signed POD / border exit stamp / electronic customs discharge message from SARS or SACU customs office)
  • Bank Settlement Certificate or SARB exchange control documentation evidencing receipt of export/sales proceeds within the prescribed 180-day period

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping for all VAT invoices. However, SARS has progressively implemented e-invoicing standards aligned with the OECD/Peppol framework, and large vendors must be able to submit e-invoices via the SARS eFiling / e-invoicing platform. Customs clearance for cross-border and TIR operations is fully electronic: exporters and agents must lodge customs declarations and movement messages through the SARS Customs Modernisation Programme (SAD500 series), and TIR transports require submission of the TIR-EPD electronic pre-declaration and closure of the TIR operation at destination. Electronic tax invoices must contain all s20 VAT Act particulars and be retained in the vendor's records for audit purposes.

SARS and customs officials apply high scrutiny to zero-rated exports. If the required export documentation is incomplete, false, or destroyed, SARS may reverse the zero-rating and assess VAT at 15% on the value of supply, together with understatement penalties under s222 of the Tax Administration Act (up to 200% of the shortfall in cases of intentional tax evasion). Additional penalties and interest apply under the Value-Added Tax Act and Customs and Excise Act, including administrative fines and possible forfeiture of goods. Misdeclaration or fraudulent use of TIR carnets exposes the holder to recovery under the IRU guarantee chain, suspension of TIR authorization, and criminal prosecution. The (Pty) Ltd must retain all export records for at least five years; failure to repatriate freight proceeds within 180 days may also trigger exchange-control penalties and potential criminal liability under the Currency and Exchanges Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a managed-float ZAR regime. Current account transactions are freely convertible through an authorised dealer upon documented proof (invoices, contracts, TIR Carnets and CMR waybills). Capital account transactions are restricted and require prior FinSurv approval or an authorised-dealer dispensation. Non-resident profit remittance is permitted subject to a SARS tax clearance certificate, the dividend declaration, the authorised dealer's dividend form, and proof that the original inward investment was properly registered (Form MP for intercompany loans or the inward equity notification issued at the time of the capital import). A 20% dividends withholding tax, or a reduced rate under an applicable double taxation agreement, is levied at the point of remittance. There is no blanket 'convertibility percentage' for capital, but all current export earnings are subject to the realisation rules in the next field.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 6, the full invoice value of exported goods and services must be made available to the exporter's South African authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of export (the statutory realisation period). There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; the mandatory element is repatriation and accrual to the authorised dealer, after which the exporter may convert to ZAR or, with prior approval, retain the proceeds in a Foreign Currency Account. For a cross-border TIR trucking operator, every consignment must be evidenced by the TIR Carnet serial number, the CMR waybill, the commercial invoice and the customs transit log, so that SARB and SARS can reconcile 100% of freight revenue within the 180-day window.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1956 (as amended). Day-to-day authority is delegated to Authorised Dealers, being the South African banks licensed by the SARB under section 9A of the Act; the National Treasury retains policy ownership of the exchange control framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low – legally constrained. A South African-resident company may not maintain operational, payables or receivables accounts at unlicensed offshore digital EMI platforms (Revolut Business, Airwallex, Wise or similar) because such platforms are not SARB authorised dealers and holding foreign currency outside the authorised-dealer perimeter is prohibited under the Exchange Control Regulations absent a FinSurv exemption. EMIs can only support peripheral foreign-leg payments, e.g., a bank-issued corporate card attached to the South African authorised-dealer account, where ZAR exposure always remains with the licensed bank. For TIR trucking operations, the compliant practical alternative is a licensed fuel-card clearing network (Engen, Shell, TotalEnergies or similar) settled in-country by the authorised dealer, not an offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection must be routed exclusively through a SARB authorised dealer in South Africa. The non-resident parent or incorporating shareholder remits via SWIFT MT103 from its own bank account into the (Pty) Ltd ZAR account at the authorised dealer, tagged with the statutory balance-of-payments purpose code for inward direct investment in equity and the client reference of the share subscription. The dealer will require: (i) a foreign investment application/acknowledgement under the Exchange Control Regulations 1956 (Form MP where the injection is structured as an intercompany loan); (ii) board resolution, share register and CIPC amended return; (iii) SARS registration confirmation; (iv) a source-of-funds declaration identifying all ultimate beneficial owners; and (v) for loan capital, a signed loan agreement compliant with SARB dispensation thresholds. Funds are escrowed only pending verification of the above; thereafter they are fully available for working capital. For a trucking start-up the typical split is 51-70% issued share capital and the balance as a USD intercompany loan, with the Form MP regime applying to the loan component.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 outbound transfers from the ZAR or Foreign Currency Account for fuel-card settlement, insurance premiums, spare parts procurement, TIR guarantee premia and road tolls; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) for the import of trailers, truck-tractors and tyres from EU/Asian suppliers; (3) SADC-region settlement of truck-stop and toll expenses via ZAR through SIRESS (SADC Integrated Regional Electronic Settlement System) where the counterpart bank participates; (4) fuel-card clearing networks (Engen, Shell, TotalEnergies, etc.) issuing consolidated monthly MT103 settlements to the card brand in the country of purchase; and (5) border-clearance payments made through licensed customs clearing agents on a non-cash mandate, reconciled monthly to the TIR Carnet and national duty/VAT accounts.Deliverable forward exchange contracts (FECs) on ZAR/USD, ZAR/EUR and ZAR/BWP with the authorised dealer covering actual and anticipated fuel, maintenance and operational spend, evidenced by underlying CMR/TIR contracts (SARB trade-contingent documentation required).Non-deliverable forwards (offshore ZAR NDFs) permissible only where the South African resident establishes a firm underlying commercial exposure to the authorised dealer and the counterparty is an offshore bank; the onshore ZAR NDF market (TOM/NEXT) is not authorised for domestic corporates.Deliverable currency options (call/put) on ZAR pairs purchased from the authorised dealer to cap hedging costs and protect freight margins against adverse directional currency movements.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) for converting the foreign-currency shareholder loan into ZAR-denominated obligations, requiring ISDA 2002/CSA documentation, an underlying loan agreement, and SARB/FinSurv approval where the swap creates a security interest over South African assets.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (ordinary South African company tax rate for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% South African dividends tax imposed on dividends paid by a South African resident company, collected from the beneficial owner of the dividend. Resident beneficial owners are generally exempt; non-resident shareholders are subject to the 20% tax unless a double-taxation agreement reduces the rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5,000,000 aggregate annual value of cross-border connected-person transactions (including management fees, fleet leases, fuel card clearing, loans, and cost recharges). Above this threshold contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation satisfying the OECD Guidelines must be maintained. A Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting obligation may arise for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above ZAR 10,000,000,000, and SARS may in any case require a full Local File where connected-person transactions are higher value or high risk.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not impose a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but substance is a decisive legal and factual element. SARS and the South African courts apply substance-over-form, the general anti-avoidance provisions in s80A–s80L of the Income Tax Act, the controlled foreign company rules in s9D, and the s31 arm's-length principle. Every South African trucking company must demonstrate actual operational substance: locally employed drivers, owned or leased vehicles that are managed and controlled in South Africa, fuel and procurement contracts concluded locally, maintenance and logistics decision-making located in South Africa, and board minutes reflecting commercial risk-bearing decisions. A company with only a registered address, passive invoices, and foreign decision-making is vulnerable to GAAR attack, CFC inclusion, and transfer-pricing recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Suppressed Turnover and False TIR Carnet Reporting

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall for intentional tax evasion, criminal conviction for tax fraud with a fine of up to ZAR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years, plus interest at the prescribed rate on unreported tax and potential forfeiture of the TIR guarantee or bond. SARS may also raise an assessment on an estimated basis and impose additional customs penalties under the Customs and Excise Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorise the false reporting can be joined by SARS as personally liable for the unremitted tax under the Tax Administration Act. The controlling mind may be criminally prosecuted personally, receive a travel ban pending finalisation of a tax matter, be disqualified from serving as a director under the Companies Act, and be subject to civil restitution and asset recovery proceedings.
Shell Company / Letterbox Operation Without Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS recharacterises the arrangement under s80A–s80L, recomputes taxable income as if the arm's-length principle had been observed, disallows artificial deductions, applies an aggravated understatement penalty of 150%–200%, and may include passive overseas profits in the South African company's taxable income under the CFC regime. Tax clearance and customs bond approval can be revoked, and an aggravated penalty can be imposed for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: A director acting as the sole mind of a shell company can be held personally responsible for knowingly permitting tax avoidance arrangements, may be held civilly liable for corporate tax debts if the veil is pierced, and can be imprisoned for up to 10 years on conviction for participation in an impermissible tax avoidance arrangement. SARS may also apply civil asset restraints where the entity is used as a conduit for illicit outbound profit stripping.
Disguised Profit Repatriation Avoiding Dividend Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: SARS disallows the deduction under s23A and s31, recharacterises the payment as a taxable dividend in the hands of the recipient, imposes dividends tax at 20% if the recipient is a non-resident, and imposes interest withholding tax at 15% if interest was disguised, together with an understatement penalty of 50%–200% of the resulting tax shortfall. The transaction can be set aside as void under the general anti-avoidance provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under withholding-tax statutes if the company collected or should have withheld dividends tax or interest withholding tax and failed to remit it to SARS. SARS can pursue director personal assets, apply travel restraints, and in egregious cases institute criminal proceedings for theft of SARS revenue and fraud.
AML and Financial Crime - Undeclared Cash Freight Payments and Suspicious Cross-Border Settlement

Statutory sanction: South African accountable institutions and businesses that engage in suspicious or high-value cash transactions must comply with the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001. Non-compliance exposes the company to administrative penalties, criminal fines, freezing orders, and confiscation of assets under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act. Customs may seize the TIR fleet and suspend the company's bonded carrier authorisation.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be prosecuted as natural persons for failing to implement an adequate anti-money-laundering risk management programme or knowingly facilitating suspicious transactions. Conviction may result in personal fines, imprisonment, director disqualification, and confiscation of assets obtained through unlawful activities. Directors may also face civil liability in damages to SARS or third parties for enabling unlawful cross-border fund movements.
