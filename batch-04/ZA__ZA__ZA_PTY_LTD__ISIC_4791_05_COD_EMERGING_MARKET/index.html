Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; head office: 299 Bronkhorst Street, Brooklyn, Pretoria 0181.

E-government filing portal

Domestic (Pty) Ltd: on incorporation under the Companies Act 71 of 2008, the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) issues the registration certificate (COR14.3); the company must apply for a Company Income Tax Reference Number through SARS eFiling within 21 business days of becoming liable to register, submitting the COR14.3, Memorandum of Incorporation, and bank confirmation. VAT registration is a separate compulsory step: the company applies on the VAT 101 form within 21 days after the value of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period exceeds ZAR 1,000,000, or voluntarily from ZAR 50,000 with SARS approval; SARS issues a VAT vendor registration with an effective date calculated from the date of liability. Foreign entity: a non-resident company deriving South African-source income or having a permanent establishment must register as a non-resident company with SARS eFiling to obtain a non-resident taxpayer reference number; separate VAT registration is compulsory when its South African taxable supplies exceed the ZAR 1,000,000 threshold, and SARS may appoint or require a registered representative taxpayer for compliance purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box election; a (Pty) Ltd is automatically a 'company' under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 (s1) and is taxed at entity level at the 27% corporate income tax rate for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2023, with a graduated 0%-27% rate available to qualifying Small Business Corporations that elect s12E treatment. Residence is determined by incorporation or place of effective management; hybrid mismatch rules under s23N recharacterise instruments and arrangements lacking economic substance. Relevant elections include voluntary VAT grouping (fiscal unity) under s50 of the VAT Act, corporate restructure rollover elections under ss42-47 of the Income Tax Act, and hybrid instrument elections under s8E/8EA. Dividends, interest, and royalty withholding taxes (20%, 15%, and 15% respectively) apply at source and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration applies when taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period exceed ZAR 1,000,000; voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000 per annum at SARS' discretion; SARS may compel registration where economic activity exceeds the threshold or where registration is justified in the public interest.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act to movable goods supplied to a recipient outside South Africa where the vendor or an agent on its behalf physically removes the goods from the Republic within 90 days of the time of supply and the vendor retains documentary proof of export. For mail-order and cash-on-delivery e-commerce, the vendor must evidence physical dispatch beyond the South African border through courier or customs movement records; services exported to non-residents are zero-rated under s11(2)(k) only where the recipient is outside South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected to South African immovable property or movable property located in South Africa. Imported electronic services supplied by foreign vendors to South African customers are taxable in South Africa at 15%, which does not affect the South African exporter's zero-rating position on genuine offshore dispatches.

  • Customs SAD500 Export Declaration (South African customs clearance record evidencing physical export)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or multimodal transport document) naming the South African vendor as shipper
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (vendor-issued, showing zero-rated status and SARS VAT registration number)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in South Africa
  • Courier manifest and delivery confirmation for COD mail-order parcels demonstrating clearance across the customs border

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in force in South Africa. Vendors must issue a full tax invoice for supplies exceeding ZAR 5,000 and an abridged tax invoice for supplies of ZAR 5,000 or less, containing the mandatory fields prescribed by s20 of the VAT Act (vendor name, address and VAT number, invoice serial number, date, description, quantity, consideration and VAT amount). SARS is progressively introducing an e-invoicing interoperability framework aligned with UN/CEFACT data standards, but the prevailing compliance obligation is electronic filing of VAT 201 returns through eFiling, with bookkeeping records retained as an audit file. There is no government-operated live transaction approval API for standard mail-order or cash-on-delivery retail sales.

If goods are not exported within 90 days of the time of supply or are subsequently returned to South Africa, the zero-rating lapses and output VAT at 15% becomes payable retroactively from the original supply date, with interest at the prescribed rate. SARS may disallow input VAT credits and export refund claims where documentary substantiation is incomplete; prescription periods for additional assessments do not run where fraud, misrepresentation, or non-disclosure is established. Understatement penalties under ss222-223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 apply on a sliding scale: 0% for a bona fide inadvertent error with reasonable care, 10% for a substantial understatement, 25% for failure to take reasonable care, 50% for gross negligence, and 100% for intentional tax evasion, with increased percentages for repeat cases; administrative non-compliance penalties may also be imposed for record-keeping failures. Deliberate false export declarations create criminal liability under s104 of the VAT Act and s235 of the Tax Administration Act, including fines and imprisonment, and non-repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days may additionally breach South African Reserve Bank exchange control regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a floating ZAR and a partially controlled capital account. Current-account items — trade payments, dividends, interest, royalties, and freight — are freely settled through a licensed Authorised Dealer without case-by-case FinSurv approval, provided the underlying obligation is documented and SARS tax clearance is produced where applicable (e.g., dividend remittances require a withholding-tax clearance event). Capital-account inflows, such as a non-resident share subscription into a (Pty) Ltd, are permitted but must be routed through an ADLA with an inward Balance of Payments (BOP) declaration, while outbound capital transfers, including loan repayments to foreign shareholders and offshore investments, are restricted by FinSurv limits and the loop-structure rules for non-resident-controlled domestic companies. The practical profit-remittance chain is: after-tax profits, declared dividend in terms of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and the company's memorandum of incorporation, non-resident shareholder completing a Non-Resident Declaration and obtaining bank validation of the original inward-funding evidence and SARS dividend-tax clearance where applicable, and payment effected in the source currency via SWIFT MT103. The main convertibility hurdles for the target e-commerce COD business are: (a) banks may treat aggregated cross-border logistics settlements as potentially unlicensed remittance unless fully reconciled to underlying sales; (b) SARS may require tax clearance and good standing for any distribution; and (c) customer markets in Africa and Asia may have their own convertibility and surrender rules that delay receipts beyond the SARB 180-day export window.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds of the (Pty) Ltd must be received in full within 180 days from the date of shipment, or an earlier date if stipulated in the commercial contract, and 100% of the foreign currency proceeds must be surrendered to the South African banking system. The exporter must either credit the funds to a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an ADLA or convert them into ZAR at the ADLA's ruling rate. There is no statutory percentage that may remain offshore; retention in an FCA is a discretionary ADLA/FinSurv approval, not a conversion exemption, and retention above approved limits or beyond approved maturities triggers administrative penalty and cancellation of foreign-exchange allocations. For the cash-on-delivery model, the exporter must evidence that the payment received from a logistics remittance partner or EMI is trade export proceeds and not a fee or remittance of a person, supported by SAD500 customs declarations, courier waybills, order-level invoices, and a reconciliation statement. Non-compliance, including late receipt, short-shipment, or under-declaration, exposes the company to SARB exchange-control penalties, SARS customs interest, and bank de-risking.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Currency and Exchanges Regulations 1963, and the Authorised Dealer in Foreign Exchange (ADLA) Manual issued by FinSurv.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-Low. Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar digital EMI/PSPs are not authorised as banks under the Banks Act 94 of 1990; in South Africa they may operate only through a licensed ADLA partner or as a payment service provider under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998, holding settlement accounts at a domestic bank. The (Pty) Ltd may use such an EMI for high-frequency cross-border collection from its logistics remittance partners, but the EMI cannot lawfully hold the company's primary ZAR liquidity, execute SARB surrender obligations, or accept inward capital subscriptions. Any EMI corridor must be documented as a clearing and payout aggregator with bank-to-bank funding, never as an unlicensed treasury deposit.
