Governing statute

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up share capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 71 of 2008 for a Proprietary Limited Company; a nominal issue of at least one share is sufficient. No escrow account is required. Shares may be issued to non-residents.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a purely virtual EdTech operation. If a commercial office is occupied, the lessor must supply a Certificate of Occupancy confirming compliance with the National Building Regulations, and the local fire authority must issue a fire safety clearance before staff physically begin work.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up share capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 71 of 2008 for a Proprietary Limited Company; a nominal issue of at least one share is sufficient. No escrow account is required. Shares may be issued to non-residents.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

Register the foreign entity as an external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) under the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Thereafter apply for income tax registration with SARS via eFiling by submitting the CIPC registration extract, constitutional documents, and SARS registration forms. Upon acceptance, SARS issues a 10-digit income tax reference number (TIN). If the person supplies taxable supplies that cross the VAT registration threshold, file a VAT101 application through eFiling or a SARS branch, whereupon a VAT vendor number is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African resident company is taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide income; a foreign external company carrying on a South African trade is taxed on income with a South African source or deemed source, without pass-through attribution.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification election; entity treatment follows legal form and tax residence. Relevant elections are (i) the headquarter-company election under section 9I of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 for qualifying South African resident companies with at least 80% foreign ownership; (ii) the VAT group election under section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991, allowing group members to be treated as a single vendor; and (iii) hybrid financial instrument and hybrid entity rules apply mandatorily under sections 8F, 8E and related provisions, with no elective mismatch treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory VAT registration when taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed ZAR 1 million in any 12 consecutive months; a foreign supplier of electronic services, including EdTech subscription services, is obliged to register for South African VAT irrespective of the ZAR 1 million threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated where they are genuinely exported from South Africa, evidenced by the Customs export declaration and proof of export within 60 days of the tax invoice date unless an extended period has been granted by SARS. Services are zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act where the recipient is a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected with South African immovable or movable property. Digital language-learning subscription services supplied to foreign non-resident end users qualify as exported services where the use and benefit of the service occurs outside South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing actual exportation
  • Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate or bank payment statement evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Copy of the VAT invoice and contract with the foreign customer

South Africa does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, API-driven pre-clearance, or digital stamping for VAT invoices. SARS accepts electronic tax invoices provided they satisfy the form and content requirements of section 20 of the VAT Act. SARS eFiling and SARS eInvoicing facilitate electronic filing and document exchange, but issuance and retention remain on an audit-basis rather than a transactional clearance mandate.

SARS will disallow zero-rating and claw back 15% VAT, plus interest, where the requisite export documentation is missing or the vendor fails to prove that services were supplied to a non-resident for offshore consumption. The Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 imposes understatement penalties determined by conduct, reaching 150% of the assessed shortfall for intentional tax evasion. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, false customs declarations, and false invoices may trigger criminal prosecution under the Customs and Excise Act and the Tax Administration Act, with imprisonment exposure. For exchange control purposes, failure to remit export proceeds within 180 days may also expose the company to administrative penalties imposed by the Financial Surveillance Department of the South African Reserve Bank.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a dual-regime convertibility framework. Current-account flows (EdTech subscription export revenue, intercompany service fees, royalties, and profit/dividend remittances) are freely convertible: ZAR may be sold to an Authorised Dealer for such payments upon production of supporting commercial documentation, without requiring prior SARB approval. Capital-account flows (foreign equity subscription, intercompany loans, offshore treasury placements and acquisitions) remain subject to residual exchange-control approvals under the Currency and Exchanges Act; Authorised Dealers may approve standard inward and outward transactions within delegated limits, while amounts above thresholds (for example, inward loans in aggregate exceeding approximately ZAR 15 million) or structurally complex arrangements require express FinSurv approval. Profit remittance hurdles are documentary rather than substantive: remitting dividends to non-resident shareholders requires (i) Form MP336 (annual dividend application), (ii) audited financial statements or an auditor's confirmation of distributable reserves satisfying the Companies Act (No. 71 of 2008) s.46 solvency and liquidity test, (iii) a SARS tax status certificate and proof of 20% dividends tax withheld or exempted, and (iv) beneficiary identity consistent with CRS/FATCA records. Interest and principal repayment on inward loans requires registration of the inward loan with the Authorised Dealer/FinSurv (purpose code 100090) and compliance with the minimum tenor and arm's-length interest provisions of the prevailing FinSurv loan guidelines. No convertibility restriction applies to bona fide current-account receipts, provided the underlying documentation demonstrates genuine trade in services.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 8 and the FinSurv Exchange Control Manual, every exporter of goods or services - including cross-border digital education services and app-store subscription revenue - must take all reasonable steps to ensure that the full value of export proceeds is received in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within the prescribed period (currently 90 days from date of invoice or settlement for service exports, as extended by FinSurv updates; a 180-day extension may be applied for on demonstrable commercial grounds). Proceeds must be repatriated into the exporter's domestic ZAR account or a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) maintained with an Authorised Dealer; repatriation itself is compulsory. Since the 2016-2017 exchange-control liberalisation, there is no mandatory conversion ratio: the historical requirement to surrender a fixed percentage of export receipts to ZAR has been abolished, and exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in an FCA and convert at their discretion on prevailing commercial rates. A formal surrender certificate is not required for service exports, but proceeds must be identifiably trade-related and traceable to the underlying export contracts. For EdTech revenue settled through Apple App Store or Google Play Billing, the South African contracting entity (ZA_PTY_LTD) must ensure that marketplace net remittances are routed to its South African bank account within the 90-day receipt window; platform commissions of 15-30% must be substantiated by periodic platform settlement reports, and any retention of proceeds offshore beyond the permitted period, or conversion undertaken outside an Authorised Dealer, constitutes an exchange-control offence attracting penalties and potential enforcement action.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which administers the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 (as amended), in conjunction with Authorised Dealers (South African banking institutions licensed as foreign exchange dealers) and Authorised Dealers in Foreign Exchange with Limited Authority.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5,000,000 aggregate annual connected-person cross-border transactions and interest-bearing arrangements triggers contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; multinational groups with consolidated turnover of at least ZAR 10,000,000,000 must file a Country-by-Country report and should retain Master File and Local File documentation on request.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not administer a separate 'ESR' statute in the offshore sense, but the GAAR under s80A-80L disregards transactions that lack commercial substance, s31 recharacterises transfer pricing without real functional allocation, s9D attributes undistributed CFC profits, and the MLI Principal Purpose Test denies treaty benefits. An EdTech structure holding excessive profits or IP in a zero-tax jurisdiction with no employees, premises, or decision-making will fail these substance standards.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; on conviction, imprisonment for up to five years and/or a fine. Civil understatement penalty of 200% for intentional tax evasion, plus prescribed-rate interest and collection costs.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or participate in the evasion may be prosecuted, ordered to make restitution, and subjected to preservation and travel-banishment orders; the corporate veil can be pierced under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 if the company was used fraudulently.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the consideration to an arm's length price under s31, disallows the excessive deduction, imposes an understatement penalty of up to 200%, and levies interest from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the return may be personally assessed for unpaid withholding or corporate tax if the arrangement is a reportable arrangement designed to avoid tax, and the company's assets are dissipated.
Shell Company / Sham Transaction / GAAR

Statutory sanction: GAAR permits SARS to combine, disregard, or re-characterise the transaction; the MLI Principal Purpose Test denies treaty protection; the CFC provisions of s9D attribute the underlying profit to South African shareholders; an understatement penalty of 100%-200% may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who implement a structure with a sole or main purpose of tax avoidance may be jointly and severally liable for evaded tax, exposed to civil damages, disqualified under the Companies Act, and barred from leaving South Africa under restraint orders.
Dividends / Interest / Royalty Withholding Tax Default

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax becomes a statutory debt due by the company as agent; SARS recovers the unpaid amount together with a 10%-200% understatement penalty and daily prescribed-rate interest, on top of the original tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for withholding taxes under the Tax Administration Act when the company disposes of assets, fails to pay the liability, and is placed in liquidation; travel bans and director disqualification are available remedies.
