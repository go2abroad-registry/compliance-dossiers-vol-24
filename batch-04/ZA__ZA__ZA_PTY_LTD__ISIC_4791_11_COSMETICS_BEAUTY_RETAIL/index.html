Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), headed by the Commissioner for SARS; official portal: https://www.sars.gov.za; eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

A South African private company (Pty) Ltd is registered for income tax automatically upon incorporation through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) integration with SARS; the company receives an income tax reference number. Where automatic activation is absent, the company must apply through SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using the prescribed registration applications and submit the certified memorandum of incorporation, Companies Registration Certificate, proof of physical and postal address, banking details, and identity documents of directors and shareholders. A foreign-owned or foreign-incorporated entity deriving taxable supplies from South Africa must apply for VAT registration using VAT 101 within 21 days after exceeding the compulsory VAT threshold; a single SARS taxpayer reference number covers income tax, VAT, PAYE, customs and other administered taxes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A South African private company (Pty) Ltd is registered for income tax automatically upon incorporation through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) integration with SARS; the company receives an income tax reference number. Where automatic activation is absent, the company must apply through SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using the prescribed registration applications and submit the certified memorandum of incorporation, Companies Registration Certificate, proof of physical and postal address, banking details, and identity documents of directors and shareholders. A foreign-owned or foreign-incorporated entity deriving taxable supplies from South Africa must apply for VAT registration using VAT 101 within 21 days after exceeding the compulsory VAT threshold; a single SARS taxpayer reference number covers income tax, VAT, PAYE, customs and other administered taxes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or analogous flow-through entity classification election exists under South African law; a (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed at entity level as a resident company if incorporated in South Africa. Statutory tax elections are limited to specific corporate, group and hybrid mismatch provisions; South Africa applies hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act, including provisions addressing hybrid debt and hybrid equity instruments, and no entity-level classification election is available to recharacterise a (Pty) Ltd as a pass-through vehicle.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration: ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies within any 12-month period; voluntary VAT registration: ZAR 50,000 in taxable supplies within any 12-month period, subject to conditions including a minimum two-year registration period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A vendor may apply the 0% VAT rate to exported movable goods where the goods are supplied to a non-resident recipient and the goods are removed from South Africa within 90 days after the date of supply, or where the vendor itself exports the goods, and the prescribed documentary proof under section 55 of the VAT Act is retained. For services, zero-rating is available when the services are supplied to a non-resident recipient and are not performed in South Africa or are not supplied directly in connection with movable property situated in South Africa; the vendor must obtain and retain sufficient commercial evidence to show the recipient is outside South Africa and that the services are exported.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing removal from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of receipt of export payment in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the exported cosmetic goods
  • Certified copy of the non-resident recipient's identity, registration or address evidence
  • Proof of delivery or logistics tracking documentation supporting the export route

South Africa has no general real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital tax stamp mandate for standard VAT vendors as of the current compliance cycle. E-invoicing is permissible if the invoice satisfies the requirements of section 20 of the VAT Act; SARS may require certain categories of taxpayers to submit data electronically through eFiling or EDI-based channels, and in audit or verification engagements SARS may prescribe specific electronic export documentation formats, but no mandatory continuous fiscal clearance infrastructure is in force.

SARS rigorously audits export zero-rating claims; if the vendor cannot produce the prescribed customs, logistics and foreign-currency remittance documents, SARS will raise an additional VAT assessment at 15% on the full supply value, with statutory interest. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, an understatement penalty may be imposed of up to 200% of the tax shortfall depending on conduct, and the Value-Added Tax Act permits additional penalties and criminal prosecution for fraudulent or knowingly false export declarations or tax invoices. Clawback exposure is retrospective and SARS may disallow input tax credits associated with an export transaction where the export conditions are not evidenced to SARS's satisfaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is convertible for current account transactions, including trade-in-goods and services, cross-border fees, and dividends, subject to documentation and authorisation-dealer oversight. Capital account transactions are restricted under South Africa's exchange control regime; inward equity contributions, shareholder loans, and outward loan repayments or portfolio investments require FinSurv approval, a designated Authorised Dealer, and the filing of prescribed capital-flow returns. Profit remittances of after-tax distributable profits are permitted, but must be supported by audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate, and a completed outward payment form. Synthetic convertibility constraints are present for an online DTC retail business because payment gateway settlements from Shopfify/PayPal are usually aggregated and may be routed through foreign payment processors, requiring reconciliation with underlying sales invoices to avoid adverse classification as 'mixed remittances' or 'unidentified inward transfers'.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days of the date of shipment. The exporter must provide a customs export declaration and the relevant invoice. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into ZAR; export receipts may be credited to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an authorised dealer. The export proceeds must be repatriated in full and may be retained in the FCA indefinitely, provided the exporter completes periodic compliance reporting to SARB FinSurv and the authorised dealer. For online cosmetics retail, all Shopify and PayPal settlement flows that are generated by sale of goods to non-residents constitute export proceeds and must be recorded and settled into the company's local FCA or ZAR account within the statutory 180-day window rather than being held permanently offshore.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

FEASIBILITY_RATING: Medium-to-Low for use as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI service providers such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex are not licensed as banks in South Africa and cannot provide a South African domestic clearing account or access to the national payment system. They may operate legally as cross-border settlement agents for inbound and outbound flows, subject to the company's ultimate reliance on a licensed authorised dealer. The Banks Act, 94 of 1990 and the National Payment System Act, 78 of 1998 prohibit unlicensed entities from accepting deposits or providing payment aggregation services to South African residents without a banking or PSP license. Therefore, the feasible use is limited to: (i) collection of retail card sales in foreign currencies via Shopfify/PayPal, (ii) settlement into a ZAR bank account through a registered payment intermediary, and (iii) managing a notional multi-currency ledger; it cannot replace the domestic corporate bank account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident corporate shareholder structure with no South African physical office

Root cause: Non-resident corporate shareholder structure with no South African physical office

Defense evidence: Certified Memorandum of Incorporation, share register and UBO declarations, notarised board resolution appointing a local authorised signatory, signed-off lease agreement or serviced-office agreement in Gauteng, and six months of projected bank statements demonstrating operational substance.
High-volume, low-value digital retail receipts from Shopfify/PayPal settling from multiple foreign processors

Root cause: High-volume, low-value digital retail receipts from Shopfify/PayPal settling from multiple foreign processors

Defense evidence: Platform settlement reports, a reconciliation ledger mapping each settlement to the underlying sales invoices, customer IP-level geotagging data, and a clear breakdown showing no crypto-related currency flows or transaction-laundering patterns.
Inward shareholder loan / capital injection classified as a 'covered foreign entity' or 'suspicious financing'

Root cause: Inward shareholder loan / capital injection classified as a 'covered foreign entity' or 'suspicious financing'

Defense evidence: Signed shareholders' resolution, subscription agreement or loan agreement, annotated SWIFT MT103 with the correct BOP purpose code, proof of the corporate ownership chain, and the company's external tax advisor's confirmation that the transaction is not a back-to-back loan or disguised dividend.
Retail end-customers located in high-risk sanctions-controlled countries

Root cause: Retail end-customers located in high-risk sanctions-controlled countries

Defense evidence: Shopify store terms of sale excluding delivery to sanctioned countries, real-time IP/geoblock settings, KYC/AML screening log of all customer transactions, and an audit trail demonstrating that payment cards issued in high-risk jurisdictions are automatically declined.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank directly to the South African authorised dealer with the company's bank account as ultimate credit. The wire must carry the explicit purpose code corresponding to 'direct equity investment' or 'intercompany shareholder loan' on the inward BOP declaration and the recipient's unique corporate reference. For an amount above ZAR 10 million equivalent, a prior exchange control approval from FinSurv is required; the authorised dealer will issue a Certificate of Capital Import. The company should maintain a capital escrow ledger, evidence of the underlying board resolution, and a statement of funding purpose to prevent reclassification of the injection as outstanding trade debt or a taxable local loan.Outbound supplier payments for cosmetics inventory, packaging, marketing, and logistics are carried through the South African banking system using SWIFT MT103 (regular direct remittance), SWIFT MT202 for correspondent bank cover, or a commercial letter of credit (MT700/MT720) for larger wholesale consignments. All outward payments must be supported by invoices and proof of trade and reported under the authorised dealer's outward payment form, citing the appropriate trade/service purpose code. Same-day ZAR-to-ZAR clearing can utilise SAMOS for local suppliers, but any USD/EUR payment to a foreign beneficiary must be settled via the authorised dealer's FX desk. The documentary requirement includes the supplier quotation, commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, and procurement contract to justify the payment and match the approved exposure horizon.Vanilla outright deliverable FX forward for USD, EUR, and GBP trade exposures with a maximum 12-month tenor and documentary underlying exposure.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) — permissible only for synthetic offset of ZAR exposure through an authorised dealer, subject to FinSurv approval and the requirement that the exposure is genuine and not speculative.Cross-currency swap (CCS) between ZAR and USD/EUR for matched capital inflows or long-term procurement contracts, requiring FinSurv authorisation for tenors exceeding 24 months.European-style FX options purchased to hedge procurement upside and cap adverse moves in the cosmetic ingredients supply chain; option premia may be paid in ZAR.FX swap or same-day swap/forward to roll over a maturing post-shipment receivable or payable without increasing the company's net open foreign-currency position.
