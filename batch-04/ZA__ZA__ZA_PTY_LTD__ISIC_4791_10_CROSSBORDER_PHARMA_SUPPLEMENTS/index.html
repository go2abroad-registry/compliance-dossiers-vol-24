Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) acting through the eFiling portal (secure.sarsefiling.co.za) and the SARS Customs eCargo module; corporate registration is administered by the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), which feeds incorporation data to SARS for automatic income tax reference number (ITRN) issuance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with CIPC using a registered agent (no statutory minimum capital; non-resident directorship and 100% non-resident shareholding permitted). Step 2: CIPC transmits the company registration number to SARS, which automatically allocates the Income Tax Reference Number (ITRN) and activates the eFiling profile; a Public Officer must be appointed for SARS correspondence within one month. Step 3: Register for VAT via eFiling form VAT101 when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1 million in any rolling 12-month period, or voluntarily from ZAR 50,000; the VAT reference is linked to the ITRN. Step 4: Register for the SARS Customs Client Code (importer/exporter code) through the Customs module of eFiling to lodge export declarations. Step 5: Register for PAYE, UIF and SDL if employees are engaged. Step 6: Obtain SAHPRA product approval or exemption letters for complementary medicines and nutraceuticals where the goods are subject to border health controls.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a resident South African Pty Ltd is taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide income at the 27% corporate rate (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); dividend distributions attract 20% Dividend Tax withheld at source; no territorial exemption applies to e-commerce export revenue; foreign taxes are relieved by credit under s6quat of the Income Tax Act and applicable double tax treaties.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections and rulings: (i) group loss surrender election under s20(4) Income Tax Act where a controlling group satisfies the 70% shareholding test; (ii) binding class or private ruling from SARS confirming classification of hybrid instruments and application of the s23M interest limitation and hybrid mismatch rules (s23F-s23N implementing BEPS Action 2); (iii) VAT deemed-supplier election for electronic marketplaces under s54A and s54B of the VAT Act, under which a marketplace facilitating cross-border supplementary supply may be treated as the supplier and must register, account for output VAT and issue e-invoices; (iv) voluntary VAT registration election from ZAR 50,000 turnover; (v) election to apply simplified VAT cash accounting under s15(2A) for small businesses below ZAR 2.5 million turnover.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies during any consecutive 12-month period (compulsory registration); voluntary registration is permitted from ZAR 50,000; non-resident electronic services suppliers also cross the ZAR 1,000,000 registration threshold for digital supplies to South African customers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where a registered vendor exports physical goods (including dietary supplements) in the course of its enterprise and retains documentary proof that the goods were exported from South Africa within the prescribed period and before the VAT return is submitted; pure services rendered from South Africa to non-residents for use outside the republic fall under the s11(2)(l) zero-rating. For B2C cross-border courier consignments, zero-rating is accepted where the goods leave the republic through a registered courier or postal service and the track-and-trace delivery event in the destination country is retained. VAT refunds are claimed on VAT201 returns and are payable within 21 business days, extendable to 90 days where SARS raises a three-way matching verification; the marketplace sales report for the supply period is the primary quantification document. The supplier must obtain an export incentive declaration where available and ensure that consignments to SACU member states are treated as local cross-border supplies rather than exports.

  • SARS Customs e-SAD500 (Single Administrative Document) export declaration showing the customs release reference number at the exit port
  • Electronic movement reference from the SARS eCargo module evidencing physical exit from a customs controlled area
  • Commercial invoice in the name of the overseas buyer or end-consumer
  • Packing list detailing SKUs, quantities and net weights of nutraceutical consignments
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Master Air Waybill / courier consignment note for air e-commerce parcels

  • SAHPRA import/export permit, product licence or exemption confirmation for complementary medicines and scheduled supplement products
  • High-risk merchant account settlement statements confirming the gross consideration paid by international card acquirers

SARS is implementing a phased e-invoicing mandate aligned to the OECD and Peppol interoperability framework; as of the current tax year the full mandate is not yet gazetted for all SME taxpayers, but large taxpayers and VAT exporters filing customs entries must transmit data in the SARS eFiling/eCargo JSON/XML protocol. Export freights require an electronic customs declaration (e-SAD500) and an electronic movement/release reference number generated in real time at the port of exit; SARS applies three-way matching (VAT return to invoice to proof-of-payment/proof of export) to validate every export zero-rated supply before refund processing. Digital stamping as known in Latin American jurisdictions is not applicable; shipment clearance is executed through the SARS Customs release number. Records of e-invoices and customs declarations must be preserved in an authenticated digital format for five years under the Tax Administration Act.

SARS audits export zero-rating through the three-way matching protocol and the customs movement database; where a zero rating is denied, output VAT is clawed back on assessment together with interest at the statutory prescribed rate under s187 of the Tax Administration Act (currently approximately 11.5% per annum, compounded monthly) from the date the relevant VAT return was due. Deliberate misdeclaration or fraudulent documentation attracts: (i) an additional penalty of 150% of the tax evaded under s60(1) of the VAT Act; (ii) an additional 200% penalty for intentional tax evasion under s223 TAA; (iii) criminal prosecution under s234 TAA with a fine of up to ZAR 10,417 or imprisonment for up to five years per offence; (iv) unlimited retrospective assessment periods where fraud or misrepresentation is established; and (v) personal liability of the Public Officer and directors as responsible persons under the penalty regime. Suspension of export zero-rating, VAT vendor deregistration and referral to the National Prosecuting Authority may follow material cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is freely convertible for current-account transactions; authorised dealers require documentary support such as commercial invoices, customs entries (SAD500), and SARS tax clearance for outward remittances. Profit and dividend remittance to non-residents is permitted on presentation of a SARS corporate income tax clearance certificate issued within the last six months, audited financial statements evidencing distributable reserves, and standard Excon remittance forms; Dividends Tax of 20% applies (with double-taxation-agreement relief available). Inward foreign direct investment is unrestricted and requires no FinSurv prior approval, while outward direct investment by resident corporates may be approved by authorised dealers up to ZAR 500 million per company per calendar year; larger amounts require FinSurv application.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be fully repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment (Exchange Control Regulation 7(1)). There is no mandatory conversion ratio to ZAR; 100% of proceeds may be credited to and retained in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) at an authorised dealer. Exporters must declare shipments on the bank's Excon electronic export system, supported by the customs export declaration (SAD500), and obtain an export letter of instruction for each consignment. Non-receipt beyond the 180-day window requires a FinSurv extension application evidenced by payment delay justification, legal disputes, or documentary collection discrepancies.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Rulings, and the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, No. 38 of 2001 (FICA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury rails; Medium as an auxiliary collection rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not authorised dealers under the SARB exchange-control regime; they cannot hold domestic ZAR corporate accounts, cannot access the SAMOS clearing system, and cannot issue SARB-recognised FCAs. Any USD/EUR collections accumulated offshore must still be repatriated to the ZA FCA within 180 days. Local fintech payment gateways (Peach Payments, PayFast, Ozow, Yoco) are viable for high-risk card-not-present acquiring, settling in ZAR via BankServAfrica rails into a tier-1 bank merchant settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For non-resident shareholder equity: route a SWIFT MT103 (USD/EUR) to the ZA company's tier-1 bank ZAR settlement account or USD-denominated FCA. The MT103 must include the beneficiary name 'ZA_PTY_LTD', the parent's remittance reference, and purpose-of-payment narration 'Inward Foreign Direct Investment — Subscription for Ordinary Shares'. The receiving bank registers the inflow on its Excon electronic reporting system (EDS) and requires: the share-subscription agreement; board resolution approving the issue; CIPC CoR 40 / Notice of Amendment; updated MOI; UBO declarations; SARS tax clearance certificate; and, above institutional thresholds, an independent source-of-funds confirmation. No FinSurv prior approval is required for inward equity; the bank issues an Excon receipt that must be retained for future disinvestment and Dividends Tax clearance. Domestic ZAR capital injections from a local shareholder are routed via real-time EFT (SAMOS/ACH) with narration 'Share Capital Subscription' and contemporaneous board resolutions.Supplier settlements for cross-border supplement procurement are cleared through: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with UETR, beneficiary bank BIC, and purpose code 'Imports — Payment for Goods'; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) issued through the tier-1 bank's trade desk; and (iii) documentary collections (D/P or D/A). Settlement requires the commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, South African customs entry (SAD500), import permits where applicable, and the bank's electronic import-payment Excon release. E-commerce B2C card receivables settle in ZAR via high-risk merchant acquiring rails (BankServAfrica / SAMOS) with daily sweeps to the main operating account; USD balances may accumulate in the FCA and convert at dealing-desk rates. Intra-group remittances such as royalties or management fees require a valid SARS tax clearance certificate, written inter-company agreement, and transfer-pricing documentation.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) — ZAR/USD, EUR/ZAR, GBP/ZAR with maturities up to 12 months, supported by firm or anticipated trade commitments evidenced by invoices, purchase orders, or cash-flow budgets.Non-Deliverable Forwards (USD/ZAR NDFs) — accessible to offshore shareholders hedging ZAR investment exposure; for the resident entity, onshore FECs are the standard permitted instrument and NDFs require FinSurv-specific authorisation for resident corporates.Cross-Currency Swaps (FX and principal-exchanges swaps) — available for matched asset-and-liability hedging of foreign-currency borrowings, subject to bank credit approval and FinSurv notification thresholds.OTC FX Options — permitted for hedging firm commercial exposures where forwards are insufficient to cover conversion uncertainty in cross-border tenders, staged settlements, or competitive price-risk scenarios.Spot FX Deals (T+2) — permissible at bank dealing-desk rates for working-capital conversion, limited to converting FCA balances into ZAR when operational obligations mature.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury rails; Medium as an auxiliary collection rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not authorised dealers under the SARB exchange-control regime; they cannot hold domestic ZAR corporate accounts, cannot access the SAMOS clearing system, and cannot issue SARB-recognised FCAs. Any USD/EUR collections accumulated offshore must still be repatriated to the ZA FCA within 180 days. Local fintech payment gateways (Peach Payments, PayFast, Ozow, Yoco) are viable for high-risk card-not-present acquiring, settling in ZAR via BankServAfrica rails into a tier-1 bank merchant settlement account.
