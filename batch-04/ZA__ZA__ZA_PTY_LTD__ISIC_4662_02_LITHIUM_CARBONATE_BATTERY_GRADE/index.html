Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual or registered-agent office is permissible provided that the registered office is a physical address within South Africa (s 23(3) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008) and a post office box is used only as a postal address. Mail-forwarding virtual offices are accepted by CIPC and by commercial banks for KYC and service-of-process purposes, but the company must retain a local agent able to receive process and produce records. For this cross-border battery-metals trading profile, FIC guidance on conduit arrangements and the expectation of local commercial substance require an operational layer: a physical registered office, a contracted South African registered agent, local governance records, and a domestic bank account evidenced by real UBO attestation.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum issued or paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 71 of 2008, and no escrow or capital deposit account is required. The incorporation subscriptions are settled by the consideration recorded in the MOI and the company's securities register; the first directors must ensure that the issued share capital is fully paid for the consideration stated and duly entered in the register of members.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office and any warehouse premises must hold a municipal Certificate of Occupancy issued under the National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act 103 of 1977 confirming structural compliance; a Fire Safety Clearance or Certificate of Fire Compliance must be obtained from the local municipal fire prevention services in terms of the Fire Brigade Services Act, 1987 and applicable by-laws, covering any storage of packaged lithium carbonate; and an Occupational Health and Safety clearance must be registered with the Department of Employment and Labour under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, 1993, including a safety file, risk assessment, and designation of a company safety representative prior to the commencement of operational activities.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum issued or paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 71 of 2008, and no escrow or capital deposit account is required. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable to the trading scope. ISIC 4662 wholesale trade of metals and minerals, including battery-grade lithium carbonate, is not a statutorily licensed or manufacturing activity in South Africa. Sectoral oversight rests with the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (the dtic); the International Trade Administration Commission (ITAC) regulates any import/export controls; and SARS Customs issues the mandatory Importer/Exporter Code.

Category: Exempt - no operating or manufacturing license is statutorily required for the wholesale procurement and distribution of battery-grade lithium carbonate. The mandatory operational prerequisites are the SARS Customs Importer/Exporter Code (Form DA185) and, for any warehousing activity, the municipal Certificate of Occupancy and a fire safety clearance issued for the relevant premises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

A South African Proprietary Limited company (Pty Ltd) is incorporated through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and receives an income tax reference number automatically upon registration. VAT registration is mandated under the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991 once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a 12-month period; voluntary registration is available above ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period. A foreign entity without South African incorporation must apply through SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using the VAT 101 application, submit its foreign certificate of incorporation or equivalent, appoint or identify a public officer/registered representative, provide a local physical address or place of business, bank confirmation, and supporting resolutions to obtain a VAT vendor number and income tax reference number where South African tax obligations arise.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident South African Pty Ltd is taxed on worldwide income; non-resident persons are taxed on South African-source income; no transparent pass-through treatment is available for a Pty Ltd. — A South African Proprietary Limited company (Pty Ltd) is incorporated through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and receives an income tax reference number automatically upon registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident South African Pty Ltd is taxed on worldwide income; non-resident persons are taxed on South African-source income; no transparent pass-through treatment is available for a Pty Ltd.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box system. Available statutory grouping and compliance elections include: VAT group registration under section 50 of the VAT Act for a group of companies; income tax group asset rollover elections under section 45 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; and adjustments or elections required to neutralise domestic and cross-border hybrid mismatch arrangements. No general entity reclassification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 (R1 million) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods by a registered VAT vendor qualifies for zero-rating where the goods are supplied under a sale or export agreement and are physically exported to a destination outside South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply, and the vendor retains proof of export. Offshore services are zero-rated where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is not present in South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected with immovable property or movable property situated in South Africa, subject to the exclusions in the VAT Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged with SARS Customs under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964
  • Commercial invoice and original VAT tax invoice containing the prescribed particulars under section 20(4) of the VAT Act
  • Packing list and transport document (Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or road manifest) evidencing physical movement to a destination outside South Africa
  • Proof of export, such as a customs-cleared exit report or container movement notification
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or Authorised Dealer confirmation evidencing receipt of the export sale proceeds from the foreign buyer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is presently imposed in South Africa. Vendors must issue valid tax invoices containing the prescribed particulars under section 20(4) of the VAT Act; electronic tax invoices are accepted. SARS eFiling and e@syFile are the official electronic channels for VAT return filing and payment. A vendor exporting goods must retain the prescribed export documentary evidence for SARS audit and refund verification.

SARS may audit any zero-rated export transaction and require full production of the export documentation bundle before accepting or releasing a VAT refund. Failure to substantiate zero-rating results in a retrospective clawback of the VAT previously zero-rated, together with prescribed interest. Penalties include the understatement penalty under section 222 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, graduated by taxpayer conduct and prejudice, reaching a maximum of 150% of the shortfall for intentional tax evasion. Fraudulent export zero-rating may also lead to criminal prosecution for false declarations under the VAT Act and the Customs and Excise Act, with imprisonment up to 10 years and additional customs penalties or forfeiture.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a partially convertible exchange-control regime: ZAR is fully transferable on current-account transactions, while capital-account flows are regulated. Import payments for battery-grade lithium carbonate are freely dispensable through an Authorised Dealer upon presentation of the SARS customs entry (SAD500), commercial invoice, transport document and an import-of-goods purpose code; no prior FinSurv approval applies to genuine trade settlements. Inward foreign equity investment is freely permitted subject to mandatory electronic reporting by the Authorised Dealer to FinSurv (the modern successor to legacy Form N reporting), and carries no minimum capital threshold for a (Pty) Ltd. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permissible on current account through an Authorised Dealer after a lawful dividend declaration and settlement of the statutory 20% dividends tax (double-tax-agreement relief may apply). Capital repatriations (share redemption, capital reduction) require proof of the original inward-investment reference, and shareholder-loan repayments require prior Exchange Control Regulation 10 approval for the underlying borrowing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), exercising delegated powers under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, with enforcement coordination through the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 2001 (FICA) framework.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: A South African private (Pty) Ltd company is required to appoint an auditor and undergo a statutory audit if: (a) its Public Interest Score (PIS), as computed under s. 59(1) and Companies Regulation 26, equals or exceeds 500; (b) a person holds a beneficial interest in the company's securities; (c) the MOI requires an audit; or (d) the company is subject to an audit under another law. A company with PIS of 130 or more but less than 500 must instead undergo an independent review by a registered accounting practitioner, unless the shareholders unanimously elect an audit. A company with PIS below 130 is exempt from both audit and independent review unless the MOI or shareholders determine otherwise. PIS is calculated as 1 point per employee, 1 point per R1 million of third-party liabilities, 1 point per shareholder, and 1 point per beneficial owner.
