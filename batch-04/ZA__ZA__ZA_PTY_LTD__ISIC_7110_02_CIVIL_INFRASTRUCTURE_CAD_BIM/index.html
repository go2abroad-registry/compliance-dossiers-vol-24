Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); statutory self-service portal is SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

Foreign and domestic entities must first complete incorporation or branch registration with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); thereafter obtain an Income Tax reference number via SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using the IT150/IT144 registration forms and appointing a Public Officer who must be resident in South Africa; a separate VAT101 application and turnover-declaration evidence are required for VAT registration; the same reference number governs PAYE, VAT, customs, and income tax account codes upon activation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign and domestic entities must first complete incorporation or branch registration with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); thereafter obtain an Income Tax reference number via SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using the IT150/IT144 registration forms and appointing a Public Officer who must be resident in South Africa; a separate VAT101 application and turnover-declaration evidence are required for VAT registration; the same reference number governs PAYE, VAT, customs, and income tax account codes upon activation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in South Africa. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) Small Business Corporation status under section 12E of the Income Tax Act for qualifying companies with gross income below ZAR 20 million; (ii) Hybrid debt/equity classification elections under sections 8F/8FA; (iii) Dividend access/declaration elections; (iv) Election to compute capital gains under IFRS versus tax-basis recognition; (v) Section 11D Research and Development intensive-filings; and (vi) Binding General Rulings or Advance Tax Rulings can be applied-for to fix tax characterisation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration permitted from ZAR 50,000 for qualifying suppliers; foreign suppliers of prescribed electronic services registered separately under special rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimable for exported goods under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act and for exported services under section 11(2)(l) where services are physically rendered outside South Africa, or under section 11(2)(m) where services are supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time of supply, the services are not directly connected to land or movable property inside South Africa, and the services are not consumed or used in South Africa. Cross-border BIM/CAD engineering services supplied by a South African Pty Ltd to non-resident clients with offshore deliverables and no local nexus qualify, subject to retention of contract, non-residency proof, detailed scope of work, and evidence that intellectual effort and final use occur outside South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) and SARS customs clearance approval for physical goods movement
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical dispatch from South Africa when goods accompany the services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Authorised Dealer / bank SWIFT MT103 or equivalent telex transfer evidence proving repatriation of export proceeds to South Africa
  • Signed service agreement stating the non-resident recipient, overseas place of supply, and absence of South African supply nexus
  • Detailed invoice with the zero-rated VAT code and full VAT-section reference, plus technical BIM deliverables register or delivery receipt evidencing offshore performance

South Africa does not currently enforce real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API clearance. VAT invoices must however satisfy section 20 of the VAT Act inclusive of supplier name, VAT number, invoice number, date, full description, consideration, and tax fraction. SARS eFiling supports electronic submissions, and a voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing framework is being introduced; no immediate fiscal-stamping API is mandatory.

SARS may retrospectively disallow the zero rating and issue an additional VAT assessment at 15%, recovering the principal VAT together with prescribed interest from the date the refund was anciently claimed. Where fraudulent or negligent misstatement is established, an additional administrative penalty of 200% of the assessed amount applies under section 222(1)(c) of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Criminal prosecution under the VAT Act and Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 may further be instituted, carrying fines up to ZAR 1 million and imprisonment of up to five years for knowingly issuing false zero-rated invoices; export-proceeds default beyond 180 days also triggers exchange-control penalties and repatriation assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current-account items (exports of services including BIM/CAD engineering, imports, and ordinary remittances) subject to documentary verification by an authorised dealer. Capital-account movements remain controlled: inward FDI in a South African (Pty) Ltd is automatically permitted via share subscription against post-issuance reporting; profit remittance (dividends) is legal but requires audited annual financial statements, a Form A application, transfer-pricing documentation, and a SARS tax-clearance (dividend withholding tax at 20% domestic rate, treaty-reduced under most DTAAs). Non-resident shareholder loans below R100 million per annum are reportable; amounts above that threshold require prior FinSurv approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory surrender/conversion of export proceeds into ZAR was abolished. Full repatriation into South Africa, however, remains compulsory: proceeds from BIM/CAD service invoices must be received through an authorised dealer within 30 days of the performance date (or the permissible exporter term, with 180-day extension approval), and may be credited to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) in USD/EUR/GBP without a conversion ratio. Each inward service fee must be documented by the underlying contract, SOW, and invoice for SARS and dealer-level invisible-export reporting; failure to repatriate within the statutory window attracts exchange-control penalties.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) — Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the National Payment System Act, 1998 (Act No. 78 of 1998). Day-to-day administration is delegated to Authorised Dealers (licensed commercial banks) under SARB's Exchange Control Manual.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

HIGH for inbound collection and conversion of third-party project fees through Wise (operating in South Africa via a licensed local arrangement with a domestic commercial bank under the National Payment System Act and PASA rules) — viable up to approximately USD 1–2 million annual service revenue. Two hard constraints apply: (i) an EMI is not an authorised dealer, so all net ZAR settlements must land on a South African bank account within the SARB zonal clearing system; and (ii) dividend remittances, intercompany capital flows, and Form A applications may not be executed through an EMI — they must route via the domestic authorised dealer. Legal risk rises if the Wise collection entity sits in an opaque offshore structure or is used to bypass repatriation rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity route: the non-resident parent subscribes for ordinary shares via SWIFT MT103 using the balance-of-payments code for inward FDI in share capital; the authorised dealer credits the FCA or converts to ZAR, and files the statutory inward-investment notification on the SARB electronic exchange-control reporting channel within 30 days. Supporting records are share certificates, CIPC resolution, tax registration, and proof-of-funds documents. Debt route (intercompany loan below R100 million per annum): MT103 with an intercompany-financing purpose code; the dealer files the foreign-liability return, and the borrower must retain the loan agreement, drawdown schedule, and financial statements evidencing an arm's-length interest rate for SARS Section 31 transfer-pricing and thin-capitalisation compliance.Trade and operational payments settle via SWIFT MT103 through the authorised dealer, with a Form A filing for all ZAR-conversion purchases of software licences, cloud infrastructure, subcontracted services, and professional fees. For Southern African (SADC) counterparties, SIRESS (SADC Integrated Regional Electronic Settlement System) provides a regional real-time gross settlement rail. Capital-intensive procurement (e.g., BIM workstations, render farms, or surveying hardware from APAC suppliers) uses irrevocable commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) with DP/DA documentary collections as secondary instruments; every outward disbursement must cite the underlying contract and invoice to withstand SARB audit.Outright FX forwards (USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR, GBP/ZAR) up to 24 months against forecast BIM/CAD service-export receivables, executed only through an authorised dealer with documented underlying exposure and no speculative cover.European-style OTC FX options (call/put on USD/ZAR and EUR/ZAR) with premium payment and matching underlying invoice exposure.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for hedging a material non-resident shareholder loan against ZAR cash-flow volatility — subject to FinSurv approval for tenors beyond authorised-dealer limits.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) against ZAR where the onshore interbank market cannot deliver the currency for the required tenor; route the NDF leg through the authorised dealer's offshore desk or a syndicated hedge desk.Exchange-traded USD/ZAR futures and options on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange (JSE) as the most transparent, daily-marked rail for tactical hedging of short-dated service-fee receipts.
