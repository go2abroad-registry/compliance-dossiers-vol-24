Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) - eFiling portal (https://www.sars.gov.za; https://efiling.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A (Pty) Ltd is incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); CIPC transmits incorporation data to SARS, after which the company activates its eFiling profile to obtain the income tax reference number. VAT registration via Form VAT101 is compulsory once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months, with voluntary registration available at ZAR 50,000; PAYE registration is required when employees are appointed; import and export activity requires a SARS Customs code. A foreign external company carrying on business through a South African branch registers the branch with CIPC, obtains a non-resident income tax reference number, and registers for VAT if the branch's taxable turnover exceeds the threshold; non-resident suppliers of electronic services use the dedicated s 54E VAT Act registration channel.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box classification election; a (Pty) Ltd is statutorily a resident company taxed at entity level. Available elections include the headquarter company regime (s 9I, Income Tax Act 58 of 1962), voluntary VAT registration at ZAR 50,000 (s 23(2), VAT Act 89 of 1991), and the micro business turnover tax election (Sixth Schedule). The OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch neutralisation rules apply mandatorily to years of assessment commencing on or after 1 January 2024 and are not elective.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand is convertible for current-account transactions through SARB authorised dealers but remains subject to partial exchange control. Current-account payments for trade, services, royalties, interest, and dividends may be remitted once the underlying commercial documentation is verified by an authorised dealer; no SARB pre-approval is ordinarily required for legitimate current-account payments by a resident (Pty) Ltd. Capital-account transfers, particularly outward capital remittances by residents, are controlled and generally require FinSurv permission or must fall within an approved allowance. Inward foreign direct investment is freely permitted and must be reported for balance-of-payments purposes. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders in the form of ordinary dividends is permitted through an authorised dealer, subject to: formal board declaration; the Companies Act 2008 solvency and liquidity test; dividends tax at 20% withheld and paid to SARS; and retention of the full dividend resolution, financial statements, and inward capital-import evidence. Additional transfer-pricing and economic-substance documentation should be maintained to reduce bank and SARS friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SARB Exchange Control Regulations, export proceeds from goods shipped from South Africa must be received in South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless a longer period is specifically approved by FinSurv. The full 100% of exported goods proceeds must be repatriated and surrendered through the local banking system; no portion may be retained offshore without FinSurv authorisation. Where payments are received in foreign currency, they must be converted into Rand or credited to a bank-approved foreign-currency account only for a permitted temporary period and must ultimately be brought onshore or applied strictly in accordance with the authorised dealer's instructions. This regime applies to the condiment-export side of the hotpot chain. Import-related settlement obligations are governed separately by the Customs and Excise Act and SARS import declarations (SAD500).

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, as delegated by the National Treasury

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary clearing rail. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital payment platforms are not SARB authorised dealers, cannot access the national ZAR settlement system (SAMOS via BankservAfrica), and cannot independently perform exchange-control clearance or capital-import reporting. They may be used only as ancillary card-issuing, POS aggregation, or FX conversion layers, but all inbound equity, trade settlements, and repatriation flows must pass through a locally licensed bank with authorised-dealer status. The use of unlicensed foreign EMI accounts for operating cash would be a regulatory red flag and should be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity subscriptions and shareholder loans must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the (Pty) Ltd's account held at the SARB authorised dealer. Each wire must clearly reference the legal entity name, the underlying shareholder, and the purpose code for inward direct investment or intercompany loan. The authorised dealer will perform FICA verification, check sanctions, and record the inward transfer for SARB balance-of-payments reporting; the bank's transaction reference becomes the capital-import evidence necessary for future dividend, interest, and capital repatriation. Shareholders should not split transfers, should not use personal accounts for company capital, and must ensure the transferred amount matches the share subscription or loan agreement exactly.Regular outbound import payments to overseas suppliers, especially Chinese condiment and equipment vendors, should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 through the authorised-dealer bank with supporting invoice, purchase order, transport documents, and customs clearance declaration. High-value or documentary trade settlements should use commercial letters of credit (LC) issued by the South African bank and advised through the beneficiary's Chinese correspondent bank. Local supplier payments and merchant settlement are cleared through BankservAfrica's ACB/SAMOS rails, while card transactions for the restaurant's POS operations are settled via the acquiring bank using the major card schemes; dynamic currency conversion should be disabled so settlement is received in Rand without FX slippage.Deliverable forward foreign exchange contracts (ZAR forwards) with a SARB authorised dealer for documented trade payables and receivables, supported by underlying invoices and with no speculative intent.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for ZAR exposures where the counterparty currency is not fully deliverable in the South African market, executed through a SARB authorised dealer or an approved international correspondent.Cross-currency swaps between ZAR and foreign currencies for long-term shareholder loan and dividend-remittance exposures, subject to FinSurv-related authorised-dealer standards and the Financial Markets Act reporting requirements, with full documentation of the underlying capital transaction.
