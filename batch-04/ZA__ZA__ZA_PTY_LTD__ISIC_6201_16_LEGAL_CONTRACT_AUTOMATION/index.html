Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); portal: SARS eFiling at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; corporate registry interface: Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) at https://www.cipc.co.za

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated (Pty) Ltd is auto-registered with SARS for Income Tax upon CIPC incorporation and receives an income tax reference number. The company must then: (1) appoint a South African-resident public officer within one month of incorporation and register the appointment with SARS; (2) register for VAT via SARS eFiling within 21 days after the end of the month in which taxable turnover exceeds or is likely to exceed ZAR 1,500,000, or voluntarily where turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000; (3) register as an employer for PAYE, UIF and SDL before making payments to employees; (4) obtain a customs importer/exporter code if cross-border goods movement occurs; and (5) declare non-resident director/shareholder particulars and ultimate beneficial ownership to CIPC and SARS. Foreign companies establishing a branch or having a place of effective management in South Africa must obtain a SARS tax reference number for the branch. Tax registration is confirmed through the SARS eFiling profile and a tax compliance status certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (residence-based): a (Pty) Ltd is a separate legal taxpayer subject to corporate income tax at 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed on South African-source income; there is no pass-through or check-the-box election; distributions are subject to 20% dividends tax; a foreign company branch is taxed at 27% on South African-source income with no separate remittance tax; the entity is not situated in a tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a (Pty) Ltd is per se a taxable entity. Available elections and special regimes include: (i) Headquarters Company election under s 9I of the Income Tax Act granting conduit treatment for qualifying foreign dividends, interest, royalties and disposal gains, subject to prescribed non-resident shareholding, foreign group income and expense/asset composition thresholds; (ii) Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) attribution elections and foreign business establishment exemptions under s 9D; (iii) VAT grouping application under s 50(2) VAT Act to treat related persons as a single vendor; (iv) VAT cash-basis election under s 15(2A) VAT Act for vendors with taxable turnover not exceeding ZAR 2,500,000; (v) Small Business Corporation progressive rate election under s 12E for qualifying companies with turnover below ZAR 20,000,000; (vi) foreign exchange rate valuation elections under s 24I; and (vii) s 11D R&D allowance election subject to Department of Science and Innovation pre-approval. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are adjusted mandatorily under ss 8E, 8EA, 8F and 23M, with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,500,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period (threshold raised from ZAR 1,000,000 effective 1 April 2024); voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000; foreign suppliers of electronic services must register for VAT when electronic services exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (1) exports of movable goods physically removed from South Africa within 90 days of supply where the vendor retains documentary proof (s 11(1)(a) and (b) VAT Act); and (2) exported services supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time the services are rendered and who does not consume or use the services in South Africa (s 11(2)(l) VAT Act). For LegalTech SaaS and e-signature automation services, the supplier must verify the foreign status of the recipient and the absence of local use/consumption. Services supplied in connection with land or movable property situated in South Africa at the time of supply, or services physically rendered in South Africa, do not qualify. All zero-rated invoices must be annotated with the applicable zero-rating reason and supported by export evidence retained before the VAT return is filed.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) electronically lodged with SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / road manifest evidencing physical removal of goods from South Africa
  • Commercial invoice annotated 'zero-rated export' showing full value, consignee, currency and applicable ISIC/code
  • Inward remittance evidence (SWIFT MT103 or Authorised Dealer certificate) proving actual receipt of foreign currency
  • For services: signed export services agreement and non-resident consumption declaration (s 11(2)(l)) supported by evidence of foreign IP/hosting and client location

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or national e-invoicing API is currently in force as at 2025. SARS operates a phased e-invoicing framework through the SARS Digital Gateway (secure PDF/XML with integrity hash) and has announced incremental mandates, but the existing statutory obligations are: registered vendors must issue a s 20 VAT tax invoice within 21 days of the date of supply, retain records for 5 years, and file VAT201 returns electronically via SARS eFiling on a monthly or bi-monthly basis as allocated by SARS; high-volume filers may be required to submit via SARS API.

SARS may reopen assessments within the statutory prescription period (generally 3 years, extended to 5 years for non-disclosure, and unlimited for fraud or misrepresentation) and retrospectively reinstate 15% output VAT where export status or documentary proof is deficient; the vendor bears the burden of proof. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties based on conduct apply to the VAT shortfall: standard case 10%, substantial understatement 25%, failure to take reasonable care 50%, unreasonable tax position 75%, and intentional tax evasion 150%, plus interest at the prescribed rate from the original due date. Additional non-compliance penalties and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under s 235 TAA may result in fines and imprisonment; directors or persons knowingly involved in false zero-rating declarations are jointly and severally liable, and SARS may suspend eFiling facilities and set off refunds.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a liberalised current account alongside a managed capital account. The ZAR is effectively convertible for current-account receipts and payments (subscription fees, professional services, royalties and trade finance) provided each transaction is routed through an Authorised Dealer with an underlying invoice or contract, a proper FICA file and the standard payment-purpose declaration; no FinSurv pre-approval is needed for most current transactions below the dealer delegation ceiling (circa ZAR 10 million per service counterparty per year). Capital-account entries are more restrictive: non-resident equity investment inbound is freely permitted without SARB pre-approval, but outbound capital movements, foreign loans by residents, loop structures, round-tripping of local ZAR and offshore treasury retention require prior FinSurv authority or an Authorised-Dealer permit. Profit remittance hurdles for a non-resident shareholder of a LegalTech SaaS (Pty) Ltd are: demonstrated source of funds, CIPC beneficial-ownership coherence, a valid SARS registration and tax compliance status, and compliance with the 20% dividends tax (or applicable double-taxation treaty relief) at source; once these are met, dividends and capital proceeds are remittable without exchange-control ceilings. Net assessment: current-account convertibility is High, capital-account convertibility is Conditional and documentary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For export of services (including SaaS, e-signature and cloud document automation), the exporter must obtain payment of the full invoice value within 180 calendar days from the date of service provision, and must surrender every foreign-currency receipt to an Authorised Dealer within 30 days of its receipt, meaning sale of the foreign currency against ZAR into the company domestic operational account at the prevailing dealer spot rate. There is no statutory partial-conversion ratio: the default legal posture is a 100% surrender obligation. The exporter may instead apply through the Authorised Dealer for permission to retain foreign currency in a designated Foreign-Currency Account (typically a discretionary retention cap of approximately ZAR 5 million equivalent), keeping the remainder due for immediate surrender; retention is granted only for genuine trade hedging or import cover and remains subject to the 180-day receipt discipline. Non-surrender, late receipt or parking of proceeds offshore is treated as unlawful externalisation under section 9 of the Currency and Exchanges Act, exposing the entity to administrative penalties, adverse FIC suspicious-transaction reporting and banking de-platforming.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administers exchange control under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 9 of 1933, its subordinate Notices and the Currency and Exchanges Manual, and has delegated daily execution and approval powers to Authorised Dealers (registered Tier-1 commercial banks). The Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) concurrently supervises AML/CFT compliance and reporting under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (as amended by the FIC Amendment Act 1 of 2017), including beneficial-ownership verification for corporate accounts.
