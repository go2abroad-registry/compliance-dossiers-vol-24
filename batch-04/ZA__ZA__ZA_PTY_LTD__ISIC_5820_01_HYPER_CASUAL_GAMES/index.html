E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register for income tax and, where applicable, VAT by submitting a completed SARS registration form (e.g., VAT101, IT registration via eFiling) accompanied by constitutive documents, alien/foreign director identification, and SARS Corporate Income Tax registration confirmation. A South African tax reference number (TIN) and separate VAT vendor number are issued. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under South African law. A (Pty) Ltd is automatically a resident tax company if incorporated in South Africa. Election mechanisms are limited to special tax regimes (e.g., deemed CFC election, hedging/valuation elections, and s11E allowances); hybrid mismatch rules in s23M of the Income Tax Act apply and no elective hybrid classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or reasonable expectation of exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in the next 12 months; voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under s11(1) of the VAT Act. Goods are zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods in the ordinary course of business under customs supervision. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time, provided the services are not directly connected to movable or immovable property in South Africa, and the supplier retains documentary proof of non-resident status. For hyper-casual mobile games, ad revenue and app-store receipts from foreign platforms may qualify as exported services where the recipient is a non-resident and the services are consumed outside South Africa, subject to a permissible invoice and proof-of-export record.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500/SAD500J) lodged via SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airwaybill evidencing physical export of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a South African Authorised Dealer proving foreign currency receipt
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, non-resident declaration or legalized proof of foreign residency

No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate applies. SARS eFiling is the mandatory electronic tax return channel, and suppliers must issue full tax invoices containing the VAT vendor registration number, invoice date, description, consideration, and VAT amount (VAT Act s20). Digital stamping and government-authorised e-invoicing clearance are not currently required; however, SARS may require electronic submission of VAT201 returns and supporting schedules.

SARS may verify export zero-rating through audits and risk assessments. Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating triggers a VAT understatement penalty under the Tax Administration Act of up to 150% of the tax shortfall, statutory interest, and potential criminal prosecution (VAT Act s73, Tax Administration Act s234). Retrospective clawbacks can apply for up to five years from the date of assessment, with extended periods in cases of deliberate tax evasion or non-disclosure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a partially liberalised exchange-control regime under the Currency and Exchanges Manual (CEM), with the rand (ZAR) as a managed-float, non-internationalised currency. Current-account transactions are fully convertible without prior SARB approval: export proceeds from software and gaming services, royalties, advertising payouts and management fees may be repatriated through any Authorised Dealer subject to documentary verification, purpose-code declaration and balance-of-payments reporting. Capital-account transactions remain subject to administered liberalisation: inbound equity investment is freely permitted but must be routed through an Authorised Dealer and formally registered as imported capital (share-subscription declaration) so that subsequent profit repatriation is recognised. Profit remittance of dividends is permissible without FinSurv approval if the paying company has a valid SARS tax-clearance status, the underlying capital inflow was registered with the AD, non-resident dividend withholding tax (statutory 15%, possibly reduced under an applicable tax treaty) is deducted and declared, and the bank's foreign dividend reporting forms are completed. Practical hurdles include the obligation to prove the provenance of every inbound capital credit, matching purpose codes to the bank's nature-of-transaction list, and the ban on speculative or uncovered ZAR hedging, which limits treasury flexibility for early-stage hyper-casual operators.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exported services (hyper-casual game publishing and programmatic in-game advertising), the full value of export proceeds must be received through an Authorised Dealer in South Africa within 180 days of the invoice date or the date on which the service is rendered. There is no mandatory percentage conversion ratio into ZAR in the current dispensation; surrender is satisfied by crediting the proceeds to the exporting entity's Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with a domestic AD, where USD may be retained indefinitely or converted at the prevailing managed-float rate. Quarterly or event-driven exchange-control reporting requires the inward SWIFT credits to match the declared SARB purpose code for software/IT services, backed by Google AdMob/AppLovin payout statements, signed publisher agreements, and reconciliation of the ad-network settlement cycle. Export proceeds not received within 180 days require a formal extension application to FinSurv; non-compliance is an exchange-control contravention carrying administrative penalties and potential criminal sanctions under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting jointly with the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) as the AML/CFT supervisor under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA). Authorised Dealers (commercial banks with exchange-control licences) administer day-to-day foreign-exchange transactions on behalf of SARB.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI rails (Wise Business, Payoneer, DLocal, Airwallex, MultiPass) are legally usable as collection wallets for hyper-casual advertising revenue but cannot replace a domestic Authorised Dealer relationship. A South African (Pty) Ltd may receive USD from Google AdMob/AppLovin into an EMI USD wallet and thereafter repatriate to a local AD-held FCA, provided the EMI acts only as a collection conduit and each repatriation is supported by purpose-code declarations, payout statements and publisher contracts. However, Wise South Africa and similar providers are accountable institutions under the FIC Act, not banks; they cannot provide onshore ZAR settlement or exchange-control surrender. Retaining export proceeds with a non-AD intermediary beyond short settlement windows creates an unrepatriated-export-proceeds red flag, and domestic banks will demand full SWIFT/beneficiary statements before accepting drawdowns from an EMI. The recommended architecture is direct settlement from ad networks to a tier-1 AD FCA; EMIs should be reserved for small-value high-frequency payouts below USD 10,000 per day or for ad networks that cannot wire to a South African FCA.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident parent transfers USD/EUR by SWIFT MT103 to the (Pty) Ltd's domestic Foreign Currency Account using the Authorised Dealer's capital-account purpose code for direct investment or equity subscription. The AD requires: (a) a board resolution of the company accepting the subscription and issuing ordinary shares; (b) the CIPC allotment and beneficial-ownership filing; (c) a signed share-purchase declaration whereby the AD registers the funds as imported capital, thereby enabling future dividend and capital repatriation without further FinSurv approval; (d) for intercompany loans, a formal loan agreement and transfer-pricing/interest-rate file satisfying the thin-capitalisation limitation under section 23N of the Income Tax Act. Every inbound credit must carry the remitter reference and be reconciled to the bank's balance-of-payments reporting within the prescribed settlement window. Where the parent funds the company through an EMI (Payoneer/Wise), the domestic AD will treat the EMI as a mere collection agent and will require the full underlying transaction chain before releasing balances for operational withdrawal.Outbound settlements to SDK providers, app-store accounts, ad-tech vendors, traffic suppliers and intellectual-property licensors are executed through: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct corporate transfers, each linked to an underlying invoice and accompanied by the appropriate current-account purpose code and, where applicable, SARS tax clearance and withholding-tax calculation on royalties; (ii) MT202/MT203 for bank-to-bank positioning between the FCA and the ZAR settlement account; (iii) commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) where the supplier requires documentary security for high-value licences or bulk advertising inventory; and (iv) the domestic real-time clearing rails (SAMOS/RBPS) for ZAR payments to South African counterparties. Offshore digital EMI rails (Wise Business, Payoneer, DLocal, Airwallex, MultiPass) are legally usable as collection wallets for hyper-casual advertising revenue but cannot replace a domestic Authorised Dealer relationship. A South African (Pty) Ltd may receive USD from Google AdMob/AppLovin into an EMI USD wallet and thereafter repatriate to a local AD-held FCA, provided the EMI acts only as a collection conduit and each repatriation is supported by purpose-code declarations, payout statements and publisher contracts. However, Wise South Africa and similar providers are accountable institutions under the FIC Act, not banks; they cannot provide onshore ZAR settlement or exchange-control surrender. Retaining export proceeds with a non-AD intermediary beyond short settlement windows creates an unrepatriated-export-proceeds red flag, and domestic banks will demand full SWIFT/beneficiary statements before accepting drawdowns from an EMI. The recommended architecture is direct settlement from ad networks to a tier-1 AD FCA; EMIs should be reserved for small-value high-frequency payouts below USD 10,000 per day or for ad networks that cannot wire to a South African FCA.
