Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling portal (www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

Non-resident companies deriving South African-source income or carrying on a VAT-able enterprise must first obtain an income tax reference number from SARS by submitting a company income tax registration declaration (ITREG/IT77) via SARS eFiling or a SARS branch. Supporting documentation includes a certified memorandum of incorporation or equivalent foreign registration proof, director/shareholder particulars, local business address, and banking details. For VAT, an application on form VAT 101 (or VAT 101E for non-resident electronic suppliers) must be filed before the compulsory registration threshold is met; a VAT vendor number is then issued. A foreign entity may appoint a local representative taxpayer and must follow SARS registration procedures under the VAT Act.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Non-resident companies deriving South African-source income or carrying on a VAT-able enterprise must first obtain an income tax reference number from SARS by submitting a company income tax registration declaration (ITREG/IT77) via SARS eFiling or a SARS branch. Supporting documentation includes a certified memorandum of incorporation or equivalent foreign registration proof, director/shareholder particulars, local business address, and banking details. For VAT, an application on form VAT 101 (or VAT 101E for non-resident electronic suppliers) must be filed before the compulsory registration threshold is met; a VAT vendor number is then issued. A foreign entity may appoint a local representative taxpayer and must follow SARS registration procedures under the VAT Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a US-style check-the-box classification regime. The Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 contains hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules (ss 23M-23T) that operate automatically and are not elective. Available statutory elections include section 12E small business corporation status, participation-exemption elections, and asset-allowance timing elections; none of these alter the fundamental entity-level taxation of a (Pty) Ltd company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available for businesses with taxable supplies below that threshold, subject to SARS conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, goods are zero-rated only if the supplier removes them from South Africa during the course of exporting them, or delivers them to a registered carrier operator for export and the goods are subsequently exported. The supplier must retain documentary proof sufficient to establish that the goods have in fact left South Africa. Offshore services supplied to non-residents (such as marine fuel logistics brokerage services performed for foreign counterparties) may be zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) or section 11(2)(m), provided the services are not used or consumed in South Africa and are supplied to persons not affiliated with the supplier. SARS Customs export protocol, VAT record-keeping rules, and South African exchange-control regulations apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration – SAD500 approved by SARS Customs and Excise
  • Bill of Lading (marine exports) or equivalent transport document (airway bill or road consignment note)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Authorised Dealer bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

South Africa does not currently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control environment. VAT returns are filed via SARS eFiling on a monthly or two-monthly basis with electronic payment and assessment. Taxpayers must issue fully compliant VAT invoices under section 20 of the VAT Act and retain electronic records for SARS audit. SARS has phased in an e-invoicing pilot project based on PEPPOL-compliant B2B messaging, but there is no API-based digital stamping or real-time clearance mandate at law.

SARS customs and VAT audit teams rigorously scrutinise export zero-rating substantiation. Failure to produce acceptable export and foreign-currency receipt documentation leads to denial of the zero rate and retrospective recovery of any VAT refunded, together with market-related interest. An understatement penalty under section 223 of the Tax Administration Act can be as high as 200% of the tax shortfall in cases of intentional evasion; administrative penalties of 10%–15% may also apply for non-reporting. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute an offence under section 59 of the VAT Act and may result in criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, and disqualification of directors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is convertible for current-account transactions, including trade payments, service fees, royalties, interest and lawful profit/dividend remittances, provided that the Authorized Dealer receives sufficient documentary support and performs anti-money-laundering, sanctions and exchange-control screening. The capital account is administered rather than freely open: an inward equity or shareholder-loan injection into an ISIC 4661 wholesale trading company does not generally require prior FinSurv approval, but it must be received through a local Authorized Dealer, be classified under the applicable Balance-of-Payments category, and receive a non-resident capital endorsement where relevant. Profit remittance hurdles are documentary and fiscal: dividends may be repatriated only out of profits evidenced in signed or audited financial statements; dividends tax must be withheld at 20% or a lower treaty rate under a valid double-taxation agreement; the bank must confirm the beneficial recipient is the non-resident shareholder; and the company must not have unresolved SARS, FICA or sanctions concerns. Round-tripping, speculative treasury transactions, or remittances based on unsupported invoices are prohibited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under exchange-control regulation 6(1)(a), all proceeds from exported crude oil and refined petroleum products must be received in South Africa through an Authorized Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless FinSurv has granted a formal extension. The proceeds must be credited either to an approved foreign currency account held by the exporter with an Authorized Dealer or converted into rand. There is no fixed statutory percentage surrender to SARB in the modern administered regime; however, absent an approved FCA, the full foreign-currency receipt is converted 100% into ZAR. If an FCA is maintained, the exporter may retain up to 100% of eligible export proceeds in that account for genuine working capital and future foreign obligations. Export proceeds may not be held offshore, netted against payables, or collected through a third-party payment intermediary without prior FinSurv approval. For petroleum-trade entities, the bank will require the export permit, customs clearance record, invoice, bill of lading/SAD500 and shipping evidence in order to reconcile the proceeds and verify the statutory 180-day receipt timeline.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the exchange control authority, jointly with National Treasury, under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1961 (R.1111); day-to-day administration is delegated to banks that have been appointed as Authorized Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low; an offshore digital EMI is unsuitable as a primary treasury, banking, or clearing rail for this entity. Under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, only registered banks may issue electronic money in South Africa and only an Authorized Dealer may perform exchange-control settlement. An offshore EMI has no SAMOS settlement account, is not an Authorized Dealer, and cannot lawfully accept export proceeds or hold foreign currency on behalf of a South African resident without triggering exchange-control and FIC compliance breaches. At most, an EMI may be used as a non-custodial data and invoice-matching layer, provided all cleared funds are routed immediately to the exporter's local Authorized Dealer account or FCA. Practical rating: 1/10 as a bank substitute and 5/10 as an ancillary information tool, conditional on full transparency to the local bank.
