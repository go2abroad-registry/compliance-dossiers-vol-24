Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — official portal: www.sars.gov.za and SARS eFiling (www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity carrying on business in South Africa must apply for income tax registration and SARS VAT vendor registration (if taxable supplies exceed the compulsory threshold). Application may be made through SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch. Required documents include the entity's certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation, directors' identification proof, proof of registered address and local business address, bank account details, and powers of attorney for tax representatives. VAT registration requires completion of VAT 101 form, proof of enterprise activities in South Africa, and projected turnover. SARS will issue a taxpayer reference number and a VAT registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — South African incorporated companies are tax resident and subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income; non-resident branches are subject to tax on South African source income. No territorial exemption for foreign active business income. Not classified as a pass-through entity. Not located in a designated tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity classification election is available under South African law. The Income Tax Act provides certain elections relevant to controlled foreign company (CFC) rules, including the high-tax exemption election under section 9D(2A), the election to defer inclusion of attributable CFC income, and the election to use the transaction price market value basis under transfer pricing rules. Hybrid mismatch elections pursuant to regulations under section 9D are limited and require prescribed documentation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any 12 consecutive months; voluntary registration threshold: ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies in any 12 consecutive months. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services must register if the aggregate value of electronic services supplied to South African customers exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods are zero-rated where the goods are supplied to a non-resident and are exported from South Africa within 90 days (or such longer period as SARS permits) of the supplier's invoice; the supplier must retain evidence of physical export. Services are zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident and the services are physically rendered outside South Africa, or where the services are supplied to a non-resident in respect of movable property situated outside South Africa. Dropshipping goods directly from foreign suppliers to foreign customers may qualify as zero-rated if the supplier is a mere intermediary and title passes offshore.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / SARS Customs Clearance Certificate) with customs clearance number
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical movement of goods from South Africa to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank forex receipt proving foreign currency settlement for the exported goods
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Proof of foreign customer's non-resident status (e.g., registered business address abroad)

South Africa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for VAT invoicing. VAT invoices must comply with section 20 of the VAT Act, containing the supplier's name, VAT number, invoice serial number, date, description of goods/services, consideration and tax amount. SARS eFiling requires upload of VAT201 returns and supporting data; no mandatory API-based continuous transaction control clearance. The SARS Tax Administration System does not require pre-clearance of individual invoices.

SARS may disregard zero-rating and assess VAT at the standard rate (15%) if export documentation is incomplete, contradictory, or fraudulent. Additional tax may be imposed under section 89q of the Income Tax Act? Under the Tax Administration Act, an understatement penalty of up to 100% of the shortfall can apply for intentional tax evasion or gross negligence. Criminal prosecution for fraud under the Tax Administration Act and the Customs and Excise Act can lead to imprisonment up to 10 years, and SARS may invoke a full retrospective clawback for all prior export zero-ratings without valid export evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand (ZAR) is a managed-float, fully convertible currency for bona fide current-account transactions, including dropshipping revenue, import settlements, and cross-border services. Capital-account transactions remain subject to Authorized Dealer reporting and, for specified outflows, FinSurv prior approval. Non-resident direct equity investment is freely permitted without prior SARS or FinSurv sanction, provided the inward funds are routed through an Authorized Dealer, recorded under the inward direct-investment purpose classification, and evidenced in the dealer's exchange-control records so that future dividend and capital repatriation via Form A2 is legally protected. Profit remittance is permitted once profits are declared, dividends tax at 20% (or a reduced treaty rate evidenced by a SARS withholding-tax certificate) is settled, and standard banking documentation is submitted. There are no hard convertibility quotas on the corporate sector, but exchange-control penalties apply to unreported foreign receivables and offshore retention of export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa through the exporter's Authorized Dealer within 180 days of the shipment date; extensions may be requested from FinSurv through the dealer upon proof of justified delay, such as extended supplier credit terms or disputed receivables. There is no fixed statutory percentage conversion ratio: proceeds may either be converted to ZAR at the prevailing interbank rate or retained in a permitted Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held at an Authorized Dealer. However, every receipt must be routed through the named dealer and supported by shipping and order documentation. For automated dropshipping, each PayPal/Stripe payout batch must be mapped to order-level export evidence (customer, SKU, price, tracked consignment, commercial invoice) to satisfy the 180-day rule and FICA record-keeping; failure to repatriate or leaving funds on an offshore EMI/PSP balance is an exchange-control offence subject to criminal sanctions and administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), is the statutory exchange-control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961. Banking prudential oversight is exercised by the SARB Prudential Authority; the national payment system is governed by the National Payment System Act, 1998, with the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system serving as the large-value real-time gross settlement rail and the Automated Clearing Bureau (ACB) handling domestic retail ZAR clearing.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (normal corporate tax rate for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% Dividends Tax on the amount of any dividend (cash, in specie, or deemed dividend) paid by a South African resident company to shareholders, payable to SARS by the end of the month following the dividend distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 must be filed within 12 months after the end of the company's financial year (year of assessment). A (Pty) Ltd with a 31 March year-end must therefore file by the following 31 March; SARS may issue a default assessment under s95 of the Tax Administration Act where a return is not delivered.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional payment: on or before the last day of the sixth month after the start of the financial year. Second provisional payment: on or before the last day of the financial year. Optional third voluntary top-up payment: within six months after the financial year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South African law does not provide a statutory de minimis threshold for Local File or Master File preparation. The Country-by-Country Report threshold is ZAR 10 billion consolidated annual group revenue for MNE groups. SARS expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for every material cross-border connected transaction; in practice, disclosure of cross-border related-party transactions on the ITR14 transfer-pricing schedule is monitored at an annual transactional value of ZAR 5 million or more. Taxpayers should prepare a Local File to justify arm's-length pricing for any transaction with a non-resident connected person, regardless of size, and be able to produce it within 30 days of a SARS request.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE. However, the general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) in s80A–s80L, s103 of the Income Tax Act, controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution in s9D, and the OECD-aligned transfer-pricing doctrine require the local (Pty) Ltd to demonstrate commercial substance – including local board decision-making, personnel, premises, bank accounts, contractual risk and actual risk assumption. A drop-shipping company that is tax-resident in South Africa but functions as a bare pass-through with no local economic activity and no risk exposure is vulnerable to being recharacterised as a limited-risk distributor, leading to upward profit adjustments, deemed-dividend treatment, and denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent ITR14 Filing

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 25% to 125% of the shortfall under s222 of the Tax Administration Act; criminal prosecution under s234 TAA with a fine of up to ZAR 10 million per count and imprisonment for up to five years; interest at the prescribed rate runs on the unpaid taxes without interruption.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false ITR14s or authorise diversion of revenue are personally liable as 'persons who knowingly permit a false return' under s235 TAA; SARS may obtain a civil restraint order or travel ban, and imprisonment is a standard outcome in fraudulent revenue-diversion cases.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation / Offshore Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: SARS has power to adjust the taxable income under s31 of the Income Tax Act, impose a secondary deemed-dividend adjustment, apply understatement penalties up to 125%, and impose administrative fines per non-compliant ITR14 schedule. Interest on the additional assessment is mandatory.

Director personal liability: A director who caused the company to transfer profits offshore without arm's-length justification can be jointly and severally liable for the additional tax under common law breach of fiduciary duty; criminal tax investigations may disqualify directors and prohibit travel while money-laundering charges are assessed.
Shell Corporation / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS and the Companies Tribunal can reject the corporate form as a sham under substance-over-form doctrine, disallow all deductions, and treat the income as accruing to the real foreign beneficial owner under s7(2)-(4) of the Income Tax Act; failure to maintain registered address and annual returns leads to automatic deregistration under the Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Depositing commercial receipts into a director's personal account triggers statutory personal liability under s20(9) of the Companies Act and s172/s179 of the Tax Administration Act, enabling garnishee orders, attachment of foreign assets, travel bans and director disqualification.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML/CFT) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 38 of 2001, administrative penalties of up to ZAR 10 million may be imposed on accountable institutions for each contravention; criminal sanctions for serious, wilful non-compliance reach 15 years' imprisonment and asset forfeiture. Payment processors and banks are independently required to terminate the account and report to the Financial Intelligence Centre.

Director personal liability: Directors who enable AML breaches with knowledge enjoy no corporate shield; personal liability, criminal prosecution, travel bans and freezing orders are routinely applied to beneficial owners of online payment infrastructures.
Failure to Withhold Dividends, Interest and Royalty Taxes

Statutory sanction: The company remains liable as withholding agent for the full tax amount not withheld, plus a 10% late-payment penalty and interest at the prescribed rate; SARS may also impose administrative non-compliance penalties and deny the deduction for the underlying royalty or interest if the WHT is unpaid.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise a distribution or cross-border payment knowing that withholding tax has not been remitted are personally liable under s235 of the Tax Administration Act and the Fourth Schedule; civil recovery plus travel restrictions and criminal conviction can follow.
