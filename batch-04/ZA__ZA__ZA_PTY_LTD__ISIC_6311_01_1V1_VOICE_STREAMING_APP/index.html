Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), administered via the SARS eFiling portal at https://www.sars.gov.za/

E-government filing portal

For a proprietary limited company (Pty Ltd), obtain a SARS Income Tax Reference Number by registering the company through SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch using the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) registration certificate, memorandum of incorporation, directors' details, and proof of address. If the entity is a foreign company, it must register as an 'external company' with CIPC and then register for tax with SARS; where taxable supplies exceed the VAT threshold, register as a VAT vendor on VAT101 form, submitting supporting documentation including the foreign entity's constitutional documents, a local registration number, and the place of effective management determination.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide a generic check-the-box election for entity classification; all Pty Ltd companies are taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act, 1962. Tax-specific elections available include: VAT grouping election under sections 50-52 of the Value-Added Tax Act, cash-basis VAT accounting election, and various tax rollover elections on corporate restructurings. Hybrid mismatch and anti-avoidance rules apply under Sections 25D, 9D, and the Controlled Foreign Company rules, without elective check-the-box treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R 1,000,000 in taxable turnover (including electronic/digital services) during any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available where the threshold exceeds R 50,000 per annum. A foreign supplier of electronic supplies to South African consumers is mandatorily required to register at the R 1,000,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods made to a non-resident provided the goods are exported within 90 days of the date of supply, and evidence of export is retained by the vendor before the due date for the relevant VAT return. For services, zero-rating applies if the recipient is a non-resident and the services are not supplied to a person in South Africa, with specified exclusions (e.g., services related to movable property situated in South Africa, intellectual property used in South Africa, and services physically rendered in South Africa). For a voice streaming app, export zero-rating can be claimed for subscription and in-app services supplied to non-resident customers if the place of effective use and enjoyment of the service is outside South Africa and documentary proof of non-residence is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based pre-clearance mandate currently applies. SARS requires VAT invoices to comply with Section 20 of the VAT Act, with a supply date, vendor details, and VAT component; invoices may be issued electronically (e.g., PDF, XML, or electronically generated) and submitted via SARS eFiling API integrations. Phyllis a phased implementation of OECD Peppol-based e-invoicing for large enterprises is under way, but it is not yet a mandatory pre-clearance control mechanism.

SARS may audit export zero-rating claims and will disallow the zero-rated treatment and impose VAT plus interest if the goods are not exported or the services do not meet the non-residence/beneficial-use test. Fraudulent export zero-rating triggers additional tax under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 of up to 200% of the assessed shortfall, with monthly understatement penalties, criminal prosecution for tax evasion, and potential civil liability for directors where the claim arises from gross negligence or intentional evasion. Retroactive clawback under Section 16(2)(e) of the VAT Act applies where goods are re-imported into South Africa.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is partially convertible. Current account transactions (cross-border services, licensing fees, royalties, platform commissions) are administratively liberalized: conversion through an Authorized Dealer with documentary verification of the underlying agreement and invoice. Capital account flows are restricted: inward FDI requires no prior FinSurv approval when routed through an Authorized Dealer but must be declared via the FDI reporting system; outward direct investment and loans by South African residents require FinSurv approval above threshold. Profit remittance (dividends) is lawful provided: (i) Companies Act 71 of 2008 solvency/liquidity test is satisfied by board resolution; (ii) any applicable dividends tax (20% standard, 5% under an applicable double-taxation agreement where treaty relief is claimed) is withheld and paid to SARS; (iii) the declaration is lodged with the Authorized Dealer together with audited financial statements and a tax clearance certificate (SARS Pin). No general blocking order exists on remittance to non-resident shareholders, but a digital social-app revenue profile attracts enhanced source-of-funds scrutiny due to the absence of physical export documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Proceeds from exported digital services (including Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements) must be repatriated to South Africa within 30 days from the date of invoice settlement or (where banked offshore under prior approval) within the term of the Authorized Dealer's Foreign Currency Account (FCA) approval. The surrender ratio is 100%: all ZAR-equivalent proceeds must be sold to an Authorized Dealer unless the exporter obtains FinSurv/Authorized Dealer consent to retain funds in a designated FCA for approved working-capital purposes. Retention in an FCA is subject to quarterly reconciliation and annual abridged returns. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline constitutes an exchange-control contravention punishable by administrative penalties and may trigger FICA risk reclassification from Medium_Risk_Enhanced to High Risk.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), exercising delegated authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder; operational enforcement is delegated to Authorized Dealers (qualifying domestic commercial banks), with SARS responsible for tax-clearance verification ancillary to FX approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility for primary treasury function. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not registered as Authorized Dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations and therefore cannot lawfully effect ZAR conversion for a South African resident entity on capital account flows; they may only provide ancillary multi-currency collection where the underlying funds remain offshore and are declared to the Authorized Dealer. Any offshore EMI holding of Apple/Google export proceeds beyond 30/180-day ceilings without FCA declaration creates an automatic Exchange Control contravention. The mandatory clearing spine must be a SARB-supervised bank. EMIs are acceptable only as a secondary collection channel holding immaterial balances, with monthly reconciliation and full audit trail.

Compliant routing for foreign equity or shareholder-loan capital: (i) remitter instructs its bank to issue SWIFT MT103 (unilateral credit) or MT202 (cover) with purpose codes 6010 (equity capital) or 6011 (intercompany loan to SA resident); (ii) payment narrative must name the SA (Pty) Ltd and reference the share subscription agreement; (iii) funds are credited to a blocked Capital Escrow Account (inward listing account) in the name of the non-resident subscriber, administered by the Authorized Dealer; (iv) within 30 days of receipt the entity submits the FDI declaration (Form FIA) on the Authorized Dealer's system together with the subscription agreement and CIPC share-issue documentation; (v) only after FIA reference is issued may the funds be released to the operational ZAR account. Purpose-code misstatement (e.g., using a current-account service code for equity) triggers FICA/reporting sanctions. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% applies to prior periods)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% final dividends withholding tax on 'dividends' under section 64D of the Income Tax Act, payable by the shareholder but withheld and remitted by the South African paying company, unless an exemption applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 12 months of the company's financial year-end; for non-resident companies without an appointed representative, SARS notifies a specific filing date and the ITR14 must be submitted by that date, which is generally the last day of the 12th month after the year-end unless otherwise stated.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Payment 1: due within the first six months of the financial year. Payment 2: due on or before the last day of the financial year. Payment 3: optional, due within six months of the year-end where the estimated taxable income exceeds the prescribed threshold and the first two estimates were insufficient.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File and transfer-pricing policy must be prepared and maintained when affected cross-border related-party transactions exceed ZAR 5 million in aggregate gross value in a year of assessment. A Master File and OECD-behavioural CbC report are required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 1 billion. In addition, the ITR14 TP addendum must be completed when the controlled transaction threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no greedometric Economic Substance Regulation in the Caribbean or Gulf form. Substance is nonetheless enforced through the general anti-avoidance rules (sections 80A-80L), the source and trade tests for permanent establishments, the anti-conduit arrangement doctrine, exchange-control approvals, and the beneficial-owner requirement in DTAs. A ZA (Pty) Ltd that merely books app-store revenue while the offshore parent orchestrates functions, customers, and intangible design without local employees, office capacity, or real decision-making will have its intercompany deductions and treaty rates challenged by SARS.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false tax return

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under section 104(1) of the Tax Administration Act: a fine of up to double the tax evaded per year, imprisonment up to five years, or both. Administrative understatement penalties range from 75% to 150% of the understated tax, plus interest at the prescribed rate and recovery of the full tax shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or cause a false return may be held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties under section 159 of the Tax Administration Act. In egregious cases, corporate-veil piercing allows creditors and SARS to reach personal bank accounts; travel bans may be imposed through SARS's 'outstanding tax debt' flagging process, and conviction may result in imprisonment.
Shell company with no economic substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply the general anti-avoidance rules to disregard a transaction, recharacterise income, deny treaty benefits, and impose understatement penalties of up to 150%. The controlled foreign company regime may attribute the offshore parent's passive income to South African shareholders. Exchange-control contraventions may attract administrative fines of up to ZAR 1 million per transaction and, in serious cases, criminal sanctions under the Currency and Exchanges Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who form the shell without substantive business activity face personal civil liability as the company is treated as an alter ego façade. Courts may pierce the veil to order restitution of avoided tax. Directors may also be banned from future board appointments and are subject to criminal liability when the shell is used to facilitate tax evasion or money movements in breach of exchange-control approval conditions.
AML / Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties under the FIC Act can amount to ZAR 10 million per contravention, with criminal fines up to R100 million and imprisonment up to 15 years for repeated or intentional non-compliance. SARS may also deny a deduction for penalties paid and impose additional understatement penalties for income connected to unrecorded tranches of in-app value.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons, directors, and senior managers are personally liable for the entity's FICA obligations. Conviction blocks them from directorship in any regulated business and exposes personal assets to restraint orders and confiscation; travel bans may be applied under section 45(1) of the FIC Act when a director is suspected of funds flows linked to predicate offences.
Transfer pricing / thin capitalisation violations

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the consideration to arm's length under section 31, disallow the exceeding deduction, and impose understatement penalties from 50% to 100%. Non-submission of TP documentation attracts specific administrative penalties of up to R2,500 per month plus interest agitation. Recharacterised legal financing is disallowed as excessive interest, and dividends disguised as interest are reclassified for dividends-tax and exchange-control purposes.

Director personal liability: A director who certifies the company's assessment or directs the adoption of fabricated comparables can be held jointly and severally liable for penalties realised on the TP adjustment. Personal liability also attaches where the director knew that the subsidiary was under-capitalised and continued to strip profits via debt; such conduct is treated as fraudulent preferencing or sham-transaction facilitation and can carry imprisonment along with financial restitution.
