Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) - SARS eFiling portal (https://efiling.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity establishing South African operations: (1) register the external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); (2) apply to SARS for an Income Tax Reference Number via Form IT/TR (or eFiling 'Register a Taxpayer' functionality); (3) if taxable supplies exceed R 1,000,000 p.a., apply for VAT vendor status on Form VAT 101 (or eFiling); (4) apply for PAYE/UIF/SDL employer registration on Form EMP 101 if hiring employees. A South African (Pty) Ltd is pre-registered for income tax at incorporation and must activate its SARS profile within 60 days of incorporation by submitting notice of registration on eFiling with company documents and founder ID copies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election; a (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxable at entity level on worldwide income. Hybrid mismatch rules (ss 23M-23P Income Tax Act) apply automatically, with limited elections to treat arrangements as aligned with BEPS Action 2. Available operational elections include: CFC high-tax exemption opt-out election (s 9D(2A)); VAT grouping election (s 50 VAT Act) for 50%-related companies to file as one vendor; s 24C provisions election; s 11D R&D allowance election; and employer payroll elections for retirement fund contributions and PAYE annual reconciliation (EMP 501) on SARS eFiling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: R 1,000,000 actual or expected annual taxable turnover; voluntary registration: R 50,000 annual taxable turnover; a non-resident supplying electronic services to South African residents is compulsorily registerable regardless of turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (1) movable goods supplied and exported to a non-resident under s 11(1)(a) VAT Act, provided the exporter retains prescribed documentary proof and the goods leave South Africa within 90 days (or a longer SARS-approved period); (2) services supplied to a non-resident that are physically rendered outside South Africa under s 11(2)(l); (3) services connected to exported goods under s 11(2)(a)-(e). For the payroll SaaS scope, offshore SaaS and payroll automation services will qualify under s 11(2)(l) only where the recipient is a non-resident and the services are not used or consumed in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500/SAD501) lodged via SARS Customs eCSP
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill of loading evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange control / RMC or General Banking Declaration)
  • Commercial invoice and VAT export log
  • Contract or sales order evidencing non-resident counterparty
  • Proof of receipt of payment in foreign currency
  • Consignment records or export permits for restricted goods

No real-time fiscal clearance system, continuous transaction controls (CTC), or digital stamping is currently mandated by SARS. Vendors must issue a full or abridged tax invoice containing statutory particulars (s 20 VAT Act). Electronic tax invoices (including XML/e-invoicing) are acceptable, and SARS accepts automated filing via eFiling and its API channel; a unilateral mandate for e-invoicing may be announced by SARS on notice, but none is in force for this regime.

If zero-rating is claimed without the full export evidence bundle, SARS may assess VAT at 15%, claw back input tax deductions and disallow the refund (ss 16(2)(f) and 54 VAT Act). Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties range from 10% (substantial non-disclosure) to 150% (intentional tax evasion) of the shortfall; deliberate fraudulent export refund schemes face a 200% penalty, interest, criminal prosecution, and personal liability of directors under s 155 TAA. SARS's customs-vat data matching with the SAD500, bank foreign-exchange records, and IRC system is the primary audit trigger.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is fully convertible on the current account: SaaS export receipts, dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees are remittable without prior FinSurv approval, provided the resident entity moves funds through a South African authorised dealer with correct BOP purpose codes, underlying contracts/invoices, and a valid SARS tax-clearance status (TCC PIN). The capital account is restricted under a positive-list approval regime: inward direct equity investment by non-residents into a (Pty) Ltd is freely permitted without prior approval, but outward capital transfers, offshore account opening, guarantees, and foreign-currency borrowing by the resident require FinSurv approval or delegated authorised-dealer authority. Profit remittance hurdles for a payroll-SaaS exporter include: (i) dividend remittance requires a board declaration, SARS TCC PIN, BOP coding, and 20% dividend withholding tax (treaty-reduced where applicable); (ii) connected-party software licence or service payments must satisfy the s.31 transfer-pricing rules and the FinSurv royalty/fee formula (generally 0-6% of net turnover for technology licensing); (iii) shareholder loans must be documented, reported to the authorised dealer as inward loans, and must respect s.23M earnings-stripping limits (30% of tax EBITDA) and debt:equity norms; (iv) beneficial-ownership filings at CIPC and the FICA BO register must be current; and (v) all export earnings must be repatriated to South Africa within the prescribed timeline—they cannot be accumulated in offshore EMIs or bank accounts without FinSurv approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since 1 January 2021, the historical mandatory 10% surrender of export proceeds to the SARB has been abolished; the statutory conversion ratio for export receipts is now 0%. The general timeline is that services-export proceeds (including SaaS subscription revenue) must be received in South Africa within 180 days of the invoice date, extendable to 360 days by an authorised dealer on application; goods-export proceeds must be received within 180 days of shipment. Upon inward receipt, funds must be converted to ZAR within 30 days unless the exporter maintains an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with a South African authorised dealer permitting retention in USD/EUR. For a payroll-SaaS platform invoicing global clients in foreign currency, each inward MT103 credit must be matched to a signed SaaS agreement and invoice, coded under the SARB BOP computer-services category, and retained for five years in the FinSurv/SARS compliance file.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), the statutory exchange-control regulator for South African residents. Authority derives from the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder, the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA), and the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998. SARB also supervises the national payment infrastructure through the National Payment System Department (NPSD) and the Payments Association of South Africa (PASA), which operates the ACB (Automated Clearing Bureau) debit-order and EFT clearing rails. Foreign-currency allocation, force-conversion powers, and hedging approvals are administered exclusively through authorised dealers, being registered commercial banks, not through private fintech entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for primary treasury operations. A South African resident (Pty) Ltd may not maintain undisclosed offshore balances with non-authorised fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or EU/UK EMI shells) because such accounts violate the Exchange Control Regulations and the compulsory repatriation/retention framework—only FinSurv or an authorised dealer may approve an offshore facility. Offshore EMIs are not authorised dealers, do not participate in SARB reporting, and cannot operate ACB debit-order or SAMOS settlement rails. Their permissible role is limited to peripheral client-side collection gateways, provided funds are swept immediately into the South African bank account of the entity without creating an overnight offshore balance. Every payroll disbursement or vendor settlement for the SaaS operation must clear through a PASA-member sponsored account and a licensed authorised dealer, with BOP reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors wire USD/EUR via SWIFT MT103 (gpi) to the company's USD/EUR FCA or directly to the ZAR operating account at the selected South African authorised dealer. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'INW/EQ - share subscription - ZA_PTY_LTD - allotment [date]' and reference the SARB BOPDIR purpose code for inward direct investment in equity capital; no FinSurv pre-approval is required for inward ordinary shares in a software (Pty) Ltd. The bank will treat the credit as conditionally cleared until the company files: (i) board resolution allotting the shares; (ii) signed share certificates; (iii) CIPC notice and amended MOI/CoR proof; (iv) SARS tax-status confirmation; (v) inward-investment declaration; and (vi) updated FICA/CIPC beneficial-ownership records. Upon verification, funds convert to cleared ZAR or remain in an approved FCA; the allotment must then be lodged at CIPC and the capital registered with the authorised dealer so that future dividends, interest, and disinvestment proceeds are freely remittable. If the injection is instead structured as a shareholder loan, the loan must be documented, reported as an inward loan, and its interest/repayment will require SARS tax clearance, BOP coding, and compliance with s.23M and debt:equity limits.Vendor, infrastructure, and subcontractor payments are executed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV through the authorised dealer with underlying invoice/contract evidence and BOP service codes (e.g., computer and information services, business services). Commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 are available for physical hardware or delivery-guaranteed software projects, but recurring SaaS licensing is normally settled open-account via MT103. The payroll automation model also requires the South African ACB direct-debit (ACH) rail: the company, or its PASA-sponsored payment partner, originates debit orders from local client bank accounts, then settles any foreign net obligation by MT103 through the authorised dealer. Outbound current-account payments in sensitive categories—royalties, connected-party services, or transactions exceeding the authorised dealer's delegated threshold—require FinSurv pre-approval, SARS tax-clearance, and a royalty/service agreement. Outbound dividends to the non-resident shareholder are remitted by MT103 with BOP outward-dividend coding, the dividend declaration, SARS TCC PIN, 20% dividend-WHT proof (or treaty-rate annex), and must originate solely from the South African banking book; no routing through offshore EMIs or third-country conduits is permitted.Deliverable forward exchange contracts (FECs) in USD/ZAR and EUR/ZAR, with a maximum tenor of 12 months, against committed or highly probable SaaS receivables and vendor payables, executed exclusively through a South African authorised dealer.FX rollovers and tom-next swaps on approved Foreign Currency Account balances for short-dated cash-flow alignment.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) to hedge long-term offshore shareholder loans or preference-share liabilities.Vanilla OTC currency options (USD/ZAR and EUR/ZAR calls and puts) on a covered basis with premium paid up front; naked option writing is prohibited.Exchange-traded USD/ZAR futures and options on the JSE (Safex) for centrally cleared, collateral-efficient hedging.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/ZAR offshore—permissible only with specific FinSurv approval and demonstrable underlying offshore exposure; generally disfavoured for South African resident corporates because the ZAR is deliverable and the onshore FEC market is the compliant alternative.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary treasury operations. A South African resident (Pty) Ltd may not maintain undisclosed offshore balances with non-authorised fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or EU/UK EMI shells) because such accounts violate the Exchange Control Regulations and the compulsory repatriation/retention framework—only FinSurv or an authorised dealer may approve an offshore facility. Offshore EMIs are not authorised dealers, do not participate in SARB reporting, and cannot operate ACB debit-order or SAMOS settlement rails. Their permissible role is limited to peripheral client-side collection gateways, provided funds are swept immediately into the South African bank account of the entity without creating an overnight offshore balance. Every payroll disbursement or vendor settlement for the SaaS operation must clear through a PASA-member sponsored account and a licensed authorised dealer, with BOP reporting.
