Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official portal: SARS eFiling (https://efiling.sars.gov.za); corporate website: https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign (Pty) Ltd transactions in South Africa must obtain a single SARS tax reference number before trading. Procedure: (1) appoint a South African resident public officer; (2) secure a physical South African business address; (3) register via SARS eFiling using the 'Register for Tax' facility under 'Company / Foreign Company'; (4) lodge the supporting statutory documents, including the foreign certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation or constitutive documents, certified IDs or passports of directors and the public officer, proof of the South African trading address, and details of the South African bank account; (5) simultaneously register for Corporate Income Tax, VAT (where the turnover threshold applies or voluntary registration is selected), PAYE, UIF, and SDL where applicable; (6) receive a tax reference number and, if registered for VAT, a VAT vendor number linked to that same reference number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign (Pty) Ltd transactions in South Africa must obtain a single SARS tax reference number before trading. Procedure: (1) appoint a South African resident public officer; (2) secure a physical South African business address; (3) register via SARS eFiling using the 'Register for Tax' facility under 'Company / Foreign Company'; (4) lodge the supporting statutory documents, including the foreign certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation or constitutive documents, certified IDs or passports of directors and the public officer, proof of the South African trading address, and details of the South African bank account; (5) simultaneously register for Corporate Income Tax, VAT (where the turnover threshold applies or voluntary registration is selected), PAYE, UIF, and SDL where applicable; (6) receive a tax reference number and, if registered for VAT, a VAT vendor number linked to that same reference number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box elective classification; every company, including a (Pty) Ltd, is per se an entity-level taxpayer. Hybrid debt and equity instruments are recharacterized under sections 8E, 8F, 8FA and hybrid entities under sections 8J-8M of the Income Tax Act; no elective hybrid mismatch check-the-box exists. Statutory elections available include: (i) the Small Business Corporation (SBC) election under section 12E(4) for a qualifying (Pty) Ltd with gross income not exceeding R20 million per year, producing graduated corporate rates; (ii) voluntary VAT vendor registration under section 23(2) of the VAT Act where taxable supplies exceed R50,000 but remain below the R1,000,000 compulsory threshold; and (iii) election to apply zero-rating to export supplies only when the section 11 export conditions are satisfied, as an election is not required for zero-rating once a valid export has occurred.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration: ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected in the following 12 months; voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 in taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months. All amounts are in South African Rand (ZAR).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act for the supply of goods that are physically exported from South Africa to a foreign destination. The vendor must prove that the goods were supplied to a non-resident or to the export carrier or freight-forwarder, and that the goods were removed from South Africa within the prescribed export period (normally 90 days from the date of supply). For an online automotive parts retail operation, overseas sales of replacement parts and tuning accessories qualify for 0% VAT only if the goods are actually exported and accompanied by a customs-cleared export declaration; domestic in-country deliveries remain standard-rated at 15%. Certain cross-border services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated under section 11(2)(k) and related provisions if the recipient is outside South Africa and does not use the services in South Africa. The vendor must maintain sufficient documentary proof and preserve the records for at least five years.

  • Valid South African Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / Bill of Entry) cleared by SARS Customs
  • Transport document evidencing export: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR Road Consignment Note, or rail manifest
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the exact goods supplied and shipped
  • Tax invoice or customer order record issued by the (Pty) Ltd
  • Proof of settlement / Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank foreign exchange receipt) evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Evidence of customer non-resident status, such as a foreign passport, overseas business registration, or foreign delivery address

There is currently no statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate for VAT invoices. SARS recognises electronic tax invoices under section 20(5)(b) of the VAT Act where the invoice satisfies the prescribed substantive details and is retained digitally. VAT 201 declarations and corporate income tax returns are filed through SARS eFiling, EDI, or approved API channels; SARS does not require third-party full-time equivalence control or continuous transaction control stamping for e-commerce vendors as of the current filing period. SARS has announced a phased e-invoicing and digital reporting modernisation roadmap that will eventually require structured electronic invoice data submission via SARS APIs.

SARS rigorously verifies all export zero-rating claims. If the vendor cannot produce the prescribed export documentation or fails to satisfy the section 11(1)(a) conditions, SARS will re-assess the supply at the standard 15% VAT rate, recovering the full output tax previously zero-rated. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, a retrospective assessment may be raised within five years of assessment, or up to ten years in the case of deliberate tax evasion or fraud, allowing SARS to claw back refunds with statutory interest. In addition, understatement penalties range from 10% to 150% of the tax shortfall depending on the degree of negligent or intentional conduct. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating may result in fines, imprisonment, personal liability of directors and the public officer for VAT liabilities, and disqualification from serving as a company officer; the transactional record will be subject to forensic review under SARS's export risk engine.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is fully convertible on current-account transactions, subject to Authorised Dealer reporting and SARS tax-compliance clearance. Capital-account flows are more constrained: inward foreign direct investment, equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, and outbound profit repatriation must be executed through an Authorised Dealer and may require SARB/FinSurv approval where they exceed applicable allowances or exemption limits. Dividend remittances are freely permitted on a revenue-account basis once the profits have been reflected in audited financial statements and all tax liabilities, including dividends tax and income tax, have been settled or provided for; a tax clearance statement from SARS is normally required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be fully repatriated to South Africa and credited to an Authorised Dealer account in the name of the exporting entity within 180 days from the date of shipment. There is no statutory fixed percentage conversion ratio; exporters may retain the foreign currency in a foreign-currency account with an Authorised Dealer or convert to rand at their discretion. Failure to meet the 180-day repatriation timeline requires a written extension application to the Authorised Dealer and may attract exchange-control penalties.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, and delegated to registered Authorised Dealers for day-to-day exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Offshore fintech and electronic-money institutions are not recognised as banks or Authorised Dealers under South African exchange control and the Banks Act, 1990. They may be used as front-end collection and disbursement rails for low-value e-commerce flows, particularly via Stripe and PayPal, but the ultimate ZAR clearing and any exchange-control settlement must occur through an SA Authorised Dealer. Offshore EMIs cannot hold a domestic ZAR settlement account, cannot issue inward FDI receipts, and cannot act as the sole treasury rail for statutory trade payments or dividend repatriation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI equity or shareholder-loan proceeds must be remitted by the non-resident shareholder from the investing entity's own bank account, using SWIFT MT103 wire instructions, with the beneficiary being the ZA (Pty) Ltd operational account held at an SA Authorised Dealer. The purpose code must be the inward Balance of Payments code prescribed by the Authorised Dealer for 'Direct investment in South Africa – equity capital' or 'Shareholder loan', and the payment narrative must reference the share subscription agreement or loan agreement. The Authorised Dealer will require the board resolution approving the share issue, executed subscription agreement, memorandum of incorporation, share register, tax compliance status, and proof of identity of the non-resident investor; the inward funds may be held in a temporary escrow account pending issuance of the ordinary shares, with strict anti-money-laundering verification of the ultimate beneficial owner and source of funds.Settlement for imported automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories should be routed through the SA banking network by SWIFT MT103, with MT202 COVER where applicable, against a valid commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, packing list, SAD500 customs declaration, and the applicable South African exchange-control import form, including Form CF1 where prescribed by the Authorised Dealer. For larger and recurring supplier relationships, a commercial documentary letter of credit is the preferred clearing rail, issued via SWIFT MT700 and settled against UCP600-compliant presentation; local ZAR disbursements to domestic fulfilment and logistics providers are cleared through BankservAfrica's SAMOS system using domestic electronic funds transfer rails.Deliverable ZAR forward contracts with a registered SA Authorised Dealer, backed by documented current or anticipated trade exposures arising from the import of motor-vehicle parts and components.FX swaps and short-dated currency swaps for working-capital liquidity management where the underlying principal is a real receivable or payable and the transaction is recorded with an Authorised Dealer.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) for hedging the currency and interest-rate risk of offshore shareholder loans or external borrowings, provided the exposure is genuine, covered, and approved by the Authorised Dealer.Plain-vanilla FX options and structured forwards with Authorised Dealers or JSE-licensed derivative providers where the sole purpose is hedging economic exposure, not speculation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible for onshore South African residents because the ZAR is a deliverable currency; offshore NDFs may arise only between non-residents or with explicit FinSurv approval and cannot be used as a direct hedge by an SA corporate.
