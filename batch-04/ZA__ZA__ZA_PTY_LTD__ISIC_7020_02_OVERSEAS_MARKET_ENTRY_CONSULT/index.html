Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a proprietary limited company; no escrow account or banker's certificate is required. Shares are issued for consideration that need only satisfy insolvency and liquidity tests under section 4 and section 31 of the Companies Act.

Funding & unlock path: No special civil defence licence applies. The company must ensure leased office premises meet fire safety requirements under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Act 85 of 1993) and applicable municipal fire regulations; this includes obtaining a valid certificate of compliance from the landlord or local fire authority where required.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a proprietary limited company; no escrow account or banker's certificate is required. Shares are issued for consideration that need only satisfy insolvency and liquidity tests under section 4 and section 31 of the Companies Act.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official portal: https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African company must first incorporate as a private company (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Upon incorporation, SARS automatically issues a provisional income tax reference number, but the company must register separately for VAT, PAYE, UIF, and SDL via the SARS eFiling portal. The company may also apply for a SARS tax reference number directly on eFiling, with the required documents including the Notice of Incorporation (CoR 14.3), company registration certificate, and proof of registered address. Alternatively, pending CIPC registration, the company can obtain an interim tax reference number through the SARS branch network, for key operational registrations such as VAT and customs when transacting with other registered South African businesses.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned South African company must first incorporate as a private company (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Upon incorporation, SARS automatically issues a provisional income tax reference number, but the company must register separately for VAT, PAYE, UIF, and SDL via the SARS eFiling portal. The company may also apply for a SARS tax reference number directly on eFiling, with the required documents including the Notice of Incorporation (CoR 14.3), company registration certificate, and proof of registered address. Alternatively, pending CIPC registration, the company can obtain an interim tax reference number through the SARS branch network, for key operational registrations such as VAT and customs when transacting with other registered South African businesses.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not offer a general 'check-the-box' entity classification election. A (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed as a separate tax entity at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (financial years ending on or after 31 March 2023). Companies are not permitted to elect to be taxed as a partnership or disregarded entity for income tax. Hybrid mismatch rules in the Taxation Laws Amendment Act apply to cross-border arrangements (e.g., hybrid entity mismatches under Section 25D and the Eighth Schedule to the Income Tax Act) and must be considered in cross-border structuring, but there is no statutory elective mechanism to alter the default status of a South African private company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1 million (ZAR 1,000,000) in taxable supplies per 12-month rolling period; compulsory VAT registration applies once this threshold is exceeded. A foreign supplier of imported electronic services remains subject to the electronic services VAT registration regime, with registration required regardless of a threshold in certain circumstances; foreign suppliers providing electronic services to South African residents must register for VAT when supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies to the export of goods from South Africa to a non-resident recipient outside the common customs area, including supplies under Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act (exports made under a customs agreement). Goods must be exported within 60 days of the time of supply, or within 90 days if an advance payment is received, and the exporter must retain proof of export. For export of services, zero-rating applies if the services are physically rendered outside South Africa, or in cases of services supplied for goods or services exported, and for services supplied directly to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time the services are rendered (Section 11(2)(a)). To claim zero-rating, the supplier must hold the applicable documentation evidencing export: customs export declaration (or exemption letter), commercial invoice, transport documents (bill of lading / airway bill), and proof of payment. If goods are destined for a SACU member state, zero-rating does not apply to the SACU supply because those are generally treated as domestic supplies within the common customs union; VAT rules for SACU exports are addressed separately.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (EC326 or electronic declaration via EO interface via the Trade Hub / Damsel)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Airway Bill (air) as proof of export
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the registered supplier
  • Proof of payment / bank transfer advice or SWIFT confirmation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FI/144/IRC, issued by an authorised dealer, where foreign settlement is required for refund/zero-rating substantiation)
  • Registered exporter's SARS VAT registration certificate
  • Export packing list, where applicable
  • Proof of the non-resident status of the recipient (e.g., tax registration certificate or sworn affidavit)

South Africa has not implemented a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) system. SARS uses a post-audit electronic invoicing framework under the VAT Act and the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002. VAT invoices may be issued electronically and must include: company name, VAT number, invoice number, supplier/recipient details, description, quantity, price, VAT amount, and date. A 2015 SARS VAT guide requires a valid tax invoice and provides for the unique identification of the supplier. There is currently no mandatory e-invoice stamping, fiscal digital signature, or real-time SARS API clearance mandate, although SARS is developing an online e-invoicing pilot. For export proof purposes, a commercial invoice, proforma invoice, bill of lading, EC326 export declaration, and any relevant tender documentation are required.

SARS may disallow zero-rating and raise an additional VAT assessment where the documentary proof does not establish physical export or the service is not rendered outside South Africa. Section 55 of the VAT Act applies to recover VAT where the goods are subsequent to a zero-rated export found to be diverted for local use or where export does not occur. Penalties: under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (TAA), a taxpayer may incur an understatement penalty (UP) of 10% to 150% of the understated VAT liability depending on classification (e.g., substantial understatement, reasonable care not exercised), plus interest at the prescribed rate (reference rate). Fraudulent zero-rating (deliberate misrepresentation or aiding false declarations) is a criminal offence; SARS may impose a non-compliance penalty of up to ZAR 2,000 per month (or 10% of the tax for a default) and may refer the matter for prosecution, carrying potential imprisonment and civil recovery of the VAT with penal interest. SARS also applies an administrative non-compliance penalty under sections 209 and 212 of the TAA for failure to keep the required export records, and a customs penalty under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 for false export declarations (up to 100% of the value of the goods or a fine of up to ZAR 50,000). Where an exporter fails to obtain the inward remittance certificate or the foreign consideration is not received within the applicable exchange-control allowed period, SARS may also disregard the zero-rating and claw back the VAT, together with interest and understatement penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction; the rand is fully convertible for bona fide current-account payments, subject to authorised-dealer verification. Capital-account transactions remain controlled: non-resident inward direct investment into a (Pty) Ltd is freely permitted, but resident outward direct investment and informal cross-border transfers require prior FinSurv approval. Profit remittances, including dividends and interest, are permitted to non-resident shareholders and lenders once the company has satisfied SARS tax-compliance requirements (e.g. dividend withholding tax, interest/royalty withholding tax) and the bank has cleared the payment through exchange-control reporting. No quantitative profit-remittance caps apply, but all cross-border flows must be effected via an authorised dealer and accurately coded.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Chapter B of the SARB Exchange Control Manual, export proceeds for physical goods must be received in full into South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days of the date of shipment. The exporter may retain the full amount of foreign currency in an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) without any mandatory conversion ratio; alternatively, the proceeds may be converted to rand at prevailing rates. The 180-day limit is strict; an extension may be requested from FinSurv where the exporter can demonstrate genuine commercial delay. For ISIC 7020 consulting services, the same 180-day timeline applies to inward settlement of service export invoices unless the underlying contract expressly provides another payment schedule and the authorised dealer approves the deviation.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), operating under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations of 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a principal treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs and cross-border platforms such as Wise Business/B2B are not authorised dealers and cannot replace a licensed South African banking account for exchange-control purposes. A ZA-resident (Pty) Ltd may use such platforms only for low-value supplementary settlement or petty disbursements, and all funds must ultimately be repatriated or funded through an SARB authorised dealer with full transaction coding. Use of an unregulated EMI as the main cross-border account creates FATCA/CRS reporting ambiguity, FIC Act risk, and FinSurv obstacles on capital repatriation, therefore the primary treasury ledger should remain on a tier-1 domestic bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's overseas bank to the ZA (Pty) Ltd's operational ZAR account at an authorised dealer. The MT103 must include the beneficiary name and account number, the bank's SWIFT BIC, and a clear purpose narrative such as 'Foreign Direct Investment - equity subscription' or the applicable SARB purpose-of-payment code for inward equity capital. The receiving bank will verify that the company is not under a SARS tax-compliance hold, apply FICA/KYC checks, and automatically report the inward transfer to FinSurv. Amounts above the bank's reporting threshold will require a source-of-funds declaration and, in some cases, a signed non-resident declaration. Once cleared, the funds are credited in ZAR and the share subscription must be recorded in the company's share register and CIPC annual return.Outbound payments for consultancy fees, trade settlements, and overseas market-entry disbursements should be executed via SWIFT MT103 through the authorised dealer on the company's online banking platform. For physical goods associated with client projects, use commercial letters of credit (LC at sight or tenor) and documentary collections; for pure services, SWIFT MT103 remains the primary rail. The company must provide a tax-compliance PIN issued by SARS for all foreign payments, and the bank will apply the specified Exchange Control purpose code (e.g. consultancy fees, professional services, royalties). Low-value cross-border services may be settled through platforms such as Wise B2B, but the ZA entity must keep a clear invoice trail and not use the platform to avoid SARB financial-surveillance reporting. Non-resident counterparties may transact in the offshore NDF market, but the ZA entity's books and settlements must remain onshore.
