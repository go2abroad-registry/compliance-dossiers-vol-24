Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box regime: a (Pty) Ltd is statutorily a separate taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023) and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Relevant statutory elections and mandatory classifications: (i) s 24I(4) Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 functional-currency election for foreign-exchange transactions; (ii) corporate-reorganisation elections under ss 42-47 (asset-for-share, amalgamation, intra-group transfers, intra-group loans, liquidation distributions) requiring a 70% 'group of companies' relationship with roll-over relief; (iii) s 11A pre-trade expenditure deduction election; (iv) s 50 VAT Act election enabling associated enterprises to form a single VAT group; (v) micro-business turnover tax election (Sixth Schedule, s 48 ITA) for qualifying entities with annual turnover not exceeding ZAR 1,000,000; (vi) hybrid-debt, hybrid-interest and hybrid-instrument rules (ss 8F, 8FA, 23M and 23N ITA) are mandatory re-characterisation provisions, not elections; (vii) s 11D research-and-development incentive requires prior endorsement by the Department of Science and Innovation and SARS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory registration (taxable supplies made in the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected in the next 12 months); ZAR 50,000 voluntary registration threshold; non-resident vendors must register where they carry on an 'enterprise' in South Africa (s 23 VAT Act)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under s 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the vendor exports the goods physically from South Africa within the prescribed period and retains the export declaration and transport evidence. Services: zero-rated under s 11(2)(l) where supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time of supply, and under s 11(2)(m) where the services are supplied directly to a non-resident and the services are consumed or used outside South Africa; the statutory exclusions in s 11(2)(l) (services directly connected with South African land, movable property located in South Africa, or a South African event or performance) must be excluded. For a cross-border M&A advisory boutique, the engagement letter, client board/management situs, decision-making location, governing law, economic benefit and place-of-consumption analysis are the decisive zero-rating determinants; success fees related to acquisitions or divestitures of offshore targets by non-resident clients are generally eligible for the 0% rate where the recipient is outside South Africa and the services are not consumed in South Africa, subject to the s 11(2) exclusions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 / electronic EEI where goods are exported)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (proof of physical export of goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign-currency receipt evidencing settlement from the non-resident client)
  • Commercial invoice and s 20 VAT tax invoice
  • Signed engagement letter, underlying transaction agreement, non-resident status evidence and place-of-consumption analysis

No universal real-time fiscal stamping or ex-ante clearance is currently imposed on advisory services in South Africa. VAT vendors must issue s 20-compliant tax invoices (paper or electronic) or combined invoices within 21 days of the time of supply, retain records for 5 years (s 55 VAT Act), and file monthly or two-monthly VAT 201 returns electronically via SARS eFiling. SARS has launched a phased, PEPPOL-based e-Invoicing programme (voluntary onboarding of the large-business segment from 2025 with progressive expansion), coupled with SARS e@sys digital document verification, automatic third-party data matching, and continuous audit analytics. Physical digital tax stamps/permits remain mandatory only for specified excisable goods (e.g., alcoholic beverages and tobacco products); they do not apply to M&A advisory services supplied by a (Pty) Ltd.

SARS rigorously audits export zero-rating claims: if goods are not exported within the prescribed period, or the prescribed documents are unavailable, the supply is deemed standard-rated and VAT at 15% is clawed back with additional tax of 10% under s 39 of the VAT Act plus statutory interest. Incorrect refunds attract additional tax under s 60 of the VAT Act (100% of the VAT shortfall; 200% where fraud is involved), understatement penalties under ss 222-223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 ranging from 10% to 200% of the shortfall (25% for careless conduct or substantial understatement, 50% for no reasonable grounds, 100% for gross negligence, 150-200% for intentional evasion), monthly non-compliance penalties under s 210 TAA, criminal fines of up to ZAR 2,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 years under s 234 TAA, and imprisonment of up to 5 years under s 235 TAA for deliberate tax evasion. Export evidence must be retained for 5 years (s 55 VAT Act) and produced on SARS demand; fraudulent or reckless zero-rating claims may also trigger a full enterprise-wide VAT audit, suspension of refunds, and the issue of additional assessments in respect of prior tax periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a fully deliverable currency with liberalised current-account convertibility for bona fide service fees, dividends, interest, royalties, and trade-related payments, subject to the provision of supporting documents, FICA-compliant beneficial-ownership verification, and a valid SARS tax-clearance certificate where required by the authorised dealer's risk limits. Capital-account transacting is controlled: inward non-resident investment in a South African (Pty) Ltd is permitted without prior FinSurv approval, provided the inward funds are routed through an authorised dealer, correctly reported under the Balance of Payments (BOP)/CSD-RTT framework, and are not derived from non-compliant sources. Outbound remittances of dividends, loan capital, and shareholders' loans require authorised-dealer verification of the underlying corporate resolution, audited or reviewed financials, tax compliance (dividends tax / interest withholding tax), and, for sums above bank thresholds, a SARS tax-clearance certificate (TC01/TRC). The main practical remittance hurdle for a foreign-owned M&A boutique is not absolute capital controls but documentary integrity: a fully executed engagement agreement, proper invoice trail, proof of economic substance, and a demonstrated absence of structuring solely to circumvent South African exchange-control or tax rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa does not impose a fixed percentage-based export-proceeds surrender requirement, but residents are obliged to ensure that all export proceeds from services (including cross-border M&A success fees) are received into a South African authorised-dealer account within 180 days of the date of the service invoice or performance. Upon receipt, foreign currency must be converted into ZAR unless the client has obtained an authorised-dealer-approved dispensation or a foreign-currency retention facility (e.g., a status entity or an approved offshore concentration account). Failure to repatriate within the 180-day window must be explained to the authorised dealer, and the bank will require a debtor-ageing analysis and SARS completion declaration to avoid an adverse exchange-control finding. For deal-contingent success fees, the 180-day clock commences from the later of contractual due date or actual closing; practitioners should contemporaneously document closing statements to pre-empt FinSurv queries.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv); day-to-day execution is delegated to Authorised Dealers (ADs), being registered South African banks bound by the Currency and Exchanges Manual (CEM), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (as amended), and FinSurv circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium with material legal constraints. South Africa does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences, and offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not authorised dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations. Cross-border success-fee collections routed through an unregulated EMI risk breaching the requirement that all foreign-currency transactions be executed via a South African authorised dealer, and may cause inbound receipts to be reclassified as non-compliant capital inflows. Offshore EMIs may, however, be used for ancillary purposes such as receiving low-value referral payments in USD/EUR before conversion via the AD, provided (i) the AD is disclosed as the ultimate settlement channel; (ii) all amounts are fully declared in the company's BOP reporting; and (iii) the company maintains its primary treasury relationship with a regulated South African bank. The authors advise that a South African (Pty) Ltd should not rely on an EMI as its primary cross-border collection rail because FinSurv can refuse to give clearing confirmation to receipts that bypass the national payment system, and FIC-AML exposure increases.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders subscribe for Pty Ltd shares by wiring funds directly to the company's capital-receipts account at the authorised dealer. The bank records the transfer under the inward-transfer process, assigns the prescribed Balance of Payments (BOP) purpose code for equity capital under the CSD-RTT reporting framework (e.g., code 1100 for inward direct investment — equity), and issues an inward-transfer certificate evidencing the foreign capital in South Africa. The company must provide its board resolution authorising the share issue, the amended memorandum of incorporation if required, the shareholders' register reflecting the subscription, and, where the shareholder is a corporate entity, its constitutional documents and UBO declaration. Loan capital from non-resident shareholders requires a signed loan agreement with an arm's-length interest rate, and must be filed with the authorised dealer for acknowledgement so that future interest and principal repayments can be cleared outward without additional FinSurv approval. To avoid misclassification of the remittance as operating revenue, the investor must state 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION — EQUITY' in SWIFT field 70 (Remittance Information) and reference the designated capital account, not the general operational account. For amounts above the bank's threshold, SARS tax-clearance and FICA/UBO documentation will be required before activation.Outbound clearing is executed via SWIFT MT103 through the authorised dealer's nostro network. For an M&A boutique, the principal outbound flows are: (i) success-fee payments to non-resident referral partners, (ii) professional fees to foreign legal and accounting advisors, (iii) data-room and virtual data-room subscriptions, and (iv) deal-related travel and administrative costs. Each payment requires a completed outward-payment instruction with the correct BOP purpose code (e.g., service code 7200 for business/management services or an equivalent professional-services code) and, for payments above the bank's internal threshold, a SARS tax-clearance certificate, the underlying engagement agreement or invoice, proof of service completion, and a declaration confirming the payment is not a disguised outward investment or capital flight. The bank debits the ZAR account and performs the FX conversion at the daily dealing rate, or debits the corresponding foreign-currency sub-account if the company maintains multi-currency accounts. Letters of credit are not relevant to a service-based transaction; the appropriate rail is clean wire transfer (MT103) with full supporting documentation. To reduce FX conversion leakage, the treasury blueprint should maintain ZAR, USD, and EUR accounts at the same authorised dealer, enabling same-day conversion and settlement without intermediary bank delays.Deliverable forward exchange contracts (FECs) with the authorised dealer covering committed USD or EUR success-fee receivables, with a maximum tenor typically of 12 months, renewable upon re-verification of the underlying deal documentation and subject to the historical-test/(trade-related) rule.FX swaps (swap deposits) executed with the authorised dealer to match the timing of an anticipated fee receipt with an identified outbound disbursement, structured on a non-speculative basis.OTC foreign-currency options (call or put) written by the authorised dealer to hedge pipeline deal exposure, provided the notional is capped at the expected fee amount and the option is not used for leveraged speculation.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS), permissible only where the company has long-dated foreign-currency debt or material funding liabilities, and then only with the authorisation of the dealer and, where required, FinSurv clearance; not a standard instrument for advisory-fee treasury management.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): not permitted onshore as a primary hedging tool because ZAR is a fully deliverable currency; any NDF use would require an express FinSurv dispensation demonstrating an inability to execute a deliverable contract in the relevant currency pair, and is therefore excluded from the standard toolkit.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium with material legal constraints. South Africa does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences, and offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not authorised dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations. Cross-border success-fee collections routed through an unregulated EMI risk breaching the requirement that all foreign-currency transactions be executed via a South African authorised dealer, and may cause inbound receipts to be reclassified as non-compliant capital inflows. Offshore EMIs may, however, be used for ancillary purposes such as receiving low-value referral payments in USD/EUR before conversion via the AD, provided (i) the AD is disclosed as the ultimate settlement channel; (ii) all amounts are fully declared in the company's BOP reporting; and (iii) the company maintains its primary treasury relationship with a regulated South African bank. The authors advise that a South African (Pty) Ltd should not rely on an EMI as its primary cross-border collection rail because FinSurv can refuse to give clearing confirmation to receipts that bypass the national payment system, and FIC-AML exposure increases.
