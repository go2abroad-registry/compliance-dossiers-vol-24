Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), a public entity under the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (dtic), operating the CIPC e-Services online registry.

Governing statute

Companies Act 71 of 2008, as amended by the Companies Amendment Act 16 of 2022 — ss. 13 (incorporation), 14 (notice of incorporation), 15 (Memorandum of Incorporation), 19 (legal capacity), 31 (share capital/no par value), 66 (board of directors), 86 (company secretary) and Chapter 5 (fundamental transactions); Companies Regulations, 2011, including Regulation 14 (name objections) and Forms CoR 9.1, CoR 14.1 and CoR 44.1.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form CoR 14.1 (Notice of Incorporation) together with the Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI) filed through the CIPC e-Services 'New Companies' workflow; optional prior name reservation on Form CoR 9.1.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 32 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum statutory share capital is prescribed by the Companies Act 71 of 2008; the company must, however, allot at least one ordinary share upon incorporation, and the MOI may stipulate the authorised share classes. Issued share subscription proceeds must be credited to a South African corporate bank account in the company's name. For a foreign subscriber, the inward payment is subject to SARB Exchange Control Regulations administered through an Authorised Dealer (bank); funds exceeding the prescribed threshold require a blocked 'share consideration' escrow arrangement or prior SARB approval before shares are issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the local fire authority (e.g., Fire and Rescue Services of the relevant metropolitan municipality) confirming compliance with the National Building Regulations and SANS 10400; Electrical Certificate of Compliance (CoC) for the leased office premises; Certificate of Occupancy or structural load clearance for the registered office; compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, including a risk assessment, emergency evacuation plan, first-aid kit, and public indemnity insurance covering premises access.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za, administered by SARS Head Office, Lehae La SARS, 299 Bronkhorst Street, Brooklyn, Pretoria

E-government filing portal

A South African (Pty) Ltd is registered with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and receives a company registration number. SARS eFiling is then used to obtain the income tax reference number. VAT vendor registration is applied for via SARS form VAT 101. Compulsory VAT registration applies once taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration may be granted where taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in the preceding 12 months, subject to SARS conditions. Foreign-owned local (Pty) Ltd entities follow the standard resident company registration route. Foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African residents may be required to use the simplified SARS section 54 VAT registration route and to appoint a local representative taxpayer where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a separate taxpayer on its worldwide income. There is no pass-through treatment. Dividends tax is withheld at shareholder level; capital gains tax is imposed on the company's taxable capital gains; the branch income of a non-resident foreign company carrying on business in South Africa is taxed on a representative basis, but the domestic (Pty) Ltd is the planned entity. — A South African (Pty) Ltd is registered with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and receives a company registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a separate taxpayer on its worldwide income. There is no pass-through treatment. Dividends tax is withheld at shareholder level; capital gains tax is imposed on the company's taxable capital gains; the branch income of a non-resident foreign company carrying on business in South Africa is taxed on a representative basis, but the domestic (Pty) Ltd is the planned entity.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Tax classification is determined by the Companies Act and Income Tax Act. Relevant elections include VAT group vendor registration under section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act; voluntary VAT vendor registration; and specific Income Tax Act elections such as the section 11D research and development allowance election. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory statutory rules designed to deny cross-border mismatch outcomes and cannot be elected out of.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration for any vendor whose taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period. Voluntary registration is available where taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in a 12-month period. A non-resident supplier of electronic services to South African residents has a VAT nexus and must register once the total value of such electronic services exceeds R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies to goods physically removed from South Africa where export is completed within the statutory period and SARS receives the required export declaration. For offshore software, cloud and SaaS services supplied to a non-resident customer, zero-rating is available under section 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act where the services are supplied to a non-resident, the services are consumed outside South Africa, payment is received in foreign currency, and the services are not directly connected to South African immovable property, movable property in South Africa, or enjoyed by a South African resident. The supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence of the customer's non-resident status, including registered address, foreign VAT IDs, IP geolocation, and foreign banking or billing details.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) or electronic customs declaration evidencing physical export from South Africa, where goods are involved
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or transport document evidencing physical removal of goods from South Africa
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export supply contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange bank statement demonstrating receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • For pure SaaS offshore exports: service agreement, invoice, payment records, and evidence of customer non-resident status in lieu of a customs declaration

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control for invoices. Vendors must issue valid full or abridged VAT tax invoices within 21 days under section 20 of the VAT Act and must retain electronic records under the Tax Administration Act. SARS has been progressively piloting e-invoicing, but mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance is not yet in force.

SARS may disallow zero-rating where the export or non-resident supply documentation is incomplete or fraudulent, and may retrospectively assess the uncollected output VAT at 15% plus interest. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties may be imposed at rates determined by the conduct table, with the highest penalties reserved for intentional tax evasion and fraud. Criminal prosecution, additional penalty tax, and personal liability of directors or representative taxpayers may follow in serious cases. Statutory prescription periods may also be extended or removed where a false statement or fraud has been committed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand is fully convertible on the current account without prior SARB approval; capital account movements are subject to administrative documentary controls rather than quantitative ceilings. Non-residents may subscribe for Pty Ltd shares without exchange-control approval, but all inward subscriptions must be reported through an Authorised Dealer under South Africa's Balance-of-Payments (BOP) reporting framework. Current-account repatriations (dividends, royalties, software licence fees) are freely permitted once the SARB requirements on source of funds, tax compliance (SARS Tax Compliance Status) and external loan/non-resident share register declarations are met. Practical hurdles are documentary: BOP classification, inward/outward investment declarations, non-resident share registers, and the strict substance review of prohibited 'loop-structure' arrangements that attempt to externalise ZAR-denominated value.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exchange Control Regulation 7(4), read with the FinSurv Currency and Exchanges Manual: proceeds from the export of goods or services, including SaaS subscription fees and PropTech platform licence services rendered by the Pty Ltd to non-resident counterparties, must be received into South Africa within 180 calendar days of the date of export/performance. Since the August 2016 liberalisation, the mandatory conversion ratio is 0% — an exporter may retain 100% of export receipts in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with a licensed Authorised Dealer, or convert any portion into ZAR at the ruling market rate; no cash surrender quota applies. The operative obligation is repatriation into the SA banking system by deposit into an FCA, not physical conversion to ZAR. Every receipt must be matched to the underlying export invoice in the bank's BOP exchange-control reporting and may be re-deployed only for approved expenditures or conversion.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv); day-to-day exchange control administration is delegated to commercial banks licensed as Authorised Dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (as amended) and the Currency and Exchanges Manual (CEM).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail and legally impermissible as the sole receipts account. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Mercury, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not Authorised Dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations and cannot satisfy the FCA repatriation requirement; retaining SaaS export receipts in an unlicensed offshore EMI account beyond the 180-day repatriation window is an exchange-control breach absent a SARB departure approval or external-account authorisation. EMIs may function only as a voluntary collection gateway when swept daily (T+0/T+1) into a licensed SA Authorised-Dealer FCA, with each sweep BOP-tagged and the foreign account formally declared. Any long-term treasury balance held on an EMI ledger is a high-risk compliance event; the documented FinSurv framework requires the Pty Ltd's transactional liquidity to reside within the South African banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder remits via SWIFT MT103 into the Pty Ltd's ZAR current account or dedicated FCA at an Authorised Dealer; payment instructions must state the BOP purpose code for direct investment — equity subscription — and reference the board resolution approving the share allotment under section 40 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008. The Authorised Dealer records the inward investment through the South African BOP reporting system (inward investment declaration) and the company updates its non-resident share register. Compliance protocol: (1) deliver to the bank the share subscription agreement, Memorandum of Incorporation and director resolution for issuance before receipt of funds; (2) at receipt secure the dealer's FX transaction slip with BOP classification and verification that the remitter is the named non-resident shareholder; (3) apply funds promptly to issued share capital. Any shareholder loan injection must instead be registered under the external-loan rules with the loan approval letter raised before disbursement; subsequent divestment remittances require proof that equity was originally imported through Authorised-Dealer rails.SWIFT MT103 remains the primary clearing rail for all cross-border trade disbursements of a PropTech SaaS treasury: subscription licence fees to cloud providers, commissions paid to non-resident sales affiliates, software royalties, intercompany service fees and dividend distributions. Dividend and interest remittances require, in addition to the SWIFT instruction: SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) verified by the bank, audited annual financial statements, a solvency and liquidity board resolution under section 4 of the Companies Act, dividends tax withheld at 20% (treaty-reduced where a DTA applies) and non-resident registration evidence in the share register. For physical hardware imports used in the property-management arm (IoT access devices, CCTV, smart-lock readers), Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) and documentary collections (SWIFT MT400) are available through the Authorised Dealer, with the import declaration and 180-day settlement currency rules applied. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% prior years)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax (Dividends Tax Act 31 of 2011, s.64E) on any dividend paid by a South African resident (Pty) Ltd to a non-resident beneficial owner of shares; the company is the withholding agent and must remit the tax to SARS within 30 days of the dividend payment date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: SARS TP record-keeping rules under s.29 TAA (effective for years of assessment commencing on/after 1 April 2023): Local File required where the aggregate value of covered connected-person transactions exceeds ZAR 5 million in the year; Master File required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion; Country-by-Country Report due where the group exceeds the same ZAR 10 billion threshold. Penalties for non-compliance: administrative penalty and understatement penalty up to 150%.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute; substance is enforced through: (i) s.103(1) Income Tax Act general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) – arrangements lacking commercial substance and aimed at tax avoidance are voided; (ii) s.31 transfer-pricing secondary adjustments (deemed dividend or loan if the primary adjustment is not repatriated); (iii) s.9D controlled-foreign-company rules requiring a 'foreign business establishment' (real premises, employees, local management) before foreign passive income escapes attribution; (iv) the OECD Multilateral Instrument – Principal Purpose Test (PPT) denying treaty benefits where obtaining the treaty benefit is a principal purpose; and (v) the common-law substance-over-form and sham doctrines. A (Pty) Ltd operating a PropTech platform must maintain real board decisions, local employees, SA cloud/data operations, and genuine R&D in South Africa; offshore management fees or IP royalty stripping without local substance will be re-characterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% of the shortfall (s.223 TAA) plus interest at repo rate + 7%; criminal conviction under s.234 TAA: fine up to ZAR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; SARS may also make additional assessments for 3 to 6 years (s.99 TAA; 6 years where fraudulent).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate attract criminal liability as co-perpetrators; representative-taxpayer liability under s.159/160 TAA for unpaid corporate tax where the default is attributable to the director's fraud, negligence or default; civil claims by SARS for the shortfall; travel bans via court interdict in pending prosecutions; disqualification as directors under the Companies Act 71 of 2008.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment increasing taxable income under s.31; secondary adjustment deeming the excess a dividend or loan to the connected person (triggering 20% dividends tax); understatement penalty of 50%-150% for 'substantial understatement' or 'intentional tax evasion'; interest; possible CFC-attribution adjustments under s.9D.

Director personal liability: Directors approving sham transfer-pricing structures may be assessed as 'relevant persons' participating in an impermissible avoidance arrangement (s.103); personal liability for the company's tax debt under s.160 TAA; restitution claims by shareholders/creditors under s.77 Companies Act; criminal referral for tax evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Insufficient Economic Substance (GAAR and Treaty Abuse)

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the shell arrangement under s.103(1) GAAR and tax the substance-owner directly; denial of treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test; deemed dividends on secondary adjustments; 75% understatement penalty for impermissible avoidance; re-characterization of payments as taxable distributions; criminal prosecution for assisting in tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face personal exposure for restitution of corporate assets diverted abroad; courts may lift the corporate veil where the company is a mere façade (common-law veil piercing); travel restrictions and contempt proceedings for failing to produce records; imprisonment up to 5 years for aiding a tax-evasion scheme.
Failure to Withhold Remittance Taxes (Dividends, Royalties, Interest)

Statutory sanction: The company as withholding agent is personally liable for the un-withheld tax (s.38 TAA); additional understatement penalty of up to 150% of the un-withheld amount; interest at repo rate + 7%; criminal fine up to ZAR 1,000,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years for failure to withhold (s.236).

Director personal liability: Directors are joint and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under s.160 TAA, collectible by SARS by civil execution against personal assets; SARS can prevent foreign travel and financial transfers where amounts are in dispute; criminal conviction of responsible officers on the basis of personal participation or negligent oversight.
AML / FICA Non-Compliance (Payment Intermediation)

Statutory sanction: FICA administrative penalties up to ZAR 10 million per non-compliance plus daily penalties; the Conduct of Financial Institutions ('COFI') and Damages: supervisory referrals; funds may be frozen by the Financial Intelligence Centre; SARS will treat unexplained deposits as gross income with a 150% understatement penalty; criminal referral for money laundering (POCA) carries fines and imprisonment up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved in laundering proceeds or circumventing FICA duties face predicate-offence liability, conviction-based confiscation of assets, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 15 years; civil liability to the Financial Intelligence Centre for penalty amounts where the non-compliance resulted from willful blindness; disqualification from serving as directors.
