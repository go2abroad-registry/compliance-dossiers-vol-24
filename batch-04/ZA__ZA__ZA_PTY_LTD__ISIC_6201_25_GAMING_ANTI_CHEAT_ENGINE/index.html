Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – national tax portal and filing system: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated company carrying on a South African enterprise must register with SARS for income tax and VAT. Step 1: Register as a non-resident company via SARS eFiling using the IT77 (Non-Resident Company Registration) application together with certified incorporation documents (Memorandum of Incorporation, Certificate of Incorporation, proof of foreign registration) and a local business address. Step 2: SARS issues an Income Tax Reference Number. Step 3: Register for VAT under Section 23 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991 using form VAT101 (or VAT101e for non-resident suppliers of electronic services) once taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed the compulsory threshold; voluntary VAT registration is available from the R50,000 threshold. A foreign external company may also register through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain a SARS Customs/PRN code for import-export purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (A Pty Ltd is a resident company subject to the standard 27% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income; a non-resident company is taxed on South Africa-sourced income only. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (A Pty Ltd is a resident company subject to the standard 27% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income; a non-resident company is taxed on South Africa-sourced income only. No territorial pass-through applies.)

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows the legal incorporation form and the place of central management-and-control. Electable regimes available include: VAT group registration under Section 51 of the VAT Act for connected entities; the participation exemption for qualifying foreign dividends and foreign branch income under Section 9D and Section 9E of the Income Tax Act; the accounting-period election for provisional taxpayers; and mandatory hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 9DA to 9E of the Income Tax Act, with reportable arrangement disclosure for cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (ZAR 1 million) compulsory registration threshold applied over the preceding 12 months or expected next 12 months; voluntary registration from R50,000 (ZAR); zero threshold for non-resident suppliers of electronic, digital and gaming-security services under the SARS electronic-services VAT registration regime

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, exported goods are zero-rated where the exporter holds a customs Bill of Entry (SAD500) evidencing export before or at dispersal and provides proof of shipment abroad within the prescribed period. Under Section 11(2)(l), exported services (including gaming anti-cheat software licences, kernel-level middleware subscriptions and cloud-based integrity services) are zero-rated where the supply is to a non-resident for use wholly outside South Africa, the services are not supplied to a resident, and the supply is not physically performed in South Africa; connected-person exclusions in Section 11(2)(l)(iii) require market-value consideration. Exporters may also claim zero-rating for direct exports of the embedded anti-cheat SDK to foreign gaming studios with simultaneous ownership transfer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 Bill of Entry) finally cleared by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / transport evidence proving physical movement to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a South African authorised-dealer bank account
  • Commercial invoice, software licence or SaaS agreement proving the non-resident customer and the transaction's commercial substance
  • Digital-evidence package for middleware exports (SDK delivery logs, activation keys, and end-user destination metadata) supporting the Section 11 export clawback review

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, central digital stamping or compulsory API stampede currently exists. SARS recognises paper, PDF, EDI and XML invoices and has introduced a voluntary e-invoicing framework aligned to OECD and CIAT standards, transitioning toward the SARS E-Invoicing Pilot (2024-2025). Large enterprises may use the SARS eFiling API to submit VAT201 returns and data extracts; record retention of 5 years applies under Section 18 of the VAT Act, and invoices must contain the mandatory VAT fields (vendor name, VAT number, invoice number, supply date, tax invoice wording, consideration, VAT amount).

Under Section 59 and Section 60(1A)(a) of the VAT Act, incorrect zero-rating attracts an additional 'non-declaration' penalty of up to 195% of the assessed VAT together with interest at 15% per annum under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Criminal prosecution for deliberate export fraud may impose fines of up to R10 million and imprisonment of up to 10 years. SARS has a retrospective 5-year audit window and may claw back input tax credits and zero-rated output treatment where the exporter fails to produce the mandatory SAD500, shipping and foreign-currency receipt evidence. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers suspicion under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 and may invalidate the zero-rating claim or trigger additional assessments with understatement penalties of 35% to 200% for 'substantial understatement' or 'gross negligence' findings.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for bona fide current-account transactions: trade in goods, services, software licensing, royalties, interest, and dividends remitted from after-tax profits require no prior SARB approval provided the resident effects the payment or receipt through an authorised dealer. Inward foreign direct investment into an unlisted private company is liberalised under the active-monitoring framework: the non-resident shareholder may subscribe for shares without prior FinSurv approval, but the inward capital transfer must be reported electronically by the authorised dealer and the shares issued only after the funds land in a South African account. Capital-account outflows remain the principal hurdle: loans to non-residents, offshore accounts, and investments by the South African resident outside South Africa are subject to formal approval, documentary substantiation, and the prohibition on loop structures (South African resident participation held abroad). Profit remittance hurdles are limited to verifiable form: dividends may be freely remitted once the dividend withholding tax (20%, or treaty-reduced rate) is accounted for and the company holds a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status Pin, a board resolution, audited annual financial statements, and proof of solvency per section 46 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Branch or royalty remittances require associated SARS filings and the applicable withholding tax on royalties (15% or treaty-reduced). There is no general blocked-rand regime for current returns on non-resident investment; blocked-rand mechanisms arise only in legacy emigration contexts and are irrelevant to a standard gaming-software export company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory baseline under SARB CEMAD and the Exchange Control Regulations, as amended by Amendment No. 30/2021 effective 1 January 2022: export proceeds in respect of goods must be received in South Africa within 180 days of shipment, and service or software-licensing proceeds within 180 days of invoicing. The authorised dealer may, on application, extend the 180-day window to a maximum of 365 days where the underlying commercial contract justifies extended settlement and the exporter is fully tax-compliant. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% — the 2021 liberalisation abolished any obligation to convert export proceeds into rand; the company may retain 100% of its export receipts in a designated foreign currency account (FCA) held with a South African authorised dealer. Repatriation to the South African FCA remains compulsory, and set-offs of foreign receivables against foreign payables require prior dealer approval. Receipts from the anti-cheat-software licensing business are classified as export of services and intellectual property and must be documented with signed licensing agreements, commercial invoices, delivery/acceptance evidence, and a valid SARS tax-compliance standing at the moment of receipt.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), the statutorily designated exchange-control administrator under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, read with the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA). All foreign-exchange transactions of a South African resident company must be routed through an authorised dealer, being a registered South African commercial bank that SARB has licensed to operate the national payment and foreign-exchange clearing infrastructure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary account-holding rail. A South African resident private company is legally prohibited from opening or maintaining a foreign bank or electronic-money-institution (EMI) account without prior FinSurv approval, and the Exchange Control Regulations mandate that all foreign-currency receipts and payments be cleared through a South African authorised dealer. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not authorised dealers and cannot lawfully serve as the repository of export proceeds or as the exclusive paying agent for outbound current payments. Permissible ancillary role: an offshore EMI may act as a supplementary collection node for card-acquiring flows or marketplaces only if the funds are swept into the company's authorised-dealer FCA within the applicable 180-day (or dealer-approved 365-day) repatriation deadline; the arrangement must be pre-disclosed to the bank's transactional-compliance group and formalised in the treasury policy. The target payment rail 'Stripe' is acceptable at the collection layer because Stripe Payments South Africa (Pty) Ltd operates as a local regulated payment-service provider settling ZAR into the company's resident bank account; Stripe itself is not a foreign-currency clearing path.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the non-resident equity injection into ZA_PTY_LTD: (1) The non-resident investor instructs a SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA equivalent) to the company's authorised-dealer bank, quoting the South African SEF/BOP purpose code for inward direct investment in equity (category 'I' direct investment, equity/DI) and referencing the executed share subscription agreement. (2) The authorised dealer credits the funds to a non-resident inward-capital suspense escrow; where remitted in foreign currency, the funds are held in a non-resident Foreign Currency Account until conversion; where remitted in rand, they are held in the escrow until the share allotment is documented. (3) On presentation of the board allotment resolution, the updated register of members, the amended MOI (if any), and proof of lodgement with CIPC, the dealer converts at the prevailing market rate and releases the net proceeds to the operating ZAR account. (4) The authorised dealer files the inward FDI declaration with FinSurv electronically through the Currency and Exchanges (COFE) platform; no prior SARB approval is required for unlisted private-company equity from a non-resident, provided no loop structure exists and no inward funds derive from any South African-resident source held abroad. (5) Verification bundle: board resolutions, amended incorporation documents, share certificates, complete beneficial-ownership filing, source-of-funds documentation, and the dealer's conversion ticket. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary account-holding rail. A South African resident private company is legally prohibited from opening or maintaining a foreign bank or electronic-money-institution (EMI) account without prior FinSurv approval, and the Exchange Control Regulations mandate that all foreign-currency receipts and payments be cleared through a South African authorised dealer. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not authorised dealers and cannot lawfully serve as the repository of export proceeds or as the exclusive paying agent for outbound current payments. Permissible ancillary role: an offshore EMI may act as a supplementary collection node for card-acquiring flows or marketplaces only if the funds are swept into the company's authorised-dealer FCA within the applicable 180-day (or dealer-approved 365-day) repatriation deadline; the arrangement must be pre-disclosed to the bank's transactional-compliance group and formalised in the treasury policy. The target payment rail 'Stripe' is acceptable at the collection layer because Stripe Payments South Africa (Pty) Ltd operates as a local regulated payment-service provider settling ZAR into the company's resident bank account; Stripe itself is not a foreign-currency clearing path.
