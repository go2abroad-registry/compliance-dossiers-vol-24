Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); portal: SARS eFiling at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za (official site: https://www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A South African incorporated (Pty) Ltd is automatically allocated an Income Tax Reference Number at CIPC incorporation; activation is completed through SARS eFiling 'Register for Tax' using the RAV01 form, confirming the public officer (mandatory under s246 Tax Administration Act). VAT registration is made on form VAT101: compulsory once taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in any 12-month rolling period, or voluntary if taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in any 12-month period (VAT Act s23). Foreign-owned subsidiaries must submit the acceptance-of-appointment letter for the public officer, certified memorandum of incorporation, and local physical address proof; a SARS excise/customs code is separately obtained if the entity exports goods. SARS issues a VAT vendor number, PAYE number and a single reference number; confirmation is usually issued within 21 business days after submission of the RAV01/VAT101 through eFiling.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at 27% corporate income tax (as applicable to years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); no pass-through regime available to companies; dividend distributions attract 20% dividends tax with withholding by the entity; branch profits remitted by a foreign-owned South African branch are subject to an additional 15% branch profits tax; capital gains are included at 80% (21.6% effective rate for companies). — A South African incorporated (Pty) Ltd is automatically allocated an Income Tax Reference Number at CIPC incorporation; activation is completed through SARS eFiling 'Register for Tax' using the RAV01 form, confirming the public officer (mandatory under s246 Tax Administration Act). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at 27% corporate income tax (as applicable to years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); no pass-through regime available to companies; dividend distributions attract 20% dividends tax with withholding by the entity; branch profits remitted by a foreign-owned South African branch are subject to an additional 15% branch profits tax; capital gains are included at 80% (21.6% effective rate for companies).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Applicable elections for a South African (Pty) Ltd: (a) VAT grouping election (s50 VAT Act) allowing related entities to be treated as a single vendor; (b) two-method input apportionment election for mixed taxable and exempt supplies (s17(1) VAT Act); (c) election under s9D(9)/s9D(2A) for the high-tax exemption or low-tax exclusion of Controlled Foreign Company income; (d) s24I election to treat exchange differences on foreign currency obligations on a translation basis; (e) s11D R&D tax incentive pre-approval election with the Department of Science and Innovation, and (f) s12B renewable energy allowance election. Hybrid mismatch adjustments (s23M and s8E/s8EA) are mandatory, not elective, and require full disclosure in the corporate income tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (compulsory once value of taxable supplies exceeds R1,000,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration if taxable supplies exceed R50,000 per annum; a foreign e-services supplier under s47A isn't relevant to this locally incorporated (Pty) Ltd whose supply of ERP/CRM cloud services to non-residents is zero-rated or standard-rated resident cloud services at 15%).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (1) exported goods under s11(1)(a) where the goods are removed from South Africa within the prescribed period and documentary proof of export is retained; and (2) services supplied to non-residents under s11(2)(k) — including hosted ERP/CRM, software licensing and technical support — where the recipient is a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time the services are rendered, the services are not directly connected to land or movable property situated in South Africa, and the services are not supplied to a resident acting as intermediary. The vendor must retain contracts, bank records and support logs demonstrating offshore consumption; simple invoicing to a foreign address without proof of payment from abroad or service performance outside South Africa is insufficient to support 0% treatment.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500/DA550) electronically submitted and released through the SARS Customs Single Administrative System
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill or Cargo Carrier Waybill evidencing physical departure from the South African port of exit
  • Commercial invoice/tax invoice in the name of the non-resident buyer indicating 'Zero-rated export' and VAT registration number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (COT3) or bank confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency/offshore settlement within 180 days
  • Service supply contract, IP licensing chronology and technical performance log demonstrating supply to a non-resident outside South Africa for s11(2)(k) treatment

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance/CTC or digital stamping is currently imposed by SARS. Electronic tax invoices are lawful under s20(4) VAT Act if the invoice contains all 15 prescribed particulars (including identifier 'tax invoice', supplier VAT number, dated transaction, description, price, VAT amount) and ensures authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility. SARS has announced a voluntary National e-Invoicing Standard aligned with the ITU/UN standard; pilot filings and an API-based clearance framework are being developed, but until a SARS Notice is gazetted, no statutory obligation exists for API pre-clearance, e-stamping or real-time validation of B2B invoices.

SARS reconciles zero-rated VAT claims against Customs export declarations, banking data and eFiling VAT201 disclosures. Insufficient documentation causes the zero-rating to be rejected with output VAT at 15% assessed plus interest (TAA ss89 and 90), and an understatement penalty under TAA s222-223 of up to 150% of the shortfall for intentional tax evasion (10% substantial understatement; 25% no reasonable grounds; 50% gross negligence; 150% intentional evasion). Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract additional tax of up to 100% of the tax amount under VAT Act s44, possible criminal prosecution (TAA s234, imprisonment up to 5 years) and disclosure in SARS public defaulters lists. Overstated refunds also trigger a 10% penalty under VAT Act s39(2) and a VAT refund fraud flag that restricts future eFiling refunds for up to 3 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is a managed-float currency with a substantially liberalised current account: cross-border payments for software subscriptions, hosting services, royalties, and trade settlements may be executed through an Authorised Dealer without prior FinSurv approval once supporting invoices and balance-of-payments (BOP) declarations are provided. The capital account remains exchange-control-regulated: non-resident equity capital may be injected freely and must be reported to the Authorised Dealer, while non-resident shareholder loans above the ZAR 3 million aggregate threshold require prior FinSurv approval, and outward capital remittances (dividends, capital reductions, share buy-backs, loan repayments to non-residents) require SARS Tax Compliance Status, CIPC beneficial-ownership proof, and Authorised Dealer adjudication. For the software exporter, profit remittance hurdles are low: a (Pty) Ltd with non-resident shareholders may declare dividends and remit them through an Authorised Dealer subject to a valid SARS tax compliance certificate and an auditable distributable reserve; the practical hurdles are documentary (FICA customer due diligence, source-of-funds verification, and underlying contract evidence) rather than quantitative rationing, and no discriminatory conversion restriction is applied to current-account transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Rulings, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.
