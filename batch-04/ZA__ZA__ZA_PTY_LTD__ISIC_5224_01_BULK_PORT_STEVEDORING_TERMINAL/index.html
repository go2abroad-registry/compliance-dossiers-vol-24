Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; official website: https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

After incorporation of the (Pty) Ltd through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), the entity must register for income tax with SARS via eFiling or a SARS branch, obtaining a South African Income Tax Reference Number. VAT registration is compulsory if taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period. Voluntary VAT registration may be made where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period. Foreign-owned entities must appoint a local representative taxpayer for SARS purposes and may be required to furnish security for VAT registration. A non-resident entity must register as a VAT vendor where it carries on an enterprise in South Africa and has a South African bank account or other prescribed security arrangements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a US-style check-the-box regime. A (Pty) Ltd is automatically a separate corporate taxpayer. Available elections relevant to a company include: voluntary VAT registration and VAT group registration under section 50 of the VAT Act; small business corporation (turnover-based) income tax elective regime under section 12E of the Income Tax Act (subject to turnover limits and activity exclusions); controlled foreign company (CFC) allocation or exclusion elections under section 9D of the Income Tax Act for foreign subsidiaries. No hybrid entity pass-through election is available for a South African private company under South African tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration may apply once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a rolling 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated for VAT if supplied to a non-resident and physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of supply (or such extended period as SARS permits), provided no subsequent re-import occurs. Services may be zero-rated under section 11(2) of the VAT Act where they are supplied directly to a non-resident and are not directly connected with immovable property in South Africa or movable property situated in South Africa at the time of supply. Stevedoring services supplied directly to a non-resident vessel operator or non-resident cargo owner for the loading of bulk cargo destined for export may qualify for zero-rating where the cargo is cleared for export by SARS Customs and the documentary evidence chain is complete. Services rendered to local freight forwarders, consignors or South African resident principals will generally be standard-rated at 15%.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 or electronic equivalent via SARS Customs e-filing)
  • Bill of Lading (original or certified electronic copy)
  • Commercial invoice for the exported cargo
  • Packing list or cargo tally sheet
  • Stevedoring/terminal outturn report or vessel load confirmation
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank credit advice evidencing foreign payment receipt
  • Berth order and port terminal service order tying the stevedoring service to the exported goods

South Africa does not currently mandate a real-time continuous fiscal clearance, digital stamping or government API clearance regime for all VAT invoices. Tax invoices must satisfy the prescribed particulars in section 20 of the VAT Act. Electronic tax invoices are permitted subject to integrity, authenticity and duplication controls. VAT returns and supporting documentation are submitted through SARS eFiling and e@syFile channels; the SARS Customs environment operates separately through the SAD500 electronic customs declaration system.

SARS may disallow the zero-rating and retrospectively assess standard-rate VAT if the export documentation is deficient or the goods do not leave South Africa within the prescribed period. The clawback also carries interest from the due date of the original return. Under the Tax Administration Act, understatement penalties may apply on a sliding scale from 0% to 200% of the understated tax, depending on conduct (reasonable care, substantial non-compliance, gross negligence, or intentional evasion). Administrative non-compliance penalties may also be imposed for failure to keep satisfactory records. Deliberate fraudulent export VAT evasion may result in criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment in terms of the VAT Act and the Tax Administration Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is free-floating and fully convertible for current-account transactions; authorised dealer banks hold delegated authority to process service-related external payments (stevedoring fees, charter-hire, agency commissions) upon documentary review without individual FinSurv consent. Capital-account convertibility is liberalised but subject to reporting and source-of-funds verification: inward foreign direct investment (equity subscription or shareholder loan) is permitted and is registered on an inward investment file at the receiving authorised dealer; outward repatriation (dividends, loan redemptions, disinvestment proceeds) is permitted after (i) submission of an auditor's certificate in the prescribed 'External Income Remittance' template evidencing realised profits, (ii) satisfaction of South African dividend withholding tax at 20% (treaty relief may apply under an applicable DTA), and (iii) a SARS tax-compliance status PIN. There is no profit-remittance licence or annual ceiling; the operative constraint is documentation completeness rather than statutory quotas. The material practical hurdles are: (a) proving the provenance of the non-resident sponsor's funds (12-month bank statements plus corporate transcript), (b) reconciling the inward investment file before any later disinvestment, and (c) complying with the 180-day settlement rule on export services (terminal stevedoring invoices to non-resident shipowners).

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the ISIC 5224 stevedoring model, port-handling fees invoiced to non-resident vessel owners are treated as exports of services. Statutory timeline: the full invoice value must be received by the (Pty) Ltd within 180 days from the date of the service invoice (the authorised dealer may extend to 365 days upon documented justification, such as bulk-commodity discharge cycles). Mandatory conversion ratio: nil - the 2021 exchange-control modernisation abolished the historical requirement to surrender foreign currency to the authorised dealer within 30 days of receipt. The receiving dealer must credit the funds to the company's Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or ZAR account; the funds may be retained, converted to ZAR at market rate, or applied to authorised payments without any percentage-selling obligation. The exporter must file the SARS/FinSurv service-export electronic declaration (the services equivalent of the goods EX1), attach the vessel performance/throughput certificate issued by the terminal or port authority, and close each collection to the underlying export file; any overdue receivable must be justified or the dealer may report an 'unpaid export' irregularity to FinSurv, attracting administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administering the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (Government Notice R1111, as amended, effective from 1 January 2021 under the exchange-control modernisation regime) together with the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a standalone clearing solution; MODERATE as an auxiliary collection/FX layer partnered with a licensed domestic bank. Legal constraints: (i) an offshore e-money institution (EMI) is not an 'authorised dealer' under the Exchange Control Regulations and holds no ZAR clearing licence, so it cannot settle rand-denominated payments or provide the statutory inward/outward investment file; (ii) the Banks Act 94 of 1990 (section 11) prohibits any unlicensed entity from conducting 'the business of a bank' in South Africa - EMI wallet receipts must therefore be swept via SWIFT MT103 into the domestic FCA; (iii) for foreign shipping SPVs, an EMI multi-currency account can accelerate invoice collections, but source-of-funds traceability and BOP purpose-code reporting are still required at the point the funds cross into the ZA banking system; (iv) recommendation: use the EMI only for pre-clearing aggregation of US$/EUR receipts where the underlying service contract names ZA Pty Ltd as beneficiary, then clear daily into the tier-1 FCA; never use an EMI as the principal disbursement ledger for payroll or port dues.
