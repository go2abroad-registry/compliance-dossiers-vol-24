E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd already incorporated under the Companies Act: (1) register for income tax with SARS via eFiling by submitting Form IT77 together with the Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI), CIPC registration certificate, directors' IDs and proof of physical address; SARS issues an income tax reference number (TIN). (3) Obtain a customs code from SARS Customs if importing/exporting. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election; legal form determines classification. A (Pty) Ltd is automatically a resident company taxed at entity level. No hybrid mismatch elections are available; the anti-hybrid rules in the Income Tax Act apply coercively from 1 January 2021. Elective mechanisms relevant to indirect tax include the VAT grouping election under Section 47 of the VAT Act, which permits two or more corporate group members in South Africa to register as a single vendor.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, goods supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated if they are exported from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply, the exporter retains documentary proof of physical export, and the supply is made to the non-resident. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under Section 11(2)(l) if the services are not supplied to a resident recipient and are physically rendered outside South Africa or not connected with South African property; a B2B cross-border platform service supplied to foreign business customers will generally qualify when the foreign customer is the sole recipient and the service is not consumed in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged with SARS Customs
  • Commercial invoice and tax invoice issued to the non-resident purchaser
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing physical removal from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorised foreign-exchange dealer

No real-time national fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping is currently in force. Electronic tax invoices are permitted if they meet the statutory particulars in Section 20 of the VAT Act. SARS is progressively implementing an e-invoicing framework and has the power to require prescribed data formats submitted through eFiling/API; exporters must retain all shipping and customs records for inspection.

SARS may disallow zero-rating and assess additional output VAT at 15% if the documentary proof is deficient. A 10% understatement penalty, 10% late-payment penalty and statutory interest apply under the Tax Administration Act; intentional evasion can trigger a penalty of up to 200% of the tax, retrospective clawback of refunds and input tax credits, and criminal prosecution for fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a managed convertibility regime under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, administered by SARB FinSurv through authorised dealers. Current account transactions (trade in goods and services, interest, royalties, and dividends declared out of distributable reserves) are generally convertible against documentary proof without FinSurv pre-approval where within authorised dealer delegated limits. Capital account flows (inward foreign equity, inward/outward loans, direct investment) are permitted but strictly controlled: foreign equity capital requires registration on the company's non-resident share register and a capital import record with the authorised dealer; inward loans above the prevailing authorised dealer threshold (currently ZAR 50 million) require FinSurv approval. Profit remittance hurdles include: (i) dividend payments require audited or signed financial statements confirming distributable reserves, a SARS corporate income tax clearance, and a dividend tax withholding directive (20% statutory rate, treaty relief available on application to SARS); (ii) all outward remittances require correct BOP purpose codes and supporting commercial documentation; (iii) funds may not be remitted where the exporter has failed to repatriate export proceeds; (iv) remittances of royalties and service fees require a SARS tax directive covering the 15% royalty/interest withholding tax. Overall, the rand is convertible on current account with declared purpose, but capital account mobility is constrained by reporting, authorisation thresholds, and tax clearance gatekeeping.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 6(1), all proceeds from the export of goods and services must be paid into a South African authorised dealer account within 180 days from date of shipment (goods) or date of performance (services). The obligation is full repatriation: 100% of export proceeds must be surrendered to an authorised dealer, meaning they must be credited to the exporter's South African banking account; the proceeds are automatically converted to ZAR unless the exporter maintains an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with the authorised dealer, in which case the foreign currency may be retained for bona fide trade purposes. There is no statutory ratio mandating conversion of a percentage of proceeds into ZAR; the mandatory component is the complete inward repatriation into the South African banking system and the associated exchange control declaration and reporting by the authorised dealer to SARB FinSurv. For longer credit terms on capital goods, exporters may apply for extension or pre-approval; any unauthorised retention offshore or payment to an offshore non-authorised dealer account constitutes a contravention of the regulations and will trigger AML/exchange control penalties and loss of banking facilities.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) — Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Legal constraints: an offshore electronic money institution (EMI) such as Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, or Airwallex is not an authorised dealer in foreign exchange under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933. A South African resident company may not lawfully collect or hold export proceeds through an offshore EMI because all export proceeds must be repatriated to a South African authorised dealer within 180 days. Retaining funds offshore in an EMI would constitute an unauthorised foreign currency account and violate Exchange Control Regulation 6(1)(a), exposing the entity to penalties, forced surrender, and criminal liability. Offshore EMI accounts are available only with explicit FinSurv dispensation for defined operational needs. Consequently, the B2B marketplace target rails (Alibaba_Pay and similar PSPs) must be structured as pass-through collection mechanisms into the South African authorised dealer account, or replaced by SWIFT wire collection directly into the SA bank account; any PSP aggregation model must be disclosed to the bank and approved as compliant under FICA and exchange control rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign equity capital must be remitted by the non-resident shareholder via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for large value) to the entity's account at a South African authorised dealer, using the approved BOP purpose code for direct investment equity capital (SA BOP code 100 — direct investment into the reporting economy). The authorised dealer must receive a Compliance File containing the Share Subscription Agreement, board resolution approving the allotment, updated register of members reflecting the non-resident shareholding, FAIS/AML declarations, and the completed Inward Foreign Investment questionnaire; funds are credited to the Capital Escrow Account and thereafter transferred to the operating account once the bank confirms the non-resident status on the Capital Register. For inward loans from non-resident shareholders or third-party lenders, the loan must be documented and reported; authorised dealers may approve inward loans up to the prevailing monetary limit (currently ZAR 50 million) without FinSurv approval, with amounts above that threshold requiring a formal FinSurv application via the authorised dealer. The bank will record the proceeds in the Borrower's EMV / Loan Register; repayment of principal and interest is remittable subject to a SARS tax clearance via Form A and proof of the original inflow. Verification steps: (i) bank confirms the remitter is the same party as the shareholder/lender; (ii) BOP and EMV coding applied; (iii) exchange control forms completed; (iv) funds released to operations only after full KYC/AML clearance; (v) annual Financial Reporting Forms (Form C) submitted through the authorised dealer.The B2B platform will disburse cross-border trade payments through the following cleared rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier/vendor payments, supported by commercial invoices, contracts, and SARS tax clearance; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit and Documentary Collections issued/negotiated by the authorised dealer for goods imports, requiring the pro-forma invoice, insurance/cargo documents, and customs Bill of Entry; (iii) high-volume seller payouts via MT101/ISO 20022 batch files for SME merchants, with each payment subject to sanctions screening and FICA verification of the beneficiary; and (iv) local ZAR clearing via the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS)/Real-Time Clearing infrastructure for domestic disbursements. For service fees, royalties, and payments to foreign contractors, the remitter must present a SARS directive confirming the correct withholding tax treatment (royalty/interest at 15% or applicable treaty rate) and an approved BOP purpose code; no outward payment may be made for purely speculative or capital preservation purposes. The authorised dealer will also require written confirmation that export proceeds have been repatriated in compliance with Regulation 6(1) before permitting outsized or high-frequency trade disbursements. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW. Legal constraints: an offshore electronic money institution (EMI) such as Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, or Airwallex is not an authorised dealer in foreign exchange under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933. A South African resident company may not lawfully collect or hold export proceeds through an offshore EMI because all export proceeds must be repatriated to a South African authorised dealer within 180 days. Retaining funds offshore in an EMI would constitute an unauthorised foreign currency account and violate Exchange Control Regulation 6(1)(a), exposing the entity to penalties, forced surrender, and criminal liability. Offshore EMI accounts are available only with explicit FinSurv dispensation for defined operational needs. Consequently, the B2B marketplace target rails (Alibaba_Pay and similar PSPs) must be structured as pass-through collection mechanisms into the South African authorised dealer account, or replaced by SWIFT wire collection directly into the SA bank account; any PSP aggregation model must be disclosed to the bank and approved as compliant under FICA and exchange control rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: SARS Interpretation Note 69 and the TAA require contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation where the aggregate cross-border connected-party transactions exceed ZAR 10 million per year; a Master File is expected for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above ZAR 7 billion, and a Country-by-Country Report is required for groups with consolidated revenue above ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have a separate GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations code; the equivalent substance doctrine is applied through common-law substance-over-form, s31 (transfer pricing and permanent establishment protection), s6/ CFC rules, and the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under section 103 of the Income Tax Act. A non-resident-owned South African company must demonstrate actual office functions, employees, assets, and risk,s carried on in South Africa to sustain treaty benefits and transfer-pricing positions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Non-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Section 222 TAA understatement penalty of 150% of the understated tax for intentional tax evasion; 100% for gross negligence; compounded interest on the full past-due tax; criminal conviction may render the company liable to a higher fine and imprisonment of responsible persons. Additionally, SARS can void false tax returns and issue a statutory assessment on the best available information.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties under TAA sections governing senior management liability; SARS may prohibit their departure from South Africa via exit-control procedures, seize personal assets, and institute criminal charges leading to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS will apply the GAAR under section 103 to disregard the artificial transaction, re-allocate the profits to the substantive person, and impose an understatement penalty of up to 150% of the additional assessment. If treaty relief is claimed on the basis of an artificial PE, SARS may deny the DTA benefit and impose interest as well as repayment of any refunded dividend WHT.

Director personal liability: The Companies Act imposes personal liability on directors who fail to prevent reckless trading or knowingly allow the company to operate as a vehicle for tax evasion; in tax enforcement, directors may be joined as co-defendants, placed under civil restraint, have travel rights revoked, and be disqualified from acting as directors.
AML / FICA Non-Compliance in Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, the FIC may impose administrative penalties: up to ZAR 10 million for each material non-compliance episode; criminal conviction may lead to a fine up to ZAR 50 million and/or imprisonment up to 10 years for continuous and intentional breach, including aiding money laundering through platform gateways.

Director personal liability: Directors, especially the appointed compliance officer, bear personal criminal liability if the institution cannot prove the adoption of a compliant anti-money-laundering framework; directors may additionally be disqualified from holding office and be subject to a SARS/FIC joint enforcement action, with assets frozen pending proceedings.
Aggressive Transfer Pricing and Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: SARS will adjust taxable income to an arm's-length result under section 31 and assess additional tax, interest, and a 100% understatement penalty for gross negligence; if the lack of documentation is intentional, the penalty may be 150%. SARS may also impose a separate TAA non-compliance penalty for failure to retain or produce required records.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the ITR14 without verifying transfer-pricing records are personally exposed to civil liability for causing losses to the fiscus; in egregious cases a court can pierce the corporate veil, making directors liable for the adjusted tax liability and precluding them from leaving South Africa until the debt is settled.
