Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity or a South African (Pty) Ltd carrying on enterprise activities must first register with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) where applicable, then obtain an Income Tax Reference Number through SARS eFiling within 60 days of commencing business or becoming liable for tax. VAT registration must be submitted electronically via SARS eFiling using Form VAT101 within 21 days of exceeding the compulsory R1 million taxable-supplies threshold, or immediately when voluntary registration is sought. Foreign entities without a South African permanent establishment or physical presence may only require registration for VAT on imported digital services or specific withholding obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-incorporated entity or a South African (Pty) Ltd carrying on enterprise activities must first register with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) where applicable, then obtain an Income Tax Reference Number through SARS eFiling within 60 days of commencing business or becoming liable for tax. VAT registration must be submitted electronically via SARS eFiling using Form VAT101 within 21 days of exceeding the compulsory R1 million taxable-supplies threshold, or immediately when voluntary registration is sought. Foreign entities without a South African permanent establishment or physical presence may only require registration for VAT on imported digital services or specific withholding obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists. Statutory elections available to a (Pty) Ltd include the VAT group registration election under s50 of the VAT Act, corporate restructure rollover relief under s45 and s47 of the Income Tax Act, dividend withholding tax concession elections, and hybrid mismatch disallowance under s23M. All elections are made by formal notice to SARS through eFiling and require prescribed supporting documentation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 compulsory turnover threshold in any 12-month period; voluntary registration permitted where taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in the preceding 12 months

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods applies under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the supplier exports the movable goods in the course of the supply and retains prescribed documentary proof of export. Services supplied to a non-resident are zero-rated under s11(2)(l) where the services are supplied directly in connection with land or movable property situated outside South Africa, or where the services are not supplied directly in connection with South African land or movable property and are supplied to a non-resident. For the virtual office and mail scanning service, the supplier must evidence that the recipient is a non-resident and that the services are not connected with South African situs assets to claim 0% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) containing the unique export clearance reference
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing actual export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange receipt demonstrating payment from outside South Africa
  • Commercial invoice with VAT zero-rating indicator and total consideration in foreign currency
  • Service agreement or contract identifying the non-resident recipient and the offshore nature of the service

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, central e-invoicing validation, or digital stamping. SARS requires full tax invoices with all statutory particulars and permits electronic invoicing via accredited platforms. SARS has published a draft e-invoicing strategy and is gradually implementing an e-invoicing exchange, but no mandatory real-time clearance or VAT crypto-certificate regime is in force as of the current tax period. Vendors must retain all e-invoice source documents for at least five years.

SARS may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and claw back VAT refunds if documentary proof is not contemporaneous and complete. Understatement penalties can reach up to 150% of the assessed shortfall under the Tax Administration Act, interest is imposed on underpayments, and fraudulent zero-rating claims may trigger criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines and imprisonment. Refund claims are subject to SARS verification, field audits, and enhanced due diligence before payment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is extensively convertible for current account transactions (trade and non-trade) subject to documentary verification by Authorised Dealers. Capital account transactions, including foreign equity injections and certain intercompany funding structures, require FinSurv approval or are governed by specific limits. Profit and dividend remittances to non-residents are permitted after full South African Income Tax and Dividend Withholding Tax compliance, via an Authorised Dealer, upon presentation of a valid SARS Tax Clearance Certificate (TAXCOM) and proof of the onshore source of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, export proceeds from services rendered (including CMRA digital mail scanning contracts) must be received within 180 days from date of invoice or date of shipment as applicable. No mandatory percentage of export receipt conversion to ZAR is imposed; the exporter may retain proceeds in an approved foreign currency account with an Authorised Dealer, but full repatriation into a South African bank account is required within the prescribed timeline. Late receipt or any deviation requires FinSurv consent.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), exercising exchange control authority delegated by the National Treasury.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-high feasibility for collecting foreign subscription fees via offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise and Payoneer; these can serve as acceptable front-end collection rails for low-value recurring transactions. However, EMIs are not Authorised Dealers under SARB exchange control and cannot issue ZAR-denominated accounts; all received funds must ultimately be repatriated through a licensed South African bank. Legal constraints include FIC Act reporting, EMIs must hold a valid license in their home jurisdiction, and any EMI account balances must be declared to SA banking compliance to avoid circumvention of the 180-day repatriation rule.
