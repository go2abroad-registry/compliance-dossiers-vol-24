Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — unified national tax and customs authority; SARS eFiling portal https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; Customs & Excise declarations via SARS Customs (SAD500) on the SARS Customs online platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must first register as an external/domestic company with CIPC under the Companies Act, appoint a resident public officer within one month of commencing business (s 245 TAA), then obtain an Income Tax Reference Number via SARS eFiling (IT77 / 'Non-Resident Company Registration'), followed by VAT registration (VAT101) within 21 days of exceeding the R1 million compulsory threshold; for cross-border export services, also register as an importer/exporter with SARS Customs using DA185. Contractual non-resident clients do not require an SA VAT ID, but the supplier must link the foreign client's name, country, and VAT ID (if any) to the zero-rated tax invoice. Turnaround is typically 1–5 business days once CIPC certificate, MOI, directors' IDs, and public officer acceptance are uploaded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand (ZAR) is a floating, market-determined currency with full convertibility on current-account transactions—including trade in services, dividends, interest, royalties, and consulting fees—provided the underlying documentation is held and a valid SARS tax-clearance certificate (TCC) exists. Capital-account transactions remain controlled: inward foreign direct investment via equity subscription is freely permitted but must be reported by the Authorised Dealer through the inward-listed-investment notification (Form NIP/MP) for balance-of-payments (BOP) purposes. Outbound capital remittances, including shareholder loans and capital repatriation, require Authorised Dealer or FinSurv approval above prescribed thresholds. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is lawful where the company is not a prohibited 'loop structure' (i.e., has not obtained domestic credit facilities without approval), the dividends are declared from realised reserves, and the SARS TCC pin code is presented at the time of transfer. Treaty-based withholding rates may apply (statutory South African dividends tax is 20%, subject to reduction under an applicable double-taxation agreement).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers, all export proceeds in respect of goods must be received in South Africa within 30 days from the date of shipment, or up to 180 days where an extended realisation period has been approved. For services rendered to non-residents—including legal, accounting, and anti-dumping trade-defence consulting fees—the foreign-currency fee income must be repatriated to an Authorised Dealer within 30 days from the date of invoicing or the agreed contractual settlement date. Since the 1994–2004 exchange-control liberalisation, no mandatory surrender or fixed conversion ratio applies; the exporter may credit 100% of the realised foreign currency to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer, convert it to ZAR at the prevailing market rate, or apply it to approved foreign-currency obligations. Failure to realise proceeds within the prescribed period requires a letter of non-realisation and may attract an administrative penalty (typically 10–20% of the non-realised amount, or a discretionary FinSurv penalty).

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and Exchange Control Division, under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933). Day-to-day exchange-control administration is delegated to Authorised Dealers, being South African commercial banks designated by SARB. The National Treasury sets exchange-control policy, while the South African Revenue Service (SARS) enforces tax-clearance verification on all external fund transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium as a complementary FX and payment treasury rail, but Low as a substitute for the primary regulated banking account. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Siyabonga, Mercury, Airwallex, Payoneer) may offer ZAR-denominated virtual accounts through partnership arrangements with licensed South African banks; however, such accounts are legally payment-service-provider allocations (money transmission) rather than clearing-bank accounts and cannot satisfy South African Reserve Bank inward-FDI reporting, SARS tax-clearance verification, or FICA accountability duties. International EMIs that hold or transfer funds on behalf of South African residents may trigger FICA obligations and National Payment System Act licensing requirements unless the volume and structure are carefully ring-fenced. The (Pty) Ltd should maintain the Tier-1 commercial bank account as the regulated anchor, use the EMI channel only for low-value non-core payments, cross-border FX market-making, and sub-account treasury sweeps, and ensure all material FCY inflows are captured in the SWIFT-based BOP reporting framework.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual ITR14 must be filed within 12 months of the company's financial year-end; provisional tax payments are due within six months of the commencement of the year of assessment (first provisional) and not later than the last business day of the company's financial year (second provisional); the third provisional top-up payment is due within six months after financial year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Company Registration Number Enter the unique CIPC registration number (e.g. 2024/000000/07). Non-resident companies must register as 'external companies' with CIPC and use their assigned registration number; include the country of incorporation code. Failure to file: 10% of unpaid tax as a late payment penalty; additional 1% monthly interest on unpaid tax for each month outstanding; administrative non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per month for a large company; criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to register or file, attracting a fine up to ZAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to two years.
Box 2 – Financial Year-End Date State the last day of the reporting period (e.g. 28 February 2025). Non-resident companies with a South African permanent establishment (PE) must align the South African return to the foreign year-end if no SA GAAP accounts; otherwise adopt the SA PE book year. Failure to file: 10% of unpaid tax as a late payment penalty; additional 1% monthly interest on unpaid tax for each month outstanding; administrative non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per month for a large company; criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to register or file, attracting a fine up to ZAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to two years.
Line 10 – Gross Income Disclose worldwide gross income, excluding exempt dividends and foreign branch income where a section 10(1)(oA) election applies. Non-resident PE must include only South African-source income attributable to the PE; foreign-source income is excluded unless effectively connected to the PE. Failure to file: 10% of unpaid tax as a late payment penalty; additional 1% monthly interest on unpaid tax for each month outstanding; administrative non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per month for a large company; criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to register or file, attracting a fine up to ZAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to two years.
Line 12 – Exempt Income Declare exempt income, including dividends received (subject to section 10(1)(k)) and foreign dividends meeting participation exemption conditions. Foreign dividends received by a non-resident PE are exempt only if the shareholder holds at least 10% of the equity and the dividend is subject to tax in the foreign jurisdiction. Failure to file: 10% of unpaid tax as a late payment penalty; additional 1% monthly interest on unpaid tax for each month outstanding; administrative non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per month for a large company; criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to register or file, attracting a fine up to ZAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to two years.
Line 18 – Taxable Income Before Deductions Computed gross income less exempt income less allowable deductions. Deductions for non-resident PE must be apportioned under section 24D; head office costs are not automatically deductible unless directly attributable to the PE. Failure to file: 10% of unpaid tax as a late payment penalty; additional 1% monthly interest on unpaid tax for each month outstanding; administrative non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per month for a large company; criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to register or file, attracting a fine up to ZAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to two years.
Line 25 – Assessed Losses Bring-forward assessed loss from prior years, capped at 80% of taxable income before the loss set-off, per section 20(7). Assessed losses of a foreign company are usable only against South African-source income of the same PE; lapsed if business ceases. Failure to file: 10% of unpaid tax as a late payment penalty; additional 1% monthly interest on unpaid tax for each month outstanding; administrative non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per month for a large company; criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to register or file, attracting a fine up to ZAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to two years.
Line 38 – Taxable Income Taxable amount after deduction of assessed losses, subject to the 27% headline rate. For non-residents, this is the PE-attributable taxable income; no loss relief outside PE accounts. Failure to file: 10% of unpaid tax as a late payment penalty; additional 1% monthly interest on unpaid tax for each month outstanding; administrative non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per month for a large company; criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to register or file, attracting a fine up to ZAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to two years.
Line 50 – Dividends Tax Withheld Offset dividends tax withheld by the company as an agent on distributions to shareholders. Non-resident PE distributing dividends to foreign shareholders must withhold 20% dividends tax, reduced to 5% under the South Africa–Netherlands DTA or 10% under the South Africa–UK DTA, based on Treaty relief documentation. Failure to file: 10% of unpaid tax as a late payment penalty; additional 1% monthly interest on unpaid tax for each month outstanding; administrative non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per month for a large company; criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to register or file, attracting a fine up to ZAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to two years.
Section E – Tax Rebates and Alternative Minimum Tax Claim foreign tax credits under section 6quat for foreign taxes paid on foreign-source income. Non-resident PE may claim a section 6quat rebate for foreign taxes paid on foreign-source income included in the South African return; rebate is ring-fenced to South African tax on that foreign income. Failure to file: 10% of unpaid tax as a late payment penalty; additional 1% monthly interest on unpaid tax for each month outstanding; administrative non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per month for a large company; criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to register or file, attracting a fine up to ZAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to two years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million (local file) and ZAR 1 billion (master file) in annual turnover or transaction values; ZAR 5 million threshold for interest deduction limitations under section 31(3).

Economic substance scope: South Africa applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) through the controlled foreign company (CFC) regime in section 9D of the Income Tax Act; A CFC's passive income (rent, royalties, interest, dividends) is attributed proportionally to South African residents unless the CFC has substantial economic substance, defined as: at least 50% of directors, at least 50% of gross income and assets in the foreign jurisdiction, and full compliance with books and records; Additionally, transfer pricing secondary adjustments under section 31 treat 'informal' loans as deemed interest-bearing instruments, requiring imputed interest at the statutory rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Section 235 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, criminal tax evasion carries a fine up to ZAR 2 million or imprisonment up to five years; SARS administrative penalty of 200% of the understated tax under Section 223; plus interest at 10.5% per annum compounded daily.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid taxes where they knowingly participated in the evasion, failed to appoint adequate accounting staff, or allowed the company to trade recklessly; SARS may pierce the corporate veil under Section 9 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008; directors face travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from serving as directors for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income under Section 31, impose a 50% of the adjustment as a penalty under Section 212, and charge interest on the primary adjustment; secondary adjustment requires a deemed loan (interest at the prescribed rate) to be brought into income; non-filing of IT36 attracts a penalty up to ZAR 1.5 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the tax return containing transfer pricing adjustments cannot claim reliance on professional advisers if they failed to monitor; SARS may hold directors liable as 'persons in control' for the full amount of the tax debt under Section 155 of the Tax Administration Act; director's assets and bank accounts can be garnished before final judgment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the CFC rules, the full passive income is attributed to the resident shareholder and taxed at 27%; the company loses any deduction for management fees paid to the foreign affiliate; SARS may impose a 100% penalty on undisclosed offshore arrangements under Section 234; the company may be deregistered by CIPC for failing to file annual returns.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for debts of the company if the company is found to be a mere façade, under the Companies Act veil-piercing provisions; criminal sanctions include a fine up to ZAR 20 million or imprisonment up to 10 years for fraud; directors cannot leave South Africa without a SARS clearance certificate confirming no outstanding tax debts.
AML / Terrorist Financing Red Flags in Trade Remedy Consulting

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) imposes a fine up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment up to 15 years for money laundering or terrorist financing; SARS may impose a 100% administrative penalty on the entire trust/entity if the wire transfer is linked to tax evasion; the company's banking license is revoked and all bank accounts frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal prosecution under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998, with mandatory jail time of 10 years for the deliberate structuring; personal assets are forfeited to the Criminal Assets Recovery Account; the director is permanently barred from holding any office in a regulated financial institution and is subject to a global travel ban.
Anti-Dumping Trade Defense Regulatory Breach

Statutory sanction: CIPC deregistration and prohibition from conducting business for up to five years; SARS may assess undeclared VAT at 21% plus penalties of 200% of the tax; the International Trade Administration Commission (ITAC) may disqualify the entity from appearing as a representative in future anti-dumping proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for all unpaid tax and penalties; complaints to the Legal Practice Council may result in a director being struck off the roll of legal practitioners; a director's passport is flagged, and a warrant of arrest is issued for failure to appear at SARS debt collection proceedings.
