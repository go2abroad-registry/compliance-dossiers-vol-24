Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) - national tax and customs administration; taxpayer portal: SARS eFiling (accessible via www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in South Africa (including an e-commerce B2B MRO supplier) must first incorporate a Proprietary Limited company via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). On CIPC incorporation, SARS automatically issues an Income Tax Reference Number, activated through SARS eFiling by registering taxpayer details and appointing a public officer per s24B of the Income Tax Act (a resident individual responsible for the company's tax affairs). For VAT, the entity applies using the SARS VAT101 registration form (electronically through eFiling or at a SARS branch) once taxable supplies exceed the compulsory threshold or where voluntary registration is sought; the application must include company registration documents, banking details, physical place of business, and public officer details. SARS issues a unique VAT registration number; non-resident vendors without a local bank account must appoint a representative vendor or agent under s47(2) of the VAT Act. All registrations are processed online and turnaround is generally within 21 business days; penalties may apply for trading before a VAT number is issued while registration is compulsory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elections available under domestic law include: (i) VAT group registration election under s50A of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991; (ii) s45 Income Tax Act intra-group asset-for-share rollover election; (iii) s23M hybrid mismatch ordering and adjustment elections; (iv) controlled foreign company high-tax exemption election in respect of CFC income; and (v) incentive elections under s11D and s12H. All elections are effected through prescribed SARS forms submitted via SARS eFiling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period (VAT Act s23(1)); voluntary registration permitted where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 (s23(2)); registration is per entity and no sub-national sales tax applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the supplier supplies goods that are exported from the Republic within the prescribed 90-day period (with a 60-day extension available) and the vendor retains documentary proof prescribed in s11(3) and the SARS VAT Export Guide. The supply must be made under a sale or consignment to a non-resident and the goods must physically depart from South Africa to a destination outside the common customs area (SACU). For B2B MRO e-commerce supplies, zero-rating applies where industrial maintenance hardware is dispatched from a South African warehouse to a foreign buyer located outside SACU; qualifying exported services under s11(2)(k)/(l) are also zero-rated where the services are rendered for a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time supply is made and the services are not of a kind consumed locally.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 form) / SASPEDI customs clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Airwaybill (through bill issued to the foreign buyer)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank proof of receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Contract or Purchase Order evidencing the sale to the non-resident customer

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is currently in force in South Africa for domestic VAT as at 2024. VAT vendors must issue full tax invoices meeting s20 VAT Act requirements (vendor name, VAT registration number, date, description, consideration, and the words 'Tax Invoice'). VAT returns and related data are submitted through SARS eFiling APIs; SARS has published a modernisation roadmap contemplating future real-time e-invoicing and continuous transaction controls, but no live clearance mandate is yet operational.

SARS operates a dedicated high-risk export zero-rating audit and VAT fraud enforcement programme. Incorrectly zero-rated exports attract additional tax of 200% under s60(1) of the VAT Act in cases of fraud or evasion, or 100% under s60(2) otherwise. Under the Tax Administration Act, understatement penalties of up to 200% of the shortfall may be imposed together with interest. Retrospective clawbacks may extend to ten years in assessed-loss or fraud/non-disclosure situations under s99 of the TAA; deliberate schemes are referred for criminal prosecution under s235 of the TAA (tax evasion) and are punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to five years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account items, including cross-border trade settlements and profit/dividend remittances, subject to supporting documentary evidence and a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCC). The capital account remains subject to exchange control: 100% non-resident equity ownership of a (Pty) Ltd is freely permitted through a SARB Authorized Dealer without case-by-case SARB approval, but all inbound foreign equity must be recorded as Balance of Payments (BOP) statistics by the receiving bank. Non-resident shareholder loans exceeding ZAR 3 million require registration of the foreign loan mandate with the Authorized Dealer. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful but is conditioned on: (1) annual financial statements reflecting declared dividends, (2) a SARS tax clearance for foreign payments, (3) the 15% dividend withholding tax (or reduced treaty rate) being deducted, and (4) the transaction being executed through an Authorized Dealer. No exchange control limitation applies to the quantum of current account payments for MRO inventory purchases, but SARS customs import declarations (SAD500) and commercial invoices are required for each foreign payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received into South Africa through an Authorized Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment, as set out in Section E of the Currency and Exchanges Manual. The exporter must submit a customs SAD500 export declaration to SARS and later provide proof of payment and shipping evidence to its Authorized Dealer bank. Since the abolition of the prior 50% conversion rule on 1 November 2016, there is no mandatory ZAR conversion ratio: export receipts may either be converted into ZAR at the prevailing market rate or be credited in full to a licensed Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with the Authorized Dealer. Where export proceeds are not received within the 180-day statutory window, the exporter must report the overdue debt to FinSurv and submit an application for extension together with documentary proof of the underlying trade receivable.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for South Africa

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium direct viability. A foreign digital EMI (e.g., Wise Business or Revolut Business) cannot lawfully serve as the primary transactional account of a South African resident company because deposit-taking without a banking licence is prohibited under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 and exchange control requires all resident cross-border receipts and payments to flow through a SARB Authorized Dealer. Non-resident EMIs may be used only as ancillary payment initiation tools where the merchant-of-record is the foreign platform, not the South African entity. The compliant structure is to use a licensed South African payment gateway (e.g., Peach Payments, PayFast/Yoco with bank settlement accounts) to aggregate inbound B2B MRO e-commerce receipts and settle them into a Tier-1 commercial bank account, while outbound supplier disbursements are executed by SWIFT through the local Authorized Dealer. Any offshore EMI arrangement that bypasses the Authorized Dealer would constitute a breach of the Currency and Exchanges Manual and expose the company to administrative penalties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity funding must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the target (Pty) Ltd account held with a SARB Authorized Dealer. The SWIFT remittance information should identify the purpose as 'Equity Subscription / Foreign Direct Investment' and the receiving bank will allocate its internal BOP code (SARB 'S201' for share capital). The company must lodge the board resolution allotting shares, the shareholder register, and a signed BOP declaration with the Authorized Dealer before the funds are released for operational use; the Authorized Dealer subsequently reports the inflow on its monthly BOP return to FinSurv. Where the injection is temporarily warehoused pending share registration, the bank will credit a Capital Imported Money (CIM)/escrow account; no prior SARB approval is needed, but the entire amount must be declared and traceable to the subscribing non-resident shareholder. Foreign currency funds may be converted to ZAR by the Authorized Dealer at the wholesale market rate, or retained in a Foreign Currency Account if the company intends to fund offshore MRO inventory purchases directly.Outbound disbursements for MRO inventory procurement use three primary clearing rails: (1) open-account trade settlement via SWIFT MT103 supported by commercial invoices, transport documents, and customs SAD500 declarations; (2) documentary collections on a documents-against-payment (D/P) or documents-against-acceptance (D/A) basis through the local Authorized Dealer; and (3) irrevocable commercial Letters of Credit issued in accordance with UCP600, advised and confirmed by the overseas supplier's bank, with the issuing bank being the South African Authorized Dealer. All foreign payments must use the correct exchange control purpose code for imported goods (SARB 'T001') and must reference the underlying SARS customs entry. Where the supplier is located in the Common Monetary Area (Lesotho, Namibia, Eswatini), settlement may be effected in ZAR on a SIAC-clearing basis without any FX conversion, reducing cost and operational latency. For repeated high-frequency MRO online marketplace payouts, the treasury function should establish a batch SWIFT MT101 arrangement with the Authorized Dealer to consolidate supplier remittances and maintain full audit trail compliance.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contract (FEC) through a SARB Authorized Dealer — used to hedge firm import exposures evidenced by pro-forma invoices or purchase orders; no upfront premium; settlement by physical ZAR delivery at maturity.Single-payment currency options (European-style) — permissible for corporates with an approved underlying trade exposure; premium-based hedge giving the right to buy or sell ZAR against foreign currency at a fixed strike; subject to Authorized Dealer internal risk approval.Cross-currency swap (CCS) — available for approved non-resident shareholder loans or foreign-currency-denominated trade finance facilities; allows the exchange of ZAR floating payments against USD/LIBOR-linked obligations.Non-deliverable ZAR forward (NDF) — NOT permissible onshore for resident corporates under SARB exchange control; resident companies must use deliverable FECs; NDFs are confined to eligible non-resident institutional counterparties and would breach the Currency and Exchanges Manual if entered offshore by the (Pty) Ltd.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% for years ending before that date

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on the gross dividend paid to a South African or non-resident beneficial owner unless exempt under Section 64F or a reduced treaty rate applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 is due within 12 months after the end of the company's financial year, unless an extension is applied for. Supervisory change: where the financial year ends before 31 March, the assessment is completed during the following tax season; the filing date is still computed from the year end, not from the fiscal tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and transactional documentation are required where the total international connected-person transactions exceed ZAR 5,000,000 in a year of assessment. Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting obligations apply to a South African constituent entity of an MNE group with consolidated group turnover of ZAR 10 billion or more, although a group may be required by SARS to deliver a Master File even below the threshold if the nature of the cross-border transactions warrants. All transactions with connected persons must be documented contemporaneously, regardless of amount, in the records retained under the Tax Administration Act.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the style of the UAE. However, economic substance is fundamental to the entire tax architecture: corporate residency may shift under the management-and-control test (Section 1 of the Income Tax Act); the GAAR in Section 80A-L disregards tax avoidance arrangements lacking a real commercial substance; the connected-person pricing rules in Section 31 require a functional and risk analysis; and the anti-hybrid and CFC rules presume that a shell entity cannot own assets or allocate profit without employees, premises, and decision-making functions. A South African (Pty) Ltd must maintain real personnel, physical or digital business infrastructure, procurement and fulfillment functions, and director meetings in South Africa to justify profit allocation; otherwise SARS may recharacterize revenue as attributable to a permanent establishment abroad, deny treaty benefits, or impose understatement penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act No. 28 of 2011 imposes a statutory understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall if the misstatement is grossly negligent or deliberate; interest accrues from the original due date. Criminal conviction for tax evasion may attract a fine of up to ZAR 50,000 (or double the benefit in certain cases) and imprisonment for up to 10 years; on a corporate conviction, criminal liability transfers to the controlling directors where SARS establishes knowledge.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax due to the piercing of the corporate veil under Section 20(9) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008. SARS may obtain an order restricting the director's departure from South Africa (travel ban) and attach personal bank accounts and immovable property for amounts owing by the company.
Shell Company and Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rules under Sections 80A-L of the Income Tax Act allow SARS to annul or recharacterize the whole arrangement, imposing tax as if the shell transaction had not been made. A penalty of 75% or 200% of the tax adjustment can be added. The Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) may deregister the company for having no beneficial trading activity; SARS can separately terminate tax registration and issue an estimated assessment. Third-party creditors may also apply to liquidate the entity.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil when the shell was used for an unconscionable abuse of the corporate form; directors become personally liable for the company's tax liabilities and any amounts paid to them as unlawful distributions. In egregious cross-border treaty-shopping cases, directors face criminal culpability for aiding a conduit arrangement and may be personally subjected to travel bans and extradition requests.
Transfer Pricing and Cross-Border Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: SARS recomputes taxable income under Section 31 of the Income Tax Act to reflect arm's-length pricing, disallowing the inflated deduction or adjusting the fee income upward. Imputed interest and royalties attract secondary withholding tax at 15% if not properly declared. Understatement penalties of up to 200% apply; where transfer pricing documentation is missing, SARS may apply the anti-offshore default penalty of 10% of the adjustment amount for a 'no reasonable grounds' filing. Deliberate mismatches could result in criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or signed off transfer pricing policies with no documented economic substance are personally liable for any penalty imposed on the company if they knowingly made a false or negligent representation. SARS can issue a direction to the South African Reserve Bank to freeze travel and can pursue assets fraudulently moved out of South Africa under the Insolvency Act and the Companies Act reckless trading provisions.
AML and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 imposes administrative fines of up to ZAR 50 million in the case of an accountable institution for failing to do FICA due diligence and reporting; criminal money laundering under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998 attracts a fine up to ZAR 100 million and imprisonment for up to 30 years for individuals. The courts may also order asset forfeiture, profit confiscation, and publication of the offence. The company may be removed from the SARS list to hold a valid business license and subject to liquidation.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are jointly liable for laundering proceeds where the company benefited from the gross negligence; personal assets and bank accounts are subject to attachment under civil forfeiture and criminal restraint orders. Travel bans apply automatically once a case is enrolled with the National Prosecuting Authority; Interpol red notice coordination is available for flight risk. Directors can be imprisoned individually even if the company itself cannot be jailed.
