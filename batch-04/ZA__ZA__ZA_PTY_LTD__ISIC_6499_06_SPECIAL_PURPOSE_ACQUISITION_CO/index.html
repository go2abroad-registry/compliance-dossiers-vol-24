Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private company under s 19(2) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008; shares may be issued at no-par value with no minimum subscription at incorporation. For a listed SPAC, the JSE SPAC regime requires the substantial majority of gross IPO proceeds (at least 90% per the trust deed, in line with international market practice, subject to JSE listing approval) to be deposited immediately into an independently administered trust/escrow account, held for the benefit of public shareholders, and released only upon a qualifying acquisition approved by shareholder vote (a qualifying acquisition must equal at least 80% of the trust assets) or returned to investors if no acquisition completes within the prescribed period (generally 24 months).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) - https://www.sars.gov.za; eFiling portal at https://efiling.sars.gov.za. Exchange control oversight for SPAC trust proceeds is shared with the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) under the Currency and Exchanges Act (Cap. 9).

E-government filing portal

(1) For an incorporated SA (Pty) Ltd, the CIPC Companies and Intellectual Property Commission filing auto-creates a SARS income tax reference number via the CIPC-SARS interoperability gateway; thereafter activate an eFiling profile. (2) For a foreign-incorporated SPAC, file Form ITREG at a SARS Domestic Branch or via eFiling for an income tax reference number, attaching certified foreign certificate of incorporation, board resolution, director IDs and Power of Attorney to a nominated SA public officer. (3) Compulsory VAT registration via Form VAT101 within 21 days of exceeding the ZAR 1,000,000 turnover threshold; voluntary VAT101 registration is open when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 (VAT Act s25(1)). (4) Register for PAYE (EMP/EasyPAYE) and UIF if directors/employees are remunerated. (5) Appoint a public officer resident in SA within one month (Tax Administration Act s246). (6) Obtain a Tax Clearance Certificate (TCS) at SARS eFiling before declaring any dividend, redemption or distribution from the SPAC trust/escrow account.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A SA resident company (incorporated in SA or effectively managed in SA) is taxed on worldwide income; corporate income tax rate is 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2024 (28% prior). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A SA resident company (incorporated in SA or effectively managed in SA) is taxed on worldwide income; corporate income tax rate is 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2024 (28% prior). Dividends to shareholders attract dividends tax (withholding) at 20%; foreign shareholders may invoke treaty relief. The (Pty) Ltd is not a pass-through entity under SA law; no US-style check-the-box applies. SPAC assets held in a separate escrow/trust account constitute an 'affected trust' or 'SPV' for specific CFC and controlled-company anti-avoidance rules, and the escrow interest is taxable at the company level regardless of distribution. The entity is within the SACU territory export zero-rating system but does not itself qualify as a tax-exempt zone vehicle unless designated under s10(1)(cN) or a binding private ruling is obtained from SARS.

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box or fiscal transparency election. Available statutory elections relevant to a SPAC holding and M&A: (1) Headquarter Company status under s9I (elected by meeting 80% gross-income and cost thresholds) exempting foreign dividends, interest, royalties and capital gains on foreign subsidiaries; (2) CFC pooling election under s9D in respect of net income of foreign target companies; (3) Hybrid mismatch recharacterisation rules under s8E, s8F, s8FA and s23H apply ex lege, with an election to apply the 'structured arrangement' safe harbor where intent is documented; (4) Election under the VAT Act s23 to voluntarily register for VAT where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000; (5) Section 64B deemed-dividend election for distributions in specie; (6) Binding Private Ruling (BPR) application to SARS for any novel SPAC trust/escrow or earn-out arrangement; (7) Dividend access and capital redemption elections under the Companies Act affecting the s46/ s44 tax-free roll-over treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration at ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months (VAT Act s23(1)); voluntary registration at ZAR 50,000 (s25(1)). For a SPAC, financial intermediation services (active management of escrow, share dealing, dividends) are exempt financial supplies under s12, which do not count toward the threshold; registration is generally not triggered unless the SPAC makes taxable supplies of goods or non-exempt services (e.g., advisory, listing fees not zoned as financial services). Once registered, the SPAC must charge VAT at 15% on taxable supplies and cannot recover input VAT attributable to exempt financial supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act to goods supplied and exported from the Republic within 90 days of payment (or such extended period as SARS approves), provided title and risk pass to a non-resident abroad. Section 11(1)(j) zero-rates supplies to foreign-going ships and aircraft; s11(1)(k) zero-rates internationally supplied services and services physically rendered outside SA to non-residents where the recipient is non-resident and the services are not connected to immovable property in SA. A SPAC's own escrow management, merger and acquisition financing services are financial supplies and therefore exempt, not zero-rated; only separate cross-border fee arrangements with non-resident target groups may qualify under s11(1)(k). Documentary proof must show physical export (customs clearing), payment in foreign currency or ZAR from a non-resident source, and the exported goods must be delivered and used overseas. Claims for refund of any excess VAT paid must be made within 5 years of the tax period (s44 TAA) and are subject to data matching with SAD500 customs declarations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SASAD500 / SAD500 transmitted via Customs EDI or eFiling) reflecting the export and SPAC cargo nature
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill / Road Consignment Note) evidencing physical movement of goods offshore within the 90-day period
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-issued IRC or Foreign Exchange Proof) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in ZAR/foreign currency within 180 days

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping akin to Latin American regimes is currently in force. SARS is phasing in mandatory e-Invoicing for selected large vendors, aligned with the Pan-African e-Invoicing standard and the AfCFTA digital trade protocol, requiring structured XML/UBL invoices transmitted to SARS via an approved Service Provider or direct API. Existing s20(4) statutory tax invoice requirements apply to all VAT vendors: vendor name and VAT number, invoice date, invoice number, description, quantity, unit price, VAT rate, and consideration. SARS cross-matches invoice data at VAT201 return level via eFiling APIs; non-compliant transactions are flagged for audit under the Tax Administration Act (TAA) risk engines. Credit notes must comply with s21 and be issued within 21 days of the event trigger.

SARS may raise additional assessments within 5 years of the assessment date, extended to 7 years where a return was omitted or a non-deduction was claimed; unlimited prescription exists for fraud or misrepresentation. Zero-rating claims lacking the SAD500, B/L, IRC evidence bundle are disallowed and VAT clawed back with interest at the prescribed rate (currently SARB repurchase rate plus 3%). Understatement penalties under TAA s222-223 apply as a percentage of the resulting shortfall: 10% for substantial understatement, 25% for no reasonable grounds, 50% for gross negligence, and 150% for intentional tax evasion; the 150% tier triggers automatic criminal referral. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating carries a fine of up to ZAR 30,000 or 24 months' imprisonment per charge (or both). Non-repatriation of export proceeds beyond 180 days is a SARB exchange-control contravention under s9 of the Currency and Exchanges Act, subject to administrative penalties of up to 100% of the under-repatriated amount and possible forfeiture. For SPAC capital held in trust/escrow, SARS applies specific anti-avoidance validation (e.g., s103 TAA general anti-avoidance) when the zero-rating is used to recover VAT on expenditures not linked to genuine export of goods or services.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a dual-tier exchange control regime supervised by SARB FinSurv. Current account transactions are substantially liberalised and may be processed through authorised dealer banks without prior SARB approval. Capital account transactions, including inward equity subscriptions and outward acquisition funding by a SPAC, require documentation of a SARB-issued deal/registration reference or use of the electronic Investment Register. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are freely convertible once supported by audited financial statements, tax clearance confirmation, and a properly filed dividend withholding tax declaration; however, the underlying share capital must be duly registered with SARB to evidence the inward leg. Practical hurdles include strict beneficial ownership disclosure, source-of-funds substantiation for sponsor contributions, FIC Act section 28A enhanced due diligence expectations, and the prohibition on using unlicensed offshore escrow custodians for mandated trust proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The historical statutory surrender obligation requiring exporters to convert export proceeds into ZAR within 30 days has been abolished under the revised SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers. Exporters in good standing may retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts, subject to monthly reporting via Form A and the authorised dealer's reconciliation against shipping and invoicing documentation. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for eligible export receipts. For a SPAC, any foreign receipts arising from the disposal of underlying assets or reverse-merger consideration must be reported and reconciled with the SARB-approved deal number; unexplained hard-currency inflows are recharacterised as capital account transactions and trigger FinSurv review and potential FIC suspicious transaction reporting.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise, Payoneer) are not licensed by SARB as authorised dealers and cannot lawfully hold the SPAC's trust-escrow proceeds or execute domestic ZAR clearing. Their viability is rated Low-Medium and is restricted to ancillary functions such as aggregating sponsor commitments from foreign jurisdictions or making small operational disbursements, provided the funds never touch the onshore regulated trust account. The onshore statutory trust-escrow obligation for a listed SPAC under the JSE Listings Requirements and the Companies Act 71 of 2008 requires custody with a registered bank; any use of an unregulated EMI for mandate-critical escrow funds would constitute a compliance breach and trigger FIC enforcement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward equity subscriptions from non-resident sponsors must be effected by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wires through a SARB authorised dealer bank. Each remittance must reference the SARB-issued deal number (inward investment registration), the company registration number, and the remitter's upstream bank reference. The prescribed SARB purpose code for SPAC share subscriptions is 4013 (inward equity participation); convertible loan participations use code 4012 (financial loan). All inbound funds must be matched on the electronic Investment Register or Form A within 30 days, and cumulative variances exceeding ZAR 5 million automatically trigger FIC Act section 28A enhanced due diligence, requiring a documented source-of-funds memorandum and independent verification of each sponsor.Outbound disbursements should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 standard commercial payments for acquisition consideration, sponsor fees, and administrative expenses, and via MT700-series documentary credits for trade-backed settlements involving the target's operating subsidiaries. Distribution of the SPAC trust-escrow to target shareholders at closing must be executed by SWIFT MT103 referencing the share-purchase agreement and the SARB-approved outward transaction; where the outward remittance exceeds the ZAR 15 million threshold, prior FinSurv approval or a specific authorised-dealer dispensation must be obtained. Commercial letters of credit may be issued by the onshore authorised-dealer bank in favour of the overseas beneficiary, with all supporting documents mirroring the wire instruction narratives in the closing agreement to preserve a single, auditable trail.Forward exchange contracts (FEC) with authorised dealer banks for up to 24 months to hedge anticipated USD and EUR exposure created by the escrow appreciation and acquisition considerationNon-deliverable forward (NDF) structures executed with offshore investment banks, permitted only when hedging genuine ZAR/USD commercial exposure and prohibited for speculative purposes under SARB exchange control rulingsCross-currency swaps (CCS) and interest-rate swaps through SARB-regulated authorised dealers, subject to documentation of an underlying treasury or acquisition-related exposure and compliance with FSCA derivative conduct standards

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise, Payoneer) are not licensed by SARB as authorised dealers and cannot lawfully hold the SPAC's trust-escrow proceeds or execute domestic ZAR clearing. Their viability is rated Low-Medium and is restricted to ancillary functions such as aggregating sponsor commitments from foreign jurisdictions or making small operational disbursements, provided the funds never touch the onshore regulated trust account. The onshore statutory trust-escrow obligation for a listed SPAC under the JSE Listings Requirements and the Companies Act 71 of 2008 requires custody with a registered bank; any use of an unregulated EMI for mandate-critical escrow funds would constitute a compliance breach and trigger FIC enforcement.
