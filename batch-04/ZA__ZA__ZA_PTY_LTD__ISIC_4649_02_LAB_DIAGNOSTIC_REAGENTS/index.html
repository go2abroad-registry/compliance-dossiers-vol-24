Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); national self-assessment tax administration portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); corporate registry interface: Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC).

E-government filing portal

A South African (Pty) Ltd must: (1) incorporate through CIPC to obtain a company registration number; (2) apply to SARS for Income Tax registration via eFiling within 21 business days of incorporation to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number; (3) file a VAT 101 application when taxable supplies exceed or are projected to exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (voluntary registration above R50,000 per annum), thereby obtaining a 10-digit VAT vendor number; (4) register as a SARS Customs client through the Customs eFiling platform to secure a Customs client code for import and export declarations; and (5) register for PAYE/UIF/SDL once the company becomes an employer. Foreign-incorporated principals without a fixed South African place of business must separately apply for a SARS non-resident tax reference number and may be registered as non-resident vendors for electronic services or physically delivered goods where South African VAT nexus arises.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a statutory 'company' under s1 Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate, with no pass-through eligibility and no sub-jurisdictional tax distinction) — A South African (Pty) Ltd must: (1) incorporate through CIPC to obtain a company registration number; (2) apply to SARS for Income Tax registration via eFiling within 21 business days of incorporation to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number; (3) file a VAT 101 application when taxable supplies exceed or are projected to exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (voluntary registration above R50,000 per annum), thereby obtaining a 10-digit VAT vendor number; (4) register as a SARS Customs client through the Customs eFiling platform to secure a Customs client code for import and export declarations; and (5) register for PAYE/UIF/SDL once the company becomes an employer. Foreign-incorporated principals without a fixed South African place of business must separately apply for a SARS non-resident tax reference number and may be registered as non-resident vendors for electronic services or physically delivered goods where South African VAT nexus arises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a statutory 'company' under s1 Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate, with no pass-through eligibility and no sub-jurisdictional tax distinction)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; classification follows the statutory legal form, so a (Pty) Ltd is invariably taxed at entity level. Available alignments and elections: (i) voluntary VAT registration under s23(2) VAT Act 89 of 1991; (ii) VAT group-of-companies registration under s50 VAT Act; (iii) s42 asset-for-share and s45 intra-group rollover elections; (iv) s9I headquarter company election and s9D foreign business establishment designation; (v) anti-hybrid mismatch alignment under ss8E-8F Income Tax Act (hybrid debt and equity instruments), applied statutorily under the BEPS Action 2 framework; (vi) automatic CFC exclusion for a controlled foreign company's foreign business establishment income under s9D.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory: R1,000,000 (taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period or 12-month projection); Voluntary: R50,000 per annum for a person carrying on a South African enterprise; the R1,000,000 compulsory threshold equally applies to foreign electronic-services vendors registered under s54A VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under s11(1)(a) VAT Act, a supply of diagnostic reagents is zero-rated where the exporting vendor physically removes the goods from South Africa under the Customs and Excise Act and obtains the prescribed documentary proof before the relevant VAT return is submitted, or within such further period as the Commissioner allows. Offshore services qualify under s11(2)(k) (services physically rendered outside South Africa), s11(2)(l) (services to a non-resident not present in South Africa at the time of supply, subject to statutory exceptions for land, movable property, and machinery in South Africa), and s11(2)(m) (movable property temporarily in South Africa supplied to a non-resident). The vendor must be the exporter of record with a SARS Customs client code, issue a s20(4)-compliant zero-rated tax invoice, maintain SAHPRA licensing for the IVD reagents, retain all records for 5 years, and prove receipt of foreign-currency proceeds within the exchange-control window.

  • SARS Customs export declaration (SAD500) with electronic clearance code
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure from South African customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) and bank DE foreign-currency account statement evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Zero-rated commercial tax invoice containing all s20(4) VAT Act particulars
  • Packing list and certificate of origin as required under SACU rules
  • SAHPRA import/export permit for clinical diagnostic ELISA assays and IVD reagents
  • Proof of the customer's non-resident status where offshore-services zero-rating is claimed

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API stamping, or continuous-transaction-control (CTC) digital-stamping mandate is currently in force for standard VAT invoices. SARS eFiling is the statutory lodgement channel for VAT returns and refunds, while electronic Customs export declarations (SAD500 via Direct Trader Input) are compulsory. SARS has announced and is piloting a phased CTC-style e-invoicing reform; until legislated, vendors must issue s20(4)-VAT-Act-compliant tax invoices within 21 days of supply, with export invoices expressly marked zero-rated. IVD reagent exports additionally require SAHPRA import/export permits under the health-products regulatory regime.

SARS may reverse the zero-rating and assess output VAT at 15% plus interest where export documentation is incomplete, late, or non-compliant, and will scrutinise refund claims through the VAT refund-verification unit and the BEAT/fraud desk. The Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 imposes understatement penalties of 10% (substantial understatement), 25% (no reasonable care), 150% (gross negligence), and 200% (intentional tax evasion) of the shortfall, together with fixed administrative penalties for each month of continuing non-compliance. The VAT Act separately creates criminal liability for fraudulent zero-rating claims, exposing a company to a fine or imprisonment of its controlling officers for up to 24 months. SARS may reopen assessments within 3 years generally, extended to 5 years where fraud or misrepresentation is shown, and all supporting export records must be retained for 5 years under s29 Tax Administration Act.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including trade payments, service fees, royalties and ordinary profit repatriations, without prior SARB approval where the transaction is documented and routed through an authorised dealer. Capital-account convertibility is selectively controlled: inward non-resident ordinary equity investment in a private company is generally permitted without prior FinSurv approval if the investment is made through an authorised dealer with correct balance-of-payments coding and full AML/KYC documentation. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily documentary: settlement of the 20% dividends withholding tax, evidence of distributable profits, compliance with the Companies Act solvency and liquidity test, issuance of a SARS tax compliance status letter, and authorised-dealer verification of source of funds and beneficial ownership. Outward capital flows by the South African entity, such as offshore loans or guarantees, may require prior FinSurv approval and will be subject to stricter substance and pricing review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Regulation 5 of the Exchange Control Regulations and the Currency and Exchanges Manual, exporters must receive the full export proceeds within 180 days from the date of shipment. FinSurv may grant an extension on application for specific long-dated transactions, in some cases up to 365 days. Foreign-currency export receipts must be repatriated to an authorised dealer in South Africa; where no approved foreign-currency retention account or exemption exists, 100% of the exported invoice value must be sold into ZAR through the authorised dealer at the prevailing market rate. The exporter must retain the SAD500 customs declaration, commercial invoice, transport documents and conversion/receipt records for a minimum of five years.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, is the central bank and exchange-control administration. SARB-licensed authorised dealers, namely the major commercial banks, administer day-to-day foreign-exchange approvals, reporting and settlement.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory dividends withholding tax, payable by the South African company on behalf of the beneficial owner; exemptions apply under section 64F, including dividends paid to South African resident companies and certain headquarter companies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No single statutory ZAR transaction threshold applies universally, but SARS expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where affected transactions with connected persons exceed ZAR 5 million in a year, and a Master File is expected where the South African entity forms part of an MNE group with consolidated annual revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion. SARS may request documentation at any time during an audit regardless of transaction value.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have a stand-alone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute. However, section 31 of the Income Tax Act and the general avoidance rules in sections 80A-L require the South African entity to demonstrate real functions, assets, and risks corresponding to its reported profit. An IVD distribution shell that books margin in South Africa but lacks employees, premises, inventory control, or decision-making power can be recharacterized by SARS, with correlative transfer pricing adjustments and treaty benefit denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: SARS will reject the return, assess using the best of available information, apply a 150% understatement penalty for intentional tax evasion under TAA section 223, impose a 10% late-payment penalty plus prescribed interest, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution. On conviction, individuals face a criminal fine of up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment of up to five years for tax fraud, or higher sentences under common law fraud where the loss is substantial.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false returns or directed the company to conceal income may be personally liable under section 77 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 for resulting company losses. SARS may issue a tax blockage order preventing departure from South Africa, attach personal assets, and seek civil restitution plus punitive damages.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: SARS makes a primary transfer pricing adjustment under section 31 to restore an arm's length margin, recharacterizes excess payments as deemed dividends or loans, and imposes dividends withholding tax at 20%. The understatement penalty is 75% for an impermissible avoidance arrangement, rising to 150% for intentional concealment; interest accrues at the prescribed rate from the date of underpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors who negotiated non-arm's length offshore transactions can be joined as defendants in civil recovery actions if the company has been stripped of assets. The corporate veil may be pierced where the subsidiary is merely an instrumentality of the parent, and directors involved in the scheme can be subjected to travel bans and criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
Money Laundering / Unlawful Remittance Network

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, accountable institutions face administrative fines of up to ZAR 10 million per violation, with criminal penalties of up to 15 years' imprisonment for money laundering. SARS may also impose the 150% intentional evasion understatement penalty on undisclosed income connected to the unlawful payment network.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit the company to receive laundered proceeds can be prosecuted as principals. Under section 20(9) of the Companies Act, a court may pierce the corporate veil where the company is used to conceal illegality, allowing civil restitution claims against directors personally and imposition of travel bans during criminal proceedings.
