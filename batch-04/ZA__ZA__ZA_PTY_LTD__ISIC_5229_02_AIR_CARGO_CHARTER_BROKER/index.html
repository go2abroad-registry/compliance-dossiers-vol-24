Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) under the South African Revenue Service Act 34 of 1997; electronic filing portal: eFiling at https://efiling.sars.gov.za or www.sars.gov.za.

E-government filing portal

A foreign juristic entity, including a branch or intermediary of an overseas air cargo charter broker, must apply to SARS via eFiling or at a SARS branch. Procedure: (i) submit the relevant SARS registration application accompanied by the company's constitutional documents, certificate of incorporation, foreign registration evidence, names and identity/passport copies of directors, and a resolution appointing an authorised SARS representative resident in South Africa if no physical presence exists; (ii) obtain an income tax reference number (TIN) and, where taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period exceed ZAR 1 million, a VAT vendor number; (iii) register with SARS Customs and Excise to obtain a customs code for export declarations, clearing and bonded operations; (iv) file all tax returns and correspondence electronically through eFiling. Non-residents without a South African permanent establishment must nevertheless register for VAT when they make taxable supplies in South Africa above the statutory threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable — South Africa does not have a check-the-box or elective entity classification regime. A (Pty) Ltd is statutorily classified as a company and taxed at entity level; no pass-through election exists. Hybrid debt/equity mismatches are recharacterised automatically under sections 8E–8L of the Income Tax Act, without an elective opt-out. Limited statutory elections available to an entity include the small business corporation tax rate concession under s12E and the headquarter company regime under s9I for qualifying foreign-owned South African companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 (R1 million) of taxable supplies made in the preceding 12 months, or reasonably expected in the next 12 months; voluntary registration is available where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 (R50,000). Non-resident suppliers of taxable electronic services to South African recipients are similarly required to register where the R1 million threshold is exceeded, unless a concession or exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimable where goods are supplied under the VAT Act and are physically exported from South Africa within the prescribed period (normally within 90 days after the date of supply), provided the vendor receives foreign consideration and retains documentary proof of export. International airfreight and ancillary cargo charter services may be zero-rated where the service is supplied in connection with goods being transported from or to South Africa, or where the service is physically rendered outside South Africa to a non-resident customer. A cargo charter brokerage fee may be zero-rated if the underlying international air transport is zero-rated or the brokerage service is supplied for export cargo and the intermediary holds appropriate evidence.

  • Validated SARS Customs export declaration (SAD500 or eCargo electronic export declaration)
  • Air Waybill (AWB) or Bill of Lading evidencing physical departure from South Africa
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying exporter, consignee, cargo description, unit value and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit confirmation issued by an authorised dealer in foreign exchange proving receipt of foreign currency into South Africa within 180 days
  • Destination-country customs entry / certificate of landing where available, confirming arrival of cargo abroad
  • Cargo release or custody movement evidence, such as carrier release order, container tracking statement or warehouse export receipt

South Africa has not introduced a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping requirement for ordinary B2B invoices. SARS accepts fully electronic tax invoices (PDF, XML, EDI or eFiling-generated) provided they satisfy the particulars prescribed in section 20 of the VAT Act and are retained for audit. Customs exports are cleared electronically via SARS Customs using the SAD500 declaration or eCargo electronic data exchange, and clearing records must be retained and presented to SARS on request.

SARS applies heightened scrutiny to export zero-rating and VAT refund claims. If documentation is insufficient or fraudulent, SARS will disallow the zero-rating, raise output VAT at 15% on the full supply, claw back any refund previously paid, and charge statutory interest. The Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 further imposes understatement penalties escalating with culpability, including intentional tax evasion, and dishonest export declarations may constitute a criminal offence, leading to a fine or imprisonment up to five years. Directors and representative taxpayers may also be personally liable where the entity knowingly participated in fraudulent export schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is a controlled currency administered under exchange control regulations, but current account transactions (trade settlement, dividends, interest, royalties, freight payments) are freely convertible through authorised dealers without SARB approval, provided the customer submits the requisite documentary package and tax compliance status. Capital account transactions (inward equity, external loans, outward direct investment) are subject to thresholds and reporting: authorised dealers approve applications up to their discretionary limit (currently ZAR 250 million for many consent categories) and must report all flows to FinSurv via the Balance of Payments system. Profit remittance by a resident (Pty) Ltd to a non-resident shareholder is permitted upon (i) confirmation of SARS tax compliance (electronic tax clearance status – e-TCS), (ii) a valid board dividend declaration, (iii) an audited/reviewed financial statement if required by the bank, and (iv) proof of beneficial ownership matching the shareholder register. No separate SARB approval is required for ordinary dividend remittances, but the transaction must be effectuated through an authorised dealer and be reported; disinvestment or capital reduction remittances require an Auditor's Certificate and FinSurv approval if the amount exceeds bank discretion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exchange Control Regulations and SARB rulings require that export proceeds for air cargo and related services be received in South Africa within 180 days from date of shipment or performance of service. Upon receipt, the export proceeds must be surrendered to an authorised dealer in South African rand within 30 days unless the exporter is the holder of a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) at an authorised dealer, in which case the proceeds may be retained in foreign currency. There is no partial/conversion ratio: the default statutory requirement is 100% surrender to the domestic banking system, with the FCA as the only discretionary deviation. Exports to Common Monetary Area (CMA) countries are exempt from surrender/repatriation formalities. The exporter must submit a full documentary trail, including the air waybill, commercial invoice, exchange control declaration (electronic 'Export Card' system / CCA form), bill of entry, and evidence of actual receipt in the authorised dealer account. Any non-arrival of export proceeds within the 180-day window requires a formal extension application supported by a renewed exporter contract and credible reason (e.g., insurance claim, arbitration).

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under delegated authority from the Minister of Finance pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and Exchange Control Regulations 1961; Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) provides AML/CTF oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise Business, MultiPass, Currencycloud via a partner bank) are not authorised dealers or banks for South African exchange-control purposes; a resident (Pty) Ltd cannot lawfully maintain an external account with such EMIs for operational liquidity without SARB approval. The EMIs may be used only as supplementary collection or disbursement rails where the ultimate settlement flows through a licensed South African authorised dealer (bank). The platform must be integrated via a local underlying bank or banking-as-a-service partner to ensure exchange control reporting and FICA obligations are satisfied. Standalone EMI accounts for receiving export proceeds will be refused unless the EMI is itself a regulated bank branch in South Africa with an FSB/FIC authorisation; the exchange control requirement remains repatriation of proceeds into the domestic FCA or conversion to rand within the statutory timelines.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Sanctions and geopolitical exposure through charter routes, overflight rights, fuel stops, or counterparties in OFAC/EU/UN-sanctioned jurisdictions.

Root cause: Sanctions and geopolitical exposure through charter routes, overflight rights, fuel stops, or counterparties in OFAC/EU/UN-sanctioned jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: A consolidated dossier containing: (i) counterparty due diligence for every charter operator and broker, including IATA/AEO or equivalent cross-border trade certifications; (ii) sanctions-screening evidence from an integrated aviation and trade database (e.g., LexisNexis, Dow Jones, OFAC SDN list, SARB/FinSurv circulars) verbally or via a signed compliance attestation; (iii) documents showing the entity's own sanctions-denial policy and escalation procedures; (iv) air charter contract clauses prohibiting routing through or business with sanctioned parties; and (v) an undertaking to submit an annual sanctions-compliance audit to the bank.
Beneficial ownership opacity, nominee shareholder structures, or high-risk trust beneficiaries obscuring the ultimate controllers of the (Pty) Ltd.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity, nominee shareholder structures, or high-risk trust beneficiaries obscuring the ultimate controllers of the (Pty) Ltd.

Defense evidence: A FIC-compliant beneficial ownership file comprising: CIPC company extracts and certified memorandum of incorporation; full share register with all share classes and nominee agreements; signed FIC beneficial ownership declaration (as per FIC Guidance Note 1/2021) identifying natural persons holding more than 25% or exercising ultimate control; copies of trust deed and trustee letters (if applicable); board resolution authorising account opening; certified passports or IDs and recent proof of residential address for all directors and UBOs; and if any shareholder is an offshore entity, the underlying chain of ownership documentation up to the natural person.
Trade-based money laundering red flags: under/over-invoicing of charter fees, fragmented payment routing, or inconsistent cargo documentation.

Root cause: Trade-based money laundering red flags: under/over-invoicing of charter fees, fragmented payment routing, or inconsistent cargo documentation.

Defense evidence: A transaction file containing the original charter agreement (e.g., ACMI charter, block space agreement) showing the negotiated rate; broker's signed commission invoice; independent market pricing references (IATA cargo rates, aviation fuel databases, insurer broker valuations); air waybill / bill of lading; customs export/import declarations and packing lists; matching inventory and weight certificates; and an internal transfer-pricing or valuation memo where the trade is with a related party. The bank will also expect a written rationalisation for any split payment or circuitous routing of funds.
Unexplained third-party payments, rapid refunds to non-contractual persons, or source-of-funds mismatches (e.g., client paying charter invoice from an unrelated offshore account).

Root cause: Unexplained third-party payments, rapid refunds to non-contractual persons, or source-of-funds mismatches (e.g., client paying charter invoice from an unrelated offshore account).

Defense evidence: A complete payment matrix showing each incoming/outgoing wire, the contractual payer/beneficiary, and the underlying charter/commission obligation; source-of-funds documentation (e.g., investor bank statement, signed facility agreement) for any third-party payer, plus a written collateral or subrogation agreement where applicable; a registered founder or intermediate party confirmation when the third-party funds are received on behalf of the charter client; internal 'Know Your Payee' procedure documents; and an escalation policy for unexpected refunds, including a checklist to ensure refunds replay the exact original funding instrument.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from a non-resident shareholder must be routed via a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for aggregated interbank) to an authorised dealer in South Africa. The beneficiary line must reflect the exact company name (ZA_PTY_LTD) and the reference narrative: 'Equity Subscription Agreement dated [date]' or 'Shareholder Loan Agreement dated [date]'. The receiving authorised dealer will screen the payment, credit the company's local currency (ZAR) account, and issue the inward Foreign Investments Record (Form C / electronic BALOF return) using the appropriate Balance of Payments functional code for inward direct equity. For amounts exceeding the authorised dealer's current discretionary threshold (circa ZAR 250 million per transaction), FinSurv no-objection must first be obtained. The required verification dossier includes: certified board resolution approving the allotment, signed share subscription / loan agreement, investor's source-of-funds statement, confirmation of non-resident status (e.g., Swiss or other residency certificate), and the company's SARS tax clearance status (e-TCS). Any existing foreign shareholding in excess of 50% of the issued capital should be disclosed to the bank prior to remittance to avoid an adverse AML classification.Outbound trade disbursements for charter payments, fuel, ground handling, insurance, and broker commissions must be executed via SWIFT MT103 using the authorised dealer's online platform. Each payment instruction must carry a SARB-valid outward payment reason code (e.g., 'Trade Services', 'Freight & Charter', 'Aviation Services') and be accompanied by an underlying invoice / agreement. For higher-value transactions (typically above ZAR 5 million), the bank may require additional evidence of trade completion, such as the executed charter agreement, air waybill, or the operator's invoice. Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700 / MT707) are used where the foreign beneficiary requires payment security, particularly for aircraft lease or fuel procurement; the bank will issue the LC against a completed application and underlying documentary contract. Documentary collections (SWIFT MT202/MT205) with attached transport and title documents are also permissible. All outbound payments must be matched to the entity's documented commercial purpose; any surplus or perceived overpayment must be refunded via re-credit to the original funding source, and all instruction records must be retained for a minimum of five years to satisfy exchange control audit requirements.Forward foreign exchange contracts (FX forwards) concluded with an authorised dealer in South Africa, with maximum tenor of 24 months, for hedging the rand/dollar exposure of trade and capital flows;Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only as offshore overlay hedges by non-resident counterparties or through the authorised dealer's London/New York desk when underlying trade settlement currency is illiquid; local resident entities may use NDFs in rare circumstances with SARB FinSurv approval, not as primary onshore hedging;Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) and plain-vanilla currency swaps, subject to authorised dealer approval and limited to the notional amount of an underlying commercial borrowing or receivable;Purchased currency options (put and call structures) on a cost-only premium basis, permissible without prior FinSurv approval provided the option is traded through an authorised dealer and the underlying exposure is bona fide trade or external loan exposure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A South African resident company that enters into 'affected transactions' with connected persons must prepare a Local File when the aggregate value of those affected transactions exceeds ZAR 5 million in that year of assessment. A Master File is required where the South African group or the multinational group has consolidated group revenue exceeding ZAR 10 billion. Country-by-Country reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations as a standalone statutory code, but economic substance is separately enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (s80A - s80L), the controlled foreign company rules (s9D), beneficial ownership clauses in double taxation agreements and the CFC attribution rules. An air cargo charter broker must demonstrate genuine South African commercial substance, including local board decisions, charter negotiation personnel, operational risk management, accounting records, bank accounts and physical office presence. A Pty Ltd used as a shell or conduit solely to obtain treaty benefits will be challenged by SARS under substance-over-form and treaty abuse doctrines.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Disclosure

Statutory sanction: An understatement penalty of up to 150% of the shortfall under s222 of the Tax Administration Act; administrative non-compliance penalties under s210; interest on underpaid tax; criminal prosecution under s235 TAA with fines up to ZAR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years for statutory offences; prosecution under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act may result in substantially higher fines and custodial sentences.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally participate in tax evasion face joint and several liability for unpaid withholding tax obligations under s11-12 TAA, civil claims for reckless trading under the Companies Act 71 of 2008, criminal liability leading to travel bans, asset seizures and imprisonment in serious cases.
Transfer Pricing and Connected-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust taxable income under s31 of the Income Tax Act and impose a transfer pricing adjustment plus interest; understatement penalties up to 150% of the additional tax assessed; record-keeping penalties and imprisonment for wilful destruction of relevant contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if they knowingly caused the company to fail to maintain transfer pricing records or mispriced affected transactions, resulting in an insolvent company; personal liability may also arise under s77 of the Companies Act and s12 of the TAA for tax debts.
Money Laundering and AML (FIC Act)

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 imposes administrative fines up to ZAR 10 million per contravention; criminal prosecution under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act may result in heavy fines and imprisonment; SARS and the FIC jointly investigate unexplained wealth; foreign-exchange control penalties may also apply for unauthorised cross-border flows.

Director personal liability: Accountable institution officers are personally exposed to FIC sanctions, including fines and personal criminal liability; directors can be banned from serving as directors; convictions for money laundering trigger travel restrictions, asset confiscation and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rules under s80A-L to cancel the tax effect of the arrangement, deny DTA benefits for lack of beneficial ownership, re-characterise the offshore flow as a dividend or royalty taxable in South Africa, and impose a 150% understatement penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure the company as a shell may be held personally liable under the Companies Act for reckless trading; they may be assessed for the company's unpaid tax if the shell structure was used to avoid withholding obligations; criminal prosecution for perjury or fraud may lead to travel bans and imprisonment.
