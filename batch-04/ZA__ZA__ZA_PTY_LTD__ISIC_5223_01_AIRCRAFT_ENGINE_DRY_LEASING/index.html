Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); online portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-resident company deriving South African-source income or making taxable supplies in South Africa must apply to SARS for an income tax reference number and, if a VAT vendor, for a VAT registration number. Non-resident companies typically file through an appointed South African tax representative or public officer; registration is completed via SARS eFiling by submitting the relevant entity registration form (e.g., VAT 101 for VAT and supporting corporate documents such as the certified Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum of Incorporation, and proof of foreign residency). SARS allocates a unique income tax reference number and VAT number upon successful registration; the process is digital-first with physical verification only in exceptional cases.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (a South African Proprietary Limited company is a resident company taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate; no default pass-through treatment) — A foreign-resident company deriving South African-source income or making taxable supplies in South Africa must apply to SARS for an income tax reference number and, if a VAT vendor, for a VAT registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a South African Proprietary Limited company is a resident company taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate; no default pass-through treatment)

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a 'check-the-box' or elective pass-through regime. Entity classification follows legal form under the Companies Act and the Income Tax Act. Controlled foreign company (CFC) rules in section 9D apply automatically to foreign subsidiaries of South African residents. Hybrid mismatch elections may be required or available under sections 23M and 23N of the Income Tax Act, and SARS must be notified where anti-hybrid rules recharacterise a deduction. Additionally, the VAT Act contains limited elections (e.g., voluntary VAT registration, certain enterprise unit elections) unrelated to entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory registration where the total value of taxable supplies exceeds R1 million in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration available from R50,000 per annum)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act applies to the supply of movable goods supplied to a non-resident and exported from South Africa within 60 days of the time of supply, provided the goods are exported in the same state as supplied and documentary proof is retained. For services, section 11(2)(l) applies to services supplied to a non-resident if the services are not directly in connection with land or movable property situated in the Republic at the time of supply and are not supplied for use in South Africa. For aircraft-engine dry leasing to a foreign lessee, the lease of an engine physically located outside South Africa will generally be zero-rated as a supply of services to a non-resident offshore asset, provided the lessee is a non-resident and the engine is not situated in South Africa at the time of supply. Full supporting evidence must demonstrate the non-resident status of the customer, the offshore use, and the actual export or offshore situs of the asset.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SARS SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of goods (or equivalent evidence for offshore service supply)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign-currency receipt
  • Signed lease agreement identifying the non-resident lessee and offshore asset location
  • Certificate of Origin (if required by destination customs)
  • SARS VAT 264 or export-invoice records showing zero-rating applied

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time electronic invoice clearance, fiscal stamping, or API-based e-invoicing clearance. Taxable vendors must issue compliant tax invoices within 21 days of supply under section 20 of the VAT Act. SARS has announced a voluntary Peppol-based e-invoicing framework (SARS eInvoicing) but it remains non-mandatory and does not require digital stamping or pre-clearance; auditable records and invoice data must be submitted on demand.

SARS conducts targeted audits of zero-rated export supplies, requiring exact-match documentation in terms of the VAT Act and Tax Administration Act. If zero-rating is wrongly claimed or supporting documents are fraudulent, SARS can raise additional assessments adjusting VAT output tax, impose understatement penalties up to 150% of the shortfall (depending on conduct), apply interest, and issue VAT clawback notices. Deliberate tax evasion or false declaration may trigger criminal prosecution with fines of up to R2 million or imprisonment of up to 10 years per offence. Exporters are also subject to exchange-control scrutiny by the South African Reserve Bank for late or non-repatriation of export proceeds beyond 180 days, which can result in administrative penalties and loss of entity-level export facilitation status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a controlled, non-fully convertible offshore currency. Current account transactions are substantially liberalised and may proceed through Authorised Dealers without SARB approval, provided documentary requirements are met. Capital account transactions remain subject to exchange control approval, either by general ruling or specific FinSurv authorisation. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders by way of dividends are allowed once the company has complied with SARS corporate tax and dividends tax obligations, proof is furnished via a valid SARS tax clearance/corporate status pin, and the remittance is made through an Authorised Dealer. Detailed hurdles for dry-leasing include proving the underlying lease invoices, demonstrating that the engine is physically deployed in eligible cross-border operations, and ensuring that the lease consideration is at arm's length. For any capital reduction, share buy-back, or repatriation of loan capital, a specific FinSurv approval or an Authorised Dealer's no-objection confirmation is generally required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds arising from dry-leasing or disposal of aircraft engines must be received in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the date of export/shipment, unless an extension is granted by FinSurv. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100%: all foreign currency export proceeds must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer and converted to rand, unless the South African exporter has obtained prior approval to retain and operate a Foreign Currency Account under the Exchange Control Rulings. Any outstanding export proceeds beyond the statutory timeline must be reported and justified, with non-compliance exposing the entity to exchange control penalties and possible criminal liability. Lease periods involving temporary exports of engines must be documented to distinguish lease rental receipts from capital equipment export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), operating through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (as amended), with delegated implementation by Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium as a supplementary payments channel; Not Viable as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not Authorised Dealers under South African law and cannot independently hold or convert ZAR, nor may they lawfully provide cross-border payment services into the South African banking system without a partnership with a licensed South African bank or an approved Authorised Dealer. Such EMIs may be used only as a narrow front-end instruction channel that ultimately settles via a FICA-compliant South African bank account. A full EMI account relationship is insufficient for purposes of SARB Exchange Control reporting, and may create unlicensed deposit-taking risks under the Banks Act, 1990.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed through an Authorised Dealer using SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the ZA Pty Ltd's tier-1 capital escrow account. The remittance instruction must clearly state the inward foreign investment purpose, reference the ordinary share subscription agreement or shareholders' loan agreement, and quote a correct Balance of Payments purpose code (typically BOP code 2110 for equity capital, or BOP code 2120 for inter-company/investment loans, as accepted by SARB). The South African entity must submit Form MP336(b)/Filing to the Authorised Dealer for non-resident share purchases, and the Authorised Dealer must verify that share issue documentation, tax clearance, and FICA/KYC records are complete. Loan capital injections require SARB FinSurv approval or reliance on an applicable general ruling; for equity ordinary shares, no SARB approval is required but the bank must record the transaction as an inward FDI. The escrowed funds can be released to the operational account only after receipt of a written advice or bank confirmation that exchange control reporting has been settled.Outbound trade disbursements for engine lease rentals, spare parts, maintenance reserves, and lessee refunds should be settled through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 correspondent banking payments via Authorised Dealers. For acquisition of high-value engines or contractual security structures, irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (L/Cs) issued by the South African bank and advised through a prime offshore correspondent bank are the recommended clearing rails, especially when the engine title documentation is flow-controlled against payment. All export payments must be supported by original lease invoices, SARS customs export declarations (for engines crossing the border), and exchange control forms (EX-1/ECC) as applicable. The company should maintain a group treasury calendar displaying monthly lease rental inflows, lessor debt-service outflows, and VAT mismatches, and all ZAR-denominated disbursements above a preset threshold must be released only after dual-approval by the CFO and account compliance officer.Forward exchange contracts (FX forwards) with a South African Authorised Dealer, with a real trade/lease-rental exposure and no speculative net open position.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) executed offshore or with international counterparties, subject to the company's documented hedging policy and SARB approval if propriety requires an underlying tangible economic exposure.Cross-currency swaps arranged through an Authorised Dealer for matched swap of fixed/floating USD and ZAR cash flows on the engine lease receivables and debt-service liabilities, supported by an approved treasury risk mandate.Currency options purchased (not written) for protection against adverse USD/ZAR movements in multi-year lease contracts, subject to FinSurv's prohibition on speculative OTC derivatives unless hedging genuine exposure.Money market basis swaps for rolling monthly conversion of USD lease collections into ZAR operating reserves under a pre-approved internal hedge line.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5 million in connected-person cross-border transactions for contemporaneous Local File preparation; Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting may be triggered for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of ZAR 10 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have standalone ESR; substance is enforced through s9D CFC attribution, s80A-L General Anti-Avoidance Rules, and permanent establishment concepts under double tax treaties. An aircraft-engine leasing company must maintain commercial substance through local board functions, employees, lease-management decisions, and records in South Africa; inadequate substance may cause income recharacterisation, treaty benefit denial, or shareholder attribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 50% to 75% of the tax shortfall plus interest at repo + 7%; criminal conviction can lead to fines of up to ZAR 10 million and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Tax Administration Act and common-law fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false tax returns or orchestrate sham transactions may be held personally liable for the company’s unpaid tax, receive departure prohibition orders, and face criminal prosecution.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may void the arrangement under s80A-L, disallow assessed losses, defer deductions, attribute rental income to the controlling foreign shareholder under s9D, and impose a 75% intentional-tax-evasion penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed jointly and severally for the resulting tax debt; travel bans and disqualification from directorship can follow where the company is found to be a sham or trading recklessly.
Money Laundering / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act and Prevention of Organised Crime Act, conviction may result in a fine up to ZAR 100 million and imprisonment up to 30 years; SARS and the NPA may also seize the aircraft engine and ancillary assets.

Director personal liability: Directors with actual or constructive knowledge of the money-flow scheme face personal criminal liability, asset forfeiture, and civil claims for damages by creditors.
Reckless Trading and WHT Collection Breach

Statutory sanction: The company is liable for the full unpaid withholding tax, plus a 75% understatement penalty and interest; under the Companies Act, a reckless-trading order may require directors to reimburse the company’s losses.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated in failing to pay taxes or WHT can be held personally liable for the tax debts, placed under a SARS travel ban, and imprisoned for up to 10 years in serious cases.
