Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); official website: https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register through SARS eFiling. Steps: (1) appoint a South African representative or fiscal agent; (2) complete non-resident company registration for income tax via eFiling, attaching certified incorporation documents, a South African physical address or agent address, and banking details; (3) once the compulsory VAT threshold is exceeded, complete the eFiling VAT registration (VAT101 or equivalent electronic application), including a declaration of taxable supplies; (4) SARS issues a separate income tax reference number and VAT/vendor number; (5) foreign suppliers of electronic services must complete the specific non-resident vendor registration pathway in eFiling and designate a South African fiscal representative for compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must register through SARS eFiling. Steps: (1) appoint a South African representative or fiscal agent; (2) complete non-resident company registration for income tax via eFiling, attaching certified incorporation documents, a South African physical address or agent address, and banking details; (3) once the compulsory VAT threshold is exceeded, complete the eFiling VAT registration (VAT101 or equivalent electronic application), including a declaration of taxable supplies; (4) SARS issues a separate income tax reference number and VAT/vendor number; (5) foreign suppliers of electronic services must complete the specific non-resident vendor registration pathway in eFiling and designate a South African fiscal representative for compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by legal form and residency. Elective mechanisms include VAT group registration (s.50 VAT Act) and the appointment of a representative vendor by a non-resident (s.23(4)). Hybrid mismatch rules (s.8F, s.8FA, s.23M) are mandatory anti-avoidance measures and cannot be waived or elected.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period is the compulsory VAT registration threshold for all taxable supplies, including foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African recipients. Voluntary VAT registration is available where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Vendors may zero-rate the supply of movable goods physically exported from South Africa to a non-resident where removal occurs within 90 days of the time of supply and documentary evidence is retained. Services supplied to a non-resident fall within s.11(2)(k) zero-rating unless used in South Africa; email/newsletter infrastructure and SaaS services supplied to offshore clients qualify when the recipient is non-resident and the service is not for use in a South African enterprise. All zero-rated supply claims require preservation of the export/evidence bundle for inspection.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in force. Electronic tax invoices are accepted if they satisfy the substantive VAT Act s.20 document requirements. SARS has announced and piloted Peppol-based e-invoicing interoperability, but there is no statutory API clearance or mandatory live-stamping obligation as of the current tax compliance framework.

SARS conducts focused audits of zero-rated exports, verifying customs clearance, transport documents, invoices, and foreign currency proceeds. Where substantiation fails, output VAT at 15% is assessed as a clawback, plus statutory interest (currently 10% per annum) and understatement penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Act. Fraudulent zero-rating or deliberate false documentation may trigger criminal prosecution, personal director liability, and retroactive assessment outside the ordinary 5-year prescription period where fraud is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is effectively convertible for current-account items, including software subscription revenue, cloud infrastructure fees, and cross-border SaaS disbursements, subject to the presentation of commercial invoices, a valid SARS tax clearance, and authorised-dealer documentation. Capital-account inflows are lawful: non-resident equity subscriptions are accepted without prior SARB authorisation provided the authorised dealer lodges a Form G and the inward remittance is declared in the Foreign Electronic Data Reporting (FED) system; non-resident shareholder loans up to ZAR 50 million per company benefit from the general Regulation 3(1) exemption, and borrowings above that threshold require prior FinSurv approval. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are legal upon compliance with the 20% Dividend Tax and SARS Notice of Liability procedures. There are no direct exchange restrictions on IT-services receipts, but practical frictions arise from documentary integrity, FICA account-opening standards, FED reporting, and the 180-day export-proceeds repatriation rule.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the export of digital services (bulk transactional email, newsletter hosting, and associated SaaS deliverables), the full invoice value in foreign currency must be received in South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days from the invoice date or the date of service completion, whichever is later. The funds must be surrendered to an authorised dealer and converted into ZAR at the prevailing market spot rate; the effective surrender ratio is 100% of the export consideration. The exporter may apply to the authorised dealer for a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) to retain export receipts temporarily, but retention beyond the 180-day window without a prior-approved FCA constitutes an exchange-control contravention. Service exports exceeding ZAR 50,000 must be recorded in the authorised dealer's FED service-export declaration with the applicable SA Balance of Payments service code; the commercial bank will require a copy of the export invoice and proof of delivery of the SaaS services.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv); prudential supervision complemented by the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA). Exchange control is administered in a decentralised manner through Authorised Dealers (ADs) pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations 1961, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers (as amended). Anti-money laundering and FICA duties are enforced under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FIC Act).

Low-Medium. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity subscription by the non-resident shareholder: remit via SWIFT MT103 using the company's authorised dealer as the receiving bank; beneficiary account is the ZA_PTY_LTD operational ZAR current account or the bank's designated Capital Escrow sub-account; payment reference should explicitly read 'ZAR Equity Subscription - Share Issue [date]'. The authorised dealer will require the Form G (issue of ordinary shares to non-resident), the board resolution approving the issuance, the share subscription agreement, and a source-of-funds declaration before crediting; settlement occurs at the dealing spot rate through the ZAR RTGS system (SAMOS). Upon credit, the bank issues an inward remittance certificate, which is the legal evidence of foreign direct investment and is required for future dividend remittances and disinvestment. For a shareholder loan below ZAR 50 million, the inbound MT103 must be referenced as 'Regulation 3(1) exemption shareholder loan'; the bank will require a signed loan agreement, the interest rate, and a loan repayment schedule, and will record the loan in the FED system.For routine SaaS supplier expenditures (AWS, Twilio, SendGrid, Snowflake, Datadog and supporting software licences), the primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 payment order initiated through the authorised dealer's electronic banking treasury module. The payment instruction must include the supplier's invoice number, a B2B reference, and the corresponding SA export/import code; the bank will debit the ZAR operational account, convert at spot or the agreed forward rate, and settle via the correspondent banking network. Current-account payments do not require prior SARB approval, but the bank mandates an invoice, a SARS tax clearance, and supporting proof of the underlying trade for transactions above the bank's de minimis threshold. A Commercial Letter of Credit (irrevocable, sight or term) is available for substantive technology procurement contracts and infrastructure purchases, but is generally unnecessary for subscription-based SaaS; where a supplier contractually requires it, the company can open an MT700 LC through the authorised dealer. Recurring monthly disbursements to the same supplier may be automated through a standing SWIFT mandate, which reduces operational friction and improves bank scoring.Forward Exchange Contract (FEC) — outright deliverable ZAR/USD and ZAR/EUR forwards on committed current-account receipts and disbursements, entered into with the authorised dealer under the Currency and Exchanges Manual; no prior FinSurv approval required within the corporate dealing mandate.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS) — permissible with an authorised-dealer counterparty to hedge multi-year hard-currency liabilities (e.g., a 3-year USD cloud-infrastructure commitment), subject to the bank's ISDA/CSA documentation and credit approval.Currency Option — permitted for hedging committed foreign-currency cash flows where the option is written by or arranged through the authorised dealer and the underlying exposure is evidenced.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) — NOT permissible for a resident South African entity on an onshore basis; offshore ZAR NDFs are outside the exchange-control perimeter and a resident company may only enter such contracts with prior FinSurv approval.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Company Registration Number Registration number issued by CIPC under the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Non-resident shareholders' details are recorded in the ITR14 declaration, not on this line. For the company, the local registration number is mandatory. Late filing penalties: an administrative penalty of up to ZAR 4,000 per month (capped at ZAR 16,000) for failure to file the annual return, plus a 10% understatement penalty for any declared amount that is understated, and interest on unpaid tax at the prescribed rate (currently 10.5% per annum).
Box 2 – Financial Year-End Date The date on which the company's annual financial statements are prepared. For a non-resident-owned company, the financial year-end is the date chosen by the directors, and must be consistent with the annual financial statements submitted to CIPC. Late filing penalties: an administrative penalty of up to ZAR 4,000 per month (capped at ZAR 16,000) for failure to file the annual return, plus a 10% understatement penalty for any declared amount that is understated, and interest on unpaid tax at the prescribed rate (currently 10.5% per annum).
Box 3 – Residency Status Indicate whether the company is a South African resident for income tax purposes (incorporated in South Africa or management and control exercised in South Africa). This field is critical for determining the scope of taxable income. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5,000,000

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applicable to companies. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under section 80A-80L of the Income Tax Act and section 31 (transfer pricing) empower SARS to disregard transactions lacking commercial substance. A shell company or an owner-managed entity with minimal substance and cross-border related-party transactions will be scrutinized and may have its deductions disallowed or its income recomputed at arm's length.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, a penalty of up to 200% of the understated tax is imposed, plus interest. Criminal prosecution may lead to a fine of up to ZAR 500,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the company to evade tax may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. SARS may issue travel bans and, under section 155 of the Tax Administration Act, directors may be barred from leaving South Africa. Imprisonment is a possible criminal sanction for directors personally involved in the evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply GAAR to disregard the entity or transaction, reallocate income and deductions, deny losses, and impose a 200% understatement penalty. In egregious cases, the company's registration may be deregistered under the Companies Act and the company dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors may be declared personally liable for the company's debts under the Companies Act if they knowingly allowed the company to engage in fraudulent or reckless conduct. Directors may also face a delinquency order, disqualification from acting as a director, and civil claim for restitution if the shell company structure is abused to evade taxes.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA), a company can face a fine up to ZAR 10 million per offense, and directors may be personally liable for fines up to ZAR 10 million or imprisonment for up to 15 years for repeated non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and criminally liable for failing to ensure the company has an effective AML compliance program. They may be subject to civil penalties, asset freezing orders, and criminal imprisonment. A senior manager or director who knowingly enables money laundering through the company's infrastructure may be prosecuted as a principal offender.
