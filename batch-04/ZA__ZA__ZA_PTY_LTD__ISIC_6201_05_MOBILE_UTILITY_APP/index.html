Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) via the SARS eFiling portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African company (Pty Ltd) must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), then register for income tax with SARS through eFiling or an authorised tax practitioner using Form IT77 and supporting documents (registration certificate, directors' particulars, registered address, and banking details). The SARS income tax reference number is issued immediately; if the company makes taxable supplies exceeding the VAT threshold, separate VAT vendor registration (Form VAT101/201) is mandatory.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation | resident company subject to South African corporate income tax on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders do not alter the entity-level taxation model; no pass-through eligibility for a Pty Ltd — A foreign-owned South African company (Pty Ltd) must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), then register for income tax with SARS through eFiling or an authorised tax practitioner using Form IT77 and supporting documents (registration certificate, directors' particulars, registered address, and banking details). The SARS income tax reference number is issued immediately; if the company makes taxable supplies exceeding the VAT threshold, separate VAT vendor registration (Form VAT101/201) is mandatory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | resident company subject to South African corporate income tax on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders do not alter the entity-level taxation model; no pass-through eligibility for a Pty Ltd

Available election forms

No check-the-box election under South African law; a Pty Ltd is a per se taxable corporation. Statutory elections available include voluntary VAT registration for vendors making taxable supplies above R50,000 per annum but below the compulsory threshold; the micro business turnover tax election in the Sixth Schedule (turnover not exceeding R1 million) in lieu of income tax; and intra-group section 45 transactions relief election for companies in the same group of companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration where taxable supplies exceed R1 million in a 12-month period; voluntary registration is available where taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in a 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) the supply of movable goods physically exported from South Africa by the vendor or on its behalf, under section 11(1)(a), provided the vendor obtains and retains acceptable proof of export; and (b) the supply of services to a non-resident person under section 11(2)(k) where the recipient is outside South Africa and the services are physically performed or used outside South Africa. The mobile utility app supply via app stores is zero-rated as an exported service only if the end-user recipient is located outside South Africa and the service is effectively consumed offshore.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) / SARS Customs clearances
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (non-negotiable or original)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of export (e.g., third-party freight or courier evidence and landing certificates)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt evidencing payment in foreign currency

South Africa does not impose a general real-time e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate. VAT vendors must issue tax invoices containing the prescribed legal particulars, and submit periodic VAT201 returns via eFiling. SARS has introduced electronic data integration and risk-based verification layers for invoices, but there is no statutory continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance for all vendors.

SARS rigorously audits zero-rated export claims and may disallow the zero rating where documentation is incomplete, leading to VAT re-assessed at 15% plus interest. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, an improper 'substantial understatement' penalty of 10% to 200% of the tax shortfall applies depending on the conduct and lack of reasonable care; deliberate ​​tax evasion or fraudulent export documentation can trigger criminal prosecution, civil penalties up to 200% of the evaded amount, and retrospective clawback of all VAT refunds claimed within a statutorily limited period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a free-floating rand under a dual current/capital account framework. Current account transactions — including cross-border software licensing, mobile app subscription receipts, and professional services — are generally unrestricted and remittable without prior SARB approval, subject to electronic exchange control reporting and SARS tax compliance. Capital account movements (non-resident equity injections, shareholder loans, foreign inward investments) are permitted under the recognised 'loop structure' whereby non-resident funds introduced into a domestic (Pty) Ltd as share capital or subordinated loans do not require individual exchange control approval, provided the funds originate outside South Africa and are reported through the receiving Authorized Dealer. Profit repatriation (dividends) is lawful after deduction of dividends tax and upon presentation of a valid SARS tax clearance/directive to the bank. Practical hurdles: the rand's emerging-market 'restricted' currency status requires underlying documentation for every ZAR/offshore conversion; all outward payments above prescribed thresholds require Form A2 reporting via the bank; and any transaction involving a non-resident connected party must satisfy both exchange control substance checks and transfer-pricing documentation to avoid SARS recharacterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the South African Exchange Control Regulations, export proceeds (including earnings from the export of software services, app-store licensing, and digital maintenance) must be fully repatriated to South Africa and disposed of within 180 days of the date of shipment or service rendering. There is no percentage surrender requirement imposed by SARB as of the current regime; the exporter must credit the convertible currency to an authorized dealer bank account and the bank will convert the proceeds to ZAR at the dealing rate unless the exporter holds a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account. All export receipts must be supported by the relevant export declaration documents, app-store payout certificates, and tax invoices, and the Authorized Dealer will complete the electronic export proceeds declaration for SARB. Failure to repatriate within the statutory 180-day timeline constitutes a contravention of the Exchange Control Regulations and attracts administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which delegates day-to-day exchange control administration to Authorized Dealers (tier-1 South African commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW (Restricted) — Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise Business, Currencycloud) are not licensed in South Africa as banks or Authorized Dealers and therefore cannot open local ZAR-denominated accounts, cannot intermediate SWIFT payments into the domestic system, and cannot complete the statutory SARB exchange control reporting. Their lawful utility is confined to being foreign collection accounts for offshore-originating app-store payouts (e.g., USD/EUR received from Apple and Google), with the proceeds subsequently routed to the domestic Authorized Dealer account and declared to FinSurv. Accordingly, an offshore EMI may be retained as a supplementary pay-in rail, but it cannot serve as the company's primary treasury account or the account of record for SARS and CIPC.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Dossier must include: SARS income tax number and Tax Compliance Status Pin for the entity; CIPC Beneficial Ownership Register extract; sworn declaration of beneficial ownership; notarized and apostilled passports of all non-resident directors/shareholders; proof of residential address; founding resolution appointing a local resident signatory; and a corporate structure chart evidencing the exchange control 'loop structure' arrangement for the inward share subscription.

Root cause: Digital-only revenue concentration from offshore App Stores (Apple IAP / Google Play Billing) with no conventional invoices and volatile micro-transactions.

Inward foreign capital injection from a holding company or investor seated in a low-tax / mixed jurisdiction without clear exchange control documentation.

Root cause: Inward foreign capital injection from a holding company or investor seated in a low-tax / mixed jurisdiction without clear exchange control documentation.

Defense evidence: Dossier must include: executed share subscription agreement or intercompany loan agreement; board resolution allotting shares; investor source-of-funds evidence (prior six months bank statements); complete group structure chart; SARS transfer-pricing file or exemption position; FinSurv loop-structure notification submitted through the Authorized Dealer; and an exchange control compliance declaration confirming no circumvention of SARB regulations.
Cross-border contractor disbursements to foreign software developers flagged as 'IP leakage / disguised dividend' red flags.

Root cause: Cross-border contractor disbursements to foreign software developers flagged as 'IP leakage / disguised dividend' red flags.

High-frequency in-app purchases creating sudden spikes in transaction count and total turnover that exceed the originally projected business profile.

Root cause: High-frequency in-app purchases creating sudden spikes in transaction count and total turnover that exceed the originally projected business profile.

Defense evidence: Dossier must include: projected and actual revenue waterfall by month; app-store consumer transaction records; audited or reviewed financial statements for the prior period; a documented transaction monitoring system; FICA risk assessment and onboarding risk-scoring output; and bank-covenanted commitments to file Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act where anomalous activity appears.
Unexplained third-party payments or crypto/OTC-derived funds landing on the corporate treasury account from non-App-Store sources.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity or loan funding must be transferred by SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent authenticated wire) directly from the investor's bank to the (Pty) Ltd's account held at a South African Authorized Dealer. The remittance instruction must carry a clear purpose/description: 'Capital Contribution – Share Subscription' for equity, or 'Shareholder Loan – Section 31 Companies Act compliant' for debt, and must be supported by the share allotment resolution and executed subscription agreement. The receiving bank will trigger the electronic Balance of Payments / Exchange Control declaration (capital flow); where the 'loop structure' applies, the inward transfer is recorded without individual SARB approval. ZAR conversion occurs at the Authorized Dealer's dealing spot rate on receipt unless the company holds an approved Foreign Currency Account. The company's post-issuance share records must be updated at CIPC and the Beneficial Ownership register must reflect the new non-resident shareholder; for a shareholder loan, the terms must respect arm's length pricing to avoid SARS transfer-pricing adjustments on interest.Outbound trade payables for software development, licenses, and cloud infrastructure are cleared via outward SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers executed by the Authorized Dealer bank, which files the mandatory electronic Form A2 outward payment declaration. The company provides each underlying invoice, service contract, or license agreement, and the SARB service code is applied (e.g., code 338 for computer/software services). Domestic ZAR liquidity and Rand clearing between South African banks settle through SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement), the SARB-operated RTGS system, ensuring same-day finality for local payments. Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) are not suited to intangible app development disbursements and are reserved for physical goods procurement; for software and IP, MT103/telegraphic transfer is the default compliant rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions for mandatory Local File documentation; ZAR 500 million aggregate for mandatory Master File documentation.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but enforces substance through transfer pricing rules under Section 31, the Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules, and anti-avoidance doctrine. A mobile utility app company with central management and control in South Africa, but assigning valuable software IP to a zero-tax foreign shell, will be repriced by SARS absent demonstrable business operations, strategic decisionmaking, personnel resources, and financial risk in the foreign entity. The COVID-19? No, this falls under SARS's 'substance over form' and section 31 re-characterisation powers.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act and Preferential Procurement Regulations? The specific sanction is a conviction for tax evasion with a fine of up to ZAR 2,000,000? More precisely: under the TAA, wilful non-disclosure or false declaration is an offence carrying imprisonment for up to 16 years, or a fine. Civil understatement penalty of up to 50% of the shortfall for intentional evasion; negligence penalties up to 30%; substantial understatement penalty of 20%. Interest runs on the full underpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or direct the concealment of revenue become personally jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax under the common law and the South African Companies Act. SARS may apply for a personal restitution order, asset attachment, travel ban, and criminal imprisonment of the directors involved.
Shell Company / Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the sham structure under common law substance-over-form principles, re-characterise the transactions under Section 31 (transfer pricing), determine taxable income on the actual economic activities performed in South Africa, disallow deductions claimed for purportedly unpaid royalties or service fees, and issue a formal assessment with understatement penalties of up to 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly establish or maintain artificial linked entities to defeat SARS can be ordered by court to pay all taxes evaded, be disqualified from holding directorships under the Companies Act, face prohibited departure orders, and be subject to criminal prosecution for fraud post the conclusion of the tax investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering / Record-Keeping Failure

Statutory sanction: Beyond tax penalties, this conduct may constitute an offence under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA implementation): the entity can be fined up to ZAR 10,000,000, and the responsible directors may receive a fine up to ZAR 1,500,000 or imprisonment for up to five years. SARS will also impose non-compliance penalties and criminal tax penalties if money laundering forms the predicate for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure proper record-keeping and transparency can be held personally liable for the financial loss suffered by the company or SARS. A court may impose civil restitution, restrict travel through an enforcement order, and, in egregious cases, imprisonment for up to five years under the FIC Act in addition to corporate tax liability.
