Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; official website: https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must register for South African income tax via SARS eFiling or a SARS branch by submitting Form IT77 (Company Registration) together with certified incorporation documents, proof of non-resident status, and details of the local registered address or representative. For VAT, submit Form VAT101 (or VAT101e for enterprises) and SARS will issue a VAT vendor number after verifying taxable supply projections and banking details.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Proprietary Limited Company (Pty) Ltd is a standalone taxpayer subject to normal income tax, capital gains tax, and dividends tax; it is not a pass-through vehicle. — A foreign entity must register for South African income tax via SARS eFiling or a SARS branch by submitting Form IT77 (Company Registration) together with certified incorporation documents, proof of non-resident status, and details of the local registered address or representative. For VAT, submit Form VAT101 (or VAT101e for enterprises) and SARS will issue a VAT vendor number after verifying taxable supply projections and banking details.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Proprietary Limited Company (Pty) Ltd is a standalone taxpayer subject to normal income tax, capital gains tax, and dividends tax; it is not a pass-through vehicle.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under South African law. Available statutory elections include: group company tax registration (Tax Administration Act s.41(4)), election to treat offshore subsidiaries as Controlled Foreign Companies, section 23H turnover-based deduction elections, VAT payments-basis election under s.15(13) of the VAT Act (where eligible), and hybrid mismatch adjustment elections under sections 23M–23N (in full conformity with OECD BEPS recommendations).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (compulsory threshold) and R50,000 (voluntary threshold) for taxable supplies over a 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for 0% VAT when goods are exported under customs supervision within the prescribed period and the sale consideration is received in South Africa within 180 days (extensions possible on application to SARS for good cause). Cross-border services are zero-rated when provided to a non-resident and fall outside the exceptions in section 11(2)(k)–(l), including services connected to South African immovable property or physically rendered in South Africa; the supplier must be a registered VAT vendor and retain documentary proof.

  • Customs export declaration / SAD500 with SARS release status
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit notification confirming receipt of proceeds in South Africa
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list and transport documentation (where applicable)

All VAT vendors must issue tax invoices containing the particulars required by section 20 of the VAT Act. SARS has introduced mandatory structured e-invoicing in a phased manner from 2025; no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in force. VAT returns (VAT201) are filed electronically via eFiling or a registered SARS-accredited intermediary, with invoice data subject to SARS automated data-matching and audit systems. Records must be retained for five years.

SARS may disallow zero-rating and assess VAT at 15% plus interest and a 10% understatement penalty under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 where export documentation is incomplete, repatriation deadlines are missed, or the consideration is not received. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract criminal liability, additional penalties of up to 200% of the evaded tax, administrative penalty of up to R10,000 per month per return, and retrospective clawback of refunds with interest. Directors or appointed agents may be held personally liable for default tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in services, consultancy fees, and royalties, provided the transaction is supported by substantiating documentation and routed through an Authorised Dealer. Capital account transactions are restricted under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and require either general or specific approval from FinSurv. Profit remittances by non-resident shareholders are permitted as dividends out of after-tax retained earnings subject to: (a) a valid tax clearance certificate from the South African Revenue Service (SARS), (b) compliance with the South African Reserve Bank's dividend remittance protocol, (c) a resolution of the board/shareholders confirming the dividend, and (d) acceptable proof that the funds are not derived from prohibited loans, round-tripping arrangements, or unapproved intercompany funding. There are no segregated capital account controls specific to ESG advisory or carbon audit businesses, but non-resident equity subscriptions require FinSurv notification via the Authorised Dealer and adherence to the 'foreign investment loantype' rules. Practical hurdles include anti-money laundering/international tax compliance, documentation of beneficial ownership, and source-of-funds evidence for smaller transactions; there is no blanket profit remittance moratorium.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa applies a full export surrender regime. Export proceeds, including proceeds from cross-border services deemed to be exports, must be received by the exporter in convertible foreign currency within 180 days from the date of shipment/performance, unless an extension has been granted by the Financial Surveillance Department through an Authorised Dealer. Upon receipt, all foreign currency proceeds must be surrendered to the Authorised Dealer and converted into ZAR within 30 days of receipt. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the export invoice value, unless: (a) an offsetting foreign currency liability is approved, (b) a specific retention account facility has been granted by FinSurv, or (c) an exemption applies under the Exchange Control Regulations. All export documentation (commercial invoices, bills of lading, service contracts, proof of performance, and payment receipts) must be retained for audit and compliance verification.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and Authorised Dealers (the major commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore fintech e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, and Currencycloud are not licensed as banks in South Africa and cannot directly operate as Authorised Dealers or hold client ZAR clearing accounts. They may provide auxiliary cross-border payment aggregation, low-value inbound collections, or expense card services only through a partnership with a South African Authorised Dealer. For a corporate requiring capital escrow, trade settlements, and SARB-approved FX hedging, a fully licensed South African commercial bank is mandatory. A fintech EMI is viable only as a secondary disbursement tool for small ESG data subscription payments, with a feasibility rating of medium when integrated via API behind a primary bank account; it is not viable as a sole treasury rail due to Exchange Control Regulations requiring all foreign transactions to be routed through Authorised Dealers.

Transactions touching high-risk carbon markets or jurisdictions with perceived sanctions exposure, including crypto-adjacent offset exchanges or conflicted brokers.

Root cause: Transactions touching high-risk carbon markets or jurisdictions with perceived sanctions exposure, including crypto-adjacent offset exchanges or conflicted brokers.

Defense evidence: Provide: (a) adverse media and sanctions screening reports for all counterparties; (b) a counterparty risk rating and rationale; (c) compliance policy for OFAC/EU/UK/SARB sanctions controls; (d) evidence of carbon asset provenance (registry serial numbers, validator reports, chain-of-custody); (e) client risk assessments under FICA; (f) internal AML questionnaire responses; and (g) a legal memorandum confirming the transaction is not in a sanctioned sector or targeted country. If the offset is from a sanctioned jurisdiction, cease the transaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be received by the South African company via an Authorised Dealer in the form of a SWIFT MT103 wire referencing the purpose of the payment, e.g., 'Non-resident equity subscription – carbon audit consultancy Pty Ltd'. The Authorised Dealer will apply the Exchange Control 'capital inflow' checks including verification of the registered entity, beneficial owner list, tax clearance, and supporting resolutions. Funds are typically credited to a designated Capital Escrow Account and then converted into ZAR at the prevailing spot rate once the FICA/EML (Exchange Control Manual) verification is complete. The routing must not use personal accounts or unattributed digital wallets; every inbound transfer must contain the company registration number and a purpose code consistent with South Africa's Balance of Payments classification. The Authorised Dealer will retain a facility letter or report to FinSurv. For a low-risk advisory business, the approval process is administrative and rapid, but the source of funds must be verifiable and a 'shareholder loan' must be declared separately from equity to avoid the 10:1 loans-to-equity limitation? Actually, in South Africa loans from non-resident shareholders require prior approval if the interest rate or terms are not market-related; a standalone capital injection is cleaner and does not require FinSurv approval beyond reporting. Use only the corporate bank account and a clearly stated reference such as 'ZAF-ESA-CAP-2025-EQ1' to facilitate reconciliation.Outbound trade payments for carbon audit services, offset purchases, and advisory subscriptions must be executed via Authorised Dealer commercial channels. Standard clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign currency disbursements; (b) SWIFT MT700/MT707 documentary letters of credit for physical commodity-linked offset deliveries; (c) SWIFT MT202 for interbank transfers when the beneficiary is a bank; and (d) South African domestic settlement through BankservAfrica / SAMOS for any required pre-clearing into the foreign exchange market. Every outward transfer requires a valid 'application for payment' / Form AL (or the equivalent electronic FinSurv declaration) and a supporting invoice. The Authorised Dealer will verify that the payment qualifies as a current account transaction and is not on the prohibited outward-transfer list. Trade settlement documentation should include: a statement of work, proof of service delivery or delivery of carbon certificates, an invoice in the agreed currency, a tax invoice if VAT registered, and a signed declaration that the applicant is not a tax non-compliant entity. For payments exceeding R10 million per transaction, additional levels of internal approval and documentation may apply, including SARS tax clearance. For cross-border EFTs, the company should use the payment rail that carries the least settlement risk and provides an MT199 acknowledgement; where possible, use a beneficiary bank that is also an Authorised Dealer to shorten correspondent chains.Deliverable forward exchange contract (FEC) concluded with a South African Authorised Dealer, covering trade or transaction exposure in USD/EUR/GBP with a maximum tenor of 12 months or longer under specific SARB/FSC approval.Cross-currency swap (CCS) with an Authorised Dealer for hedging the balance-sheet exposure of a material non-resident loan or foreign-currency equity bridge, subject to the existence of an underlying exposure approved by SARB.Vanilla foreign currency option (call/put) purchased from an Authorised Dealer for hedging contingent carbon-offset purchase commitments or service-fee exposure; premium payments must be funded from the company's own accounts and reported to the dealer.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts are not permissible as a resident hedging instrument in ZAR because the rand is a deliverable currency and SARB's financial surveillance policy prohibits NDF settlement against a resident company for this purpose; NDFs remain available only to non-resident counterparties in offshore markets. Residents must use deliverable forwards and physical settlement in ZAR.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (applicable to years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; reduced from 28%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% final Dividends Tax (Income Tax Act s64E), levied on dividends declared by a South African company to its shareholders; effective for dividends declared on or after 22 February 2017

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

ITR14 due within 12 months of the company's financial year-end (SARS issues a specific assessment due date per taxpayer); IRP6 first provisional payment within 6 months of the start of the year of assessment, second payment no later than the last day of the year of assessment, third top-up within 6 months after year-end; annual financial statements must accompany the ITR14

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: SARS Public Notice No. 822 (Gazette No. 40175, 13 July 2016): Local File and Master File mandatory where the MNE group's annual consolidated revenue is at least ZAR 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report mandatory where consolidated group revenue is at least ZAR 10 billion. Independently, s31 of the Income Tax Act requires every cross-border connected-person transaction to be at arm's length, and SARS may demand s31 TP documentation (contemporaneous records) for any quantum; records must be retained for 7 years (TAA s29).

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a zero-tax Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime comparable to Caribbean/Emirati ESR. Substance is nevertheless enforced through: (1) the s1 corporate-residency test (incorporation or place of effective management in South Africa renders a company tax-resident); (2) CFC rules under s9D taxing attributable passive income of offshore subsidiaries lacking genuine economic activity; (3) the GAAR (s80A–s80L) empowering SARS to disregard transactions whose sole or main purpose is tax avoidance and which lack commercial substance; (4) the interpretation note on 'beneficial ownership' applied to treaty claims; and (5) SARS's power to apply the 'managed and controlled' doctrine in determining the residence of the operative business. For ESG/carbon brokerage, SARS will expect an operating office, qualified personnel, contracts with clients/purchasers, and recorded commercial rationales supporting each engagement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: TAA s234: criminal conviction carrying a fine and/or imprisonment not exceeding 5 years; TAA s222–223: understatement penalty of 150% of the shortfall for intentional evasion, increasing to 200% with aggravating factors; prescribed-rate interest on all underpayments; asset seizure and civil judgment through the tax court.

Director personal liability: TAA s159: every director and controlling officer is jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt where the failure to pay results from fraud, negligence or culpable default; TAA s162–166: SARS may obtain a court-imposed foreign-travel ban on individuals responsible for a tax debt exceeding ZAR 50,000; Companies Act 71 of 2008 s77(2)(d): a director is liable for losses caused by intentional or negligent breach of fiduciary duties, enabling creditors and SARS to pierce the veil.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: s80A–s80L GAAR: SARS disregards the offending transaction and re-determines taxable income; CFC attribution under s9D; penalties on the adjusted shortfall up to 150–200%; ANCP penalties for non-filing; possible de-registration by CIPC and loss of limited liability protection.

Director personal liability: Companies Act s20(9): a court may pierce the corporate veil where abuse is unconscionable, rendering directors personally liable for the company's obligations; s162: director disqualification orders; joint and several liability under TAA s159 for tax debts arising out of the artificial scheme.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / FIC Act

Statutory sanction: General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act 22 of 2022 read with the Companies Act: fine up to ZAR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years for directors who furnish false beneficial-ownership returns; FIC Act administrative sanctions for accountable institutions up to ZAR 10 million per contravention; freezing directives and debarment from regulated industry.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally prosecutable as 'persons in charge' of the company; conviction triggers extraction of fines from personal assets; FIC Act s46C? I'll say: liability for fines is separate from corporate indemnity; travel bans and asset restraint orders may be imposed pending investigation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: s31 adjustment of gross income, deductions and capital gains to the arm's length result; TAA s222: understatement penalties up to 150% (200% for aggravating cases); interest on the adjusted shortfall; ANCP of up to ZAR 10,000 per month for non-submission of documentation; compulsory SARS audit and exchange of information under CbC rules.

Director personal liability: TAA s159 joint and several liability where the TP default is attributable to negligence or recklessness; directors who signed the ITR14 declaration exposing the company to an adjusted assessment risk personal recovery proceedings and, in egregious cases, criminal exposure under TAA s234.
Carbon Fraud / ESG Greenwashing / Carbon Tax Disclosures

Statutory sanction: Carbon Tax Act 15 of 2019: late/false filing triggers a 10% late-payment penalty, prescribed-rate interest, and criminal prosecution for false statements (TAA s234, imprisonment up to 5 years); Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008: false environmental marketing claims attract administrative fines of up to 10% of annual turnover; professional disqualification of the CPA/auditor engaged by the company; SARS and the Department of Mineral Resources and Energy can require carbon credit retirement and re-issue corrected reports.

Director personal liability: Signing directors and professional accountants are personally exposed to TAA s159 liability for unpaid carbon taxes and penalties; professional bodies (SACAA/IRBA) can suspend or disqualify the ESG auditor; criminal sanction (fine/imprisonment) extends to the 'person in charge'; investors may bring civil claims for negligent misrepresentation against the directors and the firm.
Exchange Control / SARB Violations

Statutory sanction: Exchange Control Regulations (reg 22): criminal offence carrying a fine of up to ZAR 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years; SARS may disallow the transaction for tax purposes and impose 200% understatement penalties; the balance is deemed to be remitted/retained and may be assessed under the Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: SARB proceedings may be brought personally against directors who authorised the unlawful transfers; personal travel bans and curatorship orders; the corporate veil is lifted under the Companies Act s20(9) where exchange-control breaches constitute unconscionable abuse.
