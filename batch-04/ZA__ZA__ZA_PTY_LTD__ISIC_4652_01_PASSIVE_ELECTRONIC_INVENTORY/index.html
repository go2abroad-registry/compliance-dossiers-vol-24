Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity wishing to conduct taxable supplies in South Africa must first apply for an income tax reference number via SARS eFiling using the prescribed registration forms and supporting corporate documents (certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation, directors' identification, and bank confirmation of the foreign jurisdictional address). Where the entity conducts an enterprise with taxable supplies exceeding the compulsory VAT threshold, a separate VAT registration application (form VAT 101) must be submitted within 21 business days from the end of the month in which the threshold was exceeded. SARS may require a local representative taxpayer or registered address for service of process. The eFiling platform issues a legal TIN/VAT reference number upon verification, after which the entity is capable of invoicing and customs declaration.

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a US-style check-the-box election. The (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a separate legal personality under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Available elections affecting tax identity include: (1) VAT group registration election under section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991; (2) income tax R&D incentive pre-approval in terms of section 11D; (3) specified hybrid mismatch elections and disclosures under sections 8E through 8L of the Income Tax Act; (4) application for an advance pricing agreement in respect of cross-border transactions; and (5) explicit deemed supply election on specific VAT transactions where provided by the VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of movable goods exported by the supplier or a direct agent if the goods are consigned from South Africa to a non-resident destination (section 11(1)(a), VAT Act). For services, zero-rating applies to services supplied to non-residents where the services are not used or consumed in South Africa (section 11(2)(k)). A passive electronic components distributor can claim zero-rating on the vehicle of export when the goods are removed from the Republic and supported by documentary proof such as a customs export declaration, transport documents (bill of lading or air waybill), commercial and packing documentation, and proof that payment for the goods is being received from a non-resident.

  • SARS Customs export declaration (SAD500 or electronic equivalent lodged in the ACE environment)
  • Bill of lading (sea freight) or air waybill (air freight) evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Inward foreign remittance certificate or bank statement proving settlement in convertible foreign currency
  • Proof of non-resident recipient identity where required by SARS

SARS permits electronic tax invoices that comply with section 20 of the VAT Act, including EDI-based invoices and full electronic VAT invoices with advanced digital signatures. South Africa does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital-stamping infrastructure comparable to Italy or Mexico, but SARS operates the eFiling portal and the Customs ACE (Advance Clearing Environment) for electronic customs lodgements. All VAT vendors must issue valid tax invoices within 21 days of the supply, and SARS reserves the right to require electronic invoice data in the format published on its digital tax compliance platform. There is no API-only clearance mandate for ordinary VAT invoices, but customs export declarations are processed electronically through SARS Customs.

SARS applies strict documentary verification to zero-rated exports. False or unsupported zero-rating claims trigger additional assessments under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties of up to 200% of the tax shortfall where deliberate conduct is proven, and interest on the unpaid VAT. In severe cases, criminal prosecution may be initiated under section 58 of the VAT Act for false statements; senior SARS personnel may impose a prescribed penalty of 100% of the VAT concerned and require reopening of previous refund periods. Export proceeds received after 180 days without prior SARS approval may cause the deemed supply to be undone and VAT liabilities to be clawed back, including under the anti-avoidance rules in the VAT Act and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a free-floating exchange rate arrangement with full current-account convertibility for trade and service payments, subject to documentary verification and AML/KYC due diligence by Authorised Dealers. Capital account transactions are subject to exchange-control reporting and, where applicable, prior approval by FinSurv or an Authorised Dealer. Direct foreign investment into a (Pty) Ltd is permitted on a notification basis, but profit remittances (dividends) require a valid company declaration, tax clearance status issued by SARS, and compliance with the Companies Act 2008. Remittance hurdles include anti-money laundering provenance checks for the source of funds, transfer pricing barriers under s.31 of the Income Tax Act, and withholding tax on dividends at 20% under s.64F, subject to treaty relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods must be received in South Africa within 30 days from the date of export (shipment), unless FinSurv has granted a longer period. Upon receipt, the foreign currency must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer (converted into ZAR) without delay, unless the exporter has been permitted to hold proceeds in an approved Foreign Currency Account or to offset permissible foreign-currency obligations. The statutory conversion requirement effectively expresses a 100% surrender obligation; non-compliance is subject to administrative penalties and exchange-control enforcement, and each exporter must retain export documentation (customs bill of entry, invoice, proof of shipment) for verification.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which serves as South Africa's exchange control authority and regulator of capital movement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., UK or EU electronic money institution) is not an Authorised Dealer for South African exchange control purposes and cannot process ZAR settlement through the SAMOS clearing system or issue SWIFT instructions in the required format to meet SARB's surrender requirements. Legal constraints include the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA), the Banks Act 94 of 1990, the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998, and the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual. Such EMIs may be used only as auxiliary non-ZAR payment channels and cannot replace the mandatory clearing rails operated by a licensed South African Authorised Dealer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign share capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident holding bank to the target (Pty) Ltd's Authorised Dealer-designated capital account, using the inward direct investment purpose code applicable to equity subscriptions. The deposit must be accompanied by (i) a board resolution authorising share issuance, (ii) a completed inward investment notification form as required by the specific Authorised Dealer, (iii) the company's registration documents and FICA file, and (iv) evidence that the foreign bank account is held by the non-resident shareholder. FinSurv receives the transaction through the Authorised Dealer's electronic reporting; for amounts exceeding the Authorised Dealer's discretionary threshold, a FinSurv clearance certificate or non-resident confirmation may be required before release of funds into the operational account. The transfer must indicate 'SA capital injection for ordinary shares — no securities exchange control code' to ensure correct classification.Outbound payments for imported electronic components are cleared via SWIFT MT103 if made by telex transfer, or by documentary collection/letter of credit. For a commercial LC, the South African bank issues an irrevocable LC in favour of the seller through SWIFT MT700, and reimbursement is effected by MT103/MT202 from the issuing bank to the beneficiary bank. ZAR-side settlement occurs through the South African Multiple Option Settlement system (SAMOS) in accordance with national clearing rules, while forex transactions are subject to the exchange control surrender and reporting regime. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 50 million in aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions in a year of assessment triggers the mandatory preparation of a South African Local File; a consolidated group revenue of ZAR 10 billion or more requires the OECD Master File and Country-by-Country Report submission.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations code equivalent to the UAE regime; substance principles are embedded in s.31(2) of the Income Tax Act (arm's length consideration for each affected transaction), the general anti-avoidance rules of s.80A-80L, and the place-of-effective-management tie-breaker in double-taxation agreements. A local Pty Ltd that merely holds legal title to passive electronic inventory while procurement, warehousing, logistics, and credit risk are all managed by a foreign affiliate will be treated as lacking economic substance and commercial rationale, exposing the arrangement to full recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under s.235 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; conviction carries a fine or imprisonment up to five years, or both; the National Prosecuting Authority may additionally apply for asset forfeiture under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or caused the filing of a false return are jointly and severally liable with the company under s.250 of the Tax Administration Act; SARS may obtain a travel-ban interdict under s.194 where tax remains outstanding; a criminal conviction disqualifies the director from holding office under the Companies Act 71 of 2008.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment under s.31 of the Income Tax Act recomputing the taxable income to the arm's length result; understatement penalty under s.222 of 50% (no reasonable grounds) to 150% (intentional misconduct); interest runs from the original due date until payment.

Director personal liability: The financial director and any board member who approved the pricing are personally exposed under s.250 of the Tax Administration Act where the company is insolvent and the tax debt was incurred through negligence; civil claims by SARS may be pursued in insolvency proceedings and travel bans may be imposed pending settlement.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation or disregarding of the arrangement under s.80A-80L of the Income Tax Act; deemed reconstruction of the commercial nature of the transaction; understatement penalty of 150% on the tax shortfall; the arrangement may additionally be treated as circumventing the dividends withholding tax and interest withholding tax statutory base.

Director personal liability: Directors may be sued fraudulently for misrepresentation by creditors and held personally liable for the company's tax debts under s.250 of the Tax Administration Act; disqualification orders can be issued under the Companies Act; travel interdiction and extradition may follow in cases involving attendant money-laundering.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 of up to ZAR 10 million per failure; the Financial Intelligence Centre may issue a directive to terminate the business relationship; conviction for intentional failure to submit a suspicious-transaction report carries imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to implement FICA reporting and compliance programmes are liable to criminal prosecution individually; civil restitution may be claimed jointly and severally from the board where the company's systems were deliberately inadequate; prohibition orders can bar the directors from holding positions in regulated industries.
