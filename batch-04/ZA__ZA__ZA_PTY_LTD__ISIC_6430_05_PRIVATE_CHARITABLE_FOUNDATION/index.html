E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections equivalent to US or OECD rules exist; however, a proprietary limited company may elect to apply for SARS approval as a Public Benefit Organisation (PBO) under section 30 of the Income Tax Act, elect to be an approved section 18A recipient for donation deductibility, elect voluntary VAT registration under section 15A of the VAT Act, and within certain limits elect to be treated as an institution exempt from income tax under section 10(1)(cN) if the charitable endowment activities qualify.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act applies to goods exported from South Africa to non-SACU destinations, provided the exporter retains proof of export; certain services supplied to non-residents, export-related services, and goods subject to outward processing may also qualify, but the charitable foundation must prove that goods or services left South Africa and that the consideration was received in foreign currency or as otherwise approved.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proving receipt in foreign exchange)

SARS mandates electronic filing via eFiling for VAT201 returns and requires that all VAT invoices contain prescribed particulars under the VAT Act; although there is no real-time central fiscal stamping or API-driven pre-clearance of every invoice, SARS uses integrated digital data audits, may require electronically generated tax invoices, and mandates that certain suppliers issue e-invoices via SARS-approved interoperability platforms where applicable.

SARS may impose understatement penalties ranging from 10% to 200% of the tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Act, criminal prosecution for fraudulent zero-rating under the VAT Act and Customs and Excise Act, retrospective VAT claw-backs where export evidence is deficient, and an additional 100% penalty for non-declaration; directors and controlling officers may be held personally liable for VAT fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is a managed-float currency with no dual-rate mechanism. Current account transactions are freely convertible through an Authorised Dealer subject to documentary supporting evidence, including SARS tax clearance certificates for outward payments of dividends, royalties, interest, and certain service fees. Capital account convertibility is restricted: inward foreign direct investment by non-residents into a South African (Pty) Ltd requires no prior SARB approval but mandates full reporting to the Authorised Dealer for Balance of Payments (BOP) statistics and capital endorsement. Outward capital flows, disinvestments, and foreign loan repayments require FinSurv approval or the Authorised Dealer's delegated authority. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are lawful under the CEM: the bank requires an executed Form A, an audited dividend certificate, board resolution, and valid SARS tax clearance; the Dividends Tax (currently 20%) is withheld by the company and remitted to SARS. No separate profit-repatriation tax is imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CEM general export rules: the full gross export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment, documented via the bill of entry and proof of receipt. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio; South African exporters are not required to convert a percentage of proceeds into ZAR. The exporter must bring the full proceeds onshore (repatriation obligation), but may credit the proceeds to an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer. Any FCA balance not required for approved local or foreign expenditure must be liquidated into ZAR on the Authorised Dealer's instruction. Non-compliance triggers FinSurv penalties, including potential denial of future transaction approvals.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), and the Currency and Exchanges Manual (CEM), implemented exclusively via Authorised Dealers (registered commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury rails. South African law requires cross-border payments to be cleared through an Authorised Dealer under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 and the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or MultiPass cannot hold the foundation's capital escrow balance nor act as the exclusive settlement bank without a local Authorised Dealer partner. An offshore EMI may be used only as a low-value, ancillary inbound donor collection channel, with immediate onward settlement to a SARB-regulated South African bank account. The IFWG regulatory sandbox and the pending Conduct of Financial Institutions (COFI) Bill may enable future digital banking licensing, but current compliance viability for a charitable endowment entity is marginal and AML risk-weighted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders or endowment donors remit funds via SWIFT MT103 to the (Pty) Ltd's ZAR account (or registered Foreign Currency Account) at a South African Authorised Dealer. The bank requires the following package at the point of credit: (i) notarised share subscription or endowment grant agreement; (ii) board resolution approving the inward capital injection; (iii) certificate of incorporation and CIPC extract; (iv) source-of-funds/wealth evidence for each remitter (bank statements covering 6 months, tax returns, or audited financial statements); (v) beneficial ownership register for any shareholder with a 25%+ interest. The Authorised Dealer will capture the transaction in SARB Balance of Payments (BOP) reporting using the Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Capital classification. The bank issues an inward transfer notification, the company records it as foreign-liability exposure, and no prior FinSurv approval is required for equity injections by non-residents. The purpose code in the MT103 must reference 'equity capital contribution – charitable endowment foundation' to avoid mismatch with current-account reporting.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 executed through the Authorised Dealer for grant disbursements and operational payments to beneficiaries outside the Common Monetary Area (CMA). A Form A (Application for Transfer of Funds Abroad) must be submitted with: the grant agreement, board resolution of the grant committee, evidence of beneficiary due diligence, and a valid SARS tax clearance. Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) are permissible but practically unnecessary for charitable grant distribution; they are recommended only when the foundation procures goods/services from foreign suppliers under ISIC 6430 project execution. Payments inside the Common Monetary Area (Lesotho, Namibia, Eswatini) are free of exchange-control permit requirements and can be paid by electronic transfer without Form A. All outbound disbursements above SAR 5 million require FinSurv/Authorised Dealer special project approval with a 5-year cash-flow forecast.Onshore deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) with a licensed Authorised Dealer, strictly limited to genuine underlying exposures such as USD/EUR/GBP-denominated endowment receipts and committed grant disbursements.Cross-currency swaps (ZAR vs hard currency) executed through an Authorised Dealer for asset-liability matching of the endowment portfolio, subject to FinSurv sign-off where the notional or tenor exceeds the Authorised Dealer's delegated limit.Plain-vanilla over-the-counter FX options and currency collars purchased from an Authorised Dealer on a cost basis, requiring the same underlying exposure documentation as forward contracts.Offshore ZAR non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not available to resident corporates in the onshore market; they may be accessed by non-resident counterparties only, and resident foundations must use deliverable instruments onshore.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Gross income and South African-source turnover, including endowment revenue, investment returns, grant management fees, and interest. A non-resident company with no South African permanent establishment (PE) reports only income from South African sources (e.g., immovable property or services rendered in South Africa). Foreign-sourced income is excluded unless attributable to a SA PE. Late filing or non-filing attracts an administrative non-compliance penalty under sections 210 and 211 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, typically up to ZAR 4,500 per month for a continuing failure (capped at 24 months). Box 2 Exempt income line for PBO-approved receipts and qualifying public benefit grant distributions; deductible s18A donations, investment expenses, and operating expenses. Non-residents may only claim deductions to the extent expenses relate to South African-source income or a SA PE; PBO exemption is not available for non-resident companies. Late filing or non-filing attracts an administrative non-compliance penalty under sections 210 and 211 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, typically up to ZAR 4,500 per month for a continuing failure (capped at 24 months). Box 3 Assessed loss brought forward and adjusted taxable income after deductions; computation of the 80% assessed-loss offset limit. Understatement penalties under section 222 range from 10% to 150% of the shortfall, with interest accruing at the prescribed rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Master File and Local File must be maintained where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion, and South African cross-border related-party transactions exceed ZAR 100 million for financial assistance, ZAR 75 million for intellectual property, ZAR 50 million for goods, or ZAR 50 million for services.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not separately impose Economic Substance Regulations akin to offshore jurisdictions; however, the Income Tax Act, Tax Administration Act, and GAAR under s103 require transactions to carry real commercial substance. For a charitable endowment (Pty) Ltd, ongoing local governance, grant approval records, actual grant disbursements, independent asset management authority, and a verified local bank account are required; substance deficiencies can lead to recharacterisation or denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

PBO Exemption Abuse / Charitable Facade

Statutory sanction: Retrospective withdrawal of PBO approval by SARS; all historical exempt income taxed at 27%; understatement penalty under s222 of 10-150% of the additional assessment; interest payable from the original due date; criminal sanction under s235 TAA for unauthorised PBO distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for tax debts if they knowingly controlled and directed the abusive scheme; courts may impose civil restitution, travel bans restricting departure from South Africa, and imprisonment of up to 10 years for fraud or intentional tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment by SARS under s31 of the Income Tax Act, plus secondary adjustment treating the resulting amount as a deemed dividend; understatement penalty of 25-150% of the adjustment amount, interest, and possible administrative penalties under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or provide false TP documentation may be personally liable under s159 TAA where the company is insolvent; courts may impose personal assessment, civil restitution, travel restrictions, or imprisonment for tax evasion.
Treaty Shopping / Shell Shareholding

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief under the principal purpose test (BEPS) and the South African GAAR; 20% dividends tax or 15% interest/royalty withholding tax recouped from the company as agent; understatement penalty of 50-150% for the failure to disclose full beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate the purely artificial cross-border arrangement are personally exposed for unpaid withholding taxes; penalties may include director disqualification, travel bans, surcharge on unpaid tax, and disqualification from future directorships.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance and False Information

Statutory sanction: The company, as withholding agent, is jointly and severally liable for the unwithheld 20% dividends tax or 15% interest/royalty tax, plus understatement penalty, prescribed interest, and criminal fine or imprisonment under s235 and s236 TAA.

Director personal liability: Controlling directors can be personally liable for the entire unpaid liability; SARS may impose a travel ban under s163 TAA, attach personal assets, and pursue criminal conviction leading to imprisonment of up to 5 years for failure to comply with withholding obligations.
