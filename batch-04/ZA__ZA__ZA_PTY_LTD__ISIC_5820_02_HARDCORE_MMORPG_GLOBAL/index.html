Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A South African (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxpayer taxed on worldwide income at the current corporate income tax rate of 27% (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); residence is determined by incorporation or effective management in South Africa. No pass-through treatment is available for a (Pty) Ltd.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; legal form is determinative and a (Pty) Ltd is a company per se. Available elections and mandatory regimes include: (a) Small Business Corporation status under Section 12E (progressive corporate rates where all shareholders are natural persons and gross income does not exceed the statutory cap); (b) Section 11D research and development super-deduction (150% expenditure deduction, subject to advance SARS approval); (c) voluntary VAT grouping under Section 50 of the VAT Act for closely linked persons; (d) voluntary VAT registration at the ZAR 50,000 threshold under Section 23(3) of the VAT Act; (e) hybrid debt and hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 8E, 8F, 8FA, and 23M–23N are mandatory and may not be elected out of; (f) controlled foreign company rules under Section 9D apply where the (Pty) Ltd holds interests in non-South African subsidiaries, with certain active-business exemptions claimable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory registration for taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or where expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in the next 12 months); voluntary registration threshold is ZAR 50,000 (Section 23(3), VAT Act). Foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African residents are required to register for VAT irrespective of turnover with effect from 1 April 2019.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported services applies under Section 11(2)(k) of the VAT Act where services are supplied to a non-resident who is not present in South Africa at the time the services are rendered, and under Section 11(2)(l) where services are physically rendered wholly or partly outside South Africa; in both cases the supply must not be directly connected with land or movable property situated in South Africa. For the hardcore MMORPG global model, in-app purchases, subscriptions, and virtual access supplied to non-resident end-users and/or remitted through Apple App Store or Google Play (as non-resident sellers of record or intermediaries) qualify for zero-rating, whereas supplies to South African resident end-users attract VAT at 15%. The supplier must obtain and retain sufficient documentary evidence of the non-resident status of the recipient and offshore enjoyment of the service; inadequate substantiation results in VAT at 15% being assessed on the supply.

  • Executed service or distribution agreement with the non-resident app store operator (Apple App Store / Google Play) or direct non-resident end-customer contract
  • Section 20(4)-compliant VAT invoice issued by the (Pty) Ltd clearly indicating the zero-rated nature of the supply
  • Foreign-currency inward remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103 or Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an authorised dealer bank; where consolidated platform payouts are received, the app store payout statement serves as the supporting reconciliation
  • App store payout statements and platform transaction-level analytics evidencing purchaser country, user ID, and supply to non-residents
  • Residency evidence of the counterparty where available (e.g., foreign VAT registration number, certificate of residence, or public company registry extract)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance authorisation regime currently applies. SARS has adopted a phased e-invoicing framework aligned with the PEPPOL 4-corner model (pilot commenced August 2023; mandatory adoption is being phased by taxpayer segment and is not yet universally effective). Vendors must issue Section 20(4)-compliant VAT invoices (full tax invoice where consideration exceeds ZAR 5,000; abridged invoice at or below ZAR 5,000) and file VAT200/VAT201 returns electronically via eFiling or SARS-certified API solutions. SARS performs risk-based data matching, including cross-matching app store payout data, banking transactions, and VAT returns, but does not require an invoice-level clearance code before issuance.

SARS operates a dedicated VAT Business Unit and a cross-border digital economy audit programme that cross-matches app store payout data, bank statements, and VAT201 filings. If zero-rating is unsupported, misapplied, or accompanied by fraudulent documentation: (i) the full 15% VAT is retrospectively clawed back under an additional assessment within the extended prescription period of 5 years, or 10 years for material non-disclosure (Section 99, Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011); (ii) understatement penalties under Section 222/223 TAA are imposed at graduated rates of 10% (substantial understatement), 25% (failure to exercise reasonable care), 50% (no reasonable grounds for tax position), or 150% (intentional tax evasion); (iii) a late- or non-payment penalty of 10% of the outstanding amount plus interest at the SARS prescribed rate is added; (iv) failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days breaches exchange-control rules under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, attracting administrative penalties and possible forfeiture or Suspension of the TCS status; and (v) deliberate evasion may result in criminal prosecution under Section 234 TAA (fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years) and personal liability of directors under Section 19 of the VAT Act where VAT was deducted or collected but not accounted for to SARS.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible on the current account, including software licensing and game publishing royalties, with payments routed through an authorised dealer. On the capital account, non-resident inward equity investment in a South African private company does not require prior SARB approval if reported through an authorised dealer and funded through normal banking channels; no mandatory inward listing is required for a standard Profit/Risk-taking (Pty) Ltd. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders, primarily dividends, is permissible through an authorised dealer without SARB pre-approval provided the underlying capital was lawfully introduced, the remittance is matchable to distributable reserves, SARS tax clearance is in order, and applicable dividend withholding tax has been administered. Hurdles are documentary rather than structural: shareholder resolutions, proof of foreign direct investment declaration, audited or reviewed financial statements, and SWIFT source documentation must be preserved for FinSurv audits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa operates a floating exchange rate and does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; however, all export receipts must be surrendered into the South African banking system through an authorised dealer in ZAR. Payment for exported goods or services must be received within 180 days from the date of shipment or provision of service, or any extended period approved by the authorised dealer and FinSurv. The full proceeds must be credited to the exporter's ZAR account with an authorised dealer, evidenced by Form B/ECS documentation. For digital publishing, Apple App Store and Google Play payouts routed as contractual service proceeds qualify as export revenue and must be received in the same manner, with applicable VAT and corporate tax obligations discharged before onward treasury deployment.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated in terms of the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury and settlement infrastructure. Offshore digital EMI/neobanking providers are not licensed as banks by SARB and cannot independently offer ZAR settlement capability, participate in SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement), or provide a fully regulated receiving account for Apple App Store and Google Play payouts. Their use should be limited to supplementary virtual-card spend and foreign-currency management under an authorised dealer relationship; all primary inbound capital, operational receipts, and outbound trade settlements must stay within the licensed South African banking system to comply with the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services Act, the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, and Exchange Control Regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must remit equity subscription proceeds in a permitted foreign currency or ZAR from a non-resident designated account through an authorised dealer. The compliant SWIFT protocol is an MT103/MT202 from the shareholder's licensed bank, with the beneficiary bank being the South African account-holding authorised dealer, the beneficiary account matched exactly to the company's registered entity name, and remittance information indicating an inward direct investment in equity. The remittance should be designated as foreign direct investment (FDI) for Balance of Payments reporting, not as a trade invoice; the authorised dealer completes the inward BOP declaration and any Form/ECS requirement. Supporting evidence is the board resolution approving the share allotment, the subscription agreement, proof of banking source in the foreign jurisdiction, and immediate entry into the CIPC-issued share register and SARB financial surplus declaration where required.Outbound settlements for external trade, software licensing, cloud infrastructure, marketing, and international vendors are routed through (i) domestic ZAR RTGS via SAMOS for local transaction parties, (ii) SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border vendor wire transfers, and (iii) International Commercial Letter of Credit (Documentary Credit) where the transaction demands bank-intermediated trade payment protection. For every outward remittance above low-value thresholds, the company must obtain an authorised dealer foreign-exchange approval, present the underlying contract, invoice, licence agreement, or purchase order, and produce a SARS tax compliance and tax withholding analysis where royalties or service fees are involved. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is generally required when the South African taxpayer's gross turnover exceeds ZAR 100 million, or when the aggregate value of cross-border connected-person transactions in a year exceeds ZAR 5 million. Master File and Country-by-Country reporting are required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rules in sections 80A to 80L of the Income Tax Act, the substance-over-form doctrine, the place-of-effective-management residence test, controlled foreign company rules, and SARS's power to disregard arrangements lacking commercial substance. A South African (Pty) Ltd operating as a mere conduit without employees, premises, decision-making authority, or risk-bearing capacity will be recharacterised and its deductions denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under sections 234 and 235 of the Tax Administration Act, with fines up to ZAR 10 million and imprisonment up to 10 years; civil understatement penalties up to 100% of the tax shortfall plus prescribed-rate interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in or knowingly authorised the evasion can be joined in the assessment, subjected to asset preservation orders, placed on SARS departure prohibition lists, and prosecuted personally. The corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as a facade for fraud.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the company under the general anti-avoidance rules, tax the non-resident recipient as effectively managed in South Africa, apply CFC inclusion, or recharacterise the arrangement; the resulting additional assessment carries understatement penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors acting without substance are personally liable under section 77 of the Companies Act for reckless trading. SARS may attach personal assets, prohibit departure, and seek criminal penalties where the shell is used to defraud revenue.
