Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); operational portal: SARS eFiling (www.sarsefiling.co.za), supplemented by SARS branch walk-in services and registered tax practitioners; export customs processing via SARS Customs division through the SARS EDI/Web pockets.

E-government filing portal

A South African incorporated (Pty) Ltd is automatically registered for income tax by SARS following CIPC incorporation via the CIPC-SARS data integration, and receives an income tax reference number. VAT registration is effected on form VAT 101 (company application) submitted through eFiling or a SARS branch within 21 days after the company's taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period; voluntary VAT registration is available from an annual turnover of ZAR 50,000. Foreign-incorporated entities or foreign branches must apply for a SARS income tax reference number manually by submitting the DA 185/IT-REG application with certified incorporation documents, Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of registered address in South Africa, and identification of directors; the issued SARS tax reference number is then used for VAT registration. For non-resident suppliers of 'electronic services' to South African residents, a separate VAT registration via the SARS eFiling 'foreign vendor' channel is mandatory when supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in 12 months.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the (Pty) Ltd is a South African resident company taxed in its own right at a flat corporate income tax rate of 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (28% for earlier years); there is no pass-through election available for a company. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the (Pty) Ltd is a South African resident company taxed in its own right at a flat corporate income tax rate of 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (28% for earlier years); there is no pass-through election available for a company. Dividends declared by the company are subject to a 20% dividends tax withheld at source, and the company remains within the South African territorial tax net with worldwide residence-based taxation of its income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in South Africa. Statutory elections available to the Pty Ltd include: (i) headquarter company election under section 9I of the Income Tax Act (conferring a 0% rate on foreign dividends, interest and royalties where the company meets the 80% foreign-shareholding and services thresholds); (ii) intra-group reorganisation and loss-grouping elections under sections 41-47 of the Income Tax Act; (iii) voluntary VAT group registration election under section 52 of the VAT Act for connected persons; (iv) debt-instrument effective interest rate election under section 24J; (v) R&D tax incentive approval election under section 11D; (vi) automatic application of hybrid mismatch rules under sections 8J-8M, with taxpayer ordering choices for the primary and defensive deduction-denial rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory ZAR 1,000,000 taxable turnover within a rolling 12-month period, applied on a continuous forward-looking basis; voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000 annual turnover. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African residents must register from the same ZAR 1,000,000 threshold in respect of supplies made to South African customers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods are supplied to a non-resident under an agreement of sale, delivered to an address outside South Africa, and the exporter obtains and retains documentary proof of physical export within 90 days of the time of supply. Services are zero-rated under section 11(1)(h) where supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not present in South Africa at the time of supply and where the services are not supplied for use in South Africa; for a video-match social telephony app, cross-border subscription, virtual-currency and advertising services delivered to non-resident end-users qualify as zero-rated exports if the company maintains geo-location logs, IP verification, foreign payment-rail settlement evidence (Apple IAP, Paymentwall) and marketing documentation demonstrating the offshore consumer base. Supplies to South African residents remain standard-rated at 15%; reverse-charge rules and section 54A foreign electronic services provisions apply only to non-resident suppliers making supplies into South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SARS SAD500/Electronic DA 500) or, for digital services, a certificate of export/foreign supply evidence package
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / electronic cargo movement reference for physical goods; for pure digital services, platform payment settlement reports proving non-resident receipt
  • Foreign-Currency Inward Transfer (FCIT) certificate issued by a SARB Authorised Dealer (local bank) evidencing receipt of the export sales proceeds
  • Tax invoice and underlying service agreement showing non-resident billing address, user IP-geolocation logs, and payment-rail settlement statements from Apple IAP and Paymentwall
  • SARB exchange-control declaration (Form 561/Exports or manual export proceeds record) where an Authorised Dealer dispensation applies

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or government pre-clearance for e-invoices. Suppliers must issue a full tax invoice within 21 days of the time of supply, containing section 20 requirements (seller and buyer names and addresses, SARS VAT numbers, aggregate value, VAT amount, tax invoice serial number, date of issue, description, and quantity). SARS accepts electronic invoices under the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act and has published an e-invoicing policy roadmap; submission to SARS is at data level via eFiling (VAT 201 returns), with automated reconciliation against banking, customs and CIPC data. There is no nationally mandated digital signature stamp, but SARS may require interoperability, audit trails and valid electronic signature authentication during VAT audits.

SARS may claw back zero-rated treatment and assess output VAT at 15% if export documentation is absent or the export/foreign supply is not effected within the statutory 90-day window for goods or 180-day FX repatriation period. Late-received export proceeds are subject to SARS/SARB audit on foreign exchange compliance; SARS may raise additional assessments within 5 years of assessment, or 10 years in fraud cases. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, an understatement penalty of between 10% and 200% of the shortfall applies, with aggravating factors producing the higher tiers; interest accrues at the SARS prescribed late-payment rate (approximately 10%-11.5% p.a. in 2024/2025, quoted quarterly). Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating is a criminal offence punishable by a fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years, and personal liability may attach to directors or trust beneficiaries under common-law fraud, lifting of the corporate veil, or section 6 VAT provisions where the entity is used as a conduit for VAT evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is a controlled but practically convertible currency on current account. Current-account transactions - including export of digital platform services, app store revenue, advertising services, data processing and software licensing - are liberalised and may be executed freely through an Authorised Dealer, subject to documentary verification (contracts, invoices, PSP payout reports) and mandatory balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting. Capital-account transactions remain regulated: inbound direct investment (share subscription) does not require prior FinSurv approval but must be reported to the Authorised Dealer on the prescribed inward direct investment/BOP declaration, supported by board resolutions, the share register and the Companies Act 71 of 2008 issuance mechanics. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted by the Authorised Dealer without prior SARB approval provided: (i) the company holds a valid SARS tax compliance status; (ii) dividends are declared legally from distributable profits per a board resolution; (iii) the non-resident shareholding is properly recorded; (iv) dividend withholding tax (20%, treaty-reduced where applicable) is deducted and remitted to SARS; and (v) the remittance is reported on the BOP return. Remaining hurdles for this entity: any SARS filing lapse will freeze remittances; UBO transparency under FICA 2023 must be complete; service-export proceeds must be repatriated within the statutory window; and any offshore bank account or offshore treasury vehicle requires prior FinSurv approval, which is generally refused for a fully resident operating (Pty) Ltd.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For services exports (including app store collections via Apple IAP and Paymentwall), foreign currency proceeds must be received in South Africa within 30 days of the date the service is rendered; for goods exports the window is 180 days. The pre-2017 rule mandating conversion of 50% of export proceeds has been abolished, so the required mandatory conversion ratio is 0%, provided the proceeds are either: (a) sold immediately into the interbank market through an Authorised Dealer, or (b) retained in an SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer. Amounts not retained in an FCA must be converted to ZAR on receipt and reported on the BOP service-export return. For the Company, every Apple and Paymentwall payout statement must evidence the underlying user transactions and the funds must land in the South African operational account within the 30-day window to avoid a contravention of the Authorised Dealer Manual and attendant bank compliance action.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations (1961), the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers, and the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA); day-to-day exchange control administration is delegated to Authorised Dealers (registered commercial banks) and Authorised Dealers with Limited Authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail; Medium only as a supplementary settlement corridor. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud, Mercury) cannot provide a domestic ZAR account or clear ZAR payments without being licensed as a bank or registered as an Authorised Dealer under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 and the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998. A South African resident company is also prohibited from maintaining an offshore bank account without prior SARB FinSurv approval, which is rarely granted to a fully resident operating entity; consequently, treasury funds must sit onshore with an Authorised Dealer. EMIs remain commercially viable only as the payout/settlement gateway used by Apple and Paymentwall from their licensed acquiring entities, or as a disbursement rail for small vendor payments where the foreign vendor is paid directly by the EMI from funds remitted outbound under the relevant current-account dispensation. The Company must not maintain float, user funds, or capital injection proceeds in any unlicensed EMI, as such routing would trigger a bank rejection and exchange-control contravention.

Compliant routing for non-resident equity injection: (1) the non-resident shareholder remits USD/EUR by SWIFT MT103 to the Company's FCA or the Authorised Dealer's inward nostro account in South Africa; (2) the Company files with the bank: board resolution authorising the share issue, updated Memorandum of Incorporation, share subscription agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and the prescribed inward direct investment/BOP return (successor to the legacy Form FIE) within 30 days of receipt; (3) the bank applies the SARB BOP purpose code for direct investment equity, converts to ZAR at the interbank rate, and credits the operational account only after the share issue is recorded in the share register; (4) for shareholder loans, a written loan agreement is executed, the inward loan is reported to the Authorised Dealer, interest must be at arm's length, and repayment requires loan reporting, 15% interest withholding tax (treaty-reduced where applicable) and SARS TCS verification; (5) no injection may be routed through unlicensed EMIs, personal accounts, crypto exchanges, or offshore trusts without prior FinSurv approval; (6) the bank will perform sanctions screening of the remitting bank and confirm the FIC 2023 UBO registration before releasing funds.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 international credit transfer (TT) for all operating payments - cloud and bandwidth (AWS, Google Cloud, Cloudflare), digital marketing (Meta, Google, TikTok), software subscriptions, contractor and KYC vendor fees, and platform fees to Apple/Paymentwall where not netted. Outbound current-account payments require no prior SARB approval, but the Authorised Dealer demands a valid VAT/tax invoice, service contract, proof of service performance, and the correct SARB BOP purpose code (e.g., computer and information services, telecommunications, advertising/media). Related-party payments require transfer-pricing documentation under s31 of the Income Tax Act; royalties require DWT at 15% (treaty-reduced) and SARS documentary confirmation. For significant import-heavy capital expenditure (servers, head-end hardware), a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) through the bank's trade desk is available and recommended above large open-account TTs. Funds can be paid from the Company's USD FCA to minimise FX spread. Payments must be made within 30 days (services) or 180 days (goods) of service/goods delivery; cash advances, cryptocurrency transfers, and personal-account aggregations are prohibited.Spot USD/ZAR and EUR/ZAR conversion through an Authorised Dealer - mandatory on receipt for proceeds not retained in an approved FCA; no separate approval required.Outright Deliverable Forward Exchange Contract (FEC) on USD/ZAR or EUR/ZAR - the primary permitted hedge; maximum tenor 12 months; must be supported by demonstrable underlying exposure (expected Apple IAP and Paymentwall settlement inflows and committed USD vendor payables); executed under a bank master agreement (ISDA/SAFEX equivalent) with margin/collateral and quarterly re-validation.FX Swaps (FEC rollover / spot-forward swaps) - permitted for cash-flow timing mismatches between USD receipts and ZAR disbursements, provided the swap is executed with the same Authorised Dealer and the tenor remains within the underlying trade cycle.Cross-Currency Swaps - permissible only with prior Authorised Dealer/FinSurv approval, typically limited to material long-dated USD liabilities (e.g., capitalised shareholder loans); not economically necessary for this app treasury below ZAR 250m notional and therefore excluded from the initial hedging blueprint.Vanilla FX Options - not a standard corporate product for ZAR; available only on a bespoke basis with FinSurv consent for proven hedging requirements; excluded from the initial plan.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - structurally inapplicable for a resident company because the ZAR has a fully deliverable onshore interbank market; executing an NDF offshore would constitute unauthorised exchange-control avoidance; non-resident counterparties may use NDFs only outside South Africa.Natural and commercial hedges - mandatory as the baseline: USD-denominated vendor contracting matched to USD inflows, maintenance of a working-capital USD FCA balance, and an automatic 5-business-day conversion policy to cap unhedged ZAR exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary treasury rail; Medium only as a supplementary settlement corridor. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud, Mercury) cannot provide a domestic ZAR account or clear ZAR payments without being licensed as a bank or registered as an Authorised Dealer under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 and the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998. A South African resident company is also prohibited from maintaining an offshore bank account without prior SARB FinSurv approval, which is rarely granted to a fully resident operating entity; consequently, treasury funds must sit onshore with an Authorised Dealer. EMIs remain commercially viable only as the payout/settlement gateway used by Apple and Paymentwall from their licensed acquiring entities, or as a disbursement rail for small vendor payments where the foreign vendor is paid directly by the EMI from funds remitted outbound under the relevant current-account dispensation. The Company must not maintain float, user funds, or capital injection proceeds in any unlicensed EMI, as such routing would trigger a bank rejection and exchange-control contravention.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million (total gross receipts) or ZAR 50 million in certain cross-border related-party transactions; Local File / Master File thresholds per SARS Notice may require documentation if annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed ZAR 5 million, and a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for groups with consolidated group revenue above ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa applies the Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) through SARS transfer-pricing and controlled foreign company (CFC) rules, and through the general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR). A company must demonstrate real economic substance (board meetings, key decisions, staff, assets and risk in South Africa) to justify the tax residence and profit allocation; absence of substance triggers recharacterisation, denial of treaty benefits, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act No. 28 of 2011: understatement penalty of 25%–200% of the understated tax depending on conduct (substantial understatement, reasonable care not taken, gross negligence, intentional tax evasion); criminal conviction liable to fine of up to SAR 50,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years, or both.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax if they knowingly caused or allowed the company to fail to pay tax; SARS may issue a notice of personal liability under section 155 of the Tax Administration Act. Directors face civil restitution for amounts wrongfully diverted, travel bans (withdrawal of passport / blocking departure from South Africa), and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for fraudulent evasion.
Shell Company (Substance Deficiency)

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the entity as a sham, reallocate income to the owner or connected persons under transfer-pricing rules, and levy an additional assessment plus 150%–200% understatement penalties. The entity may be struck off by the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) for failure to comply with beneficial ownership filing and annual return filing requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for company debts (including tax liabilities) if the company carries on business recklessly or with gross negligence (section 22 of the Companies Act). Directors face director disqualification, personal restitution orders, and possible criminal sanction for reckless trading.
AML / CFT Violation (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) No. 38 of 2001: administrative fines of up to SAR 10 million for contraventions (failing to conduct customer due diligence, failing to report suspicious and unusual transactions); criminal penalties of up to 10 years imprisonment for money laundering activities under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently fail to implement AML controls are personally liable for penalties, may have their assets frozen, be subjected to travel bans, and face imprisonment up to 10 years. SARS and the FIC may also impose personal confiscation orders for funds laundered through the corporate vehicle.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the taxable income to an arm's-length price, disallows the deduction in full, and applies an understatement penalty of 50%–200% on the additional tax. Specific interest and late-payment penalties are applied to the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if they participated in or knowingly allowed transfer-pricing documentation to be false or misleading, or if the company remains non-compliant after a SARS demand. Imprisonment for tax fraud (up to 5 years) and travel bans apply.
