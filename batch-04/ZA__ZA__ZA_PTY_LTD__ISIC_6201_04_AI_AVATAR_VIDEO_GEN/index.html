Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — portal: www.sars.gov.za; eFiling platform: https://efiling.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate ZA_PTY_LTD at the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); the CIPC-SARS data link automatically issues an Income Tax Reference Number for the company. Step 2: Appoint a public officer who is a resident of South Africa within one month of incorporation (s250 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011). Step 3: Register the company on SARS eFiling, activate the tax profile, and obtain a Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN. Step 4: Register for VAT (VAT101 form via eFiling) where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period, or elect voluntary VAT registration from ZAR 50,000 turnover; non-resident suppliers of electronic/digital services to South African recipients must register for VAT irrespective of turnover. Step 5: Register for PAYE (EMP101), UIF, SDL and COIDA where employees are engaged. Step 6: Obtain a SARS Customs Client Code (Importer/Exporter Code) via SARS Customs for export declarations. Step 7: Register foreign-currency banking arrangements with an authorised dealer bank to facilitate export proceeds and Inward Remittance Certificates under SARB Exchange Control.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African (Pty) Ltd is a resident company taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through or transparency. Dividends distributed attract 20% Dividend Withholding Tax; no branch tax election applies. Non-resident shareholders are entitled to full ownership. CFC and hybrid mismatch rules apply on a mandatory basis.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid entity election exists under South African law; a (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Available statutory elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration (s23 VAT Act 89 of 1991); (ii) VAT group registration for related companies under common control (s50 VAT Act); (iii) joint elections to apply corporate restructure rollover relief (ss42–47 Income Tax Act 58 of 1962); (iv) CFC high-tax exemption elections (s9D(2A)/(2E) Income Tax Act); (v) election to treat software licences as depreciable intellectual property for income tax. No partnership pass-through is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration for resident vendors: ZAR 1,000,000 taxable turnover in any 12-month period; voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000. Non-resident suppliers of electronic/digital services to South African residents: mandatory VAT registration with no turnover threshold (in effect since 1 April 2019).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (a) goods physically exported from South Africa within the prescribed period (s11(1)(a) VAT Act), and (b) services supplied to non-residents under s11(2)(l) VAT Act where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident of South Africa; (ii) the recipient is outside South Africa at the time the services are rendered; (iii) the services are not supplied directly in connection with land, fixed property, or movable property situated in South Africa; (iv) the recipient is not a registered VAT vendor; and (v) payment is received in foreign currency. Licences of intellectual property and electronic information, including AIGC video-generation software and virtual avatar outputs, may additionally qualify under s11(2)(m). Digital services sold to South African resident end-users remain standard-rated at 15%. The vendor must hold contemporaneous documentation evidencing the non-resident status of the customer (e.g., declaration, foreign corporate registration extract, passport, IP address/geo-location logs, foreign payment trail).

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) submitted via SARS Customs EDI / Modernised Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for physical goods or media shipped; for pure SaaS exports, a service delivery/performance report evidencing offshore supply)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the SARB-authorised dealer bank evidencing foreign-currency repatriation
  • Commercial Invoice containing zero-rated VAT notation and export value in foreign currency
  • Packing List (where physical goods or media are consigned)
  • Non-Resident Recipient Declaration certifying customer non-resident status and overseas place of use
  • Foreign Exchange Control registration documentation (relevant for exporters under SARB Exchange Control Regulations)

South Africa currently imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-linked tax authority validation, or digital stamping for tax invoices. Vendors must issue tax invoices satisfying the substantive requirements of s20(4) VAT Act in paper or electronic (PDF/XML) form. VAT returns are filed electronically via SARS eFiling with supporting schedules; SARS has issued a public consultation on a Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC)/e-invoicing framework, but no mandatory clearance go-live is yet effective. Export vendors must retain all zero-rating evidence for at least 5 years and produce it on SARS verification or audit demand.

SARS's Export Verification Unit and automated risk engine cross-match customs declarations, bank FX records, Inward Remittance Certificates, and taxpayer eFiling data. Failure to substantiate zero-rating or to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers: (i) an additional VAT assessment at 15% on the export consideration; (ii) reversal of related input VAT claims; (iii) additional tax of up to 200% of VAT evaded (ss60–61 VAT Act); (iv) understatement penalties of up to 125% of the shortfall (s223 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011); (v) interest at the statutory prescribed rate; and (vi) criminal liability for fraud or false declarations, with fines and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years for tax offences, or up to 10 years where false documentation is knowingly used, in addition to suspension or restriction of the taxpayer's tax clearance status for trade and tender purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a selective exchange control regime. Current account transactions, including software exports, licensing royalties and subscription service payments, are generally freely convertible for residents and non-residents, provided supporting documentation is retained. Capital account transactions - share capital injections, intercompany loans, acquisitions and outward investments - remain subject to approval or must be effected through an Authorised Dealer, which performs a positive screening function. Profit remittances by way of dividends are freely transferable after the entity has satisfied the Companies Act solvency and liquidity test, declared a solvency-certified resolution, and complied with dividend withholding tax procedures, subject to FinSurv reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods and services must be fully received in South Africa within 90 days from date of shipment or invoicing, extendable upon formal application. Proceeds must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer for rand unless a general or specific exemption permits retention in a designated foreign currency account. For software-as-a-service revenue, the exporter must preserve the underlying contract, evidence actual supply/performance, and channel all inbound payments through a local Authorised Dealer. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, but every export receipt must be identified to the underlying export and reported to FinSurv by the settlor bank.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMIs cannot independently provide a full local ZAR transactional account or act as an Authorised Dealer without SARB licensing under the Banks Act. They may integrate with South African banking partners for payment collection and route liquidity via rails, but for treasury settlement and FX compliance, a direct relationship with a licensed South African Authorised Dealer is mandatory. Domestic fintech platforms can be used as a software orchestration layer only.
