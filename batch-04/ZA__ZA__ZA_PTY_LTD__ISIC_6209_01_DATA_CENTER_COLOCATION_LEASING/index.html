E-government filing portal

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available. A Pty Ltd may elect small business corporation tax treatment under section 12E if it meets the qualifying criteria; make an s 50A VAT group election with connected companies; and utilise roll-over elections for group restructurings under sections 41–43. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 or more in taxable turnover over 12 months; voluntary registration available from ZAR 50,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% VAT if exported from South Africa under an export contract, removed from the Republic within the prescribed period, and supported by customs export documents; services qualify if supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply, or if the services are physically performed entirely outside the Republic (excluding services relating to immovable property situated in South Africa). For data-center colocation services rendered to foreign residents, zero-rating generally applies when the recipient is absent from South Africa and the supply does not consist of the use of South African fixed property.

  • SAD 500 Customs Export Declaration (stamped by SARS Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (issued to the foreign customer)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (issued by an authorised dealer for foreign-currency payment of the export)

South Africa does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. VAT vendors must issue full tax invoices (containing at least all prescribed particulars under section 20 of the VAT Act) electronically or on paper. SARS eFiling and automated VAT 201 submissions constitute the digital filing channel. For exported services, retrospective documentation audit tools and SARS electronic data capturing (e.g., SAS export tables) apply. There is no API-based continuous verification clearance except for the optional digital e-invoicing framework proposed in SARS’ e-invoicing roadmap.

Zero-rating is audited against the export declaration and proof of foreign payment. SARS can claw back refunds and raise additional VAT assessments where documentation is deficient. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties range from 0% to 150% depending on behavior. Fraudulent export VAT refund claims attract potential criminal penalties of up to ZAR 250,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years. Interest runs from the date payment of refund was received.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand (ZAR) is a freely issued managed-float currency with an active onshore and offshore interbank market. Current-account convertibility is high: payments for imported equipment, software, licences and operational services are routinely approved by Authorised Dealers against invoices, contracts and SARS tax-compliance screening. Capital-account convertibility is asymmetric: inward foreign direct investment into an operating (Pty) Ltd is not subject to prior SARB approval, but every inward capital receipt must be routed through a domestic Authorised Dealer and declared on the SARB Inward Direct Investment report within 30 days; the issued FinSurv reference number is the legal anchor for later profit remittances, dividend repatriation and capital disinvestment. Outward capital flows from the local entity (offshore lending, guarantees, portfolio investments) are restricted and require FinSurv applications. Dividend and royalty remittances are permitted only after SARS tax-compliance validation and settlement of withholding taxes (dividends tax 20%, royalties 15%, interest 15%, each reducible under an applicable double-taxation agreement). Cross-border netting between group receivables and payables is not permitted without bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, a resident exporter of goods or services must collect full proceeds within 180 days from the date of export or service rendering and must retain those proceeds in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer. There is no statutory percentage split; in substance a 100% repatriation and surrender duty applies, settled by conversion into ZAR at the ruling market rate unless the bank has approved a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) facility to retain USD, EUR or GBP subject to turnover and balance limits. The 180-day deadline is strict; any extension must be applied to FinSurv before maturity. Cross-border colocation fees billed to offshore tenants are invisible-service exports and must be reported to the bank on the prescribed FX receipt form at credit time, with late receipt constituting a criminal exchange-control offence.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB); Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) administers the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Currency and Exchanges Manual; the National Payment System Department (NPSD) supervises clearing and settlement. The top-tier banks execute these functions locally under delegated Authorised Dealer licences.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail and not feasible as the sole account structure. SARB does not issue EMI licences and a digital EMI (Wise Platform, Nium, Currencycloud, Airwallex) cannot accept ZAR deposits or process Form-A/FDI/FCA workstreams without a sponsoring registered bank under the Banks Act 94 of 1990. EMIs may serve only as auxiliary collection wallets in USD/EUR/GBP for international tenants, with all balances periodically repatriated to the ZA operational account to satisfy exchange-control reporting. The core treasury, FX execution and liquidity-clearing architecture must reside at a tier-1 South African bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for companies whose year of assessment ends on or after 31 March 2023; Small Business Corporations benefit from graduated rates from 0% to 27%; Micro Business Turnover Tax may replace normal CGT and VAT in limited cases.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax withheld by the South African resident-paying company on qualifying dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; South African-resident corporate shareholders may receive exemptions under s64FA of the Income Tax Act.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due date prescribed by SARS on the Notice of Registration/Assessment; generally no later than 12 months after the company's financial year-end. Provisional returns are due six months after commencement of the year and at year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Section A - Taxpayer Identification Company legal name, SARS Tax Reference Number, company registration/enterprise number, registered and postal addresses, principal business code (ISIC/NAICS), eFiling agent details and contact information. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: a South African taxpayer is required to prepare and retain a Local File where the aggregate of all affected (connected-person) cross-border transactions exceeds ZAR 5 million in a year, or where any transaction involves a participation exemption or is otherwise selected by SARS. Master File threshold: where the multinational group's annual consolidated turnover exceeds ZAR 1 billion and the local entity has affected transactions above the Local File threshold. SARS may nonetheless request documentation on a risk-assessment basis regardless of thresholds.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a specific Economic Substance Regulation as in some offshore centres. Economic substance is nevertheless enforced through general anti-avoidance rules (s103 and s80A-L of the Income Tax Act), CFC attribution under s9D, corporate-residence tests ('incorporation' or 'effective management'), transfer-pricing re-characterisation under s31, interest-deduction caps under s23M, and the 'purpose vs effect' substance-over-form doctrine. A (Pty) Ltd without revenue, employees, premises, business activity, or bank activity will be treated as a shell/paper company and its transactions may be void for tax purposes; SARS will pierce the entity and attribute income and deductions to the substance location.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty from 0% to 200% of the shortfall under Part III of the Tax Administration Act, plus interest and criminal prosecution. Fines up to ZAR 10 million or imprisonment up to 5 years for tax evasion; repeated evasion may attract a criminal penalty of up to ZAR 200,000 or two years' imprisonment per count.

Director personal liability: Directors and persons 'responsible for tax offences' are jointly and severally liable under ss159 to 164 TAA for criminal fines and unpaid tax if they intentionally caused the company to fail compliance; personal assets may be attached; travel bans under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act are possible.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: SARS can set aside the transaction under s80A-L GAAR, deny deductions, tax the local shell as a resident on worldwide income, and assess additional tax at the normal 27% plus understatement penalty of up to 100%; the company and its assets are subject to SARS civil enforcement and liquidation proceedings.

Director personal liability: Director who signed false certificates or permitted sham transactions may be declared personally liable under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and TAA; a court may order restitution, prohibit the director from leaving South Africa, and impose imprisonment of up to 5 years for perjury in tax filings.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Artificial Profit Strip

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts taxable income to arm's-length prices under s31; imposes additional normal tax at 27%, understatement penalty (up to 100% for substantial misstatement; up to 200% for intentional), and interest. Interest expense disallowed under s23M (EBITDA cap) if net interest expense exceeds 30% of adjusted taxable income; excess may be carried forward.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the mispriced contracts are jointly liable for the additional tax and penalty; SARS may seek civil damages and a personal assessment if they derived benefit from the tax shelter; failure to produce TP documentation at audit may separately trigger a ZAR 100,000 documentary non-compliance penalty.
Dividend/Interest Repatriation Evasion and AML Breaches

Statutory sanction: SARS reclassifies the loan/deemed dividend and claims 20% dividends tax; interest paid to offshore parent without withholding attracts 15% WHT and understatement penalties. Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, accountable institutions (including payment service providers or companies managing customer funds) may be fined up to ZAR 10 million per contravention and senior management may face imprisonment of up to 15 years for money-laundering or financing activities.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly assist a patient to evade dividend WHT or FICA reporting face joint and several civil liability to SARS and FIC, debarment from acting as a director under the Companies Act, combined with criminal fines and imprisonment; travel bans and extradition are available under the extradition treaties and the UN Convention against Transnational Organised Crime.
