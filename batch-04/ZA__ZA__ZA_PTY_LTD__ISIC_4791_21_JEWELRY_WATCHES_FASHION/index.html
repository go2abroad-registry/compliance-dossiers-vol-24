Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at https://www.sars.gov.za and https://efiling.sars.gov.za

        
E-government filing portal
For a South African-incorporated (Pty) Ltd, obtain the CIPC registration number and SARS income tax reference number via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission/SARS integrated registration; foreign-owned entities must also file beneficial-ownership and director details. Register as a VAT vendor by submitting the VAT101 form through SARS eFiling before commencing taxable supplies if VAT liability is triggered, or within 21 days after taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of electronic services must register for VAT from the first date of exceeding the ZAR 1,000,000 electronic-services turnover threshold. Supporting documents for foreign shareholders: constitutional documents, registration certificate, tax residency certificate, power of attorney for the South African public officer, and local bank confirmation; SARS eFiling will issue the income tax reference number and VAT vendor number within the statutory processing period. A foreign company with a local enterprise or branch is also required to file ITR14; a resident (Pty) Ltd files ITR14 and VAT201 electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation — a (Pty) Ltd is a separate legal taxpayer under South African Income Tax Act No 58 of 1962 and is taxed at corporate rates on worldwide income. No pass-through or transparent treatment applies to the entity itself; only income attributed from controlled foreign companies is taxed at the South African shareholder level under CFC rules.

        
Available election forms
South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; a (Pty) Ltd is statutorily opaque. For cross-border group structures, CFC rules include statutory exemptions for high-taxed attributable income; no general election exists to classify the CFC as transparent. Hybrid mismatch rules introduced under OECD BEPS Action 2 do not provide a general entity classification election; they require symmetrical tax treatment and allow prescribed correlative adjustments or offset elections for hybrid instruments and hybrid entities. VAT vendors may elect the two-month tax period and may choose to register voluntarily where annual taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 but are below the compulsory threshold.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
15%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of electronic services use the same ZAR 1,000,000 threshold applied to electronic services supplied to South African residents, measured on the value of supplies from 1 April 2019.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
VAT Act s11(1)(a) zero-rates goods supplied to a non-resident where the goods are removed from South Africa to a foreign destination within 90 days of the time of supply, or within such extended period as the Commissioner may allow, and the supplier retains proof of export. Jewellery and watches exported by an online retailer qualify at 0% if the sale is to a foreign customer, the customer is outside South Africa at supply, the goods are physically dispatched through Customs, and no supply to a South African resident occurs. Services supplied to non-residents and physically rendered outside South Africa may be zero-rated under s11(2); input VAT attributable to export sales is claimable in the VAT201 return, including courier, insurance and sourcing costs, provided the export evidence file is complete. The exporter must account for VAT at 15% on local losses or damages if the goods are not actually exported within 90 days, and on domestic scrap or theft claims.

South Africa does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-by-transaction government clearance requirement. VAT-inclusive invoices must satisfy VAT Act s20(4) content rules: supplier name, VAT number, invoice date, description, quantity, price and the VAT amount. SARS uses eFiling/API channels for VAT201 submissions and Customs declarations; large taxpayers have a Standard Audit File for Tax (SAF-T) submission obligation, and all export VAT records must be retained for at least five years. Online sales through Stripe/PayPal are electronic services for VAT purposes and must be included in the VAT201 return; no additional government e-invoice stamp is required.

      
SARS cross-matches VAT zero-rating claims with Customs export records, courier manifests and bank FX receipt records. Where 0% VAT is claimed without actual export, SARS re-characterises the supply as taxable at 15% and imposes additional VAT with interest from the original due date plus understatement penalties under Tax Administration Act ss222-223 ranging from 10% to 150% for intentional evasion, and criminal prosecution provisions for material fraud. Past zero-rated exports subjected to clawback require the vendor to repay the input VAT recovered plus penalties; there is also a Customs penalty regime where falsely documented exports attract additional tax of up to 100% of the VAT evaded. Compliance prerequisite: all export files, including commercial invoice, insurance certificate, payment proof, customer record and transporter tracking, must be available for audit within a five-year retention period.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Africa is an exchange-control country with a dual-track regime: the ZAR is fully convertible for current-account transactions (trade settlements, royalties, dividends, and service fees) without FinSurv pre-approval, provided the corporate bank (authorised dealer) obtains the required documentary evidence and records the transaction in the Balance of Payments (BOP) reporting system. Capital-account transactions are regulated: inward equity/loan capital is freely admissible through an authorised dealer subject to source-of-funds verification and BOP reporting; outward capital transfers by a resident company rely on the corporate investment allowance (ZAR1 billion per company per financial year, subject to FinSurv reporting) and require prior FinSurv approval beyond that threshold, generally executed through an A2-type capital remittance declaration. Dividend/profit remittances to a non-resident shareholder are permitted without exchange-control approval if the dividend is lawfully declared under the Companies Act, 2008, is financed from after-tax retained earnings, is supported by signed financial statements or audited AFS, and the shareholder's inward-funding trail (BIL/ARF record) is verifiable; thin capitalisation and transfer-pricing adjustments under the Income Tax Act, 1962 may nonetheless restrict the remittance base and require contemporaneous documentation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Exporters must receive full payment through an authorised dealer in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment; an extension may be requested via the authorised dealer for valid trade reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to Rand: the exporter may retain export proceeds in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or convert voluntarily, so the effective surrender obligation is 100% repatriation of the export value with 0% forced conversion. The exporter must identify and match each receipt against the underlying export document (e.g., SARS DA185 export declaration, commercial invoice, airway bill) within 30 days of receipt, and must justify any shortfall or excess with supporting trade documentation.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) of the South African Reserve Bank (SARB), exercising delegated authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers. The Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) operates in parallel under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 2001 (FICA) to enforce AML/CFT obligations on banks and accountable institutions.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a primary treasury rail and MEDIUM as a supplementary collections/payout rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Payoneer, Wise Business, Airwallex) and e-commerce gateways (Stripe, PayPal) are not banks under the Banks Act, 1994 and are not authorised dealers under South African exchange control; they cannot lawfully provide ZAR-denominated settlement accounts or local payment-system clearing. Their use must therefore be limited to foreign-currency receivable collection and global disbursement, with all ZAR settlement, tax payments and statutory balances flowing through an onshore Tier-1 commercial bank. Onshore, the company should contract with a licensed payment service provider / acquiring bank (Standard Bank, FNB, Absa or Nedbank) to route Stripe/PayPal settlements into a segregated ZAR merchant account to preserve a clean audit trail under FICA and SARS record-keeping rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The capital injection must be remitted from the non-resident shareholder's own bank account to the company's account held with a South African authorised dealer. The remitting party must state the payment purpose (e.g., 'share capital contribution – ZA_PTY_LTD' or 'intercompany loan – ZA_PTY_LTD') and the authorised dealer will verify the source of funds, issue a Basic Inward Letter (BIL) for the company, and record the transaction under the applicable SARB Balance of Payments code for inward direct investment equity or related-party loan. Where the inward amount requires FinSurv approval, the company must file the Application for Approval for Inward Investment (ARF) before release of funds. The company should open a dedicated Capital Escrow Account to segregate the shareholder injection until the BIL and any withholding tax / capital duties filings are complete; the funds may then be swept into the operational ZAR account only after board approval and compliance sign-off.For settlement of imported inventory and third-party supplier invoices, the company should use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers routed through the authorised dealer, accompanied by the statutory import declaration / BOP reporting and the supporting commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, packing list and any required import permit (SARS import customs clearance). For large or strategic purchases of watches/jewellery, a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) is recommended, with the LC issued by the South African bank and advised through the beneficiary's bank; settlement occurs by SWIFT MT103/MT202 against conforming documents. Alternatively, documentary collection (D/P or D/A) may be used; in every case, the company must ensure the SARB 'current account' documentation trail is archived to avoid blocked disbursements and to secure future transfer pricing audits.Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) with an Authorised Dealer for deliverable ZAR/USD (and other major currency) covers, supported by firm trade commitments and extendable up to 24 months.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) with the authorised dealer for hedging long-term foreign-currency debt, lease, or equity-type exposures.Currency Options purchased from an authorised dealer on a transaction-specific basis to hedge commercial/investment exposures, with FinSurv or dealer reporting as applicable.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a primary treasury rail and MEDIUM as a supplementary collections/payout rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Payoneer, Wise Business, Airwallex) and e-commerce gateways (Stripe, PayPal) are not banks under the Banks Act, 1994 and are not authorised dealers under South African exchange control; they cannot lawfully provide ZAR-denominated settlement accounts or local payment-system clearing. Their use must therefore be limited to foreign-currency receivable collection and global disbursement, with all ZAR settlement, tax payments and statutory balances flowing through an onshore Tier-1 commercial bank. Onshore, the company should contract with a licensed payment service provider / acquiring bank (Standard Bank, FNB, Absa or Nedbank) to route Stripe/PayPal settlements into a segregated ZAR merchant account to preserve a clean audit trail under FICA and SARS record-keeping rules.
