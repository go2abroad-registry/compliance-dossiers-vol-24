Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); registration, filing and payments are administered through the SARS eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za (statutory gateway: https://www.sars.gov.za). VAT is governed by the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991 and administered by SARS under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that derives South African source income or carries on an enterprise in South Africa must first obtain an income tax reference number: an appointed public officer resident in South Africa files form IT150 with SARS eFiling, attaching certified constitutional documents, resolution appointing the public officer, proof of local physical address and SARS power of attorney. For VAT, a domestic (Pty) Ltd must compulsorily register as a vendor (form VAT 101) once taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period, and may voluntarily register once taxable supplies exceed R50,000 per 12 months. A foreign supplier of 'electronic services' (defined to include news, market data, information and data feeds supplied by digital media) must register for VAT when such electronic services exceed R50,000 per 12-month period. SARS issues an 8-digit income tax reference number and a 15-digit VAT vendor number; online registration is generally completed within 21 business days after the complete application (incorporation documents, directors, shareholders, banking details and place of effective management) is submitted.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity that derives South African source income or carries on an enterprise in South Africa must first obtain an income tax reference number: an appointed public officer resident in South Africa files form IT150 with SARS eFiling, attaching certified constitutional documents, resolution appointing the public officer, proof of local physical address and SARS power of attorney. For VAT, a domestic (Pty) Ltd must compulsorily register as a vendor (form VAT 101) once taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period, and may voluntarily register once taxable supplies exceed R50,000 per 12 months. A foreign supplier of 'electronic services' (defined to include news, market data, information and data feeds supplied by digital media) must register for VAT when such electronic services exceed R50,000 per 12-month period. SARS issues an 8-digit income tax reference number and a 15-digit VAT vendor number; online registration is generally completed within 21 business days after the complete application (incorporation documents, directors, shareholders, banking details and place of effective management) is submitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elective classification; tax character follows the legal form, and a (Pty) Ltd is a resident company taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate rate. Statutory elections available include: (i) group-of-companies relief elections under ss 41-47 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 for intragroup asset transfers, share-for-share, amalgamation and liquidation roll-overs; (ii) functional currency election under s 24D of the Income Tax Act, subject to SARS approval; (iii) VAT grouping election under s 50 of the VAT Act enabling related companies to register as a single vendor (form VAT 118); (iv) voluntary VAT registration and the VAT cash (payments) basis election under s 15 of the VAT Act for vendors with annual taxable supplies not exceeding R2.5 million; and (v) SARS approval under s 20(6) of the VAT Act to issue electronic tax invoices.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration: R1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary VAT registration: R50,000 of taxable supplies per 12 months; foreign suppliers of electronic services (including financial newswire, market data and API content feeds): mandatory registration once electronic services supplied exceed R50,000 per 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods: 0% VAT applies under s 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where a vendor supplies goods to a non-resident and the goods are removed from the Republic in the ordinary course of export trade. Export services: 0% VAT applies under s 11(2)(l) where services (including financial news, market data, syndicated newswire subscriptions and API data feeds) are supplied to a non-resident recipient located outside South Africa and the services are physically rendered outside South Africa, or are supplied in respect of movable property situated outside South Africa, or are supplied for the direct benefit of the non-resident in respect of goods or services. The vendor must retain documentary proof of the non-resident status of the recipient, the place of consumption and the foreign-currency consideration. Where exported goods are not removed from South Africa within 90 days of supply, output tax must be accounted under s 11(3); if export is subsequently proved within 12 months, the vendor may claim an adjusting credit/refund.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD500) lodged and released through the SARS customs module (SARSACC/UCC), with applicable SACU removal reference for goods
  • Commercial invoice / full tax invoice identifying the foreign buyer, the zero rate applied and the precise supply value
  • Bill of lading or airway waybill evidencing physical removal of goods, or equivalent offshore delivery and acceptance evidence for services
  • Packing list and transport/insurance documents (for goods exports)
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of the export consideration in convertible foreign currency
  • Certificate of incorporation and/or tax residence certificate of the non-resident buyer (for services and electronic supplies)
  • Executed subscription or service agreement (for newswire API supplies) specifying recipient, jurisdiction of consumption and settlement terms

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) network is currently mandatory in South Africa. SARS operates a voluntary e-invoicing framework aligned to the Peppol standard and requires electronic tax invoices to preserve authenticity, integrity and readability under s 20(6) of the VAT Act. VAT returns (VAT 201) must be filed electronically via eFiling or the SARS software API; vendors must retain standard audit file for tax (SAF-T) data and all underlying export documentation for at least five years from the date of supply.

SARS verifies all zero-rated export claims within the VAT 201 refund workflow by matching the SAD500 customs data, shipping documents, bank remittance credits and the non-resident status of the buyer before refund issuance. Where the vendor cannot substantiate physical removal from South Africa or the non-resident character of the recipient, SARS raises an additional assessment treating the supply as standard-rated at 15%, suspends refunds under s 187 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, and charges interest at the prescribed rate under s 187(4)/s 189 TAA. Understatement penalties under ss 222-223 TAA range up to 200% of the shortfall depending on conduct classification (no reasonable grounds, substantial understatement, reckless or intentional). Retrospective clawback arises under s 11(3) of the VAT Act (goods not removed within 90 days), s 16(3) return adjustments and s 39 default assessments. Deliberate fraudulent export invoices or false refund claims attract criminal liability under s 234 TAA and s 59 of the VAT Act, with fines up to R50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 60 months.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, subject to documentary verification by an authorised dealer. Capital account convertibility is restricted: inward foreign investment in a South African private company is permitted and may be freely remitted on liquidation or share sale only with FinSurv approval and a SARS tax clearance. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted provided the company demonstrates economic substance, has no outstanding tax obligations, and submits formal financial statements evidencing distributable reserves. Cross-border payments for services, including financial newswire data subscriptions, are treated as current account transactions and may be effected through an authorised dealer with invoices and contractual evidence. The principal hurdle is not convertibility but documentary proof of the underlying transaction and compliance with anti-money laundering (AML) obligations under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA).

Export proceeds surrender rules

In terms of Exchange Control Regulation 4(1), exporters must repatriate all export proceeds to South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment. Proceeds are surrendered for conversion into rand unless FinSurv has granted a specific authorisation to retain the proceeds in an approved Foreign Currency Account. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio for export receipts; surrender is required at the market spot rate determined by the authorised dealer. Failure to repatriate within the prescribed period is a criminal offence under the Exchange Control Regulations, and persistent non-compliance may lead to the suspension of the authorised dealer facility or the imposition of administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 and the Currency and Exchanges Manual issued thereunder.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. South Africa does not operate a standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence regime; entities other than registered banks cannot provide cross-border payment services or hold customer funds on deposit. Offshore digital EMIs are not recognised as authorised dealers and cannot lawfully open local bank accounts or clear ZAR transactions without partnering with a registered South African bank or obtaining a banking licence from the Prudential Authority of SARB. A fintech arrangement may be practical only as a front-end channel layered over a Tier 1 bank's existing clearing infrastructure, not as an independent clearing participant.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident beneficial ownership with an opaque chain of holding companies

Root cause: Non-resident beneficial ownership with an opaque chain of holding companies

Defense evidence: A full organisational chart from the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) to the South African entity, certified register of members, signed declarations of beneficial ownership under the Companies Act and FICA, passport copies of all natural persons, and an independent legal opinion on the source of funds for the subscription price.
Cross-border wires at a frequency disproportionate to the company's stated turnover and staff complement

Root cause: Cross-border wires at a frequency disproportionate to the company's stated turnover and staff complement

Defense evidence: Audited financial statements for two years, intercompany service agreements, transactional flow documentation matching each wire to an invoice or contractual milestone, and a board-approved treasury policy that designates clearing banks and authorised signatories.
Payment inflows from exchanges or third-party processors inconsistent with a financial newswire syndication business model

Root cause: Payment inflows from exchanges or third-party processors inconsistent with a financial newswire syndication business model

Defense evidence: A data licence distribution matrix showing end-client subscription contracts, API usage logs redacted for privacy, support tickets showing newswire content delivery, and a third-party independent AML review of the company's payment operations.
The presence of a director or shareholder listed on a sanctions or politically exposed persons (PEP) register

Root cause: The presence of a director or shareholder listed on a sanctions or politically exposed persons (PEP) register

Defense evidence: A written explanation of the relationship, a formal risk assessment by a South African AML advisory firm, transaction-level monitoring records, and a compliance commitment letter from the UBO undertaking enhanced due diligence and an annual independent audit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or subordinated loan funding from a non-resident shareholder must be routed through an authorised dealer and recorded in the Financial Surveillance File of the local company. The initiating bank requires an SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Equity Subscription' or 'Shareholders Loan' and must include the bank's authorised dealer exemption or instruction to convert the foreign currency to rand at the prevailing market rate. The local company must recoil with a receipt from the authorised dealer, file the BOP (Balance of Payments) code for inward foreign direct investment, and preserve a board resolution approving the subscription or loan term sheet. Where the shareholder acquires a controlling interest, the transaction may further require a Competition Commission filing if the thresholds of the Companies Act or Competition Act apply, but pure portfolio capital injection above the de-minimis limits triggers no prior FinSurv approval; the exchange control form is a post-event compliance record.Outbound settlements for trade, service fees, royalties and dividends are cleared through the same Tier 1 banks using SWIFT MT103 messages for ordinary payments and SWIFT MT700 infrastructure for Commercial Letters of Credit (L/Cs). For an ISIC 6391 financial newswire business, most outbound payments will be operational flows for data centre hosting, distribution licences, freelance content production and technology subscriptions; these are current account payments and may be disbursed immediately with supporting invoices and proof of the underlying service. All outbound payments must be submitted to the bank with a transfer reason code aligned to the IMF Balance of Payments Manual; for service fees the use of code 200 (Telecommunications services) or 250 (Business and professional services) is acceptable. Commercial L/Cs are only expected for physical goods procurement, which is outside the newswire core scope, but if used they must comply with the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600) and be issued on an irrevocable, restricted-to-authorised-dealer basis.Forward exchange contracts (FECs) concluded with an authorised dealer against a genuine underlying foreign currency exposure.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) entered into for the purpose of hedging a bond or loan liability denominated in foreign currency.Currency swaps and ordinary over-the-counter (OTC) foreign exchange swaps executed with a South African prudentially regulated bank for spot or forward settlement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for qualifying transactions only where the authorised dealer confirms that the exposure arises from an offshore subsidiary and the NDF is booked offshore; resident entities are not permitted to use NDFs as a substitute for the onshore forward market.

Within 12 months after the end of the company’s financial year, unless a specific extension is granted by SARS under section 26 of the Tax Administration Act 2011.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional payment must be received within 6 months of the beginning of the year of assessment. Second provisional payment must be received on or before the last business day of the company’s financial year. An optional third provisional payment may be made within 6 months after the financial year end if the second payment underestimates the final liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5,000,000 (annual aggregate value of cross-border transactions with connected persons triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation, including a local file). A master file may be required where the global group records consolidated revenue above ZAR 10 billion, and a country-by-country report is due where the group’s consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (or ZAR 10 billion).

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute of the type found in the UAE or BVI. However, the South African Revenue Service applies the general anti-avoidance rule under section 103(1) of the Income Tax Act and the transfer-pricing secondary adjustment under section 31(2) to recharacterise transactions lacking commercial substance. A South African company claiming treaty relief or intercompany deductions must demonstrate physical presence, employees with decision-making authority, bank accounts in South Africa, and real operational activity. Shell financing or licensing structures that direct profits to low-tax jurisdictions without substance will be disregarded and taxable income reallocated to South Africa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under sections 235 and 236 of the Tax Administration Act, intentional tax evasion attracts a fine of up to ZAR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years. Civil understatement penalties under section 222 apply at 150% of the shortfall for intentional conduct, plus interest at the prescribed rate and a 10% late-payment penalty. SARS may seize assets and attach third-party debts.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or cause revenue to be diverted are jointly and severally liable for the company’s tax debts. SARS may obtain a criminal complaint, issue a travel ban under section 42 of the Tax Administration Act, and if the director has accepted payment to reduce the tax liability while insolvent, the court may pierce the corporate veil under the Companies Act 71 of 2008.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS will disregard the attribution of income under the transfer-pricing and anti-avoidance rules, re-attribute the full subscription and syndication income to the South African entity, and impose an additional secondary transfer-pricing adjustment equal to the market rate of interest on the resulting deemed loan. The entity may also face a 20% dividends tax on any deemed distribution to the non-resident owner, with penalties up to 200% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be treated as shadow directors of the overseas affiliate and be held personally liable under sections 22 and 77 of the Companies Act. A court may issue a punitive order for reckless trading, while SARS obtains a blocked bank order and a travel prohibition against the board members who assisted the shell structure.
AML / Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: A company that fails to register as an accountable institution or implement a risk-based compliance programme may incur an administrative fine of up to ZAR 10,000,000 and imprisonment of directors up to 10 years for repeated violations. SARS, in cooperation with the Financial Intelligence Centre, may also impose a restriction on bank accounts and freeze the payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers bear personal criminal liability if they knowingly allowed the business to be used for money laundering or failed to file a suspicious transaction report. They may be barred from leaving South Africa pending investigation, and an asset forfeiture order can be made against personal vehicles, bank holdings, and property under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The South African Revenue Service may make an upward adjustment to taxable income based on the arm’s length price, disallow the related deductions, and impose a secondary adjustment interest charge under section 31(2). An understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall applies where the pricing cannot be reasonably justified. SARS also has six full years to issue additional assessments for TP cases.

Director personal liability: A director who approves a transfer-pricing policy without a contemporaneous functional analysis and benchmarking may be treated as having acted in bad faith. The director may be separately assessed as a ‘relevant person’ for a penalty, be required to repay a dividend received from the artificial profit, and be subject to a travel ban if the full amount is not settled.
