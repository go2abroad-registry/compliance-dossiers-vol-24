Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under South African law; tax classification is determined by the entity's legal form. A (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed as a company. Relevant statutory elections include VAT group registration under section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act, and certain section 24C or group-interest relief elections where the facts qualify. No pass-through election is permissible.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; voluntary VAT registration is available if taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period or for certain property-letting activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to supplies of movable goods physically exported from South Africa by the vendor or on the vendor's behalf under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, subject to documentary proof and export within 60 days of supply. Certain international services supplied to non-residents under section 11(2)(l) may also be zero-rated where performance is rendered for a non-resident and the services are not connected with South African immovable property. A single-family office's investment management and custodial services to non-resident beneficiaries may qualify for zero rating if the beneficiaries are located outside South Africa and the services are not consumed domestically.

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration / Bill of Entry (accepted by SARS Customs)
  • Bill of Lading, Airwaybill, or other transport document evidencing export
  • Supplier invoice and packing list
  • Payment proof through an authorised dealer, including an Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of receipt of foreign currency
  • Commodity or service export contract, where applicable

South Africa does not impose a real-time electronic fiscal clearance or digital stamping requirement. VAT returns and payments are filed electronically via SARS eFiling. SARS has issued an e-invoicing framework that standardizes tax-compliant e-invoice data formats and electronic archiving, but there is no mandatory API-based clearance before invoice issuance. Commercial invoices may be electronic if they meet the VAT Act and SARS record-keeping requirements.

SARS will claw back zero-rated VAT with interest and impose understatement penalties of up to 150% where export documentation is missing, goods are not actually exported, or services are in fact imported by a South African resident. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act and the Customs Control Act, including imprisonment, additional penalties of 200% of the tax avoided, and possible civil recovery from directors. Failure to receive export proceeds within 180 days without SARB approval may also expose the company to exchange-control penalties and the reversal of the zero-rating claim.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a liberalised but regulated exchange control environment under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations. Current-account payments are effectively freely convertible through authorised dealers; dividends and branch remittances to non-residents are permissible without SARB approval provided the underlying profits are audited, the remitter holds a valid SARS tax compliance certificate, and the transaction is routed via an authorised dealer. Capital account transactions remain subject to quantitative allowances and prior approvals. Non-residents may freely repatriate the disposal proceeds of listed or unlisted securities, provided the original inward investment was declared and routed through an authorised dealer. For a resident (Pty) Ltd, conversion of ZAR into foreign currency for outbound capital transfers requires either an authorised dealer approval or a FinSurv approval depending on the amount and the nature of the underlying transaction. Profit remittance hurdles: distribution must be made out of distributable reserves per the Companies Act 71 of 2008; a solvency and liquidity test certificate must be signed; non-resident shareholders may remit dividends under a dividend remittance instruction supported by an auditor's declaration and tax clearance; loans to foreign lenders must be registered with FinSurv and the interest rate must satisfy the arm's-length, market-related interest rate requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment or the date goods leave the border, as applicable under the Authorised Dealer Manual. Upon receipt, the full proceeds must be sold to an authorised dealer for rand unless the exporter has obtained a specific exchange control approval to maintain a foreign currency account. The statutory regime therefore imposes a 180-day outward repatriation deadline and, absent approval, a 100% surrender/conversion duty on all export receipts. The non-repatriation or non-conversion of export proceeds is a contravention of the Exchange Control Regulations and may attract criminal penalties and administrative fines.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) — Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million gross income (Local File trigger) or ZAR 5 million aggregate cross-border connected-person transactions per annum; Master File required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue exceeding ZAR 10 billion. The Income Tax Act s31 contains no statutory de minimis, so SARS may enquire into any related-party transaction regardless of size.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substantive requirements arise through: (i) the place of effective management (PoEM) test for tax residency; (ii) the general anti-avoidance rules (s80A–80L) and common-law sham/conduit doctrine; (iii) controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution rules in s9D, which require real substance in offshore investment vehicles; and (iv) the risk of veil-piercing under s20(9) of the Companies Act. A single-family office must maintain locally held board minutes, investment committee decisions executed in South Africa, local professional employment, and substantive bank/custody accounts, or its tax residency and asset-holding structure will be challenged.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 100%–150% of the tax shortfall; interest at the prescribed rate; criminal conviction under s235 of the Tax Administration Act: fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years per count; SARS may impose a permit-blocking (travel ban) order where there is a flight risk.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt (s155 TAA); criminal prosecution follows the individual; directors of the private company risk arrest, extradition from tax treaty partners, and seizure of personal assets under a SARS preservation order.
Shell Company / Sham Conduit / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR nullification of the entire tax benefit (s80A–L); recharacterisation of payments as dividends or deemed taxable income; understatement penalty of up to 150%; company struck off under s22 of the Companies Act for reckless conduct; revocation of banking relationships due to 'economic substance' AML/CIP diligence.

Director personal liability: Court may pierce the corporate veil under s20(9) of the Companies Act where there is unconscionable abuse of juristic personality; directors and shadow directors become personally liable for all company debts; civil restitution plus disqualification as directors; criminal fraud charges where the arrangement misleads SARS or creditors.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment under s31 to an arm's-length price; secondary adjustment deemed to be a dividend or loan (with dividends tax at 20%); understatement penalty of 50%–150%; monthly interest compounding on the adjustment; criminal referral where the TP position was knowingly false.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or caused the filing of false TP disclosures face personal criminal liability for reckless or fraudulent tax conduct; SARS can attach company assets and recover unpaid withholding tax from directors under s249 TAA (absolute liability for withheld amounts) and seek personal civil restitution from controlling shareholders.
AML / FICA Non-Compliance and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Centre administrative penalties of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention; criminal conviction under FICA: imprisonment up to 15 years and/or a fine; conviction under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act (POCA) s4 (money laundering): imprisonment up to 30 years; asset forfeiture of the family office's assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed Money Laundering Control Officer are personally liable where non-compliance was deliberate, reckless, or grossly negligent; individual criminal records, travel bans pending prosecution, personal asset freezes, and disqualification from serving as directors of regulated financial entities.
