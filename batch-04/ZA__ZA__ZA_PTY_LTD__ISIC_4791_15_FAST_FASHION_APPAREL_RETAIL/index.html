Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), with mandatory electronic filing through the SARS eFiling portal (https://efiling.sars.gov.za); customs and export control administered by the SARS Customs and Excise division.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the entity at the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) or, for a non-resident trading entity, register as an external company; (2) apply to SARS via eFiling for the enterprise income tax reference number (TIN) within 21 business days of incorporation or commencement of business (Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, s 22), submitting the CIPC registration certificate, certified director identification/passports and proof of registered address; (3) once taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period, lodge the VAT 101 registration application (with VAT 101A/B enterprise and bank schedules) electronically; foreign electronic-service suppliers must additionally file the relevant SARS non-resident vendor declaration and designate a SARS address of service in South Africa; (4) concurrently register for PAYE, UIF and SDL if employees are engaged; (5) upon approval, SARS issues a VAT vendor number (format 4XXXXXXXXX) and the 15-digit tax reference number, after which eFiling profiles, Tax Compliance Status (TCS) and binding private rulings become available.

Territorial_Scope: residence (incorporation or Place of Effective Management) triggers worldwide taxation; non-resident companies are taxed only on South African-source income, including deemed-source income for e-commerce services under Income Tax Act s 9(2)(l). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A ZA-resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a separate entity on worldwide income at the standard corporate rate of 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; foreign shareholders are not taxed at entity level but face 20% dividends withholding tax on distributions unless the participation exemption applies. Territorial_Scope: residence (incorporation or Place of Effective Management) triggers worldwide taxation; non-resident companies are taxed only on South African-source income, including deemed-source income for e-commerce services under Income Tax Act s 9(2)(l). Tax_Exempt_Zone: not generally applicable; Special Economic Zone (SEZ) incentives are preferential but do not confer a blanket exemption.

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election; classification follows legal form. Relevant elections available to a foreign-owned e-commerce group: (1) headquarter company status election under Income Tax Act s 9I (first-return election permitting certain foreign dividends, interest, royalties and controlled-company income to flow through on a tax-neutral basis); (2) controlled foreign company (CFC) participation-exemption positions under s 9D; (3) hybrid instrument and hybrid mismatch ordering elections under ss 8E, 8EA, 23M and 23N (BEPS Action 2 ordering); (4) VAT group registration election under VAT Act s 50 for associated companies to file as a single vendor; and (5) s 11D research-and-development allowance election for qualifying scientific expenditure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory VAT registration when taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted once taxable turnover exceeds R50,000; foreign suppliers of electronic services are deemed to carry on an enterprise in South Africa and are subject to the same R1,000,000 registration threshold notwithstanding any physical-presence requirement)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under VAT Act s 11(1)(a), the supply of movable goods (including fast-fashion apparel and footwear) is zero-rated where the goods are exported by the vendor within 90 days of the time of supply and the vendor retains the full prescribed proof-of-export records. Cross-border services supplied to non-residents (e.g., e-commerce platform and related inbound logistics services for offshore customers) qualify for zero-rating under s 11(2)(l), provided the recipient is outside South Africa and the services are not physically rendered in South Africa. Domestic B2C/B2B supplies to South African customers remain standard-rated at 15%, and goods imported into South Africa for fulfilment are subject to VAT at importation (15% plus customs duty where applicable).

  • Customs Export Declaration (SARS SAD500 full export entry or electronic cargo-exit declaration submitted through the Customs EDI platform)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Waybill (alternatively courier delivery confirmation evidencing physical dispatch out of South Africa)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the authorised dealer (foreign-currency bank settlement proof linked to the export transaction)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with matched HS codes, quantities, weight and declared value
  • Proof-of-Export register (SARS VAT 264 or equivalent export sales journal) demonstrating export within the 90-day statutory period
  • Foreign customer order and payment-settlement records, including the BNPL/settlement statement where payment is routed through Klarna or Shopify Payments

No real-time fiscal clearance hub, digital stamping or mandatory QR-code regime is currently operational in South Africa. SARS requires all VAT returns (VAT 201) to be filed through eFiling and requires electronic tax invoices to satisfy VAT Act s 20(4) content requirements (vendor name and VAT number, tax invoice number, date of supply, goods description, consideration and VAT amount where applicable). SARS has announced a phased mandatory e-invoicing roadmap (SARS eInvoicing) for high-turnover vendors, which will require digitally signed invoices and machine-readable data transmission via the SARS eFiling/API environment. Until activation of that mandate, proof of export is maintained in the vendor export register and exchanged electronically with SARS customs (SAD500) and the authorised dealer bank.

SARS reconciles customs SAD500 data with VAT 201 returns and bank settlement data; if export conditions are not met (including failure to export goods within 90 days or defective documentation), the zero-rating is reversed and output VAT is assessed at 15% with interest under Tax Administration Act s 187. Under TAA ss 222-223, understatement penalties escalate from 10% (substantial understatement) to 25% (failure to take reasonable care), 50% (position without reasonable grounds), 100% (gross negligence) and 150% (intentional tax evasion); criminal prosecution under TAA s 234 and the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 may follow for fraudulent export declarations, with asset seizure, director disqualification and publication of the taxpayer name on the SARS defaulters list. Unrepatriated export proceeds beyond the 180-day deadline attract exchange-control corrective measures and penalties under the Currency and Exchanges Manual administered through the authorised dealer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a managed-float, CLS-eligible currency that is freely convertible for current-account items — trade in goods and services, e-commerce platform payouts (Shopify Payments, Klarna BNPL settlements), interest, and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders — provided each transaction is executed through an authorised dealer with verified supporting documentation and the correct SARB balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical coding. Capital-account items, notably resident offshore treasury, shareholder loans to foreign group entities, and outward portfolio investment, are restricted and require prior FinSurv approval, an applicable authorised-dealer delegated limit, or a SARS tax-clearance certificate. For a fast-fashion e-commerce importer/retailer (ISIC 4791), ordinary trade payables, consumer-payout receipts, and profit remittances are current-account in nature; the principal convertibility hurdles are (i) retention of offshore PSP balances beyond approved settlement windows without a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) approval, (ii) transfer-pricing scrutiny on the margin embedded in cross-border apparel sourcing, and (iii) documentary mismatches between PSP settlement records and customs bills of entry that cause an authorised dealer to suspend payout crediting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, proceeds from exported goods must be received in South Africa within 90 days from the date of shipment (extendable to 180 days with authorised-dealer or FinSurv approval in defined cases); all receipts must be surrendered to an authorised dealer — i.e., converted/credited to a ZAR account — unless FinSurv has approved a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) retention facility; the effective statutory conversion ratio for export receivables is therefore 100%. In the platform model, where offshore PSPs such as Shopify Payments and Klarna collect end-customer proceeds on the entity's behalf, the same surrender discipline applies: net settlement flows must be swept to the ZAR operational account, traceable to order-level e-commerce data within the settlement-cycle terms disclosed to the authorised dealer; any offshore retention requires a documented business case and FCA approval, not merely a balance held on an EMI dashboard.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB); exchange control is administered by SARB's Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, through authorised dealers (resident commercial banks licensed under the Banks Act, 1990); the Prudential Authority supervises banking institutions, and SARS administers customs, import/export declarations, transfer-pricing and tax-clearance reporting that interact with cross-border treasury flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a primary operating bank. Non-resident digital banks and e-money institutions (UK/FCA EMIs, EU EMIs, offshore payment fintechs) are not authorised dealers under South African exchange control, cannot clear ZAR through the SAMOS RTGS system, and cannot lawfully receive or hold export proceeds that are subject to the SARB surrender rules. Such rails are viable only as complementary payout/collection services — e.g., Shopify Balance, Wise Business, Klarna settlement accounts — provided funds are swept promptly and traceably to a Tier-1 ZAR operational account. A SARB-licensed digital bank resident in South Africa (e.g., TymeBank, Bank Zero) may facilitate eKYC onboarding but generally lacks integrated cross-border treasury, commercial LC, and forward-FX infrastructure; the optimal stack is therefore a Tier-1 commercial bank as the authorised-dealer anchor with fintech rails limited to the consumer-payment layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity subscriptions are structured as SWIFT MT103 payments (with MT202 cover where bank-to-bank credit is routed) into the (Pty) Ltd's Capital Escrow Account or designated ZAR operational account at an authorised dealer; the remittance must bear the purpose/BOP statistical code for inward direct investment (non-resident participation in share capital) and the narrative 'ZA_PTY_LTD — share subscription' referencing the subscription agreement and board resolution. No prior SARB approval is required for a plain non-resident equity injection into an authorised share class, but the authorised dealer will not finalise credit until it has verified the subscription documents, source-of-funds, sanctions screening, CIPC beneficial-ownership position, and, where triggered, the SARS tax-clearance certificate. Shareholder loans must be evidenced by a written loan agreement, bear arm's-length interest, and be recorded for exchange-control repatriation (repayment of principal and interest is a current-account item requiring authorised-dealer documentation). PSP settlement inflows from Klarna and Shopify are routed through the same authorised dealer as BOP service/merchandise flows with platform settlement reports attached.Trade settlements to offshore apparel and footwear suppliers (Bangladesh, Vietnam, China, Turkey) are executed via (i) SWIFT MT103/gpi open-account wire transfers against commercial invoices and packing lists; (ii) commercial letters of credit (MT700, governed by UCP600) for larger seasonal purchase orders requiring documentary security; (iii) documentary collections (documents against payment/acceptance) as a cost-sensitive intermediate; and (iv) spot or forward ZAR/FX conversion at the authorised dealer with a written rate confirmation. Local disbursements (logistics, warehousing, salaries, VAT, and interbank payments) clear through the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) RTGS system using host-to-host banking APIs. Because ZAR is a CLS-eligible currency, USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR, and GBP/ZAR cross-currency legs can achieve CLS payment-versus-payment settlement, eliminating Herstatt risk on the FX transaction. Refunds and chargebacks to consumers are funded from a dedicated PSP float account and reconciled monthly so that pooled gateway funds are never used for treasury speculation.Deliverable forward exchange contracts (FECs) in USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR, and GBP/ZAR concluded with a licensed authorised dealer, supported by genuine underlying import purchase orders or export receivables.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in ZAR, permissible for non-resident counterparties and for resident corporate hedges where FinSurv and authorised-dealer rules permit an NDF as a substitute for an onshore deliverable FEC.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) and bespoke currency swaps to convert and service a USD working-capital facility used for seasonal apparel inventory purchases.European-style FX options with an authorised dealer to hedge timing risk on uncertain e-commerce cash-flow conversion windows and supplier payment dates.Multi-currency pooling and hedging accounts at authorised dealers that allow the sweep of Shopify and Klarna multi-currency settlement balances into a single hedged ZAR conversion programme.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a primary operating bank. Non-resident digital banks and e-money institutions (UK/FCA EMIs, EU EMIs, offshore payment fintechs) are not authorised dealers under South African exchange control, cannot clear ZAR through the SAMOS RTGS system, and cannot lawfully receive or hold export proceeds that are subject to the SARB surrender rules. Such rails are viable only as complementary payout/collection services — e.g., Shopify Balance, Wise Business, Klarna settlement accounts — provided funds are swept promptly and traceably to a Tier-1 ZAR operational account. A SARB-licensed digital bank resident in South Africa (e.g., TymeBank, Bank Zero) may facilitate eKYC onboarding but generally lacks integrated cross-border treasury, commercial LC, and forward-FX infrastructure; the optimal stack is therefore a Tier-1 commercial bank as the authorised-dealer anchor with fintech rails limited to the consumer-payment layer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be prepared and maintained contemporaneously where the aggregate annual value of cross-border affected transactions between the South African company and its non-resident related parties exceeds ZAR 10 million, with a Master File additionally required for multinational groups whose consolidated global revenue is at or above ZAR 1 billion. Country-by-Country reporting and notification obligations arise where the consolidated group revenue is at or above ZAR 10 billion. SARS may direct production of the documentation within 60 days without requiring a formal audit, and failure to produce contemporaneous records elevates the understatement penalty category. A de minimis practical tolerance is applied only where cross-border affected transactions are immaterial, but the e-commerce sourcing model described in this whitepaper will generally exceed the threshold.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute of the type enacted in UAE or Bermuda; instead, substance is tested through the general anti-avoidance rule (s80A to s80L of the Income Tax Act), the controlled foreign company provisions (s9D), the beneficial-ownership requirement in each tax treaty, and the earnings-based interest limitation in s23M (which restricts net interest expense to 30% of tax EBITDA for companies in a controlled group). A fast-fashion retail structure that interposes an offshore brand-owning, financing or procurement company without local premises, qualified full-time employees, managerial independence and substantive risk-bearing activity will be disregarded or re-characterised; the respective sales, logistics and royalty income will be reconstructed as taxable income of the South African operating Pty (Ltd), with DWT imposed on any consequence distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty up to 125% of the additional tax under s222 and s223 of the Tax Administration Act; interest at the prescribed rate on the full shortfall; criminal conviction under s234 and s235 of the Tax Administration Act carrying a fine and/or imprisonment up to two years per count; potential prosecution for common-law fraud where false returns are submitted with intent to prejudice SARS; administrative non-compliance penalties and jeopardy assessments for collection protection.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false ITR14 returns or direct the underreporting of e-commerce revenue face criminal prosecution as principals; SARS can invoke the representative-taxpayer doctrine to recover unpaid VAT, PAYE and provisional tax personally from directors where the company failed to comply with its withholding or filing duties; courts may pierce the corporate veil under s77 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 on grounds of reckless/wrongful trading; SARS may seek travel prohibition orders preventing directors from leaving South Africa while a serious tax debt is outstanding, and civil restorative claims may reach personal assets where company assets were dissipated to evade collection.
