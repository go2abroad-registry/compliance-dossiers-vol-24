Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is an open-market convertible currency for current-account transactions, while capital-account transactions are liberalised but remain subject to authorised-dealer due diligence, BOP/eBOP reporting and underlying documentation requirements. Since the 2021 reform, inward foreign direct investment requires no prior SARB approval and is effected through a local Authorised Dealer. Outward current payments for genuine operational needs such as testing-reagent purchases, equipment maintenance, calibration services and licences are permitted against a commercial invoice and contract; outward capital flows require authorised-dealer approval under the Manual. Profit remittance in the form of dividends to non-resident shareholders is permissible without FinSurv approval provided the dividend is declared from after-tax retained profits, the underlying inward share capital was previously reported through the banking channel, SARS withholding tax obligations on dividends are satisfied via the dividend tax system, and the bank completes its source-of-funds verification. No exchange-control bottleneck exists for profit repatriation once these conditions are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa abolished compulsory surrender and conversion of export proceeds with effect from 1 January 2021. The mandatory conversion ratio is therefore 0%, and exporters may retain export proceeds indefinitely in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with an Authorised Dealer. The statutory receipt rule requires export proceeds for goods to be received within 180 days from date of shipment, unless the authorised dealer has approved a repayments/credit-term deviation; for service exports such as UN38.3 laboratory testing, proceeds must be received within 180 days from the invoice/service date unless otherwise approved. All receipts must be presented to the receiving bank, captured as a BOP/eBOP transaction, and reconciled to the corresponding export invoice; the authorised dealer may require an Export Payment Finalisation Notice (EPFN) where credit terms, extended settlement or factoring arrangements are involved. Routine export finalisation requires no separate FinSurv approval.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB); exchange-control administration is delegated to the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and operationalised exclusively through Authorised Dealers (commercial banks) under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers as revised effective 1 January 2021, read with the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA) and its Regulations.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax levied as a final withholding tax on dividends declared and paid by the South African resident Pty Ltd; this is an entity-level remittance obligation borne by the company and withheld from the shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South African taxpayers must prepare and retain transfer-pricing documentation, being the local file and master file, where cross-border connected-person transactions exceed ZAR 5 million in any relevant category during a year; the OECD-aligned Country-by-Country Report is mandatory in South Africa where the multinational group's consolidated turnover is at least ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no separate statutory 'Economic Substance Regulations' as in the Gulf jurisdictions, but economic substance is enforced through section 31 of the Income Tax Act (transfer pricing), section 80A-L (general anti-avoidance rules), and beneficial-ownership requirements of tax treaties. A battery-safety lab Pty Ltd must show real local employees, equipment ownership, risk assumption, and decision-making; absence of these features will cause SARS to recharacterise intercompany fees, deny treaty benefits, impose understatement penalties, and disregard tax losses.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty under section 222 of the Tax Administration Act of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for intentional non-disclosure; criminal conviction under section 234 may lead to a fine up to ZAR 10 million or imprisonment not exceeding 10 years, plus the evaded tax and compound interest.

Director personal liability:
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS will apply the general anti-avoidance rules and may disregard the Pty Ltd, reallocate the income to the foreign parent, deny the section 20A assessed-loss carryforward, cancel the company's VAT registration, and impose transfer pricing adjustments; the CIPC will also flag failure to file annual returns and may deregister the entity.

Director personal liability:
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the taxable income to arm's-length under section 31, imposes understatement penalties up to 150% of the additional tax, charges interest from the original due date, and may levy withholding tax on any recharacterised deemed dividend or deemed royalty.

Director personal liability:
Improper Dividend Withholding / DTA Relief Abuse

Statutory sanction: SARS imposes the 20% dividends tax on the company, adds a 75%–150% intentional understatement penalty, and is entitled to joint recovery from the responsible company directors who authorised or knowingly allowed the unlawful distribution.

Director personal liability:
Anti-Money Laundering / Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Failure to file a Suspicious Transaction Report attracts a fine up to ZAR 10 million or imprisonment up to 15 years under the FIC Act; test certificates may be cancelled as fraudulent, export controls apply on dangerous-goods certification, and the proceeds of the unlawful testing activity are subject to forfeiture under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act.
