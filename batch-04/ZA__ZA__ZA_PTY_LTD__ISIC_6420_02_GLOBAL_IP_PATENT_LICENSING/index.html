Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), via the SARS eFiling portal at www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must first register as a 'foreign resident' taxpayer with SARS by completing the non-resident taxpayer registration process and obtaining an income tax reference number. If the entity makes taxable supplies in South Africa or is required to account for VAT, it must separately apply for VAT registration under the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991, on Form VAT 101 (or electronically via eFiling). Applicants must submit constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, SARS corporate registration details, and the physical address of the South African representative or place of business.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-incorporated entity must first register as a 'foreign resident' taxpayer with SARS by completing the non-resident taxpayer registration process and obtaining an income tax reference number. If the entity makes taxable supplies in South Africa or is required to account for VAT, it must separately apply for VAT registration under the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991, on Form VAT 101 (or electronically via eFiling). Applicants must submit constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, SARS corporate registration details, and the physical address of the South African representative or place of business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. Statutory elections are limited, including VAT grouping under section 50 of the VAT Act, group taxation relief under the Income Tax Act, and transfer-pricing considerations. Hybrid mismatch rules are largely mandatory and apply via sections 8E, 8EA, 8F, 8FA, 8J, 9D and related provisions, with no elective hybrid-mismatch avoidance mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies in any 12-month period; voluntary registration threshold: ZAR 50,000; foreign suppliers of electronic services must register when total taxable supplies to South African recipients exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available for exported goods when physical export is shipped out of South Africa within the prescribed period and documentary proof is retained. For services and intellectual property, zero rating may apply under section 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act where the supply is made to a non-resident and the services or rights are for the direct benefit or direct use of the non-resident outside South Africa. A patent licensing royalty derived by a South African IP HoldCo from a foreign licensee can qualify for zero rating if the licensed intellectual property is exploited outside South Africa and the supplier holds adequate substantiation, including proof of the non-resident status of the customer and the foreign use or benefit.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500) for physical goods
  • Signed international sales or licensing agreement
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (for goods)
  • Bill of lading or airway bill / proof of physical export
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT / FEC proof of payment in foreign currency
  • Documentary evidence of non-resident status of the foreign customer
  • Retained records showing the foreign direct benefit or foreign exploitation of the licensed intellectual property

SARS requires registered vendors to issue full tax invoices in compliance with section 20 of the VAT Act, with prescribed particulars including vendor VAT number, invoice number, date, customer identity, description, quantity, price and VAT amount. SARS is implementing an eInvoicing framework aligned to international standards, but there is no real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping currently in force. Certain high- and medium-risk vendors have electronic data submission obligations to SARS through the eFiling and data reporting channels, including VAT information verification (VIV) processes.

SARS may conduct forensic audits of zero-rated export and royalty transactions, requiring production of full documentary evidence. If zero rating is incorrectly claimed, SARS will raise an additional assessment for VAT at 15%, plus interest and a penalty under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Understatement penalties may range from 10% up to 200% of the tax shortfall depending on culpability. Where fraudulent intent is established, criminal prosecution may follow under Section 235 of the Tax Administration Act and Section 17 of the VAT Act, with potential fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a non-internationalised controlled currency. Current account transactions, including royalty receipts and outbound IP licence payments, are freely convertible through Authorised Dealers (ADs) under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers, subject to verification, documentation, and reporting. Capital account flows, such as inbound equity subscription and intercompany shareholder loans to a South African resident IP HoldCo, require prior AD 'no objection' or SARB FinSurv approval where the transaction exceeds AD delegated limits. Profit remittance by dividend is permissible without SARB approval where paid from taxed distributable reserves, supported by a dividend declaration and SARB Form MP37/MPC-185, with the AD effecting conversion. Royalty payments to non-resident licensors are permitted under Exchange Control Regulation 10(1)(c) provided the underlying licence agreement is approved by or warranted to the AD and SARS tax compliance verification is satisfied. No material current-account conversion hurdles exist for a documented IP patent licensing treasury, but meticulous purpose code filing and documentary retention are mandatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa abolished mandatory surrender/conversion of export proceeds on 1 October 1996. However, all export proceeds, including royalty income earned by a South African IP licensor from foreign licensees, must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days of the due date or date of invoice, whichever is earlier, under the Currency and Exchanges Manual (export proceeds repatriation rule). Repatriated proceeds may either be converted to ZAR on the spot market or deposited into a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer, which may be retained without conversion. The AD must be supplied with a Form FX or electronic matching of the export/royalty invoice, and late repatriation without a SARB-approved extension constitutes an exchange control offence.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the Exchange Control Authority, with the Prudential Authority for banking supervision and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) for AML/CFT oversight; National Treasury exercises policy oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for a South African resident IP HoldCo. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Currencycloud-type platforms) cannot lawfully hold ZAR operational accounts for a South African resident without being registered as an Authorised Dealer or partnering with a domestic AD; their use as a treasury centre triggers Exchange Control Regulation 10 and FICA non-compliance. An EMIs role is limited to inbound collection origination outside South Africa, with onward routing via SWIFT MT103 to the domestic AD account. A locally licensed fintech (e.g., a registered payment service provider under the National Payment System Act) may offer segregated operational rails but does not replace the AD corporate banking relationship required for royalty remittances, capital injections, and SARB reporting. The AD banking hub remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for inbound equity or shareholder loan funding into the South African IP HoldCo: (i) submit a boarding pack to the AD comprising board and shareholder resolutions, executed subscription or loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and the FICA-complete KYC file; (ii) obtain the AD's 'no objection' or rely on the AD's delegated approval where the inward amount falls within the SARB FinSurv delegated limits for inward foreign investments; (iii) execute the wire via SWIFT MT103 with an appropriate purpose code derived from the Currency and Exchanges Manual (equity subscription: Capital/Equity code; shareholder loan: Intercompany Loan code) and populate field 72 with the agreement reference; (iv) upon receipt, reconcile the inward foreign exchange against the AD's Form BF inward transfer record and retain an inward investment certificate or AD confirmation for the treasury file; (v) ensure the beneficiary reference excludes vague language and instead states the SARB purpose code, invoice or agreement number, and the licensing commercial segment to avoid AML scoring anomalies. For capital injections exceeding AD delegated limits, a formal SARB FinSurv application must precede remittance.Clearing rails for outbound trade and IP disbursements are ranked as follows: (1) SWIFT MT103 for royalty, sub-licence fee, and intercompany IP service payments to foreign beneficiaries, sent only through the AD and backed by a finance pack of invoice, executed licence agreement, royalty calculation schedule, SARS tax compliance status, and, where required, the AD or FinSurv approval letter; (2) SWIFT MT202 COV for treasury internal house transfers between the South African AD hub and offshore foreign currency accounts or for correspondent clearing of larger aggregated disbursements; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) where the IP licence is bundled with goods or technology-hardware trade, with documents negotiated via the Rand clearing mechanism and SAMOS settlement; (4) domestic rails (SAMOS RTGS and the local settlement system) for ZAR payments to South African counterparties such as service providers, refunds, or security deposits.
