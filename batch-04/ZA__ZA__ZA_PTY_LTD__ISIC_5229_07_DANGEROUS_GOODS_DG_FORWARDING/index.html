Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), 299 Bronkhorst Street, Nieuw Muckleneuk, Pretoria; digital portals: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za), SARS Customs Modernisation Single Window (https://www.sars.gov.za/customs-and-excise/customs-modernisation/); ancillary agencies: Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC, https://www.cipc.co.za) and South African Reserve Bank (SARB) Financial Surveillance Department for exchange-control matters.

E-government filing portal

No.); this triggers automatic provisional income-tax registration in SARS's data pool, but confirmation is required on eFiling. 2) Complete SARS registration through eFiling's 'Organisation Registration' function using the RA-01/IT77 form to obtain the 10-digit Income Tax Reference Number (with regional prefix). 3) Register for VAT using form VAT101: compulsory once taxable turnover exceeds or is reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a 12-month period; voluntary registration at ZAR 50,000 p.a. for non-resident suppliers of taxable services. 4) Register as a customs client (importer/exporter) on the SARS Customs Modernisation portal to obtain the unique Customs Client Reference Number (CCR No.). 5) Register for PAYE, UIF, and SDL reference numbers if employees are engaged in the DG forwarding operation. 6) Obtain the statutory DG freight approvals: letter of good standing from the SARS compliance record, Dangerous Goods Declaration accreditation (IMDG/IATA-DGR), and SARS Customs bonded-warehouse/section-60 transit permits where applicable. 7) Maintain a physical or electronic place of business in South Africa; non-resident owners may hold 100% equity without prior SARS approval, but the foreign shareholder loan must comply with SARB currency and Excon history requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a South African tax-resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide taxable income at the 27% corporate income tax rate (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% for earlier years); no fiscal-transparent pass-through classification is available domestically; the entity files an ITR14 annual return and is subject to provisional tax (first, second, and year-end top-up payments). Corporate rules apply under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962: dividends tax at 20% on distributions, no STC; CGT inclusion rate 80% of net capital gain (effective top 21.6%); interest, royalties, and management fees paid offshore are subject to withholding tax at 15% (subject to treaty relief and ITA s.35A procedures).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Available statutory elections/positions include: (i) s.11D research-and-development allowances (150% deduction for qualifying R&D activities); (ii) capital allowance elections under s.12B (depreciable plant and machinery), s.12BA (accelerated wear-and-tear), and s.13quat (industrial buildings); (iii) Special Economic Zone (SEZ) preferential tax election (12% CIT rate, VAT and customs incentives) where addressed at Coega/Richards Bay/OR Tambo premises; (iv) small-business corporation (SBC) graduated tax regime election (s.12E) if turnover ≤ ZAR 20 million and 100% owners are natural persons; (v) turnover-tax regime election under the Sixth Schedule for micro-businesses with turnover ≤ ZAR 1 million; (vi) hybrid-mismatch disallowance election/disclosure under s.23M and s.23N to map structured arrangements; (vii) s.20(1)(a)/20(4) group loss-transfer election within a 100%-owned group; (viii) s.45/47 transfer-pricing rollover elections for intragroup asset transfers. No election exists to treat the company as fiscally transitive; any intention to flow through income or losses has no legal effect.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or a reasonable expectation of exceeding that amount in the following 12 months (s.23(1) VAT Act). Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 per annum for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in South Africa (s.23(4) VAT Act); companies carrying on a wholly or mainly zero-rated or exempt enterprise may apply for voluntary registration at lower thresholds. A (Pty) Ltd engaged in DG freight forwarding must register for VAT as soon as any of the above thresholds is met; failure triggers a deemed supply and an effective date of registration set by SARS from the date liability arose.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT zero-rating is available to a registered DG/freight-forwarding (Pty) Ltd under section 11 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991: (i) s.11(1)(a) — export of goods by the vendor under an export/import arrangement where the vendor retains prescribed proof of export; (ii) s.11(1)(j) — services supplied to a non-resident or an export vendor and directly in connection with exported goods (boat, aircraft, or other movable goods, including DG chemical cargo) if the services are rendered before the goods leave South Africa and the goods are subsequently exported; (iii) s.11(1)(k) — transport services of exported goods, including ancillary loading, handling, and customs-clearance services, where the transport begins in the Republic and ends at a destination outside the SACU if the goods are actually exported; (iv) s.11(2)(l) — services physically performed outside South Africa for a non-resident that is not a vendor and not in respect of imported goods. A 0% tax invoice must reflect the zero rate, and the vendor must collect the export-proof records no later than the prescribed 30-day refund-claim window after the tax period to which the invoice relates. DG freight forwarders must also hold the valid dangerous-goods agent/customs broker accreditation to rely on zero-rating for shipping and handling fees.

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration (SARS Customs Modernisation electronic clearance, with entry number, date stamp, and exit confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing shipment within the prescribed export period, including DG notation (IMDG/IATA-DGR)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an SARB Authorised Dealer/Commercial Bank, or SWIFT MT103/elective FX receipt evidence for the foreign-currency settlement of the freight charges
  • Signed commercial invoice quoting 0% VAT, with HS/UN-number and Dangerous Goods Declaration (DGD) for regulated hazardous chemicals
  • Packing list / DG container packing certificate and Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS)
  • Proof of the SACU border post of exit and, where applicable, transit documents through other SACU member states
  • Freight-forwarding contract (s.11(1)(j)/(k) relationship evidence) identifying the exporter/non-resident recipient and the specific consignment

SARS maintains a dual digital mandate: (i) eFiling for VAT returns (VAT201) and payments is compulsory; (ii) SARS Customs Modernisation requires fully electronic submission of export/import declarations (SAD500), pre-arrival cargo records, and DG consignment notifications through the CDS API. SARS is implementing a phased e-invoicing framework based on an in-house XML/Peppol-aligned standard; from 1 January 2024, a SARS-compliant e-invoicing specification and a service-provider accreditation scheme are in force; B2B paper invoices remain legally valid during the transition, but tax invoices generated through SARS-accredited e-invoicing systems are prescribed for entities above the mandatory-e-invoicing turnover thresholds (announced in phases through 2025-2026). A real-time fiscal-clearance stamp (SARTS-style continuous transaction control) is not yet mandatory for ordinary B2B DG services; however, SARS automatically risk-scores all VAT refunds, applies pre-clearance audit filters on export zero-rating claims, and requires customs release/exit confirmation in the VAT201 interface. Digital stamping (unique QR verification digits) is mandatory for excisable goods only, not for freight forwarding services, although all export SAD500 declarations require digital approval before departure.

SARS and SARB jointly scrutinize export zero-rating and FX repatriation in the DG freight sector. If the prescribed proof of export is deficient, SARS raises a deemed supply assessment: the VAT previously zero-rated is clawed back at 15% (plus interest) from the date of the original return. The Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (TAA) understatement-penalty matrix (s.222-223) applies percentages of the shortfall based on behaviour: substantial understatement 10%, reasonable care not taken 25%, no reasonable grounds 50%, and intentional/impermissible avoidance 75%-150%, with aggravated penalties up to 150% for fraud. Interest accrues automatically at the statutory rate under s.187 TAA (currently approximately 10.5% p.a.). Criminal exposure exists under s.234 TAA (false/fraudulent statements to obtain refund) and s.235 (tax evasion), carrying a fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; repeated contraventions attract mandatory terms. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days violates the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual (export proceeds section, formally A.5.11) and the Exchange Control Regulations; consequences include administrative penalties (R10,000 per contravention), forced disposal of unrepatriated funds, suspension of the exporter's customer code, and a negative exchange-control history that blocks LC/SWIFT credit lines. A retrospective clawback of zero-rating claims can reach back five years for fraudulent conduct and three years for non-fraudulent under-declaration; SARS COVID-induced refund-hold practices have been replaced by an automated validation engine that may withhold refunds until the full SAD500/BOL/remittance bundle is electronically matched.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a managed-float rand (ZAR) that enjoys full current-account convertibility without finite quantitative limits: payments for goods, services, royalties, and repatriation of business profits (dividends) are freely permitted upon presentation of underlying transaction documents to an Authorised Dealer. Capital-account convertibility is asymmetrically controlled: inward foreign equity investment and qualifying foreign borrowings are freely permitted with mandatory reporting to the Authorised Dealer and FinSurv, whereas outward capital flows by South African residents remain constrained by permissibility allowances (single discretionary foreign allowance of ZAR 1 million per calendar year, and the foreign investment allowance of ZAR 10 million subject to SARS tax clearance). Profit remittance to a non-resident shareholder of a (Pty) Ltd is lawful only where (i) the shares carry inward-listed non-resident status and were financed with covered inward funds that were declared on the applicable currency declaration forms at the time of capital import; (ii) the solvency and liquidity provisions of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 are satisfied; and (iii) the 20% dividend withholding tax, reduced under applicable double-taxation agreements, is simultaneously remitted to SARS. All external payments must be settled through an Authorised Dealer; blocked-rand status or emigrant N-form status applies only to emigrants and non-residents holding legacy assets, and therefore a clean inward-listing record at the receiving bank is the essential predicate for lawful dividend and capital repatriation by this DG forwarding entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, export proceeds must be received within 180 days from the date of shipment (extensions are rarely granted and only with a motivated application to FinSurv). The exporting entity, including a dangerous-goods freight forwarder acting as a purchasing and re-invoicing intermediary, must declare exports on the customs SAD500 with the applicable dangerous-goods class annotations; all proceeds received in foreign currency must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer, i.e., credited to a ZAR-denominated operational account, within 30 days of receipt. Non-compliance exposes the entity to administrative penalties, mandatory reporting to the Financial Intelligence Centre for potential structuring indicators, and possible customs hold-codes that suspend future export clearances until the cover is regularised.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv, formerly the Exchange Control Department), under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated in 1961, and the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers. The Prudential Authority, a separate statutory body housed within SARB, regulates licenced commercial banks, while FinSurv delegates day-to-day exchange-control administration to Authorised Dealers (Category I and Category II banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail, and legally constrained for this high-risk dangerous-goods forwarder. Foreign-incorporated digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business, Bunq) are not Authorised Dealers under the South African Exchange Control Regulations; they cannot clear ZAR as a direct member of the South African Multiple-currency Settlement (MCS) system, cannot issue the currency-declaration cover vouchers required for export proceeds, and cannot authenticate Form A/Form C exchange-control transactions for dividend remittance or foreign-loan servicing. Any arrangement under which a South African resident entity holds funds offshore through an EMI creates a prima facie exchange-control contravention by way of an undocumented resident-held offshore balance. EMIs are therefore acceptable only as ancillary collection or disbursement channels for immaterial value outside the regulated treasury spine, and they must be ring-fenced so that all capital flows, export-surrender obligations, and trade settlement remain domiciled at a licenced Category I commercial bank.

Compliant routing for the non-resident equity injection: (i) the foreign shareholder executes a share subscription agreement and board allotment resolution; (ii) SWIFT MT103 is transmitted from the shareholder's home bank to the (Pty) Ltd ZAR operational account at the SA Authorised Dealer, with the narrative field stating 'Share subscription - [entity name] - inward capital'; (iii) the payment is referenced under IMF Balance of Payments statistical purpose code SA.1.2.0 (direct investment - equity) in the bank's BOP returns; (iv) the receiving bank verifies the currency-declaration voucher and affixes inward-listed status to the shareholding; (v) the issued shares are filed with CIPC and the inward-transfer certificate is archived for future dividend remission.
