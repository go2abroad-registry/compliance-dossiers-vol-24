Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; company incorporation via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) with subsequent mandatory SARS registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African (Pty) Ltd must first be incorporated through the CIPC and obtain a company registration number. It must then register with SARS for income tax, obtaining a taxpayer reference number (income tax number). If the entity's taxable turnover meets or exceeds the VAT registration threshold (currently ZAR 1,000,000 per consecutive 12-month period), it must register as a VAT vendor via the SARS eFiling portal (VAT101/VAT102 forms). The entity must also register for PAYE/UIF/SDL if it appoints employees, and for customs and excise if clearing export transactions through SARS Customs. A foreign parent company does not receive a South African TIN solely by owning a subsidiary; it becomes taxable only if it creates a permanent establishment in South Africa, in which case it must register for income tax and VAT through a SARS-appointed representative taxpayer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in South African law. A (Pty) Ltd is automatically taxed at entity level as a resident company if incorporated in South Africa, subject to worldwide income tax. Relevant statutory elections and mechanisms include VAT group registration under section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act, group relief/ring-fencing elections under sections 41 to 47 of the Income Tax Act, the tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping operators under section 34C (not ordinarily available to NVOCC purely as freight forwarders unless operating qualifying SA-registered ships), section 9(2C) foreign employment timing elections, and the hybrid mismatch rules under sections 7D–7L of the Income Tax Act which operate by self-assessment rather than elective check-the-box treatment. Cross-border arrangements involving hybrid instruments or hybrid entities must be documented and reported to SARS where the specified anti-hybrid outcomes apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when taxable supplies exceed or will exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available to vendors whose taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods are supplied in a sale and the vendor has, within the prescribed period (normally 90 days after date of supply), delivered the goods to a destination outside South Africa and retains prescribed export evidence. Services may be zero-rated where they are physically rendered outside South Africa to a non-resident, or where the services are directly connected with exported goods and supplied to the exporter/non-resident. For an NVOCC ocean freight forwarding operation, the arranging of foreign ocean carriage and ancillary services in respect of goods being exported may qualify for zero-rating to the extent the services are supplied to a non-resident or are directly and demonstrably connected to goods physically exported from South Africa; domestic logistics, warehousing, and forwarding services performed solely within South Africa generally remain subject to 15% VAT unless a specific zero-rating provision applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500) duly lodged and released by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing carriage of the goods to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank statement or proof of foreign payment transfer into South Africa
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Proof of delivery to the exporter's foreign destination, including transport documents, container tracking, or port handling receipts

South Africa does not currently impose a generalized real-time fiscal clearance, digital tax-stamping, or mandatory government-certified e-invoicing regime. SARS permits the issue of tax invoices in electronic form provided the invoice retains all required content and accurately reflects the supply, subject to the VAT Act section 20 and VAT Guide for Vendors. Large businesses and all VAT vendors must file VAT201 returns electronically through SARS eFiling or the SARS Modernization of Digital Systems (MODS) interface. SARS has been moving toward enhanced data verification and automated risk-flagging of VAT returns, but there is currently no 'clearance-before-invoice' API mandate for ordinary VAT vendors.

SARS systematically audits zero-rated export supplies and may claw back input tax and output VAT assessments if a vendor fails to retain the prescribed export evidence. The understatement penalty provisions of the Tax Administration Act impose penalties ranging from 10% to 150% of the assessed tax shortfall depending on careless, negligent, or intentional conduct; a 'substantial understatement' of not less than 10% of the tax charged triggers enhanced penalties. Fraudulent claims to zero-rating, false export documents, or unlawful procurement of VAT refunds are criminal offences under the Tax Administration Act and the VAT Act, carrying a fine of up to R100,000 per offence, imprisonment of up to 10 years, and joint and several civil liability for directors/controlling persons who knowingly participate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is a floating currency with asymmetric convertibility. The current account is liberally convertible: receipts and payments for ocean freight services, NVOCC commissions, co-loading margin, and ancillary logistics disbursements flow freely through Authorised Dealers upon presentation of commercial contracts, invoices, bills of lading, and clearing confirmations. The capital account is controlled: a non-resident equity subscription into a ZA (Pty) Ltd is processed on the parallel Direct Inward Listing (DIL) mechanism, requiring only an Authorised Dealer's Form C report to SARB/FinSurv without prior head-office approval, provided the corporate structure, beneficial ownership forms, and SARS tax compliance status are verified. Profit repatriation (dividend remittance) is lawful where the company holds a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN, the dividend is declared from realised retained earnings, the applicable Dividends Tax under s.64C–64H of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 is withheld, and the Authorised Dealer validates supporting board minutes. The practical barrier is not convertibility but documentary substantiation: each outward remittance above prescribed FIC/SARB thresholds requires a SARS compliance-status clearance, a source-of-funds trail, and a sharp economic-substance narrative, with 100% surrender of repatriated export consideration within the statutory window.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For an ocean-freight forwarder billing in USD/EUR/GBP for services rendered to non-residents (or to residents within SACU), SARB FinSurv applies a two-stage discipline: (i) Repatriation: export consideration for services must be received in South Africa within 180 days of the date of the export invoice; any extension requires a formal FinSurv application through the Authorised Dealer, supported by debtor ageing and default evidence. (ii) Surrender/Conversion: within 30 days of receipt, 100% of foreign-currency proceeds must be either sold to the Authorised Dealer at the ruling spot rate or, if the forwarder holds a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) authority, retained onshore for a tenor consistent with the trade cycle and natural-hedging limits. There is no statutory surcharge ratio or partial-surrender threshold for freight receivables; the default obligation is full conversion. Failure to surrender within the timeline constitutes an exchange-control offence and triggers enhanced FIW screening, restriction of the entity's dealing permissions, and possible administrative penalties under the 1933 Act.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1961 (Government Notice R.1111), with day-to-day exchange-control administration delegated to Authorised Dealers (the licensed commercial banking network).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a complementary treasury overlay, LOW as a primary financial-account substitute. A foreign digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Globalize) is not a recognised bank in South Africa; it cannot hold ZAR in a domestic settlement account or execute ACH/RTC clearing except through a licensed South African bank as sponsor under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 and the SARB RediShare/PayShap rules. For an NVOCC (Pty) Ltd, the legal constraints are: (i) FICA s.21 requires a bank or a registered accountable institution to perform CDD on the company and its 5%+ beneficial owners; most EMIs pass this back to the sponsor bank, creating duplication and 5–8 week timelines; (ii) SARB categorises cross-border remittance providers as 'money remitters' requiring registration with the SARB Prudential Authority and the FinSurv FX dealer permit; (iii) client freight money collected on behalf of shippers/carriers may touch the EMI rail only as disbursement, but the company would breach customary trust-account segregation expectations under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 client-funds practice if the EMI holds the float. Conclusion: adopt a hybrid model: SA licensed Tier-1 bank as the statutory endpoint for ZAR clearing, FIC reporting, SARS linkage, and client-fund segregation; EMIs retained exclusively for small-value offshore carrier commissions, foreign agent payables, and the group's monthly SaaS/telecom cross-border costs.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be submitted within the SARS assigned filing season, generally no later than 12 months after the company's financial year-end. A formal notice of filing date is served to the registered taxpayer.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional payment: within six months of financial year-end; second provisional payment: before the end of the financial year; voluntary third: six months after year-end.

For a foreign company branch, taxable income is the South Africa-source income attributable to the permanent establishment under the applicable DTA. Monthly late-filing penalties ranging from ZAR 250 to ZAR 2,000 per month based on taxable turnover, plus prescribed interest on unpaid tax and, for deliberate non-compliance, an understatement penalty of up to 200%.
Line 12 (Corporate Tax at 27%) Tax computed at 27%; reduced 15% rate applies only to qualifying Special Economic Zone operators. Branch remittance withholding tax applies to after-tax branch profits remitted abroad, at the same rate as dividends withholding tax. Monthly late-filing penalties ranging from ZAR 250 to ZAR 2,000 per month based on taxable turnover, plus prescribed interest on unpaid tax and, for deliberate non-compliance, an understatement penalty of up to 200%.
Box A (Estimated Taxable Income) Taxpayer's projection of total taxable income for the full assessment year. A non-resident branch must base the estimate solely on expected South African attributable taxable income. A 20% penalty on the shortfall between the estimated taxable income and the assessed taxable income, plus interest at the prescribed rate, where the estimate falls materially below the prior year taxable income or SARS-determined estimate.
Box B (Provisional Tax Payable) Half of the estimated ultimate tax liability for each semi-annual payment. Payments must be funded from South African bank accounts in ZAR. A 20% penalty on the shortfall between the estimated taxable income and the assessed taxable income, plus interest at the prescribed rate, where the estimate falls materially below the prior year taxable income or SARS-determined estimate.
Box C (Previous Provisional Payments) Cumulative total of earlier provisional tax payments to calculate the balance due. If no prior payment, this line is zero. A 20% penalty on the shortfall between the estimated taxable income and the assessed taxable income, plus interest at the prescribed rate, where the estimate falls materially below the prior year taxable income or SARS-determined estimate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory if aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed ZAR 50 million per tax year. Master File: mandatory if aggregate related-party transactions exceed ZAR 100 million. Country-by-Country Report: mandatory for a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, but the Income Tax Act applies the general anti-avoidance rules (s80A–80L), controlled foreign company rules (s9D), and substance-over-form doctrine. A non-resident intermediary or shell company without substantive functions, assets, personnel, or risk in South Africa will be disregarded and income reallocated to the entity performing the economically significant activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150%–200% for intentional or fraudulent conduct under section 222 of the Tax Administration Act; interest on understated tax; criminal prosecution with a fine and/or imprisonment of up to ten years.

Director personal liability: SARS may hold directors jointly and severally liable under section 225 of the Tax Administration Act where a company liquidates or disposes of assets to evade tax; directors may face civil restitution, travel bans, and disqualification as directors.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke GAAR to disregard the company, deny treaty benefits, recharacterize income, and apply a 150% understatement penalty for a ‘restrictive arrangement’ lacking substance; failure to maintain transfer pricing documentation attracts administrative penalties up to ZAR 1 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve intercompany transactions without substantive commercial rationale may be personally liable for resulting tax inadequacies; knowingly signing false declarations triggers criminal liability and potential restitution orders under the Companies Act.
AML / FICA Violation

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment up to 15 years for money laundering offences; administrative penalties for accountable institutions under FICA.

Director personal liability: Directors who are knowingly involved, or who fail to act with reasonable care, can face criminal liability; regulatory enforcement may impose director disqualification and personal monetary penalties.
