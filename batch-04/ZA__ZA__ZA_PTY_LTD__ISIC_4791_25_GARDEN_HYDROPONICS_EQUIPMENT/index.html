Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) - official portal: https://www.sars.gov.za; eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic company incorporated under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 must register for income tax and, if making taxable supplies in or for South Africa, for VAT. For income tax: register via SARS eFiling under 'Register for Tax' or through an appointed tax practitioner to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number. For VAT: where total taxable supplies exceeded or are reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in any 12-month period, registration is compulsory on form VAT 101; non-resident suppliers of electronic services may be subject to the simplified standard VAT registration route. A non-resident company carrying on a trade in South Africa must register for tax under section 44 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a South African-resident private company is taxed as a separate legal person under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 at the prevailing corporate income tax rate on worldwide income, with foreign tax credits available. Non-residents are subject to tax on South African-source income only, and branch operations of foreign companies are treated as permanent establishments.

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box entity classification election. A company's tax status is determined by its legal form and incorporation. Available limited elections include voluntary VAT registration where taxable supplies exceed R50,000 but do not exceed the R1,000,000 compulsory threshold, the election by qualifying non-resident electronic services suppliers to register for VAT, and certain hybrid mismatch reporting elections under the controlled foreign company and anti-hybrid rules; no general entity-level pass-through election exists for a (Pty) Ltd.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in total taxable supplies made in the preceding 12 months or on an ongoing expected basis; voluntary registration is available for persons making taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 50,000 per 12-month period. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services are subject to a ZAR 1,000,000 registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT zero-rating may be claimed under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods are sold under an agreement of sale and are physically exported from South Africa to a country other than South Africa by the vendor, provided the goods are exported within the stated statutory period and the vendor obtains and retains sufficient documentary proof of export before the relevant VAT return is due. Services physically rendered outside South Africa or relating to exported movable property may also qualify under section 11(2). The vendor must retain adequate and authentic documentation to substantiate each zero-rated supply.

  • SAD500 customs export declaration / bill of entry cleared by SARS Customs as evidence of lawful export
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing outward movement and port or airport of shipment
  • Commercial invoice and supply agreement identifying the customer, goods, values, and payment terms
  • Proof of payment / inward remittance credit in respect of the export transaction, evidencing foreign currency receipt or bank settlement
  • Transfer or release documentation from the export logistics provider confirming physical removal from South African territory

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate currently applies to standard VAT vendors. SARS eFiling is used for return submission and payment. Electronic tax invoices are permitted if issued under section 20(5) of the VAT Act and contain all prescribed particulars under section 20(1) to (4); supporting digital records and invoices must be retained in full for at least five years under the Tax Administration Act. No compulsory API-based invoice clearance platform is in force at the date of this dossier.

SARS may challenge export zero-rating where documentary evidence is missing, inconsistent, or demonstrates no physical export. In such cases the disputed supply is recharacterised as taxable at 15% VAT, with additional VAT, interest under section 187 of the Tax Administration Act, and understatement penalties according to the conduct involved: 25% for failure to take reasonable care, 50% for an unreasonable tax position, 100% for gross negligence, and up to 150% for intentional tax evasion. Fraudulent export VAT claims may also trigger criminal liability, fines, and imprisonment under the VAT Act and Tax Administration Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is convertible for current account transactions with minimal administrative friction, but capital account transactions remain subject to SARB exchange-control approval and authorised-dealer oversight. Profit remittance through cash dividends is permitted once the company has completed required compliance filings (e.g., tax clearance, dividend declaration, and inward payment documentation), but the remittance must be routed through an authorised dealer and may require pro-forma invoices, audited financial statements, and evidence that no foreign funding obligations remain unrestored.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless an extension is approved. Exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in a designated Foreign Currency Account (FCA) without a mandatory conversion ratio to ZAR; however, all export transactions must be reported through the Central Bank's electronic declarations, and prompt conversion of retained foreign currency is expected where operating expenses are incurred in ZAR. Unutilised export facilities must be reconciled by the South African Revenue Service and SARB on an annual basis.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not individually licensed as banks under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 and cannot hold ZAR client funds in South Africa without partnering with a licensed local bank. Their practical use is limited to foreign-currency collection, card issuing, and settlement orchestration; the primary business account must remain with an authorised dealer in South Africa to meet SARB reporting and payment-rail requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder-loan proceeds must be received through an authorised dealer in South Africa. The company must provide a direct-inward-investment declaration, the investor's bank reference, a board resolution allotting shares or confirming the loan agreement, and clear purpose-of-payment code references such as 'Equity Capital' or 'Shareholder Loan'. Upon receipt the authorised dealer issues and inward investment certificate, which is required for future dividend and interest remittances. This certificate must be retained as the foundational FX-repatriation evidence.Outbound supplier and operational payments are cleared through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers, SWIFT MT202/202 COV for interbank and high-value transfers, commercial letters of credit (UCP600, typically L/C at sight), and electronic funds transfers processed through the South African Automated Clearing House. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A compulsory independent financial audit is triggered under Section 30(2A) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 where the company's public interest score (PIS) is 350 or more; the PIS is calculated as one point per employee, three points per ZAR 1 million (or portion) of turnover, three points per ZAR 1 million (or portion) of third-party liabilities, and one point per individual holding a beneficial interest in the company's securities.
