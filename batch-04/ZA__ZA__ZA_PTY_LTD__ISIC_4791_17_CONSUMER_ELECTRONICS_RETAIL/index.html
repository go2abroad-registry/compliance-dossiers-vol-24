Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register through SARS eFiling: (1) obtain an Income Tax Reference Number using the non-resident/foreign company registration form and appoint a SARS-recognized local tax representative; (2) register for VAT as a vendor via form VAT 101 when taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold, or immediately from first supply for foreign electronic services suppliers; (3) obtain a SARS Customs code by submitting Customs Progressive Registration (SAD 104) for import/export activities; (4) provide proof of incorporation, foreign tax number, and banking information; compliance with exchange control documentation through an Authorised Dealer is also required for trade flows.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must register through SARS eFiling: (1) obtain an Income Tax Reference Number using the non-resident/foreign company registration form and appoint a SARS-recognized local tax representative; (2) register for VAT as a vendor via form VAT 101 when taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold, or immediately from first supply for foreign electronic services suppliers; (3) obtain a SARS Customs code by submitting Customs Progressive Registration (SAD 104) for import/export activities; (4) provide proof of incorporation, foreign tax number, and banking information; compliance with exchange control documentation through an Authorised Dealer is also required for trade flows.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists. A South African (Pty) Ltd is per se an opaque, resident company subject to income tax at entity level. Available statutory elections include: section 41/45 intra-group asset-for-share roll-over elections; section 24J debt instrument accrual election; section 11D research-and-development allowance election; and election to treat certain foreign business establishments as permanent establishments under s 9D. Hybrid mismatch rules impose mandatory non-deduction or inclusion outcomes and do not permit elective reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 taxable supplies in a 12-month period for locally established enterprises; voluntary registration permitted from ZAR 50,000 per 12-month period. Foreign suppliers of electronic services have no turnover threshold and must register from their first taxable supply to South African customers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may be zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act when supplied under an export sale or consignment and exported to a non-resident destination within 90 days of the time of supply, or within such further period as SARS may allow, provided the supplier retains proof of export. Services may be zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident not in South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not consumed or used in South Africa, or where the services are physically rendered outside South Africa to a non-resident. The supplier must retain valid documentary evidence, including export declarations, bills of lading/air waybills, commercial invoices, and proof that foreign currency consideration has been received in accordance with exchange control rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement confirming receipt of foreign currency payment within 180 days
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List and proof of despatch

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control. SARS eFiling is the statutory channel for VAT returns and payments. VAT invoices must meet the prescribed content requirements under the VAT Act and record keeping rules. SARS has published a prospective standard e-invoicing framework, but no mandatory API clearance regime is in force.

SARS may reverse an improperly claimed zero-rating, assess VAT at 15%, and impose interest. The Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 imposes understatement penalties of 10% to 100% of the shortfall, with 100% applying to intentional tax evasion. The Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 permits additional penalties of up to 100% of the value of the goods, and criminal liability can reach ZAR 75,000 fines and imprisonment up to five years for fraudulent export claims. SARS may audit retrospectively beyond the general 5-year assessment period where fraud or misrepresentation is established.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared where aggregate affected transactions exceed ZAR 100 million in a year of assessment; Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion; Country-by-Country notification and filing thresholds follow the same ZAR 10 billion group-revenue test.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate an offshore Economic Substance Regulations regime. SARS applies the common-law substance-over-form doctrine, the general anti-avoidance rules in Sections 80A-80L, and the controlled foreign company rules in Section 9D. A retail (Pty) Ltd must maintain real substance through employees, supplier contracts, inventory decisions, bank accounts, and board control in South Africa, otherwise cross-border deductions and treaty benefits may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: SARS can raise an additional assessment without limitation in cases of fraud, levy an understatement penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall, charge interest at the prescribed rate, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution with fines and possible imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or benefit from the false filing can be made personally liable for the tax debt, face civil restitution proceedings, be subjected to travel bans, and potentially be disqualified or imprisoned under the Tax Administration Act and Companies Act.
Shell Company and Abusive Lacking-Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the arrangement under Sections 80A-80L, disallow deductions, impute income to the South African entity, impose a 75% impermissible-avoidance understatement penalty, and deny dividend treaty benefits where beneficial ownership is not genuinely established.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the company is a facade; directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties, subjected to asset attachment, travel restrictions in high-value investigations, and prosecuted if fraud is proved.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: SARS may re-compute taxable income under Section 31, impose a transfer pricing adjustment, deem an interest-bearing loan as a secondary adjustment, and assess understatement penalties up to 150% of the additional tax where the non-arm's-length pricing is fraudulent or grossly negligent.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve, sign, or prepare false transfer pricing documentation may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax debt; civil recovery, travel bans, and imprisonment can follow where the arrangement is found to be a deliberate tax avoidance scheme.
AML and Financial-Crime Non-Compliance in Digital Payments

Statutory sanction: Under FICA, sanctions include administrative fines up to ZAR 10 million for accountable institutions and criminal penalties with imprisonment for money-laundering offences; payment processors may terminate the merchant account, and the company may face a compliance order or frozen funds.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who connive in or negligently permit financial crime can be held criminally liable, incur personal civil liability for corporate losses, face travel bans, and be subject to extradition or imprisonment where money laundering is established.
