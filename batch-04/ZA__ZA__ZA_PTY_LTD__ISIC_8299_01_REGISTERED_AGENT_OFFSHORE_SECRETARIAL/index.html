Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions (invisible and trade transactions) for both residents and non-residents. Capital account transactions are subject to exchange control oversight: inward foreign direct investment (equity subscription in a (Pty) Ltd) is freely permitted and only requires reporting through an Authorized Dealer, whereas outward capital remittances by residents require AD approval or FinSurv consent. Profit remittance (dividends) to non-residents is permitted without prior FinSurv approval, subject to the 20% Dividends Tax, a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status, and standard AML/KYC verification of the beneficial owner. Since the April 2021 Exchange Control Manual reforms, the regime is transaction-based rather than asset-based, and the practical archive requirement is a complete resident/non-resident status record for all counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For South African residents earning foreign currency from exports of services (including corporate secretarial and registered agent fees from offshore clients), the proceeds must be received in South Africa within 30 days of the due date for payment and within 180 days of the invoice date or service completion. Received foreign currency must be surrendered (converted) to a ZAR account with an Authorized Dealer within 30 days of receipt, unless the company maintains an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA), in which case the proceeds may be retained for up to 12 months (renewable with AD consent) for working capital. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% of export/foreign service proceeds where no FCA is held; no statutory proportionality exception applies to a Proprietary Limited company earning service fees from non-residents.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder. The Minister of Finance delegates exchange control administration to SARB/FinSurv, which in turn delegates day-to-day transactional authority to Authorized Dealers (South African banks with AD status).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW. Offshore non-bank EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Payoneer) that do not hold Authorized Dealer status under the Currency and Exchanges Act cannot lawfully serve as the primary money-movement rail for a South African resident (Pty) Ltd. Exchange Control Regulations require all receipts from and payments to non-residents to be routed through an Authorized Dealer (a South African bank with AD status) or to be specifically authorized by FinSurv. Additionally, the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) requires South African accountable institutions to verify the origin of funds; bank credit trails originating from an unlicensed EMI create adverse risk scoring. An EMI may be used only as a supplementary collection channel where the underlying client is offshore and the final settlement is swept into the company's ZAR AD account, with each remittance documented and reported. The recommended architecture is a South African AD bank account as the primary treasury hub, supplemented by an offshore branch of a licenced bank (e.g., Standard Bank/ABSA London or Isle of Man) where segregated client escrow is required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity subscription into the (Pty) Ltd requires no prior FinSurv approval: inward foreign direct investment is permissible and is reported by the Authorized Dealer bank under the South African Balance of Payments classification 'Direct Investment – equity capital'. The compliant wire protocol is an inward SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank cover) credited to the company's ZAR or Foreign Currency Account at the AD bank, accompanied by: (i) the bank's inward transfer declaration (historically Form A, now an electronic BOP declaration) lodged within 30 days; (ii) a corporate resolution allotting shares to the non-resident investor; (iii) an updated share register and CIPC beneficial ownership return; (iv) a statement confirming the investor's non-resident status and country of tax residence; and (v) source-of-funds documentation for amounts exceeding AML thresholds. Inward shareholder loans with a tenor exceeding 12 months require specific FinSurv/AD approval; short-term trade credit up to 12 months is permissible without prior approval. Conversion of inbound capital into ZAR must occur only through the Authorized Dealer; no non-AD conversion channel is lawful.Outbound service disbursements (software licensing, correspondent legal fees, offshore secretarial subcontractor fees, dividends to non-resident shareholders) are current account transactions that may be settled without prior FinSurv approval, but must be executed through an Authorized Dealer bank. The standard clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer for direct payments and MT202 COVER for interbank settlement; (ii) documentary commercial letters of credit (MT700 issue, MT710 confirmation, MT754 advice of payment) where the transaction is trade-backed; (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for invoice-backed service flows. Release conditions imposed by ADs: (a) valid SARS Tax Compliance Status / PIN verification for outward remittances above the prevailing threshold (historically ZAR 250,000; banks apply the then-current FinSurv manual); (b) settlement of dividend tax (20%) to SARS at the time a distribution is paid; (c) withholding tax on royalties (15%, or treaty-reduced rate supported by a tax residency certificate) where applicable; (d) underlying contracts, invoices, and a beneficial-interest declaration where payment is made to a non-resident; and (e) Enhanced Due Diligence and, where necessary, FinSurv approval for payments destined to high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions.Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) with an Authorized Dealer bank covering documented underlying trade or service exposures, with tenor not exceeding 12 months (extendable to 24 months with AD consent); prohibited for speculative purposes.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) executed through an Authorized Dealer to hedge ZAR volatility on non-repatriable offshore service income, provided a genuine underlying exposure is evidenced and the position is non-speculative.Cross-currency swaps (including Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps, CCIRS) with AD approval for matched foreign-currency asset-liability exposures, subject to FinSurv notification in capital transactions; plain vanilla agreements under the ISDA Master Agreement are standard.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% of taxable income (normal South African corporate income tax rate applicable to years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022; formerly 28%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on the amount of any dividend paid by a South African resident company to a non-resident shareholder, unless an exemption or a reduced treaty rate is applicable.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual ITR14 must be filed within 12 months after the end of the company's financial year, unless SARS prescribes an earlier date in a notice under section 25 of the Tax Administration Act. Provisional tax payments are due on the six-month anniversary of the tax year, at the company's financial year-end, and six months after the financial year-end for the top-up instalment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 10 billion consolidated group revenue is the threshold that triggers mandatory Master File, Local File and Country-by-Country Report preparation for multinational groups under the SARS transfer pricing documentation rules and OECD BEPS Action 13 framework. Below that threshold, the taxpayer must still maintain transaction-level documentation demonstrating arm’s length pricing, including a functional analysis and a contemporaneous selection of the appropriate OECD method.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime in the Caribbean/offshore sense. Instead, SARS applies substantive tests through the corporate residence rules (effective management and incorporation), PE attribution analysis, Controlled Foreign Company attribution rules in section 9D, and the general anti-avoidance rules in sections 80A–80L and 103. A registered-agent or offshore secretarial company earning foreign-source income, or interposing offshore vehicles, must demonstrate genuine local economic substance: physical premises, employees, board minutes, decision-making functions and commercial justification at its South African registered office. Failure to do so can result in recharacterisation, disallowance of the intended tax treatment and imposition of the full statutory penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Dishonest Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under section 234 of the Tax Administration Act: fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years. In addition, SARS may impose a 100% or 200% additional assessment for an impermissible tax avoidance arrangement under section 223, plus default assessment, a 10% late-payment penalty and statutory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or instruct evasion may be investigated by SARS for fraud and theft; upon conviction they face imprisonment, travel restrictions and personal fines. Under section 159 of the Tax Administration Act, directors may be held jointly and severally liable for company tax debts in specified circumstances, and civil recovery proceedings may reach personal assets where the evasion caused the company to fail in its revenue obligations.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke the general anti-avoidance rules (sections 80A–80L and 103 of the Income Tax Act) to disregard steps, recharacterise the arrangement and deny the tax benefit, with an added tax of 100% or 200% under section 223 of the Tax Administration Act. The Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) may also intervene under section 22 of the Companies Act where a company is being used against public policy, including potential deregistration or director disqualification.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the company to trade without genuine substance may be liable in civil proceedings for losses arising from false or reckless trading, and may be personally summoned in recovery proceedings by SARS or the company’s creditors. In serious cases, criminal liability under the tax and company law can lead to imprisonment and travel bans.
AML / FICA Non-Compliance by Registered Agent

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, administrative penalties can reach ZAR 10 million or twice the value of the benefit derived from the contravention, whichever is higher. Criminal prosecution is also possible, with a fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years for serious breaches, and the FIC may issue compliance orders requiring rectification and disgorgement.

Director personal liability: Directors who consent or negligently permit AML breaches are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, civil penalty orders, asset seizure through FIC enforcement action, and potential travel bans. Directors may also be disqualified from serving as company directors under the Companies Act for serious wrongdoing.
Transfer Pricing and Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income to the arm’s length amount under section 31 of the Income Tax Act. Failure to provide contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation can attract fixed daily penalties (up to ZAR 50,000 plus recurring penalties) under the Tax Administration Act, together with a 100% or 200% added tax for impermissible avoidance, statutory interest and possible criminal prosecution for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or negligently fail to review tax and transfer pricing arrangements may be liable under section 159 of the Tax Administration Act for unpaid corporate tax, and their personal liability may be pursued in court. Deliberate falsification of the transfer pricing file triggers criminal liability, imprisonment and travel restrictions.
