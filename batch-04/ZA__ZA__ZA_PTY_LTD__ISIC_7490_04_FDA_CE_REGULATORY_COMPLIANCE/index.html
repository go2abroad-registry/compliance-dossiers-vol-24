Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
South African Revenue Service (SARS); primary digital portal: SARS eFiling (https://efiling.sars.gov.za).

        
E-government filing portal
Foreign entities must first incorporate as a private company or register as an external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Thereafter apply for an Income Tax reference number through SARS eFiling using the CIPC registration certificate and SARS combined registration process for Income Tax, VAT, PAYE/UIF/SDL, and Customs. A South African responsible person or authorised representative may be required. For VAT, register for a VAT number if taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (compulsory) or exceed R50,000 (voluntary). For export transactions, obtain a SARS Customs client code and, where applicable, an import/export code prior to executing zero-rated supplies.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident companies are taxed on worldwide income, non-resident companies are taxed on South African-source income; a (Pty) Ltd is not a pass-through entity and is subject to corporate income tax at 27%. — Foreign entities must first incorporate as a private company or register as an external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation; resident companies are taxed on worldwide income, non-resident companies are taxed on South African-source income; a (Pty) Ltd is not a pass-through entity and is subject to corporate income tax at 27%.

        
Available election forms
No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a (Pty) Ltd. Entity classification follows the legal personality conferred by the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Statutory mechanics include VAT grouping election under section 50 of the VAT Act, annual accounting and basis of accounting elections, and the application of controlled foreign company rules and hybrid mismatch rules (sections 23M–23X of the Income Tax Act) where cross-border payments are involved.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
15%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Compulsory registration: taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period or realistic future projection; voluntary registration: taxable supplies exceed R50,000 per 12-month period.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating applies to the export of movable goods where the supplier exports the goods from South Africa under SARS customs supervision and retains the required documentary proof. Supplies of services to non-residents, including professional/regulatory consulting services, may qualify for zero-rating under VAT Act section 11(2)(l) where the services are not used in South Africa and satisfy the statutory export service conditions. Evidence must demonstrate that the goods left South Africa within the prescribed export period and that payment was received from abroad. Compliance with the VAT export rules is required to claim input tax deductions and refunds.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-mandated e-invoicing or digital stamping regime is currently imposed by SARS. VAT vendors must issue valid tax invoices with prescribed particulars, retain records for five years, and submit VAT201 returns electronically via eFiling. SARS is progressively modernising e-invoicing standards, but there is no immediate CTC/continuous transaction control mandate.

      
Invalid or unsupported zero-rating is treated as a taxable supply at 15%, resulting in assessment, output tax, interest, and understatement penalties (up to 50%–150% depending on conduct), additional tax for deliberate non-compliance, and possible criminal prosecution for fraud. SARS can retrospectively claw back refunds and apply penalties for false declarations or failure to meet documentary and export-proceeds remittance requirements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Africa operates a partially regulated exchange-control regime. Current-account payments for professional and consulting services are broadly convertible and remittable, provided that supporting documentation (service agreement, tax invoice, and proof of service delivery) is presented to the authorised dealer and the applicable Balance of Payments (BOP) purpose code is accurately captured. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity subscription, shareholder loans, and outward investment, are subject to SARB FinSurv general approvals or individual approvals and must be routed through normal banking channels with an executed SARB Form A (Inward Transfer of Funds) or Form B (Outward Transfer of Funds), as applicable. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are lawful once the underlying share capital or shareholder funding was originally introduced into South Africa through an authorised dealer and recorded on the company's Annexure 'A' (listing of foreign equity and loan funding). Any such remittance is subject to a 20% dividends withholding tax (DWT) under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, with no exchange-control conversion limit on the Rand amount to be repatriated. No surrender of services-export receipts at a fixed percentage is imposed, although all foreign-currency receipts must be converted to Rand on receipt unless an authorised-dealer-approved foreign-currency account is maintained.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under Chapter VI of the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, proceeds of exported services, including MedTech regulatory consulting fees, must be received in full within 180 days from the date of the invoice or, where a service contract specifies milestones, from the date of completion of the milestone. Receipts in USD, EUR, GBP, or other foreign currency must be credited to the resident company's Rand-denominated operational account and converted to ZAR immediately upon receipt, unless the authorised dealer has granted express approval to maintain a foreign-currency account (FCA) under a general or specific approval. There is no fixed mandatory surrender ratio for services; however, 100% of the Rand-equivalent of all export proceeds must be accounted for to the bank via the BOP reporting system, and the bank must be furnished with the supporting invoice and inward SWIFT reference. Failure to receive proceeds within 180 days constitutes an exchange-control contravention requiring a FinSurv application for extension or condonation. For consulting engagements, advance payments received as deposits are classified as current-account receipts and must be declared as such, with subsequent offsets against invoices recorded consistently.

      

      
Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which administers the Currency and Exchanges Manual for South Africa (Issue 1/2023 and subsequent amendments). All exchange control functions are delegated to authorised dealers, being Category I commercial banks, which execute, verify, and report cross-border transactions under the SARB mandate.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium feasibility with strict legal limitations. Non-bank offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment fintechs are not licensed as banks under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 and cannot lawfully accept deposits or offer full banking services in South Africa without either being registered as a South African bank or obtaining an approval as an authorised payment system participant under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 (through Bankserv/PASA membership) and, where applicable, an FSP licence under the FAIS Act 37 of 2002. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) may be used as secondary convenience rails for inbound client fee collection in foreign markets, but SA-resident treasury funds must remain with a domestic authorised dealer to preserve exchange-control traceability, BOP reporting, and the Annexure 'A' capital chain. Relying on an offshore EMI as the principal treasury account is not viable for a Pty Ltd established for export consulting, because SARB record-keeping for future dividend repatriation, SARS audit trails, and bank anti-money-laundering (FICA) compliance would be rendered incomplete.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding must be transmitted from the non-resident shareholder's foreign bank via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank settlement) to the authorised dealer in South Africa, with the beneficiary identified exactly as 'ZA Pty Ltd', and the SARB BOP purpose code '408' (equity subscription) or '410' (intercompany loan) stated in the SWIFT field 70/information. The authorised dealer will require a physically or digitally signed SARB Form A, certified board resolution, subscription or loan agreement, the shareholder register update, and the remitter bank's confirmation. Only funds thus recorded on Annexure 'A' qualify for later constitution-based dividend repatriation. For unrelated third-party clients paying for consultancy services, the proper inward purpose code is '415' (services rendered by residents to non-residents); the payment must be accompanied by an invoice reference in the SWIFT narrative and must be reported by the receiving bank as a current-account receipt convertible to ZAR without SARB approval.All outbound trade settlements and operational payments are routed through the authorised dealer using SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT202 (for higher-value interbank settlement), with the applicable SARB BOP purpose code entered in field 72 or field 70 narrative. For services-related disbursements such as subcontracting fees to foreign laboratories and testing houses, purpose code '414' (services rendered by non-residents to residents) or '416' (manufacturing services) applies, while dividend remittances to non-residents use code '472'. For larger procurement contracts with overseas contract research organisations, a commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 may be established, with the issuing bank sending an SWIFT MT700 advice to the beneficiary's bank and settlement executed via MT202/MT103; documentary requirements (commercial invoice, certificate of service completion, inspection certificate) must mirror the underlying contract. All SARB records and underlying documentation must be retained for five years, and any outbound MT103 to a restricted or OFAC/UN-sanctioned jurisdiction is automatically blocked by bank system filters.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) with an authorised dealer, permitted for genuine commercial exposures of up to 12 months and renewable on evidence of an underlying foreign-currency invoice or contract; must be concluded within the ZAR/USD or ZAR/EUR interbank regime.Cross-currency swaps and currency exchange agreements, available with authorised dealers for the hedging of multi-year service contracts, provided the notional is matched to actual expected foreign-currency receipts or disbursements.Plain-vanilla FX options and zero-premium option collars, permitted for the hedging of a confirmed export-services income stream, subject to documenting the underlying exposure to the authorised dealer.SARB-general-approval forward cover for expected but not yet invoiced export proceeds (sale proceeds cover), which may be taken out within prescribed parameters and only with an underlying service contract.Offshore Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not permitted for South African resident corporates and require explicit FinSurv approval, which is rarely granted to a consulting entity; hence NDFs are excluded from permissible instruments.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with strict legal limitations. Non-bank offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment fintechs are not licensed as banks under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 and cannot lawfully accept deposits or offer full banking services in South Africa without either being registered as a South African bank or obtaining an approval as an authorised payment system participant under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 (through Bankserv/PASA membership) and, where applicable, an FSP licence under the FAIS Act 37 of 2002. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) may be used as secondary convenience rails for inbound client fee collection in foreign markets, but SA-resident treasury funds must remain with a domestic authorised dealer to preserve exchange-control traceability, BOP reporting, and the Annexure 'A' capital chain. Relying on an offshore EMI as the principal treasury account is not viable for a Pty Ltd established for export consulting, because SARB record-keeping for future dividend repatriation, SARS audit trails, and bank anti-money-laundering (FICA) compliance would be rendered incomplete.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (historically 28%)

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
20% Dividends Tax on dividends declared and paid to non-resident beneficial owners; the Pty Ltd withholds and pays SARS by the last day of the month following the dividend declaration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold under s 31 of the Income Tax Act, but SARS transfer pricing review guidelines require contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation for cross-border connected-person transactions exceeding approximately ZAR 5,000,000 in aggregate per annum; a Country-by-Country (CbC) report notification applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least ZAR 10,000,000,000.

      
Economic substance scope: South Africa has no Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but substance is mandatorily evidenced through: (i) Place of Effective Management (POEM) in s 1 of the Income Tax Act determining tax residence; (ii) CFC attribution under s 9D for offshore subsidiaries; (iii) arm's-length recharacterization of sham arrangements under s 31; and (iv) general anti-avoidance rules in ss 80A-80L. A Pty Ltd must demonstrate local managerial decision-making, qualified operational staff, physical office facilities, and SA operating bank accounts; a substance-less shell may be disregarded.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Deliberate Understatement
        
Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall for intentional tax evasion (200% for a repeat case within 5 years); 10% late-payment penalty; prescribed-rate interest; criminal conviction under ss 234-235 of the Tax Administration Act with a fine of up to ZAR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable under TAA s 155(1) where the company's failure to pay tax is attributable to fraud or gross negligence; SARS may obtain asset preservation orders under s 163 and court-imposed travel interdictions.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: GAAR invalidation under ss 80A-80L; s 31 arm's-length recharacterization and transfer pricing adjustments; 75% understatement penalty for impermissible avoidance arrangements; denial of DTA relief; potential FIC adverse findings and bank de-risking.

        
Director personal liability: Directors face joint and several liability for the company's resultant tax debts under TAA s 155, plus sequestration and personal asset attachment in serious cases.

      

      
      

        Failure to Withhold Dividends, Interest, Royalties, or PAYE
        
Statutory sanction: SARS recovers the unwithheld tax from the Pty Ltd, plus a 10% penalty and interest; additional understatement penalties of 50%-150%; dividends tax non-compliance attracts criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act.

        
Director personal liability: Under TAA ss 155-156, directors are personally and severally liable for unwithheld employee taxes and withholding taxes; the corporate veil is pierced as a matter of statute.

      

      
      

        AML / FIC Act Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: FIC Act administrative fines of up to ZAR 10,000,000 per contravention for accountable institutions; money laundering under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act carries imprisonment of up to 15 years; assets are subject to forfeiture.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers bear personal criminal liability, travel restrictions, and civil restitution; personal and corporate assets may be frozen under POCA restraint and forfeiture orders.
