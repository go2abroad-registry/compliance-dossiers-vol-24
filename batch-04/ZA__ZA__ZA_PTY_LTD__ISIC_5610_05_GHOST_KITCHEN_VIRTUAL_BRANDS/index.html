Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); online operational portal at SARS eFiling – https://www.sarsefiling.co.za, with authoritative legal and procedural information published at https://www.sars.gov.za.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident owned South African private company (Pty) Ltd: (1) incorporate through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain a certificate of incorporation; (2) appoint a public officer who is ordinarily resident in South Africa under s 246 of the Tax Administration Act; (3) register for income tax and VAT via SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch by submitting the prescribed registration forms, including the company income tax registration form (and for VAT, VAT 101); supporting documents required comprise CIPC registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of the public officer's appointment and identity, proof of the company's physical and postal addresses (including a South African address for a foreign registered company), and, for non-resident VAT vendors, a local representative agreement; (4) SARS issues an income tax reference number and, after VAT registration, a separate VAT vendor registration number; (5) maintain updated registered particulars and file returns as required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a non-resident owned South African private company (Pty) Ltd: (1) incorporate through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain a certificate of incorporation; (2) appoint a public officer who is ordinarily resident in South Africa under s 246 of the Tax Administration Act; (3) register for income tax and VAT via SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch by submitting the prescribed registration forms, including the company income tax registration form (and for VAT, VAT 101); supporting documents required comprise CIPC registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of the public officer's appointment and identity, proof of the company's physical and postal addresses (including a South African address for a foreign registered company), and, for non-resident VAT vendors, a local representative agreement; (4) SARS issues an income tax reference number and, after VAT registration, a separate VAT vendor registration number; (5) maintain updated registered particulars and file returns as required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box or general hybrid-entity classification election for companies. A (Pty) Ltd is automatically a separate taxpayer liable on worldwide income at the 27% corporate rate. Elective options affecting tax profile include: small business corporation status under s 12E for qualifying South African-resident companies with turnover below R20 million; s 11D research and development allowance claims; s 12J venture capital company investment elections; financial instruments held may be aligned to tax under s 8E. Hybrid mismatch interest deductions are governed by ss 8F–8M and controlled foreign company rules; these operate automatically with no elective override. A foreign company may appoint a representative taxpayer under the Act, but entity classification remains based on domestic law and not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration arises when taxable supplies in South Africa exceed R1,000,000 (one million South African Rand) in any consecutive 12-month period; registration must be applied for within 21 business days. Voluntary VAT registration is possible if taxable supplies exceed R50,000. Foreign suppliers of electronic services, including delivery-interface and app-based services, are similarly required to register and charge VAT where supplies exceed the R1 million threshold. Non-established suppliers must use SARS eFiling and may be required to appoint a local representative and provide security for VAT obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods applies under s 11(3) of the VAT Act where the goods are supplied to a non-resident and are consigned to a destination outside South Africa, provided the supplier retains the prescribed documentary evidence of export. For services, s 11(2) zero-rates a supply of services to a non-resident for use wholly or partially in a foreign country, subject to exceptions such as services connected to South African land or movable property. For a ghost kitchen business, local meal sales and delivery are standard-rated; zero-rating may apply strictly to separately chargeable offshore services (e.g., management or head-office support supplied to an overseas group company) where the beneficial use is outside South Africa and proper evidence of foreign settlement is maintained. All zero-rated export transactions will be examined at audit to ensure actual payment in foreign currency and physical export.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD 500/SCC) bearing a unique movement reference number (UMRN) and confirming SARS Customs release to the exporter
  • Commercial invoice together with the original combined transport bill of lading or air waybill evidencing the consignment to a destination outside South Africa
  • Inward remittance certificate (IRC) or foreign exchange settlement certificate issued by an authorised South African bank, proving receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency within the statutory period

SARS does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or automated e-invoicing clearance at the point of sale. Tax invoices meeting s 20 of the VAT Act (electronic or paper) must be issued for taxable supplies and must include five-yearly prescribed particulars. Electronic tax invoices are accepted if performance and accuracy can be proved by normal data-processing procedures. SARS has deployed e@syFile and eFiling for VAT administrative and clearance purposes, and data is transmitted through the VAT 201 return. SARS may use automated analytics and Smart Audit to query invoice data, but there is no live tax-authority pre-clearance requirement before a document can be used.

If a zero-rating claim is not substantiated, SARS may recharacterise the supply as taxable under s 15(1), raising an assessment for VAT, interest and penalties. Under the Tax Administration Act, understatement penalties range from 10% for substantial non-disclosure with no reasonable grounds to 200% for intentional tax evasion. SARS may extend the general five-year audit prescription to ten years for non-disclosure or fraudulent conduct. Deliberately false export declarations constitute a criminal offence under both the VAT Act and the Customs and Excise Act, with prospective imprisonment of up to five years and personal liability for directors if SARS can establish negligence or fraud. A retrospective clawback of VAT refunds may be made for all affected tax periods, and SARS is not bound by previous refund allocations if the export claim is discredited. A section 204 TAA voluntary disclosure arrangement may mitigate penalties only if submitted before infection.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current-account transactions: trade in goods, food-and-beverage imports, kitchen-equipment purchases, SaaS subscriptions, and delivery-platform commissions payable offshore are permitted through an Authorized Dealer without SARB pre-approval, provided the bank holds the underlying invoice, contract, and the correct balance-of-payments purpose code. Capital-account inflows from non-residents (equity subscription by foreign shareholders of ZA_PTY_LTD) are unrestricted and do not require FinSurv consent, but the inward transfer must be recorded with an Authorized Dealer in the inward investment register to preserve the legal ability to remit dividends and repay shareholder loans. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted once the foreign capital was formally introduced, the annual financial statements are prepared, SARS tax clearance is presented, and the 20% Dividends Tax is withheld or reduced under an applicable double-taxation treaty. Practical hurdles are documentary rather than quantitative: the bank requires proof of the foreign investment inflow, a signed distribution declaration, and evidence that the company is in good standing with CIPC and SARS. Capital-account outflows by residents remain restricted; therefore all outbound treasury flows must be rationalised as current-account items (trade or service payments) and never as offshore deposits or unapproved investments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Delivery-format food service revenue is classified as an export of services when the counterparty or settlement entity is a non-resident (for example, DoorDash US settling USD for virtual-brand access on the US platform). All export service proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa and credited to an Authorized Dealer account within 180 calendar days of the invoice or service-completion date. No fixed statutory percentage must be converted to ZAR; proceeds may be retained in a Foreign Currency Account only up to the amount required for identifiable, near-term foreign disbursements, with the surplus sold to the dealing bank at the ruling spot rate. The exporter must lodge an Export Declaration for Services (Form S) or the bank's digital export module, supported by marketplace settlement statements and order-level reports. Non-compliance triggers an irregular-export report to FinSurv, which may result in administrative penalties, withdrawal of forward-exchange facilities, and adverse scoring in the bank's AML/CFT file. Domestic UberEats South Africa settlements in ZAR from Uber's local contracting entity are outside the surrender regime but must still be reconcilable in the company's VAT 201 return and management accounts.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the statutory exchange-control authority and through the Prudential Authority as the banking supervisor under the Banks Act, 1990 (Act 94 of 1990). All foreign-exchange transactions are executed by commercial banks in their capacity as Authorized Dealers in foreign exchange under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.
