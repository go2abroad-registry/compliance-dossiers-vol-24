Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — SARS eFiling portal: https://efiling.sars.gov.za and corporate governing portal https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) via bCodes/eBiz to obtain the CIPC registration number. 2) Register the company with SARS for income tax using the SARS Corporate Income Tax registration process (SARS 100) and obtain the income tax reference number, normally through an authorised tax practitioner or SARS eFiling. 3) Register as a VAT vendor either compulsorily within 21 days of exceeding the R1,000,000 threshold or voluntarily where turnover exceeds R50,000, using the VAT 101 registration form, and obtain a VAT vendor reference number. 4) Register for PAYE, UIF and SDL if employees are appointed. 5) Complete SARS eFiling user registration, mandate the company profile, and appoint a SARS representative where a non-resident director/principal holds control.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African resident Proprietary Limited company (Pty) Ltd is taxed at entity level on its worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; there is no US-style check-the-box pass-through classification; a company is resident in South Africa if incorporated in South Africa or effectively managed in South Africa.

Available election forms

South African law does not provide check-the-box elections or general entity classification elections. Statutory elections available include: (i) VAT grouping of two or more companies under Section 50 of the VAT Act to be treated as a single VAT vendor; (ii) the controlled foreign company high-tax exemption election under Section 9D(9A) of the Income Tax Act; (iii) the micro business turnover tax election under Section 48A for qualifying companies with annual turnover not exceeding the statutory threshold (currently ZAR 1,000,000); and (iv) certain capital allowance elections. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are mandatory rather than elective under Sections 23K–23N of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory annual taxable turnover threshold; voluntary registration available where turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000 per annum

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act for movable goods exported from South Africa where the vendor retains sufficient acceptable evidence of export and the goods are removed from South Africa within the prescribed period. For a fulfillment/kitting/assembly 3PL operation, the kitting and assembly services supplied in relation to goods physically exported to non-resident customers may qualify for zero rating where the vendor can demonstrate that the supply is inextricably connected to the exported movable goods and all export documentation is retained. Services rendered to non-residents may also be zero-rated under Section 11(2)(l) where the services are not connected to land or fixed property in South Africa. The vendor must be able to link each zero-rated supply to external shipping records, customs declarations and settlement received from the non-resident counterparty.

  • Valid SAD 500 (Single Administrative) Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT confirmation evidencing foreign currency settlement
  • Commercial invoice or packing list referencing the export order
  • Fulfilment/kitting work order and inventory / stock transfer records connecting the goods to the export shipment

SARS has implemented a statutory e-invoicing system (SARS eInvoicing) integrated with eFiling. Mandatory e-invoicing is being introduced in phases for large VAT vendors with turnover above prescribed SARS thresholds, with full compliance expected for such taxpayers in 2025; other vendors may use the system voluntarily. Tax invoices must comply with Section 20 of the VAT Act and Rule 5 record-keeping requirements under the Tax Administration Act, including vendor VAT number, invoice number, date, supply description and consideration. SARS validates e-invoices digitally on its eInvoicing platform; however, South Africa does not employ real-time fiscal stamping or continuous tax control device clearance in the style of Latin American tax authorities.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating is aggressively audited by SARS. Penalties include: additional tax of up to 200% of the evaded VAT under Section 59 of the VAT Act for false declarations; understatement penalties under Chapter 16 of the Tax Administration Act ranging from 10% (standard case) to 150% (intentional, willful misconduct); statutory interest compounded daily under Section 189 of the Tax Administration Act; retrospective clawback of the full output VAT previously zero-rated with corresponding reversal of any claimed input tax; and criminal prosecution with fines or imprisonment of up to five years. SARS may also withhold refunds, initiate contemporaneous document verification, and require bank statements, forex receipts and terminal cargo records before releasing export-related VAT refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade-related payments, service fees, dividends, interest, and branch profits, subject to filing of the applicable SARS tax clearance and compliance by an Authorised Dealer. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity subscriptions and shareholder loan injections by non-residents, are permitted under a liberalised allocation approval regime; outbound capital repatriations of the original investment plus declared dividends are freely allowed by non-residents once the capital originally entered South Africa through properly documented banking channels. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal for a financially sound private company, but the absence or lapse of a SARS tax clearance certificate will halt the remittance, and any transaction misrepresented as trade-related while being capital-related may render the company subject to administrative penalties under FinSurv directives.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 90 days from the date of shipment, or within an extended period approved in advance by FinSurv via the Authorised Dealer. The proceeds must be credited to the exporter's South African account or a controlled Foreign Currency Account (FCA); any amounts retained in an FCA must be funded solely from export earnings and used only for permitted offshore obligations. A mandatory conversion ratio into ZAR is assessed on a transaction basis rather than a fixed statutory percentage, but in practice all proceeds eligible for domestic settlement must remain at the disposal of the Authorised Dealer and be converted upon any local disbursement requirement.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), mandated by the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations issued thereunder.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium only for cross-border treasury support strictly as a non-resident payment rail; legally prohibitive as a primary ZAR-based transactional bank account. Offshore digital EMI platforms (for example Airwallex, Payoneer, and analogous EU-licensed or Hong Kong-licensed EMI entities) may be used to collect multi-currency inbound receipts from international fulfilment clients and to execute outbound vendor payment instructions, provided the EMI does not hold itself out as a South African bank or take ZAR deposits for onshore settlement. A registered South African AD must remain the underlying settlement channel for any onshore ZAR clearing or capital repatriation requirement, and the company must disclose the EMI relationship to its AML compliance officer so that transaction monitoring covers the offshore leg.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must inject equity by SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's own bank account to the company's ZAR capital escrow account at the Authorised Dealer. The payment must carry an explicit purpose code corresponding to 'Direct Investment - Share Capital' under the SARB's coding matrix, and be accompanied by a formal board resolution of the issuing company, a bankers' certificate from the remitting bank confirming source, and a completed FinSurv capital import declaration. The Authorised Dealer will credit the CFA capital account, issue the local capital import endorsement, and thereafter the funds may be transferred to the operational ZAR account for production distribution or physical warehouse leasing. 20% Dividends Tax on dividends declared by the South African resident company regardless of whether the shareholder is resident or non-resident. The dividend is subject to withholding at source from the beneficial owner.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

ITR14 must be filed within 12 months of the company's financial year-end, or by the date specified in the SARS income tax return notice. Provisional tax returns: first half-year payment due within 6 months of the start of the year of assessment; second payment due no later than the last business day of the year of assessment; third top-up payment due within 6 months of year-end for non-SBC companies (7 months for SBC). Assessment is raised by SARS after filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The mandatory Local File and Master File thresholds are set under the SARS Transfer Pricing Record-Keeping Rules issued under Section 29 of the Tax Administration Act. A Local File and Master File must be prepared if the company has: (a) gross local income above ZAR 100 million related to cross-border connected-person transactions, or (b) the aggregate value of affected cross-border transactions with connected persons exceeds ZAR 50 million. Where the company is part of a multinational group with total group turnover exceeding ZAR 10 billion, the Master File threshold is automatically triggered. Comprehensive transfer pricing documentation covering the functional analysis, economic analysis, and comparability analysis must be retained regardless of thresholds if the company has any cross-border intercompany transactions, because SARS may request documentation for any transaction under the general documentation duty in Section 31.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the Gulf Cooperation Council sense, but the economic substance of a legal entity is a critical tax doctrine enforced through: (i) the controlled foreign company (CFC) regime under Section 9D, which attributes undistributed foreign income to South African shareholders where the CFC's foreign business establishment is not substantive; (ii) substance-over-form and simulated transaction doctrines applied by SARS under which a kitting/fulfillment company that is a mere paper entity or lacks real operating substance may have its transactions recharacterized or ignored; (iii) place of effective management (POEM) rules under Section 1 of the Income Tax Act determining South African tax residency based on the location of day-to-day senior management decisions; and (iv) anti-avoidance rules under Section 103(2) and Section 80A-L (general anti-avoidance rules, GAAR), which can cancel any tax avoidance arrangement lacking commercial substance. For a (Pty) Ltd operating a physical fulfillment center in South Africa with employees, inventory, vehicles, leased warehouse space, and client contracts, economic substance is ordinarily evidenced by payroll records, lease agreements, inventory management systems (WMS), and service level agreements. A kitting/3PL company is not a pure IP-holding shell, so the substance risk profile is low, but directors must ensure that intercompany service fees are not pure profit extraction vehicles and that board meetings, operational control, and procurement decisions occur in South Africa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: SARS can adjust taxable income under Section 31(2) of the Income Tax Act to reflect an arm's length price. The primary adjustment is taxed at 27% corporate income tax; secondary adjustment under Section 31(3) deems a dividend or loan interest flow and may impose a 20% Dividends Tax or interest withholding tax. Understatement penalties under Tax Administration Act Section 223 can reach 150-200% of the understated tax for intentional tax evasion, plus daily interest at the prescribed rate. Failure to submit transfer pricing documentation when requested can result in an administrative penalty of up to ZAR 16,000 per month and an adverse inference that the taxpayer's position is not reasonably supported.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly approves a transfer pricing scheme without arm's length analysis can be held liable under Section 159 of the Tax Administration Act for tax due from a company controlled by that director where the failure to operate a transfer pricing policy is due to gross negligence or recklessness. Directors may face criminal prosecution under Section 234 of the Tax Administration Act for tax evasion, carrying imprisonment of up to 15 years. Civil restitution for SARS recovery can be enforced against directors' personal assets; travel bans may be issued under Section 169 where a director poses a flight risk.
Dividends Tax Evasion by Non-Resident Shareholder

Statutory sanction: The company is personally liable for the unremedied Dividends Tax under Section 53 of the Income Tax Act, including 100% of the tax owing, plus interest and understatement penalties. SARS can collect from the company's bank accounts or attach warehousing assets. The shareholder remains jointly and severally liable for the tax. Understatement penalties range from 35% to 200% depending on the degree of intentionality.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise a dividend distribution without proper WHT compliance are personally liable for the dividends tax to the extent that the company fails to pay. Under the Companies Act Section 77, a director is liable for any loss or damage sustained by the company through a culpable breach of fiduciary duties. Criminal sanctions can extend to up to 10 years' imprisonment for tax fraud; a director may be subjected to a SARB and SARS joint enforcement travel ban.
Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) Attribution Failure

Statutory sanction: The CFC's net income is included in the South African shareholder's taxable income, taxed at 27%, with a deemed dividend treatment for distributions from the CFC's income, potentially incurring 20% dividends tax upon declaration. Interest and penalties for understated tax apply; SEC (SARS) may re-open assessments for up to five years where the failure was due to negligence or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for designing the offshore structure are personally liable for the company's failure to declare attributed CFC income under Section 159 of the Tax Administration Act where the failure was in consequence of a director's own failure to take reasonable steps. Criminal prosecution may result from a false CFC declaration under Section 234, with imprisonment up to 15 years and disqualification under the Companies Act.
Provisional Tax Underestimation / Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: 20% estimated penalty on the difference between the final assessed tax and the second provisional estimate where the estimate is less than 90% of the actual taxable income (i.e., understated by more than 10%), plus a 20% penalty on the difference between the amount paid and the amount that should have been paid. Interest accrues from the original due date until payment. Failure to register as a provisional taxpayer is a separate offence with penalties up to ZAR 16,000 per month.

Director personal liability: Directors are obligated to ensure the company has sufficient cash reserves to meet provisional tax obligations; under Section 159 of the Tax Administration Act, a director is jointly and severally liable for tax debts when the company failed to pay and the director caused or allowed the failure. SARS can apply to Court for a director prohibition order, and a failure to remit provisional tax can ground criminal liability under the Companies Act for reckless trading.
Simulated / Paper Transaction (Sham Company)

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR, Section 80A-80L) to disregard the arrangement or recharacterize the transaction. The taxpayer then faces additional tax at 27%, a GAAR penalty of up to 100% of the tax avoided, and interest; or the transaction may be treated as a simulated transaction with fraud penalties of 150-200% under Section 223. In case of intentional failure to register or fraud, the company may be deregistered by the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and SARS may void tax clearance certificates (mandatory for tender/banking), effectively shutting down commercial activity.

Director personal liability: Section 77(3)(b) of the Companies Act imposes personal liability on directors who carry on business knowing that the company is unable to pay its debts (reckless trading). Directors who are complicit in a sham transaction face civil restitution for all tax losses plus damages; criminal fraud charges under the Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act, or the common law offence of fraud, carry imprisonment of up to 25 years; travel bans and asset freezes can be ordered under section 170 of the Tax Administration Act.
Non-Resident Shareholder Withoutholding on Interest / Royalty

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax is collected from the company as a debt due to SARS, with a 10% penalty for late payment or failure to withhold (Tax Administration Act Section 214). Interest runs at the prescribed rate. In a thin capitalisation or debt-leveraged arrangement, SARS may reclassify excess interest as a deemed dividend under Section 31(3), triggering a 20% Dividends Tax treatment, which is non-deductible.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign off a foreign interest/royalty payment without verifying the tax withholding are personally liable for the withholding tax amount under a South African-wide strict-liability regime for responsible third parties (Tax Administration Act Section 156 applies to withholding agents). A director's failure to ensure the remittance of funds withheld for SARS constitutes theft of public money, punishable by imprisonment not exceeding 15 years.
Money Laundering / Proceeds of Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA), the company faces administrative penalties of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention, or up to 10% of turnover for repeat offences; criminal prosecution under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act (POCA) can lead to fines up to ZAR 100 million and imprisonment up to 30 years for money laundering. SARS can impose additional penalty of 200% under Section 223 on undeclared tax included in laundered funds.

Director personal liability: Directors and the accountable institution's compliance officer face individual criminal liability under FICA Section 51A and 51B. A director who authorises or fails to prevent a money-laundering payment flow without filing an STR may be imprisoned up to 15 years. Under POCA, directors involved in the proceeds of tax evasion may be personally forfeited of all property traced to the offence; travel bans and extradition treaties with other jurisdictions can apply.
