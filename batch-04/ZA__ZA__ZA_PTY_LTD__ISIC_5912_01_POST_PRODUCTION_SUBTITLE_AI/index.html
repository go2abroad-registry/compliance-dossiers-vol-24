Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – SARS eFiling portal (https://www.sars.gov.za and https://secure.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must register as a South African taxpayer via SARS eFiling. Upon incorporation, the company is automatically issued an income tax reference number by CIPC in the incorporation process. To claim VAT registration and export benefits, the company must complete VAT 101 (VAT registration application) and submit supporting documents (e.g., Memorandum of Incorporation, ID copies, proof of business address, bank account confirmation, and revenue projection if turnover is below the compulsory threshold). Where the (Pty) Ltd is foreign-controlled, SARS may also require a representative taxpayer and audited financial statements. Non-resident vendors of services need to obtain a VAT number through the same procedure before issuing zero-rated export invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – South Africa (Pty) Ltd is a separate legal taxpayer currently subject to normal income tax on worldwide income as a resident company. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – South Africa (Pty) Ltd is a separate legal taxpayer currently subject to normal income tax on worldwide income as a resident company. No pass-through treatment is available to an incorporated private company; dividends are subject to dividends tax at source.

Available election forms

South African tax law does not operate check-the-box elections. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) the election to join a group VAT scheme under section 50 of the VAT Act; (ii) election of VAT basis of accounting (payments or invoice) under section 15; (iii) claiming assessed loss carry-forward under section 20; and (iv) application of controlled foreign company (CFC) high-tax exemption or Section 9D relief. Hybrid mismatch rules under section 8E and related tax avoidance rules apply automatically and cannot be circumvented by entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months (compulsory registration). Voluntary VAT registration is available from ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months where the entity carries on an enterprise and makes taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from South Africa are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act when supplied to a non-resident and removed from South Africa in the ordinary course of business. Services are zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) where the recipient is a non-resident and the services are not supplied for use in South Africa, including post-production subtitle AI transcription and voice cloning services supplied to offshore clients. The exporter must hold documentary proof of the export and receipt of foreign payment. Where the services are physically performed in South Africa but the benefits accrue offshore, the zero-rating is available only if the services are not connected with movable goods situated in South Africa at the time of supply and are not supplied to a person using them in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged through SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for physical goods) / Electronic service delivery evidence (for digital services)
  • Commercial invoice or tax invoice
  • Foreign contract, purchase order, or signed service agreement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/currency payment evidence showing receipt of export consideration

South Africa currently has no mandatory real-time central fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. SARS has adopted the OECD e-invoicing interoperability framework and accepts valid electronic tax invoices under section 20 of the VAT Act. Invoices must contain prescribed particulars, be authentic, and preserve integrity of origin and content. SARS eFiling is the statutory filing channel for VAT returns, and SARS provides web/API integration for tax registration and return submission; however, live real-time transaction-level clearance was not mandatory at the relevant effective date.

SARS can retrospectively claw back improperly claimed zero-rated VAT within a 5-year prescription period (indefinite in cases of fraud or misrepresentation). Penalties include additional tax up to 200% of assessed liability under the Tax Administration Act, understatement penalties ranging from 0% to 150% based on the egregiousness of the conduct, administrative non-compliance penalties (up to ZAR 16,000 per month up to 24 months), and criminal sanctions for making false export declarations, including imprisonment. Failure to satisfy FX repatriation timelines will trigger a rebuttable presumption of no genuine export and reverse the zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is a partially convertible, managed-float currency governed by the Currency and Exchanges Act No. 9 of 1933 and the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual (substantially liberalised in 2021). Current-account transactions are broadly convertible: residents may procure foreign currency through authorised dealers for genuine services, royalties, trade inputs and software licensing without prior FinSurv approval up to standard thresholds, and profit/dividend remittances to non-residents are freely permitted after 20% dividends tax withholding, SARS compliance verification and BOP declaration. Capital-account transactions retain administrative control: inward equity subscription is liberalised for unlisted companies but must be received through an authorised dealer, reported under the correct BOP inward investment code, and the issued shares must be endorsed as non-resident; outward investment, intercompany loans above R100 million, loop structures and offshore guarantees require prior FinSurv approval or exception reporting. The 2021 reforms abolished the historic 30-day export surrender regime and much of the prior-approval machinery, but full cross-border reporting discipline remains mandatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SARB regime: (i) proceeds of exported goods and services must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of export, replacing the pre-2021 30-day surrender rule; (ii) no mandatory conversion ratio applies because the exporter may retain the full export value in a designated Foreign Currency Account at an authorised dealer and convert to ZAR at its discretion or apply proceeds to approved offshore obligations; (iii) the full invoiced export value must be repatriated net only of normal banking charges and permissible deductions, and the export declaration (customs EC-form or service BOP record) must be closed by the authorised dealer; (iv) for ISIC 5912 digital services exports, including automated subtitle transcription, AI dubbing licences and post-production revenue, the export must be evidenced by an executed service agreement, tax invoice and the bank's BOP services-export code; failure to repatriate within 180 days requires FinSurv approval and may attract administrative penalties and exchange-control interest.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), in conjunction with the National Treasury and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) for AML/CFT supervision, and the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) for payment service provider licensing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary collection flows and LOW as a primary clearing rail. South Africa does not provide a standalone full EMI licence; foreign digital EMIs can interact with the ZAR payment system only through a registered authorised dealer and must themselves be FIC-licensed or partnered with an FSCA-regulated PSP. Wise South Africa (Pty) Ltd is locally operational and suitable for low-value B2B receipts up to approximately R5 million per transaction, while Stripe has no direct South African acquiring vehicle and must route through a local PSP or aggregator. EMIs cannot lawfully intermediate capital subscriptions, repatriated export proceeds, LC settlements, or treasury balances representing the company's full cross-border footprint, because SARB FinSurv requires all capital, trade and current-account transactions to be captured at an authorised dealer under an assigned BOP code with KYC of the ultimate counterparty. The recommended architecture is a SWIFT-based authorised-dealer bank as the exclusive primary clearer, with Wise and PSP aggregator rails used only as a forward-collection overlay subject to daily reconciliation and invoice-level audit trail retention.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Subscription proceeds from the offshore shareholder must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 or same-day telegraphic transfer directly to the South African authorised-dealer account in USD, EUR, GBP or ZAR, with the purpose field stating 'Equity subscription for ordinary shares in ZA_PTY_LTD'. The transfer must not be routed through Wise, Stripe or any third-country intermediary because the BOP record will then attribute the sender as the intermediary and trigger a suspicious-transaction review. The authorised dealer verifies the shareholder's KYC and source of wealth, files the SARB BOP inward investment declaration under code 1A040 (IMF BPM6 DI7), and issues a Notice of Inward Transfer. No prior FinSurv approval is required for an unlisted-entity share subscription, but the company must issue share certificates endorsed 'Non-Resident', record the inward listing in the authorised-dealer register, and, where cumulative non-resident investment exceeds R10 million, the bank submits the 'IN' register to FinSurv within 30 days. Where the parties prefer segregated custody, the bank may open a dedicated Capital Escrow Account to hold the inward funds until CIPC and SARS filings are completed, with subsequent release to the operational account under a board-approved disbursement resolution.All outbound trade and operational disbursements run through the authorised dealer's SWIFT MT103+ channel under SARB exception reporting. For routine services (software licences, AI platform usage fees, freelance subtitle QA, marketing and cloud compute) below R10 million per single outward remittance, no prior FinSurv approval is required, but the bank requires the underlying contract, commercial invoice and the correct BOP service code. Payments to non-resident connected parties must be supported by transfer-pricing documentation at the bank's request. High-value hardware, server and audio-equipment imports should be settled by a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) opened at the same authorised dealer, typically on 90–180 day usance, with documents against payment. Irrevocable service commitments above R10 million require prior FinSurv approval on the prescribed exchange-control form. Wise and PSP rails are permitted only for de minimis disbursements below USD 5,000 per beneficiary and must be reconciled monthly to the primary clearing bank to prevent BOP fragmentation.
