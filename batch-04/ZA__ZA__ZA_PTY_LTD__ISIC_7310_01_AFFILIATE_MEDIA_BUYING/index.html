Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – www.sarsefiling.co.za; eFiling platform

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must first incorporate via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain an enterprise registration number. Thereafter, the company applies to SARS for an Income Tax Reference Number by completing the SARS registration application via eFiling or a SARS branch, providing the CIPC registration certificate, memorandum of incorporation, certified identity documents or passports of directors/shareholders, and proof of registered and principal place of business in South Africa. For VAT, the company registers as a VAT vendor where the value of taxable supplies exceeds or is expected to exceed R1,000,000 within any consecutive 12-month period, or voluntarily where the threshold is met as a foreign non-resident supplier supplying electronic services in South Africa.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under South African law. South African companies are taxed on worldwide income, with special rules for foreign business establishments. Hybrid mismatch provisions under Section 8F to 8L of the Income Tax Act apply to prevent deduction/non-inclusion mismatches, but no elective classification regimes analogous to the US are available. Certain companies can elect the deemed corporate income tax incidence under the 'nil return' system, but this is not a substantive classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (ZAR) in annual taxable supplies, measured over any 12-month rolling period; compulsory for residents and foreign businesses supplying electronic services to South African residents, unless solely making exempt supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from South Africa are zero-rated under Section 11 of the VAT Act where the supplier retains documentary proof of export and the goods physically leave the country within the prescribed period. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated where the services are exported in the sense that they are not supplied to a resident, are not connected to movable or immovable property in South Africa, and are deemed to be supplied outside South Africa under Section 11(2)(l). Sales of goods and services to registered vendors in the BLNS countries (Botswana, Lesotho, Namibia, Eswatini) are zero-rated only where the buyer is a registered VAT vendor and the goods are removed from South Africa under covered cross-border arrangements, subject to strict documentary proof.

  • Customs export declaration (SA Customs SAD500) reflecting correct tariff code and final export destination
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill showing consignor and consignee names, port of loading, and port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign exchange confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in South Africa
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of physical movement such as a border crossing receipt, transport manifest, or terminal export certificate

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time tax clearance or digital stamping for standard B2B invoices. SARS supports voluntary e-invoicing and interoperable electronic data interchange, but all invoices must contain prescribed particulars under VAT Act Section 20. Tax clearance certificates are issued through eFiling, and real-time electronic submissions are required for VAT returns and customs declarations. No API-based fiscal stamping or continuous transaction controls are currently imposed for standard VAT invoices.

SARS may disallow zero-rating and impose a VAT assessment with understatement penalties from 10% to 150% where a person knowingly or negligently makes a false declaration or fails to retain prescribed documentation. Fraudulent export claims attract criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act, including fines up to R100,000 or imprisonment, and can trigger voluntary disclosure penalties and retrospective clawbacks of all refunds, interest at the prescribed rate, and additional 200% penalties for intentional tax evasion. Exchange Control Regulation R.21 requires export proceeds to be received in South Africa within 180 days; contraventions are referred to the Financial Surveillance Department and may result in administrative penalties and denial of future zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions including legitimate payments for services such as digital advertising and media buying, provided supporting documents are presented to and approved by an authorised dealer. Capital account transactions are restricted; direct inward foreign investment into a South African (Pty) Ltd is freely permitted via a share subscription but all proceeds must be credited to a South African bank account with a share certificate and beneficial ownership declaration filed. Dividends and profits may be remitted abroad by non-resident shareholders on a current account basis through an authorised dealer, subject to evidence of tax compliance (SARS tax clearance for dividends), an IMD (International Money-Dividend) declaration, and no adverse exchange-control findings. There are no dual exchange rates or surrender requirements on retained foreign currency, but all export receipts must be converted to ZAR into the local operational account. Remuneration for offshore marketing services is considered an invisible export and is therefore current-account eligible if properly documented and accurately priced at arm's length.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For services exports by an advertising and media-buying company, the service provider must receive payment of export proceeds within 180 days of date of invoice or date of service completion, whichever is earlier. Upon receipt in the authorised dealer's foreign currency account, the proceeds must be surrendered into a ZAR-denominated account of the (Pty) Ltd within 30 days of receipt, unless a specific retention facility has been granted by SARB FinSurv. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio requiring 100% conversion for service exports; however, 100% of proceeds are generally required to be remitted to South Africa and credited to the authorised dealer in either foreign currency or ZAR, with only limited retention allowed for working capital or approved offshore disbursements. All export proceeds must be accompanied by an export/bank transaction number, a valid service contract or invoice, and a proof of payment from the overseas client; no third-party settlement is permitted unless explicitly authorised by FinSurv. For digital advertising arbitrage, the intermediary service is zero-rated for VAT purposes when sold to a non-resident, and the SARB treats such revenues as current account receipts requiring no prior exchange control approval, but the bank will still require auditable evidence of the underlying performance media transactions to distinguish true sales from round-tripping.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Exchange Control Regulations and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore fintech and EMI platforms such as Mercury, Wise, and Payoneer have low-to-medium viability as the primary treasury rail for a South African corporate entity because they do not hold a South African banking licence and are not authorised dealers in foreign exchange. Such platforms can be used as ancillary payment concentrators for the receipt of advertising proceeds from international DSPs, but all funds eventually must be routed to a local bank account in the name of the Pty Ltd to satisfy SARB export proceeds surrender obligations. Wise Business may be deactivated if the South African entity's turnover exceeds exchange-control thresholds without a local bank backing. As a practical alternative, an institutional-grade solution is to use the South African branch of an international bank (e.g., Citibank or HSBC) paired with a local tier-1 bank; this arrangement offers SWIFT-based cross-border liquidity without violating the SARB authorised-dealer requirement. The compliance ceiling is that all offshore digital EMI transactions are treated as repayments, loans, or trade payments from a non-resident source; the South African entity must, without exception, maintain and export an audit trail from the EMI to the local authorised-dealer bank showing underlying client contracts, campaign invoices, and conversion evidence.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident beneficial ownership residing in an OFAC / FATF monitoring jurisdiction or a jurisdiction with high money-laundering risk, creating ambiguity on ultimate economic substance.

Root cause: Non-resident beneficial ownership residing in an OFAC / FATF monitoring jurisdiction or a jurisdiction with high money-laundering risk, creating ambiguity on ultimate economic substance.

Defense evidence: Delivery of an organisational chart showing the direct and indirect beneficial owners, notarised certificates of incorporation of all intermediate entities, official register excerpts from the corporate registry, passports of senior management, and a signed declaration identifying each ultimate natural person. The dossier should include a reputable independent corporate-service provider (TCSP) cover opinion and, where applicable, a foreign tax residence certificate to demonstrate economic nexus.
Unclear or inflated 'media buying' activity generating rapid round-tripping of funds between the affiliate company and affiliated merchants.

Root cause: Unclear or inflated 'media buying' activity generating rapid round-tripping of funds between the affiliate company and affiliated merchants.

Defense evidence: A granular business dossier containing executed advertising insertion orders, programmatic DSP contracts, ad-serving platform logs, screenshot of conversion attestations (tracking IDs, click IDs, conversion timestamps), settlement statements from affiliate networks, and an expert explanation of EBITDA margins. Evidence must show that the revenue is not self-referral or fraudulent click arbitrage; a third-party forensic audit of campaign delivery metrics and a traceable chain of funds from ad networks to the company bank account is required.
Disproportionately high foreign revenue for a newly incorporated entity with low initial staff, triggering shell company suspicion.

Root cause: Disproportionately high foreign revenue for a newly incorporated entity with low initial staff, triggering shell company suspicion.

Defense evidence: A fully substantiated operational substance file: lease or serviced-office agreement in South Africa, FICA-verified employee contracts, payroll records, invoice reconciliation showing the exact campaign periods, access logs from media-buying dashboards, and an interconnected telecom/IT invoice trail. The company must demonstrate that the marketing decision-making, creative selection, budget setting, and campaign optimisation professionals operate in South Africa, not merely the invoicing entity.
Sample/total amount of source-of-funds request for the inward capital injection cannot be matched against credible wealth accumulation.

Root cause: Sample/total amount of source-of-funds request for the inward capital injection cannot be matched against credible wealth accumulation.

Defense evidence: A bank reference from the parent company's home jurisdiction, signed audited financial statements of the parent, source-of-funds evidence (e.g., 12 months of bank statements, share sale agreements, dividend vouchers), plus the share subscription agreement and the SARB prescribed Authorised Dealer Form. If the parent is newly funded by private lenders, a legally executed loan agreement, lender due-diligence file, and evidence of loan transfer from the lending institution directly to the South African company's capital account are required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign direct investment for the South African Pty Ltd's working capital or share capital must be paid from the foreign shareholder's bank account to a dedicated ZAR capital account at a SARB authorised dealer. The wire must be SWIFT MT103 containing the correct corporate beneficiary name, legal entity identifier where available, and a clear narrative such as 'Share Subscription' or 'Direct Investment' with the authorised dealer's exchange control reference. The receiving bank must issue a 'Form ADF' declaration of inward transfers; the transaction will be classified under direct investment and credited to a 'suspense/escrow' account if needed. The company must submit a share certificate within 30 days, together with the inward transfer advice and a beneficial-ownership declaration, to enable the authorised dealer to report to SARB FinSurv. Once the share certificate is issued, funds may be transferred from the suspense account to the operational account. Alternatively, structured external borrowings (intragroup loans) require a loan/equity ratio approved by the authorised dealer under the Currency and Exchanges Manual, with formal letters of support and registration of all external debt. The same MT103 should not be used for mixed equity and trade settlement; separate purpose codes are required to avoid exchange-control reconciliation errors.Outbound payments to non-resident suppliers, DSPs, affiliate networks, and advertising publishers are current-account transactions and can be executed by the authorised dealer via SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) or SWIFT MT202 (bank transfer) at the instruction of the corporate account holder. For trade-related services, banks may require a trade IBAN or bank details of the beneficiary, but the critical compliance step is the submission of pro-forma invoices or early settlement invoices proving that the payment is directly for the admitted marketing services and is not a disguised capital outflow. When purchasing media inventory or paying advertising publishers abroad, the company should use its operational account with the instruction narrative 'Cross-border media placement settlement', attaching campaign media plan and publisher statement. Commercial letters of credit (documentary L/C) are less common for serially repeated media buying but are appropriate for large-scale performance campaigns or when contracting a foreign holding company. On the condition that the payment is denominated in EUR/USD/GBP or another freely convertible currency, the authorised dealer will debit the ZAR account and execute a spot conversion at the prevailing market rate, or use an existing forward contract. As a practical alternative, an institutional-grade solution is to use the South African branch of an international bank (e.g., Citibank or HSBC) paired with a local tier-1 bank; this arrangement offers SWIFT-based cross-border liquidity without violating the SARB authorised-dealer requirement. The compliance ceiling is that all offshore digital EMI transactions are treated as repayments, loans, or trade payments from a non-resident source; the South African entity must, without exception, maintain and export an audit trail from the EMI to the local authorised-dealer bank showing underlying client contracts, campaign invoices, and conversion evidence.
