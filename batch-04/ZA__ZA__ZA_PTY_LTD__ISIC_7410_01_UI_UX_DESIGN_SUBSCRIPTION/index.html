Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); all national tax registration, filing, and compliance administration is conducted through the SARS eFiling portal (www.sarsefiling.co.za), which is the sole digital gateway for income tax, VAT, customs, and excise matters, with integrated data exchange from the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC).

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated (Pty) Ltd first obtains its CIPC enterprise registration from the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission; SARS then automatically activates the income tax reference number once the entity has a filing obligation. A non-resident company deriving South African-source income must apply directly to SARS for income tax registration via eFiling and must appoint a resident public officer within 14 days of incorporation or commencement of business activity. Where the entity makes taxable supplies, Form VAT 101 must be lodged through eFiling for VAT vendor registration. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services, including UI/UX design subscription platforms, must use the dedicated foreign electronic-services VAT registration route and will be issued a VAT number commencing with the digit 4, with no requirement of a South African place of business. Upon acceptance, the entity receives its registration confirmation in eFiling and must thereafter maintain its compliance profile, including VAT 201 periodic returns, annual income tax returns, and export record submissions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; a (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed as a company at entity level and cannot elect pass-through treatment. The material statutory elections available are: (1) voluntary VAT registration once taxable supplies exceed R50,000 but remain below the R1,000,000 compulsory threshold; (2) election among approved VAT input-tax apportionment methods under section 17(1) of the VAT Act, with SARS approval required for any special method; (3) election to zero-rate exported services under section 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act, subject to full documentary and substantive compliance; (4) VAT group registration election under section 54 of the VAT Act for related entities with at least 70% ownership; and (5) mandatory, non-elective application of the hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act and VAT Act, which neutralize double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes for cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (South African Rand) per 12-month period is the compulsory VAT vendor registration threshold for taxable supplies, with voluntary registration available from R50,000; however, foreign suppliers of electronic services, including subscription-based UI/UX design services, are subject to compulsory VAT registration with no minimum turnover threshold under the electronic-services rules, applying the payments-based place-of-supply approach now in force.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where a vendor supplies goods that are exported outside South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply, supported by prescribed export documentation. Services are zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply, the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or movable property situated in South Africa, and the supplier has no reason to believe the services are consumed in South Africa. Remote UI/UX design subscription services delivered to non-resident enterprise clients therefore qualify for the 0% export VAT rate, whereas the same digital subscription supplied to a South African resident attracts 15% VAT under the electronic-services place-of-supply rules. Pure digital service exports do not require a customs declaration, but the vendor must retain complete contracts, subscriber records, IP-geolocation logs, payment settlement evidence, and non-resident status documentation.

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration lodged electronically via SARS Customs for any physical export consignment
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical cross-border exportation for goods
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or regulated-bank SWIFT advice confirming hard-currency settlement of the exported supply
  • Commercial invoice and design-subscription contract identifying the non-resident counterparty, payment terms, and place of performance
  • Digital-delivery and usage records, including platform logs, IP-geolocation reports, and subscriber master files, substantiating offshore consumption of UI/UX design services

SARS does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping for VAT invoices. Electronic tax invoices are expressly permitted under section 20(7A) of the VAT Act and must be issued no later than 21 days after the supply. Compliance is structurally driven by eFiling-based VAT 201 declarations, modernised SARS application programming interfaces (APIs), third-party data matching from payment rails such as Stripe and Wise, and risk-based refund verification. SARS has announced an intended evolution toward structured, SAF-T-aligned e-invoicing, but no binding real-time fiscal clearance or API stamping regime applies as at the date of this dossier.

Zero-rating claims are subject to strict SARS verification and audit. If the documentary conditions, the 90-day goods-export requirement, or the section 11(2)(l) non-resident use-and-enjoyment conditions are not satisfied, SARS will claw back the zero-rated output as 15% VAT, together with interest at the adjusted prescribed rate. The Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 understatement penalty framework applies at 0% to 150% of the tax shortfall, scaled to the conduct category from substantial understatement through gross negligence to intentional tax evasion. A fraudulent VAT refund claim attracts additional tax of up to 200% of the refund under section 40(2) of the VAT Act, and deliberate evasion or false export declarations may found criminal prosecution, personal liability of directors, and the imposition of director-level joint and several liability where the conduct is reckless or fraudulent. SARS refund audits generally reach back the applicable five-year prescription period and may withhold refunds pending full verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is freely convertible at a market-determined floating rate, with no surrender or conversion ratio for exporters since the 1995 deregulation of the exchange-control system. Current-account payments (dividends, management fees, royalties, service fees) are permitted without prior FinSurv approval provided they are supported by audited after-tax profit certification, SARS tax clearance and standard Balance of Payments (BOP) disclosures. Capital-account inflows (non-resident share subscription) are permitted through an Authorised Dealer without prior FinSurv approval for an ordinary private company; capital repatriation on liquidation is allowed upon proof that the original inflow was recorded on the BOP statement. Practical hurdles for a foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd include strict documentary chains linking inbound funds to the CIPC share register, the 180-day export-proceeds repatriation obligation for service revenue, and the requirement to quote BOP purpose codes on every cross-border wire to prevent misclassification as capital flight.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service exporters, including digital design subscription agencies, must cause the full value of exported services to be received and repatriated into South Africa within 180 days from the invoice date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may elect either to (a) convert the foreign currency to ZAR immediately through an Authorised Dealer or (b) retain the proceeds in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA), with disposal and recredits subject to the same 180-day rule and periodic reporting to the Authorised Dealer. Each receipt must be reported on the BOP/CFS declaration within 30 days of credit, and underlying service invoices and platform settlement statements (Stripe/Wise aggregated payouts) must demonstrate a clean reconciliation to exports of services to avoid deemed-interest or unexplained-credit scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the Exchange Control Authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, Act 9 of 1933, and the Exchange Control Regulations, in coordination with the National Treasury and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Wise operates in South Africa as a registered accountable institution and licensed financial services provider through a partnership with a local Authorised Dealer bank; it can facilitate inbound USD collections and international payouts but cannot act as a bank for ZAR, and all ZAR settlement must traverse the partner bank's local clearing rails. Stripe operates as a payment facilitator via local acquiring partners and settles USD/EUR via SWIFT into a local Foreign Currency Account; it is not licensed as an EMI by SARB. Offshore digital EMIs (Revolut Business, Payoneer and similar) cannot open ZAR-denominated accounts for South African corporates without a local bank licence, FSP licence and FinSurv registration; their use is limited to holding non-ZAR funds offshore, which creates an immediate 180-day repatriation exposure for the underlying service-export revenue. Recommended architecture: a local tier-1 operational account paired with Wise and Stripe for card and payout collection, with automatic conversion and same-day SWIFT settlement into the local FCA.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders remit via SWIFT MT103 (USD or EUR) to the company's Capital Escrow / Share Subscription account held at the Authorised Dealer, quoting the BOP purpose code for equity participation in a private non-financial corporation (foreign direct investment). The Authorised Dealer issues a BOP cover-note reference at conversion, applies the market rate, and credits the ZAR escrow; the allotment must be reflected in the CIPC share register within 30 days and matched to the board-resolution file. Prior FinSurv approval is not required for ordinary shares in an unlisted private company, but the bank retains the full subscription file (resolution, instrument of subscription, shareholder list, source-of-funds evidence) for a five-year audit trail. Inbound funds without a valid purpose code or with a mismatch to the FICA profile are held in suspense and are not available for disbursement.International supplier and subscription-tool payments (Adobe, Figma, AWS, OpenAI, GitHub): SWIFT MT103 from the ZAR operating account via the Authorised Dealer, quoting the current-account services purpose code, with optional conversion under a Forward Exchange Contract. Domestic disbursements (payroll, freelancers, hosting, local SaaS): BankservAfrica Automated Clearing Bureau (EFT/ACH) same-day or next-day rails, or SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement) for urgent high-value ZAR transfers. Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) are required only where a supplier demands documentary protection; standard practice for SaaS and digital-tool procurement is open-account MT103 settlement against invoice. Platform payouts: Stripe and Wise batch-settle USD/EUR in a single SWIFT MT103 into the company's Foreign Currency Account, after which the treasury desk converts to ZAR; each inbound platform settlement must be reconciled to the individual customer ledger to satisfy the 180-day service-export reporting obligation.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contract (FEC) on USD/ZAR or EUR/ZAR with an Authorised Dealer, covering committed subscription-receivable or supplier-payable exposures for up to 12 months with standard rollover provisions.Plain-vanilla foreign-currency options (purchased USD put / ZAR call and USD call / ZAR put) acquired from an Authorised Dealer for hedging current-account cash flows without speculative intent.Foreign Currency Account (FCA) natural hedge, retaining export proceeds in USD or EUR for up to 180 days as a non-derivative warehousing technique, then converting under a rolling FEC.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) solely to hedge confirmed foreign-currency borrowings or deposits, subject to FinSurv reporting; not available for speculative positions.Onshore deliverable ZAR forwards only; offshore non-deliverable forwards referencing ZAR are not permissible for resident corporate hedging and must be avoided to prevent exchange-control contravention.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Wise operates in South Africa as a registered accountable institution and licensed financial services provider through a partnership with a local Authorised Dealer bank; it can facilitate inbound USD collections and international payouts but cannot act as a bank for ZAR, and all ZAR settlement must traverse the partner bank's local clearing rails. Stripe operates as a payment facilitator via local acquiring partners and settles USD/EUR via SWIFT into a local Foreign Currency Account; it is not licensed as an EMI by SARB. Offshore digital EMIs (Revolut Business, Payoneer and similar) cannot open ZAR-denominated accounts for South African corporates without a local bank licence, FSP licence and FinSurv registration; their use is limited to holding non-ZAR funds offshore, which creates an immediate 180-day repatriation exposure for the underlying service-export revenue. Recommended architecture: a local tier-1 operational account paired with Wise and Stripe for card and payout collection, with automatic conversion and same-day SWIFT settlement into the local FCA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 must be submitted within 12 months after the end of the company's year of assessment unless SARS prescribes an earlier date by notice. For a standard 28/29 February year-end, this is effectively the last business day of the following February.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional return and payment are due within six months after the start of the year of assessment; the second is due on the last business day of the year of assessment; the optional third top-up is due no later than six months after the year of assessment.
