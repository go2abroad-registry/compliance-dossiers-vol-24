Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – official eFiling portal and website: www.sars.gov.za / secure.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic entity must register for income tax with SARS to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number, which is issued after successful registration through SARS eFiling using the company registration documents (e.g., Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of directors, identity documents, proof of business address). VAT registration is a separate process completed via SARS eFiling on forms VAT101 or VAT101F, after which SARS issues the VAT registration number/reference. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African consumers may also be required to register for VAT under the electronic-services regime if the statutory threshold is exceeded.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign or domestic entity must register for income tax with SARS to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number, which is issued after successful registration through SARS eFiling using the company registration documents (e.g., Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of directors, identity documents, proof of business address). VAT registration is a separate process completed via SARS eFiling on forms VAT101 or VAT101F, after which SARS issues the VAT registration number/reference. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African consumers may also be required to register for VAT under the electronic-services regime if the statutory threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election or a general hybrid mismatch elective classification. Tax classification follows the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 and the Companies Act 71 of 2008. A proprietary limited company is taxed at entity level as a resident company. Statutory elections relevant to classification or restructuring include: (i) VAT group registration election under section 50 of the VAT Act; (ii) corporate restructure rollover elections under sections 45–47 of the Income Tax Act; (iii) small-business corporation (SBC) status election under section 12E of the Income Tax Act; and (iv) hybrid mismatch rules implemented via the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action 2 framework, which override any elective outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration applies when the value of taxable supplies exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period (or is reasonably expected to exceed this in the next 12 months). Voluntary VAT registration is available to vendors whose taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period. For non-resident suppliers of electronic services, the compulsory registration threshold is also ZAR 1,000,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from South Africa by a VAT vendor may be zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act if the vendor has supplied the goods and retains documentary proof that the goods were physically removed from South Africa within the prescribed time periods (generally within 90 days of the date of supply). Services supplied to non-residents, including cloud software and SaaS subscriptions for consumer micro-business products, may be zero-rated under the VAT Act service zero-rating provisions when the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or movable property situated in South Africa and are effectively performed or consumed outside South Africa for the non-resident. For electronic/SaaS supplies to non-residents, the vendor must evidence the non-resident status of the customer and the absence of local consumption. Supplies of electronic services to South African residents are subject to VAT at 15%.

  • Customs SAD500 Export Declaration (SARS Customs export declaration, including the SARS Customs stamp or electronic clearance acknowledgement)
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or other transport document evidencing physical export (required for goods; not applicable to pure software exports)
  • Commercial Invoice and export sales contract
  • Proof of settlement from the foreign buyer, such as an inbound international remittance or SARB-accepted Foreign Exchange Certificate (FEC)
  • For services and digital SaaS: signed service/software licence agreement, customer's foreign IP address or other geo-location evidence, and a narrative memorandum demonstrating performance outside South Africa

South Africa does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), e-invoicing API, or digital stamping obligation. VAT vendors must issue tax invoices containing the prescribed particulars under section 20 of the VAT Act; electronic tax invoices are accepted if they contain all required data and are verifiable by SARS. VAT returns (VAT201) are filed electronically through SARS eFiling, and SARS may access invoice-level data during audits or via third-party data reporting; no government-mandated invoice clearance or real-time electronic certification is in force at present.

SARS may retrospectively disallow zero-rating where export proof is insufficient, assess VAT at the standard rate of 15% on the transaction value, and claw back any refund already paid, including interest calculated from the original refund date. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2014, an understatement penalty may be imposed as a percentage of the VAT shortfall, ranging from 10% to 200% depending on the conduct (e.g., substantial understatement, gross negligence, or intentional tax evasion). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may further trigger criminal prosecution under the VAT Act and Tax Administration Act, carrying fines up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment of up to 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand (ZAR) is a floating, fully convertible currency for genuine current-account transactions, including SaaS service-export receipts, operational disbursements and dividend remittances, all of which may be settled through an Authorised Dealer (AD) without prior FinSurv approval. Capital-account flows are liberalised but monitored: an inward equity subscription requires no prior approval but must be reported by the AD on Form F1 (Inward Foreign Investment Report) within 30 days; the non-resident shareholder may access ordinary dividends after the Companies Act 71 of 2008 solvency-and-liquidity test and upon withholding of 20% dividends tax (or a lower double-tax treaty rate). Profit remittance is not blocked, but the AD will screen for loop structures or round-tripping, and the company must keep its CIPC beneficial-ownership register current and its service-export invoices traceable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exchange Control Regulation 7 and the FinSurv Export Rules impose a full-repatriation obligation rather than a percentage surrender quota: 100% of the invoiced value of exported services must be received in South Africa into an account with an Authorised Dealer within 180 days of the service billing or invoice date, net only of commercially justifiable deductions (PSP fees, chargebacks and applicable withholding taxes shown in the PSP settlement report), and must be converted into ZAR at the prevailing market rate unless FinSurv has issued a not-for-conversion approval or an offshore foreign-currency account approval. All settlement proceeds collected by Paddle or Lemon Squeezy for the account of the (Pty) Ltd must therefore be swept to the SA AD within that 180-day window; any retention abroad constitutes a technical exchange-control contravention.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting jointly through its Prudential Authority (PA) for bank supervision and its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) for exchange control; the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) supervises market conduct, and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) administers AML/CFT enforcement.

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a payment-collection-only rail; Low as a treasury or capital-holding vehicle. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The investor must not route equity funds through an EMI or a foreign account of the SA company; the only compliant rail is a SWIFT wire (MT103 or MT202/202COV) to the SA AD's nostro account, referencing "ZA_PTY_LTD - inward equity subscription" and stating "subscription for ordinary shares in an unrestricted South African private company; ISIC 6201, no special licence required". The AD undertakes FICA/CDD, converts at the ruling market rate into the capital escrow or current account, files Form F1 with FinSurv within 30 days, and issues the company a notification of inward foreign investment. The company must then update the register of members, reflect the allotment in CIPC filings and maintain the beneficial-ownership register; funds are useable for SaaS infrastructure and trade disbursements once the AD has acknowledged the reporting, and the capital is marked in the AD's records to support future disinvestment or dividend matching. No prior FinSurv approval is required for a subscription in an unrestricted sector, but amounts above ZAR 10 million or involving high-risk counterparties trigger enhanced pre-screening by the bank.The primary clearing rail for outbound service trade is SWIFT MT103 routed through the SA AD's correspondent network, with the underlying contract, VAT treatment of imported services under the VAT Act 89 of 1991 (reverse-charge on the South African recipient, where applicable) and BOP reason code (software licence fees, cloud infrastructure, professional services) attached to each payment instruction. Commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700 series) are only relevant for physical-goods procurement and are not applicable to consumer micro-SaaS; for enterprise customers, the appropriate instruments are standby letters of credit or bank guarantees issued by the SA AD as performance security. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% standard corporate income tax rate for all companies, applicable to (Pty) Ltd from the 2023/24 year of assessment onwards.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividend tax on the gross amount of any dividend paid by the (Pty) Ltd to its shareholders; withheld by the company and paid over to SARS.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

ITR14 must be filed within 12 months after the company's financial year-end, subject to earlier administrative due dates published by SARS. Provisional tax: first payment due six months after the start of the year of assessment; second payment due on the last business day of the year of assessment; final top-up payment due within six months after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South African transfer-pricing documentation is required where cross-border related-party transactions are present. The SARS guidance expects a Local File once annual related-party turnover or comparable transaction value exceeds approximately ZAR 100 million, and a Master File for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding ZAR 10 billion. A micro-SaaS (Pty) Ltd is unlikely to meet these thresholds, but must maintain contemporaneous evidence that all cross-border related-party dealings with shareholders, affiliates and SaaS platform operators are at arm's length.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to other financial centres. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (ss.80A-80L Income Tax Act) and the Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules (ss.9D and 9E) require genuine commercial substance and business purpose. A (Pty) Ltd claiming South African tax residence must demonstrate that its board meetings, strategic decision-making, employees and operational management are situated in South Africa; the absence of such substance may lead SARS to recharacterise the local company as a conduit or to attribute the income to foreign shareholders or CFCs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to File and Pay

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty up to 200% of the tax shortfall, prescribed interest on unpaid tax, criminal fines, and imprisonment for up to five years under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or conceal non-compliance may be held jointly and severally liable for the company's fiscal obligations; civil restitution, preservation orders and travel bans may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke GAAR to disregard the structure, reallocate income to the local entity, deny deductions, and levy 100% interest on unpaid tax; CFC rules may further attribute passive foreign income to South African shareholders.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under section 77 of the Tax Administration Act and common law where the company is used as a sham, rendering directors personally liable for company debts.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust affected transactions to the arm's length price, with resultant additional assessments, prescribed interest, and an understatement penalty up to 200% for substantial understatements caused by intentional tax avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors who falsely sign or approve transfer-pricing documentation may be personally liable for penalties, and may face director disqualification proceedings.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act include fines of up to ZAR 10 million and imprisonment for up to 15 years for serious money-laundering offences; CIPC administrative penalties apply separately for beneficial ownership default.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally liable for AML failures; SARS and the National Prosecuting Authority may obtain asset forfeiture orders, bank account freezes and travel bans.
