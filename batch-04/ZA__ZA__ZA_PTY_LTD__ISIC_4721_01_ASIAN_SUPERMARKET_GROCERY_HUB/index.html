Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); national self-assessment portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); entity incorporation and company registry: Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) portal (https://www.cipc.co.za); customs clearance: SARS Customs via e@syFile and Asycuda World.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must first incorporate under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 through CIPC to obtain a company registration number. Thereafter the company registers as a taxpayer with SARS via eFiling to obtain an income tax reference number (IT number), and, upon exceeding or expecting to exceed the VAT threshold, files the VAT 101 application to obtain a VAT vendor number. A foreign entity not locally incorporated but making taxable supplies in South Africa may register directly for VAT (and PAYE if applicable) under the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991, supplying proof of non-resident status and appointing a SARS-recognized representative vendor. Non-resident shareholding is permitted and no minimum capital is required. A municipal business licence (Business Act 71 of 1991) is required before trading.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a South Africa-incorporated (Pty) Ltd is a tax resident company taxed at 27% normal tax on worldwide income (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); no pass-through eligibility and no tax-exempt zone status; distributions are subject to 20% dividends tax at shareholder level.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; classification is statutory. Available elections relevant to this entity: (1) VAT grouping election under s.50 of the VAT Act for closely linked entities; (2) voluntary VAT registration election once annual taxable turnover reaches ZAR 50,000; (3) foreign hybrid entity election under s.25B(2A) of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 for a foreign entity treated as a company in its residence jurisdiction; (4) anti-hybrid rule elections under s.23K of the Income Tax Act; (5) CFC elections and participation-exemption elections for foreign subsidiaries. All elections must be submitted to SARS within prescribed statutory timeframes via eFiling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 annual taxable turnover (s.23(1) VAT Act); voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 annual taxable turnover (s.23(2)); foreign suppliers of electronic services: compulsory registration under s.23A with no threshold. Imported goods are subject to VAT at 15% at customs, with the importer claiming an input tax deduction on receipt of the customs release documentation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies under s.11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where movable goods are supplied to a non-resident and physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of supply (extension possible for unavoidable delays). Cross-border consignment sales of packaged Asian grocery products qualify if proof of physical removal is retained. Services rendered to non-residents that are not supplied for use in South Africa qualify under s.11(2)(l). Ordinary in-store retail sales at POS terminals within the Republic are taxable at 15% and cannot be zero-rated.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD 500) lodged via SARS Customs (e@syFile/Asycuda World) reflecting HS codes, gross mass, and export justification
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from a South African port or airport
  • VAT 264 export certificate (where requested by SARS) and the supplier's full tax invoice or abridged tax invoice
  • Proof of foreign consideration: inward remittance certificate or bank foreign-exchange confirmation in compliance with Exchange Control Regulation 6
  • Supporting transport and insurance documents (CMR waybill, container receipt, packing list) corroborating the export movement

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is in force; SARS is deploying a Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC) e-invoicing pilot (SARS eInvoicing, PEPPOL-based) with voluntary participation pending gazetted mandatory timelines. Current mandates: a full tax invoice under s.20 of the VAT Act must be issued within 21 days of a taxable supply to a VAT vendor, containing prescribed particulars (vendor name and VAT number, invoice number, date, description, quantity, consideration, VAT amount, and VAT rate); abridged tax invoices are permissible for supplies up to ZAR 5,000 including VAT. POS terminals must be configured to issue SARS-compliant tax invoices because retail sales to consumers are final supplies; no fiscal device registration is required.

SARS Export Verification Unit cross-matches SAD 500 declarations, cargo manifests, bank forex receipts, and VAT 264 certificates. A false or unsupported zero-rating claim triggers additional VAT assessed under s.66 of the VAT Act plus interest (s.39 Tax Administration Act) and an understatement penalty of 10% to 150% of the shortfall under s.223 of the Tax Administration Act depending on culpability (substantial understatement, no reasonable grounds, gross negligence, or intentional evasion). Fraudulent conduct is criminally prosecutable under s.74B of the VAT Act and s.235 of the Tax Administration Act, with fines of up to ZAR 30,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 24 months per offence; refunds are clawed back retrospectively and directors may be held personally liable under s.20 of the Tax Administration Act. Failure to receive export proceeds within 180 days attracts exchange-control penalties and potential prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is freely usable for genuine current-account flows (imports, services, salaries, interest, dividends and branch profits) without prior FinSurv approval, but every remittance must be routed through an Authorised Dealer (AD) bank in South Africa with full documentary support. Capital-account flows remain administered: inbound equity subscriptions by non-residents are permitted through the AD banking system without the former loop-structure consent, but must be declared on the AD's Balance of Payments (BOP) returns and linked to an EM-code/INPF inward-transfer record so that future dividend and capital repatriations can be matched. Outward capital flows (offshore treasury accounts, loans to connected foreign parties, outward investments) generally require FinSurv approval or a clearly delegated AD consent. In practical terms, dividend/profit remittances are encumbered less by convertibility than by documentation integrity: the AD will require a valid SARS tax clearance (tax compliance status) for foreign remittance, audited or management-account reserve calculations, INPF/EM evidence that the original investment was brought into South Africa through the banking system, and CRS/FATCA self-certifications of the non-resident shareholder. As the entity's business is an Asian-specialty grocery importer/retailer, ZAR sales receipts convert naturally; the residual convertibility friction is confined to validating import payables and maintaining compliant FCA balances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is currently no fixed mandatory conversion (surrender) percentage for export proceeds; the historical partial-surrender regime was liberalised so that exporters may retain 100% of foreign export receipts in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer. The operative statutory and compliance obligations are: (i) the full export value must be received within 180 days from the date of shipment unless FinSurv has approved a longer period; (ii) proceeds must be brought into South Africa's banking system by being credited to an FCA or converted to ZAR through an AD; (iii) every receipt must be supported by the SAD500 bill of entry, commercial invoice, transport document and proof of shipment. If the entity re-exports goods or earns fees from cross-border marketing, the same 180-day repatriation rule applies. Failure to repatriate or offering proceeds to an unauthorised foreign account is an exchange-control contravention exposing the company and responsible directors to administrative penalties and criminal liability under the Currency and Exchanges Act.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Manual (as revised), and the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment-instruction utility, but Low as the principal treasury clearing rail for a South African resident company. South African exchange-control rules require residents to route all cross-border receipts and payments through an Authorised Dealer incorporated in South Africa; an offshore digital EMI (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer) cannot hold or operate the company's foreign-currency cash pool in substitution for a SARB-regulated FCA. Using an offshore EMI to park trade collections or to fund suppliers outside the AD channel would constitute unapproved retention/acquisition of foreign currency and would expose the company to FinSurv penalties, adverse findings on the next AD audit, and difficulty in evidencing the EM-code trail for future dividend remittances. Lawful limited use: the company may designate the EMI account of a non-resident supplier as the beneficiary of an MT103/OPC payment originated by the AD; supplier-side fintech receipts do not affect the SA treasury structure. The recommended posture is to operate via a major AD (Standard Bank, FNB, Absa or Nedbank) with FCA and direct SWIFT coverage into Asia and to avoid any offshore bulk-funding or collections aggregation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for non-resident shareholder funding: (1) execute the board/shareholder resolutions and CIPC allotment filings; (2) instruct the shareholder's foreign bank to wire funds to the chosen AD-nostro beneficiary account of the company, using a purpose line 'Equity Subscription - [Company Reg. No.]' and the shareholder's tax identification; (3) present to the AD the certification of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation, board resolution, share subscription agreement and control-organisation chart; (4) the AD records the inflow on an INPF/EM-code inward Foreign Direct Investment record and credits either the ZAR ordinary account (post-conversion) or a resident FCA if retention is elected; (5) issue the share certificates and ensure the AD lodges the BOP 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' entry in the monthly reporting cycle. Shareholder loans should be documented with a signed loan agreement, commercial rate rationale and the AD's inward-loan acceptance to separate 'Other Investment' liabilities from equity. No prior SARB approval is required for a straightforward unlisted equity injection into a non-resident-owned Pty, but the inward transfer evidence (INPF/EM) must be retained permanently because dividend repatriation at exit is permitted only against funds whose inward route has been proven through the AD banking system.Settlement of Asian supplier payables should use, in order of documentary strength: (i) SWIFT MT103 (OPC) current-account payments from the AD, each matched to purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list and (once shipped) transport document and SAD500 bill of entry; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600/URC522 (sight or usance 60-120 days) for high-value perishable vegetable and containerised condiment imports, with the AD issuing and the Chinese/SEA supplier bank negotiating or confirming; (iii) Documentary Collections (D/P, D/A) where LC cost is prohibitive; and (iv) SWIFT MT700/MT798 for amendments and standard bank-guarantee instruments. Payments must match the SARS importer code, customs-declared values and approved FCA balance or local ZAR conversion; the entity should centralise daily POS/card receipts through a single treasury bank in the morning and execute supplier wires only after the previous day's receivables have settled, thereby funding import disbursements from operational cash flow and reserving the FCA and an intra-day credit line for timing mismatches. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment-instruction utility, but Low as the principal treasury clearing rail for a South African resident company. South African exchange-control rules require residents to route all cross-border receipts and payments through an Authorised Dealer incorporated in South Africa; an offshore digital EMI (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer) cannot hold or operate the company's foreign-currency cash pool in substitution for a SARB-regulated FCA. Using an offshore EMI to park trade collections or to fund suppliers outside the AD channel would constitute unapproved retention/acquisition of foreign currency and would expose the company to FinSurv penalties, adverse findings on the next AD audit, and difficulty in evidencing the EM-code trail for future dividend remittances. Lawful limited use: the company may designate the EMI account of a non-resident supplier as the beneficiary of an MT103/OPC payment originated by the AD; supplier-side fintech receipts do not affect the SA treasury structure. The recommended posture is to operate via a major AD (Standard Bank, FNB, Absa or Nedbank) with FCA and direct SWIFT coverage into Asia and to avoid any offshore bulk-funding or collections aggregation.
