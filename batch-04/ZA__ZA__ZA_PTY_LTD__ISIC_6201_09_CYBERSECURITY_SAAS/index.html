Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box pass-through election; a (Pty) Ltd is universally a resident company subject to tax at 27% under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Available statutory elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration once R50,000 annual taxable supplies are reached under VAT Act s 23(3); (ii) VAT group registration election under VAT Act s 51(1)(a)(ii); (iii) election for the Small Business Corporation (SBC) concession under s 12E (0%–27% graduated rates, subject to ownership and turnover limits) where qualifying shareholders hold all shares; and (iv) intra-group interest deduction elections under s 45(3). Hybrid mismatch rules under OECD BEPS Action 2 apply and override artificial election-driven deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration at R1,000,000 actual or reasonably expected taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period (VAT Act s 23(1)); voluntary registration at R50,000 annual turnover (s 23(3)). A foreign supplier of electronic services (including cybersecurity SaaS) to South African recipients is compulsorily registrable once aggregate annual supplies of electronic services to South African customers meet the R1,000,000 threshold (VAT Act s 23(1A), as amended effective 1 April 2019, which repealed the earlier R50,000 e-services threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under VAT Act s 11(1)(a) where the vendor delivers goods from the Republic to a destination outside South Africa in the ordinary course of business, supported by prescribed documentary proof of export. Services: zero-rated under s 11(2)(l) where cybersecurity SaaS is supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not in South Africa at the time of supply, the services are not physically rendered in the Republic, and payment is received into the vendor's South African bank account in foreign currency. Under the 2019 e-services amendments, offshore SaaS supplied to a non-resident recipient outside the Republic qualifies for zero-rating; supplies to South African recipients are standard-rated at 15%. Substantiation requires a contract evidencing non-resident status, foreign-currency settlement proof, and bank or inward-remittance documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 form) and electronic clearance via the SARS Customs Acclaim/EDI platform for physical goods exports
  • Commercial invoice or full VAT invoice bearing export endorsement
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical outbound movement of goods (goods exports only)
  • Customs-stamped export declaration or carrier certificate as proof of export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank-stamped proof of foreign-currency payment received into a South African bank account (services exports)
  • Signed service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and foreign jurisdiction

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-approval invoice clearance regime is in force, unlike Latin American continuous transaction control models. The legal mandate is standards-based record-keeping and e-filing: VAT vendors must issue full VAT invoices under VAT Act s 20 with all prescribed mandatory fields, retain records for the statutory period, and file VAT 201 returns with payment via SARS eFiling on monthly or bi-monthly cycles. SARS has implemented an optional PEPPOL-based e-invoicing platform (2023-2024 pilot) with phased mandatory uptake planned, and mandates electronic data interchange for customs entries through the Acclaim/SEPA EDI messaging and SAD 500 declarations. Automated VAT-data matching and invoice-integrity audit algorithms operate on the SARS Digital Tax Platform; no de minimis exemption from the invoicing documentation standard applies.

Under South African Reserve Bank Exchange Control Regulation 5(1)(c), export proceeds must be received and banked in South Africa within 180 days of shipment or export date; failure to repatriate triggers exchange-control penalties, exporter blacklisting, and potential criminal proceedings. SARS audit powers under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (TAA) ss 40-50 and 78 permit retrospective assessment: the standard prescription period for additional assessments is 5 years, extended without limit for fraud or misrepresentation. Understatement penalties under TAA s 223(1) scale by conduct: 10% for substantial understatement, 25% for failure to take reasonable care, 50% for no reasonable grounds for the tax position, and 150% for intentional tax evasion, with an additional 20% for reportable arrangements. VAT Act s 60(1) imposes additional tax equal to the underpaid VAT, increased to 200% of the tax under s 60(2) for fraudulent or negligent misstatements; criminal sanctions for evasion under VAT Act ss 59-62 include fines and imprisonment. SARS may claw back zero-rated input VAT with interest; the only mitigation route is a voluntary disclosure application under TAA ss 226-233 submitted before SARS initiates the audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is fully convertible on the current account; Authorized Dealers may process documented current-account payments (including SaaS exports and imports of services) up to ZAR 50 million per transaction without SARB referral, following the 1 January 2021 increase from ZAR 15 million. The capital account remains regulated: inward non-resident direct investment via ordinary share subscription is permissible without prior FinSurv approval provided the funds arrive by SWIFT and the share issue is reported on Form CM-C1 (Notice of Issue of Shares to Non-Residents) within 30 days of allotment; dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are freely transferable subject to 20% dividends tax (treaty-reduced where applicable). Material convertibility hurdles for a Low_Risk_Standard cybersecurity SaaS exporter are limited to: (i) the 180-day export proceeds repatriation rule; (ii) documentary substantiation of intangible service exports; and (iii) FinSurv approval for inbound foreign borrowing, offshore guarantees, or share repurchases financed from foreign capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The historical mandatory surrender of export proceeds (formerly up to 75%) was abolished with effect from 1 February 2007. Under the Exchange Control Manual (paragraph CM-B1), proceeds from exported services rendered to non-residents must be fully repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the invoice or settlement date, unless FinSurv grants an extension; no minimum conversion into ZAR is imposed. Exporters may credit received foreign currency to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorized Dealer, retain the balance indefinitely, or convert at the prevailing market rate. For SaaS penetration-testing and vulnerability-scanning subscriptions, the exporter must retain contracts, platform usage evidence, invoicing, and SWIFT credit confirmations to evidence the export, support VAT zero-rating of exported services, and comply with SARS transfer-pricing rules.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), as national exchange-control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act No. 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations of 1961; operational enforcement is delegated to Authorized Dealers (domestic banks licensed under the Banks Act No. 94 of 1990), supervised by the SARB Prudential Authority and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act No. 38 of 2001.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: MEDIUM. South Africa has no electronic-money-institution licensing class equivalent to the UK or EU EMI regimes; under the Banks Act No. 94 of 1990 only licensed banks may take demand deposits, and foreign digital EMIs are not authorized as Authorized Dealers by the SARB Financial Surveillance Department. Offshore wallets (Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, Currenxie) therefore cannot provide a ZAR-resident clearing account and may be used only as collection vehicles for foreign-currency SaaS revenue, provided the proceeds are swept into the South African Authorized Dealer within the 180-day repatriation rule. Stripe (offered through South African payment-service-provider partners) is a permitted merchant-acquiring rail for card settlements into the SA operating account, while high-value enterprise invoices should be settled by direct SWIFT MT103.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder instructs its bank to remit via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the ZA entity's account at the Authorized Dealer, citing legal name 'ZA_PTY_LTD', CIPC registration number, and remittance narrative 'Share subscription - FDI equity capital'. The receiving bank records the inflow under the BOP statistical purpose code for direct-investment equity and issues an exchange-control acknowledgement to the company. The company must file Form CM-C1 (Notice of Issue of Shares to Non-Residents) through the Authorized Dealer within 30 days of allotment; prior FinSurv approval is required where the subscription exceeds the Authorized Dealer's ZAR 50 million threshold. To prevent rejection: the transferor must be the registered shareholder; no third-party or intermediate payment may be used; funds must originate from an account in the shareholder's name; source-of-funds evidence must be banked at onboarding; and the inflow must not remain in suspense beyond 5 business days pending FICA clearance.Operating disbursements (cloud infrastructure, cybersecurity tool licenses, overseas subcontractors) are executed via SWIFT MT103 from the ZA account under current-account liberalization without FinSurv approval up to ZAR 50 million per transaction, subject to supporting invoices and the applicable BOP code (telecommunications/computer/information services or professional/management consulting). Commercial letters of credit (UCP600) and documentary collections are available at the bank's trade desk for physical imports such as network security appliances. For SaaS exports the clearing rails are: (i) Stripe merchant acquiring with ZAR settlement into the local operating account for card subscriptions; (ii) direct SWIFT MT103 from enterprise clients into the USD Foreign Currency Account for contracts above USD 100,000, with treasury-directed conversion to ZAR; and (iii) periodic sweeps from offshore EMI wallets (Wise, Payoneer) into the South African operating account to ensure compliance with the 180-day export proceeds rule.Onshore deliverable Forward Exchange Contract (FEC) with an Authorized Dealer covering firm and quantifiable USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR, or GBP/ZAR exposures, tenor up to 12 months without FinSurv approval; tenors above 12 months require documented underlying exposure and FinSurv consent.European-style FX option (purchased call or put on ZAR) with an Authorized Dealer for hedging forecast subscription revenue, permissible on a non-speculative basis with the underlying export contract demonstrated.Offshore USD/ZAR Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) available through international bank desks or South African bank offshore branches only where the hedge aligns with real exposure and risk-management authorization is obtained; the domestic market is predominantly a deliverable-forward market.Cross-currency interest-rate swap (CCIRS) or cross-currency swap converting approved foreign-currency debt into ZAR, executed with an Authorized Dealer and backed by SARB-approved borrowing and a board-adopted treasury policy.Natural hedging via multi-currency cash pooling: retention of USD/EUR export proceeds in Foreign Currency Accounts with timed disbursement and intercompany netting, avoiding derivative instruments entirely.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: MEDIUM. South Africa has no electronic-money-institution licensing class equivalent to the UK or EU EMI regimes; under the Banks Act No. 94 of 1990 only licensed banks may take demand deposits, and foreign digital EMIs are not authorized as Authorized Dealers by the SARB Financial Surveillance Department. Offshore wallets (Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, Currenxie) therefore cannot provide a ZAR-resident clearing account and may be used only as collection vehicles for foreign-currency SaaS revenue, provided the proceeds are swept into the South African Authorized Dealer within the 180-day repatriation rule. Stripe (offered through South African payment-service-provider partners) is a permitted merchant-acquiring rail for card settlements into the SA operating account, while high-value enterprise invoices should be settled by direct SWIFT MT103.
