Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at https://www.sars.gov.za; Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) for corporate registration

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd is incorporated under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 via CIPC; post-incorporation, the entity must obtain a tax reference number by registering on the SARS eFiling platform (or by visiting a SARS branch, or through a registered tax practitioner). The application requires: (i) CIPC registration certificate (COR 14.3 / incorporation notice), (ii) Memorandum of Incorporation, (iii) Proof of residential and business address in South Africa, (iv) certified copies of director/shareholder identification and passports for non-resident beneficial owners, and (v) appointment of a South African-resident public officer. Registration is effected via the SARS 'Income Tax Registration (ITREG)' form; VAT registration is simultaneous through the VAT 101 form where turnover exceeds the R1 million compulsory threshold or R50,000 voluntary threshold. SARS issues a unique 15-digit income tax reference number and a separate VAT vendor number (VAT numbers follow the format 4-digit prefix plus sequential numbers). Processing time is typically 10–15 business days for standard corporate registrations; non-resident entities with no South African permanent establishment must follow the non-resident company registration lane through the SARS eFiling 'Non-Resident' module and would be registered for limited income tax and VAT obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style 'check-the-box' pass-through election. A (Pty) Ltd is unconditionally taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 (corporate income tax rate of 27% for years of assessment commencing on/after 1 April 2023; dividends are subject to 20% Dividends Tax withheld at source). Eligible elections available to the specialty tea salon entity include: (1) VAT group election under Section 50 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 — taxable group registration available to resident companies with ≥50% common shareholding, treating the group as a single VAT vendor with one VAT number and one return; (2) Small Business Corporation (SBC) concession under Section 12E of the Income Tax Act — progressive CIT rates (0%, 7%, 21%, 27%) for qualifying companies with gross turnover ≤ ZAR 20 million and satisfying the shareholder natural-person and passive-income tests; (3) Electoral determination of the financial year-end during the first year of registration (any month-end); (4) Section 23N hybrid mismatch rules — mandatory, no opt-in election; structured arrangements must be disclosed in the annual corporate income tax return; (5) Sections 11D and 12B capital allowance elections for eligible research/equipment investments. There is no representative-office election for tax purposes; branch profits (including remittance of the after-tax head-office branch result to the non-resident parent) are taxed at the 27% corporate rate without a further branch-remittance withholding tax, unlike in certain civil-law jurisdictions. Exchange Control approvals for foreign direct investment into a (Pty) Ltd are filed with the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as part of the CIPC/SARS compliance chain.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 (taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period). Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 (same lookback period). A specialty tea salon earning B2C income through POS/credit-card rails in excess of ZAR 1 million must register for VAT effective from the first day of the month following the crossing of the threshold. Failure to register attracts an under-declaration penalty of 10% of the VAT due plus an additional late-registration penalty; SARS may also assess VAT retrospectively from the day registration should have been effected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Although the primary activity is a domestic tea salon, the export zero-rating regime is available for: (i) export of specialty loose-leaf tea, ceramic tea ware and pre-packed retail gift sets supplied to a non-resident purchaser under Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 (goods physically exported from South Africa within 60 days of the date of supply, or such longer period as SARS may allow); (ii) export of services supplied to a non-resident person (e.g., tea-curation consulting, brewing methodology training, teahouse cultural design services) performed wholly outside South Africa under Section 11(2)(l) — the services must not be rendered to a resident of South Africa and must be physically performed in a foreign country. The supplier must retain proof of export and proof of payment in foreign currency. Where the tea salon sells to an export trading house or a local intermediary, zero-rating applies only if the goods are consigned directly by the exporter to a destination outside South Africa, and an export authority letter from the purchaser is kept on file. The tea salon is also entitled to claim input VAT credits on its domestic procurement (rents, equipment, packaging, loose-leaf stock) attributable to taxable and zero-rated supplies; it is not required to cede refunds, and the normal 21-business-day refund processing period applies once an eFiling VAT201 return reflecting a refund is submitted.

  • Customs Export Declaration — SARS Customs SAD 500 (Form DA 500) or the electronic Customs EDI clearing message evidencing export release
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) showing the tea salon as consignor and the non-resident buyer or its foreign agent as consignee, with the South African port of loading and foreign destination port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 fee/payment confirmation from a South African Authorised Dealer evidencing receipt of foreign-currency sale proceeds in the tea salon's domestic bank account
  • Tax invoice issued under Section 20(4) of the VAT Act, bearing the zero-rating indicator '0%' and an express cross-reference to Section 11(1)(a) or 11(2)(l)
  • Proof of export in the form of a SARS Customs clearance certificate / e-shipping manifest stamp confirming physical exit from the Republic within 60 days of the date of supply
  • Packing list and container/seal inspection report for containerised tea shipments
  • Commercial export contract / purchase order from the non-resident customer (to corroborate ownership and valuation)

South Africa does not impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime (no digital stamping, no pre-invoice cryptographic validation, no government-endorsed clearance API as in Latin America). SARS has introduced an optional e-invoicing framework through the eFiling/e@sys API (2024/2025 fiscal year) permitting structured ISO 20022 UBL tax invoice transmission; this is voluntary and interoperates retrospectively at audit. A valid SARS tax invoice must contain 15 mandatory fields under Section 20(4) of the VAT Act, including supplier and customer VAT numbers, sequential invoice serial, date, description of goods/services, quantity, price exclusive of VAT, the VAT amount and the total. Any POS system (local POS terminals and credit-card acquirers) must retain line-level transaction data and Z-report archives for a mandatory 5-year record-keeping period under Section 55 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. SARS performs periodic 'transaction data audit pulls' through POS data-extraction requests and may impose a penalty of up to ZAR 25,000 for record failures. Tax invoices that do not meet the Section 20(4) standard cause the input VAT deduction to be denied; the tea salon must advise its B2B and B2C customers that simplified or system-generated receipts do not satisfy input-claim evidentiary requirements.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict SARS risk-based audit. Interior tea salons that combine domestic POS retail with occasional exports face elevated verification. Failure to meet documentary or timing requirements results in the VAT being reclassified as standard-rated (15%) and the tea salon is liable for the full output VAT plus repayment of any refunded input VAT. The legal consequences are severe: (i) under Section 60 of the VAT Act, additional tax of 200% of the VAT improperly claimed; (ii) under-payment penalty of 10% of the unpaid tax plus interest at the prescribed statutory rate (currently 10.25% per annum) assessed under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; (iii) administrative non-compliance penalties up to ZAR 25,000 for record-keeping failure; (iv) criminal prosecution for tax evasion (fraudulent zero-rating of domestic supplies, false bills of lading, or staged export schemes) under Section 235 of the Tax Administration Act — punishable by a fine or imprisonment not exceeding 5 years; (v) retrospective clawback for up to 5 years from the date of the claim; (vi) suspension of the 'export vendor' status and barred access to refunds where SARS is satisfied of intentionality. Separate exchange-control exposure exists: under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and FinSurv rulings, export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days of export; non-repatriation of funds (e.g., retaining export receipts in a foreign bank account without FinSurv approval) attracts administrative penalties of ZAR 10,000 per contravention, and the entire export proceeds may be subjected to 20% exchange-control penalties; criminal liability arises where proceeds are deliberately retained offshore. If the tea salon exports through an intermediary, a joint liability agreement under Section 11(3) of the VAT Act must be concluded so that the principal exporter is protected from clawback; otherwise SARS may recover zero-rated VAT from both the salon and the intermediary. Digital platform sales (online tea orders) are additionally captured by final-consumption e-Commerce provisions if the purchaser arranges collection or the salon uses a fulfilment centre outside South Africa.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current-account transactions (goods, services, dividends, interest, royalties) provided the Authorised Dealer verifies underlying documentation and correct BOP purpose codes. Profit remittance and dividend payments to non-resident shareholders require no FinSurv approval if declared from distributable reserves supported by signed financial statements and SARS tax-compliance evidence, but remain subject to South African dividends tax at 20% and BOP1 reporting. Inbound equity injections are free of prior approval; outward capital flows and certain shareholder loan structures require Authorised Dealer approval or FinSurv notification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio applies to South African export proceeds. Export earnings must be fully repatriated to South Africa, either to an account with an Authorised Dealer or to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer, within 180 days from the date of shipment; longer periods may be granted on application. All exports of specialty tea and ceramics must be declared on the SARS SAD500 export declaration, and the payment must be matched to the export invoice for exchange-control audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the delegated authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1961 (as amended); regulatory administration is delegated to commercial banks licensed as Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as an ancillary collection channel, but low as a primary treasury solution. Offshore EMIs and digital cross-border platforms are not Authorised Dealers in South Africa and cannot lawfully operate the company's ZAR operating account, perform BOP declarations, or execute the mandatory FCA and export-proceeds reporting. They may be used for small-value inbound retail collections and multi-currency invoicing subject to a final sweep into the South African banking account, and must be evaluated against FICA and the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must wire funds via SWIFT MT103 from their own bank account into the designated capital account at the South African Authorised Dealer, using the remittance narrative 'Share Subscription Inward Foreign Direct Investment' and the appropriate BOP financial-account purpose code. The Authorised Dealer will require the share subscription resolution, updated share register and BOP instruction; no prior FinSurv approval is required for an ordinary issue of shares to a non-resident. Where funds arrive as a shareholder loan, the loan must be evidenced by a signed foreign-currency agreement with defined term and interest, approved by the Authorised Dealer or FinSurv as applicable, so that future interest and principal repayments remain remittable.Open-account trade payments for imported tea and ceramics are processed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 cover) by the Authorised Dealer against a BOP payment instruction and supporting invoice and customs records. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (28% for earlier years).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividend withholding tax (DWT) on the gross amount of any dividend declared and paid by the South African company to its shareholders. The (Pty) Ltd acts as withholding agent and must remit the tax to SARS by the last day of the month following the month in which the dividend was declared and paid; non-withholding triggers personal liability of the company under s64K.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 12 months of the end of the company's financial year (the 'year of assessment'). SARS issues an IT14 notice prompting submission; eFiling auto-calculation of balances; payment of any assessed liability falls due on the same due date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First estimate and payment: within 6 months after the start of the year of assessment (e.g., 31 August for a 28 February year-end). Second estimate and payment: by the last day of the year of assessment (last business day of February for a February year-end). Voluntary third estimate: within 6 months of the year-end, when the actual taxable amount is known.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold is codified in s31 ITA; the SARS Transfer Pricing Documentation Guidelines (2016) expect full contemporaneous documentation when the aggregate value of cross-border 'affected transactions' with connected persons exceeds R50 million in a year of assessment; a lower informal threshold of R5 million appears in SARS Practice Note 7 for record-keeping; Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated turnover is ≥ R10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not administer UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Substance is nonetheless legally relevant under: (i) the 'place of effective management' (POEM) test for tax residence — directors must exercise real and independent oversight in South Africa; (ii) the Controlled Foreign Company rules (s9D) — passive income of a CFC is attributed to SA shareholders unless a bona fide business / foreign business establishment exclusion applies; (iii) the hybrid-instrument and hybrid-payment rules (ss8E, 8F, 23M, 23N); and (iv) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ss80A–80L TAA), which voids 'impermissible avoidance arrangements' lacking commercial substance and subject to a dominant tax-avoidance purpose. A specialty tea salon with physical premises, employees, inventory, POS operations, and local management has strong economic substance; substance is threatened only if functions are artificially hollowed out (e.g., booking intangibles offshore while all value-creating activities occur in South Africa).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s235 TAA: fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years (10 years for repeat offenders); civil understatement penalty of 150% of the shortfall (s223 TAA Category 4); interest and 10% late-payment penalty compounded; tax clearance revoked, disqualifying the company from tenders, banking, and cross-border travel of principals.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the falsification are prosecuted as principals; representative taxpayer liability (s179 TAA) for unpaid withholding-type taxes; SARS may pursue the corporate veil where the company is a mere façade (Companies Act s20(9)) with civil recovery against the director's personal assets, coupled with departure prohibition orders while tax debts remain outstanding.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: SARS adjustment of the taxable income to an arm's length amount, with secondary adjustment (deemed loan/interest) and interest; understatement penalty of 50%–150% (ss222–223) for absence of reasonable care in concluding non-arm's length pricing; potential recharacterisation of the transaction under GAAR.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the pricing may be held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax under s179 if the company is wound up or is unable to pay; reckless approval of profit-stripping structures exposes directors to Companies Act s77 claims for losses suffered by the company as a result of reckless trading.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as non-resident (if POEM is abroad) subjecting the company to the 20% DWT on deemed distributions and 15% royalties/interest WHT on outbound flows; CFC attribution of profits to SA shareholders; GAAR voiding of tax benefits; estimated assessments and penalties of up to 150%.

Director personal liability: Deliberate maintenance of a facade to defeat SARS collection allows piercing of the veil (Companies Act s20(9)) and direct liability for company debts; directors may face disqualification from acting as directors under the Companies Act.
Anti-Money Laundering / FICA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanction by the Financial Intelligence Centre of up to R10 million per contravention; criminal prosecution under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act for money laundering with imprisonment of up to 15 years; SARS referral for tax evasion where the proceeds are undeclared.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to implement FICA-governance controls are individually exposed to administrative fines; connivance in laundering transactions yields criminal liability as co-principals, with travel bans and restraint orders under POCA.
Trading Without Statutory Tax Registrations

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties for each day of non-registration/non-disclosure (ss209–212 TAA); VAT and PAYE liabilities assessed retrospectively with 10% late-payment penalties and interest; criminal prosecution where deliberate concealment is shown.

Director personal liability: Representative taxpayer liability under s179 TAA extends to every director who is a 'responsible person' for the deduction and remittance of PAYE/VAT; unpaid trust taxes are recovered directly from directors' personal estates, and departure prohibition orders can be issued for outstanding debts.
Reckless Trading / Director Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil liability of directors for company debts under Companies Act s77(3)(b); SARS may apply to court to declare the company a juristic façade (s20(9)); criminal liability arises only where fraud or s235 tax evasion is also proven.

Director personal liability: Voidable preference / insolvency claims by the liquidator; personal restitution orders; travel bans under TAA s194 while outstanding tax debt exists; director disqualification orders.
