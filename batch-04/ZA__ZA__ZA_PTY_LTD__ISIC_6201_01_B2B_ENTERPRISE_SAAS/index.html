Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling portal (https://www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must apply to SARS for income tax registration using a completed CIT/IT registration form (e.g., forms IT77, CIT1, or via eFiling non-resident registration). VAT registration is separately effected via form VAT101 (VAT101e for eFiling), requiring submission of company documents, director/owner identification, proof of business address, and bank account confirmation; SARS issues a unique 10-digit income tax reference number and a separate VAT vendor number where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must apply to SARS for income tax registration using a completed CIT/IT registration form (e.g., forms IT77, CIT1, or via eFiling non-resident registration). VAT registration is separately effected via form VAT101 (VAT101e for eFiling), requiring submission of company documents, director/owner identification, proof of business address, and bank account confirmation; SARS issues a unique 10-digit income tax reference number and a separate VAT vendor number where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under South African income tax law. Entity classification follows the Income Tax Act No. 58 of 1962 (for companies) and the Companies Act No. 71 of 2008. A 'headquarter company' election (s.9I of the Income Tax Act) is available for qualifying international headquarter companies to access specific relief. VAT grouping under VAT Act s.50(3) is available for companies forming a group, and a foreign entity may elect to be treated as a vendor (with a local representative) if turnover thresholds are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (compulsory turnover threshold per 12-month period); voluntary registration from R50,000 per 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) goods exported from South Africa under a valid Customs export declaration (SAD500) where the supplier retains documentary proof that the goods were delivered to a non-resident outside South Africa within the prescribed period; and (b) certain services supplied to non-residents, including services physically performed outside South Africa or directly for export, under s.11(2) of the VAT Act. Enterprise cloud software sold to non-resident B2B customers may qualify as an exported service if the place of supply is outside South Africa and the prescribed documentary evidence is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Cargo Receipt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT transfer confirmation evidencing foreign currency payment

SARS does not operate a mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime. Vendors must issue full tax invoices under s.20 of the VAT Act and retain records electronically. SARS eFiling API integration (SARS Registered Representative and e@syFile) supports electronic submissions; no digital stamping or real-time fiscal validation is currently mandated.

SARS may impose additional VAT assessments together with understatement penalties of up to 150% of the undercharged VAT (Tax Administration Act No. 28 of 2011), statutory interest, and, in fraud cases, criminal prosecution (fine and/or imprisonment). Retrospective clawbacks of zero-rated refunds are legally enforceable if export documentation is deficient, the export does not physically leave South Africa within the legislated period, or funds are not repatriated within the 180-day exchange-control deadline without approved extension.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a managed-float ZAR with broad current-account liberalisation but selective capital-account exchange control. Bona fide service receipts and payments (SaaS subscriptions, licences, operational costs) are freely convertible through Authorised Dealers (ADs) subject to FICA/KYC and documentary checks. Dividend and profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful provided the shares were subscribed with foreign capital introduced through the banking system or with FinSurv-approved local funds, the company is tax-compliant and holds a SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN, and the applicable Dividends Tax (20% statutory, treaty-reduced as appropriate) has been withheld and paid. Capital repatriation (share buy-backs, deregistration) and non-resident shareholder loans require explicit FinSurv or delegated AD approval. ZAR convertibility is therefore unrestricted for genuine trade but is actively monitored for round-tripping, over/under-invoicing, and sanctioned financing patterns.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service-export proceeds (including cross-border B2B SaaS fees) must be realised in South Africa within six months from the date of performance or invoicing; the Authorised Dealer may grant extensions under delegated authority, and FinSurv approval is required for longer deferrals. Upon receipt, 100% of the foreign currency must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer for conversion into ZAR or credited to an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with the AD. Amounts retained in an FCA remain subject to AD utilisation monitoring, may only be applied to approved working-capital or trade obligations of the exporter, and any unutilised surplus must be converted to ZAR when the underlying requirement lapses.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and Prudential Authority, operating under National Treasury policy, the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Foreign EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are not licensed by SARB as banks or Authorised Dealers and cannot independently hold ZAR settlement balances or clear South African payments as principals. They are viable only as collection-side rails (Stripe Billing, Wise B2B) through a licensed local sponsor or correspondent AD, with all funds repatriated into the company's South African bank account under proper purpose coding. Feasibility rating: Medium for inbound receivables collection and Low for outbound treasury management, because FX conversion and exchange-control reporting must ultimately be executed by a domestic AD. Warehousing SaaS receivables offshore through an unlicensed EMI would constitute an unauthorised foreign-currency account and create FICA and exchange-control breach risk.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque UBO chain involving multiple offshore holding layers or discretionary trusts

Root cause: Opaque UBO chain involving multiple offshore holding layers or discretionary trusts

Defense evidence: Full corporate registry extract for each layer, trust deed and trustee register, notarised beneficial-ownership declaration covering all UBOs at 25% and above, group organisation chart, apostilled passports and proof-of-address for natural-person UBOs, and signed confirmation that no nominee arrangements conceal ownership.
Sanctions, PEP, or adverse-media screening hits on shareholders, directors, or major client jurisdictions

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP, or adverse-media screening hits on shareholders, directors, or major client jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Independent sanctions screening report (UN, OFAC, EU, UK OFSI), sworn written explanation of the hit, source-of-wealth and source-of-funds file for PEP or high-net-worth UBOs, institutional compliance sign-off, and client-contract evidence demonstrating that no business is conducted with comprehensively sanctioned countries or blocked persons.
Inbound equity capital originating from low-correlation, high-risk, or crypto-intermediated channels

Root cause: Inbound equity capital originating from low-correlation, high-risk, or crypto-intermediated channels

Defense evidence: Twelve months of bank statements for the investing entity, notarised confirmation that funds are not derived from illicit or prohibited sources, crypto exchange fiat on/off-ramp reports and transaction-graph analysis where crypto intermediation occurred, tax references from the home jurisdiction, and the executed share subscription agreement.
Business-model inconsistency between declared revenue, payment processor rails, and bank-account inflows

Root cause: Business-model inconsistency between declared revenue, payment processor rails, and bank-account inflows

Defense evidence: Executed enterprise subscription contracts, VAT-return extracts and tax invoices, annual financial statements, a ledger-to-bank reconciliation report, and a written service-flow narrative confirming that all foreign receipts are routed exclusively through the declared BIC/IBAN and reported to the Authorised Dealer.
Unusual transaction patterns indicating round-tripping, rapid in-out flows, or financing of third parties

Root cause: Unusual transaction patterns indicating round-tripping, rapid in-out flows, or financing of third parties

Defense evidence: Transaction-level justifications with invoices and contracts, board minutes approving the treasury policy, proof of economic substance (employees, premises, R&D expenditure), negative-news clearances from World-Check or LexisNexis, and a formal undertaking that no speculative FX or circular capital movements are undertaken.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders subscribe for shares by remitting SWIFT MT103 payments from the investor's bank to the company's capital account at a domestic Authorised Dealer, with the remittance narrative clearly stating 'Share subscription - Foreign Direct Investment - equity capital' and naming the investor. The AD screens the transfer under FICA, classifies it under EBOPS/BOP as 'Direct investment - Equity capital', records it on the SARB inward-investment reporting framework, and issues the exchange-control acknowledgment. Shares must be allotted only after funds have cleared, and the capital proceeds must not be commingled with current-account trade receipts. Shareholder loans must be separately documented and approved where they fall outside the AD's delegated borrowing limits, with interest and principal servicing subject to SARS tax-compliance confirmation and applicable withholding rules. The AD will retain purpose codes, VAT references, and invoicing documentation as part of the audit trail.Outbound settlements for cloud infrastructure, subcontractors, and vendors are cleared through the Authorised Dealer via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the ZAR operational account, with the AD executing the spot conversion and applying the appropriate BOP service code (for example, 'Computer and information services' for SaaS-related procurement, or 'Charges for the use of intellectual property' for royalty flows). Open-account payment terms of 30 to 60 days are standard for SaaS inputs; commercial documentary credits (LCs) are reserved for hardware procurement or large one-off contracts. Royalty and licence fees to non-resident IP owners are processed by ADs under delegated FinSurv authority at arm's length, subject to the 15% withholding tax on royalties (treaty-reduced) and SARS TCS confirmation. Intra-African disbursements may additionally clear via the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System (PAPSS), but USD and EUR SWIFT clearing remains the primary institutional rail.Onshore deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) traded with an Authorised Dealer against USD, EUR, GBP, and other major currencies, with tenors generally available up to 12-36 months within AD limits and longer tenors requiring FinSurv approvalNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) used for currencies without an active onshore deliverable market or where physical settlement of the hedge is impracticalCross-currency swaps (CCS) and associated interest-rate swaps for hedging foreign-currency-denominated committed capital or approved shareholder-loan exposuresPurchased FX options (put or call), zero-cost collars, and risk-reversal structures that cap adverse ZAR movement on forecast SaaS subscription receiptsSpot interbank conversions and FX swaps executed through the Authorised Dealer for short-term liquidity management within current-account limitsAll instruments are permitted only with documented underlying commercial exposure; naked speculative positions and round-tripping transactions are prohibited under the Exchange Control Regulations.
