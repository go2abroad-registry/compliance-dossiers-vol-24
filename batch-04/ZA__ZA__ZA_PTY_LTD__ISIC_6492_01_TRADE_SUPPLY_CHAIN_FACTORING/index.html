Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), eFiling portal at https://efiling.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic entity must register for income tax via SARS eFiling by completing the applicable company registration process (Company Registration in eFiling) and furnishing certified copies of the Memorandum of Incorporation, director identification, and proof of registered address. Upon acceptance, a tax reference number is issued. For VAT, the entity must submit Form VAT101 (Application for VAT Registration) through eFiling; after approval, a VAT vendor registration number is assigned. For customs purposes, the entity must separately obtain an importer/exporter code via SARS e@syFile Customs.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign or domestic entity must register for income tax via SARS eFiling by completing the applicable company registration process (Company Registration in eFiling) and furnishing certified copies of the Memorandum of Incorporation, director identification, and proof of registered address. Upon acceptance, a tax reference number is issued. For VAT, the entity must submit Form VAT101 (Application for VAT Registration) through eFiling; after approval, a VAT vendor registration number is assigned. For customs purposes, the entity must separately obtain an importer/exporter code via SARS e@syFile Customs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification election exists. A (Pty) Ltd is automatically taxed as a resident company on worldwide income at entity level. Elective mechanisms include: (i) Small Business Corporation (SBC) election under Section 12E of the Income Tax Act, subject to shareholders and turnover limits; (ii) voluntary VAT registration under Section 23(1) of the VAT Act where taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000; (iii) no statutory hybrid mismatch elections are available — hybrid instruments and entities are governed by the Income Tax Act anti-avoidance provisions (Sections 8E, 8F, 25H) without elective opt-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory registration threshold per 12-month period); ZAR 50,000 (voluntary registration threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where they are supplied to an export country in the ordinary course of business, are removed from South Africa within the prescribed period (generally 90 days from the date of supply), and documentary proof of export is retained. Services are zero-rated under Section 11(2)(l) where supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time of supply, and the services are not used or consumed in South Africa (or are supplied in connection with exported goods and physically rendered outside South Africa). Cross-border factoring / reverse factoring services supplied to non-resident clients may therefore qualify for zero-rating upon proof of non-residency, place of consumption, and receipt of foreign consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank swift/forex proof)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based continuous transaction control is legislated. SARS requires VAT vendors to issue VAT invoices (paper or electronic) that comply with Section 20 of the VAT Act, containing prescribed particulars. VAT returns (Form VAT201) must be submitted electronically via SARS eFiling.

SARS may disallow zero-rating if the documentary evidence is incomplete or the goods/services are found not to meet the physical export or foreign consumption criteria. Assessments are subject to reopening for a prescription period of up to 5 years (under normal cases). Understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act apply at tiered rates, reaching 100% for intentional/reckless conduct and 150% for fraud, plus interest. Criminal prosecution is possible for fraudulent VAT claims. In the case of unlawful zero-rating, the VAT is recoverable from the supplier together with penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a flexible-rate currency with a current account that is effectively free of quantitative limits for bona fide trade and services. Capital account operations remain subject to exchange-control registration and FinSurv/SARB approval thresholds. Factoring of export receivables is treated as a current-account trade-finance transaction, provided the fact book is evidenced by actual invoices and the underlying cross-border goods/services are verified. Profit remittance in the form of dividends or branch remittances is freely permitted through an authorised dealer once (i) the statutory dividends tax (20%) or applicable corporate tax has been settled, (ii) a valid SARS tax clearance or compliance PIN is produced, and (iii) the non-resident beneficial ownership chain (which cannot be a prohibited 'no-relevance' trust) is fully documented. For branch remittances of profits, a tax confirmation letter from SARS is generally required. Residual exchange-control risk exists for flows not supported by genuine underlying transactions, for round-tripping, or for transactions with counterparties in high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South African law imposes a repatriation rather than a conversion-to-ZAR surrender requirement. Exporters of goods and services through a registered entity must obtain full payment for export proceeds within 180 days from the date of shipment/export, as measured in accordance with SARB's Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers. The proceeds must be 'settled' through an authorised dealer: they may be retained in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held by an authorised dealer or converted to ZAR at the commercial rate. There is currently no mandatory percentage conversion into ZAR; the exchange-control rule is that the exporter must confirm receipt and account for the export on an Exchange Control Form 1 (or the dealer's electronic report) and surrender control of the funds to an authorised dealer. Any extension beyond 180 days requires a formal application to the authorised dealer or FinSurv based on trade terms, documentary delivery, or insolvency of the foreign debtor. In a factoring structure, the South African factor must demonstrate that the underlying export proceeds will be paid to a South African bank account of the exporter or, where the factor purchases the receivables, to the factor's own Authorised Dealer account under an approved trade-finance arrangement failing which the factor may be treated as having contravened the repatriation requirement.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting principally through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the delegated authority of the Minister of Finance pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations. SARS (South African Revenue Service) operates as the fiscal gateway supporting tax-clearance verification.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited / Low-viability as a primary treasury channel. Non-resident fintech EMIs and digital banks are not licensed by the South African Reserve Bank as Authorised Dealers and therefore cannot lawfully provide onshore ZAR payment, collection, escrow, or clearing services to a South African legal person. They may be used as ancillary receivable-collection portals only if (i) the funds are routed onward to the entity's SARB-authorised dealer account in South Africa, (ii) the EMI does not create a liability that violates the Exchange Control Regulations, and (iii) all FICA reporting is still effected by the domestic bank. The FIC Act 2001 requires a 'responsible institution' to identify and verify the ultimate beneficial owners; many cross-border EMIs do not satisfy the enhanced-diligence data required by South African banks and flag the entity as higher risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions (shareholder loans, equity injections, or factoring-line subordination) must be routed to the company's ZAR or Foreign Currency Account held with a SARB Authorised Dealer using SWIFT MT103/MT202. The entity must use the correct payment narrative and, if requested, BOP purpose code in the SWIFT instruction: 'EQUI' or 'Loan' for equity/debt funding, supplementing with an inward-investment reporting form as required by SARB FinSurv (the Bank will complete the electronic Balance of Payments declaration on its Trade/Cash system). The verification package that must accompany the transaction consists of (i) the board resolution approving the capital injection; (ii) a share certificate / loan agreement; (iii) a valuation certificate if a non-resident makes a contribution-in-kind; (iv) source-of-funds EV clearly identifying the subscribing or lending institution; and (v) a declaration that the funds will not be used to finance prohibited activities, such as invoicing between a resident and non-resident without economic substance. The authorised dealer will then issue a Foreign Liability confirmation or allocate appropriate exchange-control reference for future disinvestment and dividend transfer / loan redemption.For operating outflows such as payment to offshore factoring counterparties, service providers or the purchase of receivables from exporters, the entity will use: (a) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for settlement of uncomplicated fees, interest, purchase consideration, and low-value services; (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (advising, confirmation or negotiation) governed by UCP600 where the cross-border trade requires documentary presentation by the exporter of the factoring assignment notice and shipping documents; (c) Bank Payment Obligations (BPOs) on, for example, SWIFT gpi / TSU rails where the transaction is mirrored factoring payable/receivable; and (d) SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement) for ZAR-denominated interbank settlement, with the Authorised Dealer executing the cross-currency leg on behalf of the entity under the nominated purpose code. Payments must always be supported by the underlying export/import invoice, factoring agreement and customs/SARS documentation. The clearing rail must not be used to substantiate round-tripped transactions or to bypass the repatriation rule.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) booked with a resident Authorised Dealer, matched to the amount and tenor of the underlying exported or imported trade receivable/payable in accordance with SARB's Currency and Exchanges Manual.Cross-currency swaps / interest-rate swaps for term debt liabilities associated with trade-finance funding if the underlying current-account exposure is evidenced and approved by the Authorised Dealer.Embedded hedging in the factoring facility (e.g., insurance and bank offset arrangements) only where bank-held collateral covers both the trade debt and the foreign-currency receivables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not generally permissible for a South African resident unless expressly approved by SARB FinSurv; offshore ZAR/NDF derivative products are excluded from the onshore treasury blueprint.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; the previous standard rate was 28%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividend withholding tax (DWT) on the amount of any dividend paid by a South African resident company after 1 March 2012. A reduced treaty rate applies only where the beneficial owner of the dividend is a treaty resident and has submitted the required declaration and supporting documents before the dividend payment date.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 must be filed within 12 months after the company's financial year-end, unless SARS prescribes a shorter notice period in writing. Extensions are discretionary and must be applied for before the due date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional return and payment: within the last day of the sixth month of the year of assessment. Second provisional return and payment: on the last day of the year of assessment. Voluntary top-up payment: within six months after the year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 10,000,000. SARS requires a taxpayer engaged in cross-border 'affected transactions' (transactions with connected persons) to prepare and maintain contemporaneous Transfer Pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) when the aggregate value of those affected transactions exceeds ZAR 10,000,000 in a year of assessment. Documentation must be produced to SARS within 21 days of a written request.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has not adopted the Gulf-Cooperation-Council-style 'Economic Substance Regulations' as a standalone statute. Substance is nevertheless enforced through: (i) the 'place of effective management' (POEM) test for tax residence under Section 1 of the Income Tax Act; (ii) the controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution regime; (iii) the general anti-avoidance rules in Section 103(1); and (iv) the common-law substance-over-form doctrine. A trade-finance factoring (Pty) Ltd claiming tax treaty benefits or low-risk status must demonstrate substantive South African operations—physical office, employees with real decision-making authority, trade risk control and independent execution of the factoring and reverse-factoring contracts—so that the entity cannot be ignored as a conduit or pass-through or treated as a shell corporation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Section 170 and Section 233 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 and the common-law offence of fraud, a convicted person is liable on conviction to imprisonment for a period not exceeding 10 years, to a fine of up to ZAR 10,000,000 (or twice the tax understated, whichever is greater) and to cumulative administrative penalties. The SARS understatement penalty under Section 222/223 for 'tax evasion' is 150% of the shortfall plus a further 50% attributable to the intentional element.

Director personal liability: A director who actively participates in, ratifies, or conceals the fraud may be held jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax under Section 248 of the Tax Administration Act, read with Section 77 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008. The South African Revenue Service may obtain civil judgment, attach personal assets, obtain a court-ordered travel ban on the director, and the director faces the same criminal sentence as the perpetrator (up to 10 years' imprisonment).
Shell Company / Abusive Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Section 103(1) of the Income Tax Act empowers SARS to disregard the arrangement and tax the substance—leading to an immediate assessment on the South African company. The understatement penalty can be 150% of tax understated. Additional sanctions under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, where the shell arrangement is used to hide beneficial ownership, include an administrative fine of up to ZAR 50 million and, on conviction for money laundering, imprisonment up to 30 years and a fine on the company of up to ZAR 100 million.

Director personal liability: The South African courts apply the corporate-veil piercing remedy in Section 20(9) of the Companies Act. A director who allowed the entity to operate without real substance or knowingly created the shell may be personally liable for all tax debts and the costs of the SARS inquiry; the director may also be banned from serving as a director for up to 10 years and be hit with a restraining order or travel embargo pending criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Incorrect Factoring Gain Attribution

Statutory sanction: Section 31 of the Income Tax Act authorises a primary transfer pricing adjustment equal to the arm's-length difference. A secondary adjustment may be deemed a dividend or loan, with DWT 20% applying. In addition, a 50% 'adjustment-related' penalty is imposed on the amount of understated tax unless the taxpayer proves that 'bona fide reasonable care' was exercised. Late filing of the reportable arrangement under the Tax Administration Act incurs a penalty of ZAR 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior financial executives who ‘knowingly or negligently’ approved the miscalculated transfer prices may be assessed under Section 31 as connected persons jointly and severally for the primary and secondary adjustments. They may also be found personally liable under the Companies Act for reckless trading if the transfer pricing regime forced the company to trade while insolvent. SARS can obtain a prohibition order preventing the director from leaving South Africa while the outstanding tax liability is under audit.
Anti-Money Laundering / Terrorism Financing and Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, a failure to submit a required STR attracts an administrative non-compliance sanction of up to ZAR 10 million per incident; the Act also imposes a criminal fine of up to ZAR 100 million or imprisonment up to 15 years for a first offence. The Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998 criminalises money laundering with a fine of up to ZAR 100 million and imprisonment up to 30 years for natural persons. Assets linked to prohibited conduct are subject to confiscation or civil forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors or compliance executives can be charged personally if they knew or reasonably ought to have known that the factoring book was linked to illicit proceeds; they are automatically disqualified from serving as a director under Section 69(9) of the Companies Act on a conviction. Travel bans are routinely imposed as bail conditions, and personal asset seizures and compensation orders are issued against the director's personal estate.
