Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), eFiling portal at www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity, including a (Pty) Ltd, must obtain an Income Tax Reference Number from SARS via eFiling or a SARS branch. For a South African-resident company, registration is mandatory and completed using forms such as IT77 and supporting documents including certified Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of foreign incorporation, beneficial ownership schedule, and appointment of a public officer. Non-resident entities with a South African permanent establishment must register for income tax under the Tax Administration Act and for VAT under the VAT Act if taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1 million or if the entity supplies electronic services to South African residents. SARS will issue a VAT registration number and income tax reference number, which must be renewed and maintained; failure to register may incur administrative penalties and interest.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no formal 'check-the-box' classification regime. A company incorporated in South Africa is automatically resident and taxable at entity level. Foreign-incorporated entities may elect to be treated as South African residents if their place of effective management is in South Africa. Controlled foreign company elections are available under s.9D for exclusion of high-taxed income. For VAT, a foreign vendor may apply to register as a separate vendor or for group VAT treatment in certain circumstances, and may elect to account for VAT on the invoice or payments basis subject to SARS approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1 million (mandatory for taxable supplies made in South Africa; non-resident electronic services suppliers are required to register if supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 per 12-month period, subject to SARS binding general ruling)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods qualify for 0% VAT if the supplier exports the goods within 90 days of supply and retains proof of export. For services, zero-rating applies where services are supplied to a non-resident person not present in South Africa at the time the services are rendered, provided the services are not supplied under a taxable supply chain to a South African recipient. Offshore securities brokerage services supplied by a (Pty) Ltd to foreign clients may be zero-rated if the brokerage services are directly rendered outside South Africa and the beneficial recipient is a non-resident, supported by contracts, KYC, and payment documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) showing physical export from South Africa
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from an authorised South African bank
  • Proof of non-resident status of the overseas client (e.g., passport copy, certificate of residence, KYC file)
  • Brokerage service agreement and settlement instructions evidencing offshore service delivery

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. VAT invoices must be issued in electronic or paper form containing prescribed particulars under s.20 of the VAT Act. SARS requires electronic filing of VAT returns via eFiling or a SARS-approved API. SARS is implementing e-invoicing standards based on PEPPOL BIS 3.0 but, at present, there is no mandatory real-time pre-clearance or continuous transaction control requirement.

SARS may audit zero-rated export supplies and deny zero-rating if documentary proof is inadequate. Failure to export within 90 days or to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may result in retrospective output VAT at 15% plus interest. Under the Tax Administration Act, understatement penalties range from 10% to 100% depending on the degree of non-disclosure; deliberate tax evasion may carry criminal penalties including imprisonment. The general anti-avoidance rules may be applied to artificial arrangements designed to obtain undue VAT refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is fully convertible for current account flows (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, and branch remittances) processed by Authorised Dealers without SARB reference, subject to documentary substantiation. Capital account items remain controlled on the outward leg: residents require Authorised Dealer or FinSurv approval for outbound investments, foreign loans, and guarantees, whereas non-resident inward equity investment is unrestricted. Profit remittance and dividend payments to non-resident shareholders are permitted once the inward investment has been registered as an Inward Listing/Investment Certificate with an Authorised Dealer, Dividends Tax (20%, subject to treaty relief) has been settled, and SARS tax compliance status is verified where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa has abolished the historical 100% surrender ratio and the requirement to immediately convert export earnings into rand; however, all export proceeds, including fees and commissions earned from non-resident brokerage clients, must be repatriated to the Republic within 180 days from date of invoice/trade completion and must flow through a local Authorised Dealer, either to a ZAR account or to the exporter's designated Foreign Currency Account maintained with that Authorised Dealer. 100% of proceeds must be accounted for on the export/service declaration and, where conversion is made, must be routed at the ruling market rate; no partial surrenders or retention of proceeds offshore are permitted absent prior FinSurv approval.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the delegated authority of the Minister of Finance, with the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933). Authorised Dealers, being the major commercial banks, administer day-to-day exchange control decisions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for operational treasury purposes but legally prohibited as a client-money custodian: offshore digital EMIs (for example Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Currencycloud) are neither licensed deposit-takers nor Authorised Dealers under the SARB Prudential Authority framework, and cannot hold FSCA-mandated segregated client money for a securities broker. Such EMIs may be engaged only as a supplementary disbursement corridor for non-client operational expenses, while all brokerage settlement and client capital flows must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 through a South African Authorised Dealer; moreover, a resident company's maintenance of funds at a foreign EMI constitutes an offshore bank account that requires FinSurv approval under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant non-resident equity injection: the foreign shareholder remits by SWIFT MT103 to the broker's operating account at a South African Authorised Dealer, with the payment narrative 'Share capital subscription — inward direct investment in unlisted private company (ZA_PTY_LTD)'. The Authorised Dealer captures the transaction as an Inward Listing (Form IL / Inward Investment Certificate), applies the prevailing ZAR market rate, and credits the capital to the company's ZAR account; the company must evidence the subscription through a board resolution, an updated Companies Act 71 of 2008 section 56 beneficial-ownership register, issued share certificates, and the investors' source-of-funds declarations before the funds are released for operational use. The Inward Listing number becomes the permanent exchange-control identity quoted on all subsequent dividend and capital repatriations.Outbound brokerage settlements (fees to foreign executing brokers, clearing and custody charges, client pay-outs) move via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COV from the entity's Authorised Dealer account, each disbursement supported by a Form A outward transfer application and an underlying voucher (invoice, contract, client order, or clearing statement). Where a Commercial Letter of Credit is used for trade-linked payments, the issuing Authorised Dealer opens an MT700 documentary credit in favour of the offshore beneficiary; local ZAR disbursements settle in real time through the SARB SAMOS RTGS platform, and every payment is reconciled against the deal-level liquidity pool to demonstrate non-speculative, arms-length trade purpose.Deliverable outright forwards (FX forwards) with a local Authorised Dealer, strictly hedging verified brokerage income or committed payables, with tenors not exceeding 12 months and zero speculative open positions.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) executed to hedge foreign-currency borrowings or long-term receivable exposures, provided the underlying loan/asset has been declared to FinSurv and the hedging nexus is confirmed by the Authorised Dealer.Vanilla European-style currency options used to hedge firm committed transactions, with the option premium and strike reflecting arm's-length commercial parity.Restricted instrument note: Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) referencing the South African rand are NOT permissible for onshore resident entities, as the Exchange Control Regulations prohibit any structure establishing an offshore rand conversion market; only deliverable instruments settled in ZAR through an Authorised Dealer satisfy exchange-control requirements.
