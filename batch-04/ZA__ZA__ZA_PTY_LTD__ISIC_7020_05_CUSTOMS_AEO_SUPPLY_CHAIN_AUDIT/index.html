Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) - a unitary national fiscal administration with portal at SARS eFiling (www.sarsefiling.co.za). Income tax, VAT, customs and excise are centrally administered by SARS; there are no sub-national income-tax divisions in South Africa.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd is first incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and must appoint a resident public officer (Income Tax Act s 246). The company then registers with SARS via eFiling: (1) create a business profile using the CIPC registration certificate and Memorandum of Incorporation; (2) complete the corporate income tax registration wizard (IT77) to obtain a SARS income-tax reference number (TIN); (3) file the VAT 101 application for VAT vendor registration where taxable supplies exceed R 1,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period (or voluntarily from R 50,000), providing FICA/compliance documentation, a South African bank account and a public officer appointment; (4) for customs/AEO supply-chain work, register as an exporter with SARS Customs (customs client code) and, where required, apply for AEO certification under s 64G of the Customs and Excise Act. SARS issues a single income-tax reference number and a separate 10-digit VAT vendor number; VAT registration becomes effective from the date the threshold was exceeded or from a date fixed by the Commissioner. Foreign parent entities do not need a SA TIN merely by reason of shareholding, but the (Pty) Ltd itself holds the full tax registration as the taxable entity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a resident (Pty) Ltd is a separate juridical person taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate; no pass-through to shareholders; distributions attract 20% dividends withholding tax; partnerships and certain trusts, not the (Pty) Ltd, are the pass-through vehicles under South African law.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; classification follows legal form and is mandatory. Available statutory elections include: (i) Headquarter Company election under s 9I for qualifying foreign-owned groups (preferential treatment of certain interest, royalties and foreign dividends); (ii) intra-group transaction roll-over election under s 45; (iii) liquidation distribution relief under s 47; and (iv) the CFC high-tax exemption election under s 9D for foreign subsidiaries. Hybrid mismatch rules (s 8F, 8FA, 23M) apply mandatorily without an elective opt-out. Elections are submitted with the relevant income-tax return and supporting schedules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R 1,000,000 (compulsory) for taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; R 50,000 voluntary registration; suppliers of electronic services to South African residents have a compulsory threshold of R 50,000 per annum. Non-resident vendors carrying on an 'enterprise' in South Africa register at the threshold applicable to their supply category.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports is available under VAT Act s 11(1)(a) (movable goods physically exported) and s 11(2)(l) (services supplied to a non-resident, including management/security supply-chain audit services, where the recipient is outside South Africa and the services are not used or consumed in South Africa). Conditions: (i) the supply is made in the course of an enterprise by the registered exporter; (ii) goods are exported within 90 days of the date of supply or a SARS-approved longer period; (iii) the vendor maintains an export register containing contemporaneous proof of export (SAD500 exit record, transport documents, commercial invoice, proof of foreign-currency payment); (iv) export proceeds are received and repatriated in convertible currency within the prescribed exchange-control period (generally 180 days). AEO certification supports the evidentiary burden and expedites clearance but does not by itself confer the zero-rating right. An export refund of input VAT may be claimed in the relevant VAT 201 period once the supporting export proof is in hand.

  • SARS/SACU customs export declaration (SAD500) with exporter code, exit-port endorsement and movement reference
  • Bill of Lading (sea) / Airway Bill (air) / transit road manifest evidencing physical departure from South Africa
  • Export commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin or preferential-trade documentation where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or authorised-dealer foreign-currency settlement confirmation showing receipt within the 180-day repatriation window
  • AEO supply-chain security audit file (AEO certificate, scan/security records, risk-management controls) where AEO facilitation is relied upon

SARS is rolling out e-invoicing on a phased basis under the SARS E-Invoicing Roadmap using the 4-corner PEPPOL model and Peppol BIS 3.0 data format; the first mandatory phases apply to the largest vendors, with subsequent commencement notices by turnover bracket. Invoice data sets must be accessible by SARS through the eFiling/API channel; there is currently no national real-time pre-clearance or digital stamping regime, and the VAT 201 return remains the filing vehicle. For customs, the mandatory electronic filing is the SAD500 export declaration submitted via SARS Customs, with risk-based selectivity; AEO-certified traders use expedited clearance lanes with electronic data sharing.

SARS customs and desk audits verify the physical exit of goods, the completeness and timing of export proof, the genuineness of the foreign recipient, and the timeous repatriation of foreign currency to South Africa. Any defect or late repatriation reverses the zero-rating: output VAT at 15% is recaptured, interest is levied under TAA s 187, a 10% penalty may apply under VAT Act s 60, and understatement penalties under TAA ss 222-223 can reach 150% of the shortfall for intentional or grossly negligent cases. The Customs and Excise Act imposes separate penalties and potential imprisonment for false export declarations; AEO and exporter codes may be suspended or withdrawn; and retrospective assessments may be raised within 3 years of the original assessment, extended to 5 years where fraud, misrepresentation or material non-disclosure is established. Directors, public officers and responsible employees may face personal liability in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a partly convertible currency regime. Current account payments are broadly liberalised, but cross-border capital movements remain materially controlled under the Exchange Control Regulations (1961). ZAR is not freely convertible on the capital account without specific SARB approval, delegation to an Authorised Dealer, or a regulatory exemption. Profit/dividend remittance requires compliance with SARS income tax clearance, proof of distributable reserves, an audited/compliance certificate, and FinSurv approval or authorised-dealer validation. Trade-related services remittances for audit consultancy are feasible on the current account if supported by invoices and a valid SARS tax clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Currency and Exchanges Manual, all export receipts must be received in South Africa within 180 days from shipment date or within a bank-approved extended period. Export proceeds must be either made available to an Authorised Dealer in convertible currency or credited to a permitted Foreign Currency Account; exporters may retain surplus forex in such accounts rather than being forced to convert at a fixed ratio unless the Authorised Dealer exercises a conversion direction for prescribed classes. No fixed statutory surrender percentage applies to retained foreign-exchange earnings once the 180-day repatriation timeline is met.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Limited for primary operating accounts in ZAR; viable as ancillary payment conduit. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Currencycloud) are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under the South African Banks Act and therefore cannot provide a fully protected domestic banking relationship. They can service cross-border fee collections via authorised remittance/correspondent arrangements, but SARB exchange-control rules require ultimate settlement through an Authorised Dealer in South Africa; use is recommended only as ancillary payment rail, not as treasury backbone.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax (final withholding tax on dividends declared by a resident company) to any shareholder, unless exempt or treaty-reduced.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 12 months of the financial year-end, subject to SARS-published filing season dates (e.g., for February year-ends, 14 January of the following year; for March year-ends, 31 March of the following year). Provisional tax: first payment within first six months of the year, second at year-end, optional third on last business day of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5,000,000 per cross-border connected-party transaction (transaction value threshold triggering the need for contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation). For a Multinational Group Local File / Master File, group consolidated revenue must be at least ZAR 10,000,000,000 for Country-by-Country reporting in South Africa.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have an offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute; however, the Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules, general anti-avoidance rules (sections 80A-L), and transfer-pricing adjustment rules impose an economic-substance condition on cross-border profit allocation. A South African (Pty) Ltd engaged in AEO supply-chain audits must maintain real management, premises, and substantive audit activity to avoid recharacterisation of fees as South African-source income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / filing false returns

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act section 235: fine up to ZAR 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; under section 103, an understatement penalty of 100% of the additional tax plus interest at the prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: A representative taxpayer (director) may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax if the failure to file/pay is due to fraud or negligence (TAA section 159). Criminal conviction can lead to court-imposed travel bans and imprisonment.
Transfer-pricing mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer-pricing adjustment under section 31 of the Income Tax Act, plus an understatement penalty of up to 75% of the shortfall (if 'substantial understatement') and prescribed-rate interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign and knowingly submit a tax return containing insufficient TP documentation can be personally assessed as representatives. The established practice of piercing the veil may be ordered by a court if the company is conducted arbitrarily or recklessly.
Shell company / lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: Application of general anti-avoidance rules (sections 80A-L) denies the tax benefit and permits a 75% understatement penalty; a denial of treaty benefits under the Limitation on Benefits provisions; in severe cases the company is regarded as a conduit with taxable gains in South Africa.

Director personal liability: Under the Companies Act, directors may face personal liability for unpaid tax debts where the company continues to trade despite insolvency and lacks substance. Courts may impose punitive personal restitution orders and travel restrictions pending tax clearance.
AML / FICA non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty under FICA up to ZAR 10,000,000 plus (for money-laundering offences) imprisonment up to 15 years under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to report unusual transactions or who facilitate money laundering through the company's bank account bear direct criminal liability and may be personally fined and imprisoned. Additional disciplinary measures include director disqualification under the Companies Act.
