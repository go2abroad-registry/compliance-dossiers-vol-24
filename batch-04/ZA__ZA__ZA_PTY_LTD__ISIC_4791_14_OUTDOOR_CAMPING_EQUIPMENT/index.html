Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 2 — Appoint a public officer (ordinarily resident in South Africa) within one month of incorporation, per section 68 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, and file registration particulars (IT14 for companies) via eFiling. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in South Africa; a (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed as a separate taxpayer at entity level. Available statutory elections include: (i) small business corporation status under section 12E for companies with turnover of R20 million or less; (ii) headquarter company election under section 9I for qualifying multinational holding companies; (iii) VAT group registration election under section 50 of the VAT Act (single representative vendor); (iv) functional currency election under section 24I for foreign currency reporting; and (v) specific hybrid debt/equity positions governed by sections 8E–8EA (no general hybrid mismatch pass-through election). Controlled foreign company rules under section 9D attribute undistributed CFC income to resident shareholders without any pass-through election for the local company itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: taxable supplies exceeding R1,000,000 in any 12-month period (including the date when the threshold is reached); voluntary registration: taxable supplies of at least R50,000 in the preceding 12 months. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African customers are subject to a separate R1,000,000 registration threshold under the foreign electronic services regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the supplier exports the goods within 90 days of the date of supply (or a longer period allowed by SARS) and retains the prescribed proof of export. For an ISIC 4791 online retailer, zero-rating applies to tangible camping and outdoor equipment sold to non-resident customers and physically removed from South Africa within the 90-day window. Certain services supplied to non-residents qualify under section 11(2)(k) provided the services are not physically rendered in South Africa, are not connected to local immovable property or movable property situated in South Africa, and the non-resident is not a registered VAT vendor. The zero-rating claim depends on a complete export evidence file and fails if the goods do not leave South Africa or the documentation is incomplete.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500/SARS Customs EDI declaration) showing the exporter code, HS tariff classification, value, and quantity, together with the Movement Reference Number (MRW) and cargo clearance confirmation
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier/postal despatch receipt evidencing physical export of the goods from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or authorised-dealer bank advice evidencing receipt of the foreign currency export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export order, or sales contract documenting the supply to the non-resident customer
  • Proof of delivery (carrier tracking and consignee delivery confirmation) and payment-gateway settlement records (Stripe/PayPal reports) corroborating the online retail transaction
  • Non-resident status evidence for the buyer (foreign address, passport extract, or foreign business registration) where services zero-rating is also claimed

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping is currently in force. Vendors must issue a full tax invoice within 21 days of the time of supply (section 20 of the VAT Act), file VAT201 returns electronically via eFiling, and retain digital records. SARS Customs requires electronic submission of SAD500 export declarations through the customs EDI/API channels, with a Movement Reference Number (MRW) issued for cargo clearance. SARS has published a draft e-invoicing framework under its digital tax strategy, anticipating future structured e-invoice reporting, but this is not yet a hard mandate.

SARS subjects export zero-rated claims to refund integrity screening and may hold payments for validation or require the exporter to submit export evidence within 21 days. Where zero-rating is incorrectly claimed, SARS raises an additional VAT assessment and charges interest under section 88 of the VAT Act, and imposes understatement penalties under sections 222–223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 — 10% (standard case), 25% (substantial understatement), 50% (no reasonable grounds or gross negligence), and 100% (intentional tax evasion). Fraudulent export claims may also trigger criminal prosecution (up to two years' imprisonment for false statements to SARS), personal liability of the company's directors, suspension or revocation of the customs code, and retrospective clawback of all wrongly claimed refunds with compounded interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current account transactions (trade in goods, services, interest, and dividends) through an authorised dealer bank without individual FinSurv approval, subject to FICA/KYC verification, SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCSP) validation, and appropriate Balance of Payments (BOP) reporting. Capital account inflows are unrestricted for inward equity subscription and shareholder loans, requiring only bank-level reporting. Capital account outflows and offshore asset holdings require FinSurv approval or use of the Foreign Income, Capital and Asset facility. Profit remittance hurdles for this (Pty) Ltd: dividends are freely remittable once (i) a board dividend declaration is passed, (ii) 20% Dividends Tax (or treaty-reduced rate) is withheld and remitted to SARS, (iii) SARS TCSP is valid, and (iv) the paying authorised dealer files the remittance declaration (AOP2/BOP2) with supporting minutes. No individual FinSurv approval is required for ordinary trade-related current account outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As amended by Exchange Control Circular 01/2021, 100% of export proceeds from internet-retailed outdoor camping equipment must be repatriated to South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment; the bank may grant a 270-day extension upon application where payment is demonstrably delayed by documentary or trade disruptions. Upon repatriation, proceeds may be credited to the (Pty) Ltd's Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with the authorised dealer and retained in foreign currency, or converted to ZAR; no fixed statutory conversion ratio is applied post-repatriation, but FCA balances not used for approved trade purposes within the dealer's instruction cycle (typically 30 days) are converted to ZAR at the corporate spot rate. The exporter must produce Export Commission Invoices (ECIs), customs SAD500 export declarations, and obtain bank-issued Export Control Registration (ECR) numbers at cargo clearance. Non-repatriation or late repatriation constitutes an exchange-control offence under the Currency and Exchanges Act, with penalties applied by the Registrar.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), exercising delegated exchange-control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, Act 9 of 1933, read with Exchange Control Circular 01/2021 (Foreign Income, Capital and Asset regime) and the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Feasibility: Medium (Conditional). Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex are not authorised dealers in South Africa and cannot legally act as the final repository for export proceeds; retaining trade receipts in an offshore EMI without FinSurv approval constitutes unlawful exchange-control evasion. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor routes a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 cover transfer for interbank layering) denominated in USD, EUR, or ZAR to the (Pty) Ltd's ZAR operational account held with the authorised dealer bank. Prior to the wire, the company must (i) pass board and shareholder resolutions allotting new ordinary shares or documenting a shareholder loan, (ii) update the CIPC register accordingly, and (iii) submit to the bank a completed Application for Inward Foreign Investment (AOP2/BOP2) with the supporting legal documents. The bank reports the inflow under the BOP equity-injection category (direct investment shares, code 401) or shareholder loan category (long-term liabilities, code 409) and files the inward foreign investment listing with SARB. No prior FinSurv approval is required for this inward equity or loan injection; however, the full wire amount must reconcile exactly to the allotment minutes, and any excess funds must be returned or re-subscribed with amended documentation.The primary clearing rail for supplier payments is SWIFT MT103 via the authorised dealer, debited in ZAR at the ruling corporate spot rate or settled from the Foreign Currency Account. For customs-cleared merchandise, the importer must present the SAD500 Bill of Entry and supplier invoice, with the payment reported under BOP code 389 (imports of goods); freight and ancillary services use code 350 (services). For larger or credit-supported purchases, irrevocable commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) and documentary collections (MT400) are available through the bank's trade-finance desk. All outbound transfers are blocked if the SARS TCSP is invalid or if supporting import/export documentation is deficient. Dividend remittances to the non-resident shareholder are executed under BOP code 401 (direct investment income) with the dividend declaration, SARS dividends-tax proof, and confirmation that profits arose from operational trading activities.Outright deliverable forward exchange contracts (FX forwards / FECs) with an SA authorised dealer, permitted up to the full value of verified underlying trade and service exposures, rolled monthly to match supplier invoice maturities.Vanilla OTC FX options (USD/ZAR and EUR/ZAR calls or puts) on trade-related notional amounts, subject to the dealer's credit approval and premium paid in ZAR.Deliverable FX swaps (spot versus forward combination) and cross-currency swaps for capital-account hedges where an underlying approved foreign shareholder loan (BOP 409) exists, with FinSurv notification effected through the authorised dealer.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing ZAR are not permissible onshore for South African residents; all hedging must be deliverable with an authorised dealer, and synthetic or offshore ZAR derivative structures are prohibited under exchange-control rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium (Conditional). Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex are not authorised dealers in South Africa and cannot legally act as the final repository for export proceeds; retaining trade receipts in an offshore EMI without FinSurv approval constitutes unlawful exchange-control evasion. Such EMIs may be used strictly as intermediate collection gateways, provided funds are swept to a South African authorised-dealer bank account within the 180-day repatriation window and are traceable to underlying customer transactions. Onshore fintech rails (Stripe, PayPal, Peach Payments) are viable as customer collection channels only when settled into the (Pty) Ltd's domestic ZAR account held with a licensed bank, with full FICA provenance of the e-commerce trade.
