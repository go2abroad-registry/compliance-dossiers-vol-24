Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling online portal (www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

Non-resident company must first apply for a SARS income tax reference number and VAT number via SARS eFiling using a SARS-registered tax practitioner or through the non-resident electronic registration channel. Submit certified Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of registered foreign address, resolution appointing a local public officer, financial statements or certain projections, and evidence of South African business activity. Once VAT registration is approved, the enterprise must file VAT returns on eFiling and maintain a local public officer for SARS correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. South Africa applies entity-level income tax. Relevant elections include: VAT grouping election under Section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991; hybrid mismatch elective treatment under Sections 8K and 8L of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; and CFC high-tax exemption election under Section 9D(9E) where the foreign subsidiary is subject to a minimum effective tax rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration where taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 (R1 million) in a consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration threshold ZAR 50,000. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services cross this compulsory threshold if turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 globally.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Section 11(1)(a) for goods physically exported from South Africa where the supplier obtains documentary proof in the required form. Services are zero-rated under Section 11(2)(l) where they are supplied to a non-resident and the direct benefit occurs outside South Africa; related intellectual property and logistics services may be zero-rated only if specific statutory conditions are met. The supplier must be a registered VAT vendor and must retain evidence before the zero rate is applied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank proof of payment in foreign currency
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Proof of delivery/customs entry at destination

South Africa currently maintains no real-time electronic invoice clearance or digital stamping mandate. A VAT-registered supplier must issue tax invoices satisfying Section 20 of the VAT Act, or simplified invoices under Section 21 for amounts below ZAR 5,000, and submit electronic VAT returns via SARS eFiling. SARS publishes an API framework for accredited software integrators, but no mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) or fiscal data stamping is in force.

SARS audits export zero-rating strictly. If export documentation is incomplete, untimely, or fraudulent, the zero rate is reversed and VAT becomes payable, together with interest calculated from the original due date and an understatement penalty under Section 223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 of up to 150% of the shortfall for intentional misstatement; deliberate evasion may additionally trigger criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, interest, dividends) without prior SARB approval, while capital-account inflows from non-residents are liberalised; outward capital transfers by residents, external borrowing above prescribed thresholds, and certain structured derivative transactions remain subject to FinSurv approval or authorised-dealer reporting. Profit remittance via dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders is freely remittable provided the dividend is declared out of distributable profits under the Companies Act 71 of 2008, is subject to 20% (or treaty-reduced) Dividend Withholding Tax, and is supported by board minutes, audited/reviewed annual financial statements or management accounts, and evidence of non-resident status; no 'blocked rand' or standing prior-approval obstacle applies to a standard DTC e-commerce company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Exporters (2023 revision) and FinSurv enforcement policy, export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 30 days from the date of export (reduced from the former 180-day requirement in the 2021 reform). On receipt, proceeds must either be credited to a domestic ZAR account (triggering full conversion) or be retained without mandatory conversion in an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an authorised dealer; there is no partial surrender ratio in the classic sense, but the practical obligation is 100% repatriation within the 30-day window, with 100% conversion to ZAR if the exporter elects not to maintain an FCA, and continued FCA retention remains subject to authorised-dealer oversight, FinSurv reporting, and the AD's internal credit limits.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, in consultation with National Treasury; implementation is conducted through authorised dealers (commercial banks) appointed by FinSurv.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium - feasible as a supplementary offshore receivable-concentration rail, but not as a replacement for a domestic authorised-dealer bank account. Airwallex, Wise Business and Payoneer are not licensed authorised dealers in South Africa under the Exchange Control Regulations, cannot lawfully settle ZAR current-account obligations, and cannot perform FinSurv reporting. Legal constraints are: (i) a resident (Pty) Ltd must receive export proceeds through a South African authorised dealer for the 30-day repatriation rule; (ii) ZAR payouts flowing through an unlicensed EMI without an underlying AD transaction risk exchange-control circumvention; (iii) the entity may use EMIs to warehouse USD/EUR receipts from Shopify Payouts offshore only if the funds are subsequently remitted to the SA authorised dealer within the statutory timeline and a complete digital documentation trail is retained.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: wire funds via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the company's ZAR corporate account at the South African authorised dealer, stating purpose code 'EQ - Direct investment: equity' (IMF BOP classification / SARB EDP code 10.02) and referencing the company registration number. The AD requires the MOI, board resolution approving the issue, share-subscription agreement, and source-of-funds verification; no prior SARB approval is needed for an inbound equity contribution, but the company must issue the shares and file the CIPC notice of issue under section 40 of the Companies Act within the prescribed period, and the AD will report the inward transfer to FinSurv and IMF BOP statistics. A FICA cash-threshold report is not triggered by wire transfers, but a FIC report may arise on unexplained counterparty anomalies or associated cash deposits.Clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account trade settlements and supplier payments against invoices; (ii) SWIFT MT700 (irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit at sight or usance) for purchase-order or bulk-restock transactions, with shipping documents and bill of entry presented through the AD's trade desk; (iii) SWIFT MT202 COV for interbank settlement of third-party currency payments; (iv) domestic ZAR EFT (BankservAfrica eFT) and PayShap instant payments for local fulfilment and DTC refunds; (v) cross-border acquiring and Shopify payout conversion to ZAR via the AD's auto-conversion or FCA-based FX conversion. Each outbound payment must be coded with the correct EDP trade purpose code (goods import or services) and, where applicable, supported by a SARS bill of entry and import documentation for customs reconciliation under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964.Forward Exchange Contract (FEC) with the authorised dealer - permitted for firm and reasonably anticipated trade-related foreign-currency exposures (export receivables and import payables) up to 12 months' tenor, extendable by the AD with documented business rationale under the classic SARB trade-cover regime.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) - permitted onshore only where the ZAR leg can be documented as a hedge of foreign-currency cash flows and the counterparty is a bank; a FinSurv-approved authorised dealer must handle ZAR settlement, and the entity should evidence the underlying Shopify/PayPal receivable being hedged.Cross-Currency Swap (CCS) - permissible through the authorised dealer for balance-sheet hedging of multi-year external loan or reinvestment exposures (e.g., USD/ZAR or EUR/ZAR), subject to internal AD product approval and, where the tenor exceeds standard trade-cover horizons, to FinSurv approval or AD delegated authority.FX Option (put/call, premium settled in ZAR) - authorised for hedging anticipated trade flows where the AD confirms the premium is economically attributable to South African residency; delta-limited structures (zero-premium collars and participating forwards) are permissible with an approved underlying exposure.Nostro/Vostro account offset and multi-currency netting through the AD's treasury desk - used as an intraday liquidity hedge for multi-currency Shopify payout cycles, provided the arrangement does not constitute unapproved offshore borrowing.
