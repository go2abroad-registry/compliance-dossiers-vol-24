Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; all SA incorporated (Pty) Ltd companies are automatically entity-level taxpayers. Available statutory elections include: (i) Headquarter Company regime (section 9I, Income Tax Act 58 of 1962), elected on SARS eFiling, granting exemptions for foreign dividends, foreign branch income and foreign capital gains where at least 80% of shares are held by non-residents and 80% of income/test assets are foreign; (ii) section 23M/23N interest limitation elections addressing hybrid mismatch outcomes; (iii) section 11D research and development allowance election; (iv) VAT grouping election (section 50, VAT Act 89 of 1991) allowing connected persons to be treated as a single vendor; (v) section 42/45 corporate restructure roll-over elections; (vi) section 24I elective timing rules for foreign exchange gains and losses.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 taxable turnover in any 12-month period (section 23(1) VAT Act); Voluntary registration threshold: ZAR 50,000 per annum (section 23(2) at SARS discretion); a parcel consolidation hub with foreign-source revenue is subject to the same nexus rules and must register for VAT in South Africa if conducting a 'carried on enterprise' in the Republic and exceeding the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: section 11(1)(a) VAT Act zero-rates the supply of movable goods exported by the vendor to a non-resident, provided the goods are physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply (section 11(3)). Services: section 11(2)(d) zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident that are rendered directly in connection with movable property situated in the Republic at the time of supply, where that property is subsequently exported to a place outside South Africa and the SACU area. Section 11(2)(l) zero-rates certain services supplied to non-residents not consumed in South Africa. For a cross-border e-commerce parcel consolidation hub, sorting, consolidation, injection and outbound linehaul services supplied to foreign platform operators can qualify under section 11(2)(d) or section 11(1)(k) (transport of exported goods) provided the parcels physically leave South Africa, the recipient is a non-resident, and the services are not used for a local taxable purpose. The supplier bears the onus of proving the foreign destination and the non-resident status of the recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 / SEA) lodged through the SARS Customs Modernisation system
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or road freight manifest evidencing dispatch from South African port of exit to a foreign destination
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate / Inward Foreign Exchange (IFX) credit advice showing foreign-currency settlement from the non-resident customer
  • Commercial invoice / full tax invoice referencing the zero-rated supply
  • Packing list and consignment weight/measurement certificate for the consolidated parcel batch
  • Executed services agreement with the non-resident e-commerce platform and certificate of residence (or equivalent) evidencing non-resident status

South Africa does not impose a real-time electronic invoice clearance, fiscal stamping, or mandatory API-based government approval regime as of the current framework. SARS relies on periodic eFiling submissions (VAT201 returns) and requires vendors to issue full tax invoices (section 20 VAT Act) within 21 days of supply and retain records for five years. SARS's modernisation roadmap, including a structured digital e-invoicing ecosystem and data-driven audits, is being phased in for large taxpayers, but voluntary EDI/XML e-invoicing remains acceptable evidence for zero-rating; no live government pre-clearance of invoices is currently mandated.

SARS applies risk-based audit profiling to export zero-rating claims; the vendor must retain full documentary proof for at least five years. If the documentation is incomplete, the goods do not leave South Africa within 90 days, or the services are not supplied to a non-resident, SARS will raise an additional assessment adding output VAT at 15% plus underpayment interest at the prescribed rate (repo rate + 7%), and may impose understatement penalties under section 223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (ranging from 10% for standard cases to 25% for failure of reasonable care, 50% for substantial understatement, up to 200% for intentional tax evasion). Fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the entity and directors to criminal liability, fines and potential imprisonment under section 234 of the Tax Administration Act and section 60 of the VAT Act. SARS may assess beyond the default three-year prescription period where fraud, misrepresentation or non-disclosure is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a managed-float exchange control system in which the rand (ZAR) is not freely convertible offshore without an authorised dealer. Current-account transactions, including e-commerce service imports, freight payments, logistics charges, and ordinary operational disbursements, are generally permitted with completion of a BOP purpose code declaration and supporting documents. Capital-account transactions such as inbound equity injections, external shareholder loans, and repatriation of invested capital are permitted but subject to stricter authorised-dealer review and, in certain structures, prior FinSurv approval. Profit remittance and dividend distribution by a South African Pty Ltd to non-resident shareholders is permissible as a current-account remittance once the dividend is validly declared, the resident entity is in good tax standing with SARS, and the applicable South African dividend tax regime has been satisfied. The real convertibility friction lies not in availability of ZAR but in the documentary completeness of the dividend or capital-repatriation instruction and compliance with exchange-control reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a South African exporter operating a cross-border parcel consolidation hub, all export proceeds must be received in foreign currency within 30 days from the date of shipment unless FinSurv has granted a specific extension. Upon receipt, the full gross proceeds, equating to a mandatory 100% conversion ratio, must be surrendered to an authorised dealer in South Africa through sale of the foreign currency for ZAR, unless the exporter holds an approved foreign currency account with the authorised dealer for temporary retention. Retention in an offshore account or the use of unrepatriated export proceeds is not permitted without prior FinSurv approval, and any local foreign currency account must be structured through a South African authorised dealer.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), specifically the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which administers South Africa's exchange control regime under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A South African Local File is mandatory where related-party non-financial transactions exceed ZAR 100 million or aggregate related-party financial transactions exceed ZAR 50 million; a Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in certain offshore hubs, but SARS applies the common-law substance-over-form doctrine, the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rules in sections 80A–80L of the Income Tax Act, and connected-person transfer pricing rules. A parcel consolidation hub with no local personnel, premises, or substantive management functions will be susceptible to transaction re-characterisation and denial of DTA benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil understatement penalty of up to 150% of the unpaid tax plus statutory interest. Criminal conviction may result in a fine and/or imprisonment for a period of up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise evasion may have the corporate veil pierced, exposing them to joint and several liability for the corporate tax debt, civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply GAAR to disregard the transaction, re-attribute income to the beneficial owner, disallow deductions, levy a 150% understatement penalty, and deny South African treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors participating in a sham structure may incur personal liability for the resulting tax shortfall, disqualification, civil damages, and restriction on leaving the jurisdiction pending compliance.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Section 31 transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income, secondary adjustment treated as a deemed dividend or loan, disallowance of excessive expenditure, understatement penalty up to 150%, and interest on the resulting shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve intercompany arrangements lacking commercial rationale may be held personally liable for SARS's adjustments where they acted recklessly or with gross negligence; courts can order personal payment of corporate tax debts.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, administrative penalties can reach ZAR 10 million; criminal liability carries fines and imprisonment of up to 15 years for deliberate non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly tolerate deficient AML practices face personal fines, disqualification orders, travel bans, and potential criminal prosecution for money laundering facilitation.
