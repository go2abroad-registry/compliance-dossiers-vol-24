Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), established under the South African Revenue Service Act 34 of 1997; digital interface is the SARS eFiling portal (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za) and the SARS Conneqt digital engagement platform; customs and excise declarations are lodged through the SAS/SAD customs module.

E-government filing portal

For a locally incorporated (Pty) Ltd: 1) Incorporate with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain the issued company registration number; 2) On SARS eFiling, complete RAV01 (Registration, Amendments, and Verification) and attach the CIPC incorporation certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, certified copies of directors'/beneficial owners' identity documents, proof of registered and operational physical address, and an active corporate bank account record; 3) Select tax types: Income Tax, VAT (if the ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory threshold is exceeded or the voluntary ZAR 50,000 election is made), and PAYE/UIF/SDL if staff are engaged; 4) SARS validates KYC and documentation, then issues the 10-digit Income Tax Reference Number (TIN) and, where applicable, the 10-digit VAT registration number in the same electronic workflow; 5) A Letter of Registration with the taxpayer profile and filing frequencies is issued through eFiling. For an external (foreign-incorporated) company conducting business in South Africa, the same RAV01 procedure applies with the CIPC external company registration. No notarial or in-person attendance is required; ordinary processing is completed within 5-10 business days and is free of charge.

No notarial or in-person attendance is required; ordinary processing is completed within 5-10 business days and is free of charge.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box elections for locally incorporated companies; however, the following statutory elections and special regimes are available to a (Pty) Ltd: (i) Section 11E Headquarter Company election requiring at least 80% shareholding in foreign subsidiaries and a 50% foreign-source turnover ratio, granting exemptions for foreign dividends, interest, royalties, and certain service fees; (ii) Section 25B(3A) foreign hybrid entity election permitting the South African owner to align the domestic classification of an offshore hybrid with its foreign tax classification; (iii) Section 50 group VAT registration election allowing wholly-owned group companies to file as a single VAT vendor; (iv) voluntary VAT registration election where annual taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 but are below the ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory threshold; (v) Section 9D(2A) CFC high-tax exemption election where the controlled foreign company is subject to tax at 75% or more of the South African headline corporate rate (currently 27%); and (vi) small business corporation assessed-loss elections. Every election is filed using the prescribed eFiling workflow and remains subject to SARS verification and anti-avoidance review.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 annual taxable supply threshold for compulsory registration (VAT Act Section 23(1)); voluntary registration available where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period; foreign electronic-service suppliers must register once South African-sourced electronic supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 per annum

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: export zero-rating under Section 11(1)(a) requires physical removal of the goods from South Africa to a non-SACU destination within 90 days of the time of supply, supported by a cleared SAD500 customs export declaration and transport documents. Services: Section 11(2)(l) zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not in South Africa at the time the services are rendered, provided the services are not physically connected to land or movable property situated in South Africa. For outsourced virtual CFO advisory and multicurrency bookkeeping services delivered remotely to foreign clients, the evidentiary criteria are: (a) the client's non-resident status evidenced by a foreign tax residency certificate, board minutes, or foreign business registration; (b) the client's absence from South Africa during the service period; (c) the services are not electronic services deemed supplied in South Africa under Section 47; and (d) the supplier retains the signed engagement letter, periodic service reports, and payment documentation demonstrating the offshore place of supply and consumption. The supplier must declare the zero-rated amount in the VAT 201 return (field 4) and apply for the refund of input VAT in the same submission if input exceeds output.

  • Customs Export Declaration – SAD500 (electronic SAS/SAD clearing for goods; not applicable to services)
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or road export consignment note bearing the border-post exit stamp or electronic clearance reference
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or foreign-currency bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Signed export/service agreement or sales contract with the non-resident recipient
  • Proof of non-resident status of the recipient (tax residency certificate, foreign business register extract, or passport copy)
  • Deliverable evidence for services (CFO reports, ledger export files, time records, and board minutes) proving the service was rendered and consumed wholly offshore

Current obligations: every VAT vendor must issue a Section 20-compliant tax invoice within 21 days of supply, retain the invoice for five years, and file VAT 201 returns electronically through eFiling or the SARS Integration API. SARS operates automated real-time data matching of bank transactions, customs SAD500 clearances, third-party payment rails (Stripe/Wise), and recipient tax profiles to verify zero-rated claims. The SARS E-Invoicing Modernisation Programme introduces a phased mandatory e-invoicing framework: voluntary pilot in 2024, phased mandatory machine-readable e-invoicing for large VAT vendors from 2026, and expected near-universal digital reporting by 2028. No real-time fiscal stamping or device-based clearance is yet required for virtual CFO service providers; however, each e-invoice must contain the vendor's VAT number, the recipient's VAT/ID number where VATable, a sequential number, date, description, consideration, VAT rate, and an explicit 'ZERO-RATED – Section 11(2)(l)' indicator, with an immutable timestamp and hash recommended for audit-trail integrity.

SARS audit teams and the automated risk engine cross-match the export declaration, the bank's Inward Remittance Certificate, the online payment rails, and the VAT 201 zero-rating field before releasing refunds. A zero-rating claim that cannot be substantiated is reversed as additional output VAT with interest charged at the prescribed rate (repo rate plus 3.5 percentage points). Fraudulent or grossly negligent claims trigger the Section 222 understatement penalty regime of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011: 25% for a substantial understatement from lack of reasonable care, 50% for gross negligence, and 150% for intentional evasion, plus an administrative non-compliance penalty of 10% (up to 100% of the understated tax) and criminal liability under VAT Act Section 60 and Tax Administration Act Chapter 16, with imprisonment for up to five years. SARS applies a retrospective audit cycle of three to five years, and refunds may be withheld for the full verification period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a dual-account convertibility regime. Current account transactions are generally freely convertible through Authorised Dealers, subject to documentary verification. Capital account transactions remain subject to Exchange Control approval, filing, and monitoring. Profit remittance by a Private Company (Pty) Ltd to non-resident shareholders is permitted where the distribution is made out of after-tax profits, no unreported capital or loan transactions exist, and the company is in good standing with the SARB. Dividend remittances may be processed via an Authorised Dealer without prior FinSurv approval if the required dividend declaration and compliance undertakings are in place; however, encumbered capital, historical trade finance mismatches, or unreported inward transfers will trigger suspension and require formal FinSurv remedial filing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into ZAR; export receipts may be retained in a foreign currency account held with an Authorised Dealer. Full reconciliation, supported by shipping documents, invoices, and bank statements, must be available to the Authorised Dealer. All export proceeds must be credited to a South African Authorised Dealer account unless a specific Exemption or Approval has been granted by FinSurv.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) acting under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, as administered through Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Conditional – Medium Low as primary banking channel; acceptable as an ancillary payment corridor. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise are not Authorised Dealers and cannot perform exchange control functions or issue SARB-regulated clearing documentation. South African corporate entities may use such platforms for low-value, non-core disbursements and inbound settlement, but all capital account transactions, large trade payments, and SARB reporting must be routed through a South African Authorised Dealer. Resident companies are also restricted from holding large offshore treasury balances without FinSurv approval; therefore, a foreign EMI cannot act as the principal treasury spine.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold for transfer pricing documentation; SARS practice triggers contemporaneous Local File / Master File preparation for cross-border connected-party transactions exceeding ZAR 5,000,000. Mandatory Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of at least ZAR 10,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE or Bermuda; however, the Income Tax Act adopts substance over form, the place of effective management test, and the general anti-avoidance rules in sections 80A-80L. A (Pty) Ltd must demonstrate real economic substance via offices, personnel, decision-making, and operational independence to maintain tax residence, treaty access, and transfer pricing positions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Understatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% under section 222 of the Tax Administration Act; criminal conviction under section 235; imprisonment for a period of up to five years; prescribed-rate interest under section 187; SARS may reopen closed assessments without time limits in cases of intentional non-disclosure under section 99.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused or induced the filing of a false return are personally liable for the company's tax debt under sections 181-183 of the Tax Administration Act; travel bans and exit embargoes under sections 194/195; criminal conviction and director disqualification; civil restitution ordered by the court.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance rules under sections 80A-80L of the Income Tax Act allow the Commissioner to disregard the arrangement, recharacterize it, and assess the underlying individuals; 150% understatement penalty; criminal prosecution for fraudulent trading under the Companies Act and common law fraud.

Director personal liability: Nominee or shadow directors can be jointly and severally liable for all company debts under section 22/77 of the Companies Act; personal assessments on the basis of substance over form; travel bans; director delinquency orders under section 162; seizure of personal assets to satisfy SARS claims.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts taxable income to an arm's-length amount under section 31 of the Income Tax Act; secondary adjustment creates a deemed dividend and triggers dividends tax; interest and understatement penalties of 20%, 50%, 100%, or 150%; failure to file CbC reporting can attract administrative penalties under section 213/214.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign returns or approve transactions that are not arm's-length are personally liable for resultant tax adjustments under the Tax Administration Act; SARS may issue a certificate of debt which is collected by execution against movable assets; travel bans and imprisonment where the pricing conduct amounts to fraud; personal civil liability for distributions made from overstated profits.
AML / FIC Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Centre Act section 58 administrative penalty up to ZAR 50,000,000 against an accountable institution; criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 15 years for money laundering under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act; regulatory action including the debarment of the company and prohibition from providing financial services.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for failing to implement FIC compliance programmes; subject to fines, imprisonment, travel bans and directorship disqualification under the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services Act; civil claims by clients and counterparties for losses caused by non-compliance.
Profit Repatriation / Withholding Tax Non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The (Pty) Ltd is personally liable for the unremitted withholding taxes under sections 50D(2), 51A and 64G; additional penalties under section 213 and interest under section 187; understatement penalty of up to 100%; criminal prosecution for failure to withhold and pay over tax; SARS may register the debt as a secured claim and appoint garnishee agents.

Director personal liability: Directors as responsible persons are jointly and severally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under section 181(1) of the Tax Administration Act; tax debts survive corporate liquidation and may be recovered from directors' personal estates; travel bans and criminal sanctions are enforceable until the amount is paid.
