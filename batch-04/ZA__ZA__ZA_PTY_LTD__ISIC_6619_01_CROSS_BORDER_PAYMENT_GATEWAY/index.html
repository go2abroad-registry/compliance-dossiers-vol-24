Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), 77 Meintjies Street, Sunnyside, Pretoria, 0002; all filings are transmitted electronically through the CIPC e-Services portal.

Governing statute

Companies Act 71 of 2008, as amended by the Companies Amendment Acts 16 and 17 of 2024, read with the Companies Regulations 2011; sections 12, 13, 14, 15, 19, 23, 66, 70 and 90 are the operative provisions for incorporation, legal personality, registered office, board authority, first resolutions and audit appointment.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notice of Incorporation (Form CoR 14.1) together with the Memorandum of Incorporation (Form CoR 14.1A standard or CoR 14.1B supplementary); the CIPC issues the Certificate of Incorporation (Form CoR 14.3) upon successful registration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

South African law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a private company. Shares are issued as no-par value shares and consideration may be nominal, subject to the section 4 solvency and liquidity test. No escrow account or capital deposit is statutorily mandated for incorporation. However, the South African Reserve Bank payment system designation process may impose prudential capital requirements at the licensing stage. Share subscription proceeds must be held in a FICA-verified corporate bank account opened immediately after incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety and Occupation Certificate issued by the municipal Fire and Rescue Service under the National Building Regulations and SANS 10400 Part T; electrical Installation Certificate of Compliance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993; structural load approval for server rooms under SANS 10400 Parts A and B; occupational health and safety workplace clearance for the open-plan IT environment; letter of good standing under the Compensation for Occupational Injuries and Diseases Act (COIDA); emergency evacuation and business-continuity plan filed with the municipal disaster management centre.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – National portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African company must register as a taxpayer with SARS via eFiling or at a SARS branch. The process includes completing the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) registration, then SARS registration for Income Tax (ITREF number) and VAT (VAT vendor number). Required documents include the company registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, directors' identity documents, and proof of registered office address. For non-resident directors, additional supporting evidence and a local representative taxpayer are typically mandated.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (South African resident company subject to corporate income tax; foreign-controlled entities may be subject to controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution under s9D of the Income Tax Act); Territorial scope limited to source-based and residence-based rules (worldwide income for SA residents, SA source income for non-residents). — A foreign-owned South African company must register as a taxpayer with SARS via eFiling or at a SARS branch. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (South African resident company subject to corporate income tax; foreign-controlled entities may be subject to controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution under s9D of the Income Tax Act); Territorial scope limited to source-based and residence-based rules (worldwide income for SA residents, SA source income for non-residents).

Available election forms

No general 'check-the-box' regime; entity classification follows statutory legal form (Pty Ltd is inherently taxed as a company). Hybrid mismatch elections are limited to specific anti-avoidance rules under s8B–s8I of the Income Tax Act (OECD Hybrids rules) and require formal disclosure and IRS/SARS alignment, but are not 'elections' in the US sense. Foreign tax credit elections and group relief elections (s45, s41) may apply to qualifying MNE groups.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration threshold is ZAR 50,000 for commercial entities. A non-resident supplying electronic services to SA recipients may be required to register irrespective of threshold if supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 per annum (VAT Act s50A).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of movable goods supplied to non-residents and conventionally exported from South Africa within 28 days of supply (VAT Act s11(1)(a)). Exported services qualify if the services are supplied to a non-resident and are not consumed in South Africa (s11(2) read with s11(3)). Cross-border payment gateway services are, however, typically exempt financial services under s12(a) unless specifically made zero-rated by the Minister, so PSPs must carefully document the nature of each fee stream and require supporting evidence from the foreign counterparty.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / SAD501) as per Customs and Excise Act
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation showing foreign settlement
  • Commercial invoice and contract showing supply to a non-resident
  • Proof of export within 28 days (port shipping schedule, customs clearance notice)
  • Proof of service consumption outside South Africa for offshore services

SARS does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or a government-API e-invoicing stamp. However, all VAT returns must be submitted electronically via SARS eFiling or an integrated API. SARS has formally announced a phased mandatory e-invoicing framework (per SARS Strategic Plan 2025/26), but no in-force digital stamping requirement exists as of the current tax year. VAT invoices must reflect SARS-prescribed fields and be retained in original electronic form.

SARS will disallow zero-rating if the exporter has not received the exported goods' proceeds within 180 days (unless extended by the South African Reserve Bank under exchange control). Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating triggers a VAT assessment at 15% plus interest under s89(2) of the VAT Act and the Tax Administration Act; understatement penalties may reach 150% of the additional tax in cases of 'substantial understatement' or intentional tax evasion (TAA s223). Criminal prosecutions for fraud carry fines up to ZAR 30,000 per offence or imprisonment up to 24 months, and directors may be held personally liable under CIPC/SARS joint enforcement. Retrospective clawbacks may be raised for the preceding 5 years (or 10 years for intentional non-disclosure).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a partially convertible capital account with a liberalised current account. The rand (ZAR) is freely usable for bona-fide trade, services, interest, royalties and profit remittances where the underlying transaction is properly documented and effected through an Authorised Dealer. Inward non-resident capital flows (equity and qualifying shareholder loans) are generally permitted without prior FinSurv approval, but the inward remittance must be reported to the Authorised Dealer and recorded in the bank's Exchange Control Management system. Dividends and profits may be remitted to non-residents without separate exchange-control approval provided the remittance is funded from audited after-tax profits, withholding/dividends tax has been paid or declared, and the initial capital import was endorsed. The practical profit-remittance hurdle is evidencing source of funds, proving the after-tax profit calculation, and ensuring that the capital was originally admitted through the formal banking channel with a Certificate of Capital Import obtained at that time.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 3 and FinSurv rulings, exporters of goods must receive full payment in a freely convertible currency within 180 days from date of shipment. Payment must be received through an Authorised Dealer in South Africa and must be surrendered, meaning sold into ZAR or credited to an SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account held with that Authorised Dealer. The mandatory surrender/conversion ratio is 100% of the export proceeds; no residual offshore retention, netting, or set-off is permitted unless specifically approved by FinSurv. Extensions beyond 180 days can only be granted through the Authorised Dealer within its delegated discretion or by FinSurv for exceptional structured trade and project-finance receivable tenors. For a cross-border payment gateway not exporting physical goods, the equivalent compliance obligation is that all merchant settlement and cross-border payment revenues must be repatriated and cleared through an Authorised Dealer, with the same 180-day documentary discipline applied to underlying trade transactions.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933; the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; and the SARB Exchange Control Manual, as delegated by the Minister of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, with strict legal constraints. South Africa does not yet provide a domestic 'Electronic Money Institution' licence equivalent to the EU/UK EMI regime. Non-bank payment service providers require registration under the National Payment System Act and are not Authorised Dealers under exchange control. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or similar digital banks cannot operate independently as South African clearing and settlement members, and cannot open ZAR nostro accounts with local clearing participation. Any merchant payout or cross-border transfer that bypasses a registered Authorised Dealer exposes the entity to exchange-control prosecutions. The legally viable fintech structure is to contract with a licensed South African PSP/PISP or Banking-as-a-Service provider that has a sponsorship agreement with a Tier-1 bank; the bank remains the settlement sponsor and exchange-control reporting agent.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Business classification as a Money or Value Transfer Service (MVTS) / Cross-Border Payment Service Provider, which is FATF-recognised as a high-risk money-laundering sector.

Defense evidence: Provide FIC registration or required PSP/FSP licence, the entity's complete AML/CTF policy, independent AML audit or certification, sanctions and PEP screening software details, employee training register, and a risk-rating methodology demonstrating tiered merchant and corridor risk.
Complex or opaque beneficial ownership, especially non-resident shareholders, trust-based structures, or multi-tier holding chains.

Root cause: Complex or opaque beneficial ownership, especially non-resident shareholders, trust-based structures, or multi-tier holding chains.

Defense evidence: Submit the certified beneficial-ownership register required under the Companies Act 2008; notarised passport copies and proof of residential address for every natural person UBO; sworn source-of-wealth and source-of-funds affidavits; bank references from the UBOs' primary banks; and a South African legal opinion confirming that no nominee, shell, or concealed ownership arrangement exists.
High expected third-party payout velocity to unrelated merchant beneficiaries, creating potential layering and trade-money-laundering red flags.

Root cause: High expected third-party payout velocity to unrelated merchant beneficiaries, creating potential layering and trade-money-laundering red flags.

Defense evidence: Deliver executed merchant agreements, transaction flow diagrams, payout and refund procedures, merchant underwriting and rejection criteria, daily throughput limits by risk tier, and evidence of an automated transaction monitoring system with alerts and escalation workflow; commit to ISO 20022 enhanced data carriage and full audit levelling.
Planned cross-border payment corridors into FATF grey-listed or higher-risk jurisdictions.

Root cause: Planned cross-border payment corridors into FATF grey-listed or higher-risk jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Provide a documented jurisdiction risk matrix, automated sanctions screening against UN, EU, UK, OFAC and South African domestic lists, prohibition of unlicensed counterparties, board-level approval requirements for high-risk corridors, Enhanced Due Diligence files on all merchants in those corridors, and a policy of regular financial intelligence reporting to the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All non-resident inbound capital injections for the South African (Pty) Ltd must be routed exclusively through an Authorised Dealer bank via standard SWIFT MT103 wire transfer and cannot be executed through cryptocurrency, foreign exchange bureaux, or offshore EMI accounts. The protocol is: (1) execute signed board resolutions, share subscription agreements, or loan agreements reflecting the exact funding amount; (2) instruct the sending bank to remit to the entity's ZAR domestic account held at the approved Authorised Dealer, using an exchange-control purpose code for 'Inward Equity Subscription' or 'Foreign Shareholder Loan'; (3) provide the receiving bank with the entity's registered name, Companies Act registration number, SARS tax reference, and the source-of-funds evidence from the sending bank; (4) the Authorised Dealer will validate the underlying documentation, confirm beneficial ownership, perform FICA due diligence, and issue a Certificate of Capital Import or non-resident capital endorsement in the bank's Exchange Control Management system; and (5) retain this certificate permanently to support future dividend repatriation, interest payments, and permitted capital reduction remittances. For shareholder loans, transfer pricing and thin capitalisation documentation under Section 23N of the Income Tax Act must also be prepared.Trade settlement and merchant payouts must be executed through the Authorised Dealer's clearing infrastructure. The primary rails are: SWIFT MT103 for individual cross-border beneficiary payments with full payment details and SARB-inspectionable purpose codes; SWIFT MT202 cover transfers for nostro funding where the customer instruction is to be credited through the bank's own correspondent account; and commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP600) where the payment gateway supports underlying goods trade against title documents, although an LC is not the ordinary clearing rail for pure online merchant acquiring. Domestic ZAR liquidity and instantaneous merchant payouts should be handled through the South African Multiplexed Overlay System (SAMOS) for large-value critical payments, the Automated Clearing Bureau (ACB) for low-value batch payments, and PayShap for immediate payment services.
