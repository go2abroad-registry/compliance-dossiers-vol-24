Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – SARS eFiling portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first be registered as an external company or, for a South African subsidiary, incorporated through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Thereafter, the entity must register for income tax and, if applicable, for Value-Added Tax (VAT) with SARS using the prescribed registration forms via SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch. A SARS income tax reference number is issued on registration. VAT registration is compulsory if taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 per annum; voluntary VAT registration is available above ZAR 50,000 annual taxable turnover. Foreign suppliers of electronic services must register for VAT if the total value of taxable supplies from electronic services exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 per annum.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South African tax law does not provide a check-the-box entity classification regime. A (Pty) Ltd is automatically taxed as a separate entity under Income Tax Act No. 58 of 1962. No pass-through election is available. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act and the Interest and Royalty Withholding Tax Act apply to cross-border hybrid arrangements; certain controlled foreign company (CFC) rules under section 9D of the Income Tax Act require allocation of passive income to South African shareholders. No elective reclassification mechanism is available for a proprietary limited company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 11 of the VAT Act, subject to documentary proof and foreign currency payment receipt. Goods exported from South Africa are zero-rated if the exporter retains proof of export and the supplier must be a resident in South Africa or the goods must be supplied to a non-resident. Services are zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident and the services are physically rendered outside South Africa, or if the recipient is a non-resident and the services are consumed outside South Africa (excluding supplies connected with movable or immovable property in South Africa). Cross-border money transmission services may qualify as exported services only where the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not used or consumed in South Africa, but pure financial services are otherwise exempt rather than zero-rated if they fall within the definition of financial services under section 2 of the VAT Act. A remittance provider must specifically confirm that its service is not an exempt financial service or, if exempt, must determine whether section 11(2)(l) zero-rating applies to non-resident financial services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

South Africa requires VAT tax invoices to contain prescribed particulars; electronic tax invoices are permitted and must be retained in an accurate and legible form. SARS does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) approval, or digital stamping for electronic invoices. SARS is progressively implementing an e-invoicing framework aligned with PEPPOL, and large taxpayers may be required to report via specific electronic data interchange or API integration for VAT verification purposes, but no full real-time clearance mandate is in force as of the current tax period.

SARS may disallow the zero-rating applied on exports or exported services and assess VAT output tax at 15% plus interest from the date the tax became payable. Additional tax of up to 100% or 200% of the assessed VAT may be imposed under the Tax Administration Act for fraud, intentional tax evasion, or failure to keep adequate documentary proof. False export zero-rating claims may trigger criminal prosecution, penalties up to ZAR 1.5 million, and imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks are permitted where SARS proves that the export has not actually occurred or foreign payment was not received within the prescribed exchange-control period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, royalties and management fees), which require no prior SARB approval and are processed through Authorised Dealers with accurate Balance-of-Payments (BOP) purpose codes. Capital-account transactions (inward equity investment into the (Pty) Ltd, foreign shareholder loan finance, offshore guarantees, and outward investments by the company) are subject to exchange control administered by FinSurv under the Currency and Exchanges Manual (CM) and must be declared, approved or lodged through the Authorised Dealer channel. Profit remittance hurdles are operational rather than prohibitive: distributable reserves must be evidenced by reviewed or audited annual financial statements, the statutory solvency-and-liquidity test in s. 46 of the Companies Act, 2008 (Act 71 of 2008) must be satisfied, a formal dividend resolution is required, the remitting bank must hold a current SARS Tax Clearance Certificate, and the outflow must be reported with the correct foreign-investment declaration before release.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CM Chapter 5 (Trade and Export Proceeds) read with the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, a resident exporter of goods must ensure that export proceeds are received in South Africa within 90 days after shipment; proceeds received in a foreign currency account must be sold (surrendered) to an Authorised Dealer for ZAR within 30 days after receipt, unless the SARB has granted an exemption permitting retention in an approved Foreign Currency Account (CM 5.16). For a service exporter such as a cross-border remittance agency, the same surrender discipline applies to foreign-currency fee income and any foreign-currency settlement float: receipts must be credited to the Authorised Dealer within the prescribed period and converted to ZAR at the ruling market rate, with full BOP reporting through the dealer. No optional percentage-based retention ratio is available to a non-specialised resident; full surrender is the default statutory rule unless the SARB has granted a sector-specific Foreign Currency Account facility.

Central bank supervision: The Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) of the South African Reserve Bank (SARB), operating under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers, with the National Treasury of the Republic of South Africa as the ultimate exchange-control policy authority.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary de minimis; a ZA (Pty) Ltd must maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for every 'affected transaction' with a connected non-resident under s.31 ITA. SARS applies OECD BEPS Action 13 principles administratively: Local File/Master File/Country-by-Country Report expectations. CbC notification/reporting is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds ZAR 10 billion. In practice, SARS expects a full s.31 analysis where annual connected-party flows exceed ZAR 5 million, but the arm's-length duty exists regardless of quantum.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is enforced through the OECD-aligned s.31 arm's-length rule, the general anti-avoidance rule (s.80A–80L ITA), reportable-arrangement disclosure (s.80T–80U ITA), and sectoral regulators. For a remittance (Pty) Ltd, real substance is proven by: SARB exchange-control approval to operate cross-border payment rails (authorised dealer with limited authority); FIC registration as an accountable institution under FICA Schedule 1; an appointed Compliance Officer, RMCP, and AML transaction monitoring; South African offices, board, staff and treasury decisions. Tax avoidance arrangements lacking bona fide commercial substance (e.g., dividend-stripping through a shell intermediary to avoid dividends tax or WHT) are set aside, and the 75%–150% understatement penalty table is applied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False ITR14

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under s.235 TAA: fine of up to three times the evaded tax and/or imprisonment up to five years; civil reassessment plus interest at the prescribed rate; understatement penalty of 150% for intentional tax evasion (s.223 TAA Tier 8).

Director personal liability: Directors qualify as 'representative taxpayers' under s.167 TAA: SARS may hold a director personally liable for taxes the company withheld or evaded and may impose a departure prohibition order (travel ban) under s.163–165 TAA until liabilities are settled.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Shell Invoicing

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the taxable income to arm's-length amounts, recharacterizes the transaction, denies the deductions and levies understatement penalties: 50% (standard), 75% (no reasonable grounds) or 100%–150% (gross negligence or intentional avoidance) under s.222–223 TAA; undisclosed reportable arrangements attract additional penalties under s.80T ITA; interest runs from the original payment date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or sign such intercompany invoices face personal recovery under s.167 TAA as representative taxpayers and under Companies Act 71 of 2008 s.77 for knowingly causing the company to enter into unlawful/avoidance arrangements; travel bans under TAA s.163–165; director delinquency orders under s.162.
AML / FICA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FIC administrative sanctions up to ZAR 10 million per contravention (s.91 FICA); criminal conviction: fine up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment up to 10 years (s.68–71 FICA); SARB may suspend the exchange-control approval and Banks Act s.11 penalties (up to ZAR 10 million and 10 years' imprisonment) apply for unlicensed money transmission.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and senior managers are criminally liable where the contravention occurred with their knowledge or in circumstances where they failed to exercise reasonable care; personal fines and imprisonment apply in addition to the corporate penalty; professional disqualification and travel bans may be imposed.
Unlawful Dividend / Unfunded Repatriation

Statutory sanction: SARS assesses the company for the full 20% dividends tax not withheld, plus interest and understatement penalties of up to 150%; the company also owes s.64K interest; SARS may recharacterize 'quasi-dividends' (s.31(3) ITA) where the dividend is paid by way of loan or property transfer.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under s.77(3)(e) Companies Act for the amount of any dividend declared in breach of the solvency and liquidity test; they are also personally liable to SARS where they actively procured under-withholding; asset freezes and emigration/exit charge enforcement under exchange-control rules may apply.
