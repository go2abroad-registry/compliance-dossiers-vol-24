Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. Companies are taxable per se as residents or non-resident entities. Specific statutory elections exist for intra-group transactions, controlled foreign company inclusions, and hybrid mismatch adjustments, all governed by the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 and the Taxation Laws Amendment Acts.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: cumulative taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months. Voluntary registration: cumulative supplies of ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods qualify for zero-rating under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act if the supplier has exported the goods within the prescribed period and holds evidence of export. Export of services qualifies under section 11(2)(l) where the services are supplied to a non-resident and the services are physically performed outside South Africa or are directly related to exported goods, provided the non-resident is not a South African resident and the services are not consumed in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Proof of Export (terminal receipt / port clearance)
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign currency deposit evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. Tax invoices must satisfy section 20 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 and be issued electronically or on paper. SARS mandates electronic filing and digital record submission through eFiling, with progressive data-driven compliance monitoring.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract retrospective VAT clawback with interest under section 14 of the VAT Act, an additional 200% penalty under section 233 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 for substantial understatements, and criminal prosecution under section 234 of the Tax Administration Act with fines up to ZAR 5 million or imprisonment up to 5 years. SARS applies heightened documentary verification for crowdfunding-platform service exports.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is fully convertible for current account transactions, including brokerage fees, technology subscription fees, and administrative expenses, subject only to an Authorised Dealer declaration with supporting invoices. Capital account inflows for inward foreign direct investment (equity subscription into the Pty Ltd) by non-residents are freely permitted without prior FinSurv approval, but must be registered with an Authorised Dealer to obtain a Foreign Investment (FI) declaration enabling future dividend and disinvestment remittance. Outbound capital account transfers (foreign venture investments, intercompany loans, loan repayments, offshore treasury placements) require Sub-Limit approval per Authorised Dealer or FinSurv approval where the corporate limit is exceeded. Profit and dividend remittance hurdles are materially manageable: a non-resident shareholder may remit dividends upon presentation of the FI declaration, audited or reviewed financial statements confirming distributable reserves, a valid SARS tax clearance certificate for foreign payments (CSD/PIN), and evidence of no adverse sanctions or FATF-linked screening. The platform must also demonstrate that each crowdfunding investor holds beneficial ownership that is not obscured by nominee structures, and that the investment complied with the FSCA equity crowdfunding per-investor limits, if applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: all export proceeds, including service fees and platform commissions earned from non-residents in the SACU corridor, must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment or service completion, unless extended by the Authorised Dealer or FinSurv. Mandatory conversion ratio: none, the historical 100% surrender requirement was abolished in 2010, therefore the conversion ratio is 0%; however, the repatriation obligation is 100% of the invoiced export value, and the proceeds may be credited to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer. Reduced or non-receipt of proceeds must be reported with a non-receipt certificate in terms of the Currency and Exchanges Manual.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), specifically the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1961, with operational delegation to Authorised Dealers (registered commercial banks) and Authorised Dealers in Foreign Exchange with Limited Authority (ADLAs).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for primary settlement custody and Low-Medium as ancillary payout rails. Offshore digital EMIs and cross-border payment platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Currencycloud) are not authorised to take deposits or hold client monies in South Africa under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 and cannot host the ring-fenced crowdfunding escrow. They may be engaged only as technical payment gateways intermediated by a licensed Authorised Dealer, and must comply with FICA due diligence data requirements. South African PSPs registered under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 may process collections/disbursements, but the final settlement of funds in ECF transactions must be in a bank account with a registered bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors subscribe by SWIFT MT103 wire to the Pty Ltd ZAR account (or USD conversion account) at the Authorised Dealer. The compliant protocol is: (i) receive full KYC/beneficial ownership pack on the non-resident investor; (ii) wire reference carries purpose code 5211 Direct Investment - Equity Capital in the ZAR BOP reporting, with narrative 'Share subscription - ordinary shares'; (iii) the Authorised Dealer registers the inward FDI in the SARB Extranet and issues the Foreign Investment (FI) declaration to the company, a precondition for future dividend and disinvestment remittances; (iv) for crowdfunding investors below the FSCA per-investor cap, simplified EDD applies, but each investor must be individually identifiable and reconciled in the escrow ledger; (v) CIPC share allotment under sections 39 and 40 of the Companies Act is completed within statutory timelines; (vi) escrow funds are released to the issuer only after the offer threshold is met, with failed-campaign funds returned via reversing MT103 to the originating investor account. Any investment routed through offshore holding jurisdictions requires an additional economic substance questionnaire to the Authorised Dealer.Operating and technology service payments to non-residents are cleared via SWIFT MT103 through the Authorised Dealer, supported by proforma invoice, service contract, and SARS tax clearance. Commercial trade settlements in the SACU corridor use Letter of Credit (MT700 issuance, confirmation, or negotiation) with Standard Bank or Nedbank trade desks. ECF platform payouts to domestic investee companies are effected through the local ACH/EAC (Electronic Account Confirmation) or ISO 20022 (SAMOS) rails, while any return of capital to non-resident investors uses MT103 with Form A.300 and the FI declaration. For high-value disbursements, banks apply dual-approval mandates and reference the treasury script to avoid exchange control mismatches.Onshore forward exchange contracts (FECs) with the Authorised Dealer for committed trade and fee exposures, typically up to 12 months tenor, under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.Offshore ZAR non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) transacted by non-resident counterparties as hedging overlay; onshore NDFs are not available to residents without FinSurv approval.Cross-currency swaps (USD/ZAR and EUR/ZAR) between the corporate and an Authorised Dealer to hedge loan principal or long-term equity bridge exposure, subject to underlying transaction evidence.Currency options purchased from the Authorised Dealer for protection on anticipated ECF fee receipts; sale or structuring of options by residents requires FinSurv consent.FX swaps for short-term liquidity management of the escrow and operational pools, restricted to genuine commercial flow and subject to daily SARB balance report.Foreign Currency Account (FCA) balances with the Authorised Dealer to retain export proceeds and platform revenues in USD/EUR without mandatory conversion.

The ITR14 must be submitted within 12 months of the financial year-end. Provisional tax payments are generally due at the end of the sixth month of the year of assessment, at the end of the tax year, and six months after the tax year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required for any cross-border controlled (related-party) transactions with an annual aggregate value equal to or exceeding ZAR 100 million; Master File / CbC Report: mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is equal to or exceeds ZAR 10 billion. Lower thresholds apply to designated 'high risk' transactions, e.g., interest-bearing loans where ZAR 5 million triggers a transfer pricing disclosure.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to some offshore jurisdictions. However, Section 31 of the Income Tax Act mandates that transfer pricing adjustments apply to actual economic substance rather than the legal form. SARS will disregard a scheme if the substance demonstrates a paper/conduit arrangement without commercial rationale. In addition, the general anti-avoidance rule in Section 103(1) of the ITA applies to nullify any tax avoidance scheme lacking substance, and the 'GAAR' rules in Sections 80A-80L similarly collapse abusive transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 28/2011, a 'false or misleading return' can result in a criminally imposed fine up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. SARS also imposes an understatement penalty ranging from 10% to 200% of the assessed shortfall plus interest (currently 10.5%). Where a director knowingly participated, joint liability attaches directly to the responsible officer.

Director personal liability: Directors of a solvent company can be held personally liable for unpaid SARS debt under 'representative taxpayer' provisions, travel bans may be sought via a Section 162 order for debts exceeding ZAR 50,000; civil restitution, removal from the board, and criminal prosecution can follow.
Unlicensed Crowdfunding / FSCA Enforcement

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Markets Act 19 of 2012 and FAIS Act, unlicensed activity may attract an administrative penaly up to ZAR 15 million; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment up to 10 years; the FSCA may order compensation to investors and seizure of commissions.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid penalties if they knowingly caused the portal to accept regulated investments. They may be banned from acting as directors for up to 10 years and face civil claims by investors for proceeds received, plus disgorgement of profits.
Shell Arrangement / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS will disregard the shell for tax purposes under Section 80A-80L (GAAR) and recompute taxable income at fair arm's-length levels; this triggers additional 27% tax, interest at 10.5%, and understatement penalties of 100%-200%. A shell also faces CIPC deregistration under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and loss of corporate veil when used to commit fraud.

Director personal liability: Under the Companies Act 71/2008 s77(3), directors may be personally liable for reckless or fraudulent conduct, including use of a shell to misrepresent investor rights. Directors can be interdicted from leaving the Republic, and criminal negligence can lead to imprisonment up to 4 years.
AML / FICA Breach

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2011 imposes administrative sanctions up to ZAR 10 million per contravention. The FIC can also issue a directive, remove the accountable institution from the list, and refer to the NPA for money laundering prosecution under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act (POCA) – offence carries imprisonment up to 15 years for directors acting in good faith without reasonable grounds.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for AML penalties if the corporation fails to implement an AML programme (risk management, training, KYC, record keeping). Courts may pierce the corporate veil, enforcing civil recovery orders and attaching personal assets where the portal was used as a vehicle for money laundering or terror finance.
Abusive Transfer Pricing / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: SARS will adjust 'affected transactions' under Section 31 on an arm's-length basis; interest and penalties of up to 200% apply. Treaties may be denied where the transaction lacks bonafide substance; the DTA beneficial ownership test may also be rejected, resulting in 20% dividends WHT (rather than 5%) and 15% WHT on royalties/interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepared/signed false TP documentation face criminal liability under s235 TAA; civil restitution to the Crown for underpaid tax can be recovered from directors who knowingly participated in the scheme, and travel restrictions may apply during SARS investigations.
