Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sars.gov.za; company income tax reference number issued automatically through integrated CIPC incorporation filing (CoR14.1/CoR14.3) with subsequent VAT and PAYE registration administered via SARS eFiling.

E-government filing portal

For a domestically incorporated (Pty) Ltd, the Income Tax reference number is auto-generated upon Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) registration through the National Companies Register integration with SARS. VAT registration is a separate application: submit VAT 101 with certified Memorandum of Incorporation, CIPC registration certificate, identity documents of directors, and SARS-held bank confirmation; foreign-owned entities must additionally appoint a SARS-recognised representative taxpayer, provide audited financial projections, and SARS may conduct a physical verification of the principal place of business. VAT registration is compulsory within 21 days once taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed R1,000,000 in any 12-month period.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - resident South African company subject to corporate income tax at 27% on worldwide income (Income Tax Act 58 of 1962); no pass-through or transparency election available for (Pty) Ltd; capital gains included at an 80% inclusion rate; dividends distributed attract 20% Dividends Tax at shareholder level; territorial exemption does not apply to resident companies. — For a domestically incorporated (Pty) Ltd, the Income Tax reference number is auto-generated upon Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) registration through the National Companies Register integration with SARS. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - resident South African company subject to corporate income tax at 27% on worldwide income (Income Tax Act 58 of 1962); no pass-through or transparency election available for (Pty) Ltd; capital gains included at an 80% inclusion rate; dividends distributed attract 20% Dividends Tax at shareholder level; territorial exemption does not apply to resident companies.

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box entity classification regime. Available statutory elections relevant to an e-commerce luxury reseller: (i) s 9I headquarters company election subject to thresholds; (ii) VAT cash-basis accounting election (s 21(2) VAT Act, turnover not exceeding R2.5m); (iii) two-monthly VAT return filing election (s 27 VAT Act, turnover below R1.5m); (iv) hybrid mismatch rules (s 23N of Income Tax Act) apply mandatorily without elective opt-out; (v) participation exemption for foreign dividends (s 10B(2)) and CFC rules (s 9D) where offshore purchasing or fulfilment entities are used.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: R1,000,000 annual taxable turnover (actual over any consecutive 12-month period or expected in the next 12 months); voluntary registration available for persons making taxable supplies (the former R50,000 statutory minimum voluntary threshold was removed with effect from 1 May 2023); failure to register exposes the vendor to VAT on the first R1m of turnover plus penalties and interest.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 11(1)(a) VAT Act zero-rates the supply of movable second-hand luxury goods to a non-resident recipient where the goods are removed from South Africa within 90 days from the date of the tax invoice; the seller must retain prescribed documentary proof and receive full payment in South Africa within 12 months of export (VAT 'export' definition in s 1); related export logistics and fulfilment services supplied to non-residents may qualify for zero-rating under s 11(2)(l); for consignment sellers, the notional input tax claim under s 20(2) on second-hand goods acquired from non-vendors (15/115) remains available provided export substantiation is intact.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government clearance API mandate currently applies. SARS eFiling is the mandatory electronic channel for VAT returns. SARS eInvoicing is a voluntary structured electronic invoicing pilot using PEPPOL/UBL with optional digital signatures and SARS-side interoperability; tax invoices must contain the 15 prescribed particulars under s 20 VAT Act; credit notes must be issued within 21 days; online marketplace sellers must be prepared to provide CSV/XML transaction data during SARS data-matching audits.

SARS may retrospectively claw back export zero-rating where the 90-day removal or 12-month payment conditions are not met, issuing an additional assessment with interest from the original due date; understatement penalties of up to 150% of the shortfall apply (s 223 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011); criminal tax evasion convictions carry a fine and/or up to 5 years' imprisonment (s 235 TAA); false export declarations under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 attract confiscation, additional duty up to 100% of the value, and possible trader debarment; where notional input tax on second-hand goods is reversed, the clawback includes the deemed input VAT plus penalty; supporting export records must be retained for at least 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa is a 'Schedule C' exchange control country: the rand (ZAR) is convertible for trade and current-account transactions through authorised dealers without case-by-case SARB approval, subject to supporting documentation. Capital-account transactions require prior approval or reporting under the Currency and Exchanges Manual and Exchange Control Circulars. Profit remittance by non-resident shareholders is permitted upon completion of tax clearance and foreign investment registration; the local company must produce signed or audited financial statements or a dividend resolution, demonstrate that all export proceeds have been correctly surrendered or retained in approved foreign currency accounts, and maintain good SARS tax-compliance status. Shareholder capital may be repatriated after disinvestment reporting through the authorised dealer; shareholder loans must be registered and align with the SARB inward-loan guidelines before any interest or principal remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds, including service fees and consignment sales paid by foreign buyers, must be repatriated to South Africa within 90 days from the date of shipment or invoice unless an extended period is pre-approved by an authorised dealer or SARB FinSurv (normally up to 180 days, renewable). Mandatory conversion ratio: 100% of export proceeds must be sold into ZAR on the market through an authorised dealer, unless the entity holds a valid foreign currency account approval from an authorised dealer allowing retention of working capital. There is no statutory 50/50 split; the default is full conversion into ZAR, with only specifically approved importer/exporter arrangements allowed to retain foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) is the sole exchange control authority, with day-to-day administration delegated to authorised dealer banks (commercial banks) under the Currency and Exchanges Manual and Exchange Control Circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for direct core banking. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury, EPX, Multitude) cannot act as SARB-authorised deposit-taking institutions or clearing banks in South Africa; they may only provide multi-currency collection and processing through a licensed partner bank. Such EMIs are legally viable for ancillary payment facilitation, but the entity must retain a primary ZAR operating account at an authorised dealer bank. Stripe is permissible as a merchant payment gateway for online card payments, and Escrow_Pay can function as a payment intermediary for consignment settlements, but all final clearing and SARB reporting must occur through the onshore commercial banking channel. A dedicated foreign currency account at an authorised dealer bank remains the regulatory-compliant mechanism for cross-border multi-currency liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity capital must be wired by SWIFT MT103 from the founding shareholder's foreign bank account to a designated 'Capital Escrow Account' at an authorised dealer bank. The wire instruction must state the purpose as 'share subscription – inward foreign investment' and be accompanied by the SARB Form FI (inward investment report), incorporation certificate, English version of the Memorandum of Incorporation, share-allocation resolution, and audited financial statements if the company is already trading. Upon receipt, the authorised dealer issues a Foreign Investment Realisation Certificate or an FI reference number, which is mandatory for future dividend remittance and disinvestment. If the funding is structured as a shareholder loan, a formal loan agreement, SARB inward-loan application, corporates registration form, and the authorised dealer's approval letter must be completed before drawdown. Receiving capital through Stripe, Escrow_Pay, or offshore EMIs is not acceptable for regulatory capital; such channels may only be used for ordinary trade receivables and not for equity or quasi-equity injections.Trade-settlement rails for this business comprise: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers via the authorised dealer bank for direct supplier and refund payments to foreign counterparties; (ii) commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) for high-value vintage stock imports and structured consignments, opened with the authorised dealer and confirmed by a foreign correspondent bank; (iii) domestic ZAR interbank clearing through SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement) for payouts to local consignors and vendors; and (iv) gateway-based disbursements via Stripe and Escrow_Pay for consumer refunds and seller settlements, provided the funds are ultimately cleared into and from the bank's ZAR operating account. Every outward payment must be matched to underlying invoices, customs declarations, refund notifications, or settlement contracts; the authorised dealer will apply exchange-control verification and SARB cross-border payment reporting where applicable. Direct use of offshore EMI accounts as the principal clearing venue is not compliant unless the arrangement is specifically approved and reported through the authorised dealer.FX forward contracts (USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR) – permitted through an authorised dealer bank, must be supported by documented underlying trade or capital exposure and cannot be speculative.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts – executable through a South African authorised dealer as principal in the non-deliverable ZAR offshore market for hedging where physical settlement is not required.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) – permitted for long-term foreign-currency debt or capital commitments, requiring prudent tenor alignment and appropriate SARB/FinSurv notification through the authorised dealer.Zero-cost collar and option spread strategies – permissible when used exclusively for hedging of committed cross-border receipts or payments, with no speculative intent, and executed through the authorised dealer's treasury desk.
