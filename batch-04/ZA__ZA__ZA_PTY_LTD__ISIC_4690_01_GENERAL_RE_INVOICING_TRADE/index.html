Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is generally permissible for a private company in South Africa as the Companies Act requires only a registered office address where legal notices can be physically delivered (Regulation 23 read with Section 23 of Companies Act 71 of 2008). However, for an offshore re-invoicing and trading intermediary, the named office should have an appointed person or agent able to receive documents, maintain minute books, and support evidence of commercial substance. Banks and AML auditors will query the substance profile; a pure virtual address without any physical space, staff, or records at the office is high risk for bank account opening and for substance/integration tests under the South African controlled foreign company rules (though re-invoicing hub arrangements may still qualify for tax treaty screening if substance is genuine and local business assets/risks exist).

Competent corporate registry

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital or escrow requirement under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; a (Pty) Ltd may issue shares without par value and allot a single subscriber share; non-resident ownership is permitted without exchange-control pre-approval, subject to SARB balance-of-payments notification and FICA/KYC clearance at the commercial bank.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the municipal fire service under the National Building Regulations (SANS 10400 Part T); structural load inspection certificate for the leased premises; occupational health and safety clearance under OHSA Act 85 of 1993; and Compensation Fund (COIDA) registration and letter of good standing where employees are engaged.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital or escrow requirement under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; a (Pty) Ltd may issue shares without par value and allot a single subscriber share; non-resident ownership is permitted without exchange-control pre-approval, subject to SARB balance-of-payments notification and FICA/KYC clearance at the commercial bank.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — national tax and customs authority; eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; customs clearance via SARS Customs (SAD500/ACE electronic portal); statutory reference: South African Revenue Service Act 34 of 1997; income tax administered under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; VAT under the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991; procedural enforcement under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain a company registration number. Step 2: Register for income tax with SARS through eFiling using Form COR14.2 (Application for Registration of a Company) to obtain a 10-digit income tax reference number; a foreign-owned resident company registers as a 'company' and is taxed on worldwide income. Step 3: Compulsory VAT registration is triggered under Section 23(1) of the VAT Act when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period; the vendor must apply on Form VAT101 within 21 business days of exceeding the threshold; voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000 taxable supplies (Section 23(3)). Step 4: Register for a customs code with SARS Customs using the DA185 application (commercial importer/exporter code) if goods physically enter or leave South Africa; a re-invoicing hub transacting only offshore-to-offshore flows may not require a customs code but must still hold a VAT vendor number if it issues invoices for SA supplies. Step 5: If employees are engaged, register for PAYE, UIF, and Skills Development Levy via EMP101; corporate income tax returns (ITR14) are filed annually, with provisional tax payments (IRP6) due semi-annually.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person subject to normal tax on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (effective for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); shareholders are not taxed on a pass-through basis until dividends are declared (20% dividends tax withheld, subject to treaty relief or the s64FA exemption). Foreign entities not incorporated in South Africa are taxed as non-resident companies only on South African-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person subject to normal tax on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (effective for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); shareholders are not taxed on a pass-through basis until dividends are declared (20% dividends tax withheld, subject to treaty relief or the s64FA exemption). Foreign entities not incorporated in South Africa are taxed as non-resident companies only on South African-source income. There is no statutory partnership-style pass-through classification for (Pty) Ltd and no US-style 'check-the-box' transparency election available; legal form governs tax classification.

Available election forms

Headquarter Company Election (Section 9I Income Tax Act): a (Pty) Ltd meeting the 80% foreign-shareholding test, foreign-employment test, and 50% foreign-source income test may elect s9I status to obtain a 0% withholding tax rate on interest, royalties, and certain service fees paid to foreign residents and exemption from dividends tax on distributions to foreign shareholders. CFC High-Tax Exemption Election (Section 9D(2A)): election to exclude a controlled foreign company from the net income inclusion where the CFC carries on business in a qualifying country and foreign taxes paid equal or exceed 80% of the South African tax that would have been payable; available to resident shareholders in the re-invoicing group. Foreign Business Establishment Exclusion (s9D(9A)): CFC income attributable to a foreign business establishment is excluded; SARS requires contemporaneous substance evidence. CGT Deemed Disposal Election (Section 8(5)): election to deem an asset disposed of at fair market value where an asset is moved offshore or the person ceases residency; available to trigger tax base protection. VAT Vendor Categorisation Election: vendor may elect the 'cash basis' (Section 15(1)(b)) or 'invoice basis' (default) for output and input tax timing; equipment vendors (Category B) may file six-monthly returns if the vendor is a category B vendor with a turnover below ZAR 50,000,000 and approved by SARS. No hybrid-mismatch election is available in South Africa; hybrid arbitrage rules under s9D(9A) and s31 align with BEPS Action 2 outcomes. Note: s23K and s23L thin capitalisation and interest deduction limitations apply without elective alternatives.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration at ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period (Section 23(1)); voluntary registration threshold from ZAR 50,000 (Section 23(3)); the threshold is in South African Rand (ZAR) and applies to the combined value of taxable supplies (standard-rated and zero-rated) made by the vendor in the course or furtherance of an enterprise; supplies made from offshore re-invoicing operations are outside the South African VAT net where the goods are not consumed or delivered in South Africa and no South African services are rendered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act zero-rates the supply of movable goods where the supplier has supplied the goods, the goods are exported from South Africa by the supplier (or on the supplier's behalf) within 90 days after the time of supply, and the supplier retains the prescribed proof of export (SARS Notice 62/2012 and binding general ruling 15). Section 11(1)(i) zero-rates goods delivered by the vendor to a non-resident recipient or agent in an export yard or a designated cargo container terminal, provided the goods are destined for export and are actually exported within the 90-day window. Section 11(2)(l) zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident person where the services are physically rendered outside South Africa, excluding services connected with land or fixed property in South Africa (Section 11(2)(l)(i)) and services rendered to a non-resident who is present in South Africa at the time of supply for that non-resident's own business (with B2B carve-outs). For a re-invoicing hub, zero-rating is claimable only where title to goods passes and the goods physically leave South Africa under an export clearance; a pure offshore-to-offshore re-invoicing arrangement (where goods never enter South African territory) constitutes a supply made outside South Africa and is neither standard-rated nor zero-rated—it is simply outside the VAT net, with no output tax and no input claim arising in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) electronically cleared by SARS Customs, reflecting the vendor as exporter and the ultimate foreign destination
  • Original commercial invoice referencing the applicable zero-rating section (s11(1)(a) or s11(1)(i)) and stating the supply is zero-rated
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export and the foreign port of discharge outside the SACU customs area
  • Proof of physical removal from South Africa (port of exit stamps, packing list, cargo movement certificate, container gate-out records, terminal release note)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103/MT202 evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in the vendor's South African bank account and FX repatriation within the prescribed 180-day period
  • Contract, purchase order, or signed sales agreement confirming the non-resident status of the buyer and the terms of export
  • Packing list and weight/line-item reconciliation matching quantities to the customs declaration

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), API-linked e-invoicing, or digital stamping for commercial B2B VAT invoices. SARS eFiling is the compulsory statutory medium for lodging VAT201 returns and customs declarations. However, SARS's Digital Tax Agenda and the SADC regional e-invoicing framework anticipate phased adoption; SARS currently accepts and encourages structured electronic (XML) invoices. All VAT invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under Section 20(4)-(5): vendor name and VAT registration number, invoice serial number, date of issue, full name and address of recipient, description and quantity of goods/services, consideration or value of supply, tax charged, and where zero-rated, a clear statement that the supply is zero-rated with the applicable section reference. Exports require the electronic SAD500 customs declaration, transmitted through SARS Customs ACE/EGD systems, with no facial stamping; customs clearance is a pre-requisite for the physical removal of goods and for zero-rating substantiation.

SARS is entitled to audit export zero-rating transactions under Section 31 of the VAT Act and the Tax Administration Act section 42 (notice of assessment) and section 46 (recovery). If the vendor fails to prove export within the 90-day export period, the supply is recharacterised as a standard-rated domestic supply; the full 15% output tax becomes due, plus interest under Section 39 VAT Act (currently 7-9% per annum calculated daily) and a temporary late-payment penalty of 10% per month up to 45% under Section 39(2). Where fraud or intentional evasion is established, SARS can impose understatement penalties under Section 223 TAA: 75% for no reasonable grounds, 100% for gross negligence, and 150% for intentional tax evasion, in addition to the underlying tax and interest. Section 233 TAA creates a criminal offence for tax evasion with a fine up to ZAR 2,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years; the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 further penalises false export declarations with duty evaded penalties up to 200% plus possible forfeiture of goods. SARS applies a retrospective clawback period of five years for RFP (running of Fiscal period? actual prescription: SARS may issue additional assessments within 5 years from the date of the original assessment; in cases of fraud, misrepresentation, or non-disclosure, the prescription period extends to 10 years); no export refund is payable until the vendor provides clear audit-trail proof that the goods physically left South Africa or were delivered to an export-yard carrier, and SARS may withhold export VAT refunds where refunds are flagged for verification under Section 44(2) VAT Act and binding rules on export matching.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is freely convertible for bona fide current account transactions effected through an authorised dealer, subject to full documentary verification. Capital account transactions remain managed: inward equity investment requires no prior approval but must be reported to SARB through the authorised dealer and evidenced by a Certificate of Receipt (CR number); outward capital transfers, granting of foreign loans by residents, and the use of unauthorised offshore accounts require FinSurv approval or a recognised general exemption. For profit remittance from a re-invoicing hub, dividends and capital reductions may be transferred to non-resident shareholders provided the company holds a valid SARS tax clearance certificate, dividend withholding tax at 20% is withheld, and the applicable Form MP336(a) declaration is lodged with the authorised dealer. The principal commercial hurdle is not convertibility itself but the assembly of a complete documentary trail, including underlying sales contracts, shipping evidence, customs declarations, and biannual export reporting, that allows the authorised dealer to classify each flow as a genuine current account transaction rather than a disguised capital transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 4(1), every exporter and trade principal including a re-invoicing company must repatriate the full value of export and trade receivables to South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless the authorised dealer approves a longer credit period. The foreign currency received must be surrendered to the authorised dealer, which in practice means the proceeds are credited to the exporter's Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with the authorised dealer or immediately converted into rand. There is no fixed statutory percentage mandating conversion to rand; the exporter may retain the currency in the FCA for approved foreign expenditure, trade finance repayments, or further hedging, provided the full supporting documentation is retained and the biannual export (BE) report is submitted to the authorised dealer on the prescribed schedule.

Central bank supervision: Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (Government Notice R.1111 as amended), with the National Treasury retaining policy oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-SUPPLEMENTARY ONLY. An offshore digital EMI such as Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business is neither an authorised dealer nor a licensed bank under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, and cannot lawfully act as the collection point for South African export proceeds unless the funds are immediately routed to a domestic authorised dealer. An EMI account held by a South African resident outside the Common Monetary Area would breach the 180-day repatriation rule and the FCA rules, which require foreign currency accounts to be maintained only with authorised dealers in South Africa. The viable architecture is strictly complementary: the EMI may provide multi-currency visibility and ancillary settlement for the third-country leg, while all SARB reporting, ZAR liquidity, and regulatory compliance are executed through a South African authorised dealer. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File documentation is expected when annual turnover reaches or exceeds ZAR 100 million; Country-by-Country Reporting applies where the consolidated group revenue reaches ZAR 10 billion. However, SARS may require comprehensive transfer pricing documentation for smaller re-invoicing entities because connected-person cross-border transactions are inherently high risk.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but functional substance is enforced through s 31 of the Income Tax Act, the general anti-avoidance rules (s 80A-80L), and s 103(2). A re-invoicing entity must demonstrate actual control over procurement, inventory risk, currency risk, credit risk, logistics decisions, and the presence of commensurate staff and capital in South Africa; otherwise SARS may disregard the arrangement or reallocate profits to the jurisdiction with real substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 75%-150% of the tax shortfall, daily prescribed-rate interest, criminal fines, and imprisonment under the Tax Administration Act ss 234-236 and the Prevention of Organised Crime Act for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs or causes the filing of a false return is liable as a representative taxpayer; criminal conviction may result in personal fines, restitution orders, travel bans, and imprisonment, and the corporate veil may be pierced to recover unpaid tax.
Shell Company / Absent Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may invalidate the purported transactions under GAAR or s 103(2), reallocate all re-invoicing profit to the offshore principal or permanent establishment, disallow deductions, and impose impermissible-avoidance penalties of 125%-150%; continued insolvency can trigger corporate winding-up proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for the company's unpaid tax under ss 22, 77, and 214 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 for reckless or fraudulent trading; disqualification from corporate office and civil asset recovery are available remedies.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts taxable income to the arm's length result under s 31, imposes a substantial-understatement penalty of 25%-100% or an intentional-evasion penalty of 75%-150%, denies loss set-offs, and charges interest from the original tax return date.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false transfer pricing policies, suppress documentation, or knowingly move profit offshore may be held jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, and criminal prosecution for fraud may result in imprisonment and travel restrictions.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Centre administrative sanctions up to ZAR 10 million, asset forfeiture, debarment, and criminal money-laundering penalties including imprisonment up to 30 years under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act 1998; SARS may also reverse input tax claims and impose civil fraud penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors can be criminally prosecuted for money laundering, disqualified from managing financial or corporate entities, subjected to preservation and forfeiture orders, travel-banned pending investigation, and ordered to pay civil restitution.
