Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum authorised or paid-up capital applies to a Proprietary Limited company; share capital may be par-value or no-par-value, and there is no escrow account or capital deposit obligation. The Companies Act prohibits minimum capital requirements, and the solvency and liquidity test governs only distributions and financial assistance, not initial subscription.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire safety certificate for the leased premises; Electrical Certificate of Compliance (ECoC); structural load and occupancy certification where server racks or UPS equipment exceed 200 kg/m² floor loading; workplace risk assessment and first-aid and evacuation arrangements under OHSA 85 of 1993; COIDA letter of good standing; certificate of occupancy issued by the local municipality.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) for company registration.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate (Pty) Ltd with CIPC to obtain registration certificate and company registration number (which serves as the primary identifier). 2) Register for income tax and VAT with SARS via eFiling using the RAV01 application form (business registration), submitting: certificate of incorporation, Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI), proof of registered address, and certified ID/passport copies of directors/shareholders. 3) SARS issues an Income Tax Reference Number and, where applicable, a VAT number (VAT prefix '4' for registered vendors). 4) Foreign head office and non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of ordinary shares; no exchange-control approval is required for inward foreign direct investment in an ordinary South African company, but the foreign shareholder must obtain an 'emigrant or non-resident' classification status with SARB for dividend and capital remittance purposes. 5) The entity must then register for Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) and UIF via eFiling if it employs personnel.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: resident company taxed on worldwide income under s.1 Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; a South African incorporated company is tax-resident. Corporate income tax rate: 27% (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023). No general tax-exempt zone applies; certain special economic zones (SEZ) offer reduced rates (e.g., 15%) but only to qualifying enterprises engaged in approved business activities — not applicable to a typical VPN SaaS unless the company is formally designated and operational in a SEZ. Small Business Corporation (SBC) graduated rates (0%–21%) are not available where the company provides a 'personal service' or derives mainly from services.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box classification regime comparable to the US. Available statutory elections: (1) VAT grouping election under s.50 VAT Act for connected companies; (2) zero-rating election under s.11(2)(l) VAT Act for services supplied to non-residents, subject to documentary proof that the recipient is outside SA and the services are not used/consumed in SA; (3) election to apply the participation-exemption on disposal of foreign subsidiary shares under s.10B(3); (4) election to file a consolidated financial statement and apply the CFC high-tax exemption under s.9D(2A); (5) tax treaty election under s.108 to request SARS to confirm treaty benefits. Hybrid mismatch provisions under ss.8F–8M override contractual choices and disallow interest deductions on hybrid financing instruments; no elective regime exists to reclassify entity status for income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (mandatory threshold for resident businesses based on taxable supplies of goods/services in any 12-month consecutive period); voluntary registration permitted from R50,000. A separate non-resident threshold applies: foreign suppliers of electronic services (defined in s.1 VAT Act) must register if their annual supplies of electronic services exceed R50,000 to customers in South Africa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: qualify under s.11(1)(a) VAT Act where the supplier 'ships' or 'delivers' goods to a non-resident by land, sea, air or post and the goods are exported within 90 days of the date of the tax invoice, provided the supplier retains the prescribed documents. Services: qualify under s.11(2)(l) where the services are supplied to (i) a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time the services are rendered (other than a resident carrying on business outside SA for at least 12 months), and (ii) the services are not supplied directly to a resident of SA. For VPN network/privacy SaaS, zero-rating will apply to subscription fees charged to non-resident customers where the usage/subscription is paid in foreign currency and the service is not physically delivered to any tangible location inside South Africa. Where the customer is a South African resident (including a South African citizen working abroad), the supply remains standard-rated at 15% unless the recipient is a foreign branch of a resident vendor and certain conditions exist.

  • SAD500 export customs declaration (Customs Bill of Entry) as cleared by SARS Customs & Excise
  • Bill of Lading (sea) / Air Waybill (air) / Parcel post receipt with tracking record
  • Commercial export invoice and underlying supply contract
  • Certificate of Origin or other SACU preferential trade documents (where claimed)
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (Form A/T$ issued by authorised dealer bank) proving settlement in foreign currency
  • Proof of payment / SWIFT MT103 or bank transfer confirmation from the non-resident customer
  • Shipment tracking evidence and port-of-loading / destination data

South Africa has no real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping regime in force. SARS accepts electronic tax invoices (e-invoices) if they contain all statutory particulars under s.20 VAT Act: supplier name, VAT number, invoice number, date, recipient name/address, description of supply, quantity, price, Vat inclusive/exclusive amount and the rate. Invoices must be issued within 21 days after the supply. VAT returns (VAT201) are filed electronically via eFiling on a two-month standard tax period (or one month/one year for certain categories). SARS has signalled phased mandatory standard e-invoicing (2024–2026 pilot), but no binding national clearance mandate is currently operational; constrained interoperability will trigger mandatory e-invoicing at thresholds to be gazetted.

SARS conducts periodic export verification audits under s.55 VAT Act and may require the vendor to produce export documentation within the prescribed period. Failure to furnish valid proof of export results in the supply being re-characterised as standard-rated with additional assessment, VAT refund clawback, and understatement penalties under s.222 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 ranging from 10% to 150% of the 'shortfall', plus 200% for intentional tax evasion. Interest accrues at the prevailing SARS rate under s.238. Fraudulent zero-rating (false declarations, forged export documents) is a criminal offence under s.234 TAA (knowingly making a false statement) and s.58 VAT Act, carrying imprisonment up to five years. Exchange Control Regulations require export proceeds to be received within 180 days; contravention may expose the company and directors to a penalty of R25,000 per incidence or imprisonment up to five years, and may trigger a SARS 'serious non-compliance' status that suspends VAT refunds and imposes compliance penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a partially liberalised exchange control regime. The Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions such as trade in goods and services, while capital account transactions remain subject to prior approval, reporting, and procedural controls administered by Authorised Dealers. Dividends, management fees, royalties, and software subscription revenue are treated as current account remittances and may be repatriated provided that supporting invoices, tax clearance, and Companies Act solvency and liquidity certification are presented. Cross-border equity injections, shareholder loans, and acquisition of local assets require capital account approval or post-transaction reporting to FinSurv. VPN network privacy software services are classified as intangibles and technology services; transfer pricing documentation is necessary to substantiate arm's length royalty and service fees. The practical hurdle for profit repatriation is not convertibility but compliance with SARS import/export reporting, anti-money laundering screening, and FinSurv's electronic reporting through the SARB Foreign Exchange Interface System (FEX)/Excon system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender of export proceeds to the SARB for South African resident exporters. Residents are required to repatriate export proceeds to South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment or due date of payment, whichever is earlier, unless an extended period has been approved. Export proceeds must be received into a South African authorised dealer bank account and may be retained in foreign currency only within the limits of a resident's foreign currency account. For an exporter of SaaS/VPN services, the service provider must ensure that all foreign currency receipts are reported to the SARB by the Authorised Dealer using the appropriate Balance of Payments (BOP) code. No conversion ratio is imposed; the resident may hold foreign currency or convert to ZAR at ruling commercial rates.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) have low direct viability for holding South African Rand denominated accounts. An offshore EMI cannot provide ZAR clearing capability without being authorised as a South African bank or obtaining a branch licence under the Banks Act 94 of 1990. However, EMIs are viable as auxiliary cross-border rails for collecting USD/EUR from global customers and subsequently settling into a South African corporate bank account via SWIFT. Such inbound flows must be reported to the SARB under the relevant BOP code. The primary operating and liquidity account must be held at a South African Authorised Dealer, because South African revenue recognition and exchange control reporting require the corporate entity to maintain a domestic bank account. The practical rating is 'Conditionally Viable - Medium' for foreign currency collection, and 'Low' for ZAR local clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed through a South African Authorised Dealer bank. The transferring non-resident shareholder must submit a 'Notification of inward international transfer' via the domestic receiving bank, and the bank will issue a BI (Balance of Payments) number confirmation. For a subscription loan or intercompany capital contribution, the entity must file a declaration on the SARB's Excon system using the appropriate capital BOP code, typically 'Loan Liabilities' or 'Investment Liabilities' depending on whether the injection is an equity or debt instrument. The receiving bank requires a board resolution approving the loan, a signed loan agreement with clear interest rate and repayment schedule, an immovable property certificate if applicable, and evidence of the source of funds. Loans from non-resident shareholders are subject to SARB approval when the aggregate intra-company loan exceeds ZAR 50 million per year? In practice, non-resident loans must be registered for repayment, and dividends or interest remittances require an Authorised Dealer formalities approval. For equity capital, the company must file a capital importation application confirming that the foreign direct investment is in compliance with the Companies Act and that the proceeds are for business operations in South Africa.Outbound trade disbursements for software licensing, cloud infrastructure fees, and vendor payments must be cleared through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for cross-border transfers. The Authorised Dealer bank verifies the BOP code, per-transaction documentary support, and SARS tax clearance. Commercial letters of credit (LC) are used for physical goods but are generally not required for SaaS/VPN disbursements; bank-to-bank wire transfer via SWIFT MT103 is the primary settlement rail. The company may also use local real-time gross settlement (ZAR) transfers via SAMOS for domestic costs, while all foreign currency payments to vendors are made through the bank's foreign exchange desk. 27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% (Dividends Tax under section 64E, imposed on the beneficial owner of the dividend, collected by the company or a regulated intermediary). Exemptions apply where the shareholder is a South African-resident company, certain exempt organisations, or a foreign company holding at least 10% of the shares in the dividend-paying company under section 64F.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual ITR14 must be filed by the date assigned by SARS, generally no later than 12 months after the end of the financial year. Provisional taxpayers must submit the first estimate within six months of the year start, the second estimate by the last business day of the year of assessment, and the final reconciliation within six months of year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required when cross-border related-party transactions in goods, IP, or services exceed ZAR 50 million, or when financial assistance transactions (loans, guarantees, interest) exceed ZAR 5 million. Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue is at least ZAR 10 billion, subject to SARS notification thresholds.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply UAE/Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but it enforces substance through the CFC foreign business establishment exemption under section 9D, the general anti-avoidance rules under sections 80A-L, and the common-law substance-over-form doctrine. A VPN software or IP-licensing vehicle with no real employees, premises, or commercial decision-making in South Africa, or one used merely to route royalties to an offshore affiliate, will trigger attribution, recharacterisation, denial of treaty relief, and withholding tax adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False ITR14

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty under section 222 of the Tax Administration Act of 0% to 200% of the shortfall; interest under section 189; criminal fine under section 235 of up to ZAR 51,300 or double the tax evaded, and/or imprisonment of up to 24 months. SARS may make a revised assessment based on estimated taxable income and execute civil recovery against bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepare or approve false returns can be prosecuted personally. Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the Pty Ltd is an alter ego; directors face civil restitution, interdicts preventing dissipation of assets, and in serious fraud cases travel bans issued through the National Prosecuting Authority.
Transfer Pricing Abuse

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust taxable income to the arm's-length price under section 31(2), impose a secondary adjustment deemed loan with interest under section 31(3), assess understatement penalties of up to 200%, and trigger 20% dividends tax or 15% withholding tax on a recharacterised distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation may face administrative penalties. If the pricing scheme is a sham diverting profits, directors can be jointly liable for the company's tax debt to the extent they benefitted from the diverted funds.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The general anti-avoidance rules may void the arrangement and tax the real economic owner; CFC rules attribute passive foreign income; SARS may deny double-taxation agreement relief; the shell company may be deregistered by the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission for non-compliance, and tax clearance may be withdrawn.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally ordered to contribute to SARS's loss under fraudulent trading principles. Travel bans and asset forfeiture may be imposed where the shell is used to launder profits or evade tax.
AML / FICA / POCA Violation

Statutory sanction: FICA administrative fines of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention. Under POCA, money laundering carries a maximum fine of ZAR 100 million and/or imprisonment of up to 30 years; asset forfeiture and freezing orders may be imposed. SARS may also impose understatement penalties on income concealed through such payment systems.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly enable money laundering are criminally liable in their personal capacity. They may be arrested, detained, subjected to travel bans, extradition, and personal asset forfeiture, including assets held within corporate accounts.
Withholding Tax Default

Statutory sanction: SARS may assess the withholding agent for the unremitted tax plus interest. Penalties may equal the amount not withheld; continued failure is a criminal offence under section 234, punishable by a fine and/or imprisonment of up to 24 months. A final demand may escalate to judgments and asset attachment.

Director personal liability: Under section 156 of the Tax Administration Act, directors and other responsible persons are jointly and severally liable when the company fails to pay over withheld taxes. SARS may implement travel restrictions on the responsible persons and attach personal assets as if the tax debt were the responsible person's own.
