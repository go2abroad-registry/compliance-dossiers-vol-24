Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); SARS eFiling portal (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za) and principal website (https://www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A South African-incorporated Proprietary Limited Company is automatically registered for income tax through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) integration at incorporation, obtaining a SARS income tax reference number. For VAT, compulsory registration must be applied for within 21 days of the taxable supplies exceeding R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available from R50,000 using Form VAT 101. Foreign (non-resident) entities carrying on a South African enterprise must register with SARS via eFiling as a non-resident company and may appoint a representative taxpayer; a single taxpayer reference number (TIN) covers income tax, VAT, PAYE, and customs duties.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A South African-incorporated Proprietary Limited Company is automatically registered for income tax through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) integration at incorporation, obtaining a SARS income tax reference number. For VAT, compulsory registration must be applied for within 21 days of the taxable supplies exceeding R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available from R50,000 using Form VAT 101. Foreign (non-resident) entities carrying on a South African enterprise must register with SARS via eFiling as a non-resident company and may appoint a representative taxpayer; a single taxpayer reference number (TIN) covers income tax, VAT, PAYE, and customs duties.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists. Available elections under the Income Tax Act and VAT Act include: (i) Section 45 election for tax-neutral intra-group asset transfers; (ii) Section 47 election for intra-group share transfers; (iii) Section 24J election for compound interest instrument recognition; (iv) Section 11D election for R&D allowance certification; (v) Section 50 VAT Act group of companies registration election; (vi) deemed vendor payment-basis VAT accounting election under Section 15(2A); (vii) hybrid debt-equity instruments are subject to mandatory Section 8F/8FA treatment with limited anti-mismatch elections.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, a supply of fertiliser goods (prilled urea and potassium chloride) is zero-rated only if the goods are supplied under a sale to a non-resident (export consignee) and are removed from South Africa within 90 days of supply (or within a longer period approved by SARS, including up to 5 years for goods stored under permit). Section 11(2)(l) zero-rates services supplied to non-resident persons where the services are physically rendered outside South Africa. For wholesale exporters, the vendor must hold documentary proof of the export transaction, including proof of payment in foreign currency received through an authorised dealer bank.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) duly cleared by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of goods from South African territory
  • Commercial Invoice referencing supplier and recipient VAT registration numbers
  • Packing List covering the consignment
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/telegraphic transfer advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds within 180 days
  • Export permit or SARS letter for conditional exports (where applicable)
  • Proof of supply and contract documentation identifying the non-resident customer

South Africa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. VAT invoices must satisfy Section 20 of the VAT Act (15 prescribed fields). Electronic tax invoices are permitted. SARS requires submission of VAT returns and transactional data via eFiling and the VALID (VAT Automated Ledger Interface) API for large vendors; customs export declarations are filed electronically through the SARS Customs EDI using the SAD500. Continuous digital data extraction powers under Section 40 of the Tax Administration Act enable SARS to match e-invoicing, customs, banking, and foreign-exchange data without a live clearance mandate.

SARS may disallow zero-rating retrospectively where export documentation is insufficient or goods are not removed within the statutory time frame, resulting in a deemed taxable supply at 15% with interest calculated from the original date of supply. Under Sections 222-223 of the Tax Administration Act, understatement penalties range from 10% (standard case) to 25% (substantial non-disclosure), 75% (gross negligence), and 100% (intentional tax evasion); an additional 10% SARS penalty may apply for failure to pay. Criminal prosecution for fraud, customs contraventions, or aiding non-compliance can result in fines and imprisonment. Vendors claiming export zero-rating must retain all supporting evidence for a minimum of 5 years from the date of supply, and SARS cross-matches export declarations with bank exchange-control records to detect fictitious exports.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, dividends, and interest, subject to reporting requirements and documentary verification by an authorised dealer. Capital account transactions, such as non-resident equity injections and shareholder loans, require completion of inward foreign investment forms and valid commercial rationale; repatriation of capital and profits is permitted for registered non-resident investors, but is subject to verification via the company registry, the non-resident share endorsement, evidence of the original inward transfer, and compliance with SARS tax clearance or exemption requirements. No blanket convertibility restriction applies, but non-trade outward remittances above prescribed thresholds require FinSurv approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Every exporter of urea and potash must, within 180 days of date of shipment, repatriate the full export sales proceeds to South Africa via an authorised dealer. The proceeds must be received in the prescribed invoice currency and either sold to the authorised dealer against ZAR or credited to a resident Foreign Currency Account (FCA) maintained with the authorised dealer. There is no mandatory percentage conversion of export earnings into ZAR under the current exchange control framework, but the full gross proceeds must be reported and matched to the relevant SAD500 customs export declaration and supporting shipping documents; any deviation or unrepatriated balance requires a completed application for expatriation of export proceeds and may attract SARB penalty action.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the delegated Exchange Control Authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers. All foreign exchange transactions in South Africa must be routed through an authorised dealer in foreign exchange, being a commercial bank licensed by SARB and the Prudential Authority of the Financial Sector Conduct Authority.
