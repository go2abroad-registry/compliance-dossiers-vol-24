Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private company under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; shares may be no-par-value and allotment may be zero-value. No escrow or capital deposit account is mandated by law. In practice, banks require a nominal opening deposit of approximately ZAR 100 to activate the corporate current account.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the local municipal Fire Protection Service in terms of the National Building Regulations (SANS 10400, Part T) and the Fire Brigade Services Act 99 of 1987; Certificate of Occupation issued by the municipality confirming structural compliance with SANS 10400; and workplace clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, including a baseline risk assessment, emergency evacuation plan, fire extinguisher maintenance, first-aid facilities, and an Electrical Certificate of Compliance for the leased premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private company under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; shares may be no-par-value and allotment may be zero-value. No escrow or capital deposit account is mandated by law. In practice, banks require a nominal opening deposit of approximately ZAR 100 to activate the corporate current account.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR operates under a managed-float exchange rate regime. Current-account transactions are effectively unrestricted; capital-account transactions are subject to exchange-control authorisation via an authorised dealer. Non-residents may freely subscribe for shares in a South African (Pty) Ltd and may remit dividends, branch profits, loan repayments and liquidation proceeds, provided a SARS tax clearance certificate is presented and the original capital was introduced through an authorised dealer. An inward foreign investment reference number is required for capital monitoring. No general restriction applies to foreign ownership percentages, but companies must be able to demonstrate that no loop structures or unauthorised outward investments exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts (including cross-border digital service revenue from helpdesk and SaaS activities) must be received by the exporter or an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment or invoice, and the foreign currency must be surrendered to an authorised dealer for conversion into ZAR. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% of export proceeds, unless FinSurv or an authorised dealer has granted specific dispensation allowing retention in a foreign currency or offshore account. Monthly reporting and completion of the relevant Exchange Control forms (e.g., Form BCS or the authorised dealer's export proceeds schedule) are mandatory.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low - offshore digital EMI accounts (e.g., Revolut, Wise) do not constitute South African authorised-dealer relationships and cannot be used as primary settlement rails for export proceeds or capital inflows. They may be positioned only as ancillary collection gateways that immediately route funds to a licensed authorised dealer bank in South Africa. The National Payment System Act, 1998 and SARB directives prohibit unlicensed cross-border payment aggregation for resident entities and the use of EMIs would fail exchange-control reporting requirements.

Defense evidence: Certified copies of the overseas shareholder's register of members, bank statements evidencing source of wealth, notarised passports and proof of residential address, plus a written source-of-funds declaration signed by the ultimate beneficial owner.
Helpdesk SaaS business flagged as high-risk due to digital service delivery to unknown end-users

Root cause: Helpdesk SaaS business flagged as high-risk due to digital service delivery to unknown end-users

Defense evidence: Completed AML risk-assessment questionnaire aligned to the 'Low_Risk_Standard' tier; entity business plan; sample customer contracts; confirmation that clients are not in high-risk jurisdictions; evidence of transaction monitoring and payment processor utilisation limited to Stripe/PayPal as licensed merchant gateways.
Perceived lack of local substance for a newly incorporated (Pty) Ltd

Root cause: Perceived lack of local substance for a newly incorporated (Pty) Ltd

Defense evidence: Lease agreement or serviced office contract; payroll and employment records for local staff; management accounts; confirmation of a physical registered address (not a post office box) and availability of a responsible person for compliance queries.
Suspected loop structure or unauthorised offshore reinvestment by a ZA-resident UBO

Root cause: Suspected loop structure or unauthorised offshore reinvestment by a ZA-resident UBO

Defense evidence: FinSurv inward financial surveillance reference number; authorised dealer letter confirming foreign capital import; affidavit stating no loop structure or loan-back arrangement exists; CIPC register of members confirming the direct foreign ownership.
Use of Stripe/PayPal as primary payment rails may be construed as unregulated money transmission

Root cause: Use of Stripe/PayPal as primary payment rails may be construed as unregulated money transmission

Defense evidence: Processor service agreements showing the processor acts strictly as a merchant acquiring gateway; confirmation of direct settlement into the South African bank account through an authorised acquiring bank; legal memorandum confirming no separate banking licence is required under the Banks Act or National Payment System Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound non-resident equity injections must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 to the designated Capital Escrow Account at an authorised dealer. The wire instruction must carry the correct balance-of-payments (BOP) purpose code, typically '1000' (Direct Investment in Reporting Economy) or '1010' if loan capital, and cite the company's inward foreign investment reference number. For subscriptions exceeding ZAR 50 million, FinSurv approval must be obtained in advance; below this threshold the authorised dealer verifies the CIPC registration, SARS tax number, FICA documents and the company's issued share schedule before releasing funds to the operational account.Operational trade disbursements (e.g., cloud infrastructure, overseas subcontractors, software licences) must be executed through the authorised dealer via SWIFT MT103/MT202 with supporting invoices or contracts and a compliant BOP purpose code. For large-value licensing or infrastructure acquisitions, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (LC) may be used to provide the beneficiary with a bank undertaking. Dividend remittances and loan repayments to non-residents require a SARS tax clearance directive, restoration of the inward foreign investment reference, and submission of the appropriate Exchange Control form (e.g., Form RBB or TA).Plain-vanilla FX forward contract (deliverable) with an authorised dealer covering a documented underlying commercial exposureCross-currency interest rate swap with a licensed South African authorised dealer or an FSCA-approved intermediaryFX option contract on a purchased-only basis to hedge uncertain future subscription or licensing cash flowsCurrency swap agreement addressing principal exchange for an identifiable cross-border trade or financing obligationNon-deliverable forward (NDF) permitted only where FinSurv grants a specific dispensation and the counterparty is a South African authorised dealer; generally unavailable to resident corporates under current exchange control policy
