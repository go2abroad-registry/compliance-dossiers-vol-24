Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) - eFiling portal at www.sarsefiling.co.za; entity incorporation must first be completed through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) with subsequent SARS tax registration

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent must incorporate a South African Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd through CIPC, then register the company with SARS on eFiling to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number. Non-resident companies with a South African permanent establishment must register for income tax and obtain a SARS income tax reference number. VAT registration is a separate SARS application (VAT 101 form) with compulsory registration where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period, or voluntary registration where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in 12 months. The company must also register for Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE), UIF, and SDL as an employer if it has employees. SARS will issue a single tax reference number linked to the company profile which is used for income tax, VAT, customs, and other administered Acts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available in South Africa. Taxation follows residence and source principles under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. A resident company is taxed on worldwide income at a standard corporate rate of 27%; non-resident companies are taxed on South African-source income only if they have a permanent establishment. Qualifying small business corporations with all natural-person shareholders and gross income below ZAR 20 million may elect graduated rates under s12E. VAT group registration may be elected under s50(1) of the VAT Act for 100%-owned group companies to consolidate VAT accounting. Tax consolidation (group relief) under s41 of the Income Tax Act is available for 70%+ subsidiaries with a formal group election. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under s25N and align with OECD BEPS Action 2, limiting elective mechanisms that create double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies made in any 12-month rolling period. Voluntary registration threshold: ZAR 50,000 in taxable supplies in any 12-month period (subject to SARS discretion). Foreign vendors carrying on a business in South Africa and making taxable supplies are subject to the same thresholds and may be required to register from the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero-rate of 0% VAT applies to the export of movable goods under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act provided the supplier has exported the goods within the prescribed period and retains the required documentary proof. Services may be zero-rated under s11(2)(l) where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not used or consumed in South Africa. For a virtual card issuer supplying B2B card-issuing and spend-management services to non-resident counterparties, zero-rating of exported services is available if the supply is not exempt financial services; the supplier should obtain a specific SARS VAT ruling because card-issuing and payment processing may constitute exempt financial services under s2 of the VAT Act, and the exemption/zero-rating boundary is narrow. The company must demonstrate that the recipient is a non-resident, the services are performed for a foreign market, and the services are not connected to South African land, goods, or improvement. Goods exported by the company itself or on the company's behalf are zero-rated only when physical removal from South Africa is completed and proof of export is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 digitally lodged through SARS Customs and eFiling)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from South Africa
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of payment from the foreign buyer, including a Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank SWIFT confirmation
  • Transport or freight documentation and insurance certificates supporting the export movement
  • Contract or purchase order confirming the non-resident status of the recipient

South Africa does not currently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime comparable to Italy or Mexico. Electronic VAT invoices are legally valid if they contain all prescribed particulars under s20(4) of the VAT Act, including vendor name, VAT registration number, invoice description, quantity, price, and tax amount. SARS publishes a Digital Tax Register interface specification and increasingly uses eFiling data integration to cross-match transactional data; SARS is actively expanding e-invoicing adoption; however, there is no mandatory API-based clearance token or real-time XML approval as at the current effective regime. Vendors are required to retain all electronic and documentary records for at least five years and must keep them available to SARS on request.

SARS has enhanced export verification and refund risk profiling. Where an exporter fails to export goods within the prescribed period or cannot substantiate the export documentation bundle, SARS will reverse the zero-rating, assess output VAT at 15%, impose interest under s187 of the Tax Administration Act, and may impose an understatement penalty under s223 of between 0% and 150% of the shortfall depending on the conduct classification. In cases of intentional tax evasion, s60 of the VAT Act permits additional tax of up to 100% of the tax evaded, and criminal prosecution may result in a fine or imprisonment. SARS may also claw back credited input VAT, set off refunds, and apply a 'no export, no refund' approach, with retrospective assessments stretching back up to five years, or unlimited in cases of fraud. Payment service and card-issuing exporters claiming zero-rated services must maintain full audit trails of the foreign counterparty, the place of consumption, and the foreign currency settlement to avoid retrospective clawbacks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is a regulated convertible currency: current account payments are presumptively permissible through authorised dealers with documented underlying commercial transactions, while capital account transactions are liberalised only within prescribed limits and thresholds. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders require an audited or reviewed annual financial statement, SARS tax clearance (tax compliance status), board resolution, and proof of beneficial ownership filing; remittance is processed with withholding tax (Dividends Tax) and exchange-control verification. The principal hurdles are documentary completeness, SARS compliance status, and SARB FinSurv reporting obligations rather than outright remittance prohibition. Non-resident equity injections are generally permitted through authorised dealers as inward listings, but related-party loans and subsequent capital account conversions may require evidence of trade/investment bona fides and, if above regulatory thresholds, FinSurv approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must ensure foreign-currency export proceeds are received in South Africa within 180 days of shipment (or within an extended period granted by an authorised dealer/FinSurv for valid commercial reasons); once received, the proceeds must be surrendered by selling the foreign currency to an authorised dealer within 30 days. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% of repatriated export receipts, denominated in a negotiable currency, converted into rand unless a specific exemption (e.g., offsetting foreign trade financing or unavoidable offshore retention) has been granted in writing. All export documentation must be retained for 5 years and produced to the authorised dealer/SARB upon request.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting as Exchange Control Authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium viability for an offshore digital EMI: South Africa has no passporting regime for foreign electronic-money institutions; any card-issuing/payment processing business requires either a banking licence under the Banks Act, a payment-service-provider authorisation under the National Payment System Act, or a material outsourcing arrangement with a licensed South African clearing bank. Offshore EMIs can serve only as payment-facilitation or treasury-reconciliation layers, not as onshore deposit-taking or direct scheme-member entities. Credit risk, AML/CFT accountability under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FIC Act), and scheme rules (Visa/Mastercard) require onshore legal substance and a sponsor bank or principal membership.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity investment must be routed through an authorised dealer in South Africa by SWIFT MT103/202 for the credit of the (Pty) Ltd's operational or capital escrow account, referencing the share certificate/CIPC confirmation and a clearly stated payment narrative reading 'Equity subscription - ZA PTY LTD'. The authorised dealer must be furnished with the inward listing (FDI) notification information, proof of incorporation, share allotment resolution, and a copy of the bank's KYC/AML analysis. For foreign direct investment into ordinary shares, the bank will apply the applicable ZAR/BOP purpose classification (direct investment equity - inward), and no SARB prior approval is required for a standard private company equity injection from an arm's-length non-resident shareholder. Proceeds may be invested in interest-bearing call or money-market instruments pending deployment, but the company must ensure the funds are not used for prohibited speculative FX activity. If the subscription includes a share premium or an intercompany shareholder loan, additional financing documentation and FIC Act source-of-funds evidence must be provided to avoid being treated as a regulated borrowing.Trade settlements are to be made via SA-SWIFT MT103 for direct payments of card-scheme network and vendor/merchant invoices, and via commercial letters of credit (documentary L/Cs) issued by a domestic authorised dealer for high-value cross-border procurement of card production equipment or software licensing. The company must use authorised-dealer clearing channels for all ZAR-denominated outbound automated clearing (ACB/DeR) transactions. For the card business specifically, outbound settlement between the card issuer, principal member/sponsor bank, and Mastercard/Visa clearing systems must be settled through the sponsor bank's card-scheme settlement platform or a licensed payment-clearing house; the domestic card network accounts must be segregated and reconciled daily. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022; the predecessor rate was 28%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax, imposed on the beneficial owner of the dividend and collected by the company as withholding agent; the distribution amount is deemed to be net of 20% tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

ITR14 must be submitted within 12 months after the company's financial year-end. Provisional tax payments are due as follows: first provisional payment six months after the start of the year of assessment; second provisional payment on or before the last business day of the year of assessment; third top-up provisional payment within six months after the financial year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border transactions with connected persons exceeding ZAR 5 million in aggregate in a year of assessment trigger the obligation to prepare, maintain, and produce contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation. A full Local File and Master File are required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least ZAR 1 billion or where the covered cross-border controlled transaction value exceeds ZAR 100 million; simplified documentation applies only to smaller low-risk businesses.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but economic substance is a live doctrine applied through the residence test (place of effective management under s1 of the Income Tax Act), the general anti-avoidance rules (s80A to s80L), the transfer-pricing provisions (s31), and controlled foreign company attribution rules (s9D). A South African Pty Ltd acting as a pure letter-box or billing shell for an offshore group strategy, without board meetings, employees, contracts, operational systems, or decision-making power in South Africa, will not qualify for treaty benefits and may face recharacterisation as a foreign resident, disallowance of deductions, deemed dividends, and severe penalties. The virtual card business must demonstrate real executive and operational activity materially performed in South Africa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall applies for intentional tax evasion. Criminal prosecution under s235 of the same Act can result in a fine not exceeding R10 million and imprisonment of up to five years. SARS also charges interest at the prescribed rate on unpaid taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the submission of a false tax return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties. Personal liability recovery by SARS can extend to personal assets. Criminal prosecution may result in travel bans, extradition, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may recharacterise the company under the general anti-avoidance rules (s80A) and deny treaty benefits, foreign tax credits, and deductions. The company could be treated as a controlled foreign company of an offshore parent, triggering CFC attribution. Penalties include understatement penalties up to 200% of taxable income reallocated and CIPC deregistration of the entity.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors of a shell company may be personally liable under s77 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 for knowingly carrying on the business recklessly or with negligent intent. Directors face civil restraint orders, travel bans, and disqualification from acting as directors.
Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorist Financing (AML/CFT) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, administrative fines can reach ZAR 10 million per contravention. A conviction in a criminal court for deliberate non-reporting can result in a fine or imprisonment up to 15 years (depending on the offence), and the financial regulator may revoke the authorised payment or financial-services licence relevant to virtual card issuance.

Director personal liability: Directors have personal liability where they fail to install an accountable-instance control and proper AML governance. Directors may be individually fined, disqualified, and imprisoned for up to 10 years where the non-compliance was intentional or reckless.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Adjustment

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the consideration under s31 to reflect the arm's length price, disallow interest deductions for excessive debt, impose secondary adjustments, and apply understatement penalties of 100%-200% of the resulting tax shortfall. Interest at the prescribed rate runs from the original date of assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve pricing schedules or group service contracts that are not arm's length may be held personally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties if they knew or ought to have known that the arrangements were not justified by commercial substance. Civil recovery proceedings and travel bans are available to SARS.
Withholding Tax and Repatriation Failures

Statutory sanction: The company is liable for the unwithheld dividend tax, interest tax, or royalty tax as a withholding agent. SARS may collect the full 20% dividends tax payable, or 15% on interest/royalties, plus an administrative penalty of up to 10% of the unpaid withholding tax per month, and further understatement penalties. Directors face personal liability if the company failed to withhold without valid exemption documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused or permitted the company to pay dividends or cross-border fees without withholding are personally liable for the outstanding withholding tax. SARS may make a personal-liability assessment, issue a section 179 notification under the Tax Administration Act, and impose travel bans or obtain civil judgment for recovery.
