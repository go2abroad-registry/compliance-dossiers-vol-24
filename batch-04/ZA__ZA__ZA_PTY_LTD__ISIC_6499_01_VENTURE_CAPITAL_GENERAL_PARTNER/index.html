Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs division handles export entries.

E-government filing portal

Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); register as a taxpayer on SARS eFiling; obtain an income tax reference number; appoint a SARS-accepted public officer resident in South Africa; submit VAT registration (VAT101) if taxable turnover exceeds the compulsory/voluntary threshold; obtain an export customs code from SARS Customs for export transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); register as a taxpayer on SARS eFiling; obtain an income tax reference number; appoint a SARS-accepted public officer resident in South Africa; submit VAT registration (VAT101) if taxable turnover exceeds the compulsory/voluntary threshold; obtain an export customs code from SARS Customs for export transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election. A South African (Pty) Ltd is an opaque, entity-level taxpayer. An eligible resident company may elect special headquarter-company treatment under section 9I of the Income Tax Act for certain foreign intra-group income; no hybrid mismatch or pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month rolling period; voluntary registration permitted once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for exported movable goods where the exporter holds documentary proof of physical export from South Africa to a foreign destination and satisfies SARS's export evidence requirements. The general partner's supplies of management services to non-resident funds may qualify as zero-rated exported services if the services are supplied to and consumed outside South Africa, subject to contractual and payment evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time clearance, digital stamping, or fiscal storage control requirement is currently imposed. VAT tax invoices must contain prescribed particulars and be issued within 21 days of request/delivery/payment; SARS is piloting and has announced a future mandatory e-invoicing environment through eFiling/API, but it is not yet live.

SARS can disallow the zero-rating, assess output VAT, and recover refunds with statutory interest where export or service evidence is incomplete. Fraudulent or negligent export claims attract additional VAT under section 60 of the VAT Act, understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act, and criminal sanctions for VAT fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a current-account convertible regime with full transferability for bona fide trade, services, and profit repatriation, subject to documentary verification and tax clearance. Capital-account flows are administered via general allowances, institutional investment dispensations, or transaction-specific FinSurv approval. A licensed Venture Capital GP is an authorised institutional investor where funds are managed under an SARB/FSCA-approved institutional framework. Non-resident profits, dividends, and management fees may be remitted freely upon presentation of audited after-tax profit certification, SARS tax clearance (TCC), and (where applicable) dividends tax clearance. No exchange-control approval is required for routine operational remittances; capital calls from non-residents and outbound fund investments require Authorised Dealer reporting with correct BOP codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory percentage conversion or surrender requirement applies. Export and invisible proceeds (e.g., management fees, carried interest) must be received in a South African Authorised Dealer bank account within 180 days of shipment/service completion or start of entitlement. Proceeds may be retained in foreign currency via a Foreign Currency Account, but all receipts must be reported under the relevant SARB BOP code and supported by invoices, agreements, and proof of the underlying transaction. No forced conversion to ZAR applies.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, as administered via the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, subject to SARB National Payment System Act licensing / Prudential Authority authorisation. Offshore EMIs are not acceptable as the sole clearing rail for capital calls or subscription inflows because SARB exchange-control reporting requires all inward fund flows to be received through a South African Authorised Dealer. An offshore EMI may support ancillary payment processing, but the primary capital-call collection, ZAR settlement, and foreign-currency accounts must reside with a licensed domestic bank. FSCA/FIC Act AML obligations further disincentivize reliance on non-regulated foreign EMIs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital calls from non-resident LPs must be sent by SWIFT MT103 to the GP's ZAR capital-call collection account held with a South African Authorised Dealer, referencing the applicable SARB BOP code (e.g., 4480 inward investment in fund units). The bank will record the inflow under the Balance of Payments reporting framework. Each inward remittance must be matched to the executed capital call notice and LP identity record. The SARB individual discretionary allowance cannot be used for corporate fund subscriptions; instead, the GP must rely on the institutional investment dispensation or obtain FinSurv pre-approval where required. For collections in USD/EUR/GBP, the funds may be held in a fully-supported Foreign Currency Account without conversion, or converted to ZAR via the applicable spot rate upon documented instruction.Outbound disbursements to fund SPVs, portfolio companies, service providers, or co-investors are executed via SWIFT MT103 (with MT202 cover where interbank settlement is required) through the Authorised Dealer, using BOP codes for outward direct investment (e.g., 4600/4601) or services. Commercial LCs are rare in VC treasury but permissible for structured acquisition transactions; the South African bank can issue an irrevocable documentary credit in favour of the overseas seller, subject to standard due diligence and any applicable transfer-pricing and SARB approvals. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax imposed on the beneficial owner of a dividend paid by a South African company; the paying company (or transfer agent) must withhold and pay SARS.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 must be submitted within 12 months after the company’s financial year-end. Provisional tax instalments are due within 6 months after the start of the year of assessment and at the end of the year of assessment, with an optional top-up payment due within 7 months after the year-end (for companies).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 5 – Turnover / Gross Income Gross annual receipts from management fees, carried interest allocations, and any other operating income derived from acting as general partner of an early-stage technology venture fund. For a South African company owned by non-residents, report all worldwide gross income. Foreign-source income is subject to normal tax; double tax relief may be claimed as a foreign tax credit on a separate schedule. Late filing of the ITR14 triggers an administrative non-compliance penalty under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, ranging from R250 per month for small entities to R10,000 per month for high-turnover companies (capped at a fixed maximum in each category), plus a percentage penalty of 10% on any late payment of assessed tax, and interest at the prescribed rate (currently between 8.5% and 10.75% per annum, compounded daily).
Box 14 – Assessed Loss Brought Forward Unutilised assessed loss from prior years available to be set off against current year taxable income. Confirm the loss arose from the same trade, and that no s103(2) change in ownership or trading continuity event has occurred; otherwise the loss may be disallowed. Late filing of the ITR14 triggers an administrative non-compliance penalty under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, ranging from R250 per month for small entities to R10,000 per month for high-turnover companies (capped at a fixed maximum in each category), plus a percentage penalty of 10% on any late payment of assessed tax, and interest at the prescribed rate (currently between 8.5% and 10.75% per annum, compounded daily).
Box 17 – Taxable Income or Assessed Loss Net taxable amount after deducting all allowable business expenses, capital allowances, and subtracting any assessed loss carried forward. Computed under ordinary South African rules. Exempt dividend income (e.g., dividends received from South African resident companies) must be excluded from gross income and separately disclosed. Late filing of the ITR14 triggers an administrative non-compliance penalty under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, ranging from R250 per month for small entities to R10,000 per month for high-turnover companies (capped at a fixed maximum in each category), plus a percentage penalty of 10% on any late payment of assessed tax, and interest at the prescribed rate (currently between 8.5% and 10.75% per annum, compounded daily).
Box 21 – Normal Tax Payable 27% of taxable income, before rebates and credits, reduced by the annual corporate rebate if applicable. The rate is the same for resident and non-resident-owned South African companies. No additional branch remittance tax applies to a South African incorporated (Pty) Ltd. Late filing of the ITR14 triggers an administrative non-compliance penalty under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, ranging from R250 per month for small entities to R10,000 per month for high-turnover companies (capped at a fixed maximum in each category), plus a percentage penalty of 10% on any late payment of assessed tax, and interest at the prescribed rate (currently between 8.5% and 10.75% per annum, compounded daily).
Box 22 – Foreign Tax Credit Credit against South African normal tax for foreign taxes paid on foreign-source income that is included in South African taxable income. A schedule of foreign tax credits and supporting SARS assessment or tax official certificate is required; if no DTA exists, unilateral foreign tax credit relief applies. Late filing of the ITR14 triggers an administrative non-compliance penalty under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, ranging from R250 per month for small entities to R10,000 per month for high-turnover companies (capped at a fixed maximum in each category), plus a percentage penalty of 10% on any late payment of assessed tax, and interest at the prescribed rate (currently between 8.5% and 10.75% per annum, compounded daily).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed monetary threshold is set in the South African Income Tax Act. Section 31 of the Act applies to any transaction between connected persons that affects actual or future taxable income. In practice, SARS expects contemporaneous Local File/Master File documentation and an arm’s-length benchmark for every material connected-party cross-border transaction; internal administrative guidelines often trigger detailed TP review where annual connected-party turnover exceeds ZAR 10 million.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no codified Economic Substance Regulation equivalent to UAE or BVI; however, common-law substance-over-form principles, the general anti-avoidance rules in s80A-L of the Income Tax Act, and s103(1) are applicable. Further, OECD BEPS Action 6 and the principal purpose test are applied in DTA claims. The GP entity should maintain a physical office, substantive decision-making powers, a majority of local directors, and actual performance of management and investment-decision functions; otherwise SARS may disregard the entity, recharacterise transactions, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Upon an audit, SARS may impose an additional tax of up to 200% of the tax shortfall under s223 of the Tax Administration Act; criminal prosecution under s235 may lead to a fine of up to R10 million and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years, plus interest and cost of recovery.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt and additional penalties. They may face criminal imprisonment (up to 10 years), travel bans under s250 TAA, and civil damages claims from minority investors. SARS may also apply a civil fraud claim.
Shell Company / Artificial Conduit Financing

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the transaction under the s80A-L general anti-avoidance rules and adjust the taxable income as if the arrangement had not been entered into; understatement penalties of 100%-150% for negligent or grossly negligent conduct; denial of DTA benefits under the principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for the additional assessment and penalties. A court may issue a delinquency order under s162 of the Companies Act 2008, disqualifying them from serving as directors for up to 10 years, and order civil restitution of any profits improperly routed.
AML / Financial Intelligence Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 can reach up to R50 million for repeated serious non-compliance; criminal conviction for certain intentional contraventions may carry fines up to R10 million and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow a failure to comply are personally liable to criminal prosecution, with a maximum fine of R10 million and/or imprisonment for 10 years. FIC Act s58A also authorises a court to order the director to pay restitution or be banned from holding any position of influence in a financial institution.
Transfer Pricing / Management Fee Over-Pricing

Statutory sanction: SARS will make a primary transfer pricing adjustment under s31 of the Income Tax Act, increasing taxable income by the under-declared amount; if the taxpayer fails to maintain contemporaneous documentation, an understatement penalty of 150% may apply; all additional tax bears interest at the prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or directed the transfer pricing policy are jointly and severally liable for the understatement penalty if the conduct is attributable to fraud or gross negligence; they may also be subject to a civil action for recovery of company funds and disqualification from the board of any company under the Companies Act.
