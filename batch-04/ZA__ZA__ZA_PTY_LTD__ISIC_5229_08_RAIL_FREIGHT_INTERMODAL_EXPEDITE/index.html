Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account is required for a South African private company (Pty) Ltd; shares may be issued without par value, and the founding directors may accept cash or in-kind consideration in compliance with sections 40 and 41 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety or structural clearance is required for a pure administrative registered office; if the enterprise subsequently operates a rail-linked depot or warehouse, it must obtain municipal building plan approval and a certificate of occupancy under the National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act 103 of 1977, fire safety certification through the local fire authority, and workplace clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, including registration with the Department of Employment and Labour and a valid COIDA Certificate of Good Standing.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow account is required for a South African private company (Pty) Ltd; shares may be issued without par value, and the founding directors may accept cash or in-kind consideration in compliance with sections 40 and 41 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); registration and filing via the SARS eFiling portal (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs clearing via SARS Customs eFiling.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated (Pty) Ltd, the entity obtains its taxpayer reference number automatically via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) one-stop company registration interface (Corporate Registry annual filing/CSR). Alternatively, the entity must complete SARS's Company Registration (ITREG) / VAT 101 application through SARS eFiling or a SARS branch. Required supporting documents: Memorandum of Incorporation, CIPC certificate of incorporation, proof of registered office address, identity documents or passports of all directors and beneficial shareholders (with non-resident passport copies notarised), and a resolution appointing a SARS appointed representative/authorised representative. SARS issues a company income tax reference number (income tax number) and, upon VAT registration, a separate VAT vendor number in the format 4-digit-VAT-region-4-digit-7-digit-2-digit (e.g., 4012345678), with VAT activation dates. A non-resident external company must first register as an external company under s32 Companies Act with CIPC, then register for income tax and VAT as a branch; the branch is taxed as a South African tax resident only if its place of effective management is in South Africa; otherwise it is a non-resident branch.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand (ZAR) is freely floating and fully convertible for current-account transactions, including cross-border freight, logistics, and intermodal service fees, without prior FinSurv approval, provided the transaction is genuine, documented, and routed through an AD bank. Capital-account inflows, such as non-resident direct investment into a (Pty) Ltd by way of share subscription, are permitted without prior exchange-control approval but must be reported by the AD bank in the SARB balance-of-payments statistical return and reflected in the company's securities register and CIPC filings. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are treated as current-account payments: no exchange-control approval is required, but the company must evidence distributable profits through a board resolution and financial statements, and must comply with the 20% dividends withholding tax regime (treaty relief available under South Africa's DTA network). Practical hurdles include SARS tax-compliance verification by the AD bank, FICA documentary requirements, and the need for the inward FDI receipt to be recorded with the AD bank before future repatriation is permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

With effect from 1 April 2018, the mandatory conversion (surrender) of export proceeds into rand was abolished; no prescribed conversion ratio applies. However, the repatriation obligation remains: a South African resident exporter must receive payment for exported goods or services in convertible foreign currency within 180 days of the date of shipment (goods) or invoicing (services), and the proceeds must be credited to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an AD bank or sold to the AD bank for rand. Export proceeds may not remain offshore beyond the 180-day window. Goods exports must be declared to SARS Customs via the SAD500 declaration; services exports are evidenced by the service contract and invoice. The AD bank must be furnished with the export invoice/contract and monitors receipt of proceeds through the electronic export-verification system, flagging overdue amounts to FinSurv. Extensions require a motivated application through the AD bank. For the target rail-freight/intermodal forwarder earning fees from non-resident clients, the same 180-day repatriation timeline applies to service receipts, with contracts, invoices, and proof of performance retained as the supporting record.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which administers the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933. All licensed commercial banks in South Africa operate as Authorized Dealers (ADs) in foreign exchange and execute exchange-control supervision on behalf of FinSurv, while SARS Customs administers cross-border trade declarations and export monitoring.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail. South Africa has no fully operational Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime; cross-border digital payment firms such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex do not hold bank or authorized-dealer licences under the Banks Act, 1990 or the National Payment System Act, 1998, and therefore cannot provide the (Pty) Ltd with direct ZAR account infrastructure, SWIFT-cleared local settlement, inward FDI reporting, or export-proceeds repatriation compliance. These providers may be used peripherally (e.g., payout cards or expense management) in partnership with a licenced AD bank, but FinSurv treats flows routed through unregulated EMIs with heightened due diligence. A Tier 1 domestic AD bank remains mandatory for the company's primary capital and operational accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident parent or investor executes a board resolution and share-subscription agreement and remits USD, EUR, or GBP by SWIFT MT103 to the (Pty) Ltd capital account at the chosen AD bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must clearly state 'INWARD FOREIGN DIRECT INVESTMENT - SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - [company legal name]' to enable automatic FDI classification. The AD bank verifies the company's FICA status and CIPC registration, then records the inflow in the SARB balance-of-payments and exchange-control electronic returns as inward direct investment (equity). The bank issues a proof or certificate of inward capital receipt, which must be retained for future dividend-repatriation substantiation. The (Pty) Ltd must allot the shares within the Companies Act, 2008 timeframes, file the CIPC share-issue notice, update its securities register, and may move cleared funds from the capital account to the operational account. If shareholder loan capital is used instead, the loan agreement, interest rate, maturity, and repayment terms must be registered with the AD bank, and interest payments must satisfy 15% withholding tax (treaty-reduced where applicable).The primary clearing rails are (i) SWIFT MT103 straight wires for operational trade expenses such as rail haulage, terminal-handling fees, intermodal equipment costs, insurance, and subcontractor freight settlements; these are current-account payments requiring only invoices and contracts, plus the AD bank's SARS tax-compliance verification where applicable; and (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) issued by the AD bank for structured settlements where a counterparty requires a bank undertaking, for example block-train service contracts, rolling-stock or container purchases, and corridor access payments. For L/C and guarantee issuance the AD bank applies trade-facility limits or cash cover. Local ZAR legs clear through the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system; cross-border legs clear through the international correspondent network (SWIFT, CHIPS, TARGET). The AD bank retains supporting documents for five years under exchange-control audit rules.Deliverable forward exchange contracts (FECs) concluded with an AD bank against documented current-account or capital-account exposure, being the standard onshore hedging rail for USD-, EUR-, or GBP-denominated freight receivables and payables.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) used to hedge the principal and interest exposure of foreign-currency shareholder loans or long-term capital commitments.Vanilla deliverable foreign exchange options (European-style calls and puts) entered into with an AD bank to cap adverse rand movement on committed or anticipated transactional exposure, subject to credit limits and underlying documentation.Natural hedging through Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs): retention of foreign-currency service receipts in hard currency to match hard-currency payables, thereby reducing the notional derivative requirement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible for onshore SARB-resident entities because the rand is a deliverable currency; speculative, leveraged, or uncovered derivative positions are prohibited onshore and would require a ring-fenced offshore structure or specific FinSurv approval.
