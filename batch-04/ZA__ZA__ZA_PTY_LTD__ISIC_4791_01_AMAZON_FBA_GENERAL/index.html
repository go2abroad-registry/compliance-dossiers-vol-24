Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); returns and registrations are filed via the SARS eFiling portal (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs declarations are transmitted electronically to the SARS Customs Division via eFiling or approved EDI channels (SAD500 bill of entry).

E-government filing portal

STEP-1 Incorporation: register the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); the integrated e-registration automatically allocates an Income Tax Reference Number (ITRN) and provisional-tax status, so a separate income-tax application is not required for a ZA-incorporated entity. STEP-2 Public officer: within one month of commencing business, appoint a South African resident 'public officer' responsible for tax compliance (Income Tax Act s65; Companies Act s33(4)) and file the appointment with SARS. STEP-3 VAT: apply for VAT registration via eFiling (VAT 101) where taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period (compulsory within 21 days) or from ZAR 50,000 for voluntary registration. STEP-4 Customs: register as an importer/exporter through the SARS Customs 'Trader Registration' module to obtain a Customs Code (DA 185/eFiling), a prerequisite for cross-border stock movement and export bill-of-entry declaration. STEP-5 Exchange control: open a ZAR and/or foreign-currency account with an Authorised Dealer bank and comply with SARB reporting so that export proceeds are received and inward-receipt certificates are issued. STEP-6 PAYE/UIF: register for PAYE, SDL and UIF (EMP101e) within 21 days of becoming an employer if the FBA operation engages South African staff.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A ZA-incorporated (Pty) Ltd is automatically a South African tax resident (Income Tax Act s1: incorporation or place of effective management, subject to DTA tie-breaker) and is taxed at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023) on worldwide income; no pass-through eligibility exists. The company is a separate VAT 'vendor' carrying on an enterprise in South Africa for the marketplace retail business (ISIC 4791) and cannot claim territorial scope or tax-exempt-zone treatment. Dividends paid are subject to a separate 20% dividends tax withheld at entity level.

Available election forms

South Africa has no U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election: entity classification follows statutory residence and source tests, not taxpayer choice. Available elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration once turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000 (s23(2) VAT Act); (ii) discretionary VAT grouping of closely-held companies under s50A VAT Act (SARS consent required); (iii) election to apply the 'foreign business establishment' exclusion to exempt controlled foreign company income (s9D(9A) Income Tax Act); and (iv) the external-company branch election for non-resident entities (not applicable to a ZA (Pty) Ltd). Hybrid mismatch rules (s25H-s25N Income Tax Act, effective 1 January 2020, aligned with OECD BEPS Action 2) apply automatically by statutory ordering and cannot be elected away.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory threshold (taxable supplies made or expected to be made in any 12-month period; registration within 21 days of exceeding - s23(1) VAT Act); voluntary registration is permitted from ZAR 50,000 turnover (s23(2)). Non-resident suppliers of electronic services are separately registered under the FES regime from ZAR 50,000, but the ZA-resident (Pty) Ltd is subject to the standard ZAR 1,000,000 enterprise threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies under s11(1)(a) where the registered vendor supplies movable goods and the goods are physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply, provided the vendor carries on an enterprise, the export occurs through a SARS-designated port/airport, and the vendor holds the prescribed documentary proof including the VAT 264 export certificate (s54A VAT Act). Amazon FBA cross-border sales fulfilled from a South African fulfilment centre to non-resident consumers qualify if the export and payment-remittance conditions are met; stock transfers to a foreign Amazon centre without a sale are not taxable supplies, so zero-rating applies only at the point of an actual export sale. Services: s11(2)(l) zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident (not used in a South African business) and s11(2)(m) zero-rates services physically performed outside South Africa; offshore marketplace and fulfilment services consumed abroad qualify. A valid VAT 264 certificate, customs export declaration, and Inward Remittance Certificate must be in SARS's possession before an export refund is released.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 electronic bill of entry through eFiling/TradeLink)
  • VAT 264 Export Certificate (SARS s54A verification-of-export form)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or Seaway Bill showing the South African port/airport of export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Authorised Dealer confirming receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Commercial tax invoice marked 'ZERO-RATED - EXPORT' with all s20(4) VAT particulars
  • Packing list, freight-forwarder delivery confirmation and port terminal receipt evidencing physical departure of goods

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital stamping of invoices is in force in South Africa. SARS eFiling is the statutory channel for VAT returns (two-monthly by default) and customs declarations (SAD500 bill of entry). A full tax invoice must contain the 15 particulars set out in s20(4) of the VAT Act and must state 'ZERO-RATED - EXPORT' where export zero-rating is claimed. SARS has published draft proposals for a phased PEPPOL-based mandatory e-invoicing regime (public consultation 2023-2024), but it is not yet law; vendors must retain electronic records for at least 5 years (VAT Act s55; Tax Administration Act s29).

SARS conducts real-time data matching between export bills of entry, VAT 264 certificates, FX inward-receipt records and VAT refund claims; refunds are automatically suspended for manual verification where the export bundle is incomplete. If zero-rated goods are not exported within 90 days or the VAT 264 certificate is not obtained, the zero-rating is reversed: output tax at 15% plus statutory interest (Tax Administration Act s187, prescribed rate, compounded monthly) is raised by additional assessment; the standard assessment window is 3 years, extended to 6 years for material non-disclosure (s99 TAA) and unlimited in fraud cases. Understatement penalties under s222/223 TAA range from 10% (standard case), 50% (substantial understatement), 75% (failure to take reasonable care), 100% (no reasonable grounds), 150% (gross negligence) to 200% (intentional tax evasion), stacking with interest and ad valorem penalties. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating claims under s234-235 TAA and s87 of the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 may result in fines up to ten times the tax involved and/or imprisonment of up to five years, with retrospective clawback of any refund already paid.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is a floating, quasi-convertible currency. Current account items (trade in goods, e-commerce proceeds, commissions, dividends, interest) are freely convertible provided the underlying transaction is evidenced and the proceeds are repatriated within statutory deadlines. Capital account transactions (equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, quasi-capital funding, derivative settlement) require prior FinSurv approval or an Authorised Dealer notification under the Capital Flow Management framework. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted upon production of an auditor's certificate of distributable reserves, a SARS tax clearance or IT3(b) dividend declaration, and settlement of the 20% Dividends Withholding Tax (rate may be reduced under a double tax treaty). No general restriction applies to Amazon FBA sales receipts, but all USD/ZAR conversions must take place onshore through an Authorised Dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Amazon FBA and direct e-commerce exports, the full sale consideration must be received in South Africa within 180 days of the shipment date (bill of lading or airway bill). Upon receipt in the ZA operating account or a Foreign Currency Account (FCA), the proceeds must be sold/converted to ZAR (surrendered) to an Authorised Dealer within 30 days, unless retained under the FCA export retention allowance (up to 100% for bona fide exporters with an export permit code). Every export transaction must be supported by a SARS customs declaration (SAD500), commercial invoice, and delivery evidence; conversion is executed at the ruling spot rate unless covered by a forward exchange contract.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, with parallel AML/CFT oversight by the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC). Authorised Dealers (licensed commercial banks) execute exchange control on SARB's behalf.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with limited legal scope. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Stripe, Payoneer, Wise Business) are not SARB Authorised Dealers and cannot provide statutory ZAR clearing, convertibility, or exchange-control reporting. They may lawfully operate as non-bank payment facilitators under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 for collection of Amazon marketplace payouts in USD/EUR, but all repatriated funds MUST be routed to a South African Authorised Dealer for surrender and settlement. Stripe operates a licensed South African entity for local card acquiring; Airwallex and Payoneer may hold client funds offshore only. EMIs are therefore acceptable as collection-only rails, not as the primary treasury infrastructure. Their use triggers additional KYC documentation and may complicate the 180-day repatriation timeline if funds remain parked offshore.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident UBO holding shares through an opaque offshore structure (e.g., holding company in Cyprus, HK, or BVI) above the 25% ownership threshold.

Root cause: Non-resident UBO holding shares through an opaque offshore structure (e.g., holding company in Cyprus, HK, or BVI) above the 25% ownership threshold.

Defense evidence: Submit the CIPC issued company register, certificate of incorporation of all intermediate holding entities, full register of members, FIC-compliant UBO declaration (Guidance Note 7), source of wealth statement, 12-month global bank statements, and a notarised group structure summary identifying natural persons controlling the Amazon seller account.
High-frequency, low-ticket card sales profile with elevated chargebacks typical of marketplace retail (Amazon FBA).

Root cause: High-frequency, low-ticket card sales profile with elevated chargebacks typical of marketplace retail (Amazon FBA).

Defense evidence: Provide Amazon Seller Central settlement reports covering at least 3 months, net settlement amount summaries, refund and chargeback ratio below 1%, VAT/sales tax registration evidence, signed marketplace services agreement, and an explanatory memorandum on the FBA fulfilment model showing inventory shipment records.

Root cause: Third-party payments or intermingled personal funds received into the business account from unrelated parties.

Defense evidence: Provide an affidavit from the directors explaining the payment flows, underlying sales orders and invoices, Amazon payout remittance advice, logistics proof (airway bills, customs entries), and counter-party identification documents for any third-party funder.

Non-resident shareholder equity injection: remit via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank account to the ZA (Pty) Ltd Capital Escrow Account held at an Authorised Dealer. The instruction must carry BOP purpose code 'Share Capital Subscription' and reference the board resolution approving the share issue. The Authorised Dealer files Exchange Control Form A and updates the share register; funds become freely usable for operating expenditure only after the bank's compliance release. No prior SARB approval is required for ordinary share capital, but the transaction is reported to FinSurv under the Capital Flow Management framework. If the funds arrive as a shareholder loan in excess of a 3:1 debt-to-equity ratio, prior SARB approval becomes mandatory. For Amazon FBA working capital, a combined 'share capital + subordinated loan' structure with a 70:30 equity bias is recommended to streamline conversion and future dividend repatriation.Clearing rails are executed through the Authorised Dealer network: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments, freight, customs duties, Amazon platform fees, and advertising costs, with BOP purpose codes referencing the underlying proforma invoice; (2) Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 for bulk inventory procurement, covering the exporter of record and shipping documents; (3) ZAR-denominated domestic legs settled through SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement) using MT202 COV; (4) FCA-to-FCA transfers for same-currency payments without ZAR conversion. Gross settlement is mandatory - netting of Amazon payouts against supplier invoices is not permitted. All outward payments must be captured against the company's import permit and supported by SARS customs declarations to avoid exchange-control penalties.Onshore Deliverable Forward Exchange Contract (FEC) with an Authorised Dealer, maximum tenor 12 months (renewable up to 24 months with FinSurv approval), to hedge USD/ZAR Amazon payout conversionsOffshore Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) on ZAR executed through a non-resident counterparty, permissible for non-resident entities and for hedging ZAR exposure where onshore deliverability is impracticalCross-Currency Swap (CCS) between USD and ZAR executed through an Authorised Dealer, subject to FinSurv approval for maturities exceeding 12 monthsZero-Cost Collar (Range Forward) combining a purchased USD call and a written USD put with an Authorised Dealer, permitted for risk management of forecast Amazon sales proceedsCurrency Options purchased (premium paid) onshore or offshore, treated as speculative only if the notional exceeds the underlying FBA receivables; prudential limits apply to keep the hedge in the ordinary course of trade

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with limited legal scope. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Stripe, Payoneer, Wise Business) are not SARB Authorised Dealers and cannot provide statutory ZAR clearing, convertibility, or exchange-control reporting. They may lawfully operate as non-bank payment facilitators under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 for collection of Amazon marketplace payouts in USD/EUR, but all repatriated funds MUST be routed to a South African Authorised Dealer for surrender and settlement. Stripe operates a licensed South African entity for local card acquiring; Airwallex and Payoneer may hold client funds offshore only. EMIs are therefore acceptable as collection-only rails, not as the primary treasury infrastructure. Their use triggers additional KYC documentation and may complicate the 180-day repatriation timeline if funds remain parked offshore.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory Local File / Master File threshold applies to all affected transactions, but ITR14 disclosure is triggered when annual affected transactions with connected persons exceed ZAR 1,000,000. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be maintained for all material cross-border affected transactions; SARS prioritizes cases where the annual aggregate transaction value exceeds ZAR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations filing regime. The enquiry is applied through the residence test, effective-management determination, controlled foreign corporation rules, section 31 transfer pricing, and the general anti-avoidance rules. A Pty Ltd must maintain real commercial substance, local decision-making, board minutes, employment, and bank accounts to support tax residence, DTA access, and arm's length pricing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Under section 234 of the Tax Administration Act, making a false declaration is an offence punishable by a fine and/or imprisonment for a period not exceeding two years. The evasion penalty can include an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the shortfall plus interest, and a criminal conviction may follow under common-law fraud.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil if the Pty Ltd is used fraudulently or as an alter ego. Directors who knowingly participate in false tax returns may be held jointly liable for the company's unpaid tax, receive travel bans and preservation orders, and face imprisonment.
Shell Company or Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the juristic personality, recharacterize the arrangement under sections 80A-80L, include profits in the shareholder's hands under CFC rules, deny treaty relief, and impose an understatement penalty up to 150% or 200% of the tax shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable under section 77 of the Companies Act for carrying on business recklessly or knowingly as a shell, with remedies including civil restitution, disqualification from acting as directors, and travel bans for unpaid SARS debts.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Artificial Related-party Fees

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income to an arm's length amount under section 31, disallow the deduction, and assess interest plus an understatement penalty of 100% to 150% for substantial misstatements. Failure to retain the required documentation can itself attract an administrative penalty.

Director personal liability: A director who signs the return knowing the transfer pricing policy is artificial can be joined in the assessment under the Tax Administration Act, with personal liability for the shortfall, interest, and penalties, and potential criminal prosecution for deliberate misstatement.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 imposes administrative fines up to ZAR 10,000,000 on accountable institutions and directors for serious non-compliance, in addition to criminal penalties for money laundering and possible liquidation proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers responsible for AML compliance face personal fines and imprisonment up to 15 years for knowingly enabling money laundering or failing to report suspicious transactions; corporate veil-piercing and civil recovery may also apply.
