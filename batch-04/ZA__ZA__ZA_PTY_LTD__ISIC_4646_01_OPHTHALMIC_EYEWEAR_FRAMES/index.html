Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); electronic portal: SARS eFiling at https://efiling.sars.gov.za; company registration portal: CIPC BizPortal

E-government filing portal

For a local (Pty) Ltd with foreign shareholders, incorporate with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) through CIPC/BizPortal using a Memorandum of Incorporation and Notice of Incorporation. CIPC data is linked to SARS and an income tax reference number is usually created automatically. If not auto-registered, file income tax registration through SARS eFiling within 21 days. File VAT/VAT vendor registration using VAT 101/ACC01 through SARS eFiling, together with the CIPC registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, directors' identity documents, and proof of physical address in South Africa. For a branch of a foreign company, register as an external company with CIPC, then register with SARS and appoint a local authorised representative. SARS will issue a taxpayer reference number and, where taxable supplies exceed the threshold, a VAT vendor number.

Available election forms

Not applicable – South Africa has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; a (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily a separate taxable company under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Available targeted elections include the Headquarter Company election under section 9I for qualifying multinational holding companies, the research-and-development allowance election under section 11D, and certain roll-over reliefs under sections 41-47 (e.g. asset-for-share, amalgamation) where a joint election is filed. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted from ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies. A non-resident supplying electronic services to South African customers must register for VAT if total taxable supplies from such services exceed ZAR 1,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from South Africa are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the supplier exports the goods in the ordinary course of its trade, the goods are removed to a foreign destination within the prescribed time period (usually within 90 days), and the supplier retains documentary proof of export. Services supplied to non-residents and consumed wholly outside South Africa may be zero-rated under section 11(2)(l). A wholesale exporter of acetate eyeglass frames must hold a completed SARS Customs export declaration (SAD500), transport documents evidencing physical removal from SACU, and proof of payment or foreign-currency receipt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged on SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (where export proceeds are received through authorised foreign-exchange channels from outside South Africa)
  • Transport/shipping documentation (truck/rail/sea/air manifests)
  • Proof of receipt of export proceeds (bank inward/swift credit advice)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate is in force in South Africa. Tax invoices must satisfy section 20 of the VAT Act (supplier name and VAT number, invoice number, date, description, quantity, price and VAT amount); electronic tax invoices are valid if the integrity and readability of the invoice are preserved. VAT returns are filed electronically through SARS eFiling using VAT 201 returns on a monthly or two-monthly cycle, with SARS automated risk/audit referral and no statutory requirement for a pre-clearance API.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict SARS audit scrutiny. Where export conditions are not met, SARS may re-assess the supply at 15% VAT with statutory interest and claw back input tax previously deducted. Failure to produce prescribed export documentation exposes the vendor to input/output VAT disallowance, understatement penalties under section 223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (up to 200% depending on conduct), administrative non-compliance penalties, and criminal sanctions where zero-rating is claimed fraudulently. SARS may raise a revised assessment beyond the ordinary five-year prescription period where fraud, misrepresentation or non-disclosure is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a dual-tier exchange control framework. The current account—including wholesale importation and distribution of ophthalmic eyewear frames, professional fees, and trade-related service payments—is effectively convertible for residents and non-residents, subject to documentary substantiation, arm's-length pricing, and adherence to prescribed reporting requirements. The capital account is controlled. Non-resident equity investments into a South African company are admissible through Authorised Dealers with streamlined notification for ordinary share capital, but disinvestment and share buy-backs require tax directives from SARS and may require FinSurv approval where thresholds apply. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are freely permitted provided the dividends are paid out of verified after-tax profits, the company is fully tax compliant, and the remittance is effected through an Authorised Dealer using the prescribed Form A.2. Profit remittances are not subject to exchange-control approval for ordinary dividend flows, but any payment that represents hidden capital or interest on shareholder loans must be separately assessed and may trigger transfer-pricing review under Section 31 of the Income Tax Act, No. 58 of 1962.

Export proceeds surrender rules

This entity's primary trade flow is importation and domestic wholesale distribution of eyeglass frames; therefore, the statutory surrender regime is not triggered unless the company subsequently re-exports frames. For any future export transactions, a South African resident exporter must surrender foreign currency proceeds to an Authorised Dealer within 30 days of shipment or by the contractual payment due date, whichever is earlier, and must convert or credit the full amount into rand or an approved Foreign Currency Account within 30 days of receipt. There is no prescribed minimum conversion ratio because all foreign currency received without special approval must be sold to an Authorised Dealer or retained only in a FinSurv-approved Foreign Currency Account. A 100% mandatory conversion is therefore the operational baseline; exceptions require a formal exchange-control application and specific approval. The exporter must also complete and submit the applicable Form B.1 or digital equivalent, retain supporting shipping and customs documentation, and report all export proceeds on the Authorised Dealer's periodic exchange-control return.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the delegated Exchange Control authority, together with Authorised Dealers (domestic commercial banks duly appointed under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933, and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961).

Credits claimed only for foreign taxes on income also taxable in South Africa. s213 TAA penalty of 10% of the unpaid port of the provisional tax; interest at the prescribed rate; s214 TAA underestimation penalties if the estimate is deliberately or negligently too low; accountability applies to the public officer and directors.
Line 5 - Estimated Tax Payable Estimated normal tax after credits; the basis for first and second provisional payments. PE tax payable computed in the same manner. s213 TAA penalty of 10% of the unpaid port of the provisional tax; interest at the prescribed rate; s214 TAA underestimation penalties if the estimate is deliberately or negligently too low; accountability applies to the public officer and directors.
Line 6 - First Provisional Payment Amount payable within 6 months of the start of the year; typically 50% of the estimated tax under the basic minimum. PE must make the payment via the same SARS banking details. s213 TAA penalty of 10% of the unpaid port of the provisional tax; interest at the prescribed rate; s214 TAA underestimation penalties if the estimate is deliberately or negligently too low; accountability applies to the public officer and directors.
Line 7 - Second Provisional Payment Balance payable on or before the last day of the year of assessment; must bring cumulative provisional payments to at least 90% of actual liability (or 100% for SBCs). PE must meet the same top-up requirement. s213 TAA penalty of 10% of the unpaid port of the provisional tax; interest at the prescribed rate; s214 TAA underestimation penalties if the estimate is deliberately or negligently too low; accountability applies to the public officer and directors.
