Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal: https://www.sars.gov.za (eFiling: https://efiling.sars.gov.za); customs and excise front-end: SARS Customs (SAD500 via e@syFile/Web); Exchange control administered by the South African Reserve Bank (SARB).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity must first register as a 'foreign company' with CIPC and then register for income tax with SARS by submitting the IT77/Corporate Registration form through eFiling or a SARS branch, providing the certificate of incorporation, MOI, director passports, and proof of local address; SARS issues an Income Tax Reference (IT) number. For VAT, a separate application via VAT 101 (or eFiling 'Register for VAT') must be submitted with bank confirmation, business details, and turnover projection; a VAT vendor number (VAT number) is issued. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services must register through the SARS 'Foreign Supplier VAT' eFiling portal when annual taxable supplies exceed the ZAR 1,000,000 threshold; processing typically completes within 21 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a local (Pty) Ltd is a resident company taxed on worldwide income at the standard corporate rate (27% for years ending on/after 31 March 2023, transitional arrangement for March 2023); territorial sourcing rules apply only to non-resident companies; no automatic pass-through; Special Economic Zone incentives (15% rate) and R&D allowances available only upon separate SARS approval.

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Statutory elections available include: VAT grouping (mini-group registration) under section 50 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991; election to account for VAT on the invoice basis vs. payment basis under section 15; election to export goods for VAT zero-rating with SARS approval under section 11(3); CFC elections under section 9D of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; hybrid mismatch elections and reporting forms per sections 8I-8L (the anti-hybrid framework follows BEPS Action 2); and voluntary registration under section 23(3) of the VAT Act with the permitted ZAR 50,000 turnover threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory registration where annual taxable supply turnover exceeds or is likely to exceed this amount in a 12-month period); voluntary registration available at ZAR 50,000; non-resident suppliers of electronic services threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 annual taxable turnover from South African supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods is available under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods are supplied on consignment to the purchaser and are delivered out of South Africa by the supplier or at the supplier's direction (including cross-border e-commerce pet supplies), provided the goods leave South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply; zero-rating for exported services is available under section 11(2)(l) where the services are physically rendered outside South Africa (or deemed export relief applies); the supplier must retain documentary proof of physical export and of the commercial transaction; input VAT attributable to exported supplies is fully creditable, and refunds of excess input VAT may be claimed via the VAT 201 return with SARS supporting-documentation requirements; distributors supplying goods to an exporter as the subsequent export limb may also claim zero-rating under section 11(1)(b) if the exporter is the principal in the export and the supplier obtains the section 11(3) undertaking.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / da 500) stamped by SARS Customs from the point of departure
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or carrier's receipt evidencing the movement of goods out of South Africa
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and proof of ownership/beneficial interest in the exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-issued) or bank statement showing the foreign currency receipt and conversion to ZAR
  • Evidence of delivery into the buyer's custody (last-mile courier tracking/POD for e-commerce parcels)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or online B2B/B2C API clearance mandate is currently in force in South Africa. SARS eFiling is compulsory for VAT return submission and for tax invoice generation by registered vendors using the eFiling portal; full tax invoices must comply with section 20 of the VAT Act (vendor name and VAT number, sequential invoice number, date, description, value, VAT amount, rate, and consideration stating whether inclusive or exclusive of VAT); electronic tax invoices are acceptable without prior SARS approval provided the integrity and authenticity controls are retained; SARS has formally commenced a phased rollout of OECD-standard continuous transaction controls (e-invoicing via a central clearing model) with voluntary pilot participation from 2025, but no mandatory real-time clearance is yet effective.

SARS cross-matches the SAD500 customs declarations with the VAT 201 returns, transport waybills, and bank FX receipt registers to verify physical export; where zero-rated export supplies are found to be fictitious, unexported, defective, or permanently landed in South Africa, SARS will disallow the zero rating and assess VAT at the standard 15% plus additional tax (section 60 of the VAT Act, potentially up to 200% of the tax shortfall); understatement penalties under sections 222-223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 range from 0% (bona fide inadvertent error) to 25% (substantial non-disclosure), 50% (gross negligence), and 100%-200% (intentional tax evasion) of the shortfall; criminal prosecution under section 58 of the VAT Act and section 235 of the Tax Administration Act may result in fines or imprisonment of up to five years; retrospective assessments may extend back five years for non-disclosure cases, and fraudulent claims trigger immediate penalty interest under section 89 quat of the Income Tax Act absorbed into the VAT assessment framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) operates under a managed float with exchange control administered by SARB FinSurv. Current-account transactions such as trade, interest, and profit remittances are generally free and processed by Authorised Dealers under simplified documentary verification. Capital-account transactions, including foreign equity investment, inbound loans, and offshore guarantees, require prior approval via Authorised Dealers or SARB FinSurv where values exceed delegated thresholds. Non-resident shareholders may remit dividends only after obtaining a SARS tax clearance certificate and using prescribed banking channels. The ZAR is not a fully externally convertible currency; inward equity capital must be recorded through a 'Form A' submission and offshore transfers must be documented under the SARB Balance-of-Payments (BOP) framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be received in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless a formal extension is granted. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio into ZAR; however, the full export value must be repatriated onshore and surrendered to the authorised banking system either by conversion or by crediting a Foreign Currency Account held with a South African bank. Exporters must retain supporting documentation (bill of lading, commercial invoice, payment receipts) for exchange-control inspection. Failure to timeously surrender/repatriate export receipts is a criminal offence under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Medium with strict legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, or similar) are not licensed as SARB Authorised Dealers and therefore cannot independently open local ZAR-denominated accounts or process domestic settlement for a South African resident company. Such platforms are viable as auxiliary channels for collecting inbound card payments from foreign customers (via PayPal, Stripe, etc.) when paired with a licensed local bank that receives the aggregate settlement. The key regulatory boundaries are: SARB exchange-control requires all resident funds to remain within the formal South African financial system; FICA data-residency and access rules restrict offshore fintechs; and any offshore EMI maintaining ZAR accounts for South African residents would require a banking licence and Authorised Dealer status.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from non-resident shareholders must be routed through an Authorised Dealer in South Africa. The initiating bank must send a SWIFT MT103 to the South African beneficiary bank, quoting the company's CIPC registration number, the intended purpose 'Equity Capital Contribution – Subscription for Shares', and the bank's Authorised Dealer reference. The South African receiving entity must, within 21 days, complete and lodge SARB 'Form A' to formalise the inward FDI. Verification pack: final board resolution, share subscription agreement, CIPC share allotment return, SWIFT confirmation, and the authorised dealer's Form A acceptance stamp.For settlement to overseas suppliers, the company may use SWIFT MT103 direct bank transfers for standard procurements and documentary commercial letters of credit (LC) for high-value or deferred shipments. Every outward payment must be supported by a supplier commercial invoice and, where applicable, a transport document (bill of lading or airway bill) to enable the Authorised Dealer to release the foreign currency under the current-account exemption. Open-account dispatch is permitted when the counterparty is creditworthy and the payment is supported by bank statements and purchase orders. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the aggregate consideration for covered transactions (related-party cross-border and domestic transactions) exceeds ZAR 5 million for the relevant year of assessment. Master File is mandatory where the multinational group of which the South African entity forms part has consolidated group revenue exceeding ZAR 1 billion and the South African entity has covered transactions above the Local File threshold. Country-by-Country Report filing applies where the multinational group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds ZAR 10 billion. All transfer-pricing documentation must be contemporaneous and produced to SARS within the period specified in a s28 Tax Administration Act information request.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a codified Economic Substance Regulations regime similar to the UAE, but economic substance is a decisive compliance factor enforced through (1) the Controlled Foreign Company attribution rules in s9D, which impute passive and non-substantive income of a CFC to South African shareholders; (2) the general anti-avoidance rules in ss80A-80L, which empower SARS to disregard or void arrangements lacking commercial substance and re-characterise tax consequences; (3) the s31 transfer-pricing rules requiring profit allocation to reflect actual economic functions, assets and risks; and (4) the withdrawal of SA-China and other treaty benefits for treaty-shopping structures under the principal-purpose test. This (Pty) Ltd must maintain genuine local substance for its e-commerce model, including local board control, employment contracts, operational management decisions, fulfilment/logistics contracts concluded in South Africa, South African bank accounts and payment-processing agreements, and complete books and records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Suppression of E-commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction on a charge under s235 of the Tax Administration Act carries a fine of up to ZAR 60,000 or double the tax evaded, and/or imprisonment of up to 10 years. Civil consequences include additional assessment, a 10% late-payment penalty, statutory interest at the prescribed rate compounded monthly, and a s223 understatement penalty of up to 75% of the shortfall for intentional tax avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company tax debt where SARS has issued a s159 demand and the company fails to pay within 14 days; the court may declare directors delinquent under s162 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008, prohibit them from holding office, issue travel restrictions pending collection, and impose imprisonment for a criminal evasion conviction.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke ss80A-80L to disregard or void the impermissible avoidance arrangement and re-determine taxable income as if the arrangement had not been entered into; a transfer-pricing adjustment under s31 will apply at the arm's length amount; CFC attribution under s9D may impute foreign profits to the South African company; and the CIPC may deregister the company for failure to file annual returns, causing forfeiture of its capacity to trade.

Director personal liability: Directors face joint and several liability for the resulting tax assessments under s159 TAA after a 14-day demand; the court may issue a delinquency order under the Companies Act, prohibit departure from South Africa, and where the sham structure is used to conceal taxable income, criminal liability follows on the same basis as tax evasion.
