Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official portal: https://www.sars.gov.za and eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), company registration data is electronically transmitted to SARS for automatic income tax reference number issuance; if no number is issued, the appointed representative must register via SARS eFiling using the 'New Company Registration' workflow. For VAT, submit a VAT 101 application through eFiling or at a SARS branch, attaching the CIPC incorporation certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, financial projections, authorised representative particulars, and supporting evidence of trading premises; SARS will issue a VAT vendor number with an assigned filing cycle upon acceptance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A resident Pty Ltd is an opaque, separate taxpayer assessed on worldwide income; no pass-through status is available. Territorial Scope is not applicable to resident companies; non-residents are taxed on South African-source income. Tax-exempt zone status does not apply.

Available election forms

None: South Africa has no check-the-box election or elective entity classification regime. A Pty Ltd is statutorily treated as an opaque taxpayer under the Income Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch rules operate through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (section 103), the 'hybrid debt' reclassification rules in sections 8E/8F, and BEPS Action 2 domestic measures; there are no elective hybrid mismatch elections. Certain ordinary tax elections (e.g., R&D allowance under section 11D, turnover tax under Schedule 1 for micro enterprises, or minimum tax exemptions) do not alter entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies) exceeded ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) goods physically exported from South Africa under a sale or consignment agreement, provided the exporter retains prescribed documentary proof of export within the required timeframes; (ii) services supplied to a non-resident that are not connected to South African land or movable property and are consumed wholly outside South Africa, in accordance with section 11(2)(k) of the VAT Act. For an AI/LLM API platform, global API subscription and inference services rendered to foreign non-resident customers may qualify as zero-rated where the customer is outside South Africa at the time of supply, the service is not performed or utilised in South Africa, and payment is receivable from outside South Africa; the supplier must hold sufficient evidence of the customer's non-resident status and overseas payment source. Special deemed supply rules may apply to certain consumer-facing digital services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500/SAD501) lodged with SARS Customs and the South African Revenue Service
  • Transport document evidencing export (Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Other multimodal transport document issued by a recognised carrier)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or foreign currency payment instruction / letter of credit / SWIFT proof of receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer)

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-linked invoice clearance. Taxpayers may issue electronic VAT invoices provided all prescribed particulars are retained (seller name/address/VAT number, invoice date, description, quantity/value, VAT amount). SARS eFiling is used for electronic VAT return submission, not invoice clearance. SARS is progressively developing a PEPPOL-based e-invoicing roadmap, but no mandatory real-time clearance mandate is in force at this stage.

SARS may require proof of actual export and receipt of foreign currency. Failure to timeously receive export proceeds within 180 days (without SARB approval) causes the supply to be deemed not zero-rated, resulting in VAT clawback with interest and additional tax under section 60 of the VAT Act. Fraudulent or negligent zero-rating claims attract understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act up to 150% of the tax shortfall for intentional tax evasion or gross negligence, or up to 50% for unreasonable tax positions. Criminal prosecution and director liability may follow for deliberate evasion. SARS is empowered to audit supporting documentation and to issue adverse tax assessments and collection enforcement measures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a managed floating exchange rate regime. Current-account transactions are fully convertible for bona fide trade in goods and services, including profit and dividend remittances, subject to underlying documentation and applicable tax clearances. Capital-account flows are less automatic: inward equity investments, shareholder loans, and outward direct investments must be routed through an authorised dealer and, where applicable, approved or recorded with FinSurv. Non-residents may freely repatriate capital after demonstrating the original compliant introduction of funds. The rand (ZAR) is not deliverable offshore in an unrestricted NDF market; onshore conversion is permitted through authorised dealers without quantitative limits for genuine transactional demand.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation and the Currency and Exchanges Manual, export proceeds from goods must be received into a South African authorised dealer within 180 calendar days from the date of shipment. For exported services, the exporter must receive payment within 30 calendar days after the contractual due date, and in any event no later than 180 days from the date of invoice. There is no mandatory percentage conversion or surrender ratio to ZAR; the exporter may either convert the foreign currency to ZAR or credit the full proceeds to a foreign currency account (FCA) held with an authorised dealer. The full export invoice value must be repatriated, and any shortfall or extension beyond the statutory window requires a FinSurv-approved application or authorised-dealer exemption documented on the relevant exchange-control form.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Exchange Control Regulations and the Currency and Exchanges Manual, with delegated administration to authorised dealer commercial banks and enforcement through the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) for AML/CFT obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-to-Low for offshore digital EMI banking as the primary treasury account because SARB exchange control requires export proceeds and investment capital to be routed through a local authorised dealer. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, etc.) do not provide a South African ZAR settlement account and cannot substitute for a domestic FICA-compliant bank account. They may be used as supplementary collection vehicles for small recurring subscriptions if all funds are periodically swept into the onshore authorised-dealer account with full audit trails. The use of USDC/cryptocurrency treasury rails is lawful only where conversion to fiat is executed through an FSCA-licensed Crypto Asset Service Provider (CASP), followed by onshore reconciliation and SARB reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must transfer funds to the South African company's authorised dealer by SWIFT MT103, using a purpose code reflecting direct equity investment (e.g. ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQTY' or the dealer's internal 'Capital Contribution / Share Subscription' code). The remitter should reference the share subscription agreement and the company's CIPC registration number. The receiving bank will require a completed Form A (inward investment declaration) in accordance with Exchange Control, the final board resolution approving the subscription, certified identification of the investor, and confirmation that the funds originate from a non-sanctioned source. All capital funds must initially be credited to a segregated capital escrow/conversion account and may not be used for operating expenses until shares are issued and the bank verifies the underlying share register. The bank must report the inward FDI to SARB's electronic exchange-control monitoring system; verification includes reconciliation against the constitutional documents and a negative media and sanctions search.Outbound trade and service disbursements are processed by an authorised dealer via SWIFT MT103 (direct payment) and, for significant procurement, through a commercial letter of credit (LC). For imports of goods, the importer must complete the bank's exchange-control application (typically a Form C) and provide the supplier's commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, and customs import declaration (SAD500) prior to payment. For pure services transactions, underlying service contracts and invoices must be retained. A commercial LC is issued under UCP 600 using MT700/MT710 messages, with examination of shipping documents and reimbursement through MT202; all payments must be allocated to the appropriate balance-of-payments purpose code. The use of an offshore EMI or CASP to execute outbound settlement is not permissible as the primary rail unless FinSurv has granted an explicit exemption, because South African law requires cross-border trade payments to flow through the authorised-dealer banking sector.Deliverable foreign exchange forward contracts (FECs) with a South African authorised dealer for firm and anticipated trade, service, or capital commitments.Cross-currency swaps (ZAR/USD, ZAR/EUR, ZAR/GBP) for hedging long-term foreign-currency borrowing or investment obligations.Plain-vanilla FX call/put options purchased from an authorised dealer to hedge contingent transactional exposure.Rollover and currency swap of existing foreign currency loans, subject to underlying supporting trade documentation and authorised-dealer approval.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible as an onshore resident hedging tool because ZAR is a deliverable currency; offshore NDF contracts are reserved for non-resident counterparties and may not be settled by a South African treasury without FinSurv exemption.

Turnover: A statutory audit by an IRBA-registered auditor is compulsory for: (i) public companies and state-owned companies; (ii) private companies whose Public Interest Score (PIS) is at least 500; and (iii) companies whose MOI or shareholder resolution requires audit. An independent review by an IRBA-registered accountant is mandatory where PIS is 350–499. A private company is exempt from both audit and review where PIS is below 350 and all shareholders are directors (Companies Regulation 28(3)). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold is published for transfer pricing documentation. However, as a practical and compliance benchmark, SARS expects local entities to maintain documentation for all cross-border related-party transactions exceeding ZAR 5 million in a year. Multinational groups with consolidated revenue equal to or greater than ZAR 10 billion are required to submit Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting, and those groups are expected to have Local and Master Files available on request.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have a separate 'Economic Substance Regulations' statute like some other jurisdictions. Instead, the substance concept is applied through the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in Section 80A-L of the Income Tax Act, the transfer pricing carve-out in Section 31, the controlled foreign company (CFC) rules, and the determination of residence based on effective management. A company without genuine decision-making, employees, and operational substance in South Africa may have its arrangements disregarded or may be treated as a shell for treaty purposes, leading to additional assessments, penalties, and interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction for tax evasion may result in a fine of up to ZAR 100,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. The civil understatement penalty may be up to 200% of the tax shortfall, plus monthly interest at the prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable under Section 22 of the Companies Act for reckless trading, or under Section 77 for breach of fiduciary duties, if they knowingly caused the company to file false returns or concealed taxable turnover from SARS.
Shell Company / Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the GAAR, SARS may disregard the structure, recharacterise the income, and assess tax at the full 27% rate on South African-source revenue. An understatement penalty of up to 200% and interest may be added; CFC rules may attribute the hidden profits to South African shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise or cooperate in creating such artificialities without an underlying bona fide trade may be personally liable for the additional taxes, interest, and penalties imposed on the company, and may face claims by SARS as accessory parties.
AML / FICA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FICA imposes a fine of up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment for up to 15 years for non-compliance. Administrative penalties can be issued alongside criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors are required to ensure that anti-money laundering controls are embedded in the company's governance. If they knowingly fail to implement FICA compliance, they may be prosecuted individually and fined up to ZAR 10 million, serve a prison sentence, and be disqualified from serving as directors of any company in South Africa.
