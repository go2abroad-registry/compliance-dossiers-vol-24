Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — regulatory portal: SARS eFiling (www.sarsefiling.co.za); proposed taxpayer registration and electronic filing mandate is administered through the SARS branch network and eFiling API interface.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity deriving South African source income or conducting an enterprise through a South African Permanent Establishment must first register as a taxpayer via SARS eFiling (ITA application for Income Tax reference number) or on prescribed SARS forms (e.g., IT77 for companies). For VAT, Form VAT 101 must be submitted within 21 days of reaching the R1,000,000 compulsory turnover threshold; voluntary registration is available from R50,000. Supporting documents include CIPC incorporation certificate (or equivalent foreign registration), memorandum of incorporation, directors’ IDs/passports, proof of local address or registered office, and Power of Attorney for a SARS-accredited tax practitioner or representative company. SARS may require additional non-resident shareholder and beneficial ownership declarations. Separate registration as an importer/exporter through the SARS Customs division and acquisition of a customs client reference code are mandatory for physical export flows.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (2023/24 rate); the entity is a separate taxpayer without a statutory pass-through election. Foreign direct investments are generally subject to entity-level South African income tax on South African-source income, with Controlled Foreign Company attribution rules applying to residents. Non-resident shareholders receive dividends subject to Dividends Tax at 20% unless reduced by a tax treaty; no branch-level remittance tax is levied on the repatriated branch profits. — A foreign-incorporated entity deriving South African source income or conducting an enterprise through a South African Permanent Establishment must first register as a taxpayer via SARS eFiling (ITA application for Income Tax reference number) or on prescribed SARS forms (e.g., IT77 for companies). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (2023/24 rate); the entity is a separate taxpayer without a statutory pass-through election. Foreign direct investments are generally subject to entity-level South African income tax on South African-source income, with Controlled Foreign Company attribution rules applying to residents. Non-resident shareholders receive dividends subject to Dividends Tax at 20% unless reduced by a tax treaty; no branch-level remittance tax is levied on the repatriated branch profits.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election; tax classification is statutory. Relevant domestic elections include: Capex/VAT input apportionment elections under the VAT Act (s17); CFC timing elections under s9H Income Tax Act; deemed headquarter company election (s9I) for qualifying HQ companies; amalgamation corporate relief elections (s44); group restructuring rollover elections (s45); s23M hybrid-interest limitation elections for connected persons; and thin capitalisation/excess debt anti-avoidance rules limiting interest deductions without an elective safe harbour election. Hybrid mismatch controls under s23M and the interest limitation of s23N (2023 onwards) operate as mandatory anti-avoidance rules, not elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration ZAR 1,000,000 taxable supplies per 12-month rolling period; voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000 taxable supplies for entities carrying on an enterprise in South Africa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act if supplied to a non-resident (or refund/resale arrangement) and the goods are physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the tax invoice, and the supplier retains prescribed evidence of export. Services are zero-rated under section 11(3) where the recipient is a non-resident and outside South Africa at the time of supply, the services are not in respect of land/movable property situated in South Africa, and the services are not rendered to a resident of South Africa; the supplier must also secure receipt of the consideration into South Africa in accordance with exchange-control regulations. Exports to SACU countries (Botswana, Lesotho, Namibia, Eswatini) are generally not zero-rated unless the goods are physically removed from South Africa and supplied in circumstances sanctioned by the VAT Act; such intra-SACU supplies may be treated as exempt or standard-rated depending on the specific VAT treatment in the importing customs area.

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration (bill of entry) accepted by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR road consignment note or postal export receipt
  • SARS VAT 264 (export proof) or prescribed alternative export evidence in terms of s11 VAT Act
  • Inward foreign exchange remittance / bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and insurance/weight certificate
  • Any applicable export permit or restricted-goods authorization

No mandatory government real-time fiscal clearance or pre-issuance 'digital stamping' regime is currently imposed on South African VAT invoices. SARS eFiling remains the statutory submission portal, and the SARS claims/refund system applies automated risk rules before releasing VAT refunds. SARS has commenced a phased PEPPOL-compliant e-invoicing pilot programme (2023-2025) building toward mandatory e-invoicing for procurement and tax clearance, but full statutory digital clearance and API-based invoice validation are not yet in force. VAT tax invoices must comply with s20 of the VAT Act and contain the prescribed particulars; automated electronic credit notes must concurrently reflect cancellations. Export evidence must be retained in prescribed form (VAT 264 / s11 evidence) and produced upon SARS audit demand.

SARS may reclassify zero-rated supplies as standard-rated where evidence of physical export is incomplete, resulting in additional VAT of 15% on the invoice value plus interest under the Tax Administration Act. Understatement penalties (TAA s222) may be raised up to 200% of the tax shortfall where the zero-rating claim is based on intentional tax evasion or gross negligence; reckless or fraudulent export claims expose directors to joint liability and criminal prosecution. VAT refunds electronically earmarked for audit are withheld pending SARS verification inspection. Retrospective clawback applies from the date of supply (not later than the deduction/recovery period), and customs penalties may separately apply for non-compliance with SAD500 export documentation and foreign-exchange repatriation requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is not fully convertible. Current account transactions are generally unrestricted and may be processed through authorised dealers on a documentary basis, but capital account transactions are subject to exchange control surveillance and require either approval or strict compliance by the authorised dealer. Dividends and other profits from equity investments are remittable in convertible foreign currency provided the company is tax-compliant, the shareholding is registered correctly, and the applicable Foreign Investment Reference Number has been obtained where required. Non-resident shareholders should ensure that all inward equity capital is reported through an authorised dealer to maintain future repatriation rights. SMART IoT hardware retail is a standard low-risk current-account business; no special convertibility licence is required for trade-related profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment unless an extension is granted by SARB FinSurv. On receipt, 100% of the foreign exchange proceeds are subject to surrender to an authorised dealer in the prescribed manner, and the exporter must sell such foreign currency to the authorised dealer within 30 days of acquisition unless the exporter has obtained an applicable exemption. Payment must be in a freely convertible currency, and the underlying export documents (commercial invoice, bill of lading, and customs declarations) must be retained for audit by the authorised dealer and SARB FinSurv.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, and the Exchange Control Regulations, with authorised dealer banks administering day-to-day control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as primary account, MODERATE as a supplementary non-ZAR treasury rail. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital banks without a South African banking licence are not permitted to accept ZAR deposits or provide onshore corporate payment accounts that are eligible for SARB exchange control reporting. They are viable only for receiving foreign-currency customer payments outside South Africa, provided they comply with the FIC Act obligations, are registered with the Financial Intelligence Centre where applicable, and cannot serve as the statutory corporate bank account for issuing shares or receiving inward equity capital. A licensed domestic authorised dealer is legally required as the principal clearing bank for ZAR liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward equity capital should be injected by non-resident shareholders via SWIFT MT103 or another authorised international wire through a South African authorised dealer bank, using the corporate operational account designated for inward foreign direct investment. The settlement submission must be accompanied by the share subscription agreement, board shareholder resolution, updated CIPC beneficial ownership register, and, where required, the South African Reserve Bank Foreign Investment Reference Number for future profit and capital remittance. The authorised dealer will validate the source of funds, apply the applicable Balance of Payments purpose code for inward direct investment into an entity resident in South Africa, and complete the mandatory SARB FinSurv reporting.
