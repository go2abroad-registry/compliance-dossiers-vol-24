Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — official portal: SARS eFiling at https://www.sars.gov.za; physical registrations conducted at SARS branch offices nationwide.

E-government filing portal

A (Pty) Ltd incorporated under the South African Companies Act is automatically profiled by SARS for income tax upon receipt of CIPC incorporation data; the entity must activate the profile and obtain its Income Tax Reference Number via SARS eFiling within 21 business days of incorporation. Foreign-owned companies without local incorporation must appoint a representative taxpayer under s153 of the Tax Administration Act and obtain a non-resident income tax reference number. VAT registration: compulsory when taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration available where taxable supplies exceeded ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12 months. Registration is completed via SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using the CIPC registration number and supporting constitutional documents; a VAT vendor number is issued upon successful registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African-resident private company (Pty) Ltd is taxed at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (years of assessment ending on or after 1 April 2023) on worldwide income under the residence-based tax system; non-resident shareholders receive dividends subject to 20% dividends tax; no pass-through treatment is available; separate legal entity taxation applies with no group fiscal consolidation.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under South African law. Available statutory elections include: (1) Micro Business Turnover Tax election under the Sixth Schedule s4(2) for qualifying small businesses with annual turnover not exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 — irrevocable for two years and replacing normal income tax, VAT and dividends tax; (2) CFC high-tax election under s9D(9B) of the Income Tax Act to exclude a controlled foreign company's foreign income where effectively subject to tax in a foreign jurisdiction; (3) voluntary VAT registration election under s23(3) of the VAT Act; (4) hybrid mismatch arrangement adjustment elections under s8I–s8M with mandatory reporting; (5) s11(a) timing elections for certain deductible expenditure; (6) electronic services supplier VAT registration election. Entity classification itself is not elective and follows the statutory residence test under s1 of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period or reasonably expected to be exceeded in the following 12 months; Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 taxable supplies in the preceding 12-month period. Foreign suppliers of electronic services (including cloud-based no-code site builders) have no minimum turnover threshold and must register for VAT from the first supply of electronic services to South African residents under s54A of the VAT Act and the Electronic Services Regulations, unless wholly exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods are supplied to a non-resident and physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply, supported by prescribed export documentation. Services, including software subscriptions, low-code platform access, and website builder services supplied to non-residents, qualify for zero-rating under s11(2)(l) if the recipient is outside South Africa and the services are not supplied for use or consumption in South Africa. Zero-rating does not apply to electronic services supplied to South African residents; those are subject to VAT at 15% under s54A with the place of supply determined by the recipient's usual residence, payment method, IP address, bank details, and SIM card country. The business must obtain documentary proof of the non-resident status of each customer and the foreign destination/use of the service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) electronically lodged via SARS Customs EDI/ECS system
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (original or electronic) evidencing physical export of goods
  • Commercial invoice and signed contract of sale indicating export terms and foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank foreign-exchange confirmation evidencing receipt of payment from abroad
  • Proof of foreign currency remittance showing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Delivery, packing, and shipment records confirming the goods left South Africa
  • Where applicable, proof of non-resident status of the service recipient (e.g., foreign registration details, passport, or payment rail confirmation)

SARS imposes mandatory electronic filing through eFiling and SARS API channels for VAT returns, supporting documentation, and customs declarations. SARS is progressively implementing an e-invoicing framework aligned with OECD continuous transaction controls (CTC) and the SARS Modernisation Programme; a phased roll-out of real-time fiscal clearance with cryptographic integrity controls is underway, though universal mandatory real-time fiscal stamping is not yet fully operational. Vendors must issue full tax invoices within 21 days of supply under VAT Act s20, retain all electronic records for five years, and transmit invoice-level and transaction data to SARS upon its third-party data requests. The electronic services regime requires quarterly digital declaration of all supplies to South African recipients via the SARS eFiling/API channel with data-matching against payment rails (Stripe, Paddle), bank account details, IP addresses, and geolocation.

SARS Customs conducts post-clearance verification and risk-profiling of zero-rated export declarations, cross-matching SAD500 data against VAT returns, bank FX records, and payment rail data. A claim of zero-rating that is unsupported by the prescribed documentation is rejected by SARS, triggering an additional VAT assessment at the standard rate of 15% on the full supply value under VAT Act s38, plus a 10% penalty and prescribed rate interest. Intentional or fraudulent zero-rating claims attract the understatement penalty regime under Tax Administration Act s223 (up to 200% of the shortfall), criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to 60 months under VAT Act s59, deregistration from the VAT vendor roll, and retrospective clawback of refunds claimed together with compounded interest at the statutory prescribed rate. SARS routinely scrutinises export refunds before payment, and incomplete records result in refund suspension and audit.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is freely convertible for current-account transactions (software subscription fees, royalties, dividends, interest), with no SARB prior approval required for bona fide payments made via an Authorised Dealer. Capital-account inflows are liberally permitted: non-resident equity subscription into a South African (Pty) Ltd does not require prior FinSurv approval when the inward transfer is routed through an Authorised Dealer and reported in the eFinSurv system. Remittance hurdles for profits are modest: dividends must be declared out of realised profits, beneficial ownership must be evidenced, the 20% Dividend Withholding Tax (or treaty-reduced rate) must be settled, and the company must maintain a SARS tax compliance status for outward dividend processing; royalties and software licence fees to non-residents follow current-account rules with the applicable royalty withholding tax and supporting agreements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All proceeds from exported services (SaaS licences and no-code subscriptions) must be repatriated to a South African account held with an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the date of invoice or from the date of export performance. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: exporters may retain 100% of export earnings in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with no prescribed maximum retention period, and any conversion to ZAR occurs at the market rate through the Authorised Dealer. Each export receipt must be supported by commercial invoices and contracts and recorded in the bank's export-proceeds reporting return to FinSurv.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, as amended; prudential oversight of authorised dealers rests with the Prudential Authority (PA) of SARB established under the Financial Sector Regulation Act, 2017.

Stripe Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary collections and disbursement rail; Low as a primary banking substrate. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury) are not licensed banks or Authorised Dealers under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, hold no direct domestic clearing access under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998, and cannot provide a FICA-compliant ZAR local account for a South African entity. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (applicable rate for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; standard rate reduced from 28%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% under s 64E of the Income Tax Act, levied on dividends paid by the (Pty) Ltd to non-resident beneficial owners. The company acts as statutory withholding agent, must withhold before payment, and must remit DWT to SARS within 30 days of the dividend payment. Failure to withhold renders the company personally liable for the tax, interest and penalties.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Year of assessment runs 1 March to 28/29 February. First provisional payment and return: on or before the last business day of the 6th month of the assessment year (31 August for a February year-end). Second: on or before the last business day of the year of assessment (28/29 February). Third top-up: within 6 months after year-end (voluntary, recommended to limit interest exposure).

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 must be submitted within 12 months of the end of the year of assessment. SARS eFiling pre-populates and may issue an auto-assessment for certain companies; the taxpayer must verify and finalise within the prescribed window.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory when aggregate 'international related party transactions' (IRP transactions) entered into by the South African company in a year of assessment exceed ZAR 100 million. Master File and Country-by-Country Report: required where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion. The IRP6 provisional return requires a transfer-pricing declaration indicating whether TP documentation applies.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to offshore hubs; however, economic substance is legally embedded in: (1) tax residence (incorporation plus 'place of effective management' under the definition of 'resident' in s 1 ITA); (2) the s 31 transfer-pricing rules requiring a functional analysis of assets, risks and people functions; (3) the CFC regime (s 9D), which attributes passive income to South Africa unless the foreign subsidiary demonstrates adequate local substance; and (4) the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (s 80A–80L), which void 'impermissible avoidance arrangements' lacking commercial substance. Treaty benefits may similarly be denied where a company or intermediary is a mere conduit without substance or qualifies as a non-beneficial owner. A no-code website builder (Pty) Ltd acting as a UAE-route IP holder or payment intermediary without local staff, premises or risk capacity will fail substance scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under s 235 TAA: fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years per count. Civil understatement penalty under s 222–223 TAA: 150% of the shortfall for intentional tax evasion (10%–25% for lesser conduct categories). Compounded daily interest at the prescribed rate attaches to the full assessed shortfall. The company may also be deregistered and its tax clearance certificate withdrawn.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Public Officer who knowingly participated face joint criminal prosecution; SARS may recover unremitted PAYE, DWT, or VAT from directors personally under s 179 TAA; director disqualification under the Companies Act; personal estate sequestration; court-ordered travel bans may be obtained under s 194 TAA for outstanding tax debt.
Reckless Trading / Corporate Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: Companies Act 71 of 2008 s 77(3) read with s 22: court may declare the director personally liable for the company's debts, including SARS claims, without a limitation cap; the corporate veil is piercable under s 20(9) where the company's juristic personality is abused; the transaction may be set aside as a voidable preference under insolvency rules if within 6 months of liquidation.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for company tax debts; disqualification from serving as a director for up to 10 years; sequestration of personal assets; SARS may attach and sell assets personally; a director who deliberately obstructs the collection process may be imprisoned.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the transaction under s 31 TP rules and s 80A–80L GAAR; recomputation at the arm's-length price; denial of interest deductions on interest-free or below-market 'financial assistance' under s 31(3); assessment of 27% corporate income tax on imputed income; 20% DWT on deemed dividends; 15% WHT on deemed royalties/interest; understatement penalties up to 150%; compound interest. The arrangement may also be cleared under the principal purpose test in the applicable treaty, eliminating the reduced withholding rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or executed the sham arrangement are personally liable for the assessed shortfall where the veil is pierced; criminal exposure if false statements or artificial documents were submitted; SARS may issue a notice of civil recovery against directors and connected persons; travel bans and asset freezes are available during investigation, and cross-border mutual assistance procedures may expose assets held abroad.
AML / FICA Non-Compliance and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998 ss 4–6: fine up to ZAR 100 million and/or imprisonment up to 30 years. Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001: administrative sanction up to ZAR 10 million per contravention; criminal penalty for failure to report a suspicious transaction: fine and/or imprisonment up to 15 years. SARS penalty loading of 150% applies where evasion is connected to illicit flows.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and senior managers are personally liable if they knowingly failed to implement AML controls or permitted the business to be used for laundering; FIC administrative fines and criminal sanctions attach personally; bank accounts may be frozen; personal travel bans and extradition are possible under international mutual legal assistance; personal sequestration follows.
