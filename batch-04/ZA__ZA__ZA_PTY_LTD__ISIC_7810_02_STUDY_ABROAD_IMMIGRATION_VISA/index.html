Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is a free-floating currency and is fully convertible for current-account transactions. A (Pty) Ltd engaged in international student placement and immigration advisory may receive and disburse current-account payments freely through an Authorised Dealer without prior FinSurv approval, provided each transaction is accompanied by complete FICA/KYC documentation, correct SARB Balance of Payments (BOP/EDI) purpose codes, and appropriate supporting invoices. Capital-account transactions remain restricted: inward non-resident equity investment above prescribed thresholds, offshore shareholder loans, and any 'loop-structure' or hybrid debt/equity arrangements require FinSurv approval; those below thresholds may proceed through the AD with a post-arrival declaration within the statutory period. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted without FinSurv approval once the original investment has been declared to the AD, but practical hurdles remain: payment of the 20% Dividends Tax (or reduced treaty rate under an applicable double-taxation agreement), satisfaction of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 solvency-and-liquidity test, audited or reviewed financial statements supporting the dividend, a board resolution with dividend declaration, and a complete SARB dividend remittance form plus favourable SARS tax-compliance status where the bank requires it.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the entity as an exporter of services, all foreign-currency proceeds derived from advisory visa and student-placement services rendered to non-residents must be repatriated to South Africa and surrendered to an Authorised Dealer within 180 days of the invoice date or the date of service completion. There is no mandatory percentage conversion into rand: the exporter may convert the full proceeds into ZAR or retain them in a foreign-currency account (FCA) held with the Authorised Dealer. The AD must receive the commercial invoice, evidence of service delivery, the SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation, and the prescribed export/rendering declaration. Proceeds may not be retained in an offshore bank account without prior FinSurv approval, and late repatriation attracts exchange-control penalties and may trigger the AD's enhanced due-diligence review.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, with delegated exchange-control administration granted to Authorised Dealers, being the domestic commercial banks licensed by SARB in terms of the Banks Act, 1990.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW as a primary operating treasury. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) do not hold a South African banking licence and are not Authorised Dealers under Exchange Control Regulation 10; they cannot provide a ZAR-clearable, FICA-compliant corporate account with an SA clearing number, and routing client funds through such providers to bypass the domestic AD network would constitute an exchange-control contravention. Feasibility: MEDIUM only in an ancillary collection-aggregator function where the EMI maintains a formal correspondent partnership with a licensed South African AD and all funds ultimately settle into the entity's SA bank account with a complete audit trail and per-transaction tagging; the entity must disclose the arrangement to its SA bank during onboarding.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Third-party payments from a student's parents, relatives, or corporate sponsors located in a different jurisdiction than the contractual student, creating a layering red flag in tuition and visa-fee collection.

Root cause: Third-party payments from a student's parents, relatives, or corporate sponsors located in a different jurisdiction than the contractual student, creating a layering red flag in tuition and visa-fee collection.

Defense evidence: Signed client engagement agreement and itemised tuition-fee invoice; notarised sponsor affidavit confirming the third-party relationship; proof of family bond or sponsorship mandate (birth/marriage certificate, corporate resolution); source-of-funds packet for each remitter comprising three months of bank statements, salary slips or audited corporate financials, and tax documents; FICA-compliant identity verification of the payer; and a payment-to-invoice reconciliation ledger evidencing no overpayment, split-payment, or refund mismatch.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection: SWIFT MT103/MT202 routed to the South African Authorised Dealer with BOP purpose code 4011 (direct investment in equity capital) and the narrative 'share subscription'; the AD issues an inward-transfer notification and the company files the prescribed Foreign Investment Declaration/EDI return with FinSurv within the statutory timeframe. Shareholder loans: SWIFT MT103 with BOP purpose code 4012 (direct investment in loan capital), supported by a formal shareholder loan agreement with arm's-length terms, a fixed or floating rate, and an express non-conversion undertaking; FinSurv prior approval is required where prescribed thresholds are exceeded or the debt structure carries hybrid features. Client funds for study-abroad and immigration services: must be directed to a separate client-escrow or segmented receivables account using the SARB service-sector BOP category applicable to business advisory services, with each wire reflecting a unique client reference; advance client funds may not be treated as operational liquidity before service performance, and all receipts must be reconciled to signed fee agreements to avoid any inference of unlawful deposit-taking.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for university tuition, visa-authority fees, government immigration levies, and overseas partner commissions; the narrative field must quote the invoice number, a precise purpose description, and the student's unique identifier to prevent beneficiary-bank holds and AML referral. Where the beneficiary bank requires correspondent intermediation, SWIFT MT202 COV with cover is used to segregate instruction from funds. Bank-issued commercial documentary credits are not economically relevant to a service placement model, but irrevocable payment undertakings to a foreign university may be structured as a direct AD instruction with an accompanying FEC. Domestic disbursements to sub-agents and immigration consultants should settle through the South African local clearing system (ACB/Samisoft) and remain tagged by file reference, invoice, and beneficiary identity. A licensed payment-service provider may aggregate low-value disbursements only where the provider is an accountable institution registered with the Financial Intelligence Centre and every underlying transaction remains separately reconstructable by the treasury function.Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) concluded with a South African Authorised Dealer strictly against documented current-account exposure (for example, a contracted and dated tuition-fee liability in USD, GBP, EUR, or AUD); maturities must match the underlying receivable or payable and rollovers require a valid underlying exposure.Structured flexible-forward arrangements offered by the AD in respect of the entity's service invoices and escrow liabilities where the amounts are reasonably determinable and evidencing a firm commercial commitment.Onshore Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not available: South African corporate hedgers are restricted to deliverable onshore FECs quoted against the rand; NDF pricing may be accessed only through an approved offshore treasury conduit, which is ordinarily not granted to a domestic service consultancy.Currency options (vanilla foreign-currency put/call options) and zero-cost collars written by the South African AD on a back-to-back basis are permissible for bona fide transactional exposures, subject to the bank's derivatives suitability assessment under FAIS and the company's internal treasury policy; speculative premium-only or vega-focused option strategies are prohibited.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) are permissible only with prior FinSurv approval and only where the treasury holds a genuine term balance-sheet exposure, such as a long-dated shareholder loan funding the operational footprint; outright speculation through swaps is not permitted.Natural hedging via multi-currency Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) and Foreign Currency Borrowings (FCBs) maintained at the AD enables the entity to match USD, EUR, GBP, and AUD fee inflows against overseas university and partner disbursements, reducing derivative dependency; overnight FCA positions remain subject to SARB position-limits as determined by each Authorised Dealer.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% standard corporate income tax rate for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% for earlier years. A qualifying Small Business Corporation under section 12E is taxed on a graduated scale with an effective 0% to 21% low-income bracket and a 27% marginal rate above the upper threshold.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividend withholding tax under sections 64E to 64N of the Income Tax Act. The tax is imposed on the gross dividend paid by a South African resident company to a non-resident beneficial owner, subject to exemptions in section 64F and any reduced DTA rate. The company, as the withholding agent, remains liable to account to SARS unless a valid declaration and undertaking are held.

The ITR14 must be filed within 12 months of the financial year-end unless SARS stipulates a different deadline on the return. SARS will generate a filing deadline on the eFiling profile and may dispatch a reminder before the due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South African transfer-pricing documentation is generally required for cross-border affected transactions between connected persons where the aggregate value of affected transactions in a year exceeds R5 million. Contemporaneous Local File documentation must evidence comparability, functions, assets and risks, and arm's-length pricing. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated group revenue of R10 billion or more in the preceding year.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply a separate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation filing. However, the tax system enforces substantive commercial activity through the residence test (place of effective management), the controlled-foreign-company rules in section 9D, the transfer-pricing rules in section 31, the general anti-avoidance rules in sections 80A to 80L, and the common-law doctrines of sham and substance over form. A company holding client fees, escrow accounts or visa-processing contracts but lacking premises, employees, business records, director decisions and commercial risk-taking is vulnerable to being recharacterised, reassessed or disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act for tax evasion and false statements, with a fine of up to R2 million per offence and imprisonment of up to five years. SARS may also impose an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the shortfall under section 223, plus interest and seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or instruct the evasion are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, civil restitution orders, director disqualification, bank account freezes, seizure of personal assets and travel bans preventing departure from South Africa.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the arrangement under the substance-over-form doctrine or section 80A GAAR, reattribute the income to the true economic owner, impose transfer-pricing adjustments, deny deductions, and apply a 200% understatement penalty if the arrangement is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell company are jointly and severally liable for the company's taxable debts under the Companies Act where reckless trading, fraud or sham conduct is proven; directors may be disqualified and non-resident directors may face cross-border enforcement and visa restrictions.
Failure to Withhold Dividend, Interest or Royalty Tax

Statutory sanction: SARS may assess the unpaid withholding tax together with interest, a late-payment penalty, and an understatement penalty of up to 200%. Criminal prosecution may follow where the failure is deliberate or grossly negligent.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for withholding taxes that the company collected or should have collected and failed to pay over to SARS; the Tax Administration Act permits SARS to pursue directors who are or should have been aware of the company's non-payment.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the consideration to an arm's-length price under section 31, denies the deduction for the excess, recharacterises the transaction, imposes interest and understatement penalties up to 200%, and may attribute the profit to a permanent establishment of the foreign entity in South Africa.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false transfer-pricing declarations face criminal liability; foreign group directors can be assessed as having a permanent establishment in South Africa, and South African directors can be held personally liable for knowing participation in the artificial profit shift.
AML, FIC and Escrow Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act imposes administrative fines and criminal penalties for failure to register as an accountable institution, failure to report unusual transactions, and money-laundering complicity. The Prevention of Organised Crime Act allows asset forfeiture and custodial sentences for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in money-laundering or client-fund misappropriation are personally liable for restitution, can be criminally prosecuted, are subject to travel bans and extradition, and may be prohibited from managing any company in South Africa.
Exchange Control and Cross-Border Repatriation Abuses

Statutory sanction: Contraventions of the Currency and Exchanges Act and SARB Exchange Control Regulations may result in monetary penalties up to the total value involved, confiscation of the funds, revocation of authorised dealer facilities, and criminal sanctions against the entity and responsible persons.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable to restore unlawfully exported funds; personal assets may be preserved by court order, travel restrictions may be imposed, and non-resident directors may be declared persona non grata by SARS and SARB.
