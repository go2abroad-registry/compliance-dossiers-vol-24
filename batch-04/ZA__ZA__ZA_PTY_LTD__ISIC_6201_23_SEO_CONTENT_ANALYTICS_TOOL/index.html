Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — SARS eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

A South African proprietary limited company is incorporated via CIPC and automatically registered for income tax with SARS, obtaining a company income tax reference number (taxpayer identification number) through SARS eFiling. Foreign entities without a local presence must register with SARS for income tax (where a permanent establishment is created) and separately apply for VAT vendor registration via eFiling once taxable supplies in South Africa exceed the registration threshold. Registration requires company incorporation documentation, director identification, business address, and anticipated turnover declarations; SARS issues an income tax reference number and, if applicable, a VAT registration number. Non-resident vendors of electronic services must register for VAT at a lower threshold as required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A South African proprietary limited company is incorporated via CIPC and automatically registered for income tax with SARS, obtaining a company income tax reference number (taxpayer identification number) through SARS eFiling. Foreign entities without a local presence must register with SARS for income tax (where a permanent establishment is created) and separately apply for VAT vendor registration via eFiling once taxable supplies in South Africa exceed the registration threshold. Registration requires company incorporation documentation, director identification, business address, and anticipated turnover declarations; SARS issues an income tax reference number and, if applicable, a VAT registration number. Non-resident vendors of electronic services must register for VAT at a lower threshold as required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Instead, entities may make statutory elections under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, including: (i) the section 11E election for certain capital recovery claims; (ii) the anti-dividend-stripping and hybrid interest elections under sections 8E and 23N; (iii) the election to apply the South African generally accepted accounting practice for VAT apportionment; and (iv) responsible party elections for tax compliance status. Hybrid mismatch rules align with OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 per annum of taxable supplies (general VAT vendors); foreign non-resident electronic services suppliers must register once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 per annum

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act provided the goods are exported within 60 days of the date of supply and documentary proof of export is retained. Services are zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) when supplied to a non-resident provided the services are not used or consumed in South Africa, and for certain exported services the supply must be paid for in foreign currency. Electronically supplied services to non-residents will qualify if the recipient is outside the common customs area and the services are not consumed locally.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of Foreign Currency Remittance / Inward Remittance Certificate

South Africa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing through SARS. VAT vendors must issue a full tax invoice (or abridged tax invoice for supplies up to ZAR 5,000) and submit VAT returns via SARS eFiling in standard cycles (monthly or every second month). SARS has announced future e-invoicing adoption, but no statutory real-time clearance obligation currently exists.

SARS rigorously audits export zero-rating claims. If an exporter fails to obtain or preserve export documentation, the zero-rating is reversed and VAT at 15% becomes payable, together with interest and penalties under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Deliberate misstatement or fraudulent zero-rating claims attract an additional 150%–200% understatement penalty or an additional tax of up to 150% under section 223, and criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment of up to five years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a managed-float regime with largely liberalised current-account payments and controlled capital-account transactions. Profit remittance by way of dividend distributions is permitted without FinSurv pre-approval provided the dividend is paid from distributable reserves, the Dividend Tax obligation to SARS is satisfied, and payment is executed through an authorised dealer. Capital repatriation, including redemption of share capital or liquidation proceeds, requires FinSurv verification and a tax clearance certificate from SARS. There is no parallel or fixed exchange-rate mechanism for commercial transactions; ZAR is freely deliverable onshore through authorised dealers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Currency and Exchanges Manual, export proceeds for goods and services must be fully received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment or billing, unless an extension is granted by FinSurv. Proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic ZAR bank account or a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an authorised dealer. There is no statutory flat conversion percentage into ZAR; any conversion must be effected through the domestic interbank market at the prevailing market rate. The exporter must retain the customs SAD500 declaration, shipping documents, underlying invoice, and bank evidence of receipt for audit and exchange-control verification.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act (Act No. 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a non-core receivables collection channel; Low as the exclusive or primary treasury rail. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital payment institutions may provide USD/EUR/GBP virtual school or collection accounts, but they are not authorised dealers under South African exchange-control law, may not hold a resident company's ZAR funds offshore in a regulated deposit-taking format, and cannot clear through the South African National Payment System (SAMOS/PayShap). A South African (Pty) Ltd may use such an EMI only as a liquidity aggregation point, with all funds being swept promptly into a locally registered commercial bank account and reported as inbound foreign exchange receipts. Legal constraints include the Banks Act 94 of 1990, the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, and the requirement that any non-resident collection facility must be fully disclosed in the local bank's EDD.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% Dividends Tax (section 64D of the Income Tax Act) levied on the amount of any dividend declared and paid by a South African resident company, including a deemed dividend arising on share buy-backs or capital reduction distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold is codified for Local File / Master File in South Africa; section 31 of the Income Tax Act and section 29 of the Tax Administration Act require any taxpayer entering into 'affected transactions' with non-residents to obtain, retain and make available contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation sufficient to demonstrate that the price is arm's-length. SARS operates a risk-based materiality approach, and the mandatory Country-by-Country Report threshold is ZAR 10 billion consolidated group turnover. The ITR14 includes a transfer pricing self-review box where the taxpayer must confirm that a compliant transfer pricing policy and documentation set is in existence at the time of filing.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime akin to UEA or Bermuda, but substance is rigorously tested through section 31 transfer pricing rules, section 9D controlled foreign company rules (attribution of 'tainted' passive or financial service income where a CFC lacks genuine economic substance), the general anti-avoidance rules under sections 80A to 80T, and judicial doctrines of substance over form. A shell or conduit company that merely holds the SEO analytics IP and routes royalty income through South Africa will be disregarded for tax purposes, with income recharacterised to the substantive economic owner and dividends tax or royalty withholding tax applied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall where the misstatement arises from intentional tax evasion; interest at the prescribed rate from the date of underpayment; criminal prosecution under sections 234 and 235 of the Tax Administration Act with fines up to ZAR 2 million or twice the amount evaded plus imprisonment of up to 5 years; market conduct disqualification from governmental procurement.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in fraudulent non-disclosure are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax under the common-law alter ego doctrine and section 77(3) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008, which permits court orders for civil restitution against directors; personal travel bans and sequestration orders can be obtained by SARS, and a director may face imprisonment for up to 5 years on conviction.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: SARS raises a transfer pricing adjustment under section 31, taxing the difference at 27% corporate income tax; a 25% understatement penalty applies to any substantial understatement without reasonable grounds, rising to 50% for gross negligence and 150% for intentional avoidance; interest accrues on the primary adjustment; reciprocal adjustments are disallowed where the foreign recipient does not cooperate.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or direct transfer pricing documentation that contains deliberately false statements can be joined in the assessment as joint taxpayers under the Tax Administration Act; the corporate veil may be pierced where the subsidiary is found to be the alter ego or a mere conduit, exposing foreign parent directors to civil claims, asset attachment, and exclusion from the Republic.
Shell Company / Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply section 80A to 80T to disregard or recharacterise the shell company, attribute the tainted income to the controlling South African shareholder under section 9D, impose 150% understatement penalties, and subject the monetised IP flows to 20% dividends tax or 15% royalty withholding tax on deemed payments; court-sanctioned liquidation of the shell company can be pursued where insolvent.

Director personal liability: Directors of the South African entity who implement the artificial scheme are personally liable for the corporate tax debt to the extent of their fraudulent or reckless conduct under Companies Act section 77; travel bans and irrevocable civil restitution orders are routinely issued against non-resident directors who use South Africa as a profit-stripping jurisdiction.
AML / Beneficial Ownership and Statutory Reporting Infraction

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to ZAR 10 million under section 68(3) of the Financial Intelligence Centre Act; a separate criminal fine and imprisonment term not exceeding 5 years for continuing breaches; exclusion from licensed payment rails such as Stripe and Paddle for failing to evidence AML compliance; the beneficial owner record will be struck from the CIPC register and administrative penalties of ZAR 1,000 per day may accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for criminal penalties where they knowingly allow the company to operate as an unregistered accountable institution; personal civil fines of up to ZAR 10 million and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years may be imposed; travel bans are applicable where SARS or the FIC obtains a preservation order, and directors are prohibited from holding corporate office in any future South African entity.
