Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign or local private company must apply for an income tax reference number via SARS eFiling or the prescribed registration forms (e.g., IT101 for companies). A public officer ordinarily resident in South Africa must be appointed for all tax compliance matters. For VAT, registration on a compulsory basis is required once the total value of taxable supplies exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available for taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 250,000 but not exceeding ZAR 1,000,000. The entity must submit supporting incorporation documentation, memorandum of incorporation, proof of address, and identification of directors/shareholders. Foreign entities must also provide the SARS with the non-resident parent information and any applicable double-taxation agreement residency evidence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. Tax classification is determined by the legal form under the Companies Act. Pty Ltd companies are automatically taxable as resident companies subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income. Available elections relevant to a holding SPV include: the group of companies taxation election under s45 of the Income Tax Act for asset transfers, the s42 roll-over election, and elections under the controlled foreign company dividend rules. Hybrid mismatch rules under s7D and aligned with OECD BEPS Action 2 apply and limit deduction or inclusion outcomes; no separate hybrid entity election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory registration on taxable supplies over any rolling 12-month period); voluntary registration threshold from ZAR 250,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (1) movable goods supplied and exported to non-residents within 90 days of supply where documentary proof of export is retained; (2) services supplied to non-residents who are outside South Africa at the time of supply, provided the services are not used or consumed in South Africa; and (3) goods or services supplied to a foreign export establishment in certain limited cases. For a pure equity holding SPV, zero-rating commonly applies only to incidental export-related supplies; typical holding and dividend activities remain exempt from VAT, not zero-rated. All zero-rating claims require the supplier to retain prescribed evidence in respect of the actual export or foreign consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is currently operative in South Africa. SARS eFiling serves as the digital portal for submitting VAT returns and processing tax invoices. Electronic tax invoices are accepted if they satisfy the integrity, authenticity, and content requirements of VAT Act s20. SARS has announced future mandatory e-invoicing but it is not yet legally effective.

SARS may reverse unjustified zero-rating and raise an additional VAT assessment with interest. Under the Tax Administration Act, an understatement penalty applies when a shortfall results from a substantial understatement, intentional tax evasion, or gross negligence; the penalty can be up to 200% of the tax shortfall. Criminal prosecution may involve a fine of up to ZAR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. The retrospective assessment period is generally 5 years, but it is extended where fraud, misrepresentation, or non-disclosure has occurred.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is freely floating and fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, interest, and intra-group management fees. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity subscriptions by non-residents, the issuance of shares by a South African Private Company, and subsequent repatriation of profits, are subject to a structured approval and record-keeping framework under the SARB Exchange Control Manual. Passive holding companies structured as pure equity SPVs are not automatically blocked, but payments such as dividends, redemption of shares, and reduction of contributed tax capital require an Authorised Dealer to verify compliance with Section 25 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 solvency and liquidity requirements, FinSurv reporting on Form CC, and, in the case of foreign shareholder payments, the due deduction and remittance of dividends tax under Section 64F of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Effective access to profit remittance is therefore conditional on proper compliance with annual exchange control reporting by Authorised Dealers and the maintenance of accurate corporate and tax records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Exchange Control Manual, export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa and received into an authorised dealer in full, without leakage, within 180 days from date of exportation. A fixed statutory surrender ratio over and above full repatriation is not applied: exporters may retain the funds in designated Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) with authorised dealers, subject to permissible holding limits and reporting thresholds. No portion of the proceeds is legally convertible to ZAR unless needed for domestic payments; conversion to local currency is determined by the exporter's liquidity needs. Failure to repatriate within the 180-day window, without an approved extension, constitutes a contravention of exchange control regulations and may trigger administrative penalties and mandatory additional reporting.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), via Authorised Dealers mandated by the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts are not regarded as South African 'domestic banks' and cannot independently hold ZAR settlement accounts or accept residents' funds without obtaining a formal banking licence under section 11 of the Banks Act No. 94 of 1990, or without maintaining an agency arrangement with a licensed authorised dealer. A non-resident pure equity SPV seeking cross-border operational support may only lawfully use an offshore EMI as an auxiliary notification or network node, never as the sole treasury rail; all inward loans, share subscription inflows, and outbound dividend distributions must physically clear via a South African licensed authorised dealer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inward equity capital injections must be routed through a South African Authorised Dealer using a SWIFT MT103 wire with the designated purpose code corresponding to 'Direct investment – equity capital' under the SARB Balance of Payments reporting classification. The remittance instruction should explicitly disclose the underlying share subscription agreement, contain the non-resident parent's legal name and account details, and identify the final beneficiary as the ZA Pty Ltd HoldCo. For equity injections exceeding the authorised dealer's delegated limits, a prior notification to FinSurv via the announced discretion route, including form CM-1 and Board approval, is required. The authorised dealer performs verification of the corporate resolution, beneficial ownership declaration, UBO tax identification numbers, and a certification that the funds are not derived from illicit sources. Funds must not flow into a brain trust or prepaid account; they must be received directly into the capital escrow account and thereafter transferred to the operational account once the bank has confirmed the share issue has been recorded.Outbound settlements from the passive holding SPV will predominantly occur only for operational costs, permitted intercompany loans, and dividend distributions to the parent. For acquisition-related trade settlements or downstream capital contributions into operating subsidiaries, commercial documentary credits (Irrevocable LC) will be issued by the South African bank via an advised confirming bank, using UCP 600 rules. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: R100 million aggregate international affected transactions (Local File + Master File); below R100 million, a simplified TP policy and comparability evidence are still required.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no UAE-style ESR code; however, the GAAR (ss. 80A-L), substance-over-form doctrine, and transfer pricing adjustment (s31) require that the SPV has substantive decision-making authority, local directors, adequate capitalisation, and business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty up to 200% of the shortfall (TAA s223); criminal conviction for tax evasion (TAA s234) with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved are jointly and severally liable; citing Companies Act s77 and common law fraud, subject to civil restitution and travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: SARS makes primary adjustment; secondary adjustment (deemed interest); understatement penalty up to 100%-200% plus interest.

Director personal liability: Director may be liable for unpaid corporate tax debts where the company was used to avoid TP obligations and transactions are made for improper purposes.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: SARS disregards the SPV's separate existence under GAAR, imposes 200% penalty, disallows treaty benefits, and re-characterises the dividend income.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for restitution of diverted funds and criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion and money laundering.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FICA imposition of administrative fines up to R100,000,000 and/or 15 years' imprisonment for money laundering; supplementary sanctions under POCA.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons face imprisonment up to 15 years for money laundering and personal fines; directorial disqualification under Companies Act.
