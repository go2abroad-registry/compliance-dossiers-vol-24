Competent corporate registry

Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), an agency of the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (the dtic).

Governing statute

Companies Act 71 of 2008, as amended, and Companies Regulations 2011. Key sections: s.1 (definitions), s.8(2) (private company), s.13 (incorporation), s.14 (registration), s.19 (legal personality), s.40 (consideration for shares), s.66 (board), s.69 (directors), s.86 (company secretary), s.70 (registered office).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notice of Incorporation (form CoR 14.1), together with the Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI) and supporting documents as prescribed.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up share capital is required for a private company (Pty) Ltd in South Africa. Shares must be issued for adequate consideration, and any discount on shares is prohibited. There is no escrow or banked capital requirement; the company may commence business immediately after obtaining its registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate or Fire Clearance from the relevant local Fire Protection Service for the registered office premises; Occupational Health and Safety Act (Act 85 of 1993) compliance including a general workplace safety file; certificate of electrical compliance (COC) for the leased office; and for a remote workforce, a documented home-office risk assessment is recommended but no physical clearance is mandatory.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up share capital is required for a private company (Pty) Ltd in South Africa. Shares must be issued for adequate consideration, and any discount on shares is prohibited. There is no escrow or banked capital requirement; the company may commence business immediately after obtaining its registration certificate.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: (1) that the Memorandum of Incorporation executed by the initial members and incorporators is adopted as the company's constituent document; (2) that the share subscription agreements presented to the board are ratified, and that the initial directors named in the MOI are appointed effective immediately; (3) that the company's registered office be constituted at the address stated in the Notice of Incorporation and that all statutory correspondence may be served there; (4) that the chairperson, company secretary, and chief financial officer be appointed to manage statutory filing and tax compliance; (5) that the directors be authorized to open bank accounts with licensed financial institutions and to execute all applications with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission and the South African Revenue Service; and (6) that these resolutions be implemented without further formality.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A South African private company (Pty) Ltd must incorporate under the Companies Act 2008 and thereafter register for income tax via SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using the SARS Registration, Amendments and Verification (RAV) process. The company must submit its Memorandum of Incorporation, certificate of incorporation, directors'/officers' identification, registered office and postal address, bank confirmation, and financial details. SARS issues a separate income tax reference number; PAYE/UIF/SDL registration is required if the company becomes an employer; VAT registration is required once taxable turnover exceeds R1 million per 12-month rolling period, with voluntary registration possible once taxable turnover exceeds R50,000 per 12-month period. A foreign company deriving South African-source income or having a permanent establishment must register for income tax under the same RAV process and may be required to register for VAT if it supplies taxable electronic services into South Africa above the R1 million threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a check-the-box classification regime. Comparable statutory elections under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 include the controlled foreign company apportionment election under section 9D(2A), the prepayment allowance election under section 24C, the research and development allowance election under section 11D, and the VAT grouping election under section 21(1A) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 allowing two or more entities to be treated as a single vendor. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are applied under sections 8F and 8FA for hybrid debt and hybrid instruments, with no elective opt-out for covered instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 compulsory annual taxable turnover; voluntary threshold R50,000 annual taxable turnover; foreign suppliers of electronic services must register if the aggregate value of taxable supplies to South African residents exceeds R1,000,000 in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% VAT under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act when supplied to a non-resident and exported from South Africa within the prescribed timeframe, with the supplier retaining documentary proof of export. Services, including remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software, qualify for 0% under section 11(2)(l) when supplied to a non-resident who is absent from South Africa, provided the services are not supplied directly to a person in South Africa and are not attributable to movable property or land in South Africa. The supplier must obtain a written declaratory confirmation from the recipient and safeguard evidence of export/offshore supply for audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500/XML lodged through the SARS Customs clearance process)
  • Commercial invoice with full exporter, consignee, value and destination particulars
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/authorised-dealer evidence showing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • VAT 177 declaration or equivalent export voucher where applicable

South Africa does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or government API stamping requirement for B2B e-invoices. VAT vendors must issue tax invoices satisfying section 20 of the VAT Act within 21 days of the supply, retain complete digital records under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, and submit VAT returns and payment via SARS eFiling. SARS has promoted e-invoicing and standard business reporting, but no digital clearance gateway is compulsory as of the current legislative position.

SARS may disallow the zero-rating, raise an additional VAT assessment, impose interest, and apply an understatement penalty under section 222/223 of the Tax Administration Act of up to 200% of the shortfall where the failure is due to intentional tax evasion or substantial impermissible avoidance. Deliberate submission of false export evidence is a criminal offence under VAT Act section 58 and Customs and Excise Act provisions, carrying a potential fine and imprisonment. Exchange control contraventions for failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may trigger administrative penalties and further SARS audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is freely convertible for current-account transactions, including software licence fees, telemetry subscription revenue, royalties, professional fees and dividend profit remittances, via an authorised dealer without SARB prior approval, subject to FICA-driven KYC verification and the documentary requirements of the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers. Capital-account inflows are open to non-resident investors subscribing for shares or extending shareholder loans, but require formal FDI reporting to FinSurv (Form DBR filed through the authorised dealer within 30 days of receipt) and are prohibited where a prohibited 'loop structure' — a South African-resident owned foreign entity reinvesting into South Africa — is present. Dividend remittances to foreign shareholders require: (i) certification of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 solvency and liquidity test; (ii) withholding of 20% dividends tax or a reduced treaty rate supported by SARS income-tax-residence certification; and (iii) a board resolution and underlying financial statements evidencing distributable reserves. South Africa's FATF grey-listing (February 2023 to October 2025) elevated enhanced due diligence on cross-border flows; following removal from the grey list, banks still apply heightened beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds scrutiny but no material convertibility denial is expected for bona fide ISIC 6201 software-export receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Proceeds from service exports, including remote-desktop-software licensing and unattended-IoT-telemetry subscription fees, must be repatriated to South Africa and credited to the resident exporter's account with an authorised dealer within 180 days of the invoice date, in accordance with the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers and related Exchange Control Circulars. No mandatory conversion to ZAR is imposed; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a Foreign Exchange Account (FEA) or flexible FEA, but any conversion into ZAR is executed through the authorised dealer at prevailing market rates. Each inward receipt must be traceable to the underlying export invoice and reported under the applicable services-export economic measurement code. If the 180-day repatriation deadline cannot be met, the authorised dealer must flag the overdue receivable and the exporter must apply to FinSurv for an extension or a 'no-objection' ruling before the expiry of the statutory period.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) — Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administering exchange control under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder (R.1111 of 1963, as amended), with designated authorised dealer banks (commercial banks) executing day-to-day controls and reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium — Conditionally viable as a secondary rail only. An offshore digital EMI or fintech treasury provider (e.g., Wise Platform, Payoneer, Airwallex) cannot independently operate as a deposit-taking bank or settlement participant in South Africa without SARB recognition under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998; it must route local settlement through a licensed authorised dealer. The target payment rails (Stripe and Paddle, operating as Merchant of Record / payment facilitators) are permissible for collecting overseas SaaS and IOT-telemetry subscription fees and paying out the ZA (Pty) Ltd's funds by FEA wire transfer, but they are not substitutes for a domestic corporate bank account. The EMI channel cannot execute SARB FDI reporting (EM code filings), cannot maintain a SWIFT-settled ZAR account with FEA compliant aggregation, cannot provide onshore ZAR-denominated forward exchange contracts, and would trigger FICA business-relationship objections if used as the sole liquidity hub for foreign-director funding. Recommended architecture: the principal treasury anchor must be a tier-1 authorised dealer bank, with Stripe and Paddle retained as collection disbursement channels whose net proceeds are swept into the domestic operational account under the 180-day repatriation rule.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident share subscription or shareholder-loan funding must enter South Africa exclusively through an authorised dealer bank via a compliant SWIFT chain: MT103 single-customer credit for direct remittances or MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover, with the SWIFT remittance narrative expressly stating 'Share subscription — ZA_PTY_LTD — FDI equity capital' and carrying the SARB Economic Measurement (EM) purpose code for inward direct-investment equity (EM 3840) as applied by the receiving bank. The authorised dealer will demand: (i) a board resolution approving the allotment of shares or the loan facility; (ii) an updated MOI and shareholders' register or the CIPC share-allotment return (CM26); (iii) full source-of-funds evidence from the remitting shareholder; (iv) a signed Form DBR (foreign-direct-investment declaration) transmitted to SARB FinSurv within 30 days of the receipt; and (v) FICA-compliant identity and beneficial-ownership verification. No SARB prior approval is required for bona fide FDI below the applicable alert thresholds, but the transaction is rejected where a prohibited loop structure exists. Operational service-export proceeds from Stripe and Paddle settlements are routed under the computer-and-information-services services-export EM code with the 180-day repatriation obligation, and each inbound payout is matched to the underlying telemetry-subscription invoice.Outbound settlements are executed through the authorised dealer using SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers, each supported by the underlying invoice, the applicable destination EM code and FICA-compliant commercial documentation retained for SARS and SARB audit. Where a foreign supplier or distribution counterparty requires documentary security, an irrevocable sight commercial letter of credit governed by UCP 600 (or eUCP for electronic records) is the permissible clearing rail, although software subscriptions and cloud-infrastructure payments are ordinarily settled by clean wire with the corresponding FX forward cover. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders follow the regulated remittance path: solvency-and-liquidity certificate under the Companies Act, 20% dividends tax withheld (or treaty-reduced rate), board resolution, and an authorised-dealer payment instruction using the income and distributions EM category. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (standard rate for South African companies for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; previously 28%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividend withholding tax charged on the gross amount of any dividend paid by a South African company to a non-resident beneficial owner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Compulsory Local File and Master File preparation and retention apply when the value of international connected-party transactions exceeds ZAR 50,000,000 per year of assessment. South African multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion must additionally prepare Country-by-Country reports.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime in the style of the UAE, but genuine economic activity is required through the place-of-effective-management test, CFC legislation, transfer-pricing rules and the general anti-avoidance rule under section 103(1) of the Income Tax Act. A remote screen-sharing or IoT company must evidence real personnel, board decision-making, and operational delivery in South Africa to avoid recharacterisation of its cross-border arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-Declaration of Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under sections 234 and 235 of the Tax Administration Act; an understatement penalty of 200% of the shortfall plus interest; asset seizure and preservation orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax through the responsible-person provisions of the Tax Administration Act, face criminal sentences of up to five years, and may be prohibited from leaving the country while under investigation.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of intercompany payments and recharacterisation under section 31 (transfer pricing) and section 103(1) (GAAR), 200% penalty on the adjustment, non-recognition of the deemed intra-group transactions, and possible deregistration under the Companies Act.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil for reckless trading under section 22 of the Companies Act; directors are jointly and severally liable for tax liabilities and may be disqualified from managing companies.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FICA administrative penalties of up to ZAR 10 million; CIPC may issue a non-compliance notice and a delinquency order; SARS may impose a 10% monthly administrative penalty for non-filing of required tax-related information; money laundering offences attract imprisonment up to 10 years under POCA.

Director personal liability: Directors of a delinquent company may be personally liable for its debts while trading, and are individually subject to criminal sanction and travel bans when the non-compliance conceals criminal proceeds.
Improper Profit Repatriation / Dividends Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax recouped from the company, interest and penalties, non-deductibility of the distribution, deemed-dividend treatment under sections 31 and 64E, and an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors are personally liable under section 156 of the Tax Administration Act for the dividends tax not withheld and remitted; SARS may issue attachment notices to banks; continued non-compliance constitutes a criminal offence with imprisonment.
