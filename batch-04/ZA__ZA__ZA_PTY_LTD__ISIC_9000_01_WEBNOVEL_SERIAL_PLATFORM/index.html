Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); primary filing portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); corporate registration backdrop: Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) e-services.

E-government filing portal

A (Pty) Ltd is incorporated through the CIPC e-registration process; CIPC automatically notifies SARS and the company receives an Income Tax Reference Number (SARS taxpayer ID). Once the value of taxable supplies exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in a 12-month period, the company must register as a VAT vendor on SARS eFiling (form VAT101) within 21 days after the end of the month in which the threshold is exceeded; SARS then issues a VAT registration number and certificate. A resident public officer (form IT106) must be appointed and registered if requested; foreign-owned companies must obtain a SARS taxpayer reference number for the company and may appoint a resident public officer. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African consumers use the SARS eFiling non-resident registration channel for VAT, applying the same ZAR 1,000,000 threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A (Pty) Ltd is incorporated through the CIPC e-registration process; CIPC automatically notifies SARS and the company receives an Income Tax Reference Number (SARS taxpayer ID). Once the value of taxable supplies exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in a 12-month period, the company must register as a VAT vendor on SARS eFiling (form VAT101) within 21 days after the end of the month in which the threshold is exceeded; SARS then issues a VAT registration number and certificate. A resident public officer (form IT106) must be appointed and registered if requested; foreign-owned companies must obtain a SARS taxpayer reference number for the company and may appoint a resident public officer. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African consumers use the SARS eFiling non-resident registration channel for VAT, applying the same ZAR 1,000,000 threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable: South African tax law has no US check-the-box regime and no elective hybrid-transparency classification; all (Pty) Ltd entities are taxed as companies on taxable income at the 27% corporate rate. Optional regimes that effectively function as elections: micro business turnover tax (turnover ≤ ZAR 1 million, Sixth Schedule), Small Business Corporation concessional rates (s12E, turnover < ZAR 20 million, natural-person shareholders only), corporate rollover elections for internal reorganisations (s44, s45, s46 and s47 of the Income Tax Act), and the s11D research and development allowance election. Hybrid mismatch measures are mandatory anti-avoidance rules (BEPS Action 2) and do not grant any election to alter the entity's tax character.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory VAT registration threshold: taxable supplies exceeding this amount in any 12-month period; foreign electronic-services suppliers have the same ZAR 1,000,000 threshold for South Africa-linked digital supplies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from South Africa qualify for 0% VAT under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the export is completed and prescribed proof of export is retained. Services supplied to non-residents qualify under section 11(2)(k)/(l) where the recipient is outside South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not consumed or used in South Africa, subject to connected-person and anti-avoidance exclusions. A South African WebNovel subscription platform selling to non-resident end users therefore zero-rates digital content supplies delivered via Apple IAP/Google Play Billing; the vendor must evidence non-residence of each user (geo-IP, billing country, app-store storefront, registered payment method) and retain settlement statements. The VAT export substantiation is accompanied by a 180-day FX repatriation benchmark for service earnings under SARS/SARB exchange-control supervision.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / SARS export manifest) for exported goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing FX repatriation
  • Commercial invoice/contract and settlement reconciliation records
  • Apple/Google Play settlement statements and geo-IP/user-residence logs for digital services

South Africa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, server-side digital stamping or continuous API tax-authority validation of invoices. SARS requires full VAT tax invoices under section 20 of the VAT Act to be issued no later than 21 days after the time of supply and all VAT returns and filings to be made electronically through eFiling. SARS operates a Continuous Audit (e-audit) data-exchange programme and has published an e-invoicing roadmap, but no official digital stamp or pre-clearance obligation is in force. For app-store sales, Apple IAP and Google Play Billing statements are accepted commercial evidence, and the SA vendor remains responsible for correct output-VAT treatment.

SARS can reopen VAT assessments for up to 5 years (unlimited in fraud cases) and claw back any improperly zero-rated supply as output VAT at 15%, with interest at the prescribed rate. Understatement penalties under TAA s223 scale with conduct: 10% (substantial understatement), 25% (no reasonable care), 50% (no reasonable grounds), 75% (impermissible avoidance), 150% (gross negligence or fraud). Section 60 of the VAT Act additionally imposes additional tax up to 100% of the VAT evaded for non-compliance, and intentional evasion or fraudulent zero-rating claims attract criminal liability under the Tax Administration Act (fines and imprisonment up to 5 years).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa is an IMF Article VIII member: current-account payments and transfers are generally freely permitted and do not require prior SARB approval. Capital-account transactions remain subject to exchange-control oversight. Inward foreign direct investment into an ordinary South African (Pty) Ltd is permitted through an Authorised Dealer with appropriate BOP reporting; the non-resident shareholder may contribute equity capital in hard currency and receive shares without prior SARB approval, provided the funds are routed through a licensed South African bank. Profit repatriation, including dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders, is permitted but requires an Authorised Dealer to verify SARS tax clearance, audited financial statements, solvency and liquidity tests under the Companies Act, and the absence of outstanding exchange-control obligations. No mandatory surrender of retained earnings is imposed on a going-concern basis; however, all approved foreign-currency receipts must be reported and either credited to an approved foreign-currency account or converted into ZAR as directed by SARB policy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All proceeds from exports of goods and services, including digital content subscriptions collected through foreign app stores, must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment or contractual due date for services. Upon receipt, 100% of the export proceeds must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer unless the exporter has been approved to hold funds in a foreign-currency account (FCA) under the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual. For a webnovel platform receiving monthly Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlements, each app-store remittance must be routed directly into the (Pty) Ltd's South African bank account or approved FCA; no portion may be retained offshore without a specific SARB approval. The bank will apply a 100% ZAR conversion or FCA credit at the deal date, and the company must retain app-store payout statements and platform agreements for SARB and AML audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), together with the Prudential Authority, acting under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 and the Currency and Exchanges Manual.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury or clearing rail. Offshore digital EMI and cross-border payment platforms are not licensed Authorised Dealers under South African exchange control and cannot legally provide the sole settlement channel for SARB-regulated capital flows. They may be used as payment-collection merchants for Apple IAP and Google Play Billing proceeds, but all funds must ultimately be swept into a South African bank account or an SARB-approved foreign-currency account. A South African resident company maintaining an offshore EMI account without FinSurv approval would create an unlawful exchange-control exposure. Such EMIs are therefore viable only as auxiliary collection/conduit instruments, never as the exclusive liquidity warehouse.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder capital contributions must be sent by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the (Pty) Ltd's South African account at an Authorised Dealer. The payment must be designated as inward direct investment/equity capital and mapped by the receiving bank to the SARB Balance-of-Payments classification for 'direct investment in a resident enterprise – equity'. Field 70 of the MT103 should contain a concise reason-for-payment such as 'Inward equity investment – issue of ordinary shares in ZA Pty Ltd'; no funds may be routed through unrelated third-party wallets or EMI accounts. The company must deliver to the bank: a share subscription resolution, updated memorandum of incorporation, CIPC beneficial-ownership filing, declaration by non-resident investor, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation of the resulting shareholding percentage. Once credited, the bank issues an inward foreign investment reference and the company files any required share allotment return with CIPC. The inbound funds are then held in a Capital Escrow Account or released to the Operating Account only after the bank confirms identity and source-of-funds compliance.For settlement of foreign authors, service providers, royalties, and technology costs, the primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 via the (Pty) Ltd's South African AD; for interbank cover, MT202 COV is used where required. Domestic ZAR clearing runs through SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement) and is appropriate for local expenses. Documentary trade settlements, if the platform later licenses physical merchandise or content rights requiring transport evidence, should use a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) or documentary collection. Every outbound cross-border payment must be supported by underlying contracts, invoices, proof of tax withholding (where applicable), and, if the counterparty is a non-resident related party, transfer-pricing documentation. The bank will screen each payment against exchange-control regulations; royalties and service fees require SARS tax clearance or an approved SARS withholding certificate where applicable. All disbursements should be pre-registered in the bank's electronic payment system and, for amounts above regulatory thresholds, accompanied by the required exchange-control forms or AD declarations.Forward exchange contracts (FECs) through a licensed Authorised Dealer for committed current-account exposures, such as expected Apple/Google settlement in USD or scheduled royalty payments to foreign contractors.Foreign-currency options and option collars written by an Authorised Dealer for genuine economic risk hedging, with premium paid out of the operating account and subject to SARB/AD suitability review.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) and interest-rate swaps booked with an Authorised Dealer solely to hedge approved capital or borrowing exposures; the swap must be linked to an underlying cross-border transaction and cannot be speculative.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/ZAR or USD/ZAR emerging-market risk are permissible only where an Authorised Dealer procures an onshore South African hedge or where the NDF is expressly approved by SARB/FinSurv for a defined non-speculative underlying exposure; general offshore speculative NDF transactions are not permitted for South African resident companies.

20% final Dividends Tax withheld by the South African company at the time a dividend is paid or payable to a non-resident beneficial owner under section 64E of the Income Tax Act. The company is liable for the tax if it fails to withhold. Dividends paid to another South African resident company in certain group structures may be exempt, but distributions to foreign shareholders are taxable unless a DTA reduces the rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional return: due within six months after the first day of the company's financial year. Second provisional return: due on or before the last business day of the financial year. Any final top-up payment is reported with the annual ITR14 and must be settled no later than 12 months after financial year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The completed ITR14 and supporting schedules must be submitted within 12 months after the end of the company's financial year. SARS may shorten the deadline for risk-flagged taxpayers. The return is due irrespective of whether the company has taxable income, a loss, or no activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is expected when the aggregate value of affected cross-border connected-person transactions equals or exceeds ZAR 100 million in a tax year. SARS may require documentation and a transfer pricing policy below this threshold where audit risk exists. A Master File and Local File structure is expected for multinational groups with large South African operations; group revenue in excess of approximately ZAR 1 billion will normally compel a Master File. The documentation must include an economic analysis, functional and risk profile of the Pty Ltd, benchmarking study, and explanation of the selected arm's length methodology.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply a segregated Economic Substance Regulations statute in the manner of offshore financial centres. Substance is nevertheless a decisive tax factor under the arm's length principle, the General Anti-Avoidance Rules in sections 80A to 80L of the Income Tax Act, the old section 103, and the Controlled Foreign Company rules in section 9D. A Pty Ltd operating a webnovel platform must demonstrate real local employees, local decision-making for content licensing and user payments, actual operational premises, assumption of commercial risk, and the performance of distribution and platform functions. An offshore parent holding the IP must receive no more than an arm's length royalty; all economic substance and significant people functions exercised by the South African entity must result in taxable South African profit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Deficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income to the arm's length amount under section 31 of the Income Tax Act and impose interest. The Tax Administration Act understatement penalty can reach 200% of the shortfall for intentional evasion; inadequate documentation alone can attract substantial understatement penalties because the taxpayer bears the burden of proving that the results are arm's length.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise false transfer pricing declarations or cause the Pty Ltd to dispose of assets in favour of foreign connected persons may be held jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax debt. Civil asset attachment, director disqualification, travel restrictions for unpaid personal tax debt, and criminal prosecution for fraud or tax evasion are available remedies.
Shell Company and Anti-Avoidance Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: SARS can disregard, recharacterise, or treat a step as a dividend or gross income under sections 80A to 80L and section 103. Deductions may be denied, the arrangement voided for tax purposes, and a penalty of up to 200% imposed. A shell company face may also be disregarded for substance purposes, causing all group profits to be taxed in the country of actual decision-making.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes generated by an artificial arrangement where they materially assisted in implementing it. The corporate veil can be pierced under the Companies Act for reckless trading, s77 liability for improper distributions, and fraud. Travel bans may be applied where personal tax debt is assessed and remains unpaid.
Anti-Money Laundering and Financial Regulatory Breach

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre may impose administrative fines up to ZAR 10 million per contravention; criminal money laundering under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act carries a fine of up to ZAR 100 million and imprisonment up to 30 years for natural persons. SARS may also treat undisclosed foreign bank accounts as a tax offence with additional understatement penalties.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in laundering of proceeds, fails to report suspicious transactions, or permits structured cash-like transfers may face personal fines, imprisonment, disqualification from managing companies, and confiscation orders. Corporate limited liability does not shield deliberate criminal conduct.
Unreported Dividend and Improper Profit Extraction

Statutory sanction: SARS will recharacterise the payment as a dividend and hold the company liable for the full amount of Dividends Tax that should have been withheld, plus interest and administrative penalties. The understatement penalty for deliberate misclassification can reach 200%. Directors who approve a dividend while the company is insolvent may incur personal liability for the amount of the unlawful distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under the Companies Act for distributions made in violation of the solvency and liquidity tests. SARS may pierce the corporate veil and attach personal assets of directors involved in fraudulent extraction. Criminal prosecution, director disqualification, travel restrictions, and an order for civil restitution are all possible outcomes.
