Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — national tax authority; online portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); official site: www.sars.gov.za. Exchange control (FX repatriation) is administered separately by the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) of the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) through Authorized Dealers (commercial banks).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); the CIPC enterprise number is auto-linked to SARS and a provisional income tax reference number (ITREF) is issued at incorporation. Step 2: Complete registration on SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch: Income Tax (IT77), VAT (VAT101 — ten-digit VAT vendor number), and, if staff are hired, PAYE/UIF/SDL (EMP101). Step 3: Foreign shareholders and directors obtain their own South African tax reference numbers (non-resident individuals and foreign companies register with SARS directly). Step 4: Obtain the regulatory authorisations conditioning commencement of the EMI business: SARB Prudential Authority / FSCA authorisation under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 and FinSurv Authorized Dealer (Category II) approval for FX/SEPA/IBAN payment activities; each licence application requires a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN. Step 5: VAT registration must be completed within 21 days of exceeding the ZAR 1 million turnover threshold; failure to register within the statutory window attracts administrative non-compliance penalties of up to ZAR 4,000 per month (TAA s 210) plus an understatement penalty if tax shortfalls result.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a (Pty) Ltd incorporated in South Africa is automatically a resident taxpayer (Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, s 1 'resident' definition) taxed on worldwide income at the corporate rate of 27% (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023). No pass-through or partnership transparency applies; profits are taxed at entity level and dividends paid are subject to Dividends Tax of 20% withheld at shareholder level. Capital gains: 80% of net gains are taxable at 27% (effective 21.6% corporate CGT). No territorial ring-fence, no tax-exempt zone; the CFC regime (s 9D) may tax undistributed offshore profits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a (Pty) Ltd incorporated in South Africa is automatically a resident taxpayer (Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, s 1 'resident' definition) taxed on worldwide income at the corporate rate of 27% (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023). No pass-through or partnership transparency applies; profits are taxed at entity level and dividends paid are subject to Dividends Tax of 20% withheld at shareholder level. Capital gains: 80% of net gains are taxable at 27% (effective 21.6% corporate CGT). No territorial ring-fence, no tax-exempt zone; the CFC regime (s 9D) may tax undistributed offshore profits. The EMI's operating income — float margin, interchange fees, virtual IBAN issuance and digital wallet management fees — is ordinary revenue fully taxable in South Africa.

Available election forms

South Africa has no 'check-the-box' entity-classification election. Available statutory elections and hybrid-mismatch safeguards: (1) Small Business Corporation status (s 12E) — electable on the annual return for qualifying (Pty) Ltd companies with gross income under ZAR 20 million, not more than five natural-person shareholders and under 20% passive income; progressive rates 0% to 27%. (2) Headquarter Company election (s 9I) — available where at least 80% of the company's interest is held in foreign companies (with foreign shareholding exceeding ZAR 5 million); provides relief for foreign dividends, interest, royalties and service fees, and foreign capital gains. (3) VAT grouping election (s 50, VAT Act) — a group of companies linked by at least 75% ownership may elect to be one VAT vendor. (4) CFC high-tax exemption election (s 9D(9)) — excludes CFC income that bears foreign tax of at least 18% (effective rate). (5) Hybrid mismatch rules (effective 1 January 2021) are self-executing anti-avoidance provisions that neutralise deductions for hybrid payments; no opt-out election exists. (6) Global Minimum Top-up Tax (Pillar Two, effective for fiscal years commencing on or after 31 December 2023, enacted in the Global Minimum Tax Act 2024) is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million; the domestic top-up tax is filed with SARS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 — compulsory registration within 21 days of exceeding R1 million taxable turnover in any 12-month period (VAT Act s 23(1)); voluntary registration is available once annual turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000. Exempt financial services count toward the turnover threshold. Note: Budget 2025 proposes lowering the compulsory threshold to ZAR 500,000 with effect from 1 April 2026 (not yet enacted).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Financial services supplied by the EMI to a non-resident are zero-rated under s 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act when: (i) the supplier is a VAT vendor carrying on an enterprise; (ii) the recipient is a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time the services are rendered; (iii) the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or movable property situated in South Africa; (iv) the services are not entertainment services; and (v) the vendor holds and retains documentary proof of the non-resident status and absence from South Africa at the time of supply. Qualifying zero-rated services: issuance of virtual IBANs, digital-wallet reserve management, cross-border payment processing and SEPA credits supplied to non-resident clients. The same services supplied to a South African resident would be exempt financial services (s 2) with restricted input-VAT recovery, subject to the s 17(1) de minimis relief where exempt financial supplies do not exceed ZAR 5,000 per month or 5% of monthly taxable supplies.

  • Not applicable — no Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) or Bill of Lading for intangible or financial-service exports; the following documentary bundle applies instead.
  • Signed export service contract / service-level agreement with the non-resident client, evidencing place of supply, consideration and SEPA/IBAN payment mechanics.
  • SARS-compliant tax invoice (s 20 VAT Act) expressly reflecting zero-rating under s 11(2)(l).
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by the Authorized Dealer, or SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation, evidencing receipt of foreign-currency settlement.
  • Non-resident status evidence: foreign tax residency certificate issued by the client's revenue authority, or a sworn declaration (Form F178 / FinSurv FEC declaration) confirming the client was outside South Africa at the time of supply.
  • Underlying transaction records: virtual IBAN issuance register, wallet ledger extracts and central-bank-clearing/SEPA settlement confirmations reconciled to the invoiced consideration.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or mandatory government API invoice-authorisation regime operates in South Africa. Electronic tax invoices are statutorily valid if they comply with s 20 of the VAT Act and are authentic, legible and retained for 5 years. SARS is progressively modernising e-invoicing toward PEPPOL-based interoperability and B2B e-invoicing via its eFiling APIs, but clearance remains return-based (monthly or bi-monthly VAT201 filings) rather than per-invoice authorisation. EMI-specific: the payment-scheme settlement statements (Central Bank Clearing / SEPA) and SWIFT MT103 messages constitute the audit trail supporting VAT records.

Substantiation is high-risk: SARS and FinSurv review export-of-services positions for (i) misuse of s 11(2)(l), (ii) round-tripping of funds through the EMI's offshore wallets, and (iii) late or non-repatriation of foreign currency. Consequences of incorrect zero-rating or documentary failure: (1) additional VAT assessment at 15% on the full consideration plus statutory interest at the repo rate plus 3.5 percentage points per annum (s 39 VAT Act; s 187 TAA); (2) TAA understatement penalties (ss 222-223): 10% (substantial understatement), 25% (failure to take reasonable care), 50% (no reasonable grounds), 75% (impermissible avoidance), 150% (intentional tax evasion); (3) fraudulent zero-rating (e.g., forged non-resident declarations) attracts additional tax of up to 200% of the VAT shortfall under s 59(3) VAT Act, and criminal prosecution under TAA ss 233-235 with fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years for serious tax crimes (Schedule 1 TAA); (4) retrospective clawback: SARS may reopen assessments within 3 years of the original assessment, extended to 5 years for non-disclosure or negligence, and indefinitely where fraud, misrepresentation or non-disclosure of material facts is established (s 99 TAA); (5) FinSurv exchange-control violations for failure to repatriate within 180 days may result in administrative penalties and criminal sanction under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a single-floating-rand regime with dual-tier exchange control: current-account payments (trade, services, dividends, royalties, interest, management fees) are generally convertible through an Authorised Dealer (AD) without FinSurv pre-approval, subject to corroborating documentation, tax clearance and the SARB purpose-of-payment code framework; capital-account items (inward equity, shareholder loans, outward investment) remain subject to FinSurv approval or delegated AD limits. Profit remittance hurdles for a licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI): (i) dividends must be declared from realised distributable reserves evidenced by audited or reviewed financial statements; (ii) the company must hold a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN with no outstanding returns and must withhold 20% dividends tax (s64G of the Income Tax Act, 1962, subject to double-taxation-agreement relief); (iii) the remitted funds must be traceable to foreign capital imported through an AD and classified as 'external funds', with the inward transfer declaration correctly filed; and (iv) non-resident shareholders must be accurately recorded in the share register and the CIPC beneficial-ownership filing to permit clean dividend outflow. No general prior approval is required for inward equity capital below sectoral thresholds, but the AD must apply FICA verification and sanctions screening before releasing funds as convertible external capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 3(1) in terms of the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, a resident exporter must receive full export proceeds within 180 days from the date of shipment (or completion of services for services exports) and must repatriate such proceeds to South Africa. Since 1 January 2010, there is no mandatory conversion ratio: all or part of the proceeds may be retained in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with a South African AD bank, or converted to ZAR at the ruling market rate, at the exporter's election. Any foreign-currency receipts credited to the EMI's onshore accounts, including stablecoin conversions and nostro FCA balances attributable to cross-border services, must be repatriated within the same 180-day window and reported on the relevant FCA and inward-transfer returns. Merchanting trade (triangulation) and retention of proceeds offshore require prior FinSurv approval; default triggers exchange-control penalties and, in egregious cases, criminal prosecution under the Currency and Exchanges Act.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Prudential Authority for bank and payment-system licensing and through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) for Exchange Control Regulations under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933). The National Payment System Act, 1998 (Act 78 of 1998), administered by the National Payment System Department, governs clearing, settlement and e-money issuance; SARB publishes the Position Paper on Electronic Money (revised 2024) which conditions non-bank e-money issuance on SARB authorisation, segregated reserve accounts at an Authorised Dealer (AD) bank, and full backing of e-money liabilities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South Africa has no universal turnover threshold exempting a company from preparing transfer pricing documentation, but SARS requires full TP documentation to be kept where cross-border connected-person transactions exceed ZAR 5 million in gross income or deductions in a year of assessment; Master File and Local File are required consistent with the OECD TP Guidelines, with specific practical thresholds: Local File is recommended for transactions above ZAR 5 million and Master File is required for MNE groups with a consolidated group revenue exceeding ZAR 1 billion, regardless of the local transaction value.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain annual Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in the UAE or Cayman Islands, but economic substance is enforceable through (i) the general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) in section 103 of the Income Tax Act which permit SARS to disregard a transaction lacking commercial substance, (ii) the CFC rules in section 9D which attribute passive income derived by a controlled foreign company unless the CFC carries on substantial economic substance in its jurisdiction, (iii) case law and law of place of effective management (POEM); an EMI (Pty) Ltd must show real operations, banking licence, staff, treasury decisions, and technology development in South Africa, or risk having its transactions recharacterised and its tax residency or entitlement to treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement of Income

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall under Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 s 223; additional interest; criminal conviction under s 235 and s 104(1) of the TAA and s 74 of the Income Tax Act, punishable with a fine up to ZAR 5 million (or twice the amount of tax evaded) and imprisonment up to 5 years; if fraud is proven, common-law fraud charges may lead to up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in fraudulent tax filings or who fail to ensure SARS returns are submitted despite the company's insolvency can be held personally liable under s 158 of the TAA (for representative taxpayer liability) and s 22 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 for reckless trade; SARS may issue a notice of personal liability, apply for a preservation order, obtain a civil judgement for the unpaid tax, and the director may face a travel ban via a foreign travel embargo under s 193 of the TAA; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and disqualification from serving as director.
Transfer Pricing Docking / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income under section 31 to align with the arm's length principle; additional tax of up to 150% of the shortfall under a 'substantial understatement' penalty; interest at the prescribed rate; potential secondary transfer pricing adjustment recharacterising the funding as a dividend, giving rise to dividends tax at 20% on the non-resident; penalties for failure to maintain TP documentation (administrative penalty of up to ZAR 8,000 per month for each outstanding document, plus an understatement penalty).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign financial statements containing false TP declarations without reasonable care commit an offence under the Companies Act; joint liability with the company for the underpaid tax if the director knew of the wilful non-payment; the court may make a director liable for an amount equal to the tax, and under s 77 of the Companies Act a director is liable for losses suffered by the company arising from failure to exercise powers in accordance with the Act; travel restrictions apply in SARS's serious non-compliance procedures.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may ignore the company's separate personality under the substance-over-form doctrine; disallow all associated deductions; reattribute the company's income directly to its beneficial owner, subjecting the income to tax and dividends tax; penalties for failure to register, publish returns, or maintain records (up to ZAR 4,000 per month per default); the company's bank licence may be revoked by the Prudential Authority for failure to satisfy the financial soundness requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors found to be knowingly party to a sham arrangement may be jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes under s 159 of the TAA (representative taxpayer liability); a court may pierce the corporate veil and hold the director personally accountable to SARS and creditors; a travel ban and criminal investigation under Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act may follow; sanctions include imprisonment up to 10 years and a fine up to ZAR 1 million.
AML / Sanctions Violations (FIC Act)

Statutory sanction: Under Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001: administrative fines up to ZAR 10 million per contravention (recently raised under General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act 22 of 2022); remediation orders; suspension or removal of the EMI licence; criminal liability for directors who fail to report a suspicious transaction (imprisonment up to 15 years or fine up to ZAR 100 million for a corporate body); a court may additionally order forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Every director exercises control and management over the EMI's compliance function; under FIC Act s 46C, directors are personally liable where the company's failure is attributable to their fraud, negligence, or failure to act within their powers; the Prudential Authority must approve each director of an EMI, resulting in disqualification and a travel ban through the Financial Sector Conduct Authority; criminal imprisonment for up to 15 years is possible for individuals involved in money laundering.
Failure to Withhold or Pay Dividends Tax / WHT over Interest and Royalties

Statutory sanction: The company is liable for the withholding tax itself (withholding tax is a final tax borne by the company where it fails to withhold); 15%/20% unpaid amounts are recoverable by SARS with interest and penalties; SARS may charge an additional 100% penalty under s 210 of the TAA for failure to pay tax withheld; criminal sanctions apply for non-payment of the withheld amount within the prescribed period.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the company to pay dividends to non-residents without withholding are jointly and severally liable for the outstanding withholding tax and penalties as the company's representatives; travel bans under s 194 of the TAA may be issued, and imprisonment up to 5 years is possible for the intentional evasion of tax obligations.
