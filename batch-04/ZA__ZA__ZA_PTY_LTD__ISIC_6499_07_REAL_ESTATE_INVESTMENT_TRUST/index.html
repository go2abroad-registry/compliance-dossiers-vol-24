Foreign entities must apply for a South African income tax reference number via SARS eFiling or a registered tax practitioner using the ITREG (non-resident company registration) process. Default tax classification

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. A private company (Pty) Ltd is intrinsically a taxable person at entity level under section 2 of the Income Tax Act. No hybrid mismatch election is available for classification purposes, but taxpayers may make certain elections such as the equity/debtording election? No — the available elections are limited to specific allowances and reporting elections, e.g., the Section 11D R&D allowance election, turnover rent or trading stock valuation elections; no entity-level classification election exists. A REIT may elect to be a 'qualifying company' under section 25BB of the Income Tax Act, subject to listing requirements and distribution obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported movable goods supplied under an agreement of sale or lease if the supplier retains the prescribed proof of exportation against the section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act and the goods are exported within specified time periods. Services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) if the services are physically rendered outside South Africa or supplied to non-residents who are not in South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not directly consumed in South Africa. A REIT engaged in exporting a negligible amount of goods must substantiate each export transaction with documentary proof; interest-linked income derived by a non-resident is generally exempt from VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500, lodged on SARS ACS/Excise system)
  • Bill of Lading / Airwaybill (where freight is required)
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the supplier to the foreign customer
  • Proof of payment or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing actual receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Packing list, insurance certificate, and any contract of sale documenting the export transaction
  • Persal or transport documents showing movement of goods from South Africa to an export destination outside SACU

South Africa has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or pre-cleared e-invoicing regime. VAT invoices may be issued electronically, provided the electronic system ensures authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility. There is no mandatory import/export digital stamping into a government fiscal repository; SARS may request access to taxpayer accounting systems via eFiling and audit protocols. A full tax invoice is required for supplies exceeding R 5,000 (or simplified invoice for amounts up to R 5,000), and specific content requirements under VAT Rule 19 apply.

SARS and the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) apply heightened scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Incorrect zero-rating exposes the claimant to additional VAT, interest, and understatement penalties of up to 200% of the understated amount, and supporting documentation failures may result in reversal of output tax recoveries. Fraudent schemes attract criminal prosecution under tax and customs legislation, including potential imprisonment up to 5 years or more for tax evasion; SARS may also issue a 100% additional tax under administrative penalty provisions, and directors of a private company may be held personally liable for VAT debts where the company traded recklessly.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is a managed-float, non-internationalized currency. The exchange control regime is dual-tier: the current account is substantially liberalized, while the capital account is subject to authorization and reporting. For this REIT, current-account debits — operational expenses, professional fees, insurance, and localized property management costs — may be settled through an authorized dealer without prior SARB approval, provided the remittance does not exceed the tax clearance threshold of ZAR 10 million per transaction. Capital-account movements are more constrained: inward foreign direct investment (equity subscription into the (Pty) Ltd) is permitted but must be reported by the authorized dealer under the applicable Balance of Payments (BOP) code with supporting documentation (share subscription agreement, board resolutions, source-of-funds evidence); outward capital repatriation — reduction of share capital, redemption of preference shares, or return of capital on liquidation — requires prior FinSurv approval, a SARS tax clearance certificate, and proof that local taxes (including Capital Gains Tax) have been settled. Profit and dividend remittance hurdles are moderate: non-resident shareholders may receive REIT distributions freely, but the company must withhold Dividends Tax at the prevailing rate (20%) and evidence payment to SARS; a tax clearance certificate is demanded by the authorized dealer on any remittance above ZAR 10 million. Overall convertibility is therefore partial: current-account flows are open and effectively on-demand; capital-account flows, while permissible, carry a documentary burden and non-discretionary tax-clearance conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Exchange Control Manual (Export of Goods and Services), a resident exporter must receive full payment for exported goods within six months from the date of shipment; proceeds received in foreign currency must be surrendered to an authorized dealer within 30 days of receipt. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100%: all export proceeds must be converted into ZAR at the ruling rate, unless a prior FinSurv exemption permits retention in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA). This REIT does not earn export proceeds in its ordinary course; however, any supplementary cross-border services revenue (e.g., property management or hospitality services rendered to a non-resident) will be characterized as an eligible receipt and will trigger the same six-month repatriation window and the unconditional 30-day surrender/conversion obligation. Non-compliance attracts administrative penalties and a negative SARB compliance rating that would impair future dividend remittance applications.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, with policy oversight shared by the National Treasury of the Republic of South Africa; the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) supervises AML/CFT compliance in parallel.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury or escrow rail; MODERATE only for incidental low-value operating disbursements. The Banks Act 1990 reserves the business of taking demand deposits to registered banks, and a foreign electronic money institution (EMI) — such as Wise, Payoneer, or other digital wallet providers — cannot lawfully hold the REIT capital escrow, cannot originate an inbound equity subscription, and cannot execute a dividend distribution wire to non-resident shareholders because the South African authorized dealer must validate the flow with the SARB BOP code and the tax clearance certificate. Foreign EMIs could at most serve as a convenient corridor for small management recharges, but doing so introduces a second AML/KYC layer, delays the audit trail, and conflicts with the FIC's 'authorized dealer only' debt-collection and distribution guidance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol: (1) the non-resident shareholder or offshore trust instructs its bank to transmit via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the South African authorized dealer's nostro account, naming beneficiary 'ZA_PTY_LTD — Capital Escrow Account'; (2) the authorized dealer files the relevant SARB Exchange Control reporting, classifying the transaction under the prescribed BOP code — 6301 for inward direct equity investment, 6302 for intra-group foreign debt; (3) before crediting the escrow, the bank must hold a valid SARS tax clearance certificate for any tranche above ZAR 10 million, the executed subscription or loan agreement, certified board resolutions, and the FICA/KYC pack described above; (4) the foreign currency is converted at the ruling market rate unless the REIT has pre-booked a forward exchange contract; (5) the unique UETR reference, SWIFT confirmation, and due-diligence pack are archived as the permanent inward-remittance audit trail; (6) any intra-group loan must be recorded with the authorized dealer and included in the SARB BOP-FA return.
