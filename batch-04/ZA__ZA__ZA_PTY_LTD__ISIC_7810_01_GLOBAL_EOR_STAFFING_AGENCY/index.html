Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); electronic filing, registration and compliance via the SARS eFiling portal (www.sarsefiling.co.za) and SARS branch network.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African Proprietary Limited company must obtain a SARS income tax reference number by registering on eFiling (or at a SARS branch) using: the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, certified IDs of directors/shareholders, the company's banking details, and the appointment of a resident public officer. The company should also register for VAT, PAYE, UIF, and SDL if applicable. Foreign shareholders must submit the relevant SARS non-resident declarations and supporting incorporation documents. SARS issues a tax reference number and tax compliance status pin automatically through eFiling after verification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (South African resident company subject to normal income tax on worldwide taxable income, with no general pass-through regime; a (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person for income tax and VAT purposes). — A foreign-owned South African Proprietary Limited company must obtain a SARS income tax reference number by registering on eFiling (or at a SARS branch) using: the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, certified IDs of directors/shareholders, the company's banking details, and the appointment of a resident public officer. The company should also register for VAT, PAYE, UIF, and SDL if applicable. Foreign shareholders must submit the relevant SARS non-resident declarations and supporting incorporation documents. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (South African resident company subject to normal income tax on worldwide taxable income, with no general pass-through regime; a (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person for income tax and VAT purposes).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under South African law. A (Pty) Ltd is automatically taxed as a company. Available statutory elections include election under section 12E for taxation as a Small Business Corporation (reduced graduated rates, subject to shareholder and revenue limits), VAT group registration election under the VAT Act (where two or more resident companies are under common control), voluntary VAT registration for taxable supplies above ZAR 50,000, apportionment/input tax election for partially taxable suppliers, and the election to account for VAT on the payments basis for vendors with qualifying turnover.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration at ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (or where it will be exceeded in the next 30 days); voluntary registration at ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods where the supplier exports the goods by the due date for the VAT return, or supplies services to a non-resident person who is not in South Africa at the time the services are rendered and the services are physically rendered outside South Africa. For an EOR/staffing payroll business providing administrative and payroll services to foreign clients, zero-rating is available if the services are supplied to and consumed by non-residents outside South Africa and supporting documentary evidence is retained. Temporary VAT zero-rating rules also apply to movable goods supplied to recipients outside South Africa where the goods are exported under customs procedures.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500) / bill of entry with customs clearing number
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / travel document proving physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, central invoice stamping, or government API clearance for VAT invoices. Electronic tax invoices are permitted provided they comply with section 20 of the VAT Act (all prescribed particulars, issuance within 21 days of supply, and adequate data integrity controls). SARS eFiling and SARS API integration are used for filing VAT returns and data exchange, but there is no mandatory country-specific clearance API for business-to-business electronic invoices at this stage.

SARS may claw back zero-rated input VAT and impose additional output tax where export documentation is deficient, where goods or services are not genuinely exported, or where the supplier fails to prove delivery into an export destination. Under the Tax Administration Act, understatement penalties range from 10% to 75% of the shortfall depending on conduct, plus interest at the prescribed rate. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, false customs declarations, or deliberate non-repatriation of export proceeds may attract criminal prosecution, hefty fines, and imprisonment of up to five years, in addition to full VAT recovery and disqualification from VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa accepts IMF Article VIII obligations, so current-account transactions are freely convertible without SARB approval; cross-border EOR service fees, contractor salaries, and operational service payments may be settled in any permitted foreign currency and remitted through an Authorized Dealer on submission of bona fide supporting documentation. The capital account remains subject to a managed regime: inward equity investment, share transfers to non-residents, and external shareholder loans require Authorized Dealer registration, use of prescribed capital purpose codes, and, in the case of external loans above ZAR 50 million or structurally complex inward/outward investments, prior FinSurv approval. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful but practically gated by: (i) a valid SARS Tax Clearance Certificate in respect of the remitting company and the non-resident beneficial owner; (ii) audited annual financial statements or an Authorized Dealer designatory certificate confirming sufficient distributable reserves; (iii) a board resolution declaring the dividend; (iv) proof of dividends tax at 15%, or a treaty-reduced rate where applicable; and (v) the dealer's foreign-exchange reporting return. The South African rand is a freely floating currency with no exchange-rate bands or rationing; the main compliance risk for a global EOR platform is mischaracterizing equity injections as current-account payments, which triggers FinSurv verification, documentary penalties, and suspension of the dealer's delegated authority.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, the EOR platform's cross-border client fees constitute an export of services for exchange-control purposes. All export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa through an Authorized Dealer within 180 days from the date of the invoice or, if earlier, the date the services are rendered. The historical mandatory surrender ratio was abolished in the 2010 liberalization, so there is no compulsory conversion percentage: the exporter may retain 100% of service proceeds in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with a South African Authorized Dealer or, upon concession, in an approved offshore account; any elective conversion into rand must be executed through an Authorized Dealer at the prevailing market rate. Export receipts must be reported to FinSurv through the dealer's ongoing balance-of-payments submissions and, when retained in an FCA, through monthly aggregated reporting. Non-repatriation within the 180-day period or unauthorized offshore retention is an exchange-control violation exposing the entity to administrative penalties, increased monitoring, revocation of trade facilities, and potential criminal liability, making periodic cash-flow reconciliation of invoices received against incoming SWIFT credits a mandatory treasury control.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv, formerly the Exchange Control Department), pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations of 1961. FinSurv exercises delegated exchange-control authority through Authorized Dealers, being the domestic commercial banks licensed to transact foreign exchange, and coordinates enforcement with the South African Revenue Service (SARS) through tax-clearance and balance-of-payments reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: CONDITIONALLY VIABLE strictly as an execution layer, never as the sole banking rail for the South African resident entity. Wise operates in South Africa through a registered local entity licensed as an authorized financial services provider under FSCA oversight and offers efficient contractor payments into more than 40 jurisdictions; however, all incoming ZAR or hard-currency settlement of international EOR receivables must first clear through a SARB-licensed Authorized Dealer because an offshore digital EMI cannot maintain a ZAR IBAN, lawfully perform ZAR conversions, or discharge the company's FinSurv export-proceeds reporting obligations without a local banking partner. The optimal Treasury architecture is: receive client fees into a Tier-1 Authorized Dealer current account or FCA; retain the proportion payable to South African contractors for execution through the local payroll direct-debit and ACB rail; and transmit the offshore proportion through Wise or a similarly licensed payment aggregator such as Convera or Corpay, with each batch covered by a pre-approved standing mandate and a machine-readable payroll ledger. Offshore-only EMIs domiciled in the EU or UK with no FSCA authorization cannot lawfully solicit South African corporate clients and will be rejected by local Authorized Dealers for SARB reporting, thereby rendering pure-offshore EMI configuration non-viable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial capitalization of the proprietary limited company, the compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 equivalent) transfer denominated in ZAR, USD, EUR, or GBP from the foreign shareholder's bank to the company's account held at a South African Authorized Dealer. The remittance must reference the standard SARB purpose code for inward direct investment in equity capital, the receiving bank records the transaction on its balance-of-payments return as an equity contribution, and the company must provide the prescribed FinSurv non-resident declaration, its CIPC certificate, the share register extract, the board resolution approving the share issue, and the completed FICA corporate file. Proceeds are initially placed in an inward-funding suspense account and released to the operational ZAR account or the Foreign Currency Account after verification, normally within one to three business days. Subsequent working-capital injections from non-resident shareholders must follow either: a formal subordinated shareholder loan registered with the Authorized Dealer and, where the principal exceeds ZAR 50 million, approved in advance by FinSurv; or a documented intercompany settlement in respect of actual services rendered. A prohibited loop structure arises if foreign EOR fee income is routed offshore and re-injected into South Africa as equity or subordinated debt without a genuine business purpose, and FinSurv will require demonstrable use of funds, source-of-wealth evidence, and transactional flow charts before accepting the second injection.Outbound disbursements for the EOR contractor payroll and ancillary trade follow a layered clearing-rail hierarchy: (i) Local payroll rail: South African contractors are paid through the national real-time clearing and settlement system (SAMOS/RTCS) and the Automated Clearing Bureau (ACB) using a local payroll direct-debit or direct-credit code with full PAYE/UIF/SDL reconciliation; (ii) Cross-border mass payroll rail: salaries to offshore contractors are executed as aggregated SWIFT MT103 batches through the Authorized Dealer's international mass-payment platform (such as FNB Global, Standard Bank International e-Channel, or Absa Global Access) or through licensed aggregators including Wise Business, Convera, and Corpay, which localize funds into beneficiary bank accounts by RTGS or ACH in the contractor's jurisdiction; (iii) Trade settlement rail: where the employer-of-record model additionally procures hardware, equipment, or employee benefits in kind, documentary collections and confirmed or unconfirmed Commercial Letters of Credit are permitted only when underpinned by purchase contracts, transport documents, and customs entries, with payment released on complying presentation; and (iv) Statutory outbound rail: foreign withholding taxes, VAT, and payroll statutory deductions are settled through SARS eFiling using the South African banking rails. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: CONDITIONALLY VIABLE strictly as an execution layer, never as the sole banking rail for the South African resident entity. Wise operates in South Africa through a registered local entity licensed as an authorized financial services provider under FSCA oversight and offers efficient contractor payments into more than 40 jurisdictions; however, all incoming ZAR or hard-currency settlement of international EOR receivables must first clear through a SARB-licensed Authorized Dealer because an offshore digital EMI cannot maintain a ZAR IBAN, lawfully perform ZAR conversions, or discharge the company's FinSurv export-proceeds reporting obligations without a local banking partner. The optimal Treasury architecture is: receive client fees into a Tier-1 Authorized Dealer current account or FCA; retain the proportion payable to South African contractors for execution through the local payroll direct-debit and ACB rail; and transmit the offshore proportion through Wise or a similarly licensed payment aggregator such as Convera or Corpay, with each batch covered by a pre-approved standing mandate and a machine-readable payroll ledger. Offshore-only EMIs domiciled in the EU or UK with no FSCA authorization cannot lawfully solicit South African corporate clients and will be rejected by local Authorized Dealers for SARB reporting, thereby rendering pure-offshore EMI configuration non-viable.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; (28% for prior years; small business corporation turnover-based graduated rate 0%-21% if the (Pty) Ltd qualifies with annual turnover below ZAR 20 million and meets the SBC definition in s12E).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% (s64E Income Tax Act 58 of 1962) on dividends paid or declared by the South African (Pty) Ltd, calculated on the amount actually paid without set-off; exemption applies to dividends paid to resident companies exempt under s10(1)(k), the SA Government, a SA branch of a foreign company holding the shares as trading assets, and certain public benefit/retirement vehicles; international treaty rates commonly reduce the 20% rate to 5%-15% depending on shareholding (e.g., 5% for shareholdings of at least 10% under the SA-US, SA-UK, and SA-Mauritius DTAs, 10%-15% for portfolio holdings).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South Africa uses no statutory de minimis turnover or related-party threshold that automatically triggers the Local File or Master File; SARS adopts the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and expects contemporaneous arm's-length documentation for any cross-border connected-person transaction, including EOR service fees, payroll reimbursements, software royalties, and management charges; a full Local File (s31 TP policy, functional analysis, benchmarking study) is the prudent default for a global EOR operating on cross-border payroll rails and is specifically reviewed under SARS TP Practice Note 7 and the Transactional Net Margin Method; the Global Minimum Tax Act 2024 (effective for years commencing on or after 31 December 2024) imposes a 15% minimum top-up tax (IIR/UTPR) only on MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, but the SARS TP documentation expectation remains applicable to all connected cross-border dealings.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substantive tests arise through four overlapping doctrines: (i) the s31 transfer-pricing rule requires the SA EOR to demonstrate real functions, assets, and risks and prevents pricing that reflects artificial allocation of profit offshore; (ii) the general anti-avoidance rules (s80A-s80L) recharacterize impermissible avoidance arrangements lacking commercial substance, including circular payroll flows and conduit interposed entities; (iii) the CFC regime (s9D) attributes undistributed foreign passive income of a controlled foreign company to the South African shareholder; (iv) the GAAR/domestic-law PE definition may be triggered if the SA company claims to be an independent 'employer of record' but in substance merely routes funds from a foreign principal to SA workers without assuming payroll, SACU, and dismissal risk. Directors must maintain board minutes, payroll contracts, and bank statements proving that the (Pty) Ltd genuinely employs the workforce, processes PAYE/UIF/SDL, and retains employment-law liability in South Africa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Misstatement

Statutory sanction: SARS understatement penalty under s222/s223 TAA of up to 150% of the shortfall (up to 200% for withholding taxes) plus interest at the prescribed rate; criminal conviction under s235 TAA attracts a fine of up to ZAR 100,000 and imprisonment of up to five years, and up to ZAR 250,000 and ten years on a subsequent or aggravated offence; the tax debt is not discharged by criminal punishment.

Director personal liability: Under s160 and s162 TAA the SARS has a statutory right to recover unpaid company taxes (PAYE, VAT, provisional tax) from the director who is a 'representative taxpayer' if the failure is due to negligence or fraud, without proof of misconduct beyond knowledge of the deficiency; SARS may issue a s194 travel ban (blocking certificate), attach personal bank accounts, and institute civil sequestration proceedings; directors who sign false returns are individually criminally liable up to ten years' imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS recharacterizes or disregards the arrangement under s80C-s80D (GAAR), adjusts the consideration to an arm's-length amount under s31, and imposes an understatement penalty of 75%-150% for 'no reasonable grounds' or 'intentional evasion' plus interest; under the CFC rules (s9D) foreign dividends or passive income may be attributed and taxed in SA at 27%; a shell with a 100% foreign parent may also trigger the new Global Minimum Tax (15%) if the group is within the EUR 750 million threshold.

Director personal liability: SARS may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for the whole deficient tax plus penalties under s160 TAA; the court may issue a conservation order over personal assets, SARS may apply to liquidate the company (which voids all benefit of the arrangement), and directors risk an arrest warrant for criminal tax fraud (s235 TAA) as the arrangement is treated as a deliberate scheme to evade tax.
Unpaid or Diverted Payroll Taxes (PAYE/UIF/SDL) and Employee Trust Monies

Statutory sanction: SARS imposes 10%-plus prescribed interest on the outstanding amount, a 200% understatement penalty on the withheld-tax shortfall, and fixed monthly penalties for late returns; s236 TAA makes the deliberate failure to pay over tax a criminal offence punishable by a fine and imprisonment of up to ten years; SARS issues a s164 summons and a s169 estimate of assessment based on payroll records.

Director personal liability: Directors are automatically jointly and severally liable under s160 TAA as representative taxpayers for all unremitted PAYE/UIF/SDL; SARS may attach a director's personal pension, overseas property, and personal credit position without first exhausting civil recovery against the company; a travel ban is typically imposed before the start of an audit or criminal prosecution, and directors face disqualification from acting as company directors under the South African Companies Act 71 of 2008 after conviction.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) may impose administrative penalties of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention of the FIC Act (and up to ZAR 50 million in aggravated categories) plus daily penalties; a South African court may order disgorgement of the entire payroll flow; under POCA the laundering of proceeds is punishable by imprisonment of up to 15 years and a fine of up to ZAR 100 million; the company faces seizure of all funds in the payroll bank account and denial of the banking licence for its payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and the AML Compliance Officer are personally liable for FIC Act penalties where the failure is attributable to their gross negligence; courts may issue a personal forfeiture order over assets linked to the laundered payroll; directors face criminal prosecution under POCA and the FIC Act with prison terms of up to 15 years, and the beneficial-ownership register mismatch is a separate criminal offence carrying imprisonment (up to 10 years under the Companies Act as amended) plus a personal fine; the FIC may also obtain a travel ban against the director pending investigation.
