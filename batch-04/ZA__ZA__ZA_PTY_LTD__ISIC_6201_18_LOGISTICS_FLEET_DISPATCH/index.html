Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), established under the South African Revenue Service Act 34 of 1997; primary digital portal is SARS eFiling at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; customs administration is exercised under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964; exchange-control supervision is delegated to the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) Financial Surveillance Department through Authorised Dealer banks.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Pty Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 to obtain the company registration number (Enterprise number). Step 2: Register the company on SARS eFiling using the RAV01 (Registration, Amendments and Verification) form to obtain the Income Tax Reference Number; for a company with non-resident shareholders or directors, attach passport and proof-of-address documentation and appoint a SARS-registered representative or tax practitioner with power of attorney. Step 3: Apply for voluntary VAT registration through the VAT101 application (now integrated into the RAV01 flow) where future taxable turnover justifies registration; if taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in a 12-month period, compulsory VAT registration must be completed within 21 days of crossing the threshold. Step 4: Open a corporate bank account with a SARB Authorised Dealer and complete banking Customer Due Diligence; inbound non-resident equity investment requires SARB approval/reporting through the authorised dealer. Step 5: Register as an exporter under section 74A of the VAT Act (form VAT 104) to obtain export-related VAT privileges, and obtain a Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN for customs applications. All registrations are completed electronically via eFiling; no in-person appearance at a SARS branch is required for standard Pty Ltd registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification follows the Pty Ltd legal form under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Available elections include: (1) Small Business Corporation election under s.12E for qualifying entities with annual turnover below R20 million and all shareholders being natural persons, attracting progressive rates of 0%/7%/21%/27% on taxable income up to the R550,000 threshold; (2) Value-Added Tax group election under s.50 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 for related companies under common control; (3) Corporate restructure rollover elections under s.42–s.47 of the Income Tax Act; (4) hybrid mismatch elections and ordering rules under s.23M–s.23Q implementing OECD BEPS Action 2, including election to apply the deemed mismatched rule to structured arrangements; (5) Controlled Foreign Company (s.9D) regimes and transfer-pricing documentation election for s.31 audited contemporaneous records.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 annual taxable turnover triggers compulsory registration (s.23(1) VAT Act); voluntary registration is available where taxable turnover exceeds R50,000 in the preceding 12 months (s.23(2)); non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African residents are subject to a separate s.54(1) registration regime under the Electronic Services Regulations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: s.11(1)(a) VAT Act zero-rates goods supplied under a sale or instalment credit agreement where the supplier exports the goods (or the buyer exports them as a commercial export) within 90 days of the date of supply, supported by documentary proof under VAT Rule 18. Services: s.11(2)(l) VAT Act zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident unless the services are directly connected with land or movable property situated in South Africa, are rendered while the non-resident is physically present in South Africa, or are consumed or used in South Africa (SARS Interpretation Note 31). A telematics and fleet-dispatch SaaS platform qualifies for zero-rating where the non-resident business customer receives and uses the software outside South Africa; the contract must demonstrate the place of effective use, the non-resident status of the recipient (VAT registration number or equivalent documentary evidence), and the absence of local South African consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 series) fully released by SARS Customs showing the consolidated entry and exit of the goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of the goods from South African territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list mirroring the SAD500 declared values
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a SARB Authorised Dealer bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident buyer
  • For services exports: signed non-resident service agreement, dated SaaS platform usage/delivery report, and SWIFT credit advice or foreign bank statement evidencing cross-border settlement
  • Exporter registration certificate under s.74A VAT Act (VAT 104) and, where applicable, the customs valuation schedule

South Africa currently imposes no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime. A full or abridged tax invoice must be issued within 15 days after the date of supply (s.20(4) VAT Act), retained for five years (s.55 VAT Act), and filed in the bi-monthly VAT201 return via SARS eFiling. SARS operates a voluntary e-Invoice platform aligned to OECD and SAF-T standards and provides eFiling APIs for tax practitioners and enterprise systems; real-time electronic clearance applies only to customs transactions through the SARS Customs SAD500 declaration lifecycle, not to ordinary VAT invoicing.

SARS may audit export zero-rating claims under s.60–s.62 VAT Act read with the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (TAA). An invalid zero-rating determination produces an additional VAT assessment with interest at the prescribed SARS rate plus an understatement penalty under TAA s.223 of 10% (substantial understatement exceeding the greater of R1 million or 5% of tax), 25% (failure to take reasonable care), 50% (no reasonable grounds), 75% (intentional or reckless conduct), or 150% (intentional tax evasion after an assessment or flag). Fraudulent export claims constitute criminal offences under s.58 VAT Act and s.234 TAA, carrying fines up to R250,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years; retrospective clawback of input VAT, cancellation of export registration, and suspension of customs clearance privileges may also be imposed. Failure to repatriate and convert export proceeds within 180 days is an exchange-control contravention referred to the SARB Financial Surveillance Department, which may fine the exporter, suspend Authorised Dealer banking facilities, and require immediate disposal of foreign currency.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current account transactions, including profit remittances, provided bona fide documentation is submitted through an authorised dealer. Capital account convertibility requires compliance with residual exchange control regulations. Dividend distributions to non-residents are permitted under general authorisations, subject to payment of 20% dividend withholding tax and submission of audited financial statements if a sS. 64O declaration is required. Inward direct investment enjoys automatic alcohol-free approval via authorised dealers, but outward investments and some capital distributions require SARB approval or notification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received into South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment unless a longer period is approved by an authorised dealer. There is no mandatory statutory conversion ratio; however, the exported value must be declared using a Form E loading (SARS Exports), and the foreign currency receipts must be surrendered to the authorised dealer or retained in a designated Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with full reconciliation and transfer of surplus funds back to South Africa within reasonable promptness. Proceeds may be set off only for pre-approved transactions.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) and the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administering exchange controls under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, with authorised dealers (commercial banks) acting as delegated compliance intermediaries.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or loan capital from non-resident shareholders must be routed via SWIFT MT103 through an authorised dealer. The payment must reference purpose code IC400 (inward direct investment into operational entities) or IC410 for loans. Compliance paperwork includes a Form E (Foreign Investment Declaration) filed by the receiving authorised dealer, the company's registered MOI, a shareholder resolution proving the capital call, source-of-funds evidence, and FICA/FATCA self-certification. Conversion to ZAR is done at the authorised dealer's ruling rate, and the credit is reflected in a newly opened capital escrow account if the funds are intended for infrastructure or working capital purposes. For hybrid debt, a loan agreement s695A notice is filed with SARS.Trade settlement is executed by SWIFT MT103 for software subscription remittances, hosting costs, licensing royalties, and freight dispatch payments outside South Africa. For physical hardware imports (fleet dashcams, telematics gateways) a commercial letter of credit (L/C) may be opened by the authorised dealer; a documentary collection or direct wire can be used for low-value disbursements. All foreign payments above certain thresholds must complete a Form EEC (Exchange Control Form) electronically. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for financial years ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% for earlier years.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% final South African Dividends Tax imposed on the amount of any dividend declared and paid by a resident company to resident or non-resident shareholders, unless a statutory exemption or treaty reduction applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a 12-month accounting period, the ITR14 must be submitted within 12 months after the company's financial year-end; SARS may set an earlier eFiling due date based on the company's provisional tax profile.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First IRP6 payment is due on or before the last business day of the sixth month from the start of the financial year; second IRP6 payment is due on or before the last business day of the financial year. An optional third top-up payment may be made within six months after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: SARS does not prescribe a single statutory local-file threshold, but in practice contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation must be maintainable for any cross-border connected-person affected transaction above ZAR10 million per annum. A CbC report is obligatory for South African-headquartered MNE groups with consolidated revenue of ZAR10 billion or more. For this logistics-fleet SaaS entity, any intercompany royalty or management-fee payment above ZAR10 million should be benchmarked before the return is filed.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulation. However, the combined effect of the place of effective management test, s9D controlled foreign company rules, s31 transfer pricing, s80A-80L general anti-avoidance and treaty beneficial-ownership provisions creates a de facto economic substance doctrine. A ZA logistics SaaS company must show actual board meetings, employee decision-making, software development and commercial risk-bearing in South Africa; a shell invoicing entity with no operational footprint will be recharacterised and treated as unable to claim treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Understatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty up to 100% of the tax shortfall for intentional tax evasion, prescribed interest, criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act and common law fraud, judicial fines, and imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a knowingly false return are jointly and severally liable for the resulting corporate tax debt; SARS can pierce the corporate veil, obtain civil asset seizure orders, impose travel bans and apply for director disqualification.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: SARS adjustment to arm's length pricing under s31, secondary adjustment or deemed dividend treatment, 20% Dividends Tax on deemed dividends, understatement penalty of 50%-100% on the shortfall, and prescribed interest from the original assessment date.

Director personal liability: Board members who approve intercompany prices without a defensible TP policy face liability for wasted company assets and unpaid taxes under the Companies Act; SARS may recover from directors personally and request exit-control orders against non-resident directors.
