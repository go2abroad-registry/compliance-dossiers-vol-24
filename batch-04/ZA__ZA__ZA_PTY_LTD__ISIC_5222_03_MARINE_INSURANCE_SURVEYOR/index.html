Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Proprietary Limited company under the Companies Act, 2008. Share capital may be paid in cash, property, or services; escrow account rules do not apply under the Companies Act, but paid-up subscription funds must be banked in the company's corporate bank account once incorporation is registered.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); online portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sars.gov.za and https://efiling.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

After CIPC incorporation, the company must register on SARS eFiling to obtain an income tax reference number; the public officer (for a non-resident entity, a resident public officer) files the VAT101 new-vendor application for a VAT registration number and submits founding documents (CIPC registration, memorandum of incorporation, director/person particulars, bank confirmation). SARS issues separate income tax and VAT vendor numbers upon approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the (Pty) Ltd is a South African resident company taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate; no automatic pass-through to shareholders.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available; a South African (Pty) Ltd is compulsorily taxed as a company. Available statutory elections include small business corporation status, VAT vendor grouping, VAT voluntary registration election, and certain CFC and transfer-pricing elections. Hybrid mismatch rules operate as automatic anti-abuse countermeasures rather than taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration threshold: R1,000,000 taxable supplies in the preceding 12-month period; voluntary registration threshold: R50,000 taxable supplies. Marine survey fees are taxable supplies unless a zero-rating or exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies under section 56(1) of the VAT Act when movable goods are supplied to a non-resident under sale/export and physically removed from South Africa within the prescribed period, with acceptable proof of export. Services: section 54 zero-rates a supply of services to a non-resident recipient where the services are not used or consumed in South Africa; a marine insurance surveying and damage-inspection service may qualify only where the service benefit is received outside South Africa and is not supplied directly in connection with movable or immovable property situated in South Africa at the time of performance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) and bill of lading/air waybill/road manifest evidencing physical export of goods
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and survey/claim report (where services are the subject of export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank foreign-currency receipt confirmation evidencing proceeds repatriation
  • Non-resident declaration/affidavit from the recipient and contract terms demonstrating offshore use or consumption

SARS does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping requirement for every invoice. VAT vendors must issue prescribed VAT tax invoices, submit VAT201 returns via eFiling, and retain full electronic audit trails; SARS e-invoicing is partly voluntary and requires compliance with SARS technical specifications if adopted.

SARS will scrutinise export documentation, recipient non-residency, use/consumption, and receipt of foreign funds. Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating is clawed back by additional VAT assessment plus understatement penalties, statutory interest, suspension of refunds and possible criminal prosecution; failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day exchange-control window attracts SARB administrative penalties and may invalidate the export zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand (ZAR) is a partially convertible currency. Current-account transactions (cross-border payment for marine survey services, reinsurance premium/claim flows, director fees and dividends) are freely accessible through an authorised dealer on submission of the underlying contract/invoice and a valid SARS tax clearance certificate. Capital-account flows (inward equity subscription, shareholder loans, outward direct investment) remain subject to exchange control approval via Form A.2/A.1 or FinSurv rulings. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted only from after-tax reserves; a SARS 'good standing' tax clearance is mandatory, and a standard 20% dividend withholding tax applies unless reduced by an applicable DTA.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds derived from exported services (including marine hull/cargo claims inspection fees earned from non-resident insurers) must be brought into South Africa within 180 days from the invoice date. Upon receipt, the hard-currency amount is cleared through the authorised dealer and must be either credited in rand (effectively a 100% conversion obligation) or, if the exporter has obtained approval for a Foreign Currency Account, retained in that account while still being subject to the 180-day repatriation window. No reduced statutory surrender percentage applies to ZAR; exporters may not retain foreign exchange offshore without FinSurv consent.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administering the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; enforcement and day-to-day transactional approvals are delegated to authorised dealers, i.e., registered South African commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as primary clearing rail; SUPPLEMENTARY ONLY. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) do not hold a SARB banking or authorised-dealer licence and therefore cannot open a domestic ZAR clearing account or convert currency lawfully inside South Africa. They remain viable only for (i) receipt of low-value multicurrency survey fees parked outside SA and (ii) disbursement to foreign sub-surveyors, provided the underlying funds were first repatriated/converted through a local authorised dealer. Persistent routing of company revenue through an unlicensed EMI would breach FIC Act account-monitoring obligations and exchange control rules and would erode the entity's SARS/CIPC compliance position.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must transmit the injected equity by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for large nostro-covered transfers) from a bank account held in the shareholder's own name; the 20x narrative field must state the company's exact name, its CIPC registration number and the descriptor 'Subscription for shares - marine insurance surveying (ISIC 5222)'. The receiving authorised dealer validates the flow, applies the BOP direct-investment code, and credits a ZAR operational account. Foreign-currency funds are automatically converted to rand at the dealing rate; a shareholder loan requires Form A.2 completion and appears as inward lending in FinSurv records. The allotment (CoR 21.2) must be filed with CIPC within 31 days of receipt. The company should retain the MT103 confirmation, the bank's foreign-exchange certificate, the board allotment resolution and the shareholder's source-of-funds dossier for regulatory audit.Clearing rails are tiered by counterparty: (i) non-resident insurers, reinsurers and independent co-surveyors are paid via SWIFT MT103 through the authorised dealer using Form A.1 and BOP code 3600 (other business services) or the closest marine-survey service code; (ii) intra-group claims settlement excess-of-loss payments use MT202 COVER with a nostro account; (iii) high-value inspection contracts linked to exported/imported cargo are best supported by a documentary letter of credit (UCP600) issued by the corporate bank; (iv) SACU/SADC counterparties can be settled in rand through the country's RTGS (SAMOS) with SWIFT MT103 confirmation.
