Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; headquarters at 299 Bronkhorst Street, Brooklyn, Pretoria; Customs and Excise administered by SARS Customs Division via the SARS Customs Portal/UKS system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African Proprietary Limited company must first be incorporated by the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Thereafter, the company must obtain a SARS Income Tax Reference Number by registering as a taxpayer via SARS eFiling (or at a SARS branch) within 21 business days of incorporation, submitting the CIT registration form (previously IT77), the Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of directors'/shareholders' IDs or passports, and a South African business address. For VAT, a separate VAT registration (form VAT101) must be filed: compulsory registration with effect from the first day of the month following the month in which taxable supplies exceeded or are likely to exceed R1,000,000 in 12 months; voluntary registration available from R50,000. Foreign entities with no local trading presence registering for VAT on imported digital services must appoint a SARS-approved representative taxpayer (VAT101/electronic form). A customs code is separately required for import/export activities through the SARS Customs registration process (permanent trader registration, with a unique importer/exporter code). The same TIN/VAT number is used for all SARS taxes (income tax, VAT, PAYE, customs).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election exists for (Pty) Ltd companies; the entity is mandatorily subject to normal tax at 27% (applies to years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023) plus the 20% dividends tax on distributions. There is no hybrid mismatch election specifically; the hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act (s. 23M-23MB) operate mandatory and override contractual characterisations. A company may elect the VAT cash basis (VAT201/CB application) if annual taxable supplies do not exceed R2.5 million. The only sector-specific election relevant to e-commerce is the voluntary VAT registration threshold election (R50,000). Substantive shareholding participation exemptions in respect of foreign dividends may be claimed via s. 10B(2) election (subject to anti-hybrid rules).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 taxable supplies (including zero-rated) in any 12-month period; Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 threshold (for supplies in the course of an enterprise); foreign suppliers of imported electronic services (including digital downloads and online sales of instruments/audio gear having a nexus to South African consumers) must register for VAT regardless of threshold from 1 April 2019, subject to the R1 million de minimis exemption on a look-through basis for non-connected persons unless voluntarily registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods, including musical instruments and studio audio gear sold online to foreign customers, qualify for zero-rating under s. 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act provided (i) the goods are supplied to a recipient resident outside South Africa; (ii) the supplier retains proof of export and the goods are exported within 90 days of the date of the tax invoice; and (iii) the supplier obtains and keeps a valid Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) evidencing clearance. Non-resident services (offshore digital services such as downloadable audio software or remote tuning/training) qualify for zero-rating under s. 11(2)(l) (services physically rendered outside South Africa) or s. 11(2)(k) (services to non-residents not used in South Africa), with the burden of proof on the vendor to substantiate from contracts, IP addresses, payment banking details, and the recipient's foreign status. VAT refunds arise where input VAT exceeds output VAT (including on zero-rated export sales); refunds are processed after a VAT compliance audit trigger and SARS must pay within 21 business days of assessment of the refund (failing which SARS pays interest).

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / EX-1) bearing SARS customs unique consignment reference
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing delivery to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice (matching tax invoice with VAT zero-rating indicator)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign currency receipt) evidenced via South African authorized dealer bank form B641 (inward foreign currency payment) or bank statement showing conversion to ZAR with source of funds
  • Proof of the recipient’s non-resident status (e.g., foreign VAT registration number, passport, or utility bill)
  • Packing list (for containerised consolidated export shipments)
  • Contract of sale or general terms with the offshore buyer

SARS has formally adopted an OECD-aligned e-invoicing framework (SARS e-invoicing, based on the OECD’s interoperability model) and is progressively mandating real-time transactional reporting via the eFiling API gateway and the SARS Interoperability Framework. For VAT vendors, mandatory electronic submission of VAT201 returns via eFiling, and all tax invoices must be issued within 21 days of the time of supply and contain the statutory particulars under s. 20(4) of the VAT Act, including full supplier details, VAT registration number, description, quantity, price, VAT amount. SARS requires near-real-time submission of Customs declarations (SAD500) for exported goods through the Customs clearance environment, and selected high-risk goods (electronics/audio) are subject to digital pre-clearance and risk-based audit screening with machine-readable EX-1/export declarations and loading list filing. While full real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance is not yet applied to domestic B2B e-commerce, SARS mandates digital stamping for all fiscal documents and, from 2024 to 2027, is rolling out mandatory e-invoicing for VAT vendors with aggregate taxable turnover above ZAR 10 million (phased); no physical adhesive tax stamps apply to online-retailed musical instruments, but importation of goods into South Africa requires electronic customs clearance and A-rated vendor status for fast-track clearance.

SARS and the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) Financial Surveillance Department subject export zero-rating to intensive documentation scrutiny; failure to prove export within 90 days results in the VAT becoming payable at 15% plus interest (prescribed interest rate). Under VAT Act s. 60 and Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, s. 222-223, an understatement penalty is applied to the 'understatement percentage' of the shortfall: 50% for reasonable care not taken, 75% for gross negligence, and 150% for intentional tax evasion (increased to 200% where the taxpayer obstructs SARS). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion and customs fraud, triggering retrospective clawback of all previously refunded VAT for up to 5 years, additional penalties of up to 200% of the shortfall, criminal prosecution with fines up to ZAR 1,000,000 or imprisonment up to 10 years (under the Tax Administration Act and Customs and Excise Act s. 81), debarment of the vendor's VAT registration, and blocking of export facilities; directors of the (Pty) Ltd company may be held personally and jointly liable for the company's VAT debts resulting from fraudulent conduct under s. 179 of the Tax Administration Act and s. 180-181 (non-payment of trust moneys). SARS retains the power to issue a jeopardy assessment and attach bank accounts where export zero-rating refund claims are suspected of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is a partially convertible currency. Current-account transactions (cross-border payments for imported musical instruments, overseas logistics, platform fees and other trade-related disbursements) are liberalised and may be executed by any SARB-licensed authorised dealer upon submission of standard trade documentation, without FinSurv prior approval. Capital-account transactions remain controlled: non-resident equity contributions and shareholder loans must be routed through an authorised dealer, reported on Form IFR within 30 days of receipt, and registered in the company's foreign-liability record. Profit remittance (dividends) to non-resident shareholders is permitted through an authorised dealer without individual FinSurv approval provided that: (i) the dividend is declared from genuine, realised distributable reserves; (ii) the company passes the solvency and liquidity test under section 4 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008; (iii) the founding equity was admitted through a compliant inward-investment reporting trail; and (iv) 20% Dividends Tax is withheld (reduced where a Double Taxation Agreement applies). Principal remittance hurdles are unfiled IFR/IFL returns, unrepatriated export proceeds older than 180 days, disallowed management or head-office charges, utilisation of non-distributable reserves, and exchange-control contraventions that have not been regularised.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory conversion abolished: with effect from 1 January 2021, the previous 20% surrender/compulsory conversion of export proceeds into rand no longer applies; the exporter may retain 100% of export earnings in a permitted Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an authorised dealer, or convert at market rates as treasury policy dictates. The statutory ceiling is that every export consignment must be fully paid for within 180 days from the prima facie date of shipment (the bill-of-lading or airway-bill date, or courier dispatch evidence for e-commerce parcels), unless the authorised dealer grants a formal extension for a specific contract. For the ISIC 4791 online business, card-acquirer and PSP settlements (Stripe, PayPal) are treated as export proceeds: each settlement must be reconciled to the SAD500 export declarations, the courier or parcel records, and the merchant settlement statements, and any incident where the 180-day window is exceeded must be disclosed to the authorised dealer for regularisation. The mandatory conversion ratio is therefore zero, but the default rule remains that all exports must be invoiced in a permitted currency, the proceeds must be repatriated through the authorised market, and the exporter must produce, on demand, proof of the corresponding export movement and the foreign-currency disposition.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting as the designated Exchange Control authority for the National Treasury under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (GNR 1111), as amended, together with the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a secondary collection-aggregation corridor; LOW as the primary treasury or clearing bank. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, Revolut Business) hold no South African Banks Act 94 of 1990 deposit-taking licence and may not open domestic transactional accounts, take deposits from, or extend regulated banking services to, South African residents; their role is confined to the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 payment-processing perimeter, normally through a partnership with an SARB-licensed bank or PASA clearing member. Recommended architecture for this low-risk e-commerce exporter: (i) use Stripe and PayPal solely as global front-door card acquirers; (ii) allow an offshore EMI to aggregate the foreign-currency merchant payouts and sweep them automatically into the SA authorised-dealer FCA via SWIFT; (iii) execute all supplier payments, capital flows and export-proceeds reporting exclusively on the licensed bank rails; and (iv) document the EMI/bank link with a formal treasury services agreement so that no unlicensed deposit-taking or circular-loop inference arises.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a secondary collection-aggregation corridor; LOW as the primary treasury or clearing bank. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, Revolut Business) hold no South African Banks Act 94 of 1990 deposit-taking licence and may not open domestic transactional accounts, take deposits from, or extend regulated banking services to, South African residents; their role is confined to the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 payment-processing perimeter, normally through a partnership with an SARB-licensed bank or PASA clearing member. Recommended architecture for this low-risk e-commerce exporter: (i) use Stripe and PayPal solely as global front-door card acquirers; (ii) allow an offshore EMI to aggregate the foreign-currency merchant payouts and sweep them automatically into the SA authorised-dealer FCA via SWIFT; (iii) execute all supplier payments, capital flows and export-proceeds reporting exclusively on the licensed bank rails; and (iv) document the EMI/bank link with a formal treasury services agreement so that no unlicensed deposit-taking or circular-loop inference arises.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South African TP legislation has no blanket statutory monetary threshold for affected transactions; all cross-border connected-party transactions must be documented on an arm's-length basis. SARS expects contemporaneous Local/Master File preparation where material transactions exceed approximately ZAR 1 million, and Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, the Commissioner relies on statutory residence based on incorporation or effective management, the substance-over-form doctrine, the general anti-avoidance rules, and controlled foreign company attribution under section 9D. A 'letterbox' Pty Ltd with no real staff, premises, decision-making, or risk-taking is a high-risk shell whose worldwide income may be fully taxed in South Africa and whose cross-border transactions may be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Deliberate Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act may result in a fine or imprisonment of up to three years; SARS may raise an estimated assessment, apply an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall, and charge compound interest from the original due date. Records may be seized under a warrant and banking/Stripe/PayPal payment flows reconstructed by forensic audit.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorise the false filing can be personally liable for the company's unpaid tax debts under section 159 TAA, and criminal directors may face arrest, extradition, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income to the arm's-length price, disallow the excess expenditure, impose understatement penalties of 50% to 150% depending on conduct, and levy interest on the corrected tax. The OECD-aligned adjustment may also trigger an anti-avoidance recharacterisation of the transaction, including imputation of a deemed distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed tax returns knowing that the transfer pricing files were fabricated may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; failure to cooperate with an information request can result in administrative penalties up to ZAR 100,000 per issue or imprisonment for contempt.
Shell Company and Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: The company may be held to be a South African tax resident by effective management or incorporation, subjecting worldwide income to 27% CIT. The general anti-avoidance rule may countermand the chosen structure; the entity can be terminated by CIPC if non-compliant, and SARS may hold the nominee or agent personally liable.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil if the company is used as a mere facade; directors may be ordered to contribute from personal assets, issued with travel bans for outstanding tax debts, and disqualified from serving as directors under the Companies Act.
Disguised Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: SARS may recharacterise the payment as a dividend and levy 20% dividends tax on the gross amount, or deny the deduction and apply the interest/royalty withholding tax of 15% plus understatement penalties. Failure to submit the dividend return and pay withheld tax is a criminal offence under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the company to make disguised distributions while leaving tax unpaid may receive personal tax liability notices under section 159 and may be restrained from leaving South Africa until the debt is secured.
Anti-Money Laundering and Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act may impose administrative fines of up to ZAR 10 million for failures to report unusual transactions, while money laundering under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act is punishable by imprisonment of up to 15 years and civil forfeiture of the proceeds. SARS and the Financial Intelligence Centre may share intelligence and freeze bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted personally for money laundering if they knew or should have known that proceeds were the instrumentalities of tax evasion; personal bank accounts, travel documents, and personal property may be restrained during investigation.
