Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no elective hybrid mismatch election are available; a South African (Pty) Ltd is automatically a separate entity-level taxpayer. Relevant statutory elections include voluntary VAT registration under s 23 of the VAT Act where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000, VAT group registration under s 50 of the VAT Act for eligible wholly-owned group entities, and certain foreign exchange and foreign tax credit elections under the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months or are reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in the following 12 months; voluntary registration is available once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for zero-rating under s 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the supplier removes or delivers movable goods through a SARS customs-controlled area to a non-resident destination and full export evidence is retained. Indirect exports under s 11(1)(b) may be zero-rated where the non-resident recipient exports the goods on behalf of the supplier and the supplier obtains and retains proof of export. Offshore services, including international media relations, crisis PR, and communication consultancy supplied to non-resident clients, are zero-rated under s 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act only where the services are not physically rendered in South Africa, are not connected with South African movable or immovable property, and the non-resident recipient is not physically present in South Africa during performance. International transport and related ancillary services require separate statutory authorization.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD500) cleared by SARS Customs
  • Bill of lading, airway waybill or equivalent transport document evidencing export
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank foreign exchange receipt matching the export invoice
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and sale or service contract confirming the non-resident counterparty and export destination

South Africa does not currently impose a general real-time fiscal e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate. Electronic tax invoices are accepted where they preserve authenticity, integrity and readability under s 20(5) of the VAT Act. SARS eFiling is the statutory digital channel for VAT returns, and goods export clearances must be lodged electronically through SARS Customs using the SAD500 declaration; a cleared electronic SAD500 is essential evidence for export zero-rating.

SARS audits require the complete evidential bundle, matching of foreign currency receipts, and compliance with the 180-day exchange-control repatriation deadline. Missing or defective documentation invalidates zero-rating, triggering an additional VAT assessment at 15% plus interest and understatement penalties under s 223 of the Tax Administration Act of up to 200% of the tax shortfall for gross negligence or intentional evasion. Deliberately false export documentation or fraudulent refund claims expose the entity to criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, and summary VAT refund clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a liberalised but administratively supervised convertibility regime. For current account transactions — the central earnings stream of this overseas PR and international media relations firm (retainer fees, crisis-communications retainers, media placement reimbursements, software/streaming subscriptions) — the ZAR is freely convertible against all major currencies: an Authorised Dealer may effect payment or receipt of foreign currency on production of standard commercial documentation (invoice, contract, statement of work) without SARB/FinSurv prior approval. For capital account transactions, inward non-resident equity investment into the ZA (Pty) Ltd is fully liberalised (no prior approval; post-transaction reporting via FinSurv's Electronic Monitoring/EM system), while outward capital transfers, offshore shareholder loans above the AD's discretionary threshold, and certain disposals remain subject to AD due diligence and, for high-value or complex routing, FinSurv approval. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to: (i) dividend withholding tax at the statutory 20% (reduced under most double-taxation treaties, commonly 5–10% for corporate shareholders); (ii) proof of capital importation (EM record formerly generated by Form G5) for loan principal or capital repatriation; (iii) a completed CRS/FATCA self-certification confirming the beneficial shareholder's tax residence; and (iv) solvency-and-liquidity certification in the board resolution. There are no material convertibility restrictions for the expected monthly retainer inflows from OECD-based clients.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The full export consideration for cross-border services (including PR retainers and administrative service fees rendered to non-resident clients) must be received into the South African banking system — either into a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer or by conversion into ZAR — within 180 days from the date of completion of the service/invoice date (the AD may approve a documented extension up to 365 days where settlement cycles justify it). No mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts: 0% of gross proceeds must be converted to ZAR; the exporter may lawfully retain 100% of proceeds in USD, EUR, or GBP in the FCA and disburse directly from that account for permissible purposes. However, the entire gross proceeds must remain within the AD's oversight, and all inbound/outbound FCA movements must be supported by underlying service invoices, recorded in the entity's exchange-control file, and reported through the AD's monthly balance-of-payments returns to FinSurv. The service exporter is required to maintain, for a minimum of five years, a complete documentary trail consisting of executed retainer agreements, monthly invoices, proof-of-receipt SWIFT confirmations, and the AD's record of FCA utilisation.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and the nationwide network of Authorised Dealers (ADs — South African commercial banks licensed under the Banks Act, 1990), exercising delegated exchange control powers under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), as amended, and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility rating. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie and similar) are neither licensed banks nor authorised dealers in South Africa under the Banks Act, 1990, and the Exchange Control Regulations. They cannot independently hold the ZA (Pty) Ltd's ZAR account or act as a receiving agent for export service proceeds, because full export proceeds must land with a South African AD within the 180-day surrender window. Routing recurring US/EU client retainer income through an offshore EMI would be characterised as non-remitted export proceeds and could trigger FinSurv enforcement, AD account restriction, and exchange-control penalties. Acceptable use of an EMI is limited to ancillary outbound disbursements — e.g., settling small overseas subcontractor or media-vendor invoices — provided the underlying transaction is disclosed to the SA AD, covered by valid invoices, and funded from the AD-held FCA or an approved conversion. As a primary inbound clearing rail, offshore EMIs are not viable; the correct architecture is direct SWIFT/wire into the AD's USD/EUR correspondent clearing account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct equity injection by the non-resident shareholder is routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the remitting bank to the chosen AD's inbound clearing desk, with the remittance instruction quoting BOP purpose code 'GIV' (direct inward investment). The AD's documentation checklist comprises: the share-subscription agreement, the board resolution, the payer's confirmed bank details on BIC directory, and the completed inward-investment notification through which the AD issues an EM (Electronic Monitoring) capital-import record that functions as the definitive proof for future dividends, loan repayments and capital repatriation. No SARB or FinSurv prior approval is required for the equity inward transfer; the AD screens the payer, effects conversion at the ruling market rate where the entity elects ZAR, and credits the capital-escrow or operational account. Where the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, the loan agreement must be lodged with the AD, the funds must land through the same AD to preserve the capital-import record, and the term/interest rate must satisfy the AD's arm's-length review; proceeds cannot be offset offshore. For recurring monthly retainer inflows from US clients (who prefer ACH in the domestic US rail), the client should initiate a SWIFT wire into the AD's USD correspondent account at its New York clearing agent rather than a pure ACH transfer; the AD will then advise the USD FCA, preserving the export-remittance record.Outbound trade and operating disbursements are executed through the following clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 correspondent wire for all vendor payments exceeding ZAR 50 000 — media placement, crisis-communications subcontractors, travel, and licensing — supported by the vendor invoice and a plain-language purpose-of-payment descriptor; (2) commercial letters of credit (ICC UCP 600) advised by the AD, used only where physical deliverables are imported (printed campaign materials, event and production supplies), priced at approximately 0.20–0.45% of the LC value; (3) commercial cards for low-value subscription purchases (digital ad platforms such as Google, Meta and programmatic DSPs, news-monitoring databases, cloud/SaaS subscriptions) with monthly aggregation reporting to the AD; (4) standing-order batched SWIFT payments for recurring monthly retainer payouts to reputable overseas sub-agencies, each batch supported by an invoice register. Under the current FinSurv discretionary framework, the AD may process cumulative current-account outward payments up to ZAR 1 billion per entity per calendar year without FinSurv reference; above that threshold FinSurv prior approval is required, a level this entity will not approach. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium feasibility rating. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie and similar) are neither licensed banks nor authorised dealers in South Africa under the Banks Act, 1990, and the Exchange Control Regulations. They cannot independently hold the ZA (Pty) Ltd's ZAR account or act as a receiving agent for export service proceeds, because full export proceeds must land with a South African AD within the 180-day surrender window. Routing recurring US/EU client retainer income through an offshore EMI would be characterised as non-remitted export proceeds and could trigger FinSurv enforcement, AD account restriction, and exchange-control penalties. Acceptable use of an EMI is limited to ancillary outbound disbursements — e.g., settling small overseas subcontractor or media-vendor invoices — provided the underlying transaction is disclosed to the SA AD, covered by valid invoices, and funded from the AD-held FCA or an approved conversion. As a primary inbound clearing rail, offshore EMIs are not viable; the correct architecture is direct SWIFT/wire into the AD's USD/EUR correspondent clearing account.
