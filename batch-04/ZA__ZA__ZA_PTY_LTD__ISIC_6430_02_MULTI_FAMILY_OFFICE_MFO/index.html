Competent corporate registry

Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), an agency of the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (dtic), operating through the e-Services portal at eservices.cipc.co.za.

Governing statute

Companies Act 71 of 2008, as amended by Companies Amendment Acts 16 and 17 of 2024; principal sections: s.13 (incorporation), s.14 (certificate of incorporation), s.15 (Memorandum of Incorporation), s.19 (legal capacity of companies), s.66 (boards), s.69 (vacancies), s.86 (company secretary), s.23 (registered office), and s.161B (beneficial ownership filing).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notice of Incorporation (CoR 14.1) accompanied by the adopted Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI) and Declaration of Compliance (CoR 14.1A); issuance of the Computer-Generated Certificate of Incorporation is the statutory completion instrument under s.14.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Companies Act 71 of 2008 for a private company; however, pursuant to the FAIS Act Fit and Proper Determination (Board Notice 194 of 2017) and the FSCA licensing framework, a Category II discretionary Financial Services Provider (multi-family office mandate) must at all times maintain an unencumbered minimum net asset value and paid-up capital of ZAR 5,000,000 (approximately USD 275,000), evidenced by a bank certificate or audited opening balance sheet.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety and Structural Certification: obtain an approved occupancy certificate and fire-compliance certificate from the local fire protection authority in accordance with the National Building Regulations and SANS 10400 Parts T and A; Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993 compliance requires a documented risk assessment under s.8, electrical certificate of compliance under SANS 10142, asbestos and compliance surveys, emergency action plans, COIDA incident reporting, and a workplace inspection clearance from the Department of Employment and Labour before staff physically occupy the premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Companies Act 71 of 2008 for a private company; however, pursuant to the FAIS Act Fit and Proper Determination (Board Notice 194 of 2017) and the FSCA licensing framework, a Category II discretionary Financial Services Provider (multi-family office mandate) must at all times maintain an unencumbered minimum net asset value and paid-up capital of ZAR 5,000,000 (approximately USD 275,000), evidenced by a bank certificate or audited opening balance sheet.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), www.sars.gov.za; SARS eFiling portal at https://efiling.sars.gov.za (Income Tax, VAT, PAYE, Dividends Tax and Customs administered via the single eFiling channel).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity that becomes a South African tax resident (by incorporation or effective management) or that carries on a trade in South Africa through a representative office, branch or permanent establishment must register with SARS through eFiling or a SARS branch. The company registration application must include: certified certificate of incorporation and memorandum of incorporation from the home jurisdiction, certified directors' identity documents/passports, a South African physical address proof (lease or utility account), and beneficial-ownership particulars. SARS issues a company income-tax reference number; a separate VAT vendor registration (VAT 101 application) is compulsory once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period (registration within 21 days), and voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000 turnover. A discretionary portfolio manager must first obtain a Financial Services Provider (FSP) licence from the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) under the FAIS Act before commencing the regulated activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd is an opaque, separately taxable corporate person. It is subject to corporate income tax at 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; capital gains are taxed at an 80% inclusion rate (effective 21.6% for companies); dividends distributed to shareholders are subject to 20% dividend withholding tax (treaty relief available); a resident company is taxed on worldwide income, with qualifying foreign dividends and foreign business-establishment income potentially exempt under the participation exemption. No pass-through eligibility exists for a (Pty) Ltd. — A foreign-incorporated entity that becomes a South African tax resident (by incorporation or effective management) or that carries on a trade in South Africa through a representative office, branch or permanent establishment must register with SARS through eFiling or a SARS branch. A discretionary portfolio manager must first obtain a Financial Services Provider (FSP) licence from the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) under the FAIS Act before commencing the regulated activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd is an opaque, separately taxable corporate person. It is subject to corporate income tax at 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; capital gains are taxed at an 80% inclusion rate (effective 21.6% for companies); dividends distributed to shareholders are subject to 20% dividend withholding tax (treaty relief available); a resident company is taxed on worldwide income, with qualifying foreign dividends and foreign business-establishment income potentially exempt under the participation exemption. No pass-through eligibility exists for a (Pty) Ltd.

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election; a (Pty) Ltd is per se a taxable corporation. Applicable elections and special regimes under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 include: (i) small business corporation (SBC) status under s12E (unavailable to a multi-family office because more than 50% of gross income is investment income); (ii) s11D research-and-development deduction approval; (iii) group restructuring CGT roll-over elections under s42 and s45; (iv) recoupment deferral election under s24C; (v) interest deduction timing elections under s23A; and (vi) the hybrid mismatch rules (s8E, s8EA, s8F, s23N, s25H) operate with OECD BEPS-aligned substance-based carve-outs and no elective opt-out; reportable arrangement disclosure obligations may apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (R1 million) of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period; compulsory VAT registration within 21 days of exceeding the threshold; voluntary registration permitted where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period. For the multi-family office, discretionary portfolio management and investment-advisory fees are VAT-exempt financial services under s2(1) of the VAT Act and do not count toward the compulsory registration threshold; registration is triggered only where the entity separately makes taxable non-financial supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act applies where the supplier exports the goods within 90 days of the date of supply and retains actual proof of physical export. Services: zero-rating under s11(2)(k) applies where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply, the services are not connected with land or movable property situated in South Africa, and the consideration is received in foreign currency. Critical multi-family-office limitation: discretionary portfolio management and investment-advisory fees are VAT-exempt financial services under s2(1) and therefore cannot be zero-rated or give rise to VAT refunds; only separate non-financial administrative, tax-compliance or advisory services that meet the s11(2)(k) criteria can be zero-rated to foreign clients, and the entity must apportion input VAT because it makes mixed exempt and taxable supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 series) lodged via SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill or Road Consignment Note evidencing physical export within 90 days
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice showing receipt of foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice aligned with the export contract and the zero-rated VAT invoice
  • For exported services: signed service agreement identifying the non-resident recipient, proof that the recipient was outside South Africa at the time of supply, and foreign-currency payment receipt

South Africa does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime. SARS accepts electronic tax invoices that satisfy the content requirements of s20 of the VAT Act (vendor name, VAT number, invoice number, date, description, quantity, consideration and 'tax invoice' wording). Businesses must retain all source documents under the Tax Administration Act and submit risk data via SARS eFiling/API; the Standard Audit File for VAT (SAF-VAT) format is required for audits. SARS has announced a phased e-invoicing framework, but it is not yet mandatory; separate electronic-services registration rules apply to non-resident suppliers of digital services.

SARS operates a dedicated VAT refund verification unit that checks the full documentary trail before releasing refunds. Zero-rated supplies are clawed back with statutory interest where export documentation is inadequate, goods fail to leave South Africa or return within the required period, or service fees are not received in foreign currency. Assessment limitation periods: three years for standard assessments, five years where the taxpayer is negligent or fails to disclose material information, and unlimited for fraud. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, behaviour-graded understatement penalties apply from 0% for reasonable care to a statutory maximum of 150% of the understated tax for intentional tax evasion; fixed administrative non-compliance penalties apply per month of non-compliance; and fraudulent export-VAT refund claims expose responsible persons to criminal prosecution with substantial fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current account transactions without the need for separate exchange control approval, provided the underlying transaction meets the requirements of the Currency and Exchanges Manual and is conducted through an Authorised Dealer. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity investment and outward portfolio investment by residents, remain subject to case-by-case regulation and require the submission of BI-2 forms or applicable generic/specific rulings. Profit remittances and dividends to non-resident shareholders are permitted on a current account basis if funded from post-tax retained profits, subject to a 20% dividends tax withholding and production of a tax clearance certificate issued by SARS in good standing, together with an independent audit certification of the income earned in South Africa. A multi-family office (MFO) must be particularly exhaustive in documenting the non-resident beneficial owners, because exchange control approval for inward structure will hinge on whether the investment is regarded as a passive holding versus an active financial service; any element of direct domestic portfolio management may require FSCA licences and SARB FinSurv consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South African exchange control regulations do not currently mandate conversion of export proceeds into ZAR. The statutory framework requires all exporters to receive full payment for exported goods or services within 180 days from the date of export/shipment, or such longer period as approved by an Authorised Dealer with FinSurv concurrence. Export proceeds may be retained in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer, subject to the furnishing of an Exporter's Return (EX-1) and maintenance of supporting shipment documentation (bill of lading, commercial invoices, bank settlement confirmations) for exchange control audit. An MFO is not itself a goods exporter, but where it renders cross-border treasury or advisory services to foreign family clients, such services are treated as current account remittances and the related fees must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days; the MFO may also retain such fees in approved FCAs for operational treasury prepositioning.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional / Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms do not have independent legal authority to provide cross-border settlement for a South African-regulated MFO because cross-border money movement requires an Authorised Dealer appointed under the Currency and Exchanges Regulations and participation in the South African National Payment System. Offshore EMIs may be used as supplementary treasury/concierge rails for small family-office administrative payments within the annual single discretionary allowance (currently ZAR 1 million per adult per year, subject to SARB approval), but they cannot hold inward investment capital or act as the primary clearing counterparty. The MFO must route all core capital flows through one of the licensed domestic commercial banks listed above. A South African-authorised Electronic Money Issuer (with a recognisable NPS licence) can facilitate low-value inbound collections, but such activity is severely restricted by the Banks Act and National Payment System Act and is not recommended for high-risk family-office custody flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident family-office shareholders must inject capital by means of SWIFT MT103/202 wires routed through the MFO's inward escrow account at a tier-1 Authorised Dealer. The remitting bank must include the purpose code for 'equity capital contribution' (e.g., code 1001) or 'loan/subordinated debt' (e.g., code 1002) to enable the receiving Authorised Dealer to issue a Currency and Exchanges Manual BI-2 filing. For unlisted direct inward investment, the MFO must complete and submit a BI-2 application to the Authorised Dealer; once approved, the bank records the transaction as an inward foreign investment (IFI) permit/notice. The inbound capital must be physically remitted to a ZAR-denominated Capital Escrow Account (or an FCA held with the same bank), and cannot be credited until the bank has verified client FICA documentation and the source of funds. All inward wires must be accompanied by a narrative referencing the underlying family-office agreement and the ISIC 6430 regulated mandate. The MFO must also ensure SARS notification requirements are complied with for inward foreign investment declarations.Trade disbursements and family-office treasury payments are cleared through the following rails: domestic ZAR payments use the National Payment System's SAMOS/RTCS (South African Multiple Option Settlement / Real-Time Gross Settlement) to clear same-day high-value instructions; cross-border payments are transmitted via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 (financial institution transfer) for custody settlement. For foreign portfolio purchases, the MFO will operate through an Authorised Dealer to execute SWIFT MT103s denominated in ZAR with the bank performing the ZAR to foreign currency conversion; for securities settlement, a fiduciary/instruction through an offshore custodian will be routed via MT202 using the authorised dealer's nostro accounts. Commercial Letters of Credit may be used for underlying physical-asset trade positions of family-owner businesses (import/export of goods) under UCP600, with issuance via MT700 and negotiation via MT740/MT742. For advised family-office alternative assets, dedicated trade finance terms should be documented under a master collateral agreement to prevent accidental double-collateralisation. 27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax imposed under s64E of the Income Tax Act on dividends declared by a resident company; final withholding tax borne by the beneficial owner of the dividend, collected by the company that declares the dividend or by the regulated intermediary (CSDP) on payment of the dividend.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transaction-based filing thresholds: SARS requires a Local File for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding ZAR 5 million in gross arm's length remuneration for non-routine financing transactions, or exceeding ZAR 1 million for related-party services; a Master File is required when gross related-party turnover exceeds ZAR 100 million and the total value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds ZAR 50 million; SARS TP010 (comprehensive transfer pricing disclosure) applies to all cross-border related-party transactions; full contemporaneous documentation (local file, master file, and TP010) is required for transactions of any quantum if SARS issues a request.

Economic substance scope: South Africa applies the 'Economic Substance Doctrine' through the general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) under Part II of the Tax Administration Act read with s103 of the Income Tax Act, the reportable arrangement rules (s79 TAA), and the transfer pricing legislation in s31 of the Income Tax Act (effective for years commencing after 1 January 2024: the updated s31 replaces the s31(1)(d) 'POEM' and 31(1)(e) 'business purpose' tests with an 'economic substance' test for intercompany agreements). In addition, the global minimum top-up tax rules (undertaxed profits rule and income inclusion rule, implemented in 2024 under the Global Minimum Tax Act 2023) impose a 15% effective minimum tax requiring substantive personnel and premises for flow-through holding structures; financial intermediation and MFO activity does not fall under a special economic-substance regime, but must demonstrate real local decision-making, advisory staff, and management capability to sustain arm's length remuneration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% of the shortfall for intentional tax evasion under s223 TAA; criminal fine up to ZAR 30,000 or imprisonment up to 24 months per s235 TAA; civil interest at 11.75% compounded daily; additional maximum penalty of ZAR 1 million per annum for repeat offences under s210 TAA.

Director personal liability: Directors and the public officer may be jointly and severally liable for the company’s tax debt under s11 TAA if the failure to pay or file was due to fraud, negligence, or willful default; piercing the corporate veil is possible under s20(9) Companies Act 71 of 2008 where the company is used to defraud creditors or tax authorities; SARS may issue a letter of demand and pursue sequestration, travel bans, and director disqualification under s69 Companies Act; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment up to 24 months.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Excessive Management Fees

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to taxable income plus secondary adjustment deeming a loan equal to the adjustment amount with a notional interest charge levied at the official rate unless the taxpayer demonstrates actual repatriation; understatement penalty up to 150% under s223 TAA; failure to submit TP010 is a criminal offence under s234 TAA with a fine up to ZAR 2 million or imprisonment up to 24 months.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fictitious service agreements or deliberately sign false supporting documentation may face personal tax liability under s11 TAA and civil claims by the company; the veil will be pierced if the transfer pricing structure lacks commercial substance; foreign parent directors may be within SARS enforcement reach via mutual assistance treaties and tax collection agreements; travel bans and asset attachment orders may be issued against any director implicated in the evasion scheme.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of expenses and income recharacterization under the new s31 economic substance test; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) included in South African DTAs following the BEPS Multilateral Instrument; GAAR application resulting in a 100% adjustment of tax benefit plus 150% understatement penalty; deregistration by the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) under the Companies Act for failure to file annual returns for 2 consecutive years.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability to SARS for unpaid tax under s11 TAA where the entity is a façade; Companies Act s77 provides for director liability for loss incurred by the company through reckless trading; multiple family office principals could be disqualified as directors and subject to civil restitution; foreign beneficiaries' assets could be traced, and the MFO license may be revoked by the Financial Sector Conduct Authority under FAIS Act 37 of 2002, triggering statutory asset freezes.
AML / FICA Non-Compliance in an MFO Context

Statutory sanction: FICA administrative fines up to ZAR 10 million per contravention, plus civil penalty of up to ZAR 10 million; criminal sanction for conducting unlicensed financial services business: fine up to ZAR 15 million or imprisonment up to 10 years under s111 FAIS; failure to report suspicious transactions under FICA may result in a fine up to ZAR 10 million and imprisonment up to 15 years; the Financial Intelligence Centre may impose directives and freeze accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and Key Individuals of the MFO face personal criminal exposure if they knowingly permit the entity to operate without a licence or fail to report suspicious transactions; a court may issue a personal asset forfeiture order under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act; directors may be personally liable for civil damages to affected clients; travel bans, asset restraint orders, and disqualification from corporate directorships follow from a conviction under the FAIS Act or Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act.
Unlawful Dividend Repatriation and Dividends Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: The company (and its directors) are jointly and severally liable for unpaid dividends tax as a withholding agent with interest and penalties; understatement penalty of up to 150% of the unpaid tax; SARS may impose a penalty of ZAR 250 per day for the missing SD-D1; criminal conviction for failure to withhold may result in a fine up to ZAR 30,000 or imprisonment up to 24 months.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unauthorised dividend declarations under s46 Companies Act if the solvency and liquidity test was not satisfied; SARS will hold the nominated public officer and responsible directors jointly liable; a travel ban can be imposed under s194 TAA for directors failing to cooperate with a tax investigation; civil restitution to creditors for the amount of the unlawful dividend is available under s77 Companies Act.
