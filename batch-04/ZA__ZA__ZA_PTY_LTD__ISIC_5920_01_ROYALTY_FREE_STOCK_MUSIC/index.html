Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling portal at www.sars.gov.za / www.efiling.gov.za

E-government filing portal

Incorporation with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) triggers automatic provisional SARS income tax registration linked to the company registration number. Final statutory registration must be completed via SARS eFiling by submitting IRP12/ING forms (income tax) and VAT101 (VAT) within 21 days of incorporation or before commencing business. Foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd entities are treated no differently from domestic private companies; non-resident ownership is permitted. Physical verification documents, bank confirmation, and the signed MoI may be requested.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Incorporation with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) triggers automatic provisional SARS income tax registration linked to the company registration number. Final statutory registration must be completed via SARS eFiling by submitting IRP12/ING forms (income tax) and VAT101 (VAT) within 21 days of incorporation or before commencing business. Foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd entities are treated no differently from domestic private companies; non-resident ownership is permitted. Physical verification documents, bank confirmation, and the signed MoI may be requested.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or free-floating entity classification election. A South African private company is per se a separate tax resident subject to entity-level corporate income tax (currently 27%). Available statutory elections include: VAT grouping election under section 50 of the VAT Act (for qualifying group companies), section 12E micro-business turnover tax election for small qualifying companies (annual turnover ≤ ZAR 1 million), functional currency election under section 25D for businesses with cash flows substantially in a foreign currency, and the section 80A–80M hybrid mismatch provisions apply automatically to hybrid entities/instruments, not by election. Certain CFC and limited-interest relief elections may apply to cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 per annum (12-month period). Voluntary registration: taxable supplies at least ZAR 50,000 per annum. For foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African residents, a separate ZAR 50,000 mandatory registration threshold applies (s.23 VAT Act).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods is zero-rated if the goods are physically exported from South Africa and documentary proof of export is retained (s.11(1)(a) VAT Act). Export of services is zero-rated under s.11(2)(k) where the recipient is a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time performance is rendered, the services are not in respect of anything physically in South Africa, and neither the services nor the intellectual property are used or possessed in South Africa. For a royalty-free stock music licensing subscription, the digital/electronic licence supplied to a non-resident customer is typically zero-rated provided the customer is not in South Africa, the licence fee is paid from outside the common customs area, and the music is not acquired for use/exploitation in South Africa. Where the recipient is a South African resident or the music is consumed in South Africa, the supply is subject to 15% VAT as an electronic service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading / Airwaybill / Electronic Proof of Export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign currency payment proof)
  • Signed licensing agreement / music licence sale contract
  • VAT tax invoice or commercial invoice in the required form

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime is currently mandatory for domestic businesses. E-invoicing is optional but SARS has adopted PEPPOL standards to facilitate interoperable tax invoices. Where applicable, a full tax invoice under s.20(4) VAT Act must be issued within 21 days of supply and must contain the specified particulars (known seller, VAT number, invoice date, description, VAT-exclusive/inclusive amounts). VAT returns and invoice integrity checks are submitted through the SARS eFiling API; auditable records for at least 5 years are required.

SARS will deny zero-rating if the export or consumption conditions are not supported by contemporaneous documentary proof retained for at least 5 years. Where zero-rated invoices are found not to have been genuinely exported (e.g., electronic licence accessed or consumed in South Africa, or export documentation is fabricated), SARS retrospectively claws back the unpaid 15% VAT together with interest. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties of up to 200% of the tax shortfall may be imposed for intentional disregard or gross negligence, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion carries imprisonment and/or monetary sanctions. SARS also imposes a 10% late-payment penalty plus statutory interest for VAT due arising from clawback assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is a floating currency with a managed convertible regime. Current-account transactions - royalty income, digital audio-licensing fees and subscription receipts from foreign customers - are convertible and repatriable without prior SARB approval, subject to Authorized Dealer documentation and SARS Tax Compliance Status verification. Capital-account transactions are regulated: inward foreign direct investment in the form of equity subscriptions is freely permitted and only requires BOP reporting by the Authorized Dealer, while outbound resident capital flows, resident exposure to offshore NDF markets and certain intercompany loans require FinSurv approval. Profit-remittance hurdles consist primarily of tax gates rather than exchange control: dividends to non-resident shareholders attract South African dividends tax at the statutory rate of 20% (treaty relief available) and royalties paid to non-residents attract a 15% withholding tax under section 49B of the Income Tax Act; a SARS tax-clearance certificate is always required before a cross-border dividend remittance is processed by the bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 6 and the SARB Exchange Control Manual, all export proceeds - expressly including royalties, license fees and electronic-service receipts for ISIC 5920 audio licensing - must be received in the Republic of South Africa within 180 days of the invoice date or the date the service is rendered. Repatriated foreign currency must be surrendered by conversion into ZAR through the company's Authorized Dealer at the prevailing market rate, giving a mandatory conversion ratio of 100% for all repatriated proceeds, unless the company holds a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) in which qualifying export proceeds may be retained unconverted. No general permission exists for a South African resident to hold export income offshore beyond the 180-day window; retention in an overseas platform wallet (Stripe or PayPal) beyond that period without a documented Authorized Dealer arrangement constitutes a contravention. De minimis transaction and documentary exemptions follow the SARB General Authorizations; non-compliance attracts statutory penalties, interest and escalation of the entity's compliance risk rating with its lending bank.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the exchange-control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), together with the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) for AML/CFT supervision; operational execution is delegated to Authorized Dealers (licensed South African commercial banks).
