Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); administrative and filing portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs clearance executed through the SACU/SARS customs operating system (EAAS/CDS).

E-government filing portal

(1) Upon incorporation through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), the (Pty) Ltd is automatically registered as a taxpayer and SARS issues an Income Tax Reference Number; (2) within 14 days of incorporation, appoint a resident public officer as required by Section 99 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; (3) submit a VAT 101 application via eFiling or a SARS branch when taxable supplies exceed the compulsory R1,000,000 threshold, or elect voluntary registration from R50,000; (4) if employees are engaged, register for Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE), Skills Development Levy (SDL), and UIF; (5) non-resident shareholders must obtain a South African non-resident taxpayer reference number for dividend tax purposes and appoint a registered tax practitioner for local compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the (Pty) Ltd is a separate legal person taxed under the Income Tax Act at the 27% corporate rate (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); no check-the-box pass-through election is available; profits distributed to shareholders are subject to 20% dividends tax; the social commerce retail activities are within the full territorial scope of South African taxation (worldwide income for resident companies).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in South Africa. Relevant elections available: (a) VAT Act section 15 voluntary VAT registration election between R50,000 and R1,000,000 turnover; (b) Income Tax Act section 11D research and development allowance approval; (c) section 12B/12BA renewable energy allowances; (d) group relief elections under section 41 onwards for intra-group loss surrender; (e) statutory hybrid mismatch rules under sections 20A-20D apply compulsorily and cannot be elected out; (f) domestic reverse charge applies to certain imported services under section 7(1)(c) of the VAT Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R 1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months (compulsory turnover threshold); voluntary registration from R 50,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the TikTok Shop retailer supplies goods to non-residents and the goods are physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply; under section 11(2), cross-border services (including offshore advertising, platform intermediation, and international logistics) are zero-rated where supplied to non-residents; the retailer must obtain and retain the export documentary evidence prescribed by section 54(5) of the VAT Act; failure to effect physical export reclassifies the supply as taxable at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) with SARS/SACU electronic clearance
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Courier Waybill evidencing physical movement to the foreign consignee
  • Commercial invoice referencing the VAT registration number and the zero-rating ground
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank statement proving foreign currency receipt from the non-resident buyer
  • Proof of delivery or acceptance at the port of export (customs clearance advice)

Real-time fiscal clearance and continuous transaction controls are not yet fully mandated; however, full tax invoices must be issued under section 20 of the VAT Act within 21 days of the supply, and the VAT 201 return is filed electronically via the SARS eFiling API; South Africa is phasing in OECD-style enhanced digital reporting under the SARS Modernisation roadmap (2025-2027); cross-border retail consignments are cleared in real time through the SACU/SARS Electronic Automated Assessment system utilising the SAD500 declaration.

SARS audits export zero-rating claims strictly under section 54(5); where documentation is deficient, the zero-rating is reversed and output VAT is assessed retrospectively with interest at the prescribed rate (repurchase rate plus 1%) compounded daily; the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 imposes understatement penalties ranging from 25% to 150% based on conduct (substantial understatement to intentional tax evasion); fraudulent export zero-rating exposes the entity and its public officer to criminal prosecution under sections 233-234 of the VAT Act (fine and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years); an additional tax clawback of up to 200% may be imposed under section 60(2); repeated non-compliance may trigger accelerated VAT filing, security deposits, or cancellation of VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, interest, dividends, and royalties, subject to standard documentary verification. Capital account transactions, including non-resident equity investment and cross-border loans, are permissible under the Exchange Control Regulations but must be routed through an authorised dealer, and reporting obligations under FinSurv Circulars apply. Profit remittance by a proprietary limited company is allowed for distributable profits provided the company satisfies the Companies Act solvency and liquidity test, the source of funds is clearly supported by audited financial statements, and the remittance is declared on the required FinSurv forms. No quantitative restriction on profit repatriation currently exists for bona fide non-resident shareholdings, but practical hurdles include proving inward capital status, obtaining a tax clearance certificate for large distributions, and completing FICA AML/KYC documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received through an authorised dealer in South Africa within 180 days of the date of shipment or, for certain e-commerce models, within 180 days of the completion of the sale and dispatch. The exporter must declare all export transactions on the required Exchange Control form via the authorised dealer, which is reconciled against the South African Revenue Service (SARS) customs export declaration, and the foreign currency must be surrendered to the authorised dealer unless a foreign-currency account retention licence was issued by FinSurv. There is no fixed statutory percentage of compulsory conversion to ZAR beyond the obligation to repatriate the full invoice value through a SARB-compliant channel; approved foreign-currency accounts allow holding of export proceeds for allowed periods, but any extension or deviation requires FinSurv approval.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) of the National Treasury, with delegated authority to Authorised Dealers (commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a substitute for regulated banking, but High as a supplementary corridor. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and TikTok settlement partners cannot provide SARB-authorised dealer functions, local ZAR clearing, or statutory export-proceeds surrender processing. They may be used for USD/EUR/CNY pre-funded receipts and to optimise cross-border payment fees, but a South African licensed bank remains mandatory for capital injection, profit remittance, and tax reporting. The EMI must be onboarded as a foreign supplier and every flow in and out of the platform must be traceable through the authorised dealer reconciliation system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must subscribe shares via an incoming SWIFT MT103 or equivalent wire to a dedicated foreign-facilitated capital account at the authorised dealer. The remittance must reference the share-subscription agreement, use the designated balance-of-payments purpose code for 'Equity capital inflow from non-resident', and be accompanied by the correct inward declaring form required by the specific bank. The authorised dealer will verify the source of funds, register the inward listing, and allocate the proceeds into a ZAR operational current account after conversion.
