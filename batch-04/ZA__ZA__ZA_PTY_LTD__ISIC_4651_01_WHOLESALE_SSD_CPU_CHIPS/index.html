Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – SARS eFiling portal (www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign incorporated entity not otherwise registered in South Africa must register as a foreign resident taxpayer with SARS through the eFiling portal or by lodging a SARS registration form (CRA form series) at a SARS branch, and must obtain both an Income Tax Reference Number and, if making taxable supplies in the course of an enterprise, a VAT registration number. The entity must appoint a SARS public officer resident in South Africa, submit certified incorporation documents, proof of place of effective management or representative office, and obtain a tax compliance status (TSC) PIN. Registration may be voluntary or compulsory based on the enterprise's recurring taxable activity. No statutory minimum capital is required for a (Pty) Ltd.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign incorporated entity not otherwise registered in South Africa must register as a foreign resident taxpayer with SARS through the eFiling portal or by lodging a SARS registration form (CRA form series) at a SARS branch, and must obtain both an Income Tax Reference Number and, if making taxable supplies in the course of an enterprise, a VAT registration number. The entity must appoint a SARS public officer resident in South Africa, submit certified incorporation documents, proof of place of effective management or representative office, and obtain a tax compliance status (TSC) PIN. Registration may be voluntary or compulsory based on the enterprise's recurring taxable activity. No statutory minimum capital is required for a (Pty) Ltd.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in South Africa; companies are automatically taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Specific elections are available, including election for a foreign tax credit under section 6quat(2A), election for use of a foreign functional currency under section 46A(h) for CFCs, deductibility elections for research and development under section 11D, and elections under the hybrid mismatch rules (sections 8F, 8FA, 23M and 25G) when cross-border financing or structured arrangements are involved. All elections are made by notice to SARS in the prescribed form and are not equivalent to the US-style check-the-box pass-through election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months or any consecutive 12-month period. Voluntary registration threshold: ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12 months. Foreign suppliers providing electronic, digital and services through South Africa may be required to register regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to goods physically exported from South Africa under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, provided the supplier is the exporter or the recipient exports the goods under customs supervision, and the goods are shipped within the prescribed period or held in a customs and excise controlled area. Services exported to non-resident customers are zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) where the services are physically rendered abroad, or the intellectual property is supplied for use outside South Africa, subject to documentary proof. For wholesale trading in SSDs and server processors, zero rating applies when the goods are sold to a non-resident buyer and the movement to an export destination is fully evidenced.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500) with SARS customs clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing export of goods
  • Commercial invoices and packing slips
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT payment instruction proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Bonded warehouse or port authority release documentation where applicable
  • Proof of physical movement (delivery notes, vessel/aircraft arrival and departure stamps, container numbers)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping applies to domestic B2B VAT invoices. VAT-registered suppliers must issue full or abridged tax invoices containing prescribed particulars. SARS accepts electronic tax invoices where integrity and readability are assured. The SARS eFiling system is mandatory for customs declarations (SAD 500) and VAT returns; international trade clearance is electronic, but the VAT invoicing system remains based on periodic reconciliation rather than transaction-by-transaction real-time fiscal authorisation. SARS is progressively aligning with PEPPOL e-invoicing standards, but this is not yet a national clearance mandate.

SARS conducts documentary review and may disallow zero-rating and claw back input VAT where the required export evidence is incomplete. Retrospective assessments carry additional VAT, interest at the prescribed rate, and understatement penalties under section 223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, ranging from 10% up to 200% of the understated tax depending on conduct. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may result in criminal prosecution, civil penalties, and disqualification from refunds. SARS also coordinates with the South African Reserve Bank on exchange-control repatriation rules; failure to repatriate and convert export proceeds within 180 days can trigger exchange-control penalties and affect eligibility for future zero rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is not freely convertible offshore from a capital account perspective. Current account transactions, including trade-related payments for imported goods and services, are broadly unrestricted and subject to automatic approval by Authorised Dealers, provided documentary evidence is submitted. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections and intra-company loans by non-residents, are permitted with SARB notification or prior approval depending on sector and financing structure. Profit remittances by a branch to a non-resident head office are freely permitted subject to the lodgment of audited financial statements and tax clearance from the South African Revenue Service (SARS). Dividends paid by a resident subsidiary to a non-resident holding company do not require exchange control approval if funded from declared distributable reserves and tax clearance is presented. Hurdles may arise when the beneficial owner is in a tax-haven jurisdiction, when the source of inward funds is not clearly documented, or when the underlying trade goods are high-risk in terms of the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) and Enforcement Committee directives.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the South African Exchange Control Regulations, export proceeds must be received and repatriated to South Africa within 180 days of the date of shipment, unless an extension is granted by SARB FinSurv. There is no statutory mandatory conversion of all export receipts into ZAR; however, the Authorised Dealer must receive the foreign currency into an approved account held in South Africa. Exporters may retain foreign currency in a designated Foreign Currency Account (FCA) only with prior approval from FinSurv. For typical spot semiconductor trading transactions, the full invoice value must be declared via the Foreign Exchange Transaction Form (FETF) and collected by the bank within the prescribed timeline. Failure to meet the 180-day window without a formal extension may constitute a contravention of the Exchange Control Regulations, triggering penalties and forensic review.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under exchange control regulations administered by delegated Authorised Dealers (commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for unlicensed offshore digital EMI providers; Medium for internationally licensed EMIs operating through a locally authorised representative or partnering with a licensed South African bank. The South African National Payment System Framework Act and Financial Sector Regulation Act prohibit non-bank entities from accepting deposits or executing payment transaction clearing without a specific exemption or partnership. Offshore digital EMIs may not open domestic ZAR accounts directly and cannot provide SWIFT-clearing services as an authorised dealer. Any account opened with an EMI is not treated as a South African bank account for exchange control purposes and cannot receive export proceeds subject to SARB surrender obligations. Therefore, a physically registered South African bank account held at a tier-1 bank remains the mandatory treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections into the South African (Pty) Ltd treasury account must be transacted via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank using a purpose code consistent with the true commercial nature. For equity capital, the payment must reference 'Share Capital Subscription' and be accompanied by a resolution allotting shares to the non-resident investor, the updated memorandum of incorporation, and a SARB BOP-01 report submitted by the receiving bank. For intra-company loans, the request should cite 'Trade/Intra-Company Financing' and must be documented by a signed term loan agreement containing an interest rate consistent with arm's length principles. To avoid flash messages from the anti-money laundering transaction-monitoring system, the remitter should include the entity registration number (CIPC number) and the ISIC 4651 trade description in the narrative field, should not exceed SARB's any-beneficial-interest notification thresholds, and must not source funds from an account with mixed personal and corporate activity. Funds received in ZAR or a freely transferable foreign currency may be credited to the Operational Account immediately upon receipt of the required declaration. If the amount exceeds ZAR 15 million per calendar year for investment in a non-listed entity, additional SARB FinSurv approval may be required before the account is opened or funded.Trade settlements for procurement of SSDs and server processors should be cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 payments via Standard Bank or FNB correspondent banking relationships, and through documentary Letters of Credit (LC) for larger or higher-risk purchase orders. MT103 wires should carry the purpose code 'Purchase of Goods for Resale' and include the invoice number, supplier name, Incoterms, and a note confirming receipt of goods if settlement is post-shipment. Commercial LCs must be issued as irrevocable, confirmed by a first-class bank (e.g., JP Morgan, HSBC, or Standard Chartered) and aligned with the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600). For each outbound payment, the South African bank requires a valid CIPC registration record of the supplier, the underlying purchase order, a proforma invoice, a statement from the exporter confirming that the funds will not be used for restricted trade (e.g., military-end-use), and an internal treasury sign-off that the payment does not exceed the account's approved foreign-exchange allocation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR5,000,000 aggregate value of cross-border affected transactions in a year of assessment generally triggers the obligation to prepare and retain a contemporaneous transfer pricing record (Local File). Multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of ZAR10 billion or more are required to prepare a Master File and file a Country-by-Country report notification with SARS.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to Caribbean or Gulf Cooperation Council regimes. Instead, economic substance is enforced through the transfer pricing arm's length rule, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, controlled foreign company attribution, source/permanent establishment analysis, and the common law substance-over-form doctrine. A non-resident intermediary or holding entity with no employees, office, business assets, or risk-bearing function is liable to be recharacterised as a sham and its income re-attributed to the South African operating entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% of the shortfall for intentional evasion, plus interest; criminal conviction can result in a substantial fine and/or imprisonment. SARS may also issue a summons, attach assets, and obtain a preservation order over the company's bank accounts and trade receivables.

Director personal liability: A director or public officer who knowingly participates in the evasion may be held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax under the Tax Administration Act; criminal conviction carries imprisonment, civil restitution orders, travel bans, and director disqualification under the Companies Act.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Sham Intermediary

Statutory sanction: SARS will make a primary transfer pricing adjustment under section 31, recharacterise the transaction, and assess additional taxable income. Accompanying understatement penalties range from 25% (no reasonable care) to 150% (gross negligence). Failure to keep contemporaneous documentation is an aggravating factor and may trigger penalty default classification.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false intercompany invoices or who cause the South African company to enter into a value-destructive artificial arrangement may be jointly and severally liable for the resultant tax debt, with civil recovery proceedings and possible surrender/disqualification as sequestrated or unfit directors.
Shell Company / Empty Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may attack the arrangement under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, the deemed source rules, and controlled foreign company rules; profits are reallocated to the South African entity. If the arrangement was part of a tax evasion scheme, the same 150% penalty and criminal exposure apply.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners who knowingly control the shell risk personal liability for unpaid taxes, restitution to SARS, and South African cross-border enforcement including asset freezes and travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Intelligence Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Contravention of the Financial Intelligence Centre Act may expose the company to administrative fines up to ZAR10 million per offence and criminal penalties. SARS can also invoke income tax default, issue a notice of non-compliant tax conduct, and disqualify the entity from tax clearance certificates, blocking cross-border trade and bank transfers.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly or negligently permit such payments may be prosecuted, personally fined, and imprisoned; a foreign director may be declared undesirable and subjected to a travel ban or extradition procedure.
