Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal https://efiling.sars.gov.za; main website https://www.sars.gov.za; VAT and income tax administration unified under SARS, including customs administration for import/export declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) under the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Step 2: Obtain CIPC registration number; SARS automatically links incorporation data and issues/allocates the income tax reference number (primary TIN) for SA-incorporated companies. Step 3: For foreign-incorporated entities establishing a South African permanent establishment, register as an external company with CIPC (s 32, Companies Act) and complete SARS non-resident income tax registration via eFiling. Step 4: Register as a VAT vendor (Form VAT 101) via eFiling once the compulsory threshold is met (ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period) or voluntarily at ZAR 50,000. Step 5: Register with SARS Customs (customs code) for SAD 500/eSAD export declarations, and register for PAYE/UIF/SDL if employees are appointed. All registrations are executed online with SARS-issued TIN and VAT registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SA-resident company taxed on worldwide income at 27% corporate income tax for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through treatment; 20% dividends tax on distributions; no autonomous tax-exempt zone applies to a general (Pty) Ltd)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Available statutory elections/statuses: (i) micro business turnover tax election (Sixth Schedule, turnover not exceeding ZAR 1,000,000); (ii) headquarter company status election (s 9I) for qualifying intermediary holding companies; (iii) CFC high-tax exclusion election (s 9D(2A)(b), foreign effective tax rate at least 80% of the SA rate); (iv) small business corporation status (s 12E, with qualifying shareholders and gross income not exceeding ZAR 20,000,000); (v) s 11D research and development allowance election; (vi) VAT group of companies election (s 50, Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991); (vii) voluntary VAT vendor registration (s 23(3), VAT Act). Hybrid mismatch rules (ss 8F, 23M, 23N) apply mandatorily with no unilateral opt-out election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period, or reasonable grounds to expect such turnover; voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months, or reasonable expectation thereof. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services: separate compulsory threshold of ZAR 50,000 of electronic services supplied in South Africa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods (e.g., syrup concentrates): zero-rated under s 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods are supplied to a non-resident and consigned or delivered to an export country, or delivered to a carrier/customs-approved export point, with satisfactory proof of physical export retained for five years. Services (e.g., franchise licensing, training, supply-chain consultancy to foreign franchisees): zero-rated under s 11(2)(l) where the recipient is a non-resident not present in South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not supplied directly in connection with South African land, improvements, or movable property situated in South Africa; SARS Interpretation Note 70 governs documentary substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 / eSAD) issued by SARS Customs evidencing export clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (or combined transport document) evidencing physical movement to the export destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of foreign-currency payment received through an authorised dealer (SARB exchange control record)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API clearance, or digital tax stamping regime is currently in force. Vendors must issue tax invoices containing prescribed s 20 particulars within 21 days of supply; SARS eFiling is mandatory for VAT201 return submission (monthly where annual taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,500,000; two-monthly where ZAR 1,500,000 or less). SARS and National Treasury are phasing in PEPPOL/ISO 20022-based e-invoicing under the SARS digital modernisation programme.

SARS audits export zero-rating by verifying the full documentary bundle; failure to substantiate results in re-rating to 15% VAT with additional output tax assessed (ss 10 and 16, VAT Act) and disallowance of related input tax claims, creating a retrospective clawback exposure. Understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 apply on a conduct-based scale up to 200% of the tax shortfall, plus prescribed interest; intentional fraudulent export claims attract criminal prosecution for tax evasion and loss of refund set-off privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current account: ZAR is fully convertible through an Authorised Dealer for trade settlement, royalty payments, dividend remittances, interest and ordinary service fees, with no FinSurv prior approval required where the underlying commercial documentation, SARS tax clearance, and Balance of Payments (BOP) classification are provided to the bank. Capital account: inbound direct equity investment by non-residents into a local private company is within South Africa's liberalised foreign-investment framework and requires no FinSurv pre-approval for ordinary share subscriptions; however, the share issue, the conversion of non-resident shareholder loans beyond the ZAR 10 million threshold, and any reduction of capital must be recorded and verified through the Authorised Dealer. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily documentary: dividends are subject to the 20% domestic dividends tax, or treaty-reduced rate where applicable; SARS tax clearance, distributable-reserve certification, and non-resident shareholder identity/tax-residency attestations must all be satisfied before the bank will release a dividend-related MT103. ZAR is not freely tradable offshore, but onshore convertibility through the licensed banking channel is unrestricted once exchange-control compliance is established.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be fully repatriated to South Africa within 180 calendar days from the date of shipment, unless the Authorised Dealer has accepted a documented extension based on the trade terms (open account, LC tenor, or consignment practice). The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%: South Africa has abolished forced surrender percentages, so the exporter may retain 100% of the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with the Authorised Dealer, sell it for ZAR at the market rate, or apply it directly to approved offshore payables. All physical exports of syrups, powders, tapioca, and franchise kits must be declared on the SARS Customs e-SAD export declaration, and the bank must receive proof of value and payment within the mandated period; late or non-receipt must be explained via an arrear declaration, failing which FinSurv may impose exchange-control penalties. For service exports of franchise royalties and licence fees, the inflows are current-account receipts rather than export proceeds, and must be classified separately on the bank's BOP return without application of the 180-day surrender timeline.

Central bank supervision: Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) of the National Treasury of the Republic of South Africa, acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; operational supervision of banks and the national payment-system mandate rest with the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) and its Prudential Authority. All cross-border foreign-exchange transactions for this (Pty) Ltd must be executed and reported through a registered commercial bank appointed as an Authorised Dealer in foreign exchange.
