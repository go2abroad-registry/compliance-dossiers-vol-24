Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), eFiling portal via https://www.sars.gov.za and https://efiling.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A Proprietary Limited company incorporated under the South African Companies Act 71 of 2008 must, after registration with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), apply to SARS for an Income Tax reference number and, if relevant, VAT registration. The PTY Ltd's foreign shareholders may act through a SARS-registered representative, but the entity obtains its own tax identity number. Registration is performed online via eFiling (SARS INF14 form) or at a SARS branch using supporting documents: CIPC registration certificate, MOI, certified IDs/passports of directors and shareholders, and proof of registered address. For VAT, form VAT101 must be submitted where taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed the ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory threshold; voluntary VAT101 registration is possible for taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 50,000 in the prior 12-month period at SARS's discretion. An automatic tax registration number is issued for income tax; VAT numbers are issued separately.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a check-the-box regime. A resident PTY Ltd is taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 on worldwide income, with foreign dividends and branch exemption mechanisms available. Elective mechanisms include: s41 group taxation (full 100% group relief election), s45 intra-group asset transfers (roll-over), s47 company reorganisations, s9D controlled foreign company (CFC) inclusion election, and s25I hybrid mismatch rules. VAT grouping under s50 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 and s25I exchange rate elections may also apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 cumulative taxable supplies in a 12-month period; voluntary registration available at ZAR 50,000 turnover subject to SARS approval

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Claimable under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act for exported goods supplied to non-residents and physically delivered from South Africa to an export destination, and under s11(2)(l)/(k) for qualifying services supplied to non-residents outside South Africa, provided the supplier retains documentary proof of export, payment, and delivery. For ocean RORO car carrier services, the transport of vehicles by sea from a South African port is zero-rated where the service is directly connected with the export of movable goods. The seller must have exported the goods within 90 days after the time of supply and retain written proof of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) with SARS C-TS clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing shipment from a South African port to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange settlement proof issued by an authorised dealer bank (or bank statement confirming SWIFT transfer receipt)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Vendor's VAT invoice and detailed export delivery records

South Africa currently has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate for VAT. SARS, however, requires VAT invoices to meet the s20 VAT Act specification (vendor name, address, VAT number, invoice number, date, description, quantity, consideration exclusive/inclusive of VAT, and VAT amount). Electronic invoicing via eFiling is acceptable, and SARS may require electronic data transmission for customs (SAD500) logistics and audit purposes. The SARS electronic clearance system for customs (C-TS / Customs e-Clearance) applies to cross-border movements, but does not operate as a real-time VAT invoice fiscal clearance mechanism.

SARS will disallow the zero-rating and assess output VAT at 15% plus interest if the required export documents are not retained or the export evidence is false. Under Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, an understatement penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax may be imposed for intentional tax evasion (s223), and recurring administrative penalties of up to ZAR 16,000 per month can apply for non-compliance. Criminal prosecution is possible for fraudulent zero-rating claims, and there is no prescription period for claims based on fraud, with retrospective clawback for up to 5 years for ordinary understatements. SARS may also revoke VAT registration and joint liability may be imposed on directors under s332 Companies Act read with tax recovery provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is not freely convertible offshore; convertibility is administered by SARB/FinSurv through Authorised Dealers. Current account transactions (RoRo freight revenue, bunkers, port disbursements, crew wages) are fully convertible on a documentary basis without prior FinSurv approval, subject to standard SARS tax clearance. Capital account items are restricted: non-resident equity injections require post-issuance reporting (T-file/record of investment) within 30 days; shareholder loans require FinSurv approval unless eligible for simplified foreign financing thresholds; dividend remittances to non-residents require an AFS-based solvency certificate, SARS good-standing, operational documentation, and attract 20% domestic dividend withholding tax (reduced under applicable DTAAs). Migrating capital (share capital disinvestment) is not freely remittable and requires FinSurv approval with a valid record of inward investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Proceeds from exported services (deep-sea vehicle RoRo freight) must be received within 180 days from the date of service completion, extendable to 365 days by the Authorised Dealer for justifiable marine/operating delays. 100% of export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa and credited either to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer or converted into ZAR on the domestic market. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, but proceeds retained in the FCA must remain within the Authorised Dealer system and may be applied only to approved current-account payments or subsequently converted at the market rate.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), exercising delegated exchange control under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low - not viable as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) are not Authorised Dealers under SARB exchange control and cannot hold ZAR settlement accounts within the South African National Payment System (SAMOS) nor operate FCAs for a resident (Pty) Ltd. They may be used only as supplementary collection gateways for foreign charterers, with immediate onward clearing to the South African FCA/operational account; all converted ZAR must be settled through a licensed Authorised Dealer to satisfy FinSurv reporting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 10 billion consolidated group revenue triggers the Country-by-Country Report and Master File obligation; for the Local File, SARS may require contemporaneous documentation for material connected-person cross-border transactions, generally using a ZAR 5 million materiality benchmark per category of covered transaction.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the general anti-avoidance provisions in sections 80A to 80L, the simulation/sham doctrine, place-of-effective-management rules, and the principal-purpose test in tax treaties operate to disentitle benefits for entities lacking real economic substance. A Ro-Ro operator must demonstrate genuine local management, operational assets, staff, board decisions, bank accounts and risk-bearing capacity to secure treaty benefits and avoid GAAR re-characterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent ITR14

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under section 235 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, carrying imprisonment of up to five years and/or an appropriate fine; civil understatement penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall; statutory interest; possible SARS jeopardy assessment and immediate debt recovery.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs a false return or participates in the fraudulent scheme may be held jointly and severally liable under the Tax Administration Act and the Companies Act for unpaid tax penalties, subject to travel bans under SARS debt enforcement procedures, and may face disqualification from serving as a director.
Shell Company / Conduit Ro-Ro Structure

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke the general anti-avoidance rule (sections 80A-80L) to disregard or re-characterise the structure, disallow deductions and notional losses, impose deemed dividend treatment on disguised profit repatriations, and levy understatement penalties plus prescribed-rate interest; treaty relief may be denied under the principal-purpose test.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere facade used to defeat fiscal or creditor claims; directors involved in the design or execution of the conduit arrangement may be personally liable for restitution of unpaid taxes and penalties and may face court-imposed travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the consideration to the arm's length price under section 31 of the Income Tax Act, make a secondary adjustment in the form of a deemed dividend or deemed loan, impose an understatement penalty of up to 150% for deliberate mispricing, add interest, and, for CbC reporting failures, apply administrative non-compliance penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement non-arm's length connected-party contracts with knowledge of the tax benefit may be personally liable in restoration or restitution proceedings, and where the taxpayer becomes insolvent, the court may claw back amounts paid under voidable transactions.
AML / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, including fines of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention or higher after recent amendment schedules; criminal prosecution for money laundering and sanctions-busting, asset forfeiture, and possible revocation of operating licences.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers may be held criminally liable where they knew or ought to have known of the AML or sanctions breach; they face personal fines, imprisonment, debarment from financial-facing roles, and potential travel bans while criminal proceedings are pending.
