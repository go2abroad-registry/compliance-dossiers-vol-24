Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box regime. The (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 at a 27% corporate income tax rate for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (28% for earlier years). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a partially convertible currency system. Current account transactions are generally liberalized and do not require prior approval, provided they are fully documented and routed through a bank that is an Authorized Dealer in foreign exchange. Capital account transactions remain restricted and require specific reporting or prior approval. Profit remittances by a resident (Pty) Ltd to non-resident shareholders are permissible through dividend distributions, subject to: (i) compliance with the Companies Act and the Memorandum of Incorporation; (ii) the company being 'in good standing' with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); (iii) no adverse SARS tax clearance; (iv) the remittance being effected by an Authorized Dealer with the appropriate BOP coding (dividend income) and supporting board resolution; and (v) where applicable, an application form for investment income remittance. Hurdles include FICA/KYC documentation requirements for the non-resident shareholder, transfer pricing scrutiny on management fees or intercompany charges, restrictions on share loan funding via the Financial Surveillance Department, and potential exchange-control penalties for late filings or undocumented remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment and must be reported on the relevant Export Form (Ex1) through an Authorized Dealer. There is no mandatory percentage conversion into ZAR; exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with a South African Authorized Dealer, or convert all or part to ZAR at the ruling market rate. The proceeds must be fully repatriated to South Africa (net of permitted deductions such as insurance, freight, and commissions) within the 180-day period. Failure to repatriate on time constitutes a contravention of the Exchange Control Regulations and may attract penalties, unless an extension is granted under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorized Dealers.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs (electronic money institutions) may provide ancillary payment processing, pre-paid corporate cards, and low-cost foreign-exchange conversion, but they are not licensed as South African banks and cannot act as SARB Authorized Dealers. Any inbound or outbound ZAR settlement must clear through a domestic licensed bank or Authorized Dealer. Legal constraints include the Banks Act 94 of 1990 (prohibition on unauthorized deposit-taking), the Financial Sector Regulation Act 9 of 2017, FSCA licensing requirements, and FICA client-onboarding obligations. EMIs can be engaged as supplementary rails, but the core treasury architecture must remain anchored to a Tier 1 South African clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from a non-resident shareholder must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the company's Capital Escrow Account at an SARB Authorized Dealer. The payment instruction must include the corporate name, reference to the board resolution authorizing the share allotment, and the SARB BOP purpose code identifying direct investment: equity capital (share subscription). The Authorized Dealer is required to verify: (i) the incorporation and share-register update; (ii) the non-resident status of the investor; (iii) a valid Board of Directors resolution approving the issue of shares; (iv) FICA/KYC data for the ultimate beneficial owner; and (v) a signed declaration of non-resident investment. The transaction must be reported to SARB under the Balance-of-Payments framework within the applicable reporting month (typically 30 days). Capital amounts exceeding ZAR 10 million may trigger enhanced due-diligence or prior FinSurv approval depending on the underlying share-code and transactional structure. Cryptocurrency-derived funds must be cleansed through a regulated financial channel before SWIFT routing to avoid FICA refusal.Outbound payments for goods or services to non-resident suppliers are current-account transactions and may be executed by an Authorized Dealer without prior SARB approval where the applicant provides original invoices, pro forma invoices, or contracts, together with proof of receipt of services or customs declarations for goods. Acceptable clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for ordinary wire transfers; (ii) MT700 for documentary Commercial Letters of Credit for high-value commodity trade; and (iii) MT760 for standby guarantees or performance bonds. Each payment must be recorded under the correct SARB BOP code (e.g., 'Business services', 'Goods', or 'Royalties and licence fees') and supported by the underlying commercial documentation.
