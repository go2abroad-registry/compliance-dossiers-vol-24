Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; Customs & Excise division for import/export control.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the company registration number. Step 2 — Register for Income Tax with SARS via eFiling or branch; a foreign entity carrying on business through a South African permanent establishment must submit its foreign certificate of incorporation and PE details using the prescribed SARS registration forms. Step 3 — Obtain the Income Tax Reference Number (TIN) for all taxes. Step 4 — Apply for VAT registration on SARS form VAT101; the VAT vendor number is the entity's indirect tax identifier. Step 5 — Complete customs registration (importer/exporter) and PAYE/SDL/UIF registration if employees are engaged. Processing time: 5–10 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A (Pty) Ltd incorporated in South Africa is a resident company taxed at 27% (years of assessment ending on/after 31 March 2023) on worldwide income (CGT effective 21.6% on gains). Non-resident companies are taxable only on South African-source income. Territorial scope thus follows residence-basis for SA-incorporated entities; no special tax-exempt zone classification applies to a standard logistics/freight entity. — Step 1 — Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the company registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A (Pty) Ltd incorporated in South Africa is a resident company taxed at 27% (years of assessment ending on/after 31 March 2023) on worldwide income (CGT effective 21.6% on gains). Non-resident companies are taxable only on South African-source income. Territorial scope thus follows residence-basis for SA-incorporated entities; no special tax-exempt zone classification applies to a standard logistics/freight entity.

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a check-the-box (fiscal transparency) election for close corporations/private companies; a (Pty) Ltd is a CGT and income tax taxpayer at entity level. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT grouping (Section 50 VAT Act) for closely-held group companies; (ii) group tax relief intra-group asset transfers (Section 45 ITA) with joint election; (iii) micro-business turnover tax election (Sixth Schedule) for turnover up to ZAR 1 million; (iv) provisional taxpayer quarterly/annual payment elections. Hybrid mismatch rules (Sections 25F–25M ITA) operate mechanically, with no elective opt-out for mismatched branches/CFCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or expected in the next 12 months (compulsory); ZAR 50,000 voluntary registration threshold for non-resident suppliers of electronic services; registration is per-entity and applies from date of exceeding the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Section 11(1)(a) VAT Act where goods are sold/consigned and physically exported to a non-South African destination within 90 days of the time of supply (longer extension available from SARS). Services physically rendered in SA relating to the export (freight arrangement, ISIC 5229) qualify under Section 11(2)(l)/(o) when rendered to a non-resident, or in respect of exported movable goods. The exporter must hold prescribed documentary proof (Section 11(3)); for GDP pharma freight, further evidence of cold-chain compliance and delivery abroad is essential. Local inland transport bundled with export logistics can be zero-rated only if part of the export transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / Goods Declaration) approved by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill covering the international transport
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt in ZAR/FX (or foreign bank statement for export proceeds)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of export date (carrier release, delivery/collection receipt)
  • GDP temperature/excursion logs and QA certification (for pharmaceutical shipments)

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists; South Africa has not enacted compulsory e-invoicing/digital stamping. SARS eFiling is mandatory for digital submission of VAT returns (VAT201) and customs declarations (SAD500). Electronically generated invoices are valid under the ECT Act and TAA record-keeping rules; taxpayers must retain full audit trails. SARS has announced a phased national e-invoicing framework for the future, but current compliance obligations are limited to electronic return filing and records access on demand.

SARS conducts pre-refund verification and may audit zero-rated supplies; if documentation fails, the supply is re-characterised as standard-rated with additional VAT plus interest. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 penalties reach 125% of the shortfall for intentional evasion, 10% for late payments; fraudulent export refunds trigger criminal prosecution under Sections 233–234 TAA/VAT Act and retrospective clawbacks for up to 5 years (and unlimited in fraud cases). Freight forwarders acting as agents are jointly and severally liable where they participate in false export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible on current-account transactions (imports, freight charges, commissions, dividends and interest) provided the Authorised Dealer obtains documentary verification and the resident entity is tax-compliant. Capital-account flows are restricted: inbound non-resident equity investment into a ZA (Pty) Ltd requires routing via an Authorised Dealer with a formal share subscription agreement, SARB-aligned SE classification (direct investment equity or intra-group loan), CIPC return-of-allotment and beneficial-ownership filing, and maintenance of a segregated capital escrow account. Dividend and profit remittance is permitted after a SARS tax clearance certificate is produced and applicable withholding taxes (dividend WHT 20%; interest/royalty 15% with treaty reductions) are withheld and paid over; practical hurdles are documentary rather than quantitative, with no mandatory local reinvestment requirement for approved remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods exported from South Africa must be fully received in the country within 180 days from date of shipment (temporary COVID-era extension to 360 days has lapsed; further deferrals require FinSurv approval). There is no mandatory conversion to ZAR: proceeds may be credited to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) at an Authorised Dealer and retained or converted at market rates. The exporter must submit proof of export (SAD500/SAD505 customs declaration, bill of lading, airway bill) and complete the bank's export-proceeds screening; for GDP pharmaceutical freight services, service receipts from non-residents must also be repatriated within the same 180-day norm under Exchange Control Circular rules, with the underlying contracts retained for Authorised Dealer audit.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), exercising exchange control through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), together with Authorised Dealers (the major commercial banks) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1961, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium; legally viable only as a supplementary collection/payment rail, not as the primary treasury account. Non-resident digital EMIs and neobanks (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud-facilitated platforms, Payoneer) cannot settle on local SAMOS-based ZAR rails or discharge SARB exchange-control reporting, export-proceeds tracking, SE-code classification, SARS tax clearance verification, or CIPC beneficial-ownership filings. They may be used offshore to receive USD/EUR and later sweep into the ZA Tier-1 account via SWIFT, but all statutory exchange-control obligations remain with the South African company; EMIs must not underwrite the statutory export-proceeds surrender duty. Therefore a top-tier commercial bank is mandatory for capital inflows and GDP-pharma trade disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: non-resident shareholder transfers USD/EUR/GBP by SWIFT MT103 to the ZA (Pty) Ltd capital escrow account at the Authorised Dealer; the bank converts to ZAR at the prevailing market rate and effectuates settlement via SAMOS RTGS. Required documentation: share subscription agreement, board resolution approving the issue, SARB-aligned declaration classifying the flow as FDI equity (bank SE code for direct investment in equity, e.g., 4401) or FDI loan (SE code 4407/4408), shareholder identity and source-of-funds evidence, and a hold-notice preventing premature repatriation until CIPC share allotment and beneficial-ownership filings are complete. Inward loans to a South African resident where the aggregate outstanding exceeds ZAR 10 million require prior FinSurv approval or reporting under the Loan Reporting system; below-threshold loans are reported cumulatively by the Authorised Dealer. Outbound subsequent remittances of dividends or loan repayments require a SARS tax clearance certificate, withholding tax settlement, and the bank's remittance declaration showing a valid SARB SE classification.Approved clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 with GPI tracking for cross-border freight payments, forwarder invoices, supplier settlements and dividend/royalty remittances; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600 for fully shipped cargo values, with presentation of transport documents, commercial invoices and GDP cold-chain manifests; (3) Documentary collections (DP/DA) for lower-value pharmaceutical freight validations; (4) domestic ZAR disbursements via SAMOS real-time gross settlement for high-value wholesale transfers; (5) BankservAfrica ACB EFT (direct ACH) for recurring local payments such as warehouse handling, salary processing and road feeder services; (6) PayShap instant domestic payment rail for low-value operational payments. Physical trade payments must be matched against SAD500/SAD505 customs records and the company's invoice register; export-related documentary remittances must be aligned to the 180-day export-proceeds rule and the Authorised Dealer's monitoring module.Outright forward exchange contracts (FECs) for firm, committed current-account exposures (freight receivables and logistics payables) with maximum tenor of 12 months without FinSurv pre-approval; longer tenors require Authorised Dealer approval, settlement on maturity is mandatory, and roll-overs are permitted against verified underlying trade documents.Vanilla currency options and zero-cost collars on covered current-account exposures, provided the underlying is verifiable by the Authorised Dealer and the premium is self-funded; speculative option writing is prohibited.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): permissible only offshore (typically executed by the foreign parent for ZAR exposure) because onshore Authorised Dealers may not write ZAR NDFs without FinSurv approval; the local entity must hedge using onshore deliverable forwards only.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) and long-dated interest-rate and currency swaps: permissible for FDI debt and operational funding only after FinSurv approval or where executed under an Authorised Dealer programme; swap tenor must align with the underlying loan asset and must not create a synthetic short ZAR position.Forward rate agreements and ZAR interest rate swaps are available domestically for managing local funding cost; all hedging must be documented in the treasury and risk-management policy and marked-to-market for bank risk committees.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (statutory rate was 28% for earlier years).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on the amount of any dividend paid by a resident company to a non-resident beneficial owner (Income Tax Act s 64EA); the company must withhold and pay SARS by the end of the month following the month in which the dividend was declared and paid.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transfer pricing policy (conditions) must be retained where the aggregate value of affected transactions exceeds ZAR 5 million in a year; a full Local File is required where affected transactions exceed ZAR 50 million, or exceed ZAR 5 million where gross receipts/income exceed ZAR 100 million; a Master File is required for a local entity with affected transactions above ZAR 5 million within an MNE group whose consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds ZAR 10 billion. Local and Master Files must be produced within 30 days of a SARS request.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute, but substance is decisive in multiple regimes: corporate residence is determined by incorporation or place of effective management (s 1); CFC attribution under s 9D requires an active-business exclusion and excludes passive income of shell vehicles; the GAAR (ss 80A-80L) voids arrangements lacking commercial substance; anti-hybrid rules (ss 23H-25H) and Reportable Arrangement disclosure (TAA ss 34-39) apply; and DTA claims require beneficial ownership, with several treaties containing limitation-of-benefits clauses.
