Goods exported from South Africa qualify for 0% VAT when physically removed to another country within prescribed periods and substantiation proves export; services are zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply, directly in connection with land or movable property outside South Africa, or physically performed outside South Africa; for 3D art outsourcing, the service must be rendered to a non-resident client and supported by contracts, delivery certificates, and payment evidence to satisfy section 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act.

SARS does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction controls; vendors must issue full VAT tax invoices within 21 days of supply, retain electronic records for 5 years, and file VAT returns via eFiling; invoices must be in an acceptable electronic format with a SARS-verifiable audit trail; no digital stamping or API clearance is required.

Zero-rated export claims are audited against SARS customs data and bank inward remittance records; if documentation is deficient or export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, SARS may assess output VAT at 15%, impose understatement penalties of up to 100% of the shortfall, and for deliberate fraud refer for criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding R 10 million.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current account transactions, being payments for services, dividends, interest, royalties, and operational expenses, which may be executed through Authorised Dealers without individual FinSurv approval. Capital account transactions are liberalised but compliance-gated: inward equity investment is reportable only to the Authorised Dealer and requires no prior approval; outward foreign direct investment by South African corporate residents is permitted up to ZAR-denominated R1 billion per project without prior FinSurv approval under the 2021 reform; outward portfolio investments, foreign loans, and structured capital outflows above delegated thresholds require formal FinSurv application or dealer-level notification. Profit remittance hurdles: dividends may be remitted to non-resident shareholders only if (i) the underlying inward share subscription was recorded in the debtors/inward investment register of the Authorised Dealer at the time of original receipt, (ii) dividends are declared out of distributable profits, (iii) the company holds a valid South African Revenue Service (SARS) Tax Compliance Status PIN, and (iv) the company presents a board-approved dividend resolution supported by management accounts or audited statements. No restrictions apply to inward revenue for exported services; the ZAR is classified by the IMF as a convertible currency for current account purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa abolished compulsory surrender of export proceeds to the central bank. The applicable statutory framework is the FinSurv Authorised Dealer Manual (Export of Goods and Services chapter): all proceeds for services rendered, including 3D game art outsourcing, must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the date of invoice or completion of service, whichever the underlying contract specifies. The proceeds must be credited either to a domestic ZAR operating account (converted at the Authorised Dealer commercial rate) or to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with a local Authorised Dealer; offshore retention requires a specific foreign-working-capital allowance from FinSurv. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into ZAR, unlike surrender-based regimes in other African jurisdictions. Reporting obligation: the Authorised Dealer captures each receipt in the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) under the appropriate services-credit Balance of Payments code, and the company must retain contracts, invoices, SWIFT MT103 confirmations, and sales ledgers for a minimum of five years for potential exchange-control and SARS audit.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administers the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933. The SARB Prudential Authority supervises banks under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, while Authorised Dealers (domestic commercial banks with treasury licences) execute day-to-day foreign exchange transactions within delegated limits. The National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 governs the clearing and settlement rails operated by Bankserv and the South African Reserve Bank settlement system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Moderate-to-high viability strictly as a secondary collection and micro-payment rail; not a substitute for a SARB Authorised Dealer operating account. Wise Business, Payoneer, and PayPal operate in South Africa through partner banking and payment-system arrangements; they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under the Banks Act and their South African legal footprint is limited to registration or collaboration under the National Payment System Act framework. For ZA_PTY_LTD, whose target rails are wire transfer and PayPal, the following legal constraints apply: (i) service proceeds routed through an EMI must still be repatriated into the South African banking system within 180 days, meaning the EMI must settle into the company's domestic Authorised Dealer account; (ii) EMIs generally cannot disburse dividends, pay SARS, or fund statutory obligations, so the company requires a primary domestic account in parallel; (iii) as the entity qualifies as Low_Risk_Standard under banking AML classification, onboarding with Wise Business and Payoneer is attainable with UBO verification and company documents; (iv) PayPal is viable for ad-hoc studio micro-payments but triggers FICA file scrutiny where monthly inflows exceed R25,000, requiring underlying client contracts and invoice-level reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the initial ZAR capital injection from non-resident shareholders: (1) Execute a share subscription agreement and board resolution allotting ordinary shares; file the share register update with CIPC within the Companies Act prescribed period. (2) Provide the receiving Authorised Dealer with the CIPC registration, MOI, UBO register, director passports, SARS TCS PIN, and the subscription agreement; the dealer must record the investment as an inward direct investment in the company's financial surveillance register. (3) Instruct the foreign remitter to send a SWIFT MT103 with the 70 Remittance Information field stating: INWARD DIRECT INVESTMENT EQUITY SHARE SUBSCRIPTION ZA_PTY_LTD ISIC 7490 SERVICES, using the BOP purpose code for inward direct equity capital; the code must never indicate a personal gift, goods sale, or loan, as misclassification triggers a FinSurv refusal and red-flag review. (4) The bank will credit the ZAR account at the commercial exchange rate, or credit the FCA, and issue an inward transfer reference; retain this reference as the cornerstone evidence for future dividend remittances. (5) No prior FinSurv approval is required for equity subscription, and South Africa imposes no minimum capital requirement on a Pty Ltd; the capital can legitimately be R1 or any amount agreed by shareholders, though a commercially sensible capitalisation of at least three months of operating costs is recommended. (6) If the shareholder provides working capital as a loan instead of equity, a separate inward loan notification is required and FinSurv approval may apply above the dealer's delegated threshold; equity routing therefore avoids regulatory friction and is the recommended treasury structure.The clearing-rail architecture for ZA_PTY_LTD operating as a 3D art outsourcing studio is as follows: (a) Domestic operating expenditure, salaries, and local contractor payments settle via the South African interbank system (ACB/Bankserv) using ordinary EFT or batch payments from the ZAR current account; this rail is instant or same-day and requires no FX documentation. (b) Cross-border vendor subscriptions and overseas software licences are settled by commercial SWIFT MT103 instructions issued through the Authorised Dealer's electronic banking channel; the company must attach underlying vendor invoices and its SARS TCS PIN to obtain preferential pricing and avoid quarantine on the outgoing remittance. (c) Inbound export service receipts from international game studios may be credited either to the FCA or converted directly to ZAR; the FCA permits deferred conversion when the company anticipates a USD/EUR payment cycle, creating a natural treasury buffer, while the 180-day repatriation clock runs from invoice date. (e) PayPal withdrawals must be scheduled weekly or monthly into the ZAR account with a matching PayPal statement and client contract retained in the FICA file. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Local File threshold is a covered transaction value exceeding ZAR 10 million (for multinational enterprise groups with a related-party cross-border transaction); the Master File is required when the group's total consolidated annual revenue exceeds ZAR 1 billion and the group is a multinational enterprise (MNE) with South African operations. A Country-by-Country (CbC) Report must be filed by a South African resident entity that is the ultimate parent of an MNE group with consolidated annual revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion, and local filing for secondary residents applies to constituent entities with revenue thresholds per SARS Notice 22 of 2016.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate general Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to UAE/Bermuda, but SARS applies the 'substance over form' doctrine and the anti-avoidance rules of section 103(1) of the Income Tax Act, the transfer pricing adjustment under section 31, and the general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) under section 80A–80L. A 3D art outsourcing studio must demonstrate real personnel, office, equipment, software, and decision-making in South Africa to avoid recharacterisation of its income under section 31(3) (deemed separate entity) and to prevent the application of the controlled foreign company (CFC) rules to its foreign shareholders. Practical economic substance evidence: employment records of artists and riggers, studio leases, invoices for software (Maya, ZBrush, Substance Painter), active bank accounts, and service agreements with foreign principals. Failure to maintain substance may result in SARS disregarding the legal form of transactions and assessing the foreign parent on a look-through basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (SARS criminal enforcement)

Statutory sanction: Under section 235 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, tax evasion is a criminal offence carrying a fine not exceeding ZAR 2 million or imprisonment not exceeding 5 years, or both. In the case of a second conviction, the fine may be up to ZAR 4 million and imprisonment up to 10 years. SARS also imposes civil understatement penalties of 50%–150% of the shortfall under section 222 TAA, plus interest at the prescribed rate and a 20% penalty for operational non-compliance. Full restitution of the assessed tax is demanded before any settlement is considered.

Director personal liability: Directors of a (Pty) Ltd can be held personally liable for unpaid tax where they knowingly failed to cause the company to pay income tax, provisional tax, or withholding tax, under section 234 TAA (making a false statement) and section 238 TAA (representative taxpayer liability). Directors may face civil fraud claims brought by the South African Revenue Service under common law; SARS may obtain a preservation order and a civil judgment compelling asset seizure. The tax debt attaches to the director's personal estate if the company is insolvent or liquidated and the director failed to withhold employees' taxes or VAT. No travel ban is automatic, but under section 232 TAA a director who is under criminal investigation for tax evasion may have their passport blocked during the enquiry and on conviction be sentenced to imprisonment, effectively restricting travel.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may recharacterise or disregard the separate juristic personality of the company under section 31(3), treat the South African branch or the foreign parent as the true taxpayer, and adjust taxable income to arm's length. Where profit stripping is proven, the foreign parent may be assessed to South African tax on the profit attributable to the deemed South African permanent establishment, plus a 200% adverse assessment under section 31(5) if the transfer pricing adjustment is not supported by acceptable documentation or a contemporaneous TP policy. Interest accrues from the original due date. A public directive on conduit companies (SARS Interpretation Note 8) confirms that a conduit with insufficient substance may be ignored for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: The directors who incorporated the shell or signed the structured invoices may be jointly and severally liable for the tax debt under section 159 TAA (recovery of tax from persons in control) if they transferred assets out of the company knowing tax was owing. In addition to civil recovery, section 197 TAA imposes a fine and/or imprisonment for contempt of a SARS warrant. Under the Companies Act 71 of 2008, directors breach fiduciary duties when they use the company to evade tax; section 77(3) makes them liable for loss suffered by the company, and section 22 permits the court to declare the company a dissolved shell, with the directors personally responsible for the company's debts.
AML – Anti-Money Laundering (FIC Act)

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FIC Act), an accountable institution that fails to register may be fined up to ZAR 10 million; failure to report a cash threshold transaction (ZAR 25,000 or more) or a suspicious and unusual transaction is an offence with a penalty of up to ZAR 10 million or imprisonment of up to 15 years. Sanctions are imposed by the Financial Intelligence Centre; SARS and the National Prosecuting Authority may launch parallel asset forfeiture proceedings under Chapter 6 of the Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for breaching their duty to establish a money-laundering control policy under section 42A of the FIC Act. Where they knowingly fail to report suspicious transactions, they face imprisonment (up to 15 years) and a fine of up to ZAR 10 million. Civil asset seizure extends to personal assets if the proceeds of the offence are traced to the director's bank accounts. Directors can also be banned from serving as company directors under section 162 of the Companies Act for acting in a manner that amounts to reckless or fraudulent conduct.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: SARS requires the taxpayer to maintain contemporaneous documentation under section 31 of the Income Tax Act. A transfer pricing adjustment will increase the South African taxable income by the difference between the arm's length price and the price actually charged, plus interest. An additional 50% to 100% penalty is imposed if the adjustment is made on the basis of a 'gross negligence' or 'substantial understatement' (section 222 TAA). If the taxpayer fails to submit requested documentation within 30 days (or extended period) SARS may impose a fixed penalty of ZAR 25,000 per month of non-compliance under section 227 TAA, plus criminal sanctions for failure to comply with a directive.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer pricing declarations or invoice backdating schemes may be charged under section 235 TAA (tax evasion) and face imprisonment up to 5 years. The board is personally liable to the extent of the tax shortfall if they caused the company to be declared insolvent to avoid payment. SARS has powerful recovery tools: a director can become a 'person in control' under section 159 TAA and be proceeded against without requiring prior judgment against the company.
Dividend Withholding Tax Default

Statutory sanction: SARS will recharacterise the loan or fee as a dividend under section 64E(4) (if paid as a dividend in substance) and assess dividends tax at 20%, interest under section 187 TAA, and an understatement penalty of up to 50%–150% under section 222 TAA. A company that fails to withhold and pay dividends tax is liable for the full amount of the tax itself and may be subject to criminal proceedings under section 235 TAA. Penalties for late quantity remittance also accrue under section 211 TAA.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise the disguised distribution are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid dividends tax under section 64K(3) TAA. The failure to disclose the dividend to SARS can be treated as a structured transaction to avoid tax, causing the director to become subject to the anti-avoidance 'personal liability for unpaid tax' rule, and ultimately a protective arrest warrant could be issued for non-payment. The company's directors may also be prosecuted for making a false return and sentenced to imprisonment, while the foreign parent's assets in South Africa become liable for recovery.
Failure to File Provisional Tax and Annual Returns

Statutory sanction: SARS raises a default assessment under section 95 TAA that may be a grossly inflated estimate. Admin penalty per month of non-compliance under section 210 TAA is up to ZAR 16,000 per month. Interest at the prescribed rate runs from the original due date of the return. Under section 222 TAA, the later filing of a correct return after a default assessment triggers a 'failure to file' understatement penalty of 10%–25% of the difference, increasing to 50%–75% for intentional disregard. Where non-compliance continues for more than 12 months, SARS may issue an arrest warrant under section 208 TAA for failure to pay tax.

Director personal liability: The director's personal earnings and banking accounts can be attached under section 179 TAA for the company's outstanding debt if recovery from the company has been unsuccessful. A director's assets are at risk if the company has been registered and the shareholders fail to file; SARS frequently seeks consolidation orders declaring the company's bank accounts to be held for the benefit of the state and holds the directors accountable for tax debts where their conduct is fraudulent. A director who voluntarily deregisters the company to evade tax commits a criminal offence under the Companies Act.
