Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling portal

E-government filing portal

A non-resident company must register for income tax with SARS if it carries on a business through a permanent establishment, and for VAT if its annual taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold. The company must first obtain a SARS eFiling profile, then submit the relevant registration forms (IT101 for income tax and VAT101 for VAT) together with its incorporation certificate, list of directors, proof of a physical or representative address in South Africa, and banking details. SARS issues a separate income tax reference number and a VAT registration number once the application is processed. For a South African private limited company, the CIPC company number is linked to the SARS tax reference number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box election for private companies. A (Pty) Ltd is automatically taxed as a separate mainstream corporate taxpayer. Statutory elections include the headquarter company regime under section 9I of the Income Tax Act, the s. 11(a) allowance elections, and the s. 23M hybrid mismatch ordering elections. Hybrid mismatches are generally neutralised through default rule application rather than elective treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in annual taxable supplies (mandatory); ZAR 50,000 for voluntary registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies if the goods are physically removed from South Africa within the prescribed period and the exporter retains documentary proof of export. For services, the s. 11(2) zero-rating provisions apply to services supplied to non-residents not physically rendered in South Africa or not used in South Africa, subject to specific exclusions. The exporter must be a registered VAT vendor and must retain exports and supporting documents in its VAT records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of Payment / Bank Statement
  • Original/validated Letter of Credit if applicable

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. VAT taxpayers must issue compliant tax invoices under section 20 of the VAT Act; electronic tax invoices are permitted. VAT returns are filed electronically through SARS eFiling on a monthly, bi-monthly, or six-monthly cycle depending on turnover. SARS may request direct access to invoicing records and supporting data during audits.

SARS may disallow zero-rated treatment where the required documentation is incomplete or where goods or services are diverted or used in South Africa. This triggers a VAT clawback of the zero-rated output tax plus the re-input of non-deductible VAT, assessed from the date of supply. Penalties include the understatement penalty of 10% to 150% depending on the behaviour, an additional non-compliance penalty up to ZAR 16,000 per unreported fact or per non-submission, interest on outstanding VAT, and criminal prosecution in cases of fraudulent export claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a liberal but regulated convertibility regime. Current-account receipts and payments for services are freely remittable upon submission of supporting documentation. Capital-account transactions remain subject to exchange-control oversight, but a resident (Pty) Ltd may receive inward foreign equity investment without prior SARB approval provided the prescribed authorised-dealer reporting is completed. Profit remittance hurdles for a commercial inspection and testing company are primarily documentary: remittances of dividends to non-resident shareholders must satisfy Companies Act solvency requirements, be supported by a SARS Tax Compliance Status Pin, and have the standard Dividend Tax or Non-Resident Tax withholding withheld/accounted for. Related-party management or service fees must be supported by transfer-pricing documentation. There is no material ZAR inconvertibility risk for a compliance-observant firm.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa does not impose a percentage-based export surrender ratio. All proceeds from exported goods or services must be repatriated into the South African financial system, with the full contractual amount received within 180 days of shipment or service performance unless FinSurv grants an extension. Upon receipt, the foreign currency must be converted to ZAR via an authorised dealer within 30 days unless the company has been approved to maintain a Foreign Currency Account or Domestic Foreign Currency Account. Inspection/testing fees invoiced by the entity in USD/EUR are service exports and therefore subject to the same repatriation and conversion obligations, with the underlying inspection contract, test reports, and matching SWIFT payment serving as the evidentiary record.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which administers the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations issued thereunder.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low as a primary settlement account; Medium as an ancillary receivables/payments rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and Airwallex are not authorised dealers in South Africa and cannot supply the statutory reporting, service-export reconciliation, or exchange-control functionalities of a resident commercial bank. Although Wise has a South African payment-aggregation/FSP model for low-value inward and outward transfers, a (Pty) Ltd undertaking ISIC 7120 inspection services must maintain a resident commercial bank account as the master operating account; fintech rails may be used only for incidental small-value cross-border service fees where the authorised dealer's reporting threshold is not breached and all proceeds are ultimately reported and converted into ZAR through the core banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

An inward equity injection from a non-resident shareholder is routed through the SWIFT network (MT103) to the (Pty) Ltd's Domestic Foreign Currency Account or its ZAR settlement account at a South African authorised dealer. The payment narrative must clearly reference 'share capital contribution from shareholder' or 'inward investment'. The company must provide a board resolution authorising the share allotment, the signed Companies Act subscription contract, share certificates, investor KYC and source-of-funds declarations, and the authorised dealer's inward investment compliance form. The bank then files the required Financial Surveillance reporting. No prior SARB approval is normally required for issuing ordinary shares to a non-resident, but debt instruments or hybrid instruments require FinSurv registration before the funds are credited.Operational disbursements for overseas subcontractors, inspection equipment, laboratory consumables, and trade-related commissions are executed using SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers supported by the underlying contract, commercial invoice, and proof of service receipt. For high-value or procurement-intensive transactions, the bank issues an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit (MT700) or handles collections (CP/DP) under current Exchange Control Manual protocols. All outbound payments must be matched to the bank's exchange-control reporting code for current account trade/services and vetted against sanctions lists. Wise/electronic low-value rails may be used for minor disbursements if the beneficiary is pre-approved on the bank's payee list, subject to a per-transaction value cap determined by the authorised dealer.Forward exchange contract (FEC) with a South African authorised dealer for physical delivery of ZAR against USD/EUR/GBP, supported by a genuine current or forecast service/invoice exposure.Cross-currency swap (CCS) executed with an authorised dealer aligned to contractual inspection-fee flows or imported-equipment payment schedules.Plain-vanilla OTC currency options (call and put structures) entered into with an authorised dealer, permissible under SARB general hedging authority where a real economic exposure is documented.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) only for currency pairs with no deliverable spot ZAR market, executed through an authorised dealer and supported by a fully documented underlying trade/services invoice.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual ITR14 must be lodged within 12 months after the close of the company's fiscal year. For a 28 February year-end, the statutory deadline is 28 February of the following calendar year; a non-resident branch follows the same filing cycle based on its elected South African year of assessment.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Offshore income not attributable to a PE must not be entered; supporting contracts, workpapers and functional analysis should be retained. Late filing of the ITR14 exposes the company to SARS administrative non-compliance penalties, an estimated assessment, a mandatory understatement penalty ranging from 10% to 200% depending on behavior, and interest on unpaid tax at the prescribed rate. Repeated or intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act.
ITR14 - Section 6 / Allowable Deductions Operating expenditure: salaries, subcontractor fees, travel and accommodation for inspectors, vehicle costs, office lease, utilities, depreciation on testing equipment, insurance, repairs and s24C allowances. Deduct only expenditure actually incurred for the purpose of producing South African taxable income. Head-office allocations and group recharge charges must satisfy the arm's-length principle and be supported by invoices, time records and transfer pricing documentation. Late filing of the ITR14 exposes the company to SARS administrative non-compliance penalties, an estimated assessment, a mandatory understatement penalty ranging from 10% to 200% depending on behavior, and interest on unpaid tax at the prescribed rate. Repeated or intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act.
ITR14 - Section 18 / Assessed Loss Balance of prior-year assessed loss brought forward and set off against current-year taxable income, subject to the 80% annual offset ceiling for companies. Only South African assessed losses attributable to the South African PE may be carried forward. Foreign losses and losses of a controlled foreign company may not be offset unless they pass the foreign business establishment rules. Late filing of the ITR14 exposes the company to SARS administrative non-compliance penalties, an estimated assessment, a mandatory understatement penalty ranging from 10% to 200% depending on behavior, and interest on unpaid tax at the prescribed rate. Repeated or intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act.
ITR14 - Section 23 / Withholding and Provisional Tax Credits Provisional tax instalments paid, dividends tax withheld on distributions to non-resident shareholders, interest and royalty withholding taxes paid on behalf of non-residents, and any other assessed credits. Claim treaty-reduced withholding rates only where valid declaration forms and a Tax Residence Certificate (TRC) for the beneficial owner are held. Withholding is final for the non-resident recipient; the agent must file the relevant monthly withholding return even when reduced rates apply. Late filing of the ITR14 exposes the company to SARS administrative non-compliance penalties, an estimated assessment, a mandatory understatement penalty ranging from 10% to 200% depending on behavior, and interest on unpaid tax at the prescribed rate. Repeated or intentional non-filing may also trigger criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File required for connected-person cross-border transactions aggregating above ZAR 100 million in a year of assessment; Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds ZAR 10 billion. Country-by-Country notification applies to groups with ultimate South African parent and consolidated revenue above ZAR 10 billion. Below these thresholds, transactional and benchmark documentation must still be retained proportionate to the risk and complexity of related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime as found in UAE or Cayman. Economic substance is nevertheless decisive in three contexts: the controlled foreign company 'foreign business establishment' exemption under s9D; the general anti-avoidance rules under s80A–s80L where lack of commercial substance nullifies arrangements; and the interpretation of 'carrying on business' for PE profit attribution and dividend withholding exemptions. A Pty Ltd inspection agency without premises, employees, active decision-making, contracts, or risk-taking capacity is exposed to GAAR recharacterization and denial of treaty/incentive relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: SARS may issue an estimated assessment, impose understatement penalties up to 200% of understated tax, charge interest, and refer for prosecution. Criminal conviction under the Tax Administration Act carries imprisonment, a fine equal to up to ten times the tax evaded, or both.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs a false or incomplete ITR14, conceals income, or connives in fraudulent invoicing is jointly and severally liable with the company under the Companies Act and the Tax Administration Act. SARS may direct recovery from the director's personal assets, obtain preservation orders, impose travel bans, and secure the director's arrest if the matter proceeds criminally.
Shell company / absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply the general anti-avoidance rules to disregard the entity, reattribute income to the real controlling person, and tax at 27% together with understatement penalties and interest. CIPC may deregister the company for non-filing of annual returns.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell initiative may be liable for reckless and fraudulent business conduct under section 22 of the Companies Act. Liability can extend to unpaid SARS assessments, disqualification from holding future directorships, and civil claims by creditors and the fiscus.
Transfer pricing mispricing / profit shifting

Statutory sanction: SARS will adjust the taxable income to the arm's-length price under section 31 of the Income Tax Act. The adjustment attracts normal tax at 27%, interest, and an understatement penalty from 100% to 200% for failure to maintain or rely on reliable transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length cross-border arrangements and sign a knowingly incorrect ITR14 may be personally assessed under the financial-fault liability rules of the Tax Administration Act. SARS can recover from directors who materially contributed to the understatement, and foreign exchange control and DTA exchange-of-information referrals may expose controlling shareholders to cross-border investigations.
Withholding tax evasion on passive income distributions

Statutory sanction: SARS may recharacterize the payment to its true legal form, impose dividends tax or royalty withholding tax at 20% or 15% respectively, add interest, and apply the understatement-penalty regime for intentional tax avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors serving as responsible persons for the withholding company are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under the Tax Administration Act where they caused or permitted the non-withholding. Personal recovery, company deregistration, and SARS enforcement orders against personal assets are available remedies.
