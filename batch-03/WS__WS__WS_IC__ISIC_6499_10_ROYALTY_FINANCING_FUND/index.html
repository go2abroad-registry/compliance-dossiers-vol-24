Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of Samoa (MfR) — official administrative portal 'E-Services / e-Revenue' at https://www.revenue.gov.ws; the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) acts as the regulatory registrar for International Companies.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax and requires no domestic TIN for income tax purposes. Where a TIN is nonetheless required for bank account opening, customs clearance, payroll, or VAGST compliance, the IC submits Form TIN-01 to the MfR together with the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, passport copies of the ultimate beneficial owner, and proof of the registered office in Samoa. Processing is performed through the E-Services portal and the TIN certificate is issued digitally.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Samoa International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) grants a blanket income tax exemption on all IC income, regardless of the territorial source; no residence-based or remittance-based charge applies. — A foreign-owned International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax and requires no domestic TIN for income tax purposes. Where a TIN is nonetheless required for bank account opening, customs clearance, payroll, or VAGST compliance, the IC submits Form TIN-01 to the MfR together with the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, passport copies of the ultimate beneficial owner, and proof of the registered office in Samoa. Processing is performed through the E-Services portal and the TIN certificate is issued digitally.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Samoa International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) grants a blanket income tax exemption on all IC income, regardless of the territorial source; no residence-based or remittance-based charge applies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, fiscal-transparency, or hybrid mismatch election is available under Samoan law. An IC may voluntarily convert to a domestic company under the International Companies Act 1987, thereby becoming subject to the Income Tax Act 2015; however, such conversion is effectively irreversible and commercially detrimental to a cross-border royalty-financing fund. Samoa has not enacted BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch or CFC election rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

≈ WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover (VAGST Act 2009, as amended); the threshold is not engaged by an IC whose supplies are made exclusively to non-residents and that holds no Samoan business presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the physical export of goods, to specified exported services, to services supplied to a non-resident recipient for use wholly outside Samoa, and to international transport under section 7 of the VAGST Act 2009. An IC's cross-border royalty and revenue-share income from non-resident digital-brand borrowers falls outside the scope of VAGST entirely; if the IC voluntarily registers, the zero-rating rule applies where the recipient belongs outside Samoa and the service is not performed in Samoa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec) lodged through the Samoa Customs electronic single window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing foreign currency receipt
  • Executed revenue-based financing agreement with the non-resident counterparty
  • VAGST-compliant tax invoice expressly referencing the zero-rated supply

Samoa imposes no national real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. VAGST-registered persons must issue tax invoices containing the prescribed particulars. VAGST returns and payments are submitted electronically via the MfR E-Services portal; e-invoicing integration is optional rather than compulsory.

Samoa does not prescribe a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day metric is applied as a prudent internal compliance benchmark. Fraudulent or incorrect zero-rating claims are sanctioned under section 52 of the VAGST Act 2009 by an administrative penalty of up to 200% of the tax avoided, default interest as gazetted (currently 10% per annum), criminal fines of up to 500 penalty units, and imprisonment for up to five years. The MfR may issue retrospective reassessments within the extended audit window following false statements and claw back any input tax credits previously refunded.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tālā (WST) is not freely convertible. Current-account payments — such as royalties, financing fees, and operational disbursements — are generally authorised when made from a licensed bank account and supported by valid contracts, invoices, and underlying economic documentation. Capital-account flows, including inbound equity subscriptions, intercompany loans, and repayment of principal, require CBS approval in certain circumstances; however, an International Company (IC) that is non-resident, has no Samoan-resident counterparties, and maintains only external foreign-currency accounts may repatriate funds without a specific CBS permit provided the transaction is not routed through a Samoan-resident WST account. Profit remittances are permissible after audited financial statements confirm distributable reserves; for a tax-exempt IC, there is no Samoan withholding tax or domestic profit tax on licensed royalty or financing-fund income generated outside Samoa.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa imposes an exchange-control repatriation obligation on exporters: proceeds from exports and foreign-currency earnings must be received in Samoa through a licensed bank normally within 180 days of shipment unless a CBS exemption is granted. The mandatory conversion ratio is not applied automatically; the CBS may direct the conversion of export receipts into WST on a case-by-case basis for residents where reserve requirements necessitate. A non-resident International Company engaged exclusively in revenue-based financing for digitally originated royalty streams is not normally subject to mandatory conversion, provided no export of physical Samoan goods is involved. Any conversion or surrender variance requires a formal application through the servicing bank to the CBS Exchange Control Department using the applicable EC form or its electronic equivalent.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), exercising powers under the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Exchange Control Regulations 1976, and the Central Bank of Samoa Act 2005, is the sole foreign-exchange and monetary authority in Samoa.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for ordinary Samoa resident companies; 0% for an International Company (IC) because the IC is expressly outside the Samoan income tax net under the International Companies Act 1987.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by an International Company to non-resident shareholders. For an ordinary Samoa resident company, the statutory final dividend withholding tax rate is 15%; IC dividends are not Samoan-source and therefore carry zero Samoan withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None in Samoa as no comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation statute exists. The Income Tax Act 2015 imposes a general arm's-length principle for related-party transactions of resident companies, but the IC's zero-tax status removes any fiscal impact. A prudent internal transfer-pricing and substance file is recommended for all related-party transactions exceeding WST 500,000.

Economic substance scope: Applies: an IC carrying on any 'Relevant Activity' under Samoa's International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2021, including finance and leasing, funding and investment, or intellectual property licensing, must demonstrate economic substance in Samoa. The IC must be directed and managed in Samoa, perform core income-generating activities in Samoa, maintain premises and full-time employees, and incur adequate expenditure proportional to the royalty-financing business. An Annual Economic Substance Return must be filed with SIFSA. Failure triggers financial penalties and mandatory information exchange with foreign tax authorities, exposing the IC to reclassification as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and sham transaction

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the sham arrangement and assess on commercial substance. Penalties include default interest, a penalty of up to 100% of the avoided tax, monetary penalty up to WST 50,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for assessed taxes and penalties where they sign false returns or direct the sham structure. Corporate veil is pierced on proof of fraud, and directors face travel bans, asset freezes under Samoa's tax administration, and potential personal bankruptcy proceedings.
Shell company / Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) breach

Statutory sanction: Penalty of WST 50,000 for failure to file the Annual Economic Substance Return, continuing daily penalties of WST 1,000 per day, and striking-off of the IC. SIFSA will also exchange information with the foreign tax authority, exposing the structure to harsh recharacterization abroad.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for ESR penalties; the resident agent's license may be revoked, directors may be disqualified from managing Samoan entities for up to 10 years, and deliberately false ESR filings carry imprisonment up to 3 years.
AML/CTF and beneficial ownership contravention

Statutory sanction: Under the Samoa Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2018, penalties can reach WST 500,000 for corporate entities; wilful blindness or deliberate evasion of customer due diligence constitutes a criminal offense punishable by imprisonment up to 7 years, plus asset seizure.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and ultimate beneficial owners can be prosecuted personally; penalties include travel bans, asset forfeiture, a 10-year ban on holding directorships in Samoa, and extradition to treaty partners for money laundering or tax evasion.
