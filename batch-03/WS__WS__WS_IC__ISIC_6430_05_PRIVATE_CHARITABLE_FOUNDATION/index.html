Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist in Samoa. Automatic tax exemption applies by operation of Section 192 of the International Companies Act 1987, exempting the IC from income tax, VAGST, stamp duty, and exchange control. For any incidental onshore charitable activity conducted outside the IC structure, an Income Tax Exemption Certificate for charitable organizations may be obtained from the MfR under the Income Tax Ordinance 1959 (as amended).

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services supplied to non-residents—including offshore endowment management, grant administration, and philanthropic treasury and investment services to foreign beneficiaries—are zero-rated under the VAGST Act 2007. A tax-exempt IC is entirely outside the VAGST net; if the IC voluntarily registers for VAGST, full zero-rating attaches to export supplies supported by evidence of offshore consumption and settlement in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs, Ministry for Revenue)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed commercial bank in Samoa

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing approval, or digital stamping mandate is currently enforced. VAGST returns must be filed electronically via the MaF e-filing portal; invoices must disclose the supplier TIN, VAGST registration ID, and separately stated tax amount.

MfR audit powers extend six years over zero-rating claims. Improper claims are reassessed at 15% VAGST, attract a shortfall penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, and default interest at 20% per annum; fraudulent evasion is prosecuted under the Tax Administration Act 2015, with retrospective clawback, fines, and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a managed float for the Samoan Tala (WST), with the Central Bank of Samoa administering all dealings in foreign exchange through licensed authorised dealers (commercial banks). Current-account convertibility (grants, donations, fees, trade settlements) is broadly available, whereas capital-account transactions (investment flows, portfolio transfers, external borrowings) require case-by-case CBS approval. The WST is not quoted on any international electronic trading venue and is practically non-convertible offshore, so cross-border endowment flows must be settled in USD, AUD, EUR or NZD. The International Company (IC) is classified as a non-resident for exchange-control purposes: foreign-currency endowment receipts, donation inflows and grant outflows may be routed through a CBS-approved external account without forced conversion into WST, and remittances of foundation funds to foreign grantees are not subject to profit-remittance withholding, tax clearance or CBS surrender obligations. The only genuine remittance hurdle is evidentiary: the CBS and reporting banks require the underlying endowment deed, donor source-of-funds documentation and foundation grant policy to be produced whenever a wire is questioned.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1977, a resident exporter must receive the export proceeds within three months from the date of shipment and must offer 100% of the foreign currency proceeds to an authorised dealer for conversion into WST within one month of receipt; extensions are granted by the CBS only on written application. This statutory surrender timeline and full (100:100) conversion ratio apply to resident operational receipts but do not govern the IC charitable endowment: the foundation is a non-resident external entity holding an external account, and its donation and grant flows are exempt from the surrender obligation. Should the IC ever receive WST-denominated income inside Samoa through a resident account, the one-month surrender duty and 100% conversion ratio would apply to that receipt.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), acting under the Exchange Control Act 1977, the Exchange Control Regulations 1977 and the Financial Institutions Act 1996; the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) supervises the International Company register and offshore compliance interface.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Samoa maintains no digital-bank or electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing category, and the CBS does not authorise non-bank cross-border money transmission in WST. Offshore digital EMIs (multi-currency neobanks and payment platforms) are not recognised as exchange-control authorised dealers in Samoa, cannot hold a CBS-designated external account on behalf of the IC, and are commonly rejected by correspondent banks when the underlying beneficiary is a Samoan offshore entity. The only defensible use is auxiliary: an offshore EMI may collect small donor contributions in USD/EUR at the IC's instruction with immediate same-day settlement onward to the CBS-licensed external account; however, such EMIs must never become the primary treasury or grant-disbursement rail because of sanctions-screening gaps, lack of SIFA-compliant recordkeeping and elevated de-risking risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Endowment contributions and donor grants must be injected by SWIFT MT103 to the IC's CBS-approved external account at a licensed commercial bank, with SWIFT field 59 carrying the IC's exact legal name and SIFA registration number and field 70 narrative reading 'Donation - Charitable Endowment / ISIC 6430'; the remitting donor bank must use its true originator details to avoid correspondent rejection. The receiving Samoan bank verifies the CBS external-account authorisation, and wires at or above the bank's enhanced due-diligence threshold require the executed endowment deed, donor KYC pack and board resolution before the credit is released. Inward WST-denominated donations are accepted and credited to the external account at the managed-float rate with CBS reporting, while USD/AUD/EUR donations are retained in kind; no NDF or prepaid-instrument structuring is required because the IC is exchange-control non-resident. The IC should pre-agree with the bank an inbound-wire checklist covering donor name, donor country, purpose code, underlying instrument, expected date and amount so that large endowment injections do not fail at correspondent-bank screening.Grant outflows clear via SWIFT MT103 beneficiary transfers (with MT202 cover where split value-dating is used) through the bank's correspondent network, typically ANZ Australia/New Zealand, BSP Fiji/PNG or a US clearing bank, directly to grantee accounts or to research and scientific suppliers under grant-funded procurement. Because the IC is non-resident, external-account-to-external-account grant payments need no prior CBS exchange-control approval; only conversions into WST for local charitable operations require an authorised-dealer spot transaction and CBS notification. Commercial Letters of Credit are a legitimate secondary rail where endowment funds procure scientific equipment or laboratory consumables: the domestic bank may issue an irrevocable LC against the endowment's USD collateral held in a segregated escrowed account, with CBS approval of the underlying import. For repeat grantees, pre-approved SSI records and a disbursement calendar attached to the foundation's grant policy reduce friction; zero-value test wires and same-day reversal patterns must be avoided as they are recognised layering red flags.Onshore deliverable USD/WST forward contracts with a CBS-licensed authorised dealer where the IC has a scheduled WST-denominated disbursement in Samoa, tenor generally capped at 12 months and approved on a case-by-case basis by the Central Bank.Cross-currency swaps (USD against AUD, EUR or NZD) with the depository bank or a permitted overseas counterparty, used solely to match multi-currency endowment liabilities and subject to CBS notification and bank credit approval.Offshore over-the-counter non-deliverable forwards on the Samoan Tala (WST NDF), technically available to non-resident holders of external accounts but practically dormant because no continuous WST NDF benchmark exists; use is not recommended except in rare two-way quotes from large Australasian banks.Natural hedging through a multi-currency external-account ladder (USD/AUD/EUR/NZD) with maturity-matched grant liabilities, requiring zero regulatory approval and operating as the IC's default zero-derivative treasury policy for a charitable endowment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Samoa-specific Local File or Master File thresholds; the Income Tax Act 2015 applies the arm's length principle to cross-border dealings of resident taxpayers, but the exempt IC is outside the TP documentation regime and no local-currency filing threshold is triggered for the tax-exempt endowment entity

Economic substance scope: Samoa has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations in the BVI/Cayman sense. However, the IC regime statutorily requires each company to maintain a licensed registered agent, a physical registered office in Samoa, complete books and records, and an annual return; this imposed presence is the substance baseline that prevents recharacterization as a shell or as a Samoan tax resident. Endowment management must be documented with board minutes, investment records and grant approval papers to satisfy OECD/EU substance reviews and bank counterparty due diligence

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Tax shortfall penalty up to 150% of the underpaid tax, plus statutory interest and criminal fines up to WST 100,000; imprisonment for up to 7 years for fraudulent tax declarations; cancellation of the IC's exempt status and deregistration

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent are jointly and severally liable for restitution of unpaid taxes and penalties; the Samoan courts may freeze personal bank accounts, issue warrants of arrest and impose departure bans pending resolution of the tax debt
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off and deregistration under the International Companies Act; penalties for failure to keep records; disqualification of the registered agent; reinstatement granted only upon payment of arrears and restoration fees and production of substantiated records

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for reinstatement costs and third-party losses caused by the sham; court-ordered disqualification from acting as a director for up to 5 years; personal liability for false annual return declarations
AML / CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines up to WST 1,000,000; revocation of the international company license and the agent license; confiscation of the funds involved; adverse regulatory findings that cause reciprocal blacklisting of the agent

Director personal liability: Individual fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering offences; travel bans and asset restraint orders pending trial; directors personally responsible for AML compliance failures within the foundation
Unlicensed Charitable Operations / Grant Misappropriation

Statutory sanction: Cease-and-desist orders; fines up to WST 25,000 for unregistered charitable solicitation; court-ordered restitution and freezing of the foundation's bank accounts; retrospective cancellation of the tax exemption with reassessment on all managed assets

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for equitable restitution of misapplied grant monies; disqualification from managing charities; criminal prosecution for theft and fraudulent conversion carrying custodial sentences; personal travel bans imposed on evidence of dissipation of foundation assets
