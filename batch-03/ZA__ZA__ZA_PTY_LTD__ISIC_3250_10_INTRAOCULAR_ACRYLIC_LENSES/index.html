Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

Foreign owners must first incorporate the (Pty) Ltd via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); the CIPC electronic integration automatically assigns an income tax reference number. Thereafter the entity registers on SARS eFiling for a Vendor (VAT) number using form VAT101, for PAYE using form EMP101, and for customs using the Customs Client Registration (CR) code. Non-resident shareholders require a SARS Non-Resident tax reference code, obtained via the SARS branch or eFiling non-resident registration module, and each foreign director must obtain a SARS tax reference number before filing can occur.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a resident South African (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at the standard corporate rate; no look-through to shareholders; distributions are subject to Dividends Tax at 20% as a final withholding. Territorial scope: worldwide for tax-resident companies; foreign-source income may be exempt under the participation exemption (section 10B) or subject to CFC attribution rules (section 9D). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a resident South African (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at the standard corporate rate; no look-through to shareholders; distributions are subject to Dividends Tax at 20% as a final withholding. Territorial scope: worldwide for tax-resident companies; foreign-source income may be exempt under the participation exemption (section 10B) or subject to CFC attribution rules (section 9D). Tax-exempt zones: none within the national territory at the corporate level.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under the income tax code; a (Pty) Ltd is a per se corporation. Elective mechanisms available include: group restructuring elections under section 45 (asset-for-share rollovers), CFC inclusion elections under section 9D paragraph 4, the 12B/12C capital allowance elections, and the hybrid mismatch de minimis election under sections 23M-23T which applies on a reporting entity basis. The dividends declaration and deemed dividend elections are governed by section 64D. Foreign shareholder elections under double tax treaties (e.g., treaty relief for interest/royalty) are made via SARS forms for reduced withholding rates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when taxable supplies exceeded ZAR 1,000,000 in the preceding 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted without a turnover minimum where the vendor conducts taxable enterprise activities, subject to SARS approval and security requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 11(1)(a) applies to goods supplied to a non-resident customer and exported by the supplier within 90 days of supply, on condition that the supplier retains documentary proof of export in accordance with Rule 16 of the VAT Rules (bill of lading, customs export declaration, transport document, insurance certificate and payment evidence). Intraocular acrylic lenses manufactured in South Africa and shipped to SACU or foreign ophthalmic distributors qualify for the 0% rate if the full documentary chain shows physical movement from South Africa to the foreign destination. Services physically rendered outside South Africa and consumable foreign services may qualify for zero-rating under section 11(2)(l); export refunds are paid within 21 business days where the vendor is not in a VAT refund carried-forward position.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) with electronic SARS clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing export to the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange settlement advice from an authorised dealer
  • Certificate of Origin (for SACU/preferential claims) and insurance certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is in force. South Africa requires tax invoices to contain prescribed particulars, issued within 21 days of supply, and SARS operates an eFiling-based risk engine with mandatory electronic submission of VAT201 returns, customs SAD500 lodgements via the SARS EDI/APIS systems, and a voluntary SARS e-Invoice standard aligned with the OECD Digital Tax Invoice model. Authorised Economic Operator status allows relaxed documentary procedures; no cryptographic stamping or real-time online clearance is currently statutory.

SARS may disallow zero-rating and re-assess exports at 15% VAT where the documentary proof bundle is incomplete or export is not effected within 90 days of supply. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, an understatement penalty of 100% applies for intentional or grossly negligent export zero-rating fraud, plus a 10% late payment penalty (section 213), interest at the prescribed rate (currently 10.5% per annum, section 189), and criminal prosecution under section 234 with fines up to ZAR 5 million or imprisonment up to 10 years for fraudulent false declarations. The retrospective assessment window is five years, extended to ten years where fraud or intentional tax evasion is proven; export clawbacks include reversal of refund claims with interest from the original refund date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is a transferable but not fully convertible currency under exchange control. Current account transactions supporting genuine cross-border trade in goods and services are effectively freely convertible through Authorised Dealers. Capital account transactions, including inbound non-resident equity investments, intercompany shareholder loans, profit distributions and disinvestment proceeds, are liberalised for non-residents subject to submission of prescribed declarations and registration forms (notably Form C for equity, loan registration for shareholder debt, and a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status pin for outward profit remittance). Profit remittance hurdles arise only where a resident company seeks to fund a capital outflow to a non-resident shareholder from distributable reserves without proper underlying documentation or where SARS/PRA compliance clearance is unresolved. Non-resident-owned South African companies may retain export proceeds in designated foreign currency accounts only under Authorised Dealer approval supported by documentary justifications; otherwise receipts must be converted to ZAR on receipt. Resident outbound direct investment remains subject to FinSurv thresholds; inward capital generally does not require prior exchange control approval, but the mandate to file inward investment returns exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Regulation 6 and Regulation 9 of the South African Exchange Control Regulations, export proceeds must be received by the exporter in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment unless the Financial Surveillance Department or an Authorised Dealer grants an extended period based on genuine trade credit terms. Export receipts must be paid into a South African bank account maintained with an Authorised Dealer and, absent an approved foreign currency retention account, must be sold to the Authorised Dealer for ZAR on receipt; this creates a mandatory 100% conversion/surrender obligation. Every export shipment must be supported by a completed Form DA/EX (Export Declaration), the exporter's declaration, shipping documents, proof of foreign currency receipt, and matching bank processing; non-compliance exposes the company to exchange control penalties, SARS VAT assessments, foreign currency shortfall responsibilities and statutory remedies.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the national exchange control authority; the SARB delegates day-to-day exchange control administration to Authorised Dealers licensed under the South African Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. South Africa's exchange control framework does not authorise offshore FinTech electronic money institutions (EMIs), such as global payment platforms or virtual account providers without a South African banking licence, to act as Authorised Dealers for ZA residents. A South African (Pty) Ltd may not lawfully hold export receivables in an offshore EMI account without specific FinSurv approval, and inbound payments received through an unregulated EMI generally fail FICA/KYC duplication checks because the underlying beneficial-ownership evidence is not directly transferable to the South African Authorised Dealer. A compliant alternative is for foreign purchasers to pay directly by SWIFT from their own commercial bank account to the company's South African bank account; onshore fintech wallets licensed by the SARB Prudential Authority may be used only for domestic ZAR collection, not for FX clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from a non-resident shareholder must remit through SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for cover) from the overseas bank directly to the South African Authorised Dealer account, either in ZAR or hard foreign currency. The payment must be powered with the SARB-prescribed purpose code for Direct Investment (equity capital), e.g., PUR110, and a narrative referencing the board resolution allotting shares and the subscription agreement, otherwise the Authorised Dealer may reject or reverse the credit. On receipt, the company must file Form C through the bank and may request a Foreign Investment Certificate to document the contribution for future dividend and disinvestment remittances. For intercompany shareholder loans, the company must notify the Authorised Dealer, complete loan registration, and demonstrate that interest terms conform to force-of-interest guidelines; repayments of registered loans may be remitted without prior FinSurv approval after the loan is fully registered.Trade settlement for raw material imports and capital-equipment purchases is executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers, commercial documentary letters of credit issued via MT700/MT710, and documentary collections (DP/DA). A South African importer must ensure that the underlying customs declaration (SAD 500) is lodged, any import permit or SACU regulatory authorisation is secured, and SARS customs duty/VAT obligations are recorded. Export disbursements to foreign distributors are normally cleared by open-account SWIFT MT103 or a confirmed letter-of-credit structure where the issuing bank is acceptable to the South African Authorised Dealer. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File / contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required for any controlled transaction (e.g., purchase of acrylic polymer resin, patent royalties, management fees) whose aggregate value exceeds R5 million in a year of assessment; Master File documentation is required when the multinational group's worldwide gross operating revenue exceeds R10 billion and the South African taxpayer's cross-border related-party transactions exceed R50 million in aggregate.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as in certain zero-tax jurisdictions; economic substance is instead tested through the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (s80A–s80L), the transfer pricing standard in s31 of the Income Tax Act, and the controlled foreign company rule in s9D. A manufactured intraocular lens company maintains economic substance through on-the-ground lens production, tangible machinery, local clinical-assay personnel, and actual R&D activity, and any offshore IP shell without South African operational substance will be vulnerable to a recharacterization adjustment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate under-reporting of manufacturing output

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under s235 of the Tax Administration Act and s104 of the Income Tax Act; additional tax of 100% of the under-assessed tax; administrative penalty of up to 75% of additional tax in cases of intentional tax evasion; fines of up to R10 million; imprisonment for a term not exceeding 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, approve, or cause the submission of false tax returns are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest under s13 of the TAA and s20(9) of the Companies Act; SARS may issue a personal liability notice, obtain a civil restitution order, impose a travel ban, and the offending director may be sentenced to imprisonment.
Transfer pricing manipulation through an offshore shell supplier or IP holding entity

Statutory sanction: SARS will make a transfer-pricing adjustment to taxable income on an arm's length basis; understatement penalties of 50% (if substantial) or 75% (if intentional); interest on additional tax; a secondary adjustment may be imposed as a deemed dividend or deemed capital distribution, triggering 20% dividend tax or additional consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly create or maintain sham intercompany contracts and artificial pricing can be held personally liable for the company's tax shortfall; the corporate veil may be pierced and directors subjected to civil restitution, travel bans, and criminal fraud prosecution with imprisonment up to 10 years.
Illicit profit repatriation / disguised dividend distribution

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as a dividend; assessment of 20% dividend tax, understatement penalty of not less than 50% and up to 75%; interest at the statutory rate; additional penalty for failure to withhold dividend tax by the paying company; possible criminal referral for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve a non-compliant distribution or misrepresent a dividend as a loan breach the fiduciary standard under the Companies Act and may be personally liable to restore the amount to the company; personal liability for unpaid dividend tax, travel bans, and criminal fraud sanctions apply where the transaction was a sham.
AML/CFT and special-licence breach in the medical-device export trade

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine under s45A of the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) of up to R50 million for an accountable institution; mandatory report to the Financial Intelligence Centre; deregistration or suspension of the SAHPRA manufacturer's licence; confiscation of the proceeds of the offence; criminal sanctions and imprisonment for money laundering of up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and company secretaries are held jointly and severally liable for FICA non-compliance; FICA allows an accountability order requiring payment of a civil penalty or personal restitution; in serious cases the directors face travel bans, asset freeze orders, and imprisonment up to 15 years, while the corporate veil is pierced where compliance obligations were knowingly ignored.
