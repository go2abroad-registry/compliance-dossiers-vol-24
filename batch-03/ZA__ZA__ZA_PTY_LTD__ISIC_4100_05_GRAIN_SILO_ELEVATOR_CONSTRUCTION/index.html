Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); primary digital portal is SARS eFiling (https://www.sars.gov.za/efiling) including the eFiling API for third-party filing agents, with Customs and Excise entries lodged through the SARS Customs module of eFiling.

E-government filing portal

A South African-incorporated (Pty) Ltd is automatically allotted an income tax reference number upon incorporation via integration between the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and SARS. A foreign-incorporated company with a South African permanent establishment (PE) must first register the foreign company with CIPC, then apply to SARS for a non-resident company income tax reference number via Form ITREG at a SARS branch or through eFiling. VAT: a separately identifiable VAT vendor registration (Form VAT101) is mandatory on eFiling once taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted where taxable turnover exceeded R50,000 in the preceding 12 months. For the grain silo EPC project, provisional tax registration is also required once estimated taxable income exceeds the de minimis threshold. SARS issues the 10-digit income tax number and a separate 10-digit VAT vendor number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A (Pty) Ltd is a corporate resident if incorporated in South Africa or effectively managed in South Africa and is taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% previously). Non-resident entities are taxed only on South African-source income with attribution analysis applied to any PE and to immovable-property-rich activities. No pass-through applies to a Pty Ltd; the discretionary 'small business corporation' (SBC) status under section 12E of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 is a rate concession only, not a flow-through election. Dividends distributed by the Pty Ltd are exempt from dividends tax when received by a South African corporate shareholder and are otherwise subject to dividends tax at 20% (treaty rates applicable). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A (Pty) Ltd is a corporate resident if incorporated in South Africa or effectively managed in South Africa and is taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% previously). Non-resident entities are taxed only on South African-source income with attribution analysis applied to any PE and to immovable-property-rich activities. No pass-through applies to a Pty Ltd; the discretionary 'small business corporation' (SBC) status under section 12E of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 is a rate concession only, not a flow-through election. Dividends distributed by the Pty Ltd are exempt from dividends tax when received by a South African corporate shareholder and are otherwise subject to dividends tax at 20% (treaty rates applicable). No tax-exempt zone treatment applies for the construction entity.

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a US-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections available to a (Pty) Ltd include: (1) section 12E SBC election for close corporations and companies held only by natural persons with turnover under R20 million; (2) section 9I Headquarter Company election for qualifying group finance, IP, and management service companies; (3) controlled foreign company (CFC) elections under section 9D, including the election to exclude foreign income where the foreign effective tax rate is at least 80% of the South African rate and the 'high-tax' exemption election; (4) hybrid mismatch elections under sections 23M and 23N to neutralize deductible payments that give rise to a deduction/non-inclusion outcome; (5) VAT apportionment elections under section 17(1) of the VAT Act for mixed taxable/exempt use of inputs; and (6) a group VAT registration election under section 50 of the VAT Act permitting two or more group companies to be registered as a single VAT vendor.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (R1 million) taxable supplies in any 12-month period for compulsory registration; R50,000 annual taxable turnover for voluntary registration. A foreign EPC contractor carrying on a construction trade at a South African grain silo site through a fixed establishment or a South African agent will have a VAT nexus and must register once the threshold is met or exceeded; ad hoc project revenue is aggregated into the 12-month rolling test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act zero-rates the supply of movable goods exported from South Africa to an export destination outside South Africa, provided the goods are exported within 90 days of the date of the tax invoice and the supplier retains prescribed export proof. Section 11(2)(l) zero-rates services physically rendered offshore or services rendered directly and exclusively to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply, subject to exceptions for domestic consumption and services connected with South African immovable property. For this grain silo EPC scope, locally performed slip-form casting and construction site services are subject to 15% VAT; only exported prefabricated plant components, design data, engineering drawings, and engineering services delivered offshore to a non-resident client may qualify for zero rating, subject to documentary substantiation.

SARS eFiling is the statutory channel for lodging VAT 201 returns, income tax returns, and tax clearance certificates. SARS has adopted a national e-Invoicing framework based on OECD/BEEPS principles, with a mandated e-invoicing pilot and voluntary digital stamping through the SARS eInvoicing portal using UBL 2.1 / XML formats; however, industry-wide mandatory real-time transaction clearance by SARS is not yet in force. Standard requirements under the VAT Act: each full tax invoice must be issued within 21 days of the supply and contain invoice serial number, supplier VAT number and address, description, quantity, consideration, and VAT amount; construction milestone invoicing requires a separate full tax invoice for each milestone and retention of all acceptance certificates. SARS may, under section 24A of the VAT Act, impose e-invoicing standards, digital signature requirements, and mandatory electronic record keeping for specified classes of taxpayers.

SARS may retrospectively claw back zero-rated output VAT where the prescribed export evidence (SAD500, bill of lading, and inward remittance certificate) is not filed with the VAT 201 return or retained for at least five years; the VAT becomes due with interest under section 187 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 and additional assessments may be raised subject to the five-year (general) or six-year (material misrepresentation) prescription limits. Understatement penalties under section 222 of the Tax Administration Act range from 25% up to 150% of the understated tax, with 150% applicable to intentional evasion, 125% for gross negligence, and 100% for substantial understatement with intentional disregard. Criminal liability may arise under the VAT Act and the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 for false certification of exports, and under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998 for money laundering, attracting fines and imprisonment of up to 30 years; SARS also applies an automated export verification risk engine that cross-references customs declarations with bank FX receipts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is a partially convertible currency. Current-account payments are routinely permitted through an Authorised Dealer upon satisfactory commercial documentation and, where required, submission of Form A; capital inflows are permissible but must be reported through balance-of-payments (BOP) procedures, with foreign loans requiring SARB registration or Authorised Dealer approval. Profit distribution to non-resident shareholders is lawful through the ordinary dividend route, subject to 20% Dividends Tax (treaty-reduced where applicable), audited distributable profits, and confirmation that the underlying inward funding was properly reported or registered. Practical remittance hurdles include bank-level verification of the foreign shareholder's beneficial owners, source-of-funds evidence, SARS tax-clearance validation, and the requirement that the dividend not represent a disguised capital repatriation or dividend-stripping arrangement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exported under the silo-EPC scope, full export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days of shipment unless the Authorised Dealer or FinSurv grants an extension; receipts may be credited to an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with a SA Authorised Dealer or converted to ZAR at market rates. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender percentage; the exporter may retain 100% of the foreign currency in the FCA, but must disclose all receipts in periodic BOP returns and may only use the FCA for bona fide current-account purposes. Failure to timeously repatriate or report amounts exposes the company to administrative penalties, interest, and potential criminal liability under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; Authorised Dealers act as delegated gatekeepers, and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) supervises AML/CFT compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. A South African resident (Pty) Ltd may not lawfully hold operating or treasury balances with an offshore EMI that is not registered as an Authorised Dealer under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; otherwise inward and outward transactions would breach exchange control and domestic banks would reject the counterparty on FIC 'originator information' validation grounds. An offshore EMI may become workable only if it partners with an SA Authorised Dealer as settlement agent or is itself licensed in South Africa under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, and even then the main settlement hub, BOP reporting, and FX conversion must remain with the SA Authorised Dealer. The entity may use EMIs for informational or ancillary purposes, but not as the primary clearing rail for statutory trade or capital flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and debt injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the investor's home bank into a segregated capital-escrow account or a dedicated foreign-currency account at a designated SA Authorised Dealer. The wire narrative must contain an explicit balance-of-payments purpose descriptor, e.g., 'Direct investment - equity in SA construction company' or 'Inter-company loan for grain silo EPC project', and must be supported by the board resolution authorising the injection, share-allotment documentation for equity, a registered loan agreement for debt, a completed inward-investment/BOP report, the company's valid SARS tax clearance, and the remitter's FICA pack. Standard inward equity does not require prior FinSurv approval, but foreign loans must be registered if prescribed thresholds are exceeded, and the ZAR spot conversion should be deliberately sequenced only after the Authorised Dealer has verified the funds, thus avoiding an irreversibly frozen-receipt scenario.Outbound trade settlements to foreign EPC subcontractors and equipment suppliers must be executed as SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers through the Authorised Dealer, supported by the underlying supplier invoice, contract or purchase order, proof of shipment or service delivery, and the completed Form A where the transaction exceeds applicable thresholds. Large plant and machinery instalments may instead be settled under irrevocable documentary credits issued as commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT720 series) or backed by standby letters of credit (MT760) and advance-payment bonds, thereby protecting milestone liquidity. 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2022 (Financial Year 2024/25 remains 27%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: R5,000,000 (South African Rand) aggregate value of connected-person transactions per tax year – above this SARS can impose documentation obligations under Section 31 and the OECD aligned Transfer Pricing Record-keeping Guidelines. For multinational groups with total consolidated group revenue above R1 billion and local transactions above R5 million, a full Local File and Master File are expected to be prepared and retained.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no separate ‘Economic Substance Regulations’ like the UAE; however, substance is inherently required through the residence test (place of effective management), Section 31 arm's-length pricing, controlled foreign company rules in Section 9D, and anti-avoidance rules including Section 103/General Anti-Abuse Rule. A shell entity with no physical presence, no employees and artificially routed contracts will be challenged. The tax authority looks for actual local contracting, construction management, compliance with Building Industry Bargaining Council rates, and payroll substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Section 220 of the Tax Administration Act, additional penalty of 100%–200% of the understated tax; criminal conviction carries a fine up to R1,000,000 and imprisonment up to five years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors and placed executives who knowingly participate may be jointly and severally liable under Sections 159–166 of the old Companies Act (and comparable non-statutory common law) for the company's unpaid tax, and may face personal asset attachment, travel bans (Section 12 of Tax Administration Act) and imprisonment for fraud; the corporate veil is pierced when a company is used as a shield for deliberate tax evasion.
Shell Company / DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: SARS will invoking the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) to invalidate any tax benefit; penalties of up to 10% of the tax avoided plus 75%–100% under-reporting penalty; interest runs from the original due date. A shell with nil economic nexus will be denied treaty relief and DWT exemption, leading to 20% DWT and 15% royalty/interest WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve fiat-managed shell operations are personally liable for the tax debt if the company is left with no assets; they can be interdicted from leaving South Africa until assessment is settled, and criminal liability arises for associated money laundering or tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / FICA Failures

Statutory sanction: Fines up to R10 million per contravention, plus possible suspension of the company's licence to trade. Senior management may be fined up to R5 million or receive imprisonment up to 10 years under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, No. 38 of 2001.

Director personal liability: Directors are the ‘accountable institutions’ for the (Pty) Ltd; criminal conviction automatically renders them personally disqualified to serve as directors; they face personal restitution and confiscation orders under the Prevention of Organized Crime Act 121 of 1998.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: SARS may assess a deemed gross income equal to the arm's-length adjustment under Section 31; penalty of 50% of the additional tax if the adjustment exceeds R100 million (lifetime cumulative), generally a 15% non-compliance penalty for late documentation, and interest at 10.75% per annum compounded. The TP adjustment is not automatically deductible for foreign tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors are required by Section 37 of the Tax Administration Act to ensure the company’s tax returns are correct. Deliberate mispricing of cross-border silo construction contracts opens directors to civil fraud claims, dissolution of corporate shield, travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution for money laundering where TP profits are hidden offshore.
