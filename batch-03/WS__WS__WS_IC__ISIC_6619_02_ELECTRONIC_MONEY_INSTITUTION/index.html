Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue (MOR), Government of Samoa, acting through the Commissioner of Revenue; official public portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws; electronic filing and taxpayer self-service portal: Integrated Revenue Administration System (IRAS) at https://services.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 2017 with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation, the constitutional documents, and the IC registration number; SIFA is the licensed registrar and also supervises the registered agent and the annual licence renewal obligation. Step 2 — Apply to the Ministry of Revenue for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) through IRAS using Form TIN-01, submitting the following: certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation, the IC's constitutional documents, the registered agent's letter of engagement identifying the directors and the ultimate beneficial owner, a certified passport copy of each director and shareholder, and evidence of the licensed EMI activity (Central Bank of Samoa in-principle licence or authorisation letter under the Financial Institutions Act). Step 3 — If the IC will make any taxable supplies in Samoa or if annual worldwide taxable turnover in respect of supplies consumed in Samoa exceeds WS$100,000, file the VAGST registration application (Form VAGST-01) through IRAS simultaneously with the TIN application; in the absence of any Samoa nexus the IC need not register for VAGST. Step 4 — The Ministry of Revenue issues a unique nine-digit TIN, and the VAGST registration number where applicable, within statutory processing time; because the IC's international business income is statutorily exempt, no corporate income tax registration or annual income tax return is due, but the IC must file an annual economic substance declaration and pay the SIFA annual renewal fee, and must keep the registered agent informed of changes in directors, shareholders, and the principal place of business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — International Company (IC) established under the International Companies Act 2017. The default statutory classification is an exempt offshore vehicle fully outside the income tax net of Samoa in respect of international business activities: profits, gains, and foreign-source income derived by the IC are exempt from Samoa income tax, there is no capital gains tax, no withholding tax on dividends or interest paid to non-residents, and no estate, inheritance, or gift duty on offshore assets. The exemption is territorial in scope: any income falling within the domestic-source rules of the Income Tax Act 2015 (for example, profits from business carried on in Samoa or from services rendered to Samoa residents) would be assessable at the prevailing corporate rate, subject to the IC's powers under its constitutional documents. Additionally, the IC is not a resident person for exchange control purposes and benefits from the Exempt zone regime of the International Companies Act 2017, which also grants the IC freedom from statutory audit and public filing of financial statements.

Available election forms

There is no check-the-box or partnership-filing election available in Samoa; entity classification is fixed by statute. The only elections relevant to this IC are: (i) the voluntary election to maintain international company status and thereby remain outside the Income Tax Act 2015 by satisfying the ongoing requirements of the International Companies Act 2017, including the annual SIFA licence fee and economic substance declaration; and (ii) the election to voluntarily register for VAGST where annual taxable supplies are below the WS$100,000 mandatory threshold, subject to the Commissioner's discretionary acceptance. Samoa has not enacted BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch rules, and no domestic hybrid-mismatch election exists; artificial hybrid arrangements are instead recharacterized under the general anti-avoidance rule in the Tax Administration Act 2015 and the transfer-pricing rules of the Income Tax Act 2015, which would apply only if domestic-source income were present.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WS$100,000 (annual value of taxable supplies) — the compulsory VAGST registration threshold under the VAGST Act 1994; a non-resident IC supplied wholly offshore digital financial services to non-residents has no VAGST nexus and is not obliged to register, but if the IC imports the services or incurs local input-taxed costs while remaining unregistered it cannot recover input VAT, and any incidental Samoa taxable turnover above the threshold triggers mandatory registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for the 0% VAGST rate when the goods are exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply (or such longer period as the Commissioner allows) and the exporter retains acceptable proof of export; a supply of services is zero-rated when the services are supplied to a non-resident customer who is outside Samoa at the time the services are rendered, which covers the offshore digital financial services of this licensed EMI, including virtual IBAN issuance, digital wallet reserve management, cross-border payment processing, and correspondent clearing fees. Where the IC is registered for VAGST, these zero-rated export supplies remain taxable supplies for the purpose of input tax credit recovery, so the IC is entitled to refunds of VAGST charged on its local business inputs (for example, Samoan legal, audit, and registered agent fees); where the IC remains unregistered, no VAGST recovery is available and the cost of local inputs is economically borne by the IC.

  • Samoa Customs export declaration (CusDec) lodged electronically through the ASYCUDA World system, with the customs officer's release endorsement
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment and exportation within the statutory time limit
  • Commercial invoice describing the exported goods and stating the zero-rated consideration
  • Underlying cross-border service contract, SEPA or correspondent-clearing settlement statement, and the customer's non-residence supporting evidence (certificate of incorporation, registered office certificate, or passport copy) for digital financial services treated as an export supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign currency account credit advice issued by a licensed Samoa commercial bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in Samoa
  • VAGST input tax invoices issued by local suppliers and the payment gateway or wallet issuer's settlement records supporting the input credit claimed

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in Samoa. The Ministry of Revenue operates the IRAS platform for electronic VAT return filing, e-payments, and taxpayer correspondence; all registered persons must issue and retain prescribed VAGST tax invoices containing the supplier's TIN, the customer's TIN, date, description, and amount of tax charged, and must retain records for five years. Samoa is advancing its revenue-administration digitalisation roadmap under IMF technical assistance, but no mandatory SAF-T, continuous transaction control, or clearance-by-stamp regime has been enacted.

The Ministry of Revenue and the Samoa Customs Service apply enhanced audit scrutiny to zero-rated export supplies by licensed financial institutions. A false zero-rating claim, fabricated export documentation, or a knowingly incorrect tax invoice is prosecuted under the VAGST Act 1994 and the Tax Administration Act 2015: the Commissioner may issue a retrospective clawback assessment disallowing the zero rate, recover all underpaid VAGST, and deny or recover input tax refunds, with the assessment period extended to up to six years in ordinary cases and up to ten years where fraud or wilful neglect is established. Civil shortfall penalties are graduated up to 100 percent of the tax evaded depending on culpability, statutory late-payment interest continues to accrue until full settlement, and criminal conviction for tax evasion or fraudulent invoices can attract substantial monetary fines and a term of imprisonment for directors, officers, and any accomplice, including the licensed registered agent, who participates in the falsification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tālā (WST) is a non-deliverable currency subject to current and capital account exchange controls. Current account payments in respect of bona fide trade in goods or services may be converted and remitted through an authorized dealer once documentary evidence is presented and the transaction is reported to CBS. Capital account inflows such as equity injections, shareholder loans, virtual IBAN reserve funding, and crypto-to-fiat conversion-related treasury balances generally require prior CBS approval or confirmation through an authorized commercial bank. Profit remittance is available to non-resident shareholders only after annual audited financial statements prove distributable reserves and the company has settled any domestic tax or withholding obligation; the remitting bank must complete the CBS capital-account approval application and obtain an exchange control release before USD conversion and transfer. Practical hurdles for an EMI include slower remittance cycles, additional documentary layers for beneficiary ownership behind trusts, CBS statistical reporting of all non-resident transfers, and the lack of a deep offshore WST market. There is no market-driven WST convertibility outside Samoa; offshore lenders or hedge counterparties typically require WST/USD NDF or bank letters of credit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBS exchange-control notices, all export proceeds must be collected through an authorized dealer in Samoa within 90 days from the date of export or service performance. Unless an exemption is granted, all foreign exchange export receipts must be converted into WST at the prevailing bank rate within that timeline and the conversion must be reported to CBS. For a licensed electronic-money international company, the practical application is limited to (i) actual Samoan export goods or services invoiced by the Samoa entity and (ii) inward remittances characterized as service fees or settlement consideration for e-money issuance. The company must maintain the underlying commercial agreements, invoices, freight/customs evidence, and bank credit advisements for five years. If the EMI is structured as a pure offshore international company deriving only foreign-source financial intermediation income, CBS may permit foreign-currency retention with a specific surrender exemption; however, the default regulatory position is full repatriation and conversion of export-value proceeds into WST unless otherwise approved in writing.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Apia, Samoa; the CBS is the national monetary authority and exchange-control administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1978. The Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) administers the International Companies Act 2017 and acts as the registry and licensing body for international financial services entities, including the international company that will hold the electronic-money/banking special licence.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Samoa does not have a dedicated EU/UK-style electronic-money institution licence. An offshore digital wallet and virtual-IBAN business may be carried on only through a Samoa-licensed international financial services provider or an international banking special licence approved by SIFA and CBS. Foreign-regulated EMI companies cannot provide services directly into Samoa without establishing a local licensed entity or a licensed financial services provider. The business plan is viable as a non-resident-facing treasury entity if the Samoa international company holds (i) a SIFA international financial services/electronic-money licence or international banking licence, (ii) segregated reserve accounts with a Samoan authorized dealer, and (iii) a physical principal office in Samoa for AML/CFT governance. Digital-only onboarding of end-customers from the EMI platform must be governed by the Samoan bank’s AML/CFT policy; no foreign EMI passporting is recognized. SEPA Credit and central-bank clearing are not direct membership products of a Samoa international company; they require a European partner bank or an SEPA-participating agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan funds must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the Samoan bank’s receiving branch. The remitter must state the exact corporate name, SIFA registration number, and the purpose as ‘Shareholder Capital Injection / Financing of Licensed Electronic Money Institution — ISIC 6619’ (BPM6 code for direct investment equity). Before execution the Samoa bank must accept the CBS Exchange Control capital-account application, verify that the incoming funds are not encumbered by anti-money-laundering red flags, and obtain copies of the board resolution, share subscription agreement, or facility agreement. Funds are initially credited to a Capital Escrow Account and cannot be released to the operating account until the bank completes the secondary source-of-funds check and the exchange-control approval (or deemed approval) has been recorded. For non-WST currencies the bank will convert only the amount actually required for Samoan operating expenses; the balance may remain in an approved foreign-currency account if CBS has issued a retention permission. The EMI must also file a CBS inward-foreign-direct-investment report through the receiving bank, giving the transaction reference, economic sector, UBO country, and expected use of funds.Trade disbursements and EMI treasury payments are executed on SWIFT MT103, MT202 COV, and documentary commercial letters of credit using the bank’s SWIFT BIC and corresponding banking network. A Samoa-licensed International Company has no direct central-bank clearing or SEPA membership; SEPA Credit transfers are possible only through a European partner bank or a SEPA-participating account. For every outward remittance the company must provide supporting invoices, contracts, licences, and FX confirmations, and the bank will apply sanctions screening, OFAC/UN/EU/BIS list screening, and CBS reporting. Commercial LC settlements (MT700 issued or MT707 amendment) require presentation of transport documents, commercial invoices, packing lists, inspection certificates, and insurance documents; the proceeds are paid into the Samoan operating account and retain the same anti-money-laundering audit trail as cash transactions. Since the EMI is treated as a non-bank financial institution, the bank may require outbound disbursements to be pre-notified within CBS’s daily or weekly FX reporting windows.Deliverable USD/WST forward contracts with an authorized dealer in Samoa, restricted to hedging an identified current or capital account exposure, with physical WST settlement at maturity.Cross-currency swaps (USD/WST) with a licensed Samoan bank, subject to CBS approval and used exclusively for cash-flow hedging of foreign-currency liabilities or assets.Structured forwards with an authorized dealer for translation of tier-2 subordinated debt, provided the underlying debt is approved by CBS and does not consist of offshore WST retail deposits.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) against the WST executed through a CBS-approved international bank and settled in USD; Samoa is a non-fully-convertible currency, so the NDF must have an underlying aggregate treasury exposure and match the EMI’s trade and net-settlement schedule.FX spot purchases through authorized dealer banks for same-day settlement of e-money redemption obligations, with no speculative open-currency position held overnight.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium. Samoa does not have a dedicated EU/UK-style electronic-money institution licence. An offshore digital wallet and virtual-IBAN business may be carried on only through a Samoa-licensed international financial services provider or an international banking special licence approved by SIFA and CBS. Foreign-regulated EMI companies cannot provide services directly into Samoa without establishing a local licensed entity or a licensed financial services provider. The business plan is viable as a non-resident-facing treasury entity if the Samoa international company holds (i) a SIFA international financial services/electronic-money licence or international banking licence, (ii) segregated reserve accounts with a Samoan authorized dealer, and (iii) a physical principal office in Samoa for AML/CFT governance. Digital-only onboarding of end-customers from the EMI platform must be governed by the Samoan bank’s AML/CFT policy; no foreign EMI passporting is recognized. SEPA Credit and central-bank clearing are not direct membership products of a Samoa international company; they require a European partner bank or an SEPA-participating agent.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% effective rate for a Samoa International Company (wholly exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty under the International Companies Act 1987); 25% headline corporate rate applies solely to domestic taxable companies conducting Samoa-source business outside the exempt-IC perimeter

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% withholding tax on dividends paid by an International Company to non-resident shareholders where the exempt-IC income and distribution satisfy the International Companies Act 1987 conditions; the domestic non-resident dividend withholding tax rate is 15%, which applies only where an International Company derives non-exempt Samoa-source income and the distribution is recharacterised as taxable

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required where annual related-party transactions of an International Company exceed WST 1,000,000 (approximately USD 375,000); a Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds WST 25,000,000; the documentation must support the arm's-length outcome of all cross-border dealings with associated enterprises of the electronic-money and digital-wallet reserve business

Economic substance scope: The Samoa Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2021 apply to International Companies conducting relevant activities, including banking, financing, digital payment services, distribution, and headquarters functions. An Electronic Money Institution issuing Virtual IBANs and holding digital-wallet reserves must demonstrate substantial economic presence in Samoa: local board governance, a physical office, adequate qualified employees engaged in core income-generating activity, and an annual ESR report lodged with SIFSA. Passive-income-only shell structures without local substance are treated as non-compliant and exposed to exchange of information and penalty proceedings

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 150% of the tax understated plus default interest; corporate fines up to WST 250,000 under the Income Tax Act 2012; deregistration of the International Company; reference to international tax transparency blacklists by the OECD or EU where the arrangement is presented as a tax-evasion vehicle

Director personal liability: Directors and the licensed agent are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the evaded revenue; conviction carries imprisonment up to 7 years, disqualification from acting as a director or trustee in Samoa, and seizure of corporate records to support civil recovery against foreign beneficial owners
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations penalties of WST 50,000 for the first breach and WST 100,000 for each continuing breach; mandatory disclosure to the Samoan competent authority for international information exchange; strike-off and liquidation under the International Companies Act 1987; loss of the tax-exempt license for the EMI activity

Director personal liability: Directors face civil recovery for unpaid government fees and substance deficiencies, travel bans pending regulatory inquiry, and debarment from serving as directors of any Samoa International Company; criminal breach of duty carries imprisonment up to 5 years and personal liability for third-party losses caused by the non-substantive structure
Anti-Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism Violations (Electronic Money Institution)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WST 1,000,000, revocation of any special financial-services license, and suspension of the International Company's registration under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2017; asset-freezing orders, mandatory segregation and wind-down of customer reserve accounts, and referral to overseas payment-rail regulators for violations of scheme rules of Central_Bank_Clearing or SEPA_Credit corridors

Director personal liability: Senior officers and directors risk criminal prosecution carrying imprisonment up to 14 years, personal fines up to WST 500,000, confiscation of proceeds, and, given the banking_aml_risk_tier of High_Risk_Special_License, an automatic ban from all financial-sector roles within Samoa and reciprocal debarment from equivalent fintech licensing regimes abroad
Withholding Tax Evasion and Repatriation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax reassessment on the true character of the payment, a grossed-up penalty of up to 50% of the withholding shortfall, and statutory default interest; denial of the New Zealand DTA benefits where residence or beneficial-ownership substance requirements are not met; possible reclassification of the International Company as a taxable domestic company for the offending income

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the offending distribution or loan resolution are personally liable for the unrecovered withholding tax and statutory penalties; criminal prosecution for tax evasion carries imprisonment up to 7 years, while civil fraud actions permit recovery directly against personal assets of the directors, including by way of asset tracing through the licensed agent's records
