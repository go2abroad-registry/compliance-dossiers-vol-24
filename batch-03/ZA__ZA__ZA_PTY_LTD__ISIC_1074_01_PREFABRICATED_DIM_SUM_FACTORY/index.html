Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Companies Act 71 of 2008 prescribes no statutory minimum share capital and no escrow account for a standard private company (Pty) Ltd; the MoI may authorise zero par value shares. In banking practice, a nominal paid-up capital of approximately ZAR 100–1,000 is recommended to be subscribed and credited to the company account once opened, but no CIPC escrow requirement applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A (Pty) Ltd is a separate legal taxpayer under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 and is taxed at the corporate rate of 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023. Territorial_Scope: a company incorporated in South Africa or effectively managed in South Africa is a tax resident taxed on worldwide income, while non-resident shareholders are taxed only on South African-source income. Dividends are subject to a 20% dividends tax withheld at entity level, relieving double taxation on distributions. Pass_Through_Eligible is not available as a default: small business corporation (Section 12E) and micro-business turnover tax (Sixth Schedule) regimes are elective. Tax_Exempt_Zone is not automatic: Special Economic Zone (SEZ) incentives under Section 12R of the Income Tax Act require prior designation and approval.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (a) Small Business Corporation status under Section 12E, elected by companies owned entirely by natural persons and meeting the turnover cap, giving progressive concessionary rates; (b) Micro Business Turnover Tax under the Sixth Schedule, elected by entities with annual turnover below ZAR 1 million; (c) Hybrid mismatch elections and adjustments under Sections 23M and 23N (OECD BEPS Action 2) together with the hybrid debt and hybrid equity re-characterisation rules in Section 8E and Section 8EA; (d) Group roll-over relief elections under Section 41-47 of the Income Tax Act for intra-group asset transfers, mergers and divisions; (e) SEZ designation under Section 12R and the Section 12I industrial investment allowance, both requiring advance approval from SARS and the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition; (f) Section 12BA accelerated depreciation and Section 11D R&D deduction elections, subject to SARS pre-approval for R&D; and (g) voluntary VAT registration for vendors with taxable turnover above ZAR 50,000 but below the compulsory threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 (South African Rand) of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected in the next 12 months; voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000; non-resident vendors carrying on an 'enterprise' in South Africa are registrable under the same threshold rules, but purely zero-rated export supplies by non-residents may be excluded from the threshold calculation only in the narrow circumstances specified in the VAT Act.

South Africa operates a phased VAT e-invoicing regime under the SARS VAT Modernisation Project. Designated large vendors (currently those with annual taxable turnover at or above the SARS-prescribed high-value threshold) are required to issue tax invoices as SARS e-invoices transmitted to the SARS central e-invoicing platform near-real-time, either directly via the SARS eFiling API or through a certified e-invoicing service provider; each e-invoice is validated and assigned a unique e-invoice identifier. There is no physical digital stamp or QR fiscal seal; integrity is established electronically by the SARS Centralised Data Hub. The mandate is being extended in tranches to the broader vendor population. Separately, all export shipments must be cleared electronically through the SARS Customs SAS module, requiring submission of the SAD 500 export declaration, supporting documents, and transit-customs data before goods are released; SARS cross-matches the export declaration against VAT returns, e-invoices and foreign-exchange records in near-real-time.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a floating exchange rate and a largely liberalised current account. The rand is freely convertible for current transactions such as trade, services, royalties, interest and lawfully declared dividends, subject to documentary verification and prudential reporting to SARB FinSurv. Capital account transactions are more restricted: inward foreign direct investment requires no prior exchange control approval, but outward capital flows, shareholder loans, guarantees, certain cross-border mergers and acquisitions, and capital repatriation events require appropriate underlying documentation, reporting through authorised dealers, and in some cases FinSurv approval. Dividend and profit remittances to a non-resident parent are permitted if the South African entity has sufficient distributable reserves evidenced by audited or reviewed financial statements, the dividend has been validly declared in accordance with the Companies Act 2008, the dividend withholding tax obligation has been discharged, and the authorised dealer is satisfied with beneficial ownership and source of funds. The ZAR is not legal tender outside South Africa but has no surrender, rationing or forced conversion quota for current export earnings once repatriated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South African export proceeds must be fully repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the date of export shipment. The exporter must submit the shipping documents, commercial invoice, customs export declaration (SAD500) and applicable bank export documentation to the authorised dealer. There is no mandatory statutory percentage conversion into rand; export receipts may be retained in an approved foreign currency account with a South African authorised dealer or converted into rand under a documented forward exchange arrangement. If export proceeds are not received within 180 days, the authorised dealer must report the outstanding export exposure to FinSurv, and the exporter must demonstrate repayment, approved extended credit terms, or a valid extenuating trade reason.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and administered through authorised dealer commercial banks in South Africa.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Low as a primary treasury rail and Medium only as an auxiliary front-end layer for invoicing, reporting, reconciliation or payment initiation. Offshore digital EMI platforms do not hold South African banking licences and cannot lawfully operate a South African domestic ZAR settlement account or clear rand-denominated payments without SARB, Prudential Authority and National Payment System approval. All South African currency conversion and local clearing must flow through a licensed authorised dealer, typically a tier-1 commercial bank registered under the Banks Act 1990 and participating in the South African Multiple Option Settlement system. An offshore EMI may be used for non-ZAR foreign currency trade collections or as a software interface, but it must be backed by local correspondent banking and the corporate treasury must retain a fully compliant local bank account for statutory receipts, export proceeds and SARS-related flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital should be executed via SWIFT MT103 or equivalent wire transfer from the parent or a lending intercompany entity to the South African tier-1 bank account of the operating company. The remittance text must identify the transaction clearly as inward direct investment equity subscription or shareholder loan disbursement, and the authorised dealer must tag the inflow with the appropriate SARB Balance of Payments statistical reference. For equity injection, the bank requires the CIPC company registration record, Memorandum of Incorporation, shareholder resolution, share subscription agreement, issued share certificate and a director-authorised instruction to credit the operational account. For shareholder loans, the bank requires an executed loan agreement, evidence of source of funds, solvency statement and confirmation that the proceeds are intended for working capital, equipment purchase or factory construction in the frozen food manufacturing plant. No prior SARB approval is required for a genuine inward FDI subscription, but every inward receipt must be documented and retained for future outbound repatriations, dividends and capital repayment proof. ZAR-denominated domestic clearing of capital funds should use a local South African bank clearing code; foreign currency funds may be converted through the authorised dealer at a market rate under a forward contract or spot instruction.Outbound trade disbursements for imported raw materials, frozen food processing equipment, packaging supplies and logistics services should be executed as SWIFT MT103 payments to global suppliers through the South African bank, or under an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by the South African bank via SWIFT MT700 and settled at sight or deferred date against presentation of compliant shipping and commercial documents. Direct open-account payments require the commercial invoice, purchase order, bank wire instructions and, where applicable, import customs documentation and Proof of Import via the SAD500 import declaration. For Sweets, Frozen Dim Sum or Dumpling factory inputs, the bank will additionally require product-specific shipping documents, health certificates or phytosanitary permits as necessary, along with the SARS customs entries. ZAR domestic payments between South African and SACU suppliers should be cleared through the South African Multiple Option Settlement RTGS system; cross-border USD/EUR/CNY settlements should be routed over SWIFT with commercial invoices and underlying trade evidence archived in the bank's trade services file. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (previously 28% for years ending before that date)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax withheld on any dividend declared by the company (s64E of the Income Tax Act), payable to SARS by the company as withholding agent within 30 days of the dividend date (for non-resident beneficial owners, unless exempt or treaty-reduced).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions aggregating above ZAR 100 million in a year of assessment require a full Local File; a Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 1 billion; Country-by-Country Reporting is required where group consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion (thresholds per SARS TP guidance aligned with s31 ITA for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 January 2023). Below these thresholds, the company must still maintain contemporaneous records demonstrating arm's length pricing on every cross-border related-party transaction.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not administer a Gulf-format Economic Substance regime; substance is nevertheless legally mandatory through: (1) the Place of Effective Management (POEM) test that governs tax residence; (2) the CFC inclusion rules (s9D) attributing passive profits of offshore subsidiaries to the SA resident; (3) the general anti-avoidance provisions (s80A-L ITA), which void arrangements lacking commercial substance and tainted purpose; (4) the common-law substance-over-form doctrine; and (5) the MLI Principal Purpose Test (PPT) for treaty benefits. The ZA (Pty) Ltd factory must evidence real operational substance – local employees, factory premises, production equipment, board meetings and decision-making in South Africa – to protect its treaty positions, WHT reliefs and intra-group arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under s235 TAA: fine and/or imprisonment up to five years; civil understatement penalty of up to 200% of the shortfall (Category 5 – intentional tax evasion) under s223 TAA; interest on outstanding tax at the prescribed rate; SARS may attach assets and obtain preservation orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or permit the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts under s77 TAA (gross negligence or fraud); risk criminal prosecution as co-principals; cross-border travel bans and exit restrictions under s194 TAA; directors may be declared delinquent and disqualified under the Companies Act, and the corporate veil may be pierced to recover the tax.
Shell Company / Impermissible Tax Avoidance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke s80A-L (GAAR) to set aside the arrangement, re-characterise the transactions, disallow deductions, and impose a 75% understatement penalty (Category 3 – impermissible avoidance arrangement); treaty benefits may be denied under the MLI Principal Purpose Test; CFC attribution rules may tax shifted passive profits in the SA resident's hands.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company risk personal liability for the adjusted tax and penalties; criminal liability under s235 where avoidance is tainted with fraud; disqualification and delinquency orders under the Companies Act; the separate legal personality of the company will be disregarded where the company is a mere alter ego, and SARS may pursue the parent entity directly.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the price to the arm's length price (s31 ITA), disallows the excessive deduction, and may make a corresponding secondary adjustment (deemed dividend or loan); understatement penalties of 100% (no reasonable grounds) to 150% (gross negligence) or 200% (intentional evasion) apply; interest runs from the original due date; TP documentation failure is a separate offence.

Director personal liability: The public officer and directors responsible for preparing and signing TP disclosures face personal liability for penalties; failure to appoint a public officer (s121 TAA) or to maintain contemporaneous records is an offence; directors who knowingly authorise false TP positions may be prosecuted for fraud and held personally liable under s77 TAA.
Dividends Tax / Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The company remains personally liable for the unwithheld tax (s64J ITA for dividends; s49B and s50B for royalties and interest); 10% percentage-based penalty for late payment (s213 TAA); understatement penalties up to 200%; SARS may recover the unremitted amounts by collection measures, and the payments are treated as trust money.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or allow the non-withholding of trust taxes are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted amounts plus interest and penalties; travel bans and asset seizure are available; criminal prosecution for theft of trust money or fraud may follow (up to 15 years' imprisonment).
