Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling portal: https://www.sars.gov.za / https://efiling.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Following incorporation, the company registers as an income tax taxpayer through SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch, submitting the registration form (e.g., IT77 for companies), valid certificate of incorporation, Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of registered office/physical address, and banking details. SARS issues the income tax reference number. Separate VAT registration (form VAT 101) becomes mandatory if taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months exceed ZAR 1,000,000, or for non-resident electronic services suppliers if supplies to South African customers exceed ZAR 50,000 per annum. PAYE, SDL, and UIF registration are required when employing staff. Non-resident ownership is permitted without special tax clearance, but directors and beneficial owners must provide identification and may require individual tax numbers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident (Pty) Ltd taxed on worldwide income; no pass-through or check-the-box election available for standard companies)

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box system. Promptly available statutory elections include: (i) export services zero-rating election under VAT Act section 11(2)(l); (ii) hybrid debt/equity recharacterisation elections under Income Tax Act sections 8E and 8EA; (iii) asset-for-share / share-for-share rollover elections under sections 42 and 45; (iv) VAT grouping election (section 50) for connected vendors; and (v) binding private rulings for hybrid mismatch adjustments under sections 23M/23N.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory threshold where taxable supplies exceed this amount in the preceding 12 months; voluntary registration threshold of ZAR 50,000 (provided the vendor satisfies prescribed conditions). Non-resident suppliers of electronic services are liable for VAT registration when South African consumer supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where they are supplied to a non-resident under a sale and are exported from South Africa within the prescribed period (normally 30 days for air/road, 60 days for sea, or longer as allowed by customs) and documentary proof of export is retained. Services are zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident and the services are not directly in connection with land, movable property, or services performed in South Africa (section 11(2)(l)); services physically performed abroad qualify under section 11(2)(k). The supplier must be able to substantiate the transport or delivery from the Republic and receipt of payment from a foreign customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading (original or endorsed copy)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank proof of export proceeds received)

South Africa currently has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime for standard goods. SARS accepts electronic invoices in PDF or machine-readable XML format, provided they satisfy section 20 of the VAT Act (supplier details, VAT number, invoice date, description, price excluding/including VAT, and VAT amount). VAT returns (VAT 201) and customs declarations (SAD500) are submitted electronically through eFiling or the Modernized Customs environment, which supports API integration. SARS uses third-party data matching and risk engines to audit invoices, but does not require live fiscal stamping.

SARS may raise additional assessments for incorrectly zero-rated exports, claw back input tax credits previously claimed, and impose understatement penalties ranging from 10% (substantial understatement without reasonable care) up to 150% (intentional evasion) under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, plus interest at the prescribed SARB rate. Fraudulent export documentation may also result in criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act and the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964, with fines or imprisonment up to 10 years. SARS routinely audits export claims by cross-matching customs export declarations, bank proof of receipt, and VAT returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible on the current account, including trade in goods and services, interest, dividends, and royalties. Capital account flows remain subject to exchange control permissions administered by FinSurv and Authorised Dealers. Profit remittances are permissible but require evidenced economic substance, proof of the underlying dividend approval by the board, compliance with the Companies Act solvency-and-liquidity test, SARS tax clearance, and careful routing through an authorised dealer to avoid delays under anti-money laundering and financial sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment. There is no statutory mandatory conversion percentage; the exporter may retain the full amount in a foreign currency account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer, subject to FinSurv reporting and permissible use. If retained in the FCA, proceeds may be used for approved current account transactions or converted to rand at market rates; conversion of FCA funds for domestic purposes is unrestricted, but all export documentation, including bills of lading and customs invoices, must be retained for audit by FinSurv and SARB.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for cross-border FX and payment gateway aggregation, but Low feasibility for domestic ZAR clearing. Offshore digital EMIs must generally partner with a South African Authorised Dealer or participate through a correspondent banking arrangement because they cannot directly access the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) and Acquirer Clearing Management System. Legal constraints include FIC Act registration requirements, prohibited unauthorised deposit-taking under the Banks Act, and exchange control limits on holding ZAR balances without a local licensed presence.

Non-submission for more than 12 months may trigger additional administrative penalties and criminal prosecution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation (Local File and Master File) is required where the aggregate value of connected-person transactions exceeds ZAR 5 million for goods sales and purchases, ZAR 1 million for services, and ZAR 10 million for financial transactions (interest, guarantees, etc.) in a tax year. SARS may require full documentation irrespective of these thresholds where the transaction lacks commercial substance.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE or BVI, but enforces substance principles through: (1) the general anti-avoidance rule (section 103 of the Income Tax Act), (2) the controlled foreign company (CFC) rules (section 9D) requiring substantive business establishment, and (3) the 'substance over form' doctrine. A lack of economic substance in a Pty Ltd, such as using a shell entity solely to obtain treaty benefits or to shift profits, will be disregarded for tax purposes and may trigger recharacterization or penal adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act (s235 and s236), a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to ZAR 500,000 or 10 years imprisonment, plus 200% of the unpaid tax as an understatement penalty. Civil interest and compound interest also accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid tax under s161 (fiduciary liability) and for 'habitual recklessness' under s22 of the Companies Act. Practices: civil restitution of dissipated company assets, travel bans (non-resident directors), and criminal imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the legal substance under section 103(1) and recharacterize the entity as a conduit. Penalties: 100%–200% of the tax attributable to the recharacterization, plus interest. The entity may lose preferential tax exemptions and treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allowed the company to be used as a shell can be made jointly and severally liable for the primary tax debt (s161). Additional claims under the Companies Act for reckless trading (s77) include personal responsibility for the entity's contractual debts.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income under section 31 and impose a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax under section 222, plus interest. A 10%–20% additional 'non-contemporaneous documentation' penalty may apply if no valid TP file exists.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved inaccurate TP documentation without reasonable care may be liable for negligence penalties. In egregious cases, they may face prosecution under criminal tax evasion provisions.
Dividend Tax / WHT Avoidance

Statutory sanction: The company is responsible for withholding and remitting dividends tax. Failure results in a 20% assessment against the company, plus a 5% monthly penalty and interest. Under the Tax Administration Act, the company's directors may be assessed personally for the unpaid WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors are deemed 'tax representatives' and may be jointly and severally liable for the entity's withholding tax obligations if they failed to exercise reasonable care (s161). A civil travel ban can be issued to enforce collection.
