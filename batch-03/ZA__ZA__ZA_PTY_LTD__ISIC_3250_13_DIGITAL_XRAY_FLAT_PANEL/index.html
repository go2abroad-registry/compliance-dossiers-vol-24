Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za and main portal at https://www.sars.gov.za.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the (Pty) Ltd by the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), tax registration is initiated with SARS. Foreign-owned private companies register for income tax and VAT through SARS eFiling’s 'SARS for Business' facility. The application requires the certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation (MOI), audited or independent-reviewed financials where applicable, director ID documents or valid passports for non-resident directors, registered office details, and the shareholding structure evidencing foreign ownership. SARS issues an income tax reference number (tax TIN) upon verification; VAT registration is separately applied for on form VAT101. Non-resident shareholders do not require a special tax clearance to invest, but a resident director is required under the Companies Act. Where the entity is a resident company, it is automatically treated as a South African tax resident; a foreign-incorporated entity may be taxed as resident if effective management and control is in South Africa.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (private company formed under South African Companies Act is a separate taxable person; taxed on worldwide income at the corporate rate of 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through to shareholders; territorial scope is worldwide for residents; no general tax-exempt-zone status automatically applied except within designated SEZs only with applicable approvals).

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a 'check-the-box' election; entity classification follows the income tax statute and company law. Available lawful elections include: Small Business Corporation (SBC) status under s.12E (eligibility-based election), micro-business turnover tax election under s.48-48M of the Income Tax Act for qualifying small enterprises, VAT group registration election under s.50 of the VAT Act, and certain group relief elections under s.45 (intragroup transfers) and s.47 (asset-for-share transactions). Hybrid mismatch rules under ss.23M-23T of the Income Tax Act are mandatory and do not require an elective mechanism; matched-effect adjustments are applied prospectively. No optional hybrid classification election exists for a standard (Pty) Ltd other than making a section 9D attribution election where relevant to controlled foreign companies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is semi-convertible. Current-account transactions (trade, dividends, royalties, service fees) are freely convertible and remittable through an Authorised Dealer (AD) once FIC/FICA documentation and the underlying transaction have been verified; there is no absolute convertibility guarantee. Capital-account inflows in the form of foreign direct investment into a (Pty) Ltd require no prior FinSurv approval, but funds must be received directly from the non-resident shareholder through an AD and reported on the daily Balance of Payments (BOP) returns. Profit remittance is executed as declared dividends subject to 20% Dividend Withholding Tax and the AD's verification of available cash resources; interest, royalty and branch remittances may attract additional withholding taxes and SARS documentation requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory receipt window: 180 days from shipment, extendable on written application to FinSurv. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% since 1 May 2021, when the forced surrender/conversion of export proceeds was abolished. All export receipts must still be routed through a South African Authorised Dealer for disposal, but may be retained indefinitely in a Foreign Currency Account or converted to ZAR at the exporter's choice. Late receipt or non-receipt creates an overdue export that must be reported to FinSurv with a complete evidence file and may attract exchange control penalties and disallowances.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Exchange Control Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) for currency controls and the National Payment System Department (NPSD) for the licensing and oversight of payment, clearing and settlement systems; AML/CFT supervision is exercised by the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for collections, cross-border invoicing facilitation and ancillary payment services; Low for capital escrow, liquidity concentration or FX conversion. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Currencycloud, Airwallex) are not Authorised Dealers under the Banks Act 1990 or the Currency and Exchanges Act 1933. In South Africa, e-money institutions and payment service providers must be registered under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 and settle ZAR through an AD. An EMI cannot perform SARB BOP reporting, Deeds of Undertaking, inward-loan registration or trade settlement; routing export proceeds through an EMI risks breaching the 180-day route-to-AD requirement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident parent's bank sends EUR/USD via SWIFT MT103 to the AD's nostro account, quoting beneficiary ZA_PTY_LTD, the designated Capital Escrow account number, the DA185 inward BOP purpose code 'Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Capital' and the AD's inward investment reference. The AD validates FICA/KYC, secures the signed Exchange Control Deed of Undertaking, credits the Capital Escrow or FCA, converts to ZAR at spot rate where required, and confirms share allotment within 30 days while submitting daily BOP returns to FinSurv. No prior SARB approval is required for a direct equity injection; any subsequent shareholder loan must be registered/reported through the AD on the foreign loan (NLR) system, and third-party payments or indirect funding routes are impermissible.Imports of scintillator materials, a-Si TFT sensors, ASICs and packaging are settled via: (i) irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued as SWIFT MT700 on 30-60 day sight or term tenor; (ii) open-account SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers supported by supplier pro-forma invoices, Bills of Lading/AWBs and the SAD500 customs entry, with the payment matched to the import declaration; and (iii) red-clause advances under the LC for pre-shipment vendor financing. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% applicable to years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022 (s 5(1) Income Tax Act 58 of 1962)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax under s 64EA-64G Income Tax Act, withheld and paid by the SA company as the withholding agent; SA-resident corporate shareholders qualify for exemption; non-resident shareholders are subject to 20% unless reduced under an applicable Double Taxation Agreement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

ITR14 due no later than 12 months after the company's financial year-end (SARS-prescribed date). IRP6: first provisional payment within 6 months of year-end; second payment by the last business day of the year of assessment; third top-up payment within 6 months after year-end. SARS may assess or raise estimated assessments where the return is not filed.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Section A/B - Entity Registration Company registration number, SARS tax reference number, public officer details, financial year-end, contact particulars. Non-resident corporate shareholders must obtain an SA tax reference number and appoint an SA-resident public officer (s 246 TAA) before filing; the public officer is the addressee of all SARS correspondence. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory ZAR threshold for Local File or Master File; connected cross-border controlled transactions of any value must satisfy the arm's-length standard under s 31, and the Local File/Master File must be prepared and supplied to SARS on request. The statutory Country-by-Country Report obligation triggers at consolidated group revenue of ZAR 10,000,000,000 or more. SARS risk-based audit materiality commonly centers on per-transaction values of ZAR 5,000,000-ZAR 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute. Economic substance is enforced through: s 9D Controlled Foreign Company attribution rules; the general anti-avoidance rules (s 80A-80L); transfer pricing substance-over-form analysis; and the GloBE (Pillar Two) domestic minimum top-up tax of 15% for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million, legislated with effect for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2024, including a substance-based income exclusion and a CbCR notification requirement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration (Income Tax Act s 104; Tax Administration Act ss 234-237 and s 210/223)

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 25%-150% of the tax shortfall under s 210/223 TAA, plus prescribed-rate interest; criminal prosecution under s 104 of the Income Tax Act: a fine up to ZAR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years; asset forfeiture under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act; public naming and reputational censure by SARS.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or authorise false returns face individual criminal prosecution and civil restitution claims for the shortfall, penalties and interest; the corporate veil may be pierced under s 20(9) of the Companies Act where the company is used as an unconscionable façade; SARS may obtain a court-ordered travel ban and tax-clearance revocation under s 56 TAA.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion (Income Tax Act s 31; TAA s 210/223)

Statutory sanction: SARS re-determines the arm's-length consideration and imposes a primary adjustment to taxable income; excessive deductions are disallowed; interest runs at the prescribed rate; an understatement penalty of up to 150% applies for grossly negligent or intentional mispricing; s 9D CFC rules may attribute the foreign profit back to South African shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors and the public officer who certify fabricated transfer pricing documentation face perjury and fraud charges; courts may pierce the corporate veil to recover from controlling persons; SARS may apply for a travel ban; civil recovery of dividends paid out of shifted profits may be sought from directors who approved the distribution.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse (Companies Act s 20(9); Income Tax Act s 9D, s 80A-80L; TAA ss 156-160)

Statutory sanction: SARS and the courts may disregard the corporate form and treat the arrangement as a sham; income is re-attributed to the beneficial owners; deductions are fully disallowed with prescribed-rate interest and an understatement penalty up to 150%; CFC rules attribute passive income; tax clearance may be revoked and the arrangement referred for criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are personally liable for the full tax shortfall where the veil is pierced; civil asset-tracing and restitution orders may be made; a court-ordered travel ban and passport surrender may follow under s 56 TAA; directors face disqualification from office and potential criminal liability where the sham was knowingly created or maintained.
AML and Sanctions Violation (Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001; Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998; TAA ss 234-237)

Statutory sanction: FIC administrative penalties up to ZAR 10,000,000 per breach, or up to 10% of annual turnover for repeated breaches; criminal money-laundering conviction under POCA carries imprisonment up to 15 years; freezing and forfeiture of proceeds; SARS may deny expense deductions and impose tax adjustments on the concealed income; trade, import and export licences may be suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors, the compliance officer and the company's Money Laundering Reporting Officer are separately chargeable for FICA breaches; personal asset confiscation may follow under POCA civil recovery; travel bans and passport seizure may be ordered; the corporate veil is lifted where personal benefit is proven; directors may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid FIC fines.
