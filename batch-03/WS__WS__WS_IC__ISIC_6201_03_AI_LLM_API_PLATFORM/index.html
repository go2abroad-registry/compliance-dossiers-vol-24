Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), Apia, Samoa — applications are lodged exclusively through a SIFA-licensed Registered Agent under the supervision of the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA).

Governing statute

International Companies Act 1987 (Samoa), as amended by the International Companies (Amendment) Act 2015, read as a whole — incorporating the statutory provisions on application for incorporation, name restrictions, memorandum and articles, share capital, registered office, directors and secretary; supplemented by the International Companies (Fees) Regulations 2004 and the International Companies (Registered Agents) Regulations 1992.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 — Application for Incorporation of an International Company, accompanied by the executed Memorandum and Articles of Association and the registered agent's consent notice.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or bank-deposit requirement. The subscriber may issue one fully paid Ordinary Share of no par value with stated consideration of US$1.00; authorized share capital is set in the Memorandum (commonly US$1,000,000) and paid-up amount is left to subscriber discretion.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable — there is no physical workplace in Samoa to certify. The online-only configuration imposes no civil-defense obligations. If a future Apia office is opened, the following become mandatory before occupancy: fire-safety certificate under the Fire and Emergency Services Act 2006, structural-load and building-permit clearance under the Samoa Building Code 2012, and occupational-safety certification under the Occupational Safety and Health Act 1986 (as amended 2018).

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — exempt. A Samoa International Company engaged in ISIC 6201 generative-AI/LLM API services is an offshore digital service with no manufacturing plant or physical facility, and therefore falls outside the MCIL industrial/Business Licensing framework.

Category: None required — Samoa imposes no dedicated 'cloud-service provider', 'data-center' or 'AI-platform' operating permit; as an International Company, the entity is deemed to carry on business wholly outside Samoa and is exempt from domestic occupation-licensing requirements.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Apia, Samoa — statutory tax administration under the Tax Administration Act 2012; official portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws (MfR e-Services). The International Companies Act 1987 is administered by the International Companies Registry, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), which issues the IC Certificate of Incorporation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A licensed Samoa registered agent incorporates the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 and obtains the Certificate of Incorporation from the MCIL International Companies Registry. Step 2: The agent submits a TIN application (MfR TIN form) via MfR e-Services, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, the agent's licence, and director/shareholder identification. Step 3: MfR issues the Samoa Tax Identification Number (TIN) within 3–5 business days; because the IC is statutorily tax-exempt, no income tax or VAGST registration is required or effected. Step 4: For bank account opening and corporate counterparty verification, the IC furnishes the TIN, Certificate of Incorporation, and a registered-agent certificate confirming its exempt offshore status.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Samoan law. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 120,000 (Samoa Tala) of taxable supplies in any 12 consecutive months (VAGST Act 2012, s. 11). The exempt IC is outside the scope of VAGST registration at any turnover level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAGST Act 2012, exported services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply — including SaaS and LLM/AI API token-metered services — qualify for 0% zero-rating. However, the Samoa IC is statutorily exempt from VAGST on all offshore income; consequently, its subscription and usage-token revenue (whether settled in USD or USDC) is subject to a 0% effective rate with no requirement to register, charge, file, or recover VAGST.

  • Customs Export Declaration — inapplicable to intangible AI-API services; operative export evidence is the executed SaaS/API licence agreement between the IC and the non-resident customer
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill — not issued for digital services; replaced by the IC's token-usage report or metered-billing ledger documenting services rendered to offshore counterparties
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/Stripe settlement statement — issued by a Samoa licensed commercial bank or payment processor, evidencing receipt of foreign currency (USD/USDC) from non-resident clients

None. Samoa imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing obligation. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices containing the particulars prescribed by the VAGST Act 2012 (s. 87) and file monthly returns through MfR e-Services; the IC has no invoicing or filing obligations for offshore AI-API revenue.

MfR may re-open assessments and recover unwarranted refunds or tax within the statutory amendment period (generally 5 years; extended in cases of fraud). Export zero-rating or exemption obtained through false declarations is penalized under the Tax Administration Act 2012 by culpability-tiered shortfall penalties (up to 150% of the tax shortfall), statutory interest, criminal fines, and imprisonment for responsible officers. An IC that trades with Samoan residents or otherwise breaches the non-resident conditions of the International Companies Act 1987 forfeits its exemption and becomes retrospectively exposed to VAGST, income tax, and penalties.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tālā (WST) is not fully convertible on a capital-account basis. Current-account payments, including remittances for services and profits, generally may proceed through authorized dealers if supported by evidence of the underlying commercial transaction and tax clearance. Capital-account flows, including inbound equity investment and outbound repatriation of capital, are subject to CBS exchange-control approval under the Exchange Control Act 1971 and the Financial Institutions Regulations. Profit remittance requires documented distributable profits, audited financial statements and proof of settlement of all Samoan tax liabilities. Non-resident International Companies carrying on exclusively offshore business enjoy exchange-control exemptions that extend to their offshore accounts, but any on-island local expenditure must be made through licensed dealers. Cryptocurrency-based remittances are prohibited for WST conversion unless the receipt has been validated through a CBS-regulated fiat on-ramp and the purpose code and source-of-funds documentation are preserved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBS exchange-control practice, export proceeds from Samoan-origin goods must be repatriated to Samoa within 90 days of the date of shipment and must be credited to an exchange-control approved foreign-currency account held with a licensed commercial bank. Goods exports are subject to a mandatory surrender ratio of 100% of proceeds into WST unless an exemption has been granted for debt service, insured reinvoicing or capital goods replacement. Services and digital exports, including AI/LLM API token sales, are not subject to hard surrender percentages; however, all proceeds must be repatriated and accounted for within 180 days. Exporters must register with CBS and file exchange-control forms documenting invoice value, collection details, and any balances retained offshore. Failure to surrender within the statutory timeline may result in a 20% penalty assessment and suspension of FX purchase facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Samoa does not issue electronic money institution (EMI) licences to non-bank fintech entities. Foreign digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex or Stripe Treasury may be used solely as payment aggregation rails, but their balances cannot be treated as Samoa-regulated deposits and they are not permitted to open CBS credit-clearing accounts. A licensed Samoa commercial bank is required as the principal clearing counterparty and the ultimate custody bank for all WST-denominated and foreign-currency corporate funds. The use of an offshore EMI as the sole treasury rail is not viable because of legal uncertainty regarding sanctity of funds, FIN-NET remittance compliance and CBS anti-money-laundering oversight.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital and seed funding must be directed through the Bank of South Pacific (Samoa) Ltd or Samoa Commercial Bank Ltd using a pre-advised SWIFT MT103 wire. The remittance instruction must clearly cite the entity's International Company number and the phrase 'Subscription for Ordinary Shares — WS_IC'. CBS does not mandate a specific purpose code, but banks require a standard economic purpose such as 'Capital injection for technology operations'; the bearer must submit a board resolution, share subscription agreement, source-of-funds affidavit, passport copies of subscribing shareholders, and a beneficial ownership declaration. For regulated jurisdictions, the sending bank must be pre-approved under the receiving bank's clearing limits. Crypto-backed inbound injections are not accepted at the banking layer and must be converted through a CBS-compliant FSP before settlement.Outbound operating disbursements must be routed via SWIFT MT103 for amounts below USD 100,000 and SWIFT MT103 plus MT202 cover for larger transfers. Recurring supplier and trade settlements may use Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit opened by the Samoa clearing bank under UCP600, with the IC named as applicant and the overseas service provider as beneficiary. For low-value token disbursements to software vendors, SWIFT MT103 with reference to the invoiced contract and proof of digital-service consumption is permitted. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for an International Company (IC) under s.86 International Companies Act 1987, which grants total exemption from Samoan income tax; the domestic Samoan corporate headline rate is 27% but is inapplicable to an IC.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Samoa imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions made by an International Company; dividends may be paid freely without Samoan tax or exchange control approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A (0 WST) for International Companies: the s.86 exemption removes all income tax and transfer pricing filing obligations. If accidental domestic tax-resident activity arises, Samoa's Income Tax Act 2012 arm's-length rules apply to related-party cross-border transactions without a codified monetary threshold, and Master File / Local File duties attach only where a Samoan domestic resident entity is present in the structure.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations (effective 1 January 2022) apply to International Companies. A generative AI / LLM API platform is treated as IP-relevant business; the core income generating activities (strategic technology direction, contract negotiation, AI model deployment control, cost and treasury management) must be performed in Samoa with adequate qualified staff physically present, suitable premises, and local operating expenditure proportionate to revenue. An annual ESR notification is filed via the registered agent with SIFSA. Non-compliance triggers SIFSA administrative penalties, deregistration, and criminal liability for knowingly false ESR declarations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Sham Substance / ESR Evasion

Statutory sanction: SIFSA administrative penalties per breach, deregistration, and revocation of the s.86 tax exemption; a deregistered IC may not invoice customers, and ownership of its receivables, Stripe reserve, and crypto treasury is statutorily defective.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false ESR declarations face criminal prosecution for false statements, fines in penalty units, and imprisonment; courts may disregard the corporate veil and hold directors jointly liable for unpaid creditors and for assisting tax fraud in customer-market jurisdictions.
Money Laundering / AML-CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines and sanctions under Samoa's AML/CFT Act 2018 against the IC and its registered agent for failure to maintain AML programs, keep records, and file suspicious transaction reports; assets involved are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew or were wilfully blind to laundering face imprisonment of up to 14 years; travel bans, asset-freezing orders, and civil restitution to victims are also available.
Facilitation of Foreign Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Samoa imposes no tax on the IC, but the shareholder's home country may recharacterize the IC as a sham, impose civil tax-fraud penalties of 50%-150% of the tax, and obtain financial information under Samoa's TIEAs; foreign courts may enforce restitution against the IC's assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who organized the concealment are exposed to foreign civil restitution judgments, travel restrictions, extradition risk, and imprisonment for tax fraud; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud, and Samoan courts will not enforce the IC's exemption as a shield against foreign criminal process.
Chronic Non-Renewal and Striking Off

Statutory sanction: 10% surcharge on arrears, deregistration, loss of legal capacity to contract, and vesting of the IC's bank balances, Stripe reserve, and crypto holdings in the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the IC's obligations incurred after the default date; striking off does not discharge directors; restoration requires payment of all arrears, penalties, and court costs, and a deregistered IC cannot sue to collect unpaid AI platform receivables.
