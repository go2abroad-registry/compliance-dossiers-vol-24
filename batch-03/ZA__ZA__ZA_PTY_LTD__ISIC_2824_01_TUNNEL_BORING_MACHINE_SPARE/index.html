Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); national tax administration and eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; single competent authority for income tax, VAT, customs, excise, and transfer duty.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity deriving South African-source income or engaging in taxable activities must obtain an Income Tax Reference Number by registering through SARS eFiling or by submitting Form IT77 (Application for Registration as a Taxpayer) at a SARS branch, with a duly appointed public officer or tax representative for the (Pty) Ltd. VAT registration is effected on Form VAT101 (Application for Registration as a Vendor). Foreign non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in South Africa, including electronic services, must register for VAT where the statutory thresholds are exceeded. SARS issues a tax registration number (income tax reference, VAT vendor number, PAYE reference) upon successful registration, which is used for returns, assessments, and eFiling interactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-incorporated entity deriving South African-source income or engaging in taxable activities must obtain an Income Tax Reference Number by registering through SARS eFiling or by submitting Form IT77 (Application for Registration as a Taxpayer) at a SARS branch, with a duly appointed public officer or tax representative for the (Pty) Ltd. VAT registration is effected on Form VAT101 (Application for Registration as a Vendor). Foreign non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in South Africa, including electronic services, must register for VAT where the statutory thresholds are exceeded. SARS issues a tax registration number (income tax reference, VAT vendor number, PAYE reference) upon successful registration, which is used for returns, assessments, and eFiling interactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a check-the-box entity-classification election; a (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a separate resident legal person at the corporate income tax rate. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) controlled foreign company ('CFC') attribution rules under section 9D of the Income Tax Act, with possible application of the high-tax exemption; (ii) permanent establishment reliance and double tax treaty relief elections; (iii) hybrid mismatch adjustments under Part IIA of the Income Tax Act (OECD BEPS Action 2), which apply automatically without elective check-the-box treatment; (iv) corporate restructuring relief under section 45, including asset-for-share transactions; and (v) small business corporation graduated rates where the entity meets section 12E qualifying conditions. No pass-through election is available for a (Pty) Ltd.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration available where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period. Foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African residents must register for VAT when the consideration for such supplies exceeds ZAR 50,000 in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods, including tunnel boring machine spare parts, may be zero-rated where supplied to a non-resident under an export contract, provided: (i) the goods are supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply; (ii) the goods are physically exported to a destination outside South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply, or within such extended period as SARS may allow for capital equipment; (iii) the vendor retains adequate proof of export (customs declaration, bill of lading, transport documentation) and proof of payment; and (iv) the goods are not used or consumed in South Africa. Services exported to non-residents may qualify under the 'exported services' provisions of section 11(2)(l) where the services are not performed directly for a South African taxable activity and are not connected to South African immovable or movable property. The vendor must hold a valid VAT registration number and claim input VAT strictly in accordance with section 16.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / SARS Customs export processed proof)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment confirmation
  • Commercial invoice and export order or contract
  • Packing list and transport/container tracking documentation
  • Proof of export date and port/terminal release documentation
  • SARS customs clearance or electronic transactional export declaration

South Africa does not currently require mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction controls for e-invoicing; however, SARS accepts e-invoicing provided the output tax document satisfies section 20 of the VAT Act. SARS has introduced modern e-invoicing/API-based validation, VAT return eFiling, and automated risk assessments, but there is no government pre-clearance stamping requirement before tax invoice issuance. Export transactions require customs declaration and processing through SARS Customs, with export documentation retained as the fiscal record.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims are invalidated and the export supply is reclassified as standard-rated, with output tax assessed at 15% from the date of supply. Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties of up to 200% may be imposed for intentional, grossly negligent, or reckless conduct; interest is charged on unpaid or clawed-back taxes. SARS may issue additional assessments, raise jeopardy assessments, and attach assets to recover refunds. Criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment under the Tax Administration Act. For exchange control, failure to timeously receive and remit export proceeds to South Africa within 180 days (without SARS/South African Reserve Bank extension) may trigger exchange-control penalties and forfeiture or monetary sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is domestically convertible but operates under a managed-float regime with restrictive capital account controls. Current account flows such as trade receipts, service fees, interest, and genuine profit remittances are generally permitted through Authorised Dealers, subject to documentary substantiation and tax clearance. Capital account inflows (foreign equity investment, shareholder loans) are permissible, but each transaction must be reported to SARB via a BOP form and a 'Notice of Non-Resident Investment' must be issued by the receiving bank. Outbound capital repatriation (dividend, liquidation proceeds, capital disposal) requires verification of after-tax funding, audited or reviewed financial statements, and payment of tax liabilities. Non-resident sellers of unlisted shares must obtain a SARB approval, and the sale proceeds cannot be remitted without an exemption or AD-generated permission code. Consequently, although the rand is not physically pegged, full full current and capital account convertibility is not recognised; each flow is tested against FICA, exchange control, and tax obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Exchange Control Rules, all export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment, and the full 100% of the export invoice value must be either sold to an Authorised Dealer in the foreign exchange market or retained in a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with the exporter's AD. Retention in an FCA requires the bank to assess the projected use of those funds, and any conversion into rand is at the prevailing market rate. There is no partial surrender ratio; the mandate is full receipt and lawful disposition. The exporter must obtain a valid export permit or customs entry, keep the GenBank/settlement report updated, and promptly notify the AD of any MTA or extension permitted under the rules. If the 180-day period is exceeded or the proceeds are diverted through an offshore party without approval, the exporter is in contravention of the Currency and Exchanges Act and may face administrative penalties, loss of AD facilities, and criminal sanctions.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department as the delegated exchange control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act No. 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations of 1961, with most daily functions devolved to Authorised Dealers (commercial banks) and Authorised Foreign Exchange Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Conditional / Medium-Low. A purely offshore digital EMI cannot open a local rand-denominated account or obtain Authorised Dealer status under the South African Reserve Bank's exchange control framework. Such EMIs may serve only as a front-end payment instruction platform that routes via one or more SARB-licensed banks. All cross-border SWIFT and FX conversion must be released through a local Authorised Dealer. The bank must be able to identify the EMI as a mere payment channel, not the counterparty, so funds can be reconciled to the South African corporate entity. Legal constraints: the Financial Intelligence Centre Act and Exchange Control Regulations require a South African bank to be the clear intermediary. Using an unlicensed EMI as an internal treasury-sweeping vehicle, or allowing it to interface directly with the SARB reporting system, is prohibited and may cause the stoppage of outbound remittances, de-banking, and FinSurv penalties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign equity or shareholder-loop capital must comply with SARB's capital flow reporting. The receiving company's Authorised Dealer must issue a BOP form for inward FDI (new loan or equity), and the company must produce a board resolution, share subscription agreement, certificate of incorporation, and beneficial ownership schedules before the remittance is accepted. The wire should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank) with a beneficiary account in the company's South African bank and a payment reference clearly stating 'FOREIGN DIRECT INVESTMENT – EQUITY' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' and the company's CIPC number. The Authorised Dealer will assign a SARB Non-Resident Investment (NRI) code to record the capital. No specific pre-approval is required for ordinary share investments below certain thresholds, but the bank's AML unit must verify the funds against the source-of-funds statements and sanctions lists before releasing credit. Where the investment is structured through a newly incorporated (Pty) Ltd, the capital can be credited to a Capital Escrow Account and transferred to the operational account only after registration certification and tax identification have been produced.Payments for imports, subcontracts, royalties, and overseas distributors are executed through the company's Authorised Dealer using SWIFT MT103 for single cross-border credits and MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers in larger treasury operations. Where the company is importing critical components, it may instruct a commercial LC (advising or issuing side) under UCP600; the bank will issue an MT700/MT710 and price the LC in USD, EUR, or another convertible currency. For export financing, the company presents an export LC to the negotiating bank, and on compliance the bank will credit the FCA, then prepare a Forward Exchange Contracts (FEC) through an Authorised Dealer for documented trade/receivable or payable exposureNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) in offshore USD/ZAR markets, permitted only where executed by or through a South African Authorised Dealer and strictly linked to an underlying current-account trade exposureCurrency swaps between ZAR and USD/EUR for project-level cash-flow hedging under an approved corporate treasury policyEuropean-style OTC FX options (plain-vanilla puts and calls) on ZAR pairs, used for protection against adverse exchange-rate movements on verified trade or debt-service flowsCross-currency interest rate swaps where the company has foreign-currency trade obligations / receivables and an approved banking facility with the Authorised Dealer

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South Africa does not prescribe a fixed monetary threshold for preparing a Local File or Master File. All affected cross-border related-party transactions must be supported by contemporaneous TP documentation. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of ZAR 10 billion or more; a CbCR notification must be filed with SARS.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime as found in certain offshore financial centres. However, economic substance is enforced through the Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules under s9D, transfer pricing rules under s31, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under s80A-L of the Income Tax Act. A South African Pty Ltd with foreign shareholders and no actual commercial substance may be treated as a conduit; SARS may recharacterise income, disallow interest deductions, attribute passive income to foreign CFC shareholders, and disregard the corporate personality under anti-abuse doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall under s222 TAA plus interest; late payment penalty of 10% of unpaid tax; criminal prosecution under s235 TAA with a fine and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; SARS may also impose a civil restitution order for the full tax loss.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in fraudulent under-declaration is jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt via piercing of the corporate veil; SARS may issue a travel ban under s184 TAA; directors face criminal conviction and imprisonment for aiding tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income under s31 of the Income Tax Act, deny deductions, and treat the adjustment as a corrective payment. A secondary adjustment may be imposed, deeming a loan and creating taxable interest; combined with an understatement penalty of up to 150% and daily interest, total liability can exceed the original tax charge.

Director personal liability: Directors executing non-arm's length intercompany transactions in violation of fiduciary duty may be sued for civil restitution; if the transactions serve a tax evasion purpose, directors become personally exposed to criminal sanction, travel restraints, and disqualification from board service.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke GAAR (s80A-L) to disregard the entity or recharacterise transactions; CFC rules may attribute its profits to the foreign parent; treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test or Limitation on Benefits clause; administrative penalties and interest accrue for each tax year.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who allow the shell arrangement are personally liable for civil damages to SARS and any defrauded creditors; they may face a travel ban, asset freeze, and criminal prosecution for abetting the shamming of corporate substance.
AML / Money Laundering / Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FIC Act), a company may be fined up to ZAR 10 million and directors may be imprisoned for up to 15 years for money laundering; SARS may impose additional administrative penalties for failure to keep records; the company's bank accounts may be frozen and business permits cancelled.

Director personal liability: Directors as accountable persons are personally liable for FIC Act fines and can be disqualified from managing companies; travel bans may be ordered during investigation; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and permanent loss of clean legal status.
