Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa applies a mandatory entity-level classification regime with no check-the-box or elective hybrid-mismatch election; hybrid mismatch rules under ss 23M and 23N of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 apply automatically. Elective mechanisms available to the entity include: (i) Headquarter Company election under s 9I of the Income Tax Act; (ii) VAT group registration election for affiliated South African-resident companies under ss 50-52 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991; (iii) VAT accounting basis election between the invoice basis and the payments basis under s 15 of the VAT Act; and (iv) the s 24C prepaid-expenditure allowance election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000: compulsory VAT vendor registration arises at the end of any month when the aggregate value of taxable supplies made in the preceding 12-month period exceeds R1 million, or where there are reasonable grounds to believe it will exceed R1 million in the following 12 months. Voluntary registration is available from R50,000 of taxable supplies. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services must register for VAT from their first taxable supply regardless of turnover. Every exporting manufacturer must additionally hold a SARS Customs client code (customs code) irrespective of any VAT turnover test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act to a supply of movable goods — including port container gantry cranes and hoisting gear — where: (i) the supplier is a VAT vendor; (ii) the goods are sold and supplied for export; (iii) the goods are physically exported from the Republic within 90 days of the date of supply; and (iv) the vendor obtains and retains the prescribed documentary proof (export contract/commercial invoice, SAD500 customs export declaration, transport document, and payment evidence). Supplies of services to non-residents may qualify for zero-rating under s 11(2)(l) of the VAT Act. The zero rate is denied where goods are consumed or re-imported into South Africa or where export documentation is not obtained within the statutory period; late or non-export triggers a deemed standard-rated supply at 15%.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) with electronic customs release/reference number evidencing export clearance
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) showing carriage to a destination outside the Republic, issued in the vendor's or consignor's name
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Inward Transfer (FIT) notice, together with SARB Form F178, evidencing receipt and settlement of foreign export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin where required by the importing jurisdiction or letter of credit
  • Terminal receipts, weighbridge certificates, or port authority / transport-department export movement records for port-loaded container cargo

South Africa has not implemented mandatory real-time e-invoice clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or a tax-authority API for per-invoice validation. A VAT vendor must issue a valid tax invoice containing the 15 mandatory particulars under s 20(4) of the VAT Act, may issue electronic tax invoices, and must retain records for five years. VAT returns are filed electronically through SARS eFiling. For exports, SARS Customs requires an electronic SAD500 export declaration via the e-Declaration/EDI customs environment, customs release must be granted before goods depart the customs-controlled area, and shipments are subsequently risk-profiled and data-matched against shipping manifests, port movement records, and bank receipts.

SARS audits export zero-rating by reconciling the SAD500 export declaration, customs release data, shipping manifests, port exit records, transport documents, and the actual receipt of the foreign currency by an authorised dealer within 180 days of shipment. If the prescribed documentary proof is missing, false, or untraceable when originally submitted, the zero rating is reversed as a deemed taxable supply at 15%, with interest under s 24 of the VAT Act running from the due date of the relevant return. Administrative consequences under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 include understatement penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for deliberate non-disclosure, and fraudulent export schemes are criminally prosecuted as tax evasion with fines and imprisonment. The prescription window for SARS additional assessments is extended in cases of fraud, misrepresentation, or non-disclosure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million turnover/receipts or ZAR 5 million in cross-border related-party transactions. Master File threshold: ZAR 1 billion consolidated group turnover. SARS may require documentation below thresholds where audit risk assessed.

Economic substance scope: South Africa applies Economic Substance Requirements through the general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) under Section 80A-80L of the Income Tax Act, the ATAD-aligned controlled foreign company rules (Section 9D), and exchange control regulations requiring genuine operating substance for non-resident investment holding structures. Shell companies lacking commercial purpose, staff, premises, or local decision-making are exposed to deduction denial, deemed dividend treatment, and anti-avoidance assessments. Special focus on 'headquarter company' regime (Section 9I) which requires at least 50% shareholding, 80% foreign revenue, and substance criteria.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Section 235 of the Tax Administration Act: fine up to ZAR 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years. Civil understatement penalty up to 45% of shortfall. Full restitution of unpaid tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable. SARS may obtain civil judgment against personal assets. Travel bans and director disqualification apply. Imprisonment up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Deemed distributions, denial of assessed losses and deductions under Section 103(1) of the Income Tax Act; imposition of dividends tax at 20% on deemed distributions; GAAR assessments with 100% adjustment plus understatement penalties of up to 45%.

Director personal liability: SARS may pierce the corporate veil under Section 231 of the Tax Administration Act. Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes if company is a vehicle for tax evasion. Civil restraint orders and criminal sanctions on directors.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: SARS Commissioner may adjust consideration to arm's length, impose 15% withholding tax on deemed dividends or royalties, apply understatement penalties 5%-45%, and charge interest. Secondary adjustments under Section 8(1) tax the imputed loan or dividend.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign defective TP documentation knowing mispricing may face personal assessments under anti-avoidance jurisprudence; criminal liability for perjury if false declarations made to SARS.
AML / Exchange Control Violations

Statutory sanction: Exchange Control Regulations offences: fines up to ZAR 10 million per contravention and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. SARS and SARB joint enforcement. Assets may be forfeited.

Director personal liability: Directors executing illegal foreign payment schemes are personally liable. Travel bans (exit restriction orders) enforceable. Criminal record leads to director disqualification under Companies Act. Civil damages recoverable by affected parties.
Failure to File / Late Filing (Persistent)

Statutory sanction: Administrative non-compliance penalty: ZAR 250 to ZAR 6,000 per month per unfiled return, with cumulative escalation. Understatement penalty: 10%-45% of tax assessed. SARS may issue a jeopardy assessment and attach bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for penalties if SARS proves willful blindness; criminal penalty: fine and imprisonment up to 2 years (Tax Administration Act Section 234).
