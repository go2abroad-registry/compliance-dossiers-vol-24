Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a South African (Pty) Ltd. The MOI may create authorised shares with nominal value, and issued shares must be paid for in cash or in kind for adequate consideration under section 40(2). No escrow account is required by the Companies Act; however, subscriber funds may be held in a regulated escrow account pending CIPC registration and bank account opening.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling Portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Private Company (Pty) Ltd incorporated in South Africa must first register with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), then register for Income Tax, VAT, PAYE, UIF, and SDL through the SARS eFiling system or at a SARS branch. Required documents: Companies registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, authenticated identity/passport copies of directors, proof of registered address, and banking details. For customs purposes, the company must also obtain an importer/exporter code via the SARS Customs desk before conducting cross-border trade.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box classification; a (Pty) Ltd is automatically a taxable entity under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Available statutory elections relevant to this structure include: (i) VAT grouping under Section 50(1) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991; (ii) hybrid mismatch elections to align with OECD BEPS Action 2; (iii) assessed-loss ring-fencing elections; and (iv) the thin capitalization / interest restriction election under Section 23M/23N. There is no elective pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies over a 12-month period (compulsory); voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000. Non-resident vendors making taxable supplies in South Africa must register regardless of physical presence once the threshold is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from South Africa in a single transaction are zero-rated if the vendor supplies goods to a non-resident, and the goods are exported under customs control within 90 days of delivery, with proof of export retained. Services supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated if the recipient is outside South Africa, the services are not connected to South African immovable property, and the services are performed while the recipient is absent from South Africa. The plush toy manufacturer may claim zero rating only on actual exports, not local sales.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500)
  • Bill of Lading (Sea) / Airway Bill (Air) / Road Freight Manifest
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank forex receipt confirmation
  • Original commercial invoice with customs value
  • Transport document and customs release evidence

South Africa does not yet mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for VAT invoices. SARS accepts compliant electronic tax invoices satisfying Section 20 of the VAT Act and Tax Administration Act record-keeping rules. A government e-invoicing framework (PEPPOL-aligned) is planned but not yet enacted. Customs exports are cleared electronically through SARS Customs and require a SAD 500 declaration; no blockchain-based clearance exists.

SARS rigorously audits export zero-rating claims. If proof of export is not produced within 90 days, output VAT is payable plus interest (Rate prescribed under section 39 of the TAA) and a 10% penalty. Incorrect or fraudulent claims attract a 100% to 200% understatement penalty under Section 222/223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, criminal prosecution for tax evasion, and full clawback of refunds with retrospective VAT re-levied on the export. Exchange-control breaches where forex is not repatriated within 180 days are additionally reported to the SARB Financial Surveillance Department and may lead to administrative penalties or criminal sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is largely convertible on current account items, including trade, services, dividends, and interest payments, provided that the underlying transactions are documented through an authorized dealer. Capital account transactions, such as equity contributions, shareholder loans, and capital repatriation, remain controlled under the Exchange Control Regulations and require approval or post-transaction reporting to FinSurv. Profit remittances as dividends require an annual SARS tax clearance certificate and compliance with the authorized dealer's A2-documentation procedure. ZAR is not offshore-deliverable on a large scale, and non-resident investors should structure hedging and repatriation through SARB-licensed banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods shipped from South Africa must be fully repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless the authorized dealer grants an extension for bona fide commercial reasons. There is no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio; exporters may retain foreign currency in an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with a South African authorized dealer. The proceeds must be reported to the authorized dealer and, where required, to FinSurv, and all documentary evidence of shipment, invoicing, and receipt must be retained for six years.

Central bank supervision: Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) of the South African Reserve Bank (SARB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed as South African banks and cannot provide SARB-supervised ZAR settlement, exchange control reference numbers, or compliance with the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA). Feasibility is limited to non-ZAR collection facilitation, such as receiving USD card payments through a foreign acquiring partner, provided that all final settlement and repatriation flows are routed through a South African authorized dealer. Any EMS that claims to bypass exchange control or that aggregates client funds outside South Africa should be treated as legally non-viable for treasury operations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-border funding from unrelated third parties in a jurisdiction with no reciprocal tax treaty or with different FICA-equivalent standards

Root cause: Cross-border funding from unrelated third parties in a jurisdiction with no reciprocal tax treaty or with different FICA-equivalent standards

Defense evidence: A bundled defense file must contain the notarized shareholder register, share subscription agreement or formal loan agreement, corporate resolution, source-of-funds evidence in the form of audited financial statements and three months of bank statements, tax clearance certificates, an economic substance memorandum, and proof of the underlying cross-border trade contract.
Discrepancy between shipping documentation, customs declarations, and declared transaction values for the import of stuffing materials or export of finished plush inventory

Root cause: Discrepancy between shipping documentation, customs declarations, and declared transaction values for the import of stuffing materials or export of finished plush inventory

Defense evidence: The bank requires a full documentary set: finalized commercial invoices, packing lists, SAD500 customs entry declarations, air waybills or bills of lading, insurance certificates, third-party inspection reports (SGS/Intertek), and an internal reconciliation worksheet demonstrating that all physical movements match invoice values within acceptable tolerance.
High transaction velocity through personal offshore accounts of shareholders or payables routed through high-risk tax haven centers

Root cause: High transaction velocity through personal offshore accounts of shareholders or payables routed through high-risk tax haven centers

Defense evidence: Provide a complete corporate control chain from CIPC (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission) records, board meeting minutes approving each payment, executed contracts reflecting legitimate trade or financing terms, an independent transfer pricing study, and confirmatory letters from the counterparties identifying the commercial purpose of each inbound or outbound payment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity or loan capital injection must be initiated as a SWIFT MT103 from the investing entity's bank to the South African company's account at an authorized dealer. The remittance instructions must include the full corporate name 'ZA_PTY_LTD', an identified purpose code mirroring the inward foreign investment declaration, and clear narration referencing the share subscription or shareholder loan agreement. The receiving bank will assign an exchange control reference and, where applicable, require registration of the loan on the South African Reserve Bank's loan registry. Funds may remain in a foreign currency account or be sold to the bank at the prevailing spot rate upon receiving bank approval; incoming funds over approximately ZAR 10 million may prompt enhanced economic substance verification.Suppliers of fabric, PP fiber, ultrasonic welding equipment, and packaging are paid through SWIFT MT103 where the counterpart is pre-validated and risk is standard commercial; documentary Letters of Credit (MT700) are preferred for high-value machinery imports or when the supplier is in a jurisdiction with elevated trade sanctions risk. For payments to SACU-based subcontractors, compute settlement can be made via SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement) using the cross-border SAD500 movement and a commercial invoice.
