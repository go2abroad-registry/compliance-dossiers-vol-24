Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); filing portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs via SARS Customs/SYDA (Single Administrative Document system).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain a company registration number. 2) Appoint a public officer who is a natural person resident in South Africa (mandatory under s9 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, for both resident and foreign-owned entities). 3) Register on SARS eFiling by creating a user profile and completing the RAV (Registration, Amendments and Verification) application, obtaining an Income Tax Reference Number (TIN). 4) Apply for VAT registration via the same RAV application or VAT101 form once taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory) or from ZAR 50,000 (voluntary election). 5) SARS verifies the application and issues a separate VAT registration number, SARS PIN for eFiling, and customs importer/exporter code for SYDA. 6) Foreign-owned entities must file beneficial ownership data with CIPC and maintain SARS record-keeping and public officer details; no blocked minimum capital applies to the South African (Pty) Ltd.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — 1) Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain a company registration number. 2) Appoint a public officer who is a natural person resident in South Africa (mandatory under s9 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, for both resident and foreign-owned entities). 3) Register on SARS eFiling by creating a user profile and completing the RAV (Registration, Amendments and Verification) application, obtaining an Income Tax Reference Number (TIN). 4) Apply for VAT registration via the same RAV application or VAT101 form once taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory) or from ZAR 50,000 (voluntary election). 5) SARS verifies the application and issues a separate VAT registration number, SARS PIN for eFiling, and customs importer/exporter code for SYDA. 6) Foreign-owned entities must file beneficial ownership data with CIPC and maintain SARS record-keeping and public officer details; no blocked minimum capital applies to the South African (Pty) Ltd.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in South Africa; a (Pty) Ltd is a per se taxable company (resident company taxed on worldwide income at 27% headline corporate rate, lower progressive rates available only to qualifying small business corporations under s12E of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, elected/claimed in the tax return). Available elections and regimes include: (i) s12E small business corporation status for qualifying manufacturing SMEs with turnover below ZAR 20 million; (ii) s9I headquarter company election for certain qualifying 80% foreign-owned companies (not optimal for a manufacturing operating entity); (iii) voluntary VAT registration under s23(3) VAT Act 89 of 1991; (iv) functional currency election where the dominant currency is not ZAR; and (v) mandatory hybrid mismatch neutralisation rules under ss23M-23T of the Income Tax Act, which apply automatically and cannot be opted out for payments to hybrid entities or under hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration: ZAR 1,000,000 taxable turnover in a 12-month rolling period; voluntary VAT registration permitted from ZAR 50,000 (VAT Act 89 of 1991, s23(3)).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 applies where the vendor (a VAT-registered person) supplies movable goods in the course of its enterprise and exports the goods from South Africa (or supplies them to a non-resident who exports them, with the vendor's assistance) before the expiry of 90 days after the date of issue of the tax invoice for that supply. The vendor must obtain, within 90 days after the date of export (or such extended period as the Commissioner may allow), documentary proof of export as prescribed by VAT Regulation 17, including a completed Customs Export Declaration (SAD500), transport documents (Bill of Lading or Airway Bill), commercial invoice, and, where applicable, evidence of payment in foreign currency or an inward remittance certificate. Goods must physically leave South Africa and must not be re-supplied to a resident; offshore services (e.g., design, licensing or engineering services physically performed outside South Africa) may qualify for zero-rating under s11(2)(l), subject to the vendor retaining contemporaneous proof of the foreign physical performance and customer non-residency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged via SARS SYDA or an authorised customs agent
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing South Africa as origin and the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the high-precision steel injection-moulded plastic model kits
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/foreign-exchange receipt (BOP/BEN form) proving payment in foreign currency
  • Regulation 17 supplementary proof where applicable, e.g., container/shipment tracking, terminal receipts, and customs clearance records

South Africa does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, smart tax devices, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing clearance. SARS eFiling is the mandatory channel for electronic submission of VAT201 returns and customs declarations (SYDA). SARS is implementing an incremental e-invoicing framework (2023-2026) aligned with the OECD-style structured e-invoice (UBL/ISO 20022) and enhanced transactional data matching, but no vendor API clearance mandate is yet in force. VAT vendors must retain full electronic records, invoices, and export proof for the prescribed period and make them available to SARS on demand; VAT returns are due monthly or bi-monthly by the 25th day following the tax period, with payment by EFT to SARS.

SARS may deny zero-rating and issue an additional VAT assessment at 15% plus statutory interest where export documentation is missing, deficient, or inconsistent (Tax Administration Act 2011, ss187-188). Understatement penalties range from 10% to 125% of the shortfall depending on conduct (TAA ss222-223); administrative non-compliance penalties of up to ZAR 6,000 per month per non-compliance apply (TAA ss210-213). Retrospective reassessment periods are generally 3 years in 'clean' cases, 5 years for negligence/substantial understatement, and unlimited where fraud, misrepresentation, or deliberate evasion is established (TAA s99). Fraudulent or reckless export zero-rating claims attract criminal liability under VAT Act s58 and TAA s234, with fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years, plus customs penalties under the Customs Control Act 31 of 2014 for false export declarations. SARS may also claw back refunds with interest and, where a pattern of unjustified refund claims exists, impose a 'repeating' penalty of up to 200% of the assessed tax on certain transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a partially liberalised exchange control system. Current account transactions, including trade-related payments, are freely convertible via authorised dealers. Capital account transactions are subject to case-by-case approval and reporting, with the rand as the domestic settlement currency. Dividend remittances to non-residents are permitted under a Dividend Remittance Procedure requiring proof of investment, the company's tax registration qualifications, a listing of actual shareholding, and Payment of Dividends Tax before outward transfer. The principal remittance hurdles are SARS tax clearance compliance, debt obligation reporting, and the completion of the BOP form for stationary versus trade-related remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must on-board a valid Foreign Currency Export Contract (FCEC) with the authorised dealer before final shipment. The full invoice value of exported goods must be received within 180 days from the date of shipment, or within such extended period as the authorised dealer may approve. All export proceeds received in foreign currency must be surrendered to the authorised dealer for conversion into rand, unless the dealer approves a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) on demonstration of cash-flow needs. A 100% conversion is the statutory default for the portion not retained in a FCA.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the central bank and exchange control authority in terms of the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low to Medium. South African banking law under the Banks Act, 94 of 1990 requires deposit-taking or payment clearing linkage to a licensed bank as a regulated financial institution. Offshore digital EMI or payment service providers may on-board South African-based entities only to the extent they have entered into formal agency or collaboration agreements with a licensed South African bank. For a cash-flow stabilised manufacturing entity, EMIs are viable for payment collection APIs and payouts into a domestic bank account, but cannot replace a Tier-1 local banking relationship for foreign currency conversion and settlement. PayPal is accepted as a low-value trade disbursement gateway, and is classified as a payment method with no bank-grade settlement function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection by non-resident investor(s) is classified as a capital transaction requiring inward verification. The routing is a SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident remitter to a Tier-1 bank account, referencing 'Foreign Direct Investment in Proprietary Limited – ISIC_3240' and supplying the correct BOP purpose code (equity participation). The payment instruction must include the Investor’s registered name, the operational entity, the purpose of investment, and a written exchange-control declaration that the remittance is not underwritten from South African sourced funds. The authorised dealer verifies the source of funds, completes a Foreign Exchange Declaration, and reports to FinSurv for post-transaction validation. A preferential route is to use the company's statutory capital escrow account, which is opened exclusively for inward equity, and then to transfer to the operating account after compliance.The primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 executed through the authorised dealer for raw material imports and royalty payments. For commercial letters of credit, SWIFT MT700 series are used for open-account-backed documentary credit, with payment released upon presentation of title documents. Domestic clearing for lower-value cross-border invoices is processed through the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system and SADC regional payment systems; PayPal commercial transactions resemble ‘open invoice rail’ but are not considered a bank clearing rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File/Master File: no statutory de minimis; SARS requires contemporaneous arm's length documentation for all cross-border related-party transactions, and practical guidance expects documentation where individual/aggregate transactions exceed ZAR 5 million per year. CbCR: mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion (≈ EUR 750 million); notification and Master File/Local File via SARS eFiling with year-end filings.

Economic substance scope: No Gulf/Caribbean-style ESR statute is enacted. Substance is enforced through: (i) the OECD BEPS Principal Purpose Test (PPT) incorporated via the MLI in double-tax agreements; (ii) the s.9D CFC regime (attribution of tainted income to SA shareholders); (iii) GAAR under s.80A–80L; and (iv) the central-management-and-control test for corporate residence (s.1 Income Tax Act). A local manufacturer must maintain real premises, active board decisions, operational payroll, and local production/tooling substance to access treaty benefits and avoid being re-characterised as a shell or conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% of the shortfall for intentional evasion (TAA s.223); criminal conviction on indictment: fine (court's discretion) and/or imprisonment up to 5 years (TAA s.235); interest at repo + 4%

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit the evasion are jointly liable under the common-law piercing doctrine and s.77(3) Companies Act; personal civil restitution, a SARS travel ban under TAA s.194–196 (no departure without tax clearance or security), asset attachment, and imprisonment up to 5 years
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Offshore Shell Intermediary

Statutory sanction: SARS adjustment to arm's length consideration under s.31; understatement penalty of 50% (no reasonable grounds) to 150% (intentional evasion); interest; CFC inclusion under s.9D if the offshore entity is a CFC; denial of treaty benefits under the PPT

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's-length arrangements face personal liability for reckless trading (Companies Act s.22 read with s.77); SARS can pierce the veil and recover the adjusted tax, plus travel bans and potential disqualification as directors
Dividend Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The company as withholding agent remains liable for the full 20% tax (s.46A/57 Income Tax Act), plus a 10% penalty and prescribed interest; failure to register/maintain accurate dividend records attracts ANP penalties

Director personal liability: SARS may recover the unwithheld tax from directors who were responsible for the payment and knowingly failed to withhold; personal liability via TAA s.154 (representative taxpayer provisions), interest, and criminal exposure under TAA s.234 for repeated non-compliance
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach (FICA)

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Centre administrative sanction of up to ZAR 50 million per contravention (or a turnover-based amount); criminal conviction: unlimited fine and/or imprisonment up to 15 years (FICA s.68); deregistration or responsible-clerk orders under the Companies Act

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for knowingly allowing the company to be used for money laundering; civil asset forfeiture under POCA, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 15 years; disqualification as company directors under s.69 Companies Act
Reckless Trading and Insolvency

Statutory sanction: Prohibition under s.22(1) Companies Act; SARS may wind up the company; interest and penalties for unpaid provisional tax/PAYE; statutory preference for SARS in insolvency (preferential claim for employees' tax)

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for all debts (including tax) if the company is traded recklessly, with gross negligence, or fraudulently (s.77(3)(b) read with s.22); civil claims by SARS as a creditor, sequestration/bankruptcy of directors, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 10 years for fraud
Employment Tax (PAYE/UIF/SDL) Misclassification

Statutory sanction: SARS re-characterisation under the 'dominant impression' test; recovery of un-withheld PAYE (TAA s.156–160), s.210 ANP penalties, 200% deemed-employer penalty in aggravated cases, and interest

Director personal liability: Directors qualify as 'representative taxpayers' and can be held personally liable for all unremitted employees' tax (TAA s.159); criminal liability for failure to withhold or pay over; travel bans and director disqualification apply
