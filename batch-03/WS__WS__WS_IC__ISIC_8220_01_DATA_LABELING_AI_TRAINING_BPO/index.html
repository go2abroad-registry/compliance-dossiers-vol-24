Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa - the national tax administration established under the Tax Administration Act 2012, succeeding the Inland Revenue Services division of the Ministry of Finance. Official e-services and TIN/VAGST registration portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws. The Registrar of International Companies (Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour) administers incorporation and annual government fees of International Companies.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is statutorily tax-exempt and is not required to obtain a TIN for offshore operations. Procedure when a TIN/VAGST ID is needed for local procurement, input credit recovery, or bank onboarding: (1) file Form VAGST-01 (Registration Application) with the MfR; (2) attach certified certificate of incorporation, IC registration certificate, registered agent consent, registered office address in Samoa, director/shareholder particulars, and a business description covering AI data-labeling and RLHF BPO services; (3) the Commissioner of Revenue verifies and issues the TIN and VAGST registration certificate, effective from the application date or a later elected date (VAGST Act 2008, ss.12-16); (4) for pure offshore ICs, fiscal compliance is satisfied by payment of the annual government fee to the Registrar of International Companies, not by domestic TIN registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone - the Samoa IC is a non-resident offshore vehicle exempt from income tax, VAGST, and stamp duty in respect of all offshore business and foreign-source income under s.173 of the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended). The exemption is conditional on conducting no business with Samoa residents, holding no Samoa land, and paying the annual government fee. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - the Samoa IC is a non-resident offshore vehicle exempt from income tax, VAGST, and stamp duty in respect of all offshore business and foreign-source income under s.173 of the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended). The exemption is conditional on conducting no business with Samoa residents, holding no Samoa land, and paying the annual government fee. Breach of conditions subjects the IC to the domestic tax net: 27% corporate income tax and 15% VAGST assessed without transitional relief.

Available election forms

Samoa has no US-style check-the-box election, no IC-to-resident taxation election, and no hybrid/mismatch elections under BEPS Action 2. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) voluntary VAGST registration election, including below-threshold registration, under VAGST Act 2008 s.13, which enables zero-rated exporters to reclaim input VAGST; (ii) private binding ruling application to the Commissioner under the Tax Administration Act 2012 confirming offshore BPO income classification; (iii) IC continuation or migration out of Samoa under the International Companies Act 1987 to change tax residence without liquidation; (iv) amendment of the IC's memorandum of association to expand business scope without domestic tax consequences while offshore-exempt status persists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 (Samoa Tala) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period for compulsory VAGST registration of persons established in Samoa or with a Samoa fixed establishment; voluntary registration permitted below threshold with Commissioner approval (VAGST Act 2008, s.12). An offshore IC's exported data-labeling services are zero-rated or outside the scope of Samoa VAGST and do not trigger domestic registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under VAGST Act 2008, zero-rated supplies include exported goods, exported services performed directly for a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply, and services supplied in connection with goods exported from Samoa. The offshore AI data-labeling and RLHF BPO qualifies where: (a) the client is non-resident with no Samoa fixed establishment; (b) the benefit of the annotation services is consumed wholly outside Samoa; (c) delivery occurs via digital rails (MassPayPal/Wise) to offshore data platforms; (d) payment is received in foreign currency through a licensed Samoa commercial bank; and (e) contemporaneous evidence is maintained: executed master services agreement, work orders, annotation/RLHF delivery logs, dataset handover manifests, and client acceptance certificates.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Single Administrative Document (SAD) filed with the Samoa Customs Service for any physical export leg (servers, equipment, storage media); for purely digital services, substituted by the service export dossier comprising the contract, data-processing logs, and IP/annotation delivery records.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill - required only for physical goods movement; for digital BPO services, replaced by digital delivery evidence: encrypted dataset handover manifests, cloud service receipts, and signed client acceptance certificates.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - certificate or SWIFT credit advice from a licensed Samoa commercial bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency client funds; mandated by the Exchange Control Act 1976 and relied on by the MfR for zero-rating verification and input VAGST refund substantiation.

Samoa imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government e-invoicing API, and no digital stamping requirement. Tax invoices must contain the seller and buyer names and TINs, invoice number, date, supply description, quantity, and VAGST amount in WST (VAGST Act 2008, ss.55-58). The MfR eServices portal accepts electronic VAGST return filing and payment but does not pre-clear individual invoices. Zero-rated export supplies are evidenced by commercial invoices supplemented by digital service delivery records.

The MfR may audit zero-rating claims within a 4-year reassessment window, with no limitation period where fraud or wilful evasion is proven. Fabricated export invoices, false remittance certificates, or sham offshore structures trigger: (i) denial of zero-rating with assessment of 15% VAGST and back-interest; (ii) shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2012 of up to 100% of tax avoided; (iii) criminal prosecution under VAGST Act 2008 with fines up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years; (iv) clawback of refunded input VAGST with additional tax up to the refund amount; (v) forfeiture of the IC's tax-exempt status under s.173 of the International Companies Act 1987, exposing offshore income to 27% corporate income tax retrospectively; and (vi) regulatory action by the Registrar of International Companies against the licensed registered agent, including deregistration of the IC.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a managed float linked to a basket of trading-partner currencies. Current-account transactions, including AI data-labeling service fees, BPO software subscriptions and consulting invoices from abroad, are freely convertible through Authorized Dealers without case-by-case CBS approval. Capital-account transactions, such as inbound equity subscription exceeding WST 1,000,000, cross-border loans, or China-sourced client clearing amounts, require CBS prior authorization evidenced by an Exchange Control Notification (Form EC-3). Dividend and profit remittances by a validly registered International Company under the International Companies Act 1987 are exempt from surrender and repatriation requirements, and the IC may hold, retain and deploy foreign currency in external accounts provided the registered agent maintains a current UBO file and an annual licensed-agent compliance certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Domestic-flagged exporters must repatriate 100% of export proceeds to Samoa within 90 days of the date of shipment or payment, whichever is later, and surrender the proceeds to an Authorized Dealer unless an Export Retention Account (up to 50% of gross proceeds) has been approved by CBS. An International Company, once recognized as non-resident for exchange-control purposes, is expressly exempt from surrender and mandatory conversion; it is not obliged to convert USD, EUR or AUD export receipts into WST and may service all disbursements from a designated USD operational account. The exemption is conditional: no local customer sales, no securities holdings in WST, and the IC must maintain a CBS letter of non-resident status to present to its own bank and correspondents.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Apia, Samoa. Supervisory and administrative authority under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, the Foreign Exchange Act 1975 and the Financial Institutions Act 1996. CBS licenses all Authorized Dealers (commercial banks) and administers exchange-control exemptions for International Companies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW to MEDIUM (restricted). Samoa has no statutory e-money institution or payment-service licensing regime; Wise, Payoneer and PayPal operate solely as unregulated-in-Samoa cross-border aggregators and cannot provide the IC with a local settlement account, IBAN-driven banking license, or CBS-recognized guarantee. Legally, an International Company must maintain its primary treasury account at a CBS-licensed Authorized Dealer; Wise and Mass_PayPal_Payout may be used only as upstream collection rails for sub-WST-100,000 client invoices, provided the EMI's statement history is unbroken and flows are mirrored to the Samoan bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No transfer pricing documentation obligation is imposed on an International Company that is income-tax exempt. For a Samoa taxable resident company, a Local File and Master File are required where the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions exceeds WST 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations are applicable to ICs conducting relevant activities (banking, distribution, headquarters, shipping, holding, etc.). A pure AI data-labeling BPO company with no Samoa physical staffing, no local management, and no services to Samoa customers will not be treated as carrying on a relevant activity, but must file a substance declaration with the registry annually where the company is in material receipt of Samoan government tax benefits. Regular board meetings outside Samoa, sufficient overseas employees, and a clear operational nexus in a foreign jurisdiction are required to preserve the exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Income Tax Act 2015, tax evasion is punishable by a fine not exceeding WST 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years, plus an assessment of double the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, may be disqualified from serving as a director for 5 years, and travel bans may be imposed by the Ministry of Revenue pending final assessment.
Shell Company / Illegal Trading after Strike-Off

Statutory sanction: The Samoa International Companies Act allows the Registrar to strike the company off the register; any subsequent contract is void and the company becomes liable to a fine up to WST 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors of a struck-off company who continue to operate are personally liable for all debts incurred post-strike-off and may face imprisonment of up to 5 years for fraudulent trading.
AML/CTF and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: The Samoa AML/CTF Act 2018 imposes a corporate fine up to WST 2,000,000 and a natural-person officer fine up to WST 100,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to civil asset forfeiture, a 10-year travel ban, and imprisonment up to 7 years for laundering money derived from foreign criminal conduct, notwithstanding the company's corporate veil.
Offshore Management and Permanent Establishment Failure

Statutory sanction: A permanent establishment in Samoa is deemed to exist, exposing the company to Samoa corporate income tax at 27% on attributable profits, plus late payment penalties of 20% per annum and interest.

Director personal liability: Non-resident directors found to have managed the IC from Samoa are personally assessed to the unpaid tax and may be subjected to freezing orders on Samoa bank accounts and civil restitution of distributed sums.
