Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the International Companies Act 1988; share capital quantum is set at the discretion of subscribers in the memorandum. No escrow account or capital deposit is mandated by SIFSA. If par-value shares are issued, the register may record payment on call, but the first board resolution must acknowledge that the stated consideration for issued shares has been credited to the company's account before commencement of trading.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Samoa Fire and Emergency Services Authority (SFESA) certifying means of escape, extinguishers and alarm systems; building occupancy clearance from PUMA verifying structural load safety and the certificate of occupancy for the leased office premises; employer registration with the Samoa Accident Compensation Corporation (SACC) under the Accident Compensation Act 2015 covering workplace insurance; occupational clearance confirming indoor ventilation and absence of asbestos and hazardous substances.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (Malo o Tupe Maua), Government of Samoa; central tax administration and filing portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws. Tax procedures are governed by the Tax Administration Act 2020, the Income Tax Act 2012, and the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2007.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate under the International Companies Act 1987 via the Registrar of International Companies to obtain a Certificate of Incorporation and unique company number. 2. Submit Form TAA-01 (Tax Identification Number registration) to the Ministry for Revenue, providing certified passport copies of directors/shareholders, the registered office address in Samoa, memorandum and articles of association, and the Certificate of Incorporation. 3. The Ministry will issue a TIN (and, where applicable, a VAGST registration number) within 5-10 business days. 4. For a Samoan International Company claiming statutory tax exemption, no income tax registration is required; the company must maintain its status as a non-resident-owned offshore company and may obtain a letter of tax exemption confirmation from the Ministry. 5. Register for VAGST separately if the company makes taxable supplies in Samoa above the registration threshold; otherwise, the VAGST registration may be waived for purely offshore activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' classification regime exists in Samoa. Under sections 179-180 of the International Companies Act 1987, an International Company may elect to convert to a local (domestic) company and thereby become subject to ordinary Samoan income tax, or may apply to the Ministry for Revenue to waive its statutory tax exemption on a case-by-case basis to access double tax treaty benefits. Samoa's Tax Administration Act 2020 and Income Tax Act 2012 incorporate OECD hybrid mismatch limitation rules, but these apply to deny deduction/non-inclusion outcomes rather than to permit elective classification. International Companies are statutorily exempt from income tax, stamp duty, and VAGST on offshore business activities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 300,000 per 12-month period (annual value of taxable supplies) for domestic suppliers; the threshold does not apply to VAGST on imported goods, which is collected at the border, nor to non-resident suppliers of certain imported services where reverse-charge or special rules apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (1) tangible goods physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply, supported by customs evidence; (2) international transport services; and (3) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply, provided the services are not rendered to a resident and are not used or consumed in Samoa. Government affairs advisory and cross-border lobbying services delivered to a non-resident client, where all advice is prepared outside Samoa and no Samoa-source taxable supply arises, may qualify for zero-rating if the non-resident status of the recipient and offshore place of consumption are documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form CED or ASYCUDA World electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and/or bank SWIFT MT103 confirming receipt of export consideration from the non-resident buyer

Samoa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or a government-linked e-invoicing API for most businesses. Registered VAGST suppliers must issue sequential tax invoices (hard copy or PDF) containing the supplier's VAGST number, invoice date, description, quantity, price, and VAGST charged. Phased Electronic Fiscal Device (EFD) requirements apply mainly to large retailers, hospitality, and tourism-related businesses, requiring connectivity to the Ministry for Revenue's systems for audit purposes. Customs declarations are processed electronically through ASYCUDA World; no universal real-time clearance mandate exists for commercial invoices.

The Ministry for Revenue may reassess within 4 years for understated VAGST or erroneous refunds; for intentional or reckless false zero-rating claims, the reassessment period is unlimited. Penalties include a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall (or 20% where reasonable care was not taken), late payment penalty of 2% per month or part thereof, and interest on outstanding amounts. Criminal conviction for VAGST fraud or deliberate false documentation attracts fines up to WST 300,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; refunds are clawed back with immediate assessment where export documentation (customs declaration, bill of lading, and inward remittance certificate) is not maintained or is found to be false.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a partially convertible currency subject to administrative exchange control administered by CBS. Current-account transactions, including cross-border service fees, professional retainer payments, and intercompany recharges, are substantially liberalised and may be processed by an authorised dealer without prior CBS approval. Capital-account transactions, such as non-resident equity subscriptions, intra-group loans, and dividend remittances, are restricted and require CBS approval or formal post-transaction declaration. For a Samoa International Company holding Tax-Exempt Zone status, profit repatriation is permitted only after the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) certifies the entity's tax-exemption status, the company lodges a solvency declaration, and the authorised dealer records the distribution in the CBS exchange-control return.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All foreign-currency export and service-export earnings, including government-relations and lobbying retainer fees invoiced to non-residents, must be repatriated to Samoa within 180 days from the invoice date and 100% of the proceeds must be either converted into WST at the authorised dealer's prevailing market rate or credited to a CBS-approved Foreign Currency Account expressly designated for the International Company. Retention of export earnings offshore, third-country settlement, or netting against payables requires prior written approval from CBS under Exchange Control Regulation 17; any unauthorised retention is a criminal offence and triggers automatic suspension of the entity's foreign-currency account.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), operating under the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Financial Institutions Act 1996, and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018; CBS is the sole exchange control authority, the prudential regulator for all licensed commercial banks, and the repository for capital import certificates and foreign-currency account approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary settlement rail. Samoa has no electronic-money-institution licensing regime, and the Financial Institutions Act 1996 requires all deposit-taking and payment-settlement activity to be performed by a CBS-licensed commercial bank. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Mercury) are not recognised as authorised dealers, cannot hold WST balances, and cannot issue capital-import certificates or process dividend remittances for a Samoan IC. An offshore USD or GBP account with a reputable EMI may be used solely as a pre-funded ancillary collection point for retainer wires, provided all balances are swept to the CBS-licensed bank within five business days and the route is disclosed in the CBS exchange-control return.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections must be remitted from the non-resident shareholder's own bank account directly to the entity's Capital Escrow Account at BSP Samoa or SCB via SWIFT MT103. Required wire attributes: beneficiary account designated 'WS_IC Capital Escrow', purpose code 'A.11 – Direct Investment / Equity', and narrative 'Capital contribution under Samoa International Companies Act 1987'. The receiving bank must record the transaction in the daily CBS exchange-control report and issue a Capital Import Certificate to the licensed registered agent. Any inbound remittance exceeding 100,000 USD triggers a mandatory 24-hour compliance review requiring the shareholder resolution, the SIFA exemption certificate, and a written confirmation that the funds are not derived from procurement, public-tender, or foreign-government awarding activities.Outbound disbursements for retainer fees, trade-policy advisory services, and reimbursable expenses must settle via SWIFT MT103 using purpose code 'B.32 – Business and Management Consultancy Services'. Clearing protocol: USD payments are settled through the Samoan bank's New York correspondent within T+1 via Fedwire/CHIPS; EUR payments settle through RTGS via the euro correspondent; GBP payments settle via CHAPS. Documented commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are permissible only for milestone-based advisory deliverables, where the advising bank is the same correspondent shown in the original inbound transfer. Every outbound payment is matched against a board-approved retainer schedule deposited with the bank ; variances exceeding 20% per transaction require a fresh payee KYC review and CBS exchange-control approval before release.
