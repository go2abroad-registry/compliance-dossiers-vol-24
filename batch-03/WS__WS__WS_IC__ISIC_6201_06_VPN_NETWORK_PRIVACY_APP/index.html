Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MFRE), Samoa — official e-services portal: https://www.tax.gov.ws/

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) must file TIN Application Form (Form TO-01) with the Ministry for Revenue, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of International Companies, the registered-office certificate executed by a licensed Samoan registered agent, director and shareholder particulars, and a certified copy of the company constitution. MFRE processes the application within 5-10 business days and issues a TIN on a non-taxable basis because the IC is exempt from income tax; the TIN additionally enables any VAGST registration, employer registration, or customs transactor code if local transactions arise.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Samoa International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from all forms of Samoan income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty under sections 199-200 of the International Companies Act 1987, provided no business is conducted within Samoa; the IC discharges its fiscal obligations solely through an annual government fee payable to the Registrar of International Companies. — A foreign-owned International Company (IC) must file TIN Application Form (Form TO-01) with the Ministry for Revenue, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of International Companies, the registered-office certificate executed by a licensed Samoan registered agent, director and shareholder particulars, and a certified copy of the company constitution. MFRE processes the application within 5-10 business days and issues a TIN on a non-taxable basis because the IC is exempt from income tax; the TIN additionally enables any VAGST registration, employer registration, or customs transactor code if local transactions arise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Samoa International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from all forms of Samoan income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty under sections 199-200 of the International Companies Act 1987, provided no business is conducted within Samoa; the IC discharges its fiscal obligations solely through an annual government fee payable to the Registrar of International Companies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Samoan law. The IC must file a statutory declaration under section 201 of the International Companies Act 1987 confirming that it has not carried on business in Samoa and holds no Samoan-situs real property, thereby preserving the tax-exempt classification. The IC cannot elect to be taxed as an ordinary Samoan company; the exempt status is mandatory while the IC remains licensed under the International Companies Act 1987.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 per annum (approximately USD 22,000) of taxable supplies. An IC deriving wholly offshore VPN/SaaS revenue from non-resident customers has no Samoan place-of-supply nexus and is not registrable for VAGST; where the IC purchases local registered-agent, legal, or audit services, the import of such services may attract VAGST reverse-charge accounting under the VAGST Act 2008.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of digital VPN, network-security, and privacy-application services is zero-rated under section 8 of the VAGST Act 2008 when the recipient is a non-resident and the services are performed for and effectively enjoyed entirely outside Samoa. The supplier must hold a written contract specifying offshore delivery, non-resident customer identity, and settlement in foreign currency; MFRE may deny zero-rating where evidence demonstrates consumption of the service within Samoa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (required only for physical goods; purely digital services are substantiated by service contracts)
  • Bill of Lading or service delivery record (e.g., cloud-server deployment log, bandwidth handover report)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous-transaction-control mandate exists in Samoa. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue paper or PDF tax invoices containing the particulars prescribed by the VAGST Act 2008 and file periodic returns electronically through the MFRE e-Tax portal; there is no API-based e-invoicing clearance requirement.

Under the Tax Administration Act 2014, a false or fraudulent zero-rating claim attracts a misstatement penalty of up to 75% of the VAGST understated, late-payment interest accruing at 1% per month, and retrospective clawback for up to 4 years. Intentionally false declarations constitute a criminal offense punishable by a fine of up to WST 5,000 and/or two years' imprisonment. Additionally, the Exchange Control Act 1976 empowers the Central Bank of Samoa to impose penalties on non-repatriation of export proceeds beyond the 180-day deadline, notwithstanding the IC's general exchange-control exemption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is partially convertible. Samoa accepted IMF Article VIII obligations on 23 June 1994; therefore bona fide current-account transactions (trade in goods, digital services exports, profit and dividend remittances) are free of exchange-control approval barriers, while capital-account transactions (non-resident borrowings, shareholder loans, portfolio investment) remain subject to CBS permission. A Samoa International Company (WS_IC) is treated as a non-resident offshore entity for exchange-control purposes and may hold USD-denominated offshore accounts with licensed commercial banks without conversion to WST. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted and exempt from Samoan withholding tax. Practical hurdles: (i) each material inward and outward remittance requires a bank-presented exchange-control declaration; (ii) banks strictly segregate resident (WST) and non-resident (foreign-currency) accounts to prevent the IC from transacting in the local economy; (iii) correspondent banks apply conservative country-risk scoring, so USD clearing may attract enhanced screening delays of one to three additional banking days.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1981, export proceeds, including proceeds from the export of digital and VPN subscription services, must be fully repatriated and credited to a licensed Samoan bank account within six months of the date of export, and supporting invoices and processor settlement reports must be retained for five years. Mandatory conversion into WST applies only to the portion needed for local-currency expenditure; no surrender requirement applies to foreign-currency accounts designated as non-resident offshore accounts of an International Company. Digital-settlement proceeds received from Paymentwall and similar acquirers in USD may be credited directly to the IC's USD offshore account; the bank must be provided with the processor settlement statement and a narrative describing the underlying subscription service. Failure to substantiate the receipt within the prescribed timeline may cause the bank to reclassify it as export proceeds requiring CBS post-approval filing, with penalties under the Exchange Control Act 1976.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, Apia; foreign-exchange control is administered by the CBS Exchange Control Department on delegated authority of the Minister of Finance, pursuant to the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1981.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail. Leading offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Currenxie, Payoneer) generally exclude Samoa-incorporated companies from their supported-company matrices; Payoneer offers limited settlement support but does not provide a full corporate bank account. Samoa has no domestic EMI licensing regime, and SIFA-licensed international banks operate under a dedicated offshore banking licence with minimum deposit requirements typically above USD 250,000. Structural constraint: Stripe does not list Samoa as an eligible payout country, so a WS_IC cannot directly onboard Stripe with a Samoan bank account; the collection architecture must route subscription card payments through Paymentwall (which can settle to Samoa by SWIFT) or through a separate merchant-of-record in a serviced jurisdiction, a structure that must be separately governed for tax and permanent-establishment risk. Recommended design: licensed Samoan bank USD account as the settlement account; Paymentwall as the acquirer of card and alternative payment methods with MT103 USD payout; EMIs used only for targeted vendor disbursements where they accept Samoan entities. Legal constraints: no Samoa prohibition on using an EMI, but the EMI's licence applies only in its own jurisdiction and cannot be passported to Samoa; all EMI flows must be fully mirrored in the IC's accounting records for Money Laundering Prevention Act compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from shareholders must be executed as SWIFT MT103 (GPI recommended) in USD from the shareholder's personal or corporate account into the IC's USD capital-escrow or operational account at a licensed Samoan bank. Payment narrative in field 70 must state 'Share subscription - contribution to paid-up capital of WS_IC [entity number]' and must not use payment-for-services codes. The bank will require: (i) a board resolution approving the allotment; (ii) the bank's CBS inward-remittance exchange-control declaration; (iii) source-of-funds evidence for wires above USD 100,000; (iv) post-receipt filing of the allotment return with the registered agent for SIFA records. Shareholder loans are discouraged because they trigger CBS borrowing-permission review; if used, the executed loan agreement, repayment schedule and interest terms must be lodged with the bank in advance. Verification after receipt: the bank issues a transaction advice evidencing the USD balance, which serves as the supporting document for the SIFA share-allotment filing and the annual return.Primary rail is SWIFT MT103 for all non-documentary settlement: cloud-infrastructure procurement (AWS, Akamai, Microsoft Azure), software licences, contractor fees, registered-agent fees and director salaries; each MT103 must reference the invoice number in field 70 and the corresponding invoice or contract must remain in the treasury file for five years. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are paid by MT103 with narrative 'Dividend distribution of WS_IC', supported by board resolution and solvency confirmation; Samoa levies no withholding tax on International Company dividends and no exchange-control permit is required when paid from the USD offshore account. Documentary commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) are not cost-effective for a SaaS treasury and should be reserved for physical-hardware procurement where a supplier demands one, with the LC issued against the IC's USD account and reimbursements executed by MT103 or MT202. Recommended design: licensed Samoan bank USD account as the settlement account; Paymentwall as the acquirer of card and alternative payment methods with MT103 USD payout; EMIs used only for targeted vendor disbursements where they accept Samoan entities. Legal constraints: no Samoa prohibition on using an EMI, but the EMI's licence applies only in its own jurisdiction and cannot be passported to Samoa; all EMI flows must be fully mirrored in the IC's accounting records for Money Laundering Prevention Act compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required for Samoa tax residents (including ICs that have lost exempt status) with related-party transactions exceeding WST 1,000,000 in an income year; Master File is required when aggregate related-party cross-border transactions exceed WST 10,000,000 or where any single related-party royalty/licence exceeds WST 500,000.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (Samoa ESR 2019) apply to International Companies conducting 'relevant activities', including intellectual property holding, leasing, financing, and distribution/service centres. A VPN network and digital encryption software company is categorised as an IP-rich business; it must demonstrate substantive economic presence in Samoa — i.e., locally situated decision-making management, qualified employees commensurate with activity, sufficient physical office, and adequate expenditure. A shell IC with no employees, no premises, and all decisions taken offshore will fail the substance test, leading to denial of the IC tax exemption and reclassification of its entire income as Samoa-source taxable income at 27%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misdeclaration of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to WST 10,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years under the Tax Administration Act 2015; civil penalty of 150% of the tax shortfall; cancellation of the IC's exempt status.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax plus interest; automatic travel ban may be issued by the Commissioner; personal bank accounts frozen; disqualification as corporate director for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations 2019, failure to satisfy economic substance results in a fine of WST 200,000 plus WST 10,000 per month of continued non-compliance; mandatory filing of missing documentation; the IC's tax exemption is revoked retroactively, exposing all past profits to 27% corporate income tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners personally liable for the tax amount assessed (with limited liability pierced); restrictions on air travel and international transactions; the Supreme Court may issue a 'director disqualification order' under s.62 of the International Companies Act 1987.
AML Non-Compliance (Integrated Payment Flows)

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act (Samoa) 2016, corporate entity is liable to a fine up to WST 2,000,000; the IC's licence or registration may be suspended for up to 12 months; formal warning and remedial order published in the Samoa Gazette.

Director personal liability: Director and compliance officer are personally exposed to civil penalties of up to WST 250,000 each; criminal liability for 'tipping off' (disclosing a transaction report) carries up to 2 years imprisonment; directors may be stripped of personal assets and a travel ban issued until full restitution.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Related-Party Licensing Abuse

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment re-assessing taxable income at 27%; underpayment interest at 2.5% per month; negligence penalty of 50% of the additional tax; deliberate concealment penalty of 100% of the additional tax; publication of the taxpayer's name in Solomon Star and Samoa Observer.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed non-compliant intercompany agreements are personally liable for the underpaid tax amount to the extent of their personal benefit; prohibited from leaving Samoa via 'tax debt travel restraint' order under s.121 of the Tax Administration Act 2015; potential imprisonment for up to 12 months for contempt of a compliance order.
