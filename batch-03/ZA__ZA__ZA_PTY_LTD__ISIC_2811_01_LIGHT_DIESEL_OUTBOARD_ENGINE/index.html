Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); primary digital portal is SARS eFiling (www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs and excise are administered by the SARS Customs Division through the Customs Trader Portal and electronic SAD500 declarations; corporate registration and company tax reference number allocation are channelled through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) under the Companies Act 71 of 2008.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with CIPC; the issued enterprise registration number automatically activates an income tax reference number at SARS. Step 2: Register the company for VAT via form VAT 101 (Application for Registration as a VAT Vendor) submitted through eFiling or at a SARS branch; the vendor VAT number is linked to the SARS tax reference number. Step 3: Obtain a SARS customs trader/exporter code enabling lodgement of export declarations. Step 4: Where a foreign-incorporated entity has South African source income, submit IT77 (application for non-resident taxpayer registration) and obtain a SARS tax reference number. Step 5: Appoint a public officer (resident individual) as required under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 and file the registration particulars through eFiling before commencing taxable supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African (Pty) Ltd is resident by incorporation under Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 s.1 and is taxed at entity level at the 27% corporate income tax headline rate; no pass-through treatment is available for companies; distributions to shareholders attract dividends tax; the company is subject to tax on worldwide income, with limited foreign dividend and foreign branch exemptions only in statutorily defined cases.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective transparent classification exists under South African law. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT grouping under VAT Act 89 of 1991 s.50, permitting two or more resident companies with common control to be treated as a single vendor via form VAT 107; (ii) election of the small business corporation (SBC) tax regime where turnover is below ZAR 20 million; (iii) election under VAT Act s.11(2)(k)/(l) to zero-rate export-related services; and (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under Income Tax Act s.8F–8M apply automatically, not by election, re-characterising hybrid instruments and deduction timing irrespective of taxpayer choice.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month rolling period (s.23(1) VAT Act). Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period (s.23(3)). Non-resident suppliers of electronic services: ZAR 50,000 threshold. A manufacturing exporter with no domestic taxable turnover may register voluntarily to claim input tax refunds on export-related purchases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under VAT Act s.11(1)(a) where the registered vendor has supplied movable goods (including light diesel marine outboard engines under ISIC 2811) and those goods are exported from South Africa — by or on behalf of the vendor — within 90 days of the time of supply (or a longer period allowed by SARS), provided the vendor retains documentary proof of export. Export destinations include all foreign countries and SACU/BLNS states. For services (e.g., offshore commissioning, installation or training), zero-rating applies under s.11(2)(k) for services physically rendered outside South Africa or s.11(2)(l) for services supplied to non-residents not connected to South African property or use. Additionally, diesel fuel used in the qualifying manufacturing of goods for export may be refunded under the fuel levy diesel refund mechanism in s.75 of the Customs and Excise Act, which SARS reconciles in the same compliance audit.

  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure and foreign destination, including SACU/BLNS destinations
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an authorised dealer bank for foreign exchange proceeds, or equivalent SWIFT MT103 credit advice
  • Proof of delivery accepted by the foreign consignee and cargo insurance certificate

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) API obligation is in force as of the current filing period. SARS has published a consultative e-invoicing roadmap, but tax invoices may currently be issued in paper or electronic form provided the authenticity, integrity, legibility and retention requirements of VAT Act s.20 and s.20A are met. All VAT returns (VAT 201) and customs export declarations are submitted digitally via eFiling and the SARS Customs Trader Portal with EDI integration.

SARS cross-matches VAT 201 returns with customs SAD500 export data and bank remittance records. Unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-ratings are re-assessed at 15% VAT plus interest under VAT Act s.39, with input tax previously claimed on export-related inputs retrospectively clawed back. Understatement penalties under Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 s.222–223 range from 10% (substantial understatement) to 25% (reasonable care not taken), 50% (no reasonable grounds), 150% (intentional tax evasion) and up to 200% for repeat cases; administrative non-compliance penalties may be imposed monthly under TAA s.210–213. Criminal prosecution under VAT Act s.58–59 and TAA s.234 may result in fines or imprisonment of up to 24 months, and exchange-control breaches of the 180-day repatriation rule are referred to the South African Reserve Bank Financial Surveillance Department for separate penalties and enforcement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a structurally liberalised but regulated foreign exchange regime. Current account transactions are freely convertible without prior SARB approval; capital account transactions are restricted under the Exchange Control Regulations. Profit remittances by non-resident shareholders are freely permitted after corporate income tax has been paid, provided the company is in good standing and remittance is executed through an Authorised Dealer. Dividends are subject to a 20% Dividends Tax withholding, unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty. The dividend remittance requires audited or reviewed financial statements confirming distributable reserves, a board/shareholder resolution, and a SARS tax clearance PIN confirming no outstanding assessments. Inward non-resident loans must be reported via Form MP337(a), and the loan must be evidenced by a written agreement to secure future capital repayment remittances; interest is subject to South African source withholding tax. For an ISIC 2811 manufacturing entity, inward FDI is permitted but must be reported; capital cannot be used indefinitely as quasiequity without structured loan documentation and exchange-control records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa within 30 days from the date of export, and disposed of (sold for ZAR) to an Authorised Dealer within 30 days of receipt unless a specific approval allows retention. The mandatory conversion ratio effectively requires 100% disposal of eligible export receipts into ZAR, unless a foreign-currency account is authorised. Form BTF (Bill of Trade and Finance) must be lodged through the customs clearing agent; export documents must be submitted to the Authorised Dealer within 30 days of shipment. Proceeds must match the declared invoice value. Under-invoicing or over-invoicing and mispricing of marine engine exports attract SARS customs and exchange-control penalties. Trade finance advances may be granted against irrevocable LCs once export contracts and pro-forma invoices are authenticated.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and the Exchange Control Department, with delegated authority to Authorised Dealers (commercial banks). The legal basis is the Currency and Exchanges Act, 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual issued by FinSurv, as well as the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 for AML/CFT supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium-High for ancillary collections/disbursements but Low for primary treasury nesting in South Africa. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Currencycloud, Airwallex, Multitude) are not Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange and cannot perform compulsory ZAR conversions or issue South African exchange-control compliant filings (Form A2, BTF, MP337). They may operate only as ancillary collection nodes provided every collectible export receipt is swept into the licensed South African Authorised Dealer before the statutory surrender deadline. Any person other than a SARB-approved Authorised Dealer or Authorised Broker is prohibited from buying/selling foreign exchange without SARB approval under the Currency and Exchanges Act. A South African resident company may hold a foreign-currency account offshore only with FinSurv discretionary approval. The compliant architecture is therefore: offshore EMI as collection account with end-of-day SWIFT sweep into a Tier 1 SARB Authorised Dealer; the EMI must be disclosed to FinSurv and cannot store ultimate liquidity overnight beyond the permitted operational threshold.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital must be transmitted via SWIFT to a licensed Authorised Dealer in South Africa using the correct transaction purpose code. Equity must be reported via Form MP337(a) for inward loans and Form MP337(b) for inward equity. Loans above R500,000 must be reported and evidenced. The inbound MT103 field 70 must include the CIPC registration number and reference 'ZA_PTY_LTD_CAPITAL_2025'. The Authorised Dealer may convert foreign currency to ZAR at the commercial rate. A segregated internal Capital Escrow Account should quarantine injected funds until onboarding documents are verified; funds may be released only after the shareholder register or loan ledger is updated. No prior SARB approval is generally required for FDI loans below R1 billion, but 'specified' low-tax-jurisdiction or hybrid financing triggers Form MP337(a) and SARB review.Outbound trade settlements for imported raw materials and components must flow through compliant rails: (A) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for open-account transactions; (B) SWIFT MT700 documentary letters of credit for high-value components; (C) documentary collections (DP/DA) against bills of exchange for mid-value trade. All outbound foreign payments require Form A2 lodged with proof documents, including duty-inclusive invoices or SARS customs clearance. Each disbursement must be tied to an underlying commercial contract and the South African transfer-pricing arm's-length confirmation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is Medium-High for ancillary collections/disbursements but Low for primary treasury nesting in South Africa. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Currencycloud, Airwallex, Multitude) are not Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange and cannot perform compulsory ZAR conversions or issue South African exchange-control compliant filings (Form A2, BTF, MP337). They may operate only as ancillary collection nodes provided every collectible export receipt is swept into the licensed South African Authorised Dealer before the statutory surrender deadline. Any person other than a SARB-approved Authorised Dealer or Authorised Broker is prohibited from buying/selling foreign exchange without SARB approval under the Currency and Exchanges Act. A South African resident company may hold a foreign-currency account offshore only with FinSurv discretionary approval. The compliant architecture is therefore: offshore EMI as collection account with end-of-day SWIFT sweep into a Tier 1 SARB Authorised Dealer; the EMI must be disclosed to FinSurv and cannot store ultimate liquidity overnight beyond the permitted operational threshold.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under section 25 of the Tax Administration Act and SARS Practice Note 7 (1999) amended by the SARS Guide on Transfer Pricing, mandatory contemporaneous documentation is required for all cross-border connected-party transactions that exceed ZAR 10 million in annual aggregate value. Local File and Master File requirements align with OECD Chapter V: a Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds ZAR 1 billion (or ZAR 5 million total related-party transactions with SA, per SARS's revised guidance); a Local File is required when the SA entity's related-party transactions exceed R10 million per relevant category. SARS also requires an annual TP policy review and economic substance documentation.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not currently apply a formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime as in the UAE or Cayman, but the common-law 'substance over form' and the statutory anti-avoidance provisions (GAAR under section 80A–80L) effectively impose economic substance requirements. A (Pty) Ltd engaged in manufacturing of diesel outboard engines must demonstrate substantive presence: real factory/sales premises, employees engaged in design, production, and warranty service, board meetings/minutes, bank accounts, and inventory in SA. Failure to show economic substance in cross-border royalty or management-fee arrangements will trigger SARS's transfer pricing adjustment, secondary adjustment (deemed dividend), and the application of the GAAR, resulting in a 67% understatement penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underpayment of Corporate Tax

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, section 235: a taxpayer who submits a false return or declaration is guilty of an offence and liable to a fine or imprisonment not exceeding 5 years. Under the Income Tax Act, interest at the prescribed rate plus 100% additional tax may be assessed. SARS may impose a 150% understatement penalty for intentional tax evasion (part III of the Tax Administration Act, penalty of 67% of the understated amount in egregious cases).

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable under section 14 of the Tax Administration Act for taxes withheld (PAYE/VAT/employees' tax) but not paid; section 22 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 allows a court to pierce the corporate veil if the company is used to defraud creditors or SARS; directors can be barred from holding office and may face travel bans (under the Constitution of South Africa, a court may order a withdrawal of passports to prevent flight). Criminal directors face imprisonment of up to 10 years under the Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act (fraud-related). Civil restitution includes repayment of evasion amounts, punitive interest, and disqualification from public contracts.
Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke the GAAR (sections 80A–80L), disregarding the arrangement and adjusting taxable income. Administrative penalties under the Tax Administration Act can reach 200% of the tax avoided. The company may be deregistered by CIPC for failing to file annual returns, and SARS may issue a notice that it is no longer a 'tax in good standing' entity. Under section 41 of the Tax Administration Act, a SARS order may freeze bank accounts for up to 10 days.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot rely on corporate limited liability if the company is a facade; under section 22(1) of the Companies Act, courts can declare directors personally liable for losses if the company is carried on recklessly, with gross negligence, or for a fraudulent purpose. Directors may be subject to an order requiring contribution to SARS's loss, cancellation of the company's registration, and an order preventing the director from managing any South African company for up to 15 years (Companies Act section 162(7)). Where criminal conduct is proven, imprisonment for 3 to 10 years may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Section 31 of the Income Tax Act and Article 9 of DTAs authorize SARS to adjust the consideration to an arm's length value. Secondary adjustment: the adjusted amount is deemed to be a dividend, attracting the 20% dividend tax (or a 7.5% withholding under the secondary transfer pricing adjustment mechanism). A 67% understatement penalty may apply to a 'tax avoidance arrangement' under section 223(2)(d). SARS may also assess interest at the prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false TP policy or who knowingly conclude sham agreements to shift profits may be personally liable under section 22 of the Companies Act for reckless or fraudulent trading. SARS may assert a joint liability against a controlling director under section 153(3) for amounts withheld, and foreign travel is blocked for tax-defaulting directors under the Preservation of Assets Act 2 of 1993. In egregious cases, common-law fraud charges may lead to imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Dividend Tax Repatriation Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Dividend withholding tax is the liability of the company if it fails to withhold and pay to SARS. Under the Tax Administration Act, a 10% per month penalty up to 100% of the unpaid tax, plus interest. The disguised payment is recharacterized as a dividend, subject to 20% dividend tax or 15% interest withholding tax, plus understatement penalties of 150% in cases of intentional avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately ignore withholding obligations are personally and severally liable for the unpaid dividends tax (section 44 of the Tax Administration Act). A corporate veil will be pierced if the distribution was an illegal financial assistance (section 45 of the Companies Act) or was made for a fraudulent purpose. Director's office may be declared vacant, and personal assets can be attached to satisfy the tax debt.
AML / Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (as amended), administrative fines can reach ZAR 10 million per non-compliance event, and accountable institutions may have their licences revoked where applicable. Directors or officers responsible for AML compliance can be fined up to ZAR 10 million or imprisoned for up to 5 years if they knowingly fail to report a suspicious transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment for up to 10 years for deliberate circumvention of the cash threshold reporting (section 78 of the FIC Act). Directors can be banned from serving as company officers in any financial institution and may be subject to civil claims for losses. Travel bans are possible while an AML investigation is pending to prevent flight.
Failure to File Annual Returns / Deregistration by CIPC

Statutory sanction: CIPC may request the company to make good and pay a R750 annual filing fee; after persistent default, the company is deregistered (Companies Act section 82). Upon deregistration, a director cannot trade through it and the entity cannot enter contracts or own property. SARS may issue a unilateral tax assessment based on its own information. For repeat non-compliance, a personal penalty is charged at R150 per month (CIPC late filing fees).

Director personal liability: A director knowingly failing to file annual returns or who allows the company to be deregistered is liable for debts incurred after the deregistration (Companies Act section 82(6)). Civil restitution: director may be required to reimburse clients/investors. A court may disqualify the individual from holding directorships for up to 10 years, and an outstanding SARS tax debt may trigger a travel ban and asset freezing.
