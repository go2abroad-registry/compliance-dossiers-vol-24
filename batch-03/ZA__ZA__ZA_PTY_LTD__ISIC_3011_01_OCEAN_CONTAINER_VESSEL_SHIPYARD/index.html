Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling portal at www.sarsefiling.co.za; physical head office at 299 Bronkhorst Street, Brooklyn, Pretoria, 0181

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity that becomes a South African resident for tax purposes, or a non-resident derived South African-source income or carrying on a South African enterprise, must: (1) apply for registration as a taxpayer via the SARS eFiling portal or at a SARS branch, completing the applicable registration forms (e.g., IT77 for income tax, VAT101 for VAT vendor registration); (2) submit supporting documents including the certificate of incorporation, Memorandum of Incorporation, trust deed or partnership agreement, proof of registered office/place of business, and identification documents of directors/members; (3) SARS will issue a taxpayer reference number (income tax) and VAT number separately; non-resident companies without a fixed South African place of business must register for income tax through a SARS-appointed representative taxpayer under section 57 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; VAT registration by the 'enterprise' (Pty) Ltd is mandatory from the first day of the month following the end of a 12-month period in which taxable supplies exceeded R1 million, or voluntarily from commencement if taxable supplies will exceed R250,000 within the next 12 months; a non-resident supplier making supplies in South Africa in the course of an enterprise must also register if the threshold is met; no minimum share capital is required, but SARS requires physical presence verification and a letter of good standing from the home jurisdiction tax authority where offshore; the registration must be completed within 21 business days of the date the obligation to register arises

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A South African (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person subject to corporate income tax (CIT) at 27% (with a reduced rate of 21% for small business corporations meeting the turnover and shareholding criteria under section 12E of the Income Tax Act); dividends are subject to a dividend tax at 20% on distribution; capital gains are taxed at an effective CIT rate of 21.6% for the corporate (inclusion rate 80%); the entity does not automatically qualify for pass-through treatment; however, the entity may be treated as a 'controlled foreign company' (CFC) if it is a foreign-resident subsidiary of a South African resident, the ETC regime of SARS will attribute net income to the resident shareholder; section 64C re-characterisation relief applies where the CFC is a resident of a list of listed countries (including South Africa being the home jurisdiction) – the foreign business establishment (FBE) exemption under section 9D(9) applies to active trading income of a shipyard if the shipyard has a fixed place of business outside South Africa with no South African shareholder control over trading decisions; for a purely domestic SA (Pty) Ltd, no election is available to shift to pass-through or flow-through taxation; the company is taxed on its worldwide income (IFRS-based taxable income) and is subject to CFC attribution rules only for its own foreign subsidiaries

Available election forms

(1) 'Check-the-box' equivalent: South Africa does not have a functional currency check-the-box election; however, a 'headquarters company' election under section 9I allows a South African resident company to elect to be treated as a designated group company for CFC relief, thereby exempting certain foreign dividends and interest from taxation; (2) Hybrid entity election: a foreign-incorporated entity may file an election under section 1(C) of the Income Tax Act to be treated as a South African resident if it is managed and controlled in South Africa; the 'place of effective management' test governs; (3) Small business corporation (SBC) election under section 12E: an election to apply the graduated SBC CIT rates (0% on first R95,750; 7% on next R364,250; 21% on the balance up to R550,000) allowed for a (Pty) Ltd with gross income under R20 million and less than 50% of income from investment or personal service; (4) Turnover tax election under Sixth Schedule for micro businesses with turnover under R1 million, available for non-manufacturing service companies but not available for a shipyard (shipbuilding is excluded as it falls under 'professional services' or 'manufacturing' for turnover tax purposes) – therefore a shipyard (Pty) Ltd will not be eligible for turnover tax; (5) VAT group election under section 50 of the VAT Act allows a group of companies (up to 20 members) to apply to SARS for a single VAT registration where the entities are part of the same economic unit; the election will result in a consolidated VAT return (VAT 201) and no VAT charged on intra-group supplies; (6) Cross-border branch election: a resident company with a foreign permanent establishment can elect to exclude the PE's attributable profits under section 9D(9A) where a 'foreign business establishment' is present, avoiding double taxation via an exemption election instead of a credit

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration where taxable supplies (supplies made in the course of an enterprise) exceed ZAR 1,000,000 (one million rand) in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted if projected taxable supplies for the next 12 months exceed ZAR 50,000 (R50,000) but the practical minimum for a shipyard is ZAR 1 million; a non-resident supplier is required to register if it makes any supply connected with a 'South African enterprise' and total taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000; if the shipyard only exports ships (zero-rated supplies), zero-rated supplies count toward the R1 million threshold for mandatory registration, but the VAT Act permits a person whose supplies are all zero-rated to apply for a waiver from registration under section 23(3) if the person's taxable supplies consist exclusively of zero-rated supplies; nevertheless, registration is advisable for input VAT recovery on export production costs

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of movable goods (including a newly constructed ocean container vessel) from South Africa to a recipient outside South Africa, where the goods are exported by the supplier or at the supplier's direction within 90 days of the time of supply (or such longer period as SARS may allow on good cause), is zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act. The shipyard must retain proof of export (customs declaration) and proof of shipment. In the case of a ship sold, the supply constitutes a 'supply of goods' not a supply of services if title passes and the ship is physically exported by the buyer or seller. If the vessel is delivered in South Africa to a non-resident who will sail it out of South African waters, the export may be zero-rated if the ship is exported on or before the date of departure from South Africa, and the following documents are retained: (i) a commercial invoice; (ii) the bill of entry (SAD500) showing the export; (iii) the bill of lading or certificate of the Master/Port Authority confirming departure; (iv) a letter from the non-resident confirming the vessel left South Africa. Offshore services (shipbuilding engineering, design, installation, repair) supplied to a non-resident client may be zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) – 'services for moveable property that is situated in South Africa at the time of supply and is subsequently exported' – provided the vessel is a moveable and exported on or before the due date for the VAT return of the period of supply. Zero-rating of a supply of a ship to a non-resident that is subsequently registered or used as a coastal vessel in South Africa is disallowed. VAT refunds (input claims) on the costs of construction are recoverable to the extent that outputs are zero-rated or taxable at the standard rate; the refund is paid by SARS on a monthly VAT return (VAT201) within 21 business days from the date of submission if all substantiation is complete; SARS may suspend a refund if the exporter has a material audit finding or risk indicator, requiring an alternative bank guarantee for up to 100% of the refund amount for up to 18 months

  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Certificate of Vessel Registration and Port of Departure Clearing Certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange (FX) confirmation showing foreign-sourced payment; for exports, a stamped Commercial Invoice and Packing List are also required; a Free on Board (FOB) or Cost, Insurance, Freight (CIF) invoice

South Africa does not currently mandate a continuous transaction control (CTC) real-time fiscal clearance system (such as e-invoicing with government API clearance). However, SARS mandates the following e-invoicing and digital record requirements: (1) eFiling is the mandatory electronic channel for VAT return submission (VAT201) and customs declarations via the SARS Customs EDI / eSIE (Electronic Single Interface) system; (2) VAT invoices must be issued in an electronically authenticated format that satisfies Section 20(4) of the VAT Act – the invoice must contain all prescribed particulars: supplier name, VAT registration number, invoice date, description of goods/services, consideration (including VAT), and the wordings 'tax invoice'; (3) SARS accepts XML/UBL (Ubl 2.1) format e-invoices; an API integration via the SARS eFiling Partner API is available for large enterprise or service providers; there is no requirement for digital stamping or digital signatures with a government-issued certificate, but the use of an SEID (SARS Electronic ID) is mandatory for company representatives; (4) Real-time fiscal clearance is not operative for B2B VAT but is mandatory for the SARS Customs National Clearance System – all export shipments must obtain an electronic customs clearance declaration (SAD500) through the eSIE portal before departure from a South African port; SAD500 must be endorsed by the South African Revenue Service's Customs department (SARS Customs) prior to loading on an oceangoing vessel; SARS is in a phased rollout of e-invoicing ('e-Invoicing by SARS'), expected to mandate structured e-invoicing (PEPPOL) for large taxpayers from 2025; for the shipyard, the issuance of VAT tax invoices in compliance with section 20 is sufficient; no additional real-time clearance of tax invoices is currently required

SARS may, within a period of 5 years from the date of the relevant VAT return (extended to 5 years for fraudulent misrepresentation and unlimited time in the case of concealment), perform a zero-rating audit on the exporter. If SARS determines that the zero-rating was applied incorrectly or that the goods were not exported within the allowed time frame, the following consequences apply: (1) the exporter is liable to account for output VAT at the standard rate (15%) on the original supply amount plus interest at the prescribed rate (currently the SARB repo rate + 7% per annum, compounded monthly); (2) an understatement penalty under Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 is payable – the 'understatement penalty' ranges from 10% (if no reasonable grounds for the incorrect position) up to 100% of the excluded tax for 'intentional tax evasion'; in addition, a penal 'substantial understatement' penalty of 50% of the VAT avoided applies if the understatement exceeds the greater of 5% of the correct tax amount or R1 million; (3) criminal charges for fraudulent claim of zero-rating or refund under section 78 of the Income Tax Act and section 58 of the VAT Act may be laid – upon conviction, a fine of up to ZAR 5 million or imprisonment of up to 5 years; (4) a 'clawback' provision applies under section 16(2) of the VAT Act: VAT that was originally claimed as an input tax credit on the export production costs may be recaptured if a subsequent event shows that the vessel remained in South Africa or was not exported, requiring the shipyard to pay output VAT equal to the input VAT previously claimed; this reclassification is treated as a separate taxable supply on the date of the recapture event; (5) the exporter may also be blocked from receiving future VAT refunds via a 'withholding of refund' until a bank guarantee is lodged; (6) SARS reporting of foreign-exchange misstatements to the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) of the South African Reserve Bank may trigger a currency control penalty of up to ZAR 1 million per occurrence and the loss of the exporter's approved status as an 'Authorised Dealer in Foreign Exchange' (if the shipyard trades directly in FX); failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days is an exchange-control contravention under the Exchange Control Regulations (1961) – the penalty is a monetary penalty of up to 10% of the outstanding amount, with no fixed cap; the 180-day deadline is measured from the date of shipment (the bill of lading date), not the date of zero-rating claim; any foreign currency invoice granted for an extended period (beyond 180 days) must be pre-approved by SARS/FinSurv; extra time extensions may be granted on application where the debtor is an offshore project finance structured entity and documentary proof of payment guarantee is supplied. Annual audit scrutiny thresholds are low: any refund claim exceeding ZAR 2 million triggers automatic segregation and an automated audit queue; SARS's 'Deliverables Review' programme mandates that a refund is either paid or refused within 21 business days from the date of filing, and this statutory deadline is temperately enforced. Overall, a shipyard must maintain a full longitudinal file (export submission, bill of lading, fixed FX contract, proof of repatriation) for 5 years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is a managed-float, partially convertible currency. Current-account trade (imports/exports of goods and services) is freely permitted against full documentary support, with no mandatory conversion ratio imposed on export receipts; proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa but may be retained in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with a local authorised dealer. Capital-account inflows (non-resident equity subscription, shareholder loans) require authorised-dealer filing under the Balance of Payments (BOP) reporting system with a 3:1 debt/equity back-to-back loan limitation, while outbound FDI is liberalised to a ZAR1 billion per-company annual allowance. Profit remittances are permitted on a current basis subject to SARS tax clearance, a completed CMB2/remittance instruction, and statutory withholding taxes on dividends (20%) and interest (15%), potentially reduced under applicable double tax treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no fixed percentage conversion-to-ZAR surrender requirement; however, the full 100% of export proceeds (including stage milestone payments under maritime installment LCs) must be received by a South African authorised dealer within 30 days of the date of shipment or the relevant invoice milestone. Authorised dealers may extend this timeline upon application and full retention of proceeds in a designated Foreign Currency Account is permitted without mandatory conversion. Statutory export documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs export entry SAD500) must be retained in the exporter's exchange-control file for audit and verification.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder; authorised dealers (registered commercial banks) administer day-to-day FX controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail; ancillary use only. South Africa does not currently operate a European-style Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing regime, and offshore EMIs are not authorised dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations; they therefore cannot lawfully effect ZAR settlement, execute USD/ZAR conversion, or complete SARB reporting. Using an offshore EMI as the main clearing utility would create exchange-control non-compliance and documentary gaps; the shipyard should rely on SARB-authorised banks for all statutory FX flows, with EMIs confined to non-ZAR incidental fees where a registered bank remains the compliance owner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident equity subscription must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the shipyard's designated authorised-dealer account with BOP code 3610 ('Inward Direct Investment – Equity Capital') in the payment narrative. The authorised dealer opens a corporate exchange-control file, verifies the funds against the share subscription agreement, board allotment resolution and CIPC notice of share issue, then credits proceeds to a ZAR operational account or a USD-denominated FCA as elected by the company. Release of inward funds is immediate upon documentation; the bank then files the inward BOP report and the company updates its CIPC/UBO registers.
