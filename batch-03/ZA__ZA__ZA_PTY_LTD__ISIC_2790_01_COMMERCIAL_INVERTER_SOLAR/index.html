Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains full current-account convertibility for trade, services, dividends, interest and residual payments, while the capital account remains subject to exchange-control approval by SARB FinSurv. The ZAR is a managed-floating currency; non-resident investors may inject equity and shareholder loans freely, and corresponding dividend, interest and capital-repatriation flows are approved as a matter of course when the corporate files the required Form A/declaration with its Authorised Dealer, submits a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) pin, and documents the underlying investment. Residual profit-remittance friction arises from KYC and beneficial-ownership substantiation (FICA), SARS prior-filing requirements, and the need to classify all movements in the SARB Balance-of-Payments reporting; genuine no-loop structures with clean source-of-funds are not blocked.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from date of shipment (or within the extended industry-commercial term recorded with the Authorised Dealer). There is no fixed percentage mandatory conversion into rand: since the 2021 liberalisation, exporters may credit export receipts directly to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with a South African Authorised Dealer or convert at will; proceeds may be retained offshore only where the exporter qualifies under a relaxable FinSurv ruling or the company is a wholly non-resident-owned entity operating an approved offshore FCA. Full documentary support (invoice, bill of lading, SARS Customs bill of entry, and TCS pin) must be retained by the bank for BOP/export verification.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administering the Exchange Control Regulations under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933; the Prudential Authority (PA) supervises bank licence conditions and the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) supervises market conduct of Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as primary corporate banking rail; MEDIUM as ancillary receivables-collection channel. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) are legally usable by a South African resident corporate only as non-bank payment providers; they hold no South African banking licence, cannot become an Authorised Dealer under the Exchange Control Regulations, cannot intermediate ZAR for export proceeds or capital inflows, and cannot satisfy SARB BOP reporting or FICA record-keeping at the bank level. An EMI can collect foreign-currency trade receivables abroad, but the proceeds must eventually be remitted into the onshore Authorised Dealer/FCA within the statutory timelines, exposing the company to FX conversion, sanction-screening and stranded-balance risk. In practice, use EMIs only for small receivables accelerators (e.g., marketplace collections) and never as the treasury clearing backbone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident shareholders must be pre-mapped to the company's capital table. Routing protocol: the originating bank executes a SWIFT MT103 (single foreign payment) or MT202/MT205 (bank-to-bank) to the beneficiary ZAR account of the (Pty) Ltd held with the onshore Authorised Dealer; the remittance narrative (field 70/72) must state 'Inward foreign direct investment - share subscription (s40 Companies Act 71 of 2008)' or 'Non-resident shareholder loan - external finance', the CIPC registration number of the company, and the name of the ultimate shareholder. The receiving bank converts to ZAR at the market rate (or credits the company's Foreign Currency Account), and records the inflow under SARB BOP code 3310 (direct investment - equity) or 3340 (direct-investment other capital / shareholder loan). No prior approval is required for clean equity or loans up to the external-financing threshold; but the bank completes the prescribed Form A notification, verifies the TCS pin, and requires the company to lodge a share certificate post-allotment and a CIPC securities register update within 30 days. Evidence of inbound cover must be retained for future dividend/capital remittance.Outbound supplier/trade payments route via: (i) Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for high-value raw-material and semiconductor-component imports, where the Authorised Dealer opens an irrevocable LC, takes the SARS Bill of Entry and transport documents, and settles the beneficiary bank via MT103/MT202 on negotiation; (ii) Open-account SWIFT MT103 disbursements for routine component purchases, with debit of the ZAR account, spot USD/EUR allocation, and the foreign payment backed by the invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, and importer/exporter code; (iii) Export receivables are recovered through the company's advising/confirming bank under the incoming LC or as MT103 trade proceeds, satisfying the 180-day receipt rule and FCA routing; (iv) For royalty/licence and service payments to offshore licensors, the payment request must attach a valid SARS TCS pin, the relevant tax deduction/withholding documentation and the underlying technology/licence agreement before release. Timely settlement triggers: the bank captures the BOP 'imports of goods/services' classification, performs FICA sanctions screening, and retains the invoice and customs evidence for anti-money-laundering audit.Outright deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) in USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR and GBP/ZAR for committed export and import trade exposures, subject to SARB transactional-cover requirements and settlement within the FEC lifespanVanilla FX call/put options on underlying invoiced trade flows (USD/EUR) for protection on contingently tendered sales and delayed collectionsCross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) and FX swaps for the finance and liquidity alignment of USD or EUR shareholder loans and equipment trade finance facilitiesOnshore deliverable currency swaps (ZAR against USD/EUR) executed with an Authorised Dealer under the SARB capital-flow hedging regimeNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/ZAR are not standard permissible onshore hedging: a resident corporate may enter them only with a bespoke SARB/FinSurv exemption because the onshore market mandate requires physical delivery of ZAR; cross-border NDFs are available only to non-residents
