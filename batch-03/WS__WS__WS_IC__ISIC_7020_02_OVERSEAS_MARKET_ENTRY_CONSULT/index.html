Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Ministry for Revenue – Online Services portal (www.sra.gov.ws)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Samoa International Company (IC) does not automatically obtain a general income TIN because the IC is statutorily tax-exempt under the International Companies Act 2012. The IC must be registered with the Samoa Registrar of Companies, obtaining a Registry/IC number and annual government fee receipt. If the IC nevertheless undertakes a taxable activity requiring VAGST registration, it must submit Form VAGST-REG to SRA, provide constitutional documents, director and shareholder identification, proof of the registered office in Samoa, and bank account details; SRA then issues a VAGST registration number and tax compliance certificate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to: (1) goods physically exported from Samoa within the prescribed time, (2) services supplied directly to a non-resident person who is outside Samoa at the time supply occurs, (3) international transport or ancillary services, and (4) supplies to a non-resident in a duty-free or offshore zone. The claimant must be a VAGST-registered person and must hold contemporaneous documentation proving export, overseas receipt, and the non-resident status of the customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SRA Customs Form CED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange record
  • VAGST Tax Invoice
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List

No real-time fiscal clearance or national e-invoicing API/digital stamping regime exists in Samoa. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue standard tax invoices containing the supplier's SRA VAGST registration number, and retain all source records for at least 7 years for audit and SRA accessibility.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims are subject to SRA audit, reassessment, full clawback of the claimed VAT credit, and imposition of late payment penalties and statutory interest. Civil penalties may reach up to 100% of the tax shortfall, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud carries substantial fines and imprisonment under the VAGST Act 2005 and the Tax Administration Act 2012. SRA may also cancel VAGST registration of repeat offenders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a partially convertible, managed-float currency (Samoan Tala, WST). Current-account transactions are freely convertible for documented commercial flows through licensed authorized dealers; capital-account transactions require Central Bank approval unless a statutory exemption applies. A Samoa International Company (IC) is treated as a non-resident enterprise for exchange-control purposes: foreign-currency receipts may be freely credited, held and remitted offshore without CBS approval in USD, AUD, NZD or EUR, and profit or dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted when paid out of wholly foreign-currency reserves. The residual convertibility hurdles are (i) conversion of external earnings into WST and (ii) any payment to a Samoan resident, each requiring documentary substantiation and CBS balance-of-payments reporting through the authorized dealer. The IC is statutorily barred from conducting any domestic Samoan business, thereby insulating its treasury flows from the domestic convertibility regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods-export statutory baseline under the Exchange Control Regulations 1971: payment for exports must be received within 180 days of shipment; the exporter must offer the foreign-currency proceeds to an authorized dealer within one month of receipt; and the default surrender or conversion ratio is 100% unless the CBS has granted a general or specific working-balance retention exemption. For the WS_IC market-entry consultancy model, service-export receivables are 'invisible' foreign-currency receipts payable by non-residents; because the IC holds a designated foreign-currency account, performs no domestic trade, and is outside the Samoan resident economy, the proceeds are exempt from compulsory surrender and may be retained indefinitely in USD, netted against offshore operating costs, or voluntarily converted to WST. Any WST conversion nevertheless creates a controlled balance that cannot be disbursed to a Samoan resident without an underlying service contract and an authorized-dealer CBS declaration.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), acting as the exchange-control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1970 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1971, administered through licensed authorized dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High. Wise B2B and similarly regulated offshore EMIs will ordinarily onboard a Samoa-registered International Company when the UBO structure is transparent and the company demonstrates genuine non-resident activity through executed market-entry consultancy contracts. Legal constraints: (i) Samoa imposes no licensing requirement or prohibition on an IC maintaining a settlement account at an offshore EMI, but the EMI must not hold itself out as a Samoan authorized dealer, must not execute WST-denominated payments, and must respond to lawful Samoan FIU information requests; (ii) the EMI balance is an 'external asset' that remains permissible under the Exchange Control Act only where no Samoan-resident transaction is settled through it; (iii) EMIs routinely reject a bare registered-agent address in Apia as insufficient substantive proof of place of business, so the IC should maintain a verifiable serviced-office lease or sublease, a physical operating address in the target market, and a bank reference from the CBS-licensed account. Recommended structure: Wise B2B as the daily collection and disbursement rail for sub-USD 100,000 consultancy fees, with the BSP Samoa or SCB account as the formal clearing, capital and escalation account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the non-resident investor wires via SWIFT MT103, or MT202 COV for an interbank-tiered credit, to the IC's USD Operational Account at BSP Samoa or the SCB Capital Escrow Account. The wire must carry ISO 20022 purpose code INVS (Investment) and tag 70 free text reading 'Share subscription - International Company - International Companies Act 1987 - external capital account'. The authorized dealer records the credit as a CBS balance-of-payments item 'Direct investment - equity injection to non-resident enterprise' and no prior CBS approval is required because the IC is exchange-control-exempt for external foreign-currency credits. The bank's verification pack comprises: (i) the parent or shareholder board resolution authorizing the investment; (ii) the IC board resolution allotting shares and confirming that no minimum capital requirement applies; (iii) an executed share subscription agreement; (iv) a UBO declaration for all 10% or greater owners; and (v) source-of-funds evidence where the credit exceeds USD 100,000. Where the bank places the capital in escrow pending source verification, release into the operational account occurs upon submission of the subscription agreement and completion of CBS reporting.For operating disbursements, including subcontractor fees, licensing agency payments, marketing and travel, use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with the applicable ISO 20022 purpose code, principally FEEX for professional service fees, executed directly from the USD operating account without CBS approval. Large project settlements under a market-entry mandate that includes procurement logistics are cleared through a UCP600-compliant Commercial Letter of Credit or an ICC bank payment obligation issued via the BSP Samoa trade desk. Interbank liquidity concentration between the Wise B2B settlement pool and the CBS-licensed BSP account is effected through SWIFT MT202 COV cover payments. WST conversion for payments to Samoan residents requires the dealer to complete the CBS declaration and, where applicable, the authorized-dealer application form supported by the underlying service contract.Onshore deliverable USD/WST forward contracts with an authorized dealer (BSP Samoa or SCB), available solely against documented current-account exposure evidenced by signed consultancy contracts, with a maximum tenor of 12 months.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/WST executed with a licensed non-resident bank; permissible for the WS_IC because settlement is effected entirely offshore with no onshore WST movement, requiring only CBS notification through the dealer's balance-of-payments reporting.Cross-currency swaps between USD and WST with an authorized dealer, subject to prior CBS exchange-control approval and verified matching of the underlying receivables or payables.

Late filing attracts an immediate registry penalty of WST 200 for the first default, with a daily surcharge of WST 10 for each day of continued non-compliance. Persistent non-filing for three months or more triggers suspension of the IC's good standing; after six months of default the Registrar may strike the company off the International Companies Register, causing asset vesting and legal disability. Reinstatement is conditioned on payment of all arrear fees, penalties, and a reinstatement fee.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory WST filing threshold has been established for exempt International Companies; they are not required to prepare Local File or Master File documentation because they have zero Samoan taxable income. For taxable Samoa resident companies, the Minister of Revenue retains discretionary authority to demand contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where cross-border related-party transactions exceed commercially material levels; the arm's length principle applies under the Income Tax Act 2012.

Economic substance scope: The Samoa International Finance Authority administers economic substance requirements for International Companies. A Samoa IC deriving income from relevant activities, which may include market entry consultancy, service-centre operations, headquarters functions, and intellectual property holding, must demonstrate substantial economic presence in Samoa: a physical office or premises, a minimum complement of employees, sufficient operating expenditure, and board management and decision-making conducted in Samoa. Pure consultancy invoicing without local substance is disallowed; arrangement with zero substance is rebuttably presumed to be a shell company subject to SIFA investigation, information exchange, and deregistration risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abuse of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Retroactive revocation of the IC tax exemption, assessment of domestic corporate income tax at 27% on all income derived, late payment interest, and penalties of up to 200% of the tax avoided. The company may be struck off by the Registrar, and the registered agent may lose its SIFA licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are exposed to piercing of the corporate veil: civil restitution orders for the tax shortfall, asset tracing, travel bans, disqualification from holding directorship in Samoa, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud carrying imprisonment of up to seven years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty under the International Companies Act and SIFA practice of up to WST 100,000; compulsory deregistration and dissolution; referral to the Samoa exchange of information competence for automatic exchange of information under OECD standards.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for false substance declarations; they may be ordered to reimburse regulatory costs, banned from managing any Samoa company for up to 10 years, and subjected to travel restrictions pending investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under the Money Laundering and Prevention of Terrorism Act with fines up to WST 2,000,000 and seizure/freezing of bank accounts; SIFA suspension or revocation of the registered agent's licence; corporate dissolution for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors face direct criminal indictment, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans during prosecution, and imprisonment of up to seven years for money laundering; civil liability to third parties for loss arising from wilful blindness.
Fraudulent Filings and False Annual Return Declarations

Statutory sanction: Statutory offence with monetary penalties per offence and a daily fine for continuing false certification; the Registry may cancel registration with immediate effect, invalidate the corporate structure, and refer the matter to the Office of the Attorney General for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for fraudulent trading; courts may order restitution and compensation to the Registry or injured parties, impose travel bans until restitution is satisfied, and impose custodial sentences of up to five years for perjury in registry filings.
