Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a private (Pty) Ltd; shares must be authorised under the MOI and consideration per s.40 of the Companies Act must satisfy the solvency and liquidity test at allotment; no escrow deposit or pre-incorporation bank account is mandated by South African law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by Municipal Emergency Services under SANS 10400-T and the National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act 103 of 1977; structural certification of racking and storage loads (SANS 10160); electrical Certificate of Compliance (SANS 10142); Major Hazard Installation notification and HAZOP risk assessment under the OHS Act 85 of 1993 and the Major Hazard Installation Regulations, 2021; Hazardous Chemical Agents compliance (assessment, labelling, medical surveillance) under the HCS Regulations, 2021; Class 3 flammable liquid segregation; occupational clearance attesting the premises are fit for bonded wholesale trade operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — national tax and customs administration; eFiling portal at sarsefiling.co.za; customs electronic declarations via SARS Customs systems.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Appoint a resident public officer/representative and register the (Pty) Ltd through SARS eFiling to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number (ITR). Step 2: Register for VAT using VAT101 once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 or elect voluntary registration where turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000. Step 3: Register as a Customs/Excise client under the Customs Control Act 31 of 2014 and Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 to obtain a customs code and bonded warehouse licence for wine/spirits duty suspension. Step 4: Register as an employer for PAYE, UIF, SDL and Skills Development Levy if staff are engaged. Step 5: Record non-resident shareholding; the (Pty) Ltd remains a South African resident taxpayer and non-resident shareholders are subject to Dividends Withholding Tax at source.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by domestic income tax law. Relevant elections for a qualifying (Pty) Ltd include: Small Business Corporation status (s 12E) for graduated corporate rates; micro business turnover tax election (Sixth Schedule); VAT group registration election (s 50A VAT Act) for connected persons; hybrid instrument and hybrid mismatch adjustments under s 8E, 8F, 8EA and s 8FA–8L; and exchange rate base cost elections under s 24I. Binding private rulings can be obtained under s 76 Tax Administration Act for uncertainty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration is triggered where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 but do not exceed ZAR 1,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for zero-rating under s 11(1)(a) VAT Act where the supplier supplies goods for export and the goods are physically removed from South Africa within the prescribed period, normally 90 days from date of supply, subject to SARS extension. Bonded wine/spirits zero-rate only where the goods are entered for export from the bonded warehouse under customs/excise control and the duty suspension is terminated on lawful export. Services qualify under s 11(2) where physically rendered outside South Africa or supplied to non-residents and not used in South Africa, subject to statutory conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) accepted by SARS Customs evidencing entry for export from the bonded warehouse
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or road manifest proving physical departure of the goods from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange record confirming export proceeds received in foreign currency

South Africa does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping framework. VAT tax invoices must comply with s 20 VAT Act requirements for authenticity, integrity and legibility, and electronic invoices are accepted provided complete records are retained for five years. SARS eFiling mandates periodic VAT201 returns, while customs and excise movements require electronic SAD500 export declarations and bonded warehouse stock/transfer reporting through SARS Customs systems. Bonded wine/spirits removals must occur under excise duty control before zero-rated export.

SARS audits zero-rated export refunds before payment and may re-characterise the supply as taxable at 15% where export proof is deficient or the statutory export period is breached, raising a retrospective VAT assessment. Additional liabilities include understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act of up to 200% for intentional non-disclosure, interest at the prescribed rate, and potential criminal prosecution for fraudulent zero-rating claims. Bonded warehouse operators also face forfeiture of goods, cancellation of the bond licence, and retrospective excise duty clawback where wine/spirits are not lawfully exported.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is freely convertible for current account transactions, with only documentary reporting obligations. Capital account inflows and outflows are generally permitted but subject to exchange-control reporting, prescribed limits, and Authorised Dealer intervention. Non-resident profit remittances are permitted upon submission of a SARS tax clearance/pin, B-form or equivalent reporting, proof of lawful dividend declaration, and compliance with the Companies Act, 2008; no prior FinSurv approval is required for ordinary dividend remittances below the current reporting threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 4, export proceeds must be received by an Authorised Dealer in South Africa within 180 days from the date of export, unless an extension is granted in writing. All export proceeds must be repatriated to and credited in the exporter's local foreign-currency account maintained with a South African Authorised Dealer; however, there is no statutory requirement to convert the proceeds into ZAR. Funds may be held, sold, or applied in accordance with the Authorised Dealer's approved foreign-currency account limits.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and Authorised Dealers under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMI licences do not confer a direct right to conduct banking business in South Africa, as deposit-taking and cross-border payment account services are reserved to institutions licensed under the Banks Act, 1990. Legitimate fintechs can only function through partnership/white-label arrangements with a South African Authorised Dealer; full account opening and transaction clearing must ultimately flow through a domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment must be routed through a designated inward capital account opened with a South African Authorised Dealer. The transfer should be made via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 with a reference to inward investment seasoning and the reporting purpose code; the Authorised Dealer will record the transaction for exchange-control statistical purposes and issue an inward notification acknowledgement (B-form/IMF balance-of-payments reporting). Supporting documents include board resolutions, subscription/financing agreement, UBO identification, and SARS tax clearance.Treasury clears bonded-warehouse purchases, excise settlements, and foreign supplier payments through: SWIFT MT103/MT103 STP for direct cross-border wires; commercial letters of credit issued by a tier-1 South African bank under UCP600; and ZAR-denominated local payments via SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement). For the bonded-warehouse model, domestic excise and customs payments are effected by EFT/RTGS in ZAR, while offshore wine and spirits purchases are settled via LC or MT103 with full trade-document compliance.Deliverable forward exchange contracts (FECs) with a licensed Authorised Dealer for firm trade receivables and payablesCross-currency swaps with an Authorised Dealer for medium-term balance-sheet exposure linked to shareholder funding or dividend commitmentsVanilla currency options (within South African option regulations) for selective protection of bonded procurement contractsOffshore NDFs are not permissible for South African resident entities unless specific SARB approval is obtained and a real trade or interest-rate exposure is demonstrated
