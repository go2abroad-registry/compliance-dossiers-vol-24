Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), established under the South African Revenue Service Act 34 of 1997; electronic portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za) and national website (https://www.sars.gov.za).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Proprietary Limited company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), CIPC automatically transmits the corporate record to SARS, which allocates a SARS Income Tax Reference Number; the taxpayer must then activate the eFiling profile within 21 business days of incorporation and maintain registration details (public officer, directors, address, beneficial ownership) under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. VAT registration (VAT 101 application via eFiling) is mandatory within 21 days of the business exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 taxable turnover in any rolling twelve-month period and is available voluntarily from ZAR 50,000. A foreign-owned Pty Ltd must obtain SARS Customs client registration (Customs Code) for lodging export declarations and, when no South African resident director holds office, appoint a SARS-recognised resident tax representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box pass-through election; companies are taxed at entity level at the 27% corporate rate. Available statutory elections: (i) Section 12E Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 election to be taxed as a Small Business Corporation (qualifying if annual turnover does not exceed ZAR 20 million); (ii) Section 41(1A) group taxation election allowing consolidated tax treatment of intra-group transactions; (iii) Section 50(1)(b) VAT Act 89 of 1991 application for VAT group registration; (iv) voluntary VAT registration from ZAR 50,000 turnover; (v) hybrid instrument and hybrid entity mismatch rules under Sections 8F, 8FA, 23M and 25B apply automatically as anti-avoidance measures and do not permit elective mismatch treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 mandatory registration threshold (taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period); ZAR 50,000 voluntary registration threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of new CNC five-axis milling centers are zero-rated under Section 11(1)(a) VAT Act 89 of 1991 where: (i) the supplier exports the goods under a valid SAD500 customs declaration; (ii) the goods are physically delivered to a non-resident recipient outside the Republic; (iii) export occurs within the prescribed period (SARS practice treats 90 days from time of supply as standard; extended periods require SARS approval); (iv) the supplier retains documentary proof of export; and (v) the goods are not consumed or applied to taxable supplies in South Africa. Offshore engineering, installation, and maintenance services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under Section 11(2)(k) provided the services are not directly connected to movable or immovable property situated in South Africa and are not supplied to a person present in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 single administrative document lodged with SARS Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill) evidencing physical export of the machine from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Authorised Dealer bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List
  • Proof of non-resident purchaser status (buyer VAT/GST/EIN certificate and End-User Certificate where applicable)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or government digital stamping regime is presently enforced in South Africa. The SARS eInvoicing framework is under phased implementation; structured e-invoices (XML/PDF) transmitted via the SARS eFiling API must satisfy the Section 20 VAT Act invoice requirements, including supplier VAT registration number, invoice serial number, date, description, quantity, consideration exclusive of VAT, VAT amount, and recipient identification. SARS data-matching aligns SAD500 customs export declarations and bank exchange-control repatriation records with VAT 201 returns to detect false export zero-rating claims.

SARS may disallow the zero-rating and raise an output VAT assessment under Section 7(1)(a) VAT Act with additional tax of up to 100% under Sections 59 and 60 VAT Act, plus statutory interest at 10% per annum applied monthly to unpaid VAT. Under Section 234 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, understatement penalties escalate from 10% of the shortfall (standard case) up to 150% for intentional tax evasion. Fraudulent export claims additionally expose the exporter to criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to 10 years and fines under Chapter 17 TAA, debarment of the tax practitioner, suspension of the exporter's SARS Customs code, and retrospective reassessment of up to five prior tax years, with no time limitation where fraud or misrepresentation is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions (goods, services, dividends, interest, royalties) through an Authorised Dealer, subject to documentary verification, Form P/Form A procedures where applicable, and SARB Balance of Payments (BOP) coding. Capital inflows into a resident (Pty) Ltd are unrestricted: non-resident equity subscriptions may be credited directly via the banking system, with the receiving bank recording the inward investment declaration on Form B and the foreign liability on Form F for FinSurv; no prior exchange-control approval is required for ordinary equity investment in a non-restricted sector. Capital-account restrictions bind mainly outflows: residents require FinSurv approval for newly created outward investments, and all outward transfers above ZAR 10 million cumulative in any rolling three-year period require a SARS Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC). Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permissible without FinSurv approval where the original investment was introduced as approved funds (documented Form B trail), the dividend is declared from distributable reserves, the solvency and liquidity test under the Companies Act is satisfied, and dividends tax (20% statutory, reduced under applicable DTAAs) is withheld and paid to SARS. No statutory minimum capital applies to a (Pty) Ltd, but banks underwriting machinery-manufacturing, strategic-item profiles will expect adequate capitalisation commensurate with ISIC 2822 business risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds for goods shipped from South Africa must be introduced into the country through an Authorised Dealer within 180 calendar days from the bill of lading or airway bill date (amended SARB/SARS rule effective 1 January 2021), and must be surrendered within 30 days of receipt. Surrender means sale of the foreign currency to the Authorised Dealer for ZAR or crediting of the full amount to a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with that Authorised Dealer; no partial conversion quota exists, and the export proceeds are subject to 100% repatriation. Where proceeds are held in an approved FCA, no mandatory conversion into ZAR is imposed, but the FCA may only be used for permitted trade-current payments under the dealer's oversight. The exporter is required to notify the Authorised Dealer of the shipment and to provide the customs SAD500 export declaration reference; any non-return of proceeds is reportable to FinSurv and may attract administrative penalties under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), exercising delegated powers under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; supported by the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) for AML/CFT supervision and SARS Customs for trade-flow declarations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscription is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the company's designated Capital Escrow Account at a South African Authorised Dealer. The remittance narrative must cite the company's CIPC registration number, the share-subscription agreement reference, and the corporate purpose; the receiving dealer applies the SARB Balance of Payments classification for inward direct investment (equity) and records the transaction on Form B (inward investment declaration) and Form F (foreign liabilities). Source-of-wealth documentation is verified, SWIFT sanctions screening and FICA CDD are applied, and no FinSurv pre-approval is required for a non-restricted-sector machinery-manufacturing entity. Funds credited in this manner are tagged as approved funds and may later be repatriated as share-capital reduction, dividend, or liquidation proceeds subject to normal Form P procedures and tax clearance.Trade payables for imported castings, spindles, CNC controllers, servo drives, tooling, and software are settled either (i) by SWIFT MT103 through the company's Operational ZAR or Foreign Currency Account at the Authorised Dealer, accompanied by the SAD500 customs entry, commercial invoice, and Form A where conversion from ZAR is required; or (ii) by an irrevocable Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit issued by the Authorised Dealer via SWIFT MT700, payable at sight or usance against presentation of the bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, inspection certificate, and any End-User Certificate required for controlled goods.
