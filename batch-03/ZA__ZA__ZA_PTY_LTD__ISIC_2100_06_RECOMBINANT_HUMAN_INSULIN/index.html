Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

South African law prescribes no minimum paid-up capital or escrow account for a (Pty) Ltd; at least one incorporator must subscribe for at least one share, and the MOI must be signed. Foreign currency subscriptions require Exchange Control approval from the Financial Surveillance Department (South African Reserve Bank), together with a funds-source declaration, but no capital escrow is mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: Certificate of Compliance from the Municipal Fire and Rescue Services, with annual smoke and alarm inspections; structural clearance: approved building plans, structural load assessment for bioreactors and HVAC plant, and an Occupancy Certificate from the Municipal Building Control; occupational clearance: registration with the Department of Employment and Labour under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, a baseline risk assessment, a mandatory safety audit, and a Compensation Fund letter of good standing.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), via the SARS eFiling Portal (https://www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned or resident Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd entity must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Upon incorporation, the company is automatically registered with SARS for income tax and receives an income tax reference number. For VAT, the company separately applies for VAT registration through a SARS branch office or via eFiling (using the VAT101 form), which generates the VAT registration number. Where the company is a foreign subsidiary, the parent must provide certified incorporation documents, a certificate of residency, and information regarding beneficial ownership, with documents translated into English. A foreign company operating in South Africa without local incorporation must register as an external company with CIPC and obtain a SARS income tax reference number, a VAT number (as applicable), an PAYE number (if employing staff), and a customs code for import/export activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned or resident Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd entity must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Upon incorporation, the company is automatically registered with SARS for income tax and receives an income tax reference number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or similar entity classification elections are available under South African tax law. A South African private company is irrevocably taxed as a resident corporate entity at the standard corporate income tax rate. No hybrid mismatch election exists for ordinary Pty Ltd entities; however, the anti-hybrid rules under section 9D and related provisions apply to foreign hybrid instruments and income inclusions, and entities may elect to be treated as transparent only in limited statutorily prescribed situations, such as certain partnership structures, which do not apply to the standard Pty Ltd form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration applies when taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed ZAR 1 million in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An export zero-rating is available under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act for goods exported from South Africa by the supplier in the ordinary course of the supplier's enterprise, provided the goods are exported within the prescribed period and the supplier retains proof of exportation. Services supplied to a non-resident are generally zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) when the services are supplied to a person who is not resident in South Africa, the services are physically performed outside South Africa or are in respect of movable property located outside South Africa, and the services are not connected to land or property in South Africa. For recombinant human insulin products, zero-rating additionally depends on disclosure to the South African Revenue Service of the exports in the company's VAT return, retention of the customs export declaration, and valid evidence that the pharmaceutical cargo left the common customs area of the Southern African Customs Union and entered the export market.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500/Douane 500)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing actual physical export
  • Ocean/Air carrier transport documents including proof of loading at a South African customs port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • SWIFT MT103 or bank statement showing payment from the foreign customer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list corresponding to goods shipped
  • SAHPRA export permit/MA holder authorisation in the case of regulated insulin products
  • SARS VAT201 export declaration with the export zero-rating disclosure
  • Certificate of origin (if preferential duty treatment is sought in the destination market)

SARS does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. Tax invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under section 20 of the VAT Act, and electronic tax invoices are recognised as valid provided they are secure and unalterable. SARS offers an eInvoicing Application Programming Interface (API) and an eInvoicing solution as a voluntary channel through eFiling, but no government pre-clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control requirement is currently applicable. Imports and exports are cleared electronically through the SARS Customs Single Window, with mandatory submission of customs declarations via the eFiling customs interface or approved EDI providers. For pharmaceutical inputs and finished insulin products, customs clearance also must align with licensing requirements of the South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA).

SARS scrutinises export zero-rating claims rigorously through physical examination of shipping documents, foreign-exchange controlling documentation, and consignment traceability. Failure to prove actual exportation results in the VAT being a tax debt immediately repayable to SARS. Under the VAT Act, the taxpayer may be assessed for under-declared tax, an additional penalty of up to 100% of the tax under-declared, and a monthly late payment penalty; fraudulent claims constitute a criminal offence and may attract up to 24 months' imprisonment. Under the Tax Administration Act, administrative non-compliance penalties of up to 10% of the assessed amount may be imposed, and SARS may issue additional assessments within the extended prescription period of five years for a failure to disclose tax information. Exchange-control violations in relation to unauthorised export-proceeds retention can result in further administrative fines and the compulsory repatriation and possible loss of export-incentive benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a floating legal currency with fully market-determined exchange rates. Current account payments for goods, services, royalties and technical fees are generally permitted through an SARB authorised dealer with underlying supporting documentation. Capital account inflows and outflows involving non-residents, including equity investment, shareholder loans and the acquisition of South African assets, remain subject to SARB FinSurv exchange control monitoring and approval thresholds. Profit remittances by way of dividends to non-resident shareholders may be effected through an authorised dealer without prior exchange control approval on condition that the distribution is declared, funded from after-tax distributable reserves, reported in the company's exchange control records, and subject to South African dividend withholding tax at the statutory rate of 20%.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be received in South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless a longer period has been formally approved by SARB FinSurv. The full value of export proceeds must be surrendered to an authorised dealer for conversion into rand, with a mandatory conversion ratio of 100%, unless a specific foreign currency retention facility or exemption has been granted. Unauthorised retention of export receipts in offshore accounts is prohibited under South African exchange control law.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) in administering the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary banking use. Offshore electronic money institutions and digital EMI platforms are not SARB authorised dealers in foreign exchange, cannot hold a South African banking licence under the Banks Act, 1990, and cannot participate in the local ZAR clearing and settlement system. Their use is therefore restricted to ancillary cross-border FX collection and payment initiation; all regulatory clearing, liquidity management and exchange control reporting must remain with a domestic licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital must be routed to the South African company through an SARB authorised dealer using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for large aggregated capital flows. The remittance must carry a clear payment narration identifying the transaction as inward direct investment into the manufacturing subsidiary. The receiving bank will undertake FICA and exchange control verification, complete the prescribed FinSurv inward transfer reporting and the balance-of-payments purpose classification for direct investment equity or shareholder subordinated debt, and where necessary record the non-resident shareholder's investment in the exporter/issuer's exchange control records. Funds should initially be credited to a dedicated capital escrow account and only thereafter transferred to the operational ZAR account once the underlying share issue or loan registration is complete and the beneficial ownership and source-of-funds documentation has been accepted.Trade settlements for imported API, excipients, consumables and foreign equipment should use SWIFT MT103 documentary remittances routed through the South African authorised dealer with underlying invoices, customs entries and shipment evidence retained for exchange control auditing. For sovereign health procurement and large-value medical tenders, the preferred clearing rail is an irrevocable documentary letter of credit advised or confirmed through the South African bank under UCP600 rules. All outbound current account payments must be linked to genuine trade or service obligations and supported by SARS customs records where imported goods are involved.Forward exchange contracts (forwards) with an SARB authorised dealer for hedging committed export receivables and import payables denominated in USD, EUR or GBP.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for ZAR crosses where physical ZAR settlement is impractical and the hedge is matched to an underlying trade or capital exposure.Cross-currency swaps for managing long-term debt service and shareholder loan refinancing in hard currency.Currency options with economically documented underlying exposure and maturity aligned to the trade cycle.
