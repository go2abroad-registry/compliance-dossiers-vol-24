Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — administered through the SARS eFiling web portal (https://efiling.sars.gov.za) and the SARS Customs eFiling system for customs and export declarations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-controlled Pty Ltd must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain its company registration number. The entity must then register as a South African taxpayer with SARS to obtain a SARS tax reference number, which is the statutory tax identification number for South African companies. Registration is completed via SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch, and the company must appoint a South African-resident public officer. Once the compulsory VAT threshold is met, a separate VAT registration application is made using Form VAT 101; foreign suppliers of electronic services must use the SARS foreign-vendor registration channel where their South African supplies exceed the statutory threshold. The assessed VAT vendor number and the taxpayer profile are then recorded in the SARS RAV registered-details system.

Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident and branch operations are taxed on South African source income only. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African Pty Ltd is a separate taxpaying entity subject to corporate income tax at 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident and branch operations are taxed on South African source income only. South African law does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election for proprietary limited companies, and no tax-exempt zone designation applies to die-cast scale model manufacturing.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections for a qualifying Pty Ltd include: (1) Small Business Corporation election under section 12E of the Income Tax Act where all shareholders are natural persons; (2) Micro Business Turnover Tax election under section 48 of the Income Tax Act for qualifying micro businesses with annual turnover not exceeding ZAR 1 million; (3) VAT group registration election under section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act for connected persons; (4) section 11D research and development additional deduction election; and (5) hybrid financial instrument mismatch neutralisation designations under sections 8E to 8L of the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory registration where taxable supplies exceeded or are expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary VAT registration is available once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12-month period. Foreign suppliers of electronic services supplied to South African customers must register when the value of those supplies exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Movable goods — die-cast scale automotive models — are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act when supplied to a non-resident and physically exported from South Africa to a destination outside the Republic within the prescribed export window, in practice 90 days from the date of supply or invoice, provided the vendor obtains and retains documentary proof of export. Zero-rating of related services, such as design, tooling, die-casting and tampo-printing performed offshore, may apply under section 11(2) of the VAT Act where the services are supplied to a non-resident, are not consumed in South Africa, and the non-resident is outside South Africa when the services are rendered. The vendor must receive payment in foreign currency or into a South African bank account in foreign currency, and export proceeds must be physically repatriated to South Africa within the statutory 180-day exchange-control window. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export supplies is claimable via the VAT 201 return, and a refund arises where input tax exceeds output tax.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 / Form DA500) lodged via SARS Customs eFiling evidencing physical export of the goods
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing consignment to the non-resident destination
  • Full tax invoice issued under section 20 of the VAT Act showing the zero-rated supply and the non-resident customer details
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order or sale contract evidencing the supply to the non-resident and the commercial export terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of foreign-currency payment from the non-resident within 180 days of export

South Africa does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital-stamping regime for VAT invoices. Tax invoices must satisfy the form and content requirements of section 20 of the VAT Act and must be issued within 21 days after the date of supply; electronic tax invoices are permitted. SARS has published a SARS Digital Tax Invoice specification and an eFiling API integration framework as a step toward phased mandatory e-invoicing, initially targeting large vendors. Customs export declarations are filed electronically via SARS Customs eFiling using the SAD 500 declaration and associated EDI/API interfaces.

SARS may retrospectively claw back zero-rated treatment and assess VAT at 15% plus statutory interest where the exporter fails to retain the complete documentation bundle, misses the export or 180-day foreign-currency repatriation deadline, or submits false or irregular export documentation. Under sections 222 to 226 of the Tax Administration Act, understatement penalties range from 10% to 200% of the undercharged tax depending on the taxpayer's conduct, with higher ranges for substantial understatement, negligence, and intentional tax evasion. Section 59 of the VAT Act imposes additional penalties for defective invoicing and record-keeping failures. Where fraud or material non-disclosure is established, SARS is not time-barred by the standard five-year prescription period and may raise retrospective additional assessments without limitation; serious cases may also result in criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a dual-layer convertibility framework. Current account transactions, including trade payments, service fees and dividends declared from current profits, are freely transferable through authorised dealers without prior SARB approval, subject to documentary verification and tax clearances. Capital account transactions, including share subscriptions, disinvestments, capital loan admissions and capital loan repayments, remain subject to exchange control admission and repatriation reporting rules. The ZAR is a controlled currency for regulatory purposes, and every inbound and outbound transfer is reportable to SARB via an authorised dealer. Non-resident shareholders may remit distributable profits through ordinary dividends or branch remittances, but dividends tax at 20% before treaty relief is imposed, and the paying entity must hold a tax directive/certificate and an auditor's certificate confirming realised distributable reserves. For capital disinvestment, proceeds of a share sale must be transferred through an authorised dealer and require proof of admission of the original inward transfer, such as the authorised dealer's inward remittance certificate or a FinSurv reference, to support future repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Exchange Control Manual, an exporter must ensure that the full value of exported goods is physically received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment. Proceeds may be credited to an authorised dealer-backed Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or converted to ZAR. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the mandatory conversion percentage is effectively 0% where the exporter maintains an eligible FCA and demonstrates genuine operational use for the foreign currency, while an exporter without an FCA must have 100% of export proceeds credited to its ZAR account upon receipt. Each export transaction must be reported to the authorised dealer on the relevant export form (e.g., Exporter's Declaration COT 1 or the SARB Excon/MEX matrix), and a commercial invoice with the correct HS tariff classification, proof of physical export (bill of lading/airwaybill) and evidence of customs clearance must support the receipt. If extended trade credit beyond 180 days is required, prior FinSurv approval is necessary.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) exercising delegated authority under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, read with the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, and the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 2001 (FIC Act).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds ZAR 5 million per year of assessment; Master File additionally required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds ZAR 1 billion and those transactions exceed ZAR 50 million; Country-by-Country Report mandatory where group consolidated turnover is at least ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as found in offshore financial centres. Substance requirements are, however, embedded in the South African residence test (place of effective management), the CFC attribution rules (s9D), the GAAR (s80A-80L) and the beneficial-ownership condition for treaty relief. The (Pty) Ltd must evidence physical premises, employees, board control, contracts, operational assets and decision-making in South Africa; a shell-like structure will be recharacterised and treaty benefits denied.
