Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not provide a check-the-box election under domestic law. An International Company may voluntarily elect to convert to a local company under the Companies Act 2008, thereby abandoning the International Company tax-exempt status and becoming subject to the Income Tax Ordinance 1974 and VAGST Act 2008. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available under Samoa domestic law; however, treaty-based limitation-of-benefits and anti-avoidance rules may apply to prevent artificial use of the exempt IC regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 250,000 of taxable supplies made in Samoa in any consecutive 12-month period; registration is mandatory once threshold is met, but voluntary registration is available for businesses below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Samoa in accordance with the Customs Act attract VAGST zero-rating if physically removed from Samoa within the prescribed period and supported by customs export documentation. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Samoa at the time of supply, and are not performed in Samoa or not connected with land or goods in Samoa, are zero-rated for VAGST. The remote screen-share and IoT software services delivered to offshore clients qualify as exported services only where the recipient is outside Samoa, the benefit is enjoyed outside Samoa, and the contract does not create a physical performance nexus in Samoa.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Samoa does not currently impose real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping requirements. Registered VAGST suppliers must issue tax invoices containing prescribed particulars: supplier name, VAGST registration number, date, description, quantity, price, tax amount, and total. Electronic invoices are permitted if they preserve the integrity of prescribed particulars; the Samoa Revenue Authority retains the right to audit and require digital access to invoice records.

The Commissioner may conduct an audit to verify the physical export, final destination, arm’s length price, and receipt of foreign currency. Failure to produce the mandatory customs documentation, failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days, or claiming zero-rating on supplies that are not genuinely exported triggers a full VAGST clawback at 15%, plus a late payment penalty of 10% of the unpaid tax and interest of 2% per month or part thereof. Deliberate evasion or fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to civil penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall, criminal fines not exceeding WST 100,000, and imprisonment for up to 10 years, together with permanent loss of zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoa tala (WST) is a domestically settled currency with no active offshore interbank market; current-account payments for genuine trade in services are permitted through authorised dealer banks, while capital-account transactions require CBS exchange-control approval or an authorised dealer's standing power. The Samoa International Company (IC) is structured as non-resident and is tax-exempt, but deposits that enter the licensed Samoan banking system become subject to CBS surveillance, reporting thresholds, and exchange-control documentation. Profit remittance and dividend distributions are executable after evidence of substance, audited or reviewed management accounts, tax status confirmation, and a completed CBS exchange-control application or authorised dealer declaration; convertibility of WST outside Samoa remains limited and is normally achieved by USD or AUD settlement through the offshore treasury network of a licensed bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Samoa's Exchange Control Act 1976 and the CBS Exchange Control Manual, exporters of goods and services must repatriate export proceeds no later than 180 days after the date of export or service performance; proceeds received in a foreign currency must be offered for sale to an authorised dealer and, unless CBS relief is obtained, 100% of the receipt must be converted into Samoa tala within the statutory time frame. For a remote-screen-share and IoT telemetry software IC, digital service subscriptions collected by global payment processors (Stripe/Paddle) are not treated as surrenderable funds while settled offshore; only such proceeds remitted into a licensed Samoan bank account enter the surrender net. Where an IC holds a CBS-approved foreign-currency account, conversion relief may permit retention in USD/AUD for matching future vendor payments, but every export invoice, payment gateway settlement report, and bank credit advice must be retained as the evidentiary chain for the mandatory repatriation calculation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS; Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), established under the Central Bank Act 1984 and responsible for administering the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Financial Institutions Act 2012, and Samoa's AML/CFT licensing framework for authorised dealer banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Samoa does not issue a standalone electronic-money-institution (EMI) licence to non-resident fintechs, and deposit-taking activity requires a banking licence under the Financial Institutions Act 2012. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or neobanks are not adequate substitutes for a Samoan licensed bank when the IC requires WST clearing, CBS-compliant invoicing, or letters of comfort for treasury due diligence. The legally preferred architecture is a split-rail model: a regulated Samoan commercial bank for equity, statutory filings, and B2B treasury flows, plus a recognised global PSP (Stripe/Paddle) for merchant collection of low-risk SaaS subscriptions; the PSP settlement must be swept under a defined liquidity policy rather than treated as a bank facility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward equity capital to the Samoa IC must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's regulated banking institution to the IC's authorised-dealer bank account in Samoa, using the beneficiary bank's BIC, the IC legal name as account holder, and an International Transactions purpose code that maps to IMF BPM6 'Equity Capital' or Samoa CBS Form ECA-1 'Inward Direct Investment'. The authorised dealer will require a board resolution approving the share subscription, a subscription letter or share allotment certificate, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation that the Samoa IC has no minimum paid-up capital requirement. The transaction must not be split into trade-payment codes such as 'Computer Services Software' merely to bypass exchange-control review; if the funds are share capital, the bank's accounting entry and the IC's statutory register of members must both record equity. Upon receipt, the treasury function should match the SWIFT Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference, the bank advice, and the Central Bank of Samoa Form ECA-1 stamp to preserve a complete audit trail.Outbound disbursements for operational trade in the remote-support and IoT context should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 for vendor invoices, software licence royalties, data-centre hosting fees, and independent contractor remuneration, with each payment referencing the underlying service contract and the prescribed Samoa balance-of-payments service code. For physical hardware procurement (IoT edge devices, secure gateways, or development kits supplied by overseas manufacturers), the appropriate clearing rail is a commercial letter of credit issued by the Samoan authorised dealer by SWIFT MT700, with settlement effected by MT103 or MT202 under the LC terms; Standby Letters of Credit (MT760) may be used for vendor performance guarantees or contractual security. All outbound wires must quote the CBS Exchange Control application reference or the authorised dealer's standing approval where applicable, and the treasury policy should maintain a weekly reconciliation of SWIFT payments against approved tax-compliant invoices and subscription revenue recognised in the books of the Samoa IC.Deliverable outright forward contracts between WST and USD executed with a CBS-licensed authorised dealer bank, permitted solely to hedge firm projected software licensing receipts or known Samoan tala expenses.Cross-currency swaps between WST and USD involving a licensed commercial bank in Samoa, subject to board approval, CBS exchange-control reporting, and documented economic exposure arising from the remote-screen-share and IoT subscription business.Overnight foreign-exchange swaps used for treasury liquidity management by the authorised dealer to convert temporary WST surplus into USD without creating speculative open positions.Offshore non-deliverable forwards on WST permitted only as a risk-management overlay for a defined invoicing mismatch, provided the instrument has no speculative leverage and the hedging committee maintains full documentation of the underlying receivables.

27% standard Samoa corporate income tax rate for resident and non-resident companies; effective 0% for an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987, which grants a 20-year income tax exemption renewable for a further 20 years.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by an IC to non-resident shareholders under the International Companies Act 1987 exemption; the standard Samoa statutory withholding tax rate for dividends from other companies is 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File or Master File threshold is prescribed in Samoa; ICs are exempt from income tax and therefore outside the transfer pricing documentation regime. For any residual scope involving a Samoa-resident related entity, the arm's length principle under the Income Tax Act 1974 applies, and contemporaneous documentation is recommended where cross-border related-party software revenue exceeds an annual equivalent of USD 1,000,000 (approximately WST 2,700,000).

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Samoa's economic substance regime, administered by SIFA, requires an IC carrying on relevant activities (including software and remote services, distribution and service centre operations, financing and leasing, or intellectual property holding) to demonstrate that core income-generating activities are directed and managed in Samoa with adequate premises, employees, expenditure and board governance; alternatively, the IC must prove tax residence in a qualifying foreign jurisdiction. A substance declaration is filed annually with Form AR-IC, and non-compliance triggers penalties, information exchange with treaty partners, and possible strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Booking Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Compensating assessments and penalties of 100% to 200% of tax evaded under the Income Tax Act 1974; strike-off and dissolution of the IC; disclosure of the structure to foreign tax authorities under Samoa's tax information exchange agreements and DTAs.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for evaded amounts; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 7 years, civil restitution, asset freezing and travel bans.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to WST 50,000 under the International Companies Act 1987 and SIFA regulations, compulsory strike-off, loss of the IC tax exemption, and automatic exchange of beneficial ownership information with foreign regulators.

Director personal liability: Disqualification from acting as director, personal fines for false substance declarations, travel restrictions, and imprisonment up to 5 years for knowingly making or permitting a false filing.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 100,000 per breach under Samoa's Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (as amended); suspension or revocation of the registered agent's licence; freezing and confiscation orders over the IC's assets and merchant accounts; mandatory reporting to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability of up to 5 years' imprisonment for materially false KYC or beneficial-ownership declarations and personal civil restitution for moneys laundered through the IC.
Unlicensed Payment/Financial Service and Sanctions Exposure

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 250,000 and confiscation of assets for unregulated financial activity under Samoa's financial services legislation; merchant account termination, Stripe/Paddle blacklisting, designation on international sanctions lists, and consequential global asset freezes.

Director personal liability: Personal fines, extradition exposure, imprisonment up to 10 years under Samoa's financial crimes provisions and applicable foreign sanctions legislation; directors may be subject to international travel bans and personal supervision orders.
