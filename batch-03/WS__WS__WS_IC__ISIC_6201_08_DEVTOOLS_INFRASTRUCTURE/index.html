Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue (Matagaluega o Tupe Mau), Samoa; official portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a Tax Identification Number from the Samoa Ministry of Revenue by submitting the prescribed taxpayer registration form together with its certificate of incorporation, registered office certificate, constitutional documents, and proof of identity for directors and shareholders. For a Samoa International Company, the entity is formed through the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) and is generally tax-exempt; a TIN is issued by the Ministry of Revenue only when the entity undertakes a taxable activity in Samoa, such as making GST-taxable supplies, importing dutiable goods, or otherwise becoming liable for Samoan tax filings. If the International Company derives no Samoan-source income and makes no local taxable supplies, no routine TIN is required due to its statutory tax-exempt status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election is available for International Companies under Samoan law. The International Company is statutorily exempt from income tax under the International Companies Act 1987. A foreign-owned entity may only become subject to Samoan entity-level taxation by converting to or incorporating a separate local company under the Companies Act 2006.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 in annual taxable supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods from Samoa where the goods leave Samoa under customs supervision. For services, zero-rating applies to internationally supplied services, including computer infrastructure, database, API monitoring, and cloud tools, when the services are performed for a recipient who is outside Samoa, the recipient is not a Samoan resident, and the services are not effectively connected with Samoa. The International Company is eligible for zero-rating only if it is separately GST-registered for its local VAT/GST activity; the tax-exempt status of the International Company does not create automatic GST zero-rating rights outside a registration framework.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • GST-compliant tax invoice or zero-rated supply certificate

No real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate exists in Samoa. There is no statutory API required for invoice stamping, digital tax clearance, or government e-invoicing interoperability. GST-registered businesses must issue paper or digital tax invoices containing prescribed particulars and file GST returns with the Ministry of Revenue, but in a non-cleared, self-assessment environment.

The Ministry of Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims with retrospective effect. Penalties for incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating include recovery of the GST underpaid, late payment interest, a penalty up to 150% of the unpaid tax, and criminal fines of up to WST 100,000 per invoice with potential imprisonment for directors. Claimants must substantiate export and forex repatriation documentation within the prescribed 180-day window; failure to do so results in clawback of the input credit or outright denial of the zero rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is convertible for current account transactions, subject to CBS approval thresholds and supporting documentation. Capital account repatriations generally require CBS consent, especially for foreign direct investment, loan repayments, and disinvestment proceeds. Profit remittances via dividend distributions from an International Company are, in principle, permitted, but must be processed through an authorized dealer (commercial bank) with proof of tax exemption status and compliance with the International Companies Act 1987. Samoa maintains a managed float; the CBS does not impose surrender requirements on non-resident-owned ICs, but may require repatriation declarations. No material restrictions on the conversion of WST for trade settlement, subject to declaration and documentary evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Central Bank of Samoa exists for International Companies. Export receipts in foreign currency must be repatriated to Samoa and deposited into a WST or foreign currency account with an authorized dealer within a prescribed timeframe, typically 30 days from the date of shipment or service completion. Conversion into WST is optional unless specifically directed by the CBS. For trade in digital services (DevOps tools), proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts, provided the bank records the underlying contract and the IC's tax-exempt certificate.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), operating under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and the Exchange Control Ordinance 1970. The CBS is the sole authority for exchange control administration, licensing of authorized dealers, and monitoring of all cross-border capital and current account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Samoa does not issue banking licenses to foreign digital-only EMI or fintech entities. Foreign EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) are not regulated or recognized as authorized dealers by the CBS; therefore, they cannot be used as primary treasury repositories for an IC. They may be used as incoming payment collection rails only, but all WST funding and disbursement must be settled through a CBS-licensed commercial bank. Any on-island clearing requires a local banking partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder loan injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to an authorized dealer account. The remitter must reference the IC's name, account number, and the CBS clearing code 'A11' for direct investment. Banks require a copy of the board resolution approving the capital injection and, if exceeding WST 2,000,000 (or equivalent), a prior CBS Foreign Investment Approval (under the Foreign Investment Act 1985). The bank will validate the source of funds and may require a lawyer's opinion that the IC complies with the International Companies Act. Funds will be credited only after full compliance with the bank's KYC and the receipt of a signed facility letter.Trade settlements for software development tools, API licensing, and infrastructure subscription services should be executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 if interbank. For transactions exceeding WST 100,000, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) may be used with an issuing bank in Samoa and a confirming bank abroad, but for lower-value recurring payments, direct MT103 with the invoice referenced as an 'Intra-group services settlement' is standard. Each disbursement requires a completed Foreign Exchange Form (CBS Form 2) and an underlying invoice, contract, or statement of account. The bank may require the original invoice for amounts above WST 500,000; digital copies are acceptable for regular low-value payments. The beneficiary bank's BIC, account number, and remittance reference should be pre-validated to avoid AML holds.Forward contracts on WST for up to 12 months with an authorized dealer, provided the underlying transaction is a real commercial or financial obligation. Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted by the Central Bank of Samoa because WST is a deliverable currency within the CBS regulated market. Currency swaps are generally unavailable in Samoa's domestic market; only simple FX forwards and spot contracts are authorized.FX swaps with the Central Bank of Samoa may be used by licensed commercial banks, but are not directly available to International Companies. The IC may enter into cross-currency interest rate swaps only with a duly licensed bank and only after obtaining CBS approval for the specific hedging arrangement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends paid by a Samoan resident company to non-resident shareholders (as final tax). Dividends distributed by an exempt International Company are statutorily outside the scope of Samoan dividend withholding tax, yielding an effective rate of 0%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the fiscal year-end (for a 31 December year-end, no later than 31 March); provisional tax installments are payable on any taxable resident company with assessable income above the statutory threshold

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before the anniversary of incorporation (within one calendar month after each anniversary), concurrently with payment of the annual government fee

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no codified Samoa Tala threshold for mandatory Local File or Master File documentation. The Income Tax Administration Act 2012 and general anti-avoidance rules apply the arm's length standard to all cross-border related-party transactions. In practice, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation should be prepared where cross-border related-party revenue (including group charges for developer tools, database infrastructure and API monitoring services) exceeds SAT 1,000,000, or where any intra-group financing, royalty or cost-sharing arrangement exists.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations apply. Samoa, as a member of the OECD Inclusive Framework, requires every International Company carrying on a relevant activity (banking, financing, insurance, shipping, IP holding, distribution, service centres, or headquarters) to demonstrate substantial economic presence in Samoa: physical registered premises, at least one local director or employee for financing and high-risk activities, proportionate operational expenditure, and core income-generating activities performed and managed in Samoa. Pure equity-holding vehicles satisfy a reduced substance test, but a DevOps cloud, database and API monitoring IC that licenses software, invoices cross-border services, or holds intangible assets must file an annual economic substance return and evidence active strategic decision-making in Samoa. Failure triggers fines up to SAT 100,000, continuing daily penalties of SAT 500 until compliance, cancellation of the tax exemption, compulsory deregistration, and automatic exchange of information with the shareholder's residence jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of up to 150% of the tax avoided, late payment interest at 2% per month, statutory fines up to SAT 20,000 per offence, deregistration of the International Company, publication of the taxpayer's name on the Ministry of Revenue non-compliance list, and criminal conviction of the company.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil where the IC is used as a mere facade: directors become jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, interest and penalties; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to five years; civil proceedings permit departure prohibition orders (travel bans), freezing of foreign accounts, asset forfeiture and charging orders against the directors' personal estates.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the economic substance regime: administrative fine up to SAT 100,000, daily default penalties of SAT 500, revocation of the IC tax exemption, compulsory strike-off after two consecutive years of non-compliance, and automatic exchange of information leading to foreign tax reassessments and interest and penalty charges abroad.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Registered Agent are jointly liable for substance penalties; personal fines up to SAT 50,000; disqualification from managing any Samoan international company; surrender of passports pending investigation; civil restitution orders requiring repayment of distributions received; and travel bans imposed at the border.
AML/CFT Violation and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Fines under the AML/CFT Act of up to SAT 2,000,000 for the company and SAT 500,000 for individuals per violation, revocation of the Registered Agent's licence, seizure and forfeiture of the IC's assets, and international blacklisting consequences including exclusion from global correspondent banking networks.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is irrebuttably pierced for money laundering: directors face imprisonment up to 14 years, personal monetary fines, confiscation of illicit gains plus interest, indefinite exclusion from international banking platforms, standing travel bans, and extradition exposure where foreign counterparties are harmed.
Treaty, Transfer Pricing and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the whole arrangement under general anti-avoidance rules; disallowance of deductions in the payer jurisdiction; upward adjustment to the arm's length price; Samoan reassessment at 27% plus a 50% penalty uplift; refusal of mutual agreement procedure relief where the IC is not the beneficial owner; and referral to the OECD/G20 forum on harmful tax practices.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements are personally assessed for the tax shortfall up to the value of distributions they received; backdating documents constitutes criminal fraud with foreign extradition risk; officers are permanently disbarred from Samoan corporate registers and remain exposed to international arrest warrants and multilateral asset recovery proceedings.
