Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; Customs & Excise declarations lodged via the SARS Customs Clearance Declaration (SAD500) electronic channel.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned or domestic (Pty) Ltd must first incorporate with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain a company registration number. The company then registers for income tax with SARS on eFiling, resulting in a separate Income Tax Reference Number. If taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed ZAR 1 million in a 12-month period, the company must apply for VAT vendor registration on VAT 101, obtaining a VAT vendor number. A public officer and, for a foreign entity, a local SARS-appointed representative agent must be maintained. PAYE, UIF and SDL registrations are required if employees are appointed. Infrastructure EPC contractors retaining imported equipment may require customs importer/exporter number and bond registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a South African (Pty) Ltd; it is a resident company taxable at entity level on worldwide income. Available statutory elections relevant to infrastructure contractors include: small business corporation status under section 12E of the Income Tax Act (turnover threshold ZAR 20 million); VAT group registration election under section 50 of the VAT Act for grouped companies; the South African Revenue Service advance tax ruling mechanism for rating of construction services; controlled foreign company imputation elections under the Income Tax Act; and hybrid mismatch adjustment elections to ensure alignment with the OECD hybrid mismatch framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in actual or reasonably expected taxable supplies over any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration may be permitted below this threshold under section 23 of the VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied to a non-resident may be zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act if the goods are physically exported from South Africa within the statutory time frame under a sale or consignment contract and the vendor retains documentary proof of export. Services physically performed outside South Africa to a non-resident may be zero-rated under section 11(2); construction services performed outside South Africa, or services rendered to a non-resident for use wholly outside South Africa, can qualify where the recipient is outside South Africa at the time the services are rendered and the services are not connected with movable or immovable property inside South Africa. Mere invoicing a non-resident is insufficient; the supply must be an export under the VAT Act.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) and customs clearance confirmation
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical removal of goods from South Africa
  • Commercial sales contract, construction contract, or EPC agreement
  • Proof of payment such as SWIFT advice or sovereign letter of credit drawdown confirmation
  • Inward facture / foreign inward remittance certificate from an authorised dealer
  • Packing list, shipping invoice, and transport insurance documentation where applicable
  • SARS final export registration or rebate permit reference where capital equipment is temporarily exported
  • Certificate of origin and other preferential trade documentation where relevant

SARS does not currently impose a mandatory continuous transaction control or real-time fiscal clearance stamping regime for VAT invoices. Taxpayers must issue prescribed electronic or hard-copy tax invoices that satisfy the VAT Act section 20 particulars, retain them for five years, and submit periodic VAT returns through SARS eFiling. Customs export clearance is fully electronic via the SAD500, with mandatory cargo reporting and risk-profile release; no physical fiscal stamp is required.

SARS audit units closely scrutinise zero-rated supplies for proof of physical export and foreign payment settlement. If the export evidence bundle is incomplete or fraudulent, SARS may withdraw the zero-rating, assess VAT at 15% on the full supply value, and impose understatement penalties up to 100% under the Tax Administration Act, together with interest. Additional VAT Act penalties of up to 100% or 200% may apply for false statements or incorrect zero-rating claims, and fraudulent declarations may result in criminal prosecution, cancellation of registration, and denial of refunds. The South African Reserve Bank exchange control rules require export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days; failure may expose the exporter to administrative exchange-control penalties and may adversely affect VAT refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a managed-float currency regime with substantial current account convertibility but a controlled capital account. Current account trade and service payments can be effected through Authorized Dealers without prior SARB approval if supported by the required documentation. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, capital injection, inter-company shareholder loans, and capital repatriation, remain subject to Exchange Control Regulations, SARB FinSurv rulings, and Authorized Dealer oversight. Profit remittance as ordinary dividends is permitted through banking channels without prior SARB approval provided the entity has valid SARS tax clearance, profits are distributable, and the transaction is reported by the Authorized Dealer. Capital repayment and liquidation distributions require prior FinSurv approval or specific Authorized Dealer authority.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South African exchange control rules require that 100% of export proceeds arising from the sale of goods or services be repatriated to South Africa and paid into an Authorized Dealer account in Rand within 180 days of date of shipment unless the exporter has been granted approval to maintain an approved Foreign Currency Account. There is no split conversion ratio applied at law; in the absence of a specific exemption or Foreign Currency Account approval, the effective mandatory conversion ratio is 100% into ZAR. Written requests for extension must be made to the Authorized Dealer before the 180-day deadline and, for extended periods, to FinSurv.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs to operate standalone in South Africa. South Africa does not issue a full electronic money institution (EMI) license comparable to EU EMI regimes. Cross-border payment facilitation must be conducted through SARB-licensed Authorized Dealers. Offshore fintech platforms may act as invoicing or treasury aggregation utilities, but all ZAR clearing and SWIFT execution must be routed through an Authorized Dealer bank. A contractual white-label partnership with a Tier-1 bank is the most legally viable structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for the South African (Pty) Ltd must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder or lender to the Authorized Dealer bank account designated for the company, using direct origination from the investor's banking entity. The instruction should carry an appropriate ISO 20022 purpose code such as LEND for intercompany or shareholder loan proceeds or OGDC for direct foreign investment capital; the corresponding South African Authorized Dealer documentation and FinSurv reporting must be completed at the bank. The bank will require the formal corporate approvals of the South African company, the signed loan agreement or subscription agreement, a SARS tax clearance certificate, and acknowledgment that the funds will be used for construction of highway bridge infrastructure. Each transfer must be clearly marked as a capital account transaction to avoid misclassification as trade revenue and must be registered with the Authorized Dealer under Exchange Control Regulations.Outbound trade disbursements for construction materials, equipment imports, subcontractor payments, and bridge design consultancy services should primarily clear through SWIFT MT103 for direct payments and MT700-series instruments for documentary letter of credit settlements. The use of irrevocable confirmed commercial LCs governed by UCP600 provides the standard sovereign-infrastructure clearing rail. Project milestone payments from the South African employer should be received into the operational account and matched against supplier invoices. For import-heavy components, the importer of record must produce customs import documentation and may be required to complete Authorized Dealer verification reports. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million aggregate connected-person affected transactions for a mandatory Local File; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are expected for multinational groups with annual consolidated revenue of ZAR 10 billion or more. Even below the thresholds, all affected transactions must be documented on request and priced at arm's length.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the general anti-avoidance rule in section 103 of the Income Tax Act, the substance-over-form doctrine, and OECD-aligned transfer pricing rules require demonstrable commercial substance. The Pty Ltd must maintain a real South African office, construction management, staff, risk assumption, board control, and project books and records to support its infrastructure EPC income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty up to 100% of the tax shortfall under section 223 of the Tax Administration Act, plus interest; criminal prosecution for tax evasion or false statements may result in a fine up to ZAR 2 million and imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years for aggravated/syndicated evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise, sign, or benefit from false returns may be criminally liable, subject to restitution orders and asset preservation orders, and may be barred from leaving South Africa as a bail condition or through High Court travel restrictions. The corporate veil may be pierced where fraud or reckless trading is shown.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust taxable income, disallow deductions, and recharacterise the transaction; understatement penalties range from 25% to 100% of the shortfall depending on culpability, plus prescribed-rate interest and possible administrative penalties for failure to submit transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement artificial related-party pricing may be personally liable for tax debts arising from reckless or fraudulent trading under the Companies Act. SARS may recover civilly, apply for preservation orders, and refer directors for criminal prosecution where documents are false.
