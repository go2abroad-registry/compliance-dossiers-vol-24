Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum issued share capital applies to a Proprietary Limited Company under the Companies Act 71 of 2008. The initial allotment of 100 ordinary shares at ZAR 1.00 each is fully paid into the company bank account; no escrow account is statutorily mandated. Any subsequent allotment, distribution, or financial assistance must satisfy the solvency and liquidity test in s.4 and, where shares are issued against a future payment, the board must record a solvency-and-liquidity statement before allotment.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Clearance Certificate from the municipal Fire and Rescue Division complying with SANS 10400 Part T and NFPA standards; structural load and building stability approval under the National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act 103 of 1977 with an occupancy certificate; Letters of Compliance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, including appointment of s.16.2 risk assessors and issue-based SANS 45001 alignment; COIDA letter of good standing; electrical installation Certificate of Compliance; and, where battery-cell storage exceeds prescribed thresholds, a dangerous goods storage clearance under SANS 10228.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — national tax and customs administration; primary digital portal: SARS eFiling (https://efiling.sars.gov.za); customs declarations separately filed via SARS Customs eFiling.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd is incorporated through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) Business Registration Service (BR); CIPC electronically transmits the company's incorporation data to SARS, which automatically allocates the statutory Income Tax reference number (ITN), the primary corporate TIN. VAT registration is a separate application administered through SARS eFiling (form VAT 101/102): registration is compulsory once taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in any period of 12 months, and voluntary registration is available from R50,000 in 12 months. A non-resident parent entity without a local branch may obtain a non-resident taxpayer reference number via SARS eFiling. Any person other than an individual who carries on a South African enterprise must appoint a public officer ordinarily resident in South Africa (Section 246, Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011). All registration data, returns, tax compliance status (TCS) and refund claims are administered through the SARS eFiling portal.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the (Pty) Ltd is a South African resident company under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax headline rate (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023). Distributions to shareholders are subject to a separate 20% dividends withholding tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the (Pty) Ltd is a South African resident company under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax headline rate (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023). Distributions to shareholders are subject to a separate 20% dividends withholding tax. Pass-through, flow-through or territorial scope exemption is not available to a resident (Pty) Ltd.

Available election forms

South Africa does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election or an elective pass-through classification. Relevant statutory election mechanisms available to the entity include: (i) VAT group registration under Section 21 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 allowing a group of companies to be treated as a single vendor with one VAT number; (ii) the Section 11(2)(k) election/regime for zero-rating of services supplied to non-residents, subject to documented export substantiation; (iii) the controlled foreign company high-tax exemption election under Section 9E of the Income Tax Act; (iv) Section 64D elections relating to deemed dividends; and (v) the hybrid mismatch rules (Section 25E read with the National Treasury Hybrid Mismatch Regulations) which apply mandatorily as anti-avoidance measures and cannot be elected out or deferred.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 aggregate taxable supplies over any 12-month period (compulsory registration under Section 23(1)(a) VAT Act); R50,000 over 12 months for voluntary registration; foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African residents are subject to separate deemed-supply registration requirements under Section 54(1A) of the VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act zero-rates the supply of movable goods supplied to a non-resident where the goods are physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply. Services: Section 11(2)(k) zero-rates services rendered to non-residents unless those services are physically rendered in South Africa or are effectively used and consumed in South Africa; manufacturing, assembly and local engineering services physically performed by the EV bus OEM in respect of export contracts therefore remain standard-rated unless separately covered by an export-related exemption. For complete electric passenger bus units exported into the SACU region or other foreign markets, the supplier must evidence export within the statutory period and retain the documentation bundle below; non-compliant transactions are re-characterised as standard-rated domestic supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration — SARS SAD500 single export declaration filed electronically via SARS Customs eFiling or an authorised clearing agent
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent air waybill) evidencing physical movement of the complete electric bus units out of South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an authorised dealer bank evidencing receipt and repatriation of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice or VAT tax invoice stating the 0% export zero-rating notation, vehicle identification details and export destination
  • Proof of delivery and destination customs discharge records where obtainable from the importing SACU or foreign jurisdiction

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or statutory API clearance obligation applies to standard B2B supplies in South Africa. SARS requires full tax invoices compliant with Section 20 of the VAT Act (vendor VAT number, sequential invoice numbering, description of goods, date of supply, price and explicit 15% or 0% rate indication) and the electronic filing of VAT 201 returns via SARS eFiling. SARS has commenced a phased modernisation roadmap and consultation toward mandatory e-invoicing interoperability, but no legislative mandate currently requires pre-clearance or digital clearing of invoices for export or domestic sales.

On audit, SARS may disallow export zero-rating retrospectively where goods were not exported within 90 days of supply or where the SAD500, Bill of Lading, IRC or supporting invoice bundle is deficient. Disallowed zero-rating results in a full 15% output VAT clawback assessed on the supplier, plus statutory interest under Section 28 of the VAT Act read with Section 187 of the Tax Administration Act. Understatement penalties under Sections 222 and 223 TAA apply from 10% up to 150% of the shortfall, escalating to 200% for repeated intentional non-disclosure or evasion schemes. Fraudulent export refund claims constitute tax offences under Section 234 TAA and the offence provisions of the VAT Act, punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to five years for a first conviction and up to ten years for a subsequent conviction. Exchange control non-compliance arising from failure to repatriate export proceeds within the prescribed 180-day deadline is separately investigated by the Financial Surveillance Department of the South African Reserve Bank, with monetary administrative penalties, forfeiture exposure and potential criminal liability under the Currency and Exchanges Act and Exchange Control Regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions (imports, exports, service payments, dividends, interest) at the commercial interbank rate through Authorized Dealers; no prior SARB approval is required for current account remittances, only submission of the requisite BOP reporting form (Form B/CS). Capital account transactions are more restricted: non-resident inward direct equity investment requires no prior approval but must be reported via the Authorized Dealer through the issuance of a Non-Resident Share Acquisition endorsement and the completion of the inward FDI BOP code (54W - Direct Investment in a South African Enterprise). Profit and dividend remittance to foreign shareholders is permitted provided: (i) the company holds sufficient distributable reserves evidenced by reviewed or audited financial statements, (ii) the solvency and liquidity test under Section 46 of the Companies Act, 2008 is concluded, (iii) a SARS Dividend Tax return (DST-01) is complied with and the 20% dividends withholding tax is remitted, and (iv) the shareholder's non-resident status is supported by a tax residence certificate. There is no exchange control ceiling on dividend remittance once the FDI endorsement is recorded; however, inward shareholder loans above the authorized limit (currently ZAR 10 million for intra-group loans) require direct FinSurv approval, and onward conversion of ZAR into hard currency for reinvestment outside South Africa is restricted without prior consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 7 and the SARB FinSurv export guidelines, all export proceeds from the electric passenger bus manufacturing activities must be repatriated to South Africa through an Authorized Dealer within 180 days of the date of shipment or 30 days after the invoice due date, whichever is earlier; a 365-day extension is obtainable on application for OEM manufacturers who provide extended warranty and long-term municipal credit terms. Mandatory conversion: there is no split ratio in the current framework; 100% of repatriated foreign currency proceeds must be surrendered into ZAR at the ruling market rate upon receipt, unless the Authorized Dealer approves a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) retention for demonstrable offshore commitments (e.g., pending battery cell imports, director's commission, or reserve requirements for an overseas co-production contract). All export transactions must be documented on the SAD 500 export customs declaration and the Exchange Control Export Form, with the Bill of Lading, signed contract, and proof of payment attached to the Authorized Dealer within 30 days after shipment for verification; non-compliance triggers criminal liability under the Exchange Control Regulations, penalties up to 100% of the export value, and potential forfeiture of the export proceeds into the State's Consolidated Revenue Fund.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), is the sole exchange control authority and custodian of the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations of 1961 (R.1111), the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 2001 (FICA), and the Financial Markets Act, 2012. Commercial banks, in their capacity as Authorized Dealers, execute all day-to-day foreign exchange transactions on behalf of SARB and are bound by the Prudential Authority's directives on bank supervision, anti-money laundering, and counterparty due diligence.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

LOW-MEDIUM FEASIBILITY. Offshore digital EMIs (Revolut Business, Airwallex, Wise Business, Multitude/OpenPayd) are not recognised by SARB as deposit-taking institutions and cannot be licensed under the Banks Act, 1990 to hold ZAR clearing accounts on the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system. They are legally usable only as adjunct cash-flow visibility tools for non-ZAR corridors and for pre-settlement of hard currency trade disbursements; the primary ZAR operational treasury must be held at an Authorized Dealer, because municipal tender escrow, SARS tax reconciliation, VAT refunds, and BOP reporting require an SARB-regulated bank account. Any EMI routing of a payment into a South African beneficiary will result in the funds landing via a local partner bank (e.g., Standard Bank or FNB) with the EMI flagged as the instructed originator; for transactions exceeding the Banking Association of South Africa (BASA) threshold, the underlying FDI and trade documents must still be disclosed to the local Authorized Dealer to avoid exchange control contravention.

The non-resident equity subscription must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the Authorized Dealer's designated inward capital account referencing the share subscription agreement and SARB BOP purpose code 54W (direct investment into a South African enterprise). The bank will require: (i) a certified shareholders' resolution, (ii) the issued share certificates and CIPC notice of allotment, (iii) subscription payment reconciliation, (iv) the investor's completion of the 'Non-Resident Inward Transfer of Funds' form, and (v) an inward FDI endorsement stamped by the Authorized Dealer. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: LOW-MEDIUM FEASIBILITY. Offshore digital EMIs (Revolut Business, Airwallex, Wise Business, Multitude/OpenPayd) are not recognised by SARB as deposit-taking institutions and cannot be licensed under the Banks Act, 1990 to hold ZAR clearing accounts on the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system. They are legally usable only as adjunct cash-flow visibility tools for non-ZAR corridors and for pre-settlement of hard currency trade disbursements; the primary ZAR operational treasury must be held at an Authorized Dealer, because municipal tender escrow, SARS tax reconciliation, VAT refunds, and BOP reporting require an SARB-regulated bank account. Any EMI routing of a payment into a South African beneficiary will result in the funds landing via a local partner bank (e.g., Standard Bank or FNB) with the EMI flagged as the instructed originator; for transactions exceeding the Banking Association of South Africa (BASA) threshold, the underlying FDI and trade documents must still be disclosed to the local Authorized Dealer to avoid exchange control contravention.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: SARS requires contemporaneous TP documentation for all affected transactions with connected persons; the Local File must be prepared where aggregate international related-party transactions exceed R5,000,000, and the Master File is expected where aggregate cross-border related-party turnover exceeds R100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute as in the UAE, but economic substance is enforced through the place of effective management (POEM) residency test, Section 9D controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution rules, anti-avoidance provisions under Section 103, and TP secondary adjustments. The company must demonstrate genuine decision-making, operational assets, and staff in South Africa to support its residency and contractual arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty up to 200% of the assessed tax shortfall under Section 222 of the Tax Administration Act; criminal conviction may result in a fine not exceeding R40,000 or imprisonment not exceeding five years under Section 235.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the company to evade tax may be held jointly and severally liable for the tax debt, barred from acting as directors, subjected to travel bans and asset restraints under Section 164 of the Tax Administration Act, and imprisoned for up to five years.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangements

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the arrangement as an impermissible avoidance scheme under Section 103 of the Income Tax Act, re-characterise the transactions, deny DTA benefits, impose an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the shortfall, and apply the CFC rules to attribute the artificially diverted profits.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under Section 22(9) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008, rendering directors personally liable for company liabilities, and SARS may issue personal travel bans while civil claims for repayment are pursued.
Money Laundering / Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FICA administrative penalties may reach R100 million; criminal sanctions for money laundering under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act may include a fine and imprisonment for up to ten years, or up to thirty years for racketeering offences.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal prosecution for failure to report suspicious and unusual transactions; they may be disqualified as directors and subject to civil damages claims by liquidators.
Transfer Pricing / Excessive Related-Party Debt

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust taxable income to the arm’s length value, deny the interest or fee deduction, apply an understatement penalty of up to 200%, and impose a secondary adjustment deeming the residual benefit a dividend subject to DWT.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve TP documents containing false or misleading information may be held personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall, subject to criminal sanctions for perjury, and to civil liability under Section 159 of the Companies Act.
