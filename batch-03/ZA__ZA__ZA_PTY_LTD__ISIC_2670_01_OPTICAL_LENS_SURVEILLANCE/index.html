Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital applies to a South African private company; incorporation may proceed with a single issued share. The board must satisfy the solvency and liquidity test under s 4 of the Companies Act at the time of any share allotment, and the issue consideration must be fully detailed in the MOI. There is no statutory escrow or deposit account requirement for subscription proceeds, although funds must be receivable in the company's bank account.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible on the current account (trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties, interest, and operating expenses), which is transacted freely through Authorised Dealers with standard supporting documentation. The capital account remains regulated: inbound foreign direct investment (FDI) is freely permitted via Authorised Dealer notification and inward-transfer reporting; outbound corporate FDI is permitted up to ZAR 1 billion per company per calendar year under the Corporate Foreign Investment allocation, with FinSurv notification and no prior approval. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful after satisfaction of the Companies Act solvency and liquidity test and after payment of dividends tax at 20% (or a reduced treaty rate, e.g. 5–15%, where applicable); no residual exchange-control approval is required where the original inward investment was properly recorded with the Authorised Dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa and received by an Authorised Dealer within 90 days of shipment (180 days is permissible in qualifying cases with prior Authorised Dealer/FinSurv extension). No mandatory surrender ratio applies: 100% of export proceeds may be retained in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with a domestic Authorised Dealer, while proceeds not retained in an FCA must be converted into ZAR or otherwise disposed of in accordance with the Authorised Dealer's instructions. The exporter must retain the customs export declaration (SAD500), shipping documents, and proof of receipt for at least five years for FinSurv inspection.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933, as amended, and the Exchange Control Regulations (1961); authorised dealers (commercial banks) administer day-to-day exchange control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g. Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury) cannot act as a substitute for a South African Authorised Dealer. Under the Banks Act, No. 94 of 1990, no unlicensed institution may accept deposits or offer a ZAR domestic settlement account; exchange-control regulations further require that all cross-border receipts and payments pass through an Authorised Dealer. An offshore EMI may serve only as a supplementary collection/payment instruction channel if it operates under a local payment-system partnership with a licensed bank, and it must never hold the company's export proceeds outside the SARB reporting net.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 for cover, dispatched to the chosen Authorised Dealer's nostro account with a purpose code classifying inward direct investment (equity subscription or intercompany loan). The remitter must cite the company's beneficiary name and account number; the Authorised Dealer requires the share subscription agreement, board resolution, CIPC registration extract, and a completed inward FDI notification form for SARB records. Funds are credited to the Capital Escrow Account pending issuance of shares, then swept to the Operational Account. A SARS tax clearance certificate (in good standing) is required before any interest or dividend flows on the injected capital are remitted outward.Trade settlements for imported raw materials, componentry, and process equipment clear through the Authorised Dealer via SWIFT MT103 direct transfers, or through commercial letters of credit (documentary credits issued/advised under UCP600 with SWIFT MT700/MT701 issuance and MT710/MT720 advice). Import payments are current-account transactions requiring only standard documentation: invoice, bill of lading/air waybill, customs SAD500 entry, and proof of insurance.
