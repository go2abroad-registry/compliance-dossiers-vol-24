Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Apia, Samoa; official taxation portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws. The Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA, https://www.sifa.ws) functions as the registry, licensing, and statutory exemption certification authority for all International Companies incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987, operating in coordination with SRA for tax identity matters.

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders establishing a Samoa International Company (IC) must: (1) appoint a licensed international company service provider in Samoa to act as registered agent and maintain the registered office; (2) file the Memorandum and Articles of Association with SIFA under the International Companies Act 1987 and pay the prescribed government incorporation and annual licensing fees, thereby obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation; (3) rely on the automatic statutory tax exemption in section 213 of the International Companies Act 1987, whereby an IC deriving income from outside Samoa is exempt from income tax, VAGST, stamp duty, and all Samoan direct and indirect taxation; the exemption is evidenced by a SIFA-issued Certificate of Good Standing and, upon written request, a Certificate of Tax Exemption; (4) apply to the SRA for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) only where a counterparty bank or intermediary requires a Samoan TIN for KYC/AML or payment routing, using the SRA business registration form together with the IC's incorporation certificate, registered agent resolution, and certificate of tax exemption; (5) confirm that the IC carries on no business, has no place of establishment, and derives no income from Samoan sources, so that no corporate tax registration and no annual income tax return are due to SRA; the IC remains subject only to its SIFA annual renewal and any registered agent compliance obligations; (6) register for VAGST under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2014 only if the IC accidentally commenced a taxable activity in Samoa, which is structurally inconsistent with the pure equity holding SPV mandate and would be a red flag requiring immediate remediation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no foreign-entity 'check-the-box' classification election comparable to United States Treasury Regulation Section 301.7701-3. The offshore tax-exempt status of an IC is automatic and non-elective under the International Companies Act 1987. The only statutorily available elections and transitions affecting tax identity are: (a) conversion of the IC into a domestic company under the Companies Act 2001, which removes the offshore exemption and submits the entity to SRA jurisdiction, income tax at the corporate rate, full 15% VAGST registration, withholding tax obligations, and continuous residence-based reporting, effectively destroying the tax-exempt character; (b) continuance or redomiciliation of the IC to or from another recognized offshore jurisdiction under the International Companies Act, preserving the exemption where the new home regime grants an equivalent zero-tax status; (c) an election to enter the Income Tax Act 2015 group consolidation regime, available only to tax-resident domestic groups and therefore legally unavailable to a tax-exempt non-resident IC; and (d) the hybrid mismatch neutrality election under the Income Tax Act 2015, which similarly only applies to resident entities and cannot be invoked by an IC because Samoa's domestic law does not permit the hybrid entity to opt into tax residency. No special waiver of the 15% VAGST or of corporate income tax can be negotiated; the exemption derives exclusively from the offshore IC statute and is valid only while the IC remains offshore.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 in annual taxable turnover for compulsory registration; voluntary registration is available under the VAGST Act 2014 once a taxable activity is carried on, even below the compulsory threshold. For a pure equity holding IC, dividend income, interest, capital gains on disposal of shares, and all other passive offshore returns are not supplies made in Samoa, fall outside the charge of VAGST, and are excluded from the turnover calculation for the WST 60,000 threshold. The IC should never hold a Samoan VAGST registration number unless a factual taxable activity in Samoa has inadvertently arisen, which would disqualify the entity from the tax-exempt offshore regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the VAGST Act 2014 applies to: (a) goods physically removed from Samoa in an export transaction, supported by a customs export declaration and transport documents; (b) services supplied to a non-resident person and performed entirely outside Samoa; (c) supplies of international transport and ancillary freight services; and (d) supplies to approved prescribed organizations or in free trade zone arrangements where applicable. A pure equity holding IC is architecturally outside the VAGST system because its core activity (receiving and holding dividends and disposal of shares) is neither a supply of goods nor a supply of services for VAGST purposes; therefore the IC does not need to make zero-rated supplies in the ordinary course. If the IC exceptionally undertakes cross-border trade in goods or supplies offshore services, it may claim a zero rate only upon producing, at the time of filing or SRA audit, customs declarations, bills of lading/air waybills, contracts, bank remittance records, and a contemporaneous zero-rating statement in the tax invoice. Where the IC merely files an annual no-activity confirmation with SIFA and does not register for VAGST, no zero-rating refund claim procedure applies; dividend income and capital gains remain tax-exempt abroad and uncollectible in Samoa.

  • Customs Export Declaration - the electronic or paper export entry processed by Samoa Customs at the port of departure, referencing the IC as exporter of record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the ocean or air carrier evidencing physical shipment from Samoa to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and underlying purchase/sale contract describing the goods or services and expressly declaring zero-rated export supply under the VAGST Act 2014
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign exchange credit advice demonstrating receipt of payment in convertible currency from the non-resident customer for the exported goods or services
  • Packing list, certificate of origin, and such other transport or regulatory export permits as may be required for the particular goods, if the IC ever deviates from passive holding activities

Samoa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, continuous transaction controls, or API-based electronic invoicing. SRA operates an electronic TIN and returns platform, but paper and digital tax invoices are equally recognized. Where the IC issues a VAGST invoice for any taxable or zero-rated supply (an exceptional event for a passive equity holder), the invoice must contain the statutory VAGST content: supplier name and TIN, recipient name and TIN, invoice date, description of goods or services, quantity and price, total consideration, the VAGST amount or a clear zero-rating declaration, and the registered place of business of the supplier. Invoices must be retained for a minimum period of seven years and be produced to SRA on demand; the absence of an approved electronic clearance system means the substantiation burden falls on documentation integrity, retention, and contemporaneous commercial records rather than on clearance certification.

SRA audit scrutiny of zero-rated claims focuses on the physical export of goods, offshore consumption of services, genuineness of the non-resident counter-party, and the inflow of foreign currency within a 180-day documentary window. If the IC or any related trader fails the export substantiation test, the zero rate is reversed and the full 15% VAGST becomes payable on the gross consideration, together with monthly late payment interest and a shortfall penalty of 25% of the unpaid tax for inadvertent error, rising to 100% or more of the tax shortfall where the failure reflects intentional disregard, sham documentation, or circular financing. Fraudulent export schemes, false bills of lading, fabricated customs entries, or artificial round-tripping of funds trigger criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 2015 with fines up to WST 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment of up to seven years for directors, officers, and the licensed registered agent complicit in the misstatement. SRA may retrospectively claw back input VAT credits obtained by any related Samoan entity on the strength of the fraudulent zero-rated invoices and may disallow the IC's exemption certificate; SIFA may additionally strike the IC off the register for failure to maintain good standing, and the professional service provider may be deregistered from the international company licensed agents list.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is partly convertible. Current account transactions are liberalized subject to documentary reporting to CBS and participation of authorized commercial banks, while capital account transactions and remittances by residents require prior CBS approval. Non-resident International Companies (ICs) are generally outside the domestic exchange control net if all transactions are effected in foreign currency and with non-residents. Profit repatriation through the Samoan banking system may still require a Non-Resident Certificate, a certificate of good standing, and CBS approval if settlement is in WST. There is no withholding tax on qualifying dividend distributions from an IC, but dividend remittance to non-residents must be supported by audited financial statements and a board resolution to pass CBS scrutiny. Convertibility risk is low for USD/EUR/RMB/AUD transactions; WST conversion is reserved for domestic-active licensed banks and can be restricted during balance-of-payments stress.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBS Exchange Control Notices issued pursuant to the Exchange Control Act 1974, resident exporters must surrender foreign exchange proceeds to an authorized dealer within 30 days of receipt and convert 100% into WST at the prevailing market rate unless a specific exemption is granted. However, International Companies incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 are exempt from these surrender and mandatory conversion rules to the extent the company does not export domestically sourced goods or settle through the Samoan payments system. For ICs receiving fee income or consideration abroad, proceeds may be retained offshore without surrender. Any direct or indirect inflow into Samoa's domestic banking system of export proceeds for an IC will be presumed surplus to domestic reserve requirements and must be disclosed to the CBS if it exceeds compliance reporting thresholds (typically USD 100,000 per transaction).

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1974 and the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a principal banking host. Samoa does not have a fintech EMI or digital bank licensing regime; offshore digital EMIs cannot obtain a Samoa banking license or open domestic settlement accounts. They may be engaged as payment facilitators / white-label processors only if such entity holds a Financial Service Provider license under the Samoa Financial Services Authority or operates under a registered intermediary arrangement with a licensed commercial bank. Legal constraints: (i) no passporting rights; (ii) UBO and AML/CFT obligations fall entirely on the licensed Samoa bank; (iii) foreign EMIs must not handle WST-denominated transactions without express CBS approval; (iv) any SWIFT-derived clearing must be done through a corresponding banking relationship to avoid domestic settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for a standard resident Samoan company; 0% effective rate for an International Company (IC) holding a valid tax exemption under the International Companies Act 1987

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by an International Company to resident or non-resident shareholders; the general Samoan statutory rate on dividends from a standard resident company is 15%, but the International Companies Act 1987 exempts all IC distributions from withholding tax

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 30 days after each anniversary date of incorporation; the filing date is anchored to the registration anniversary, not to any fiscal year-end

Deadline: · Frequency:

Immediately upon any change in shareholders, directors, registered office, or business scope, and in any event not later than 30 days after the anniversary of incorporation; no fiscal-year-end corporate return deadline applies

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold is published in the Samoan Income Tax Act 2012; the Samoa Revenue Authority expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where aggregate cross-border related-party payments exceed WST 1,000,000 per annum, while a pure equity holding SPV normally has no intercompany sales and faces documentation exposure only through its parent's Master File and Country-by-Country obligations in the parent jurisdiction

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations apply OECD-aligned substance tests to International Companies operating in geographically mobile activities, including banking, insurance, financing, headquarters, shipping, and IP holding. A pure equity holding SPV qualifies for the reduced substance test: it must maintain a registered office and resident agent in Samoa, keep board minutes and accounting records in Samoa, hold at least one board meeting in Samoa annually, and demonstrate adequate local governance; failure to satisfy the reduced test results in penalties, strike-off, and automatic exchange of information to foreign tax authorities

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abuse of Tax-Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Retroactive revocation of the tax exemption, assessment at the 27% corporate rate on all concealed income, late payment interest, an evasion penalty of up to 150% of the tax avoided, and a criminal fine of up to WST 200,000; conviction may carry imprisonment of up to seven years under the Income Tax Act 2012

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false declarations are personally assessed jointly and severally where the company is a sham or a facade; the court may issue travel bans, freeze personal assets, and impose disqualification from acting as a director in Samoa for up to ten years
Shell Company and Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Automatic strike-off by the Registrar under the International Companies Act 1987; restoration of the company requires an originating application to the Supreme Court of Samoa, payment of all outstanding government fees and penalties, and the filing of corrected records; information on the struck-off entity is exchanged with treaty partners without notice to the company

Director personal liability: Directors of a struck-off company remain personally liable for company debts and governmental fees; the court may pierce the corporate veil on proof of sham, render directors liable for civil restitution to creditors and foreign tax authorities, and prohibit the directors from incorporating a new Samoan entity for up to ten years
Money Laundering and Concealment of Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Samoa's Money Laundering Prevention Act with penalties of up to fourteen years' imprisonment and confiscation of assets; the resident agent that failed to conduct customer due diligence also faces regulatory fines, licence suspension, and criminal sanctions for negligent reporting

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent face criminal prosecution as principals where they knowingly facilitate laundering; courts routinely issue restraint orders freezing personal bank accounts, travel bans pending trial, and disqualification orders; indemnities in the company articles are void as contrary to public policy in money laundering cases
Transfer Pricing and Profit Stripping Abuse

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income to the arm's length standard under the Income Tax Act 2012, disallowance of excessive deductions, application of the 150% penalty on understated tax, and default interest from the original filing date; the Ministry of Revenue may also issue a direction to re-domicile the foreign parent's service agreements

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fabricated invoices or backdated contracts incur personal civil liability for the tax adjustment where they acted outside bona fide commercial judgment; criminal prosecution for tax evasion is available and includes travel restrictions, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment
