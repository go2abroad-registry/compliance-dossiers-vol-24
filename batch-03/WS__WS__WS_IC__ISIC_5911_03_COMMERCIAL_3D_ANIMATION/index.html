Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MFR), Samoa — sole national tax authority under the Tax Administration Act 2016, administering income tax, Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST), and excise; online taxpayer portal: MFR e-Services at https://www.mfr.gov.ws; head office: Level 2, FMFMII Building, Sogi, Apia, Samoa.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company (IC) with the Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), by filing the company constitution, registered office consent, and director/shareholder particulars under the International Companies Act 1987; standard turnaround 1–3 business days. Step 2: Receive the Certificate of Incorporation bearing the IC registration number. Step 3: Apply to the Ministry for Revenue for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) via the MFR e-Services portal or in person, submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, identification of directors and beneficial owners, and registered office details; TIN issuance typically takes 1–5 business days and is required to open the WS_IC's Wise B2B or SWIFT banking facilities. Step 4: No VAGST registration is required for an IC conducting exclusively offshore business because the IC is outside the Samoan indirect tax nexus and is statutorily exempt under s.111 of the International Companies Act 1987. Step 5: If the IC voluntarily elects VAGST registration to recover input tax on any local procurement, a VAGST registration application must be filed with MFR; registration is mandatory only where taxable turnover actually sourced in Samoa reaches the statutory threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — a Samoa International Company is exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty on all income and transactions carried on outside Samoa by operation of s.111 of the International Companies Act 1987; the exemption is absolute, requires no election, and applies automatically upon incorporation, provided the IC refrains from trading with Samoa residents except under the limited statutory exceptions.

Available election forms

Samoa has no U.S.-style check-the-box regime and has not enacted OECD hybrid mismatch election rules. The IC tax-exempt classification is mandatory and non-elective. Available elections: (i) voluntary VAGST registration under the VAGST Act 1992 for a person making exclusively zero-rated exported supplies, enabling input-credit recovery; (ii) conversion or continuation election under the International Companies Act 1987 to redomicile the IC into or out of Samoa, or conversion into a local company under the Companies Act 2006, which would subject the entity to ordinary Samoan domestic taxation; (iii) no annual filing is required to maintain the s.111 exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 200,000 (Samoan Tala) annual taxable turnover sourced in Samoa; an International Company supplying exclusively offshore export services is outside the VAGST nexus and is not required to register regardless of turnover volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of services is zero-rated under the VAGST Act 1992 where the recipient is a non-resident of Samoa and is outside Samoa at the time of supply and the services are not performed for or supplied to a person in Samoa. The WS_IC's commercial 3D animation and VFX deliverables (rendered footage, CGI character animation assets, visual effects production services, and licence rights) supplied to overseas studios, broadcasters, and platforms qualify as exported services. Independently, s.111 of the International Companies Act 1987 exempts the IC from all Samoan taxation on offshore income, so no VAGST is chargeable even outside the strict zero-rating analysis; voluntary VAGST registration would permit refund of input tax on local expenses attributable to such exported supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration together with an itemized Airway Bill or Bill of Lading where physical media or deliverables are shipped
  • Signed service or production agreement identifying the non-resident client, scope of 3D animation/VFX deliverables, and licence territory
  • Commercial invoice referencing the applicable contract and expressly stating that the supply is exported and zero-rated
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT / Wise B2B payment confirmation evidencing foreign-currency receipt from the non-resident client
  • Digital delivery log, FTP/cloud transfer manifest, or delivery receipt evidencing cross-border transmission of animation files where no physical goods are exported
  • Client attestation of non-residence (Certificate of Non-Residence) where the client is a foreign affiliate, co-producer, or production venture

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in Samoa. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue serially numbered tax invoices stating VAGST separately, retain records in electronic or paper form, and file returns through the MFR e-Services portal; there is no third-party clearance platform or fiscal device integration requirement.

MFR audit practice applies a 180-day commercial benchmark for repatriation of export proceeds; Samoa imposes no statutory foreign-exchange deadline, but failure to evidence timely inward remittance may trigger an export zero-rating review and denial of input-credit refunds. Under the Tax Administration Act 2016, incorrect zero-rating claims or false export documentation attract a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax avoided, plus statutory interest at the prescribed annual rate from the date the tax should have been paid; assessments may be reopened for up to 5 years (7 years in cases of fraud), and criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment for knowingly false declarations. For a statutory International Company, the s.111 exemption is the operative shield, and clawback exposure is limited to the narrow circumstance of the IC engaging in non-exempt onshore Samoan activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a managed-float currency without free offshore convertibility; all lawful conversions must be executed through a CBS-licensed authorized dealer. Current-account payments are processed on documented commercial justification, whereas capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, and profit remittances) require prior CBS approval through prescribed Exchange Control forms. A Samoa International Company is non-resident and tax-exempt, and genuine offshore transactions fall outside the domestic exchange-control perimeter; however, any credit to an onshore WST account or use of a local non-resident account triggers CBS forms and reporting. Profit remittance by onshore trading entities is subject to Form EC/8, tax clearance, audited financial statements, and source-funds verification; no offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) market exists for WST.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Domestic resident exporters must repatriate foreign-currency service or goods proceeds to Samoa within 30 days of receipt and surrender them to an authorized dealer, reflecting a 100% statutory conversion ratio, unless CBS grants an External (Foreign Currency) Account concession, in which case retention may be 100% against quarterly FX returns. A Samoa International Company maintaining an approved non-resident/offshore account is not subject to onshore surrender; if export-style service proceeds are credited to a resident WST account, the 30-day repatriation and mandatory conversion obligation is triggered unless formally waived by CBS.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa / CBS), administering the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1982, and licensing commercial banks under the Financial Institutions Act 1996; offshore International Companies (ICs) are incorporated under the International Companies Act 2016 administered by the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for in-country regulated banking. Samoa has no digital EMI licensing framework, and Wise B2B is not locally licensed or directly available to a WS-domiciled IC because Samoa is not a supported Wise Business jurisdiction and no WST balance or local settlement access exists. Legal constraint: the Financial Institutions Act 1996 reserves deposit-taking for licensed banks, and the Exchange Control Act 1976 requires CBS-authorized dealers for Samoa-channeled FX. Wise B2B may be used only as an outbound disbursement rail through a supported-jurisdiction sub-entity, with underlying CBS and substance approvals where applicable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable to the IC: no local transfer pricing documentation is filed in Samoa because the IC has no assessable income. Samoa Income Tax Act 2012 ss. 66-69 apply the arm's length standard and a 3:1 debt-equity thin-capitalization ceiling to taxable residents but publish no Local File or Master File monetary thresholds. As best practice, the IC should retain transfer pricing and contract documentation in English to defend intercompany animation and 3D IP invoices in the shareholder's tax jurisdiction

Economic substance scope: No Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) modelled on BVI or Cayman apply in Samoa; the IC exemption does not condition on local staffing or physical presence. However, the International Companies Act mandates a licensed resident agent and registered office, and the 2023 OECD/EU transparency standards mean an IC without genuine commercial substance - real animation contracts, production files, cash-flow reconciliation and board oversight - will be recharacterized in the home jurisdiction as a controlled foreign company or a sham. The practical substance requirement therefore equals the head-office and record-keeping discipline of the commercial buyer

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Sham Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: Loss of the s. 217 exemption if any domestic Samoa activity is admitted; in the shareholder's jurisdiction - full tax re-assessment, understatement penalties up to 100-200%, interest compounding and criminal fines

Director personal liability: Anti-avoidance rules and fraudulent-trading doctrines pierce the corporate veil: directors are jointly and severally liable for the hidden profit pool; court-ordered travel bans and asset freezes; imprisonment of up to 7 years is available in charging jurisdictions
