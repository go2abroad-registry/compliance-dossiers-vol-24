Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Apia; official portal: www.sra.gov.ws, e-filing via the STARS online revenue system. International Companies are regulated by the Registrar of International Companies / Corporate and Business Registry (CBR), Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), Apia.

E-government filing portal

Foreign owners incorporate a Samoa International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended), solely through a licensed registered agent. Step 1: file Articles/Certificate of Incorporation (Chinese-language articles permitted) with the Registrar; Step 2: obtain Certificate of Incorporation and pay registration fee; Step 3: the IC is statutorily exempt from income tax and VAGST and therefore does not apply for a Samoan TIN/VAT ID; Step 4: file the annual return and pay the annual government fee to CBR by 30 June; Step 5: a TIN from SRA (Form IRD-1) is required only if the entity nevertheless undertakes a taxable activity, which is generally prohibited for ICs; Step 6: if the IC qualifies as an investment entity under CRS/AEOI, it must separately register with SRA as a Reporting Financial Institution.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (International Companies Act 1987 s.114: full exemption from Samoan income tax, stamp duty, capital gains tax and all other Samoan taxes for 20 years from incorporation, renewable for a further 5 years; wholly offshore territorial scope; exemption conditional on prohibition of trading with Samoan residents and on derivation of no Samoan-source income)

Available election forms

No Samoan check-the-box, US Section 7701, or hybrid-mismatch election exists; the tax exemption is automatic by statute. Available elections: (i) elect the IC's financial year-end and accounting currency under the ICA; (ii) apply to the Minister for a 5-year extension of the 20-year exemption; (iii) elect express versus standard incorporation; (iv) elect redomiciliation into or out of Samoa. For US federal tax purposes, classification of the Samoa IC is determined by the beneficial owner's US check-the-box election, not by Samoan law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT$150,000 annual value of taxable supplies made in Samoa (compulsory registration); voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. International Companies carrying on wholly offshore activities are outside the scope of VAGST because they are prohibited from making taxable supplies to Samoan residents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, and services supplied to and consumed wholly outside Samoa (including offshore professional and management consultancy services to non-residents), are zero-rated under the VAGST Act 1997-1998 s.9. The supplier must evidence physical export or offshore supply and retain the contract, logistics, and payment records. A Samoa IC engaged purely in cross-border tax structuring and advisory services to non-residents has no Samoan VAGST exposure and will not need to claim zero-rating or refunds.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration (CusDec) endorsed by Samoa Customs for physical exports
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Samoa (not applicable to pure offshore consultancy services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing settlement of the exported supply
  • Executed service agreement and zero-rated supplies declaration for cross-border professional services

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-mandated e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime exists in Samoa. Registered persons must issue standard VAGST tax invoices (paper or PDF), retain records for at least 5 years, and file periodic VAGST returns through SRA's STARS e-services portal; compliance is enforced through periodic audit rather than transactional electronic validation.

Samoa imposes no foreign-exchange controls, and therefore no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline applies to ICs. However, the Samoa Revenue Authority may impose penalty tax of up to 150% of any VAGST shortfall, daily late-filing penalties, and interest under the VAGST Act 1997-1998 and the Tax Administration Act 2012. Fraudulent zero-rating and refund claims are clawed back in full with interest and penal assessments, and expose the offender to criminal fines and imprisonment under the Crimes Act 2013. An IC that breaches the International Companies Act restrictions on trading with Samoan residents forfeits its statutory tax exemption retroactively and becomes taxable on all Samoan-source income.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a current-account convertible currency under Samoa's IMF Article VIII obligations; current-account payments (trade, services, fees) are free from CBS restrictions, but the capital account remains regulated and requires prior CBS approval through a licensed commercial bank. A Samoan International Company (IC) is treated as a non-resident offshore entity: its foreign-currency accounts may be credited and disbursed offshore without CBS prior approval, and dividends, management fees, and liquidation proceeds remitted to non-resident shareholders are exempt from Samoan income tax, withholding tax, and FX conversion controls. Profit remittance hurdles arise only when funds are converted into WST or credited to a domestic onshore account, in which case the remitting bank must undertake CBS external-transaction reporting and, for amounts above the bank's delegated threshold, obtain CBS scheduled-payment approval; a tax clearance certificate from the Samoa Ministry of Revenue and an audited or management-prepared financial statement demonstrating settled liabilities are standard conditions for any onshore-account outward remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoan legislation requires all export and service-export proceeds (including cross-border consultancy fees) to be repatriated to Samoa within 30 calendar days from the date of receipt abroad and surrendered to a CBS-licensed dealer bank for conversion into WST, with a mandatory conversion ratio of 100% of the proceeds, unless the exporter holds a CBS-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) exemption that permits retention of up to 100% of proceeds in foreign currency for verified operational needs. For a non-resident International Company, the 30-day surrender obligation is disapplied where the CBS/registered bank records the IC as a non-resident entity and the receipts are credited to a designated offshore or non-resident FCY account; however, if the IC's fees are routed through a domestic WST account, the full surrender rule reactivates and conversion into WST is mandatory.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS / Fa'aputugatupe Tutotonu o Samoa), operating under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and exercising delegated exchange-control functions under the Exchange Control Act 1976 (as amended); it is the sole licensing, foreign-exchange, payment-system, and sanctions-screening authority for all banks and money transfer operators in Samoa.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility. Samoa has not enacted an electronic-money-institution (EMI) or payment-service-provider licensing regime, and no domestic fintech offers offshore USD treasury accounts; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Mercury) generally decline to onboard Samoan International Companies because of correspondent-bank de-risking, the absence of a Samoan regulatory passport, and FATF-style jurisdictional due-diligence friction. The practical legal constraint is that an EMI domiciled in Australia, the UK, or the EU cannot maintain a WST settlement account with CBS, and a Samoan IC cannot obtain an independent EMI license; the recommended architecture is a primary account at BSP or SCB supplemented by a separately regulated Australian or New Zealand International Merchant Account for card-clearing and low-value disbursements, with all FX conversion executed through the licensed Samoan dealer bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing is via SWIFT MT103 from the parent or shareholder's bank to the IC's non-resident USD account at BSP or SCB, using the Samoan Bank-Branch Identifier plus the IC's account number and the narrative purpose code 'Share Capital – Direct Investment Equity' together with the statement 'Subscription for shares under the International Companies Act 1987'; the remitting bank must classify the payment under IMF BPM6 Direct Investment – Equity (other than reinvestment of earnings), and the receiving Samoan bank records the credit in CBS External-Transaction Reporting (ETR) with a note that the beneficiary is a non-resident IC, thereby avoiding onshore surrender. Verification required by the receiving bank comprises a certified board resolution approving the share allotment, an extract from the share register showing the subscriber, the Certificate of Incorporation, proof of payment of the SIFA annual fee, and a registered-agent compliance letter; credits to a domestic WST account would additionally require CBS approval, an FX dealing slip, and a copy of the applicable Samoan tax registration, so the non-resident FCY routing is strongly preferred.Operational trade disbursements (subcontractor fees, software licenses, professional indemnity premiums, and client-reimbursable expenses) are cleared via SWIFT MT103 with charges option 'OUR' to guarantee full value to beneficiaries; larger structured procurements may use an advised Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) issued by BSP or ANZ with documents handled through the Australian/NZ correspondent corridor, and any domestic WST payments for local registered-agent or government fees settle through the CBS-operated Real-Time Gross Settlement system in Apia. Credit-card inflows are captured through the Visa/Mastercard acquiring service of BSP Samoa or SCB, with daily settlement into the IC's WST operational account and an automatic USD/WST conversion executed at the bank's daily dealing rate; the IC should maintain a segregated USD clearing account to minimize conversion friction and should require card acquirers to remit settlement in USD where the scheme permits.Onshore outright USD/WST forward contracts with BSP, ANZ, or SCB for tenors up to 12 months, requiring an underlying current-account or capital-account exposure and CBS reporting; the instruments settle by net delivery in WST at maturity.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on WST quoted by Sydney, Singapore, and Hong Kong counterparties for non-resident ICs, with USD cash settlement on a notional principal and reference rate based on the CBS published mid-rate; no domestic CBS approval applies because settlement occurs entirely outside Samoa.Cross-currency swaps (USD/AUD/NZD versus WST) executed offshore between the IC and an international bank, limited to risk-management of forecast fee revenue and exempt from CBS approval for non-resident accounts; onshore-referenced swaps require prior CBS capital-account authorization.European-style FX options on WST purchased from international prime brokers or the Samoan dealing banks for hedging invoice exposure, with premiums payable in USD from the non-resident account; writing of options by the IC is not permissible without CBS authorization.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (resident taxable company rate, applied to taxable income exceeding WST 10,000, with a 15% rate on the first WST 10,000); 0% for a registered International Company under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends paid by a resident taxable Samoa company to a non-resident shareholder; 0% for a registered International Company distributing profit to non-resident shareholders under the International Companies Act statutory exemption

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed WST 1,000,000 or intra-group service charges exceed WST 500,000; Master File: mandatory where the consolidated group revenue exceeds WST 10,000,000; an International Company in the exempt zone is generally outside SRC's transfer pricing compliance net unless it operates a resident taxable branch or earns Samoa-source income

Economic substance scope: No standalone Samoa Economic Substance Regulations statute for International Companies; substance is evaluated through the registered office in Samoa, the engagement of a licensed registered agent, real board meetings and minutes, staffing and professional competence in the tax-structuring consultancy, and independent funding; the OECD/EU substance framework and Samoa's place-of-effective-management test under the Income Tax Act 2012 govern treaty access and foreign CFC reclassification; letterbox entities are vulnerable to foreign anti-abuse rules, EU list sanctions, and Samoan registrar strike-off

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Structuring

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 2012 sections 85-87: penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded, additional civil penalties up to WST 100,000, criminal conviction with imprisonment up to 5 years, and deregistration of the International Company by the Registrar

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties; foreign directors face travel bans, international arrest notifications, and civil restitution orders; fraudulent misuse of the corporate form pierces the corporate veil and exposes personal assets
Shell Company Abuse / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Registrar powers under the International Companies Act: strike-off, restoration conditions, and financial penalties up to WST 250,000; foreign-tax reclassification removes the IC exemption and triggers back-tax assessments with penalties in the operating jurisdiction

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false substance declarations commit a criminal offence; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud; directors are personally liable for refunds and tax restatements arising from the collapsed structure
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Samoa Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018: civil penalties up to WST 500,000, custodial sentences up to 14 years, asset forfeiture, and mandatory reporting obligations to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit; licensed agent revocation

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally criminally liable; travel bans and INTERPOL red notices are available; professional disqualification of the directors from acting as International Company service providers
Fiscal Non-Compliance (Annual Filings and Withholding Duties)

Statutory sanction: Late fee surcharges of 50% of unpaid fees, automatic strike-off, disqualification from suing in Samoa courts, and revenue debts attaching to the IC's Samoa bank accounts; SRC may impose daily default assessments

Director personal liability: Directors who act after strike-off are personally liable for all debts incurred; unpaid withholding tax imposes a director's personal statutory duty; travel bans are issued for unresolved revenue assessments exceeding WST 50,000
