Governing statute

Companies Act 71 of 2008 of South Africa, Chapter II (Incorporation), sections 14-20; section 19(1)(b)(i)-(iii) (Proprietary Limited Company), section 70 (registered office), section 66 (first directors and officers)

Formation / constitutional filings

Notice of Incorporation (CoR 14.1A) and Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI), filed electronically through CIPC e-Services Portal

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a private company; however, the board must apply the solvency and liquidity test under section 4 read with section 44/45 before issuing shares or making distributions, and retained capital must be sufficient to meet the company's anticipated obligations at commencement; no escrow bank account is statutorily mandated unless prescribed by the MOI or a professional escrow arrangement is elected for third-party verification of share subscription funds.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local fire authority under the National Building Regulations (SANS 10400) confirming access and fire-fighting equipment adequacy; structural load inspection certificate from a registered professional engineer confirming safe support of deep slurry trenching equipment and cranes; occupational clearance from the Department of Employment and Labour confirming compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Act 85 of 1993), including construction site risk assessments, fall protection, confined space entry protocols, and medical surveillance of workers.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a private company; however, the board must apply the solvency and liquidity test under section 4 read with section 44/45 before issuing shares or making distributions, and retained capital must be sufficient to meet the company's anticipated obligations at commencement; no escrow bank account is statutorily mandated unless prescribed by the MOI or a professional escrow arrangement is elected for third-party verification of share subscription funds.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal: www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must first complete CIPC incorporation, then register for income tax via SARS form IT77 (application for company registration) and for VAT via form VAT101. Registration is submitted through SARS eFiling, a SARS branch, or by email with supporting documents: original certificate of incorporation (with notarized English translation if foreign), memorandum of incorporation, public officer appointment (section 246 Tax Administration Act), directors' identity documents, share register, and proof of registered office. SARS issues the Income Tax Reference Number and VAT vendor number. Where the entity is a foreign company (external company) contracting in South Africa, SARS also requires a representative taxpayer appointment under sections 152-153 TAA and registration for PAYE/UIF/SDL where employees are engaged.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident (Pty) Ltd (incorporated in ZA) is subject to 27% corporate income tax on worldwide income (rate for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% prior). A non-resident external company is taxed on South African-source income at 27% with a 15% branch remittance tax on remitted branch profits (subject to treaty relief). No territorial exemption or tax-exempt zone is automatically available; Special Economic Zones provide targeted incentives only upon application. Profits distributed as dividends attract 20% dividends withholding tax. — A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must first complete CIPC incorporation, then register for income tax via SARS form IT77 (application for company registration) and for VAT via form VAT101. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident (Pty) Ltd (incorporated in ZA) is subject to 27% corporate income tax on worldwide income (rate for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% prior). A non-resident external company is taxed on South African-source income at 27% with a 15% branch remittance tax on remitted branch profits (subject to treaty relief). No territorial exemption or tax-exempt zone is automatically available; Special Economic Zones provide targeted incentives only upon application. Profits distributed as dividends attract 20% dividends withholding tax.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification follows incorporation and source rules. Available statutory elections include: section 9I Headquarter Company election (reduces effective tax on qualifying foreign dividends and interest to 8%); section 9D(9) CFC net-income election (alternate transactional basis for controlled foreign company income); VAT group registration election under section 50 of the VAT Act (intra-group supplies disregarded); VAT input tax apportionment election under section 17(1) (turnover-based or activity-based apportionment for mixed use); and section 11D R&D allowance election. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory under sections 23M/23N (following BEPS Action 2) with required taxpayer documentation; they are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: taxable supplies exceeding R1,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months or reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in the next 12 months (section 23(1)). Voluntary registration: taxable supplies exceeding R50,000 in a 12-month period, subject to SARS discretion and security requirements (section 23(1A)). Below R50,000 no registration is permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the vendor supplies goods under an export agreement and the goods are physically removed from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply; supplies to export hubs/couriers, to customs-controlled areas, and for diplomatic consumption also qualify. Services: zero-rated under section 11(2)(k)/(m) when supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected with immovable property located in South Africa or with movable property situated in South Africa at the time of supply. Deep foundation and diaphragm wall works performed on South African land for local developers are standard-rated at 15%; specialized geotechnical services rendered offshore on foreign sites to non-resident clients may qualify for 0%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged via SARS Customs EDI
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from the port of loading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from an authorized dealer evidencing receipt of export proceeds in South Africa
  • Commercial invoice and signed export contract
  • Packing list and freight documentation
  • Proof of export removal (e.g., container terminal export gate/goods-out reports)

SARS requires full tax invoices for taxable supplies exceeding R5,000 (abridged invoices permitted below this threshold), issued within 21 days of the supply. South Africa has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or government digital stamping regime as of the current reporting period; SARS eFiling and the SARS Customs EDI (SAD500 declarations) are the mandatory digital channels for VAT and customs filings. SARS has published an e-invoicing roadmap proposing phased mandatory e-invoicing from 2026; until then, e-invoicing is voluntary, but electronic documents must satisfy the authenticity, integrity, and 5-year retention requirements of the VAT Act and Tax Administration Act.

SARS may disallow zero-rating and recoup output VAT with interest where the documentary bundle is incomplete or the 90-day export removal deadline is missed. Under Tax Administration Act sections 222-223, understatement penalties range from 0% to 200% of the tax shortfall (substantial understatement up to 25%; reasonable care not taken up to 50%; gross negligence up to 150%; intentional tax evasion 200%), plus interest at the SARS prescribed rate (repo rate plus up to 7 percentage points). Customs & Excise Act false-export declaration penalties include fines up to R10,000 per offence, penalties up to 200% of duty, and imprisonment up to 10 years for fraudulent exports. Fraudulent refund claims trigger SARS refund-repayment holdbacks, parallel criminal referral, and possible joint and several liability of directors, the public officer, or the tax representative for the company's VAT debts under TAA sections 159-160.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a restricted-currency environment with full current-account liberalisation but controlled capital-account flows. Current-account payments for trade in goods and services, construction subcontracts, equipment imports, professional fees, and ordinary commercial expenses may be settled through an Authorised Dealer without prior SARB approval provided the bank holds documented underlying transactions. Capital inflows by non-residents into South African equity, including subscription for shares in a (Pty) Ltd, are generally permitted and are reportable to the SARB only; no prior exchange control approval is required for an equity capital injection. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are executable through an Authorised Dealer when the company is tax-compliant, the dividend withholding tax obligation has been discharged by withholding to SARS, the ultimate beneficial ownership of the shareholder is established, and there is no outstanding exchange control restriction. Capital repatriation of equity or shareholder loans requires an approved exit route, normally via a formal application through an Authorised Dealer or FinSurv, supported by SARS tax clearance and audited evidence of the original foreign injection. In practice, the principal remittance hurdles are documentary completeness, tax compliance, beneficial-ownership transparency, and the need to demonstrate the original ZAR funding did not arise from domestic borrowing that should have been classified as a local loan.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South African exchange control does not require exporters to convert export receipts into rand; however, all export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa and received within 180 days from the date of shipment or the date of export, whichever is earlier. Proceeds must be credited, in foreign currency, to a Single Foreign Currency Account maintained with an Authorised Dealer in South Africa, or sold to the Authorised Dealer for rand. The exporter is obliged to provide the bank with a customs bill of entry, commercial invoice, shipping documents, and any applicable export permit. Retention of export proceeds outside South Africa, extended repatriation periods, or conversion into rand beyond the authorised-dealer process require prior FinSurv approval and will be scrutinised under transfer-pricing and exchange-control regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), and administered onshore by Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange, principally the major South African commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail and Medium as an ancillary collection/disbursement tool. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions and pure cross-border payment platforms are not licensed South African banks and cannot provide a domestic ZAR payment account, cannot issue a South African IBAN-equivalent for local EFT, cannot be used for contractual capital escrow or as the account of the (Pty) Ltd for exchange-control reporting, and cannot act as an Authorised Dealer for SARB returns. Their use is limited to minor cross-border disbursements, expense reimbursements, or vendor payments provided the provider is licensed in its home jurisdiction and the company continues to route all regulated transactions through a South African Authorised Dealer. Any EMI collecting funds on behalf of the company may create adverse tax and exchange-control attribution risk and must be restricted from receiving construction contract proceeds.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Sanctions or adverse-media concern connected to a foreign shareholder, equipment supplier, or subcontractor in a high-risk jurisdiction.

Root cause: Sanctions or adverse-media concern connected to a foreign shareholder, equipment supplier, or subcontractor in a high-risk jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Provide a consolidated sanctions-screening report covering OFAC, UN, UK, EU, and South African sanctions lists; negative adverse-media search results; sworn source-of-funds declarations; audited financial statements of the shareholder and principal suppliers; valid passports and proof of address; commercial invoices with bills of lading proving the physical construction-equipment transaction; and a foreign correspondent-bank confirmation letter showing the original funding bank.
Non-transparent beneficial-ownership chain involving nominees, multi-tier foreign holding companies, or trusts.

Root cause: Non-transparent beneficial-ownership chain involving nominees, multi-tier foreign holding companies, or trusts.

Defense evidence: Submit the CIPC Beneficial Ownership Register filing; notarised UBO declaration identifying each natural person owning or controlling the entity; memorandum of incorporation and shareholder register; trust deed, trustee identification, and trust resolutions if any trust interest exists; a legal opinion confirming there are no nominee or undisclosed principal arrangements; and the FICA ownership-and-control structure chart.
Material mismatch between the value of the inbound equity capital injection and the company's balance-sheet capacity or construction contract pipeline.

Root cause: Material mismatch between the value of the inbound equity capital injection and the company's balance-sheet capacity or construction contract pipeline.

Defense evidence: Provide unqualified audited financial statements for the past two years; contract award letters for deep slurry trenching and diaphragm wall works; project cash-flow forecasts; board minutes approving the capital call; bank transfer instructions evidencing the source and purpose of funds; acquisition invoices for specialist diaphragm-wall plant, crawler cranes, and bentonite slurry equipment; and an independent valuation report if machinery is imported under an intra-group sale.
Construction-industry cash-payment red flags in subcontractor and site-labour payments.

Root cause: Construction-industry cash-payment red flags in subcontractor and site-labour payments.

Defense evidence: Present the company AML/CFT control manual; an internal cash-confidentiality policy expressly prohibiting uncontrolled cash payments; complete subcontractor KYC files; employer payroll records and electronic payroll remittances; SARS-compliant VAT returns showing input VAT on subcontractor invoices; signed achievement/completion certificates; site-labour attendance registers; and bank statements reflecting all payments through traceable EFT rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign equity capital for the (Pty) Ltd must be routed through a South African Authorised Dealer's beneficiary account using SWIFT MT103 or MT202, with the company's full name, registered address, and contract reference in the payment narrative. The transaction should be classified as direct investment equity capital in the SARB Balance-of-Payments reporting system, and the Authorised Dealer should be provided with a board-approved share subscription, a completed inward transfer form, and proof that the funds originate from a non-resident shareholder's bank account outside South Africa. If the capital is contributed as a shareholder loan rather than equity, a formal loan agreement is required, the interest rate and terms must be arm's length, and the loan must be declared for exchange-control monitoring; such loans may be repatriated only with the same documentary regret but without prior SARB approval when made in the ordinary course. No inward transfer may be structured as a 'back-to-back' convenience or through a cash intermediary; the bank will issue an inward payment confirmation that should be retained for future dividend and capital repatriations.Domestic disbursements to local subcontractors, site labour, and suppliers must be executed in ZAR through SAMOS, standard EFT, or the national real-time clearing infrastructure, with each payment matched to a signed purchase order, progress certificate, or subcontractor invoice. Cross-border trade settlements for imported diaphragm-wall equipment, bentonite, specialised steel, and international geotechnical consultants should be made by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COVER through the operational bank, supported by an import bill of entry, invoice, and insurance documents. For large infrastructure milestones, a commercial documentary letter of credit is preferred, with the obligee and beneficiary using ICC UCP600-compliant presentation, including test certificates for slurry-wall construction, civil works survey reports, and a bank-approved performance guarantee. Contractual retentions and performance guarantees should be structured as on-demand bank guarantees or standby LCs under URDG758, with clear expiry, release, and force majeure treatment for deep-excavation and underground-water risks.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) with an Authorised Dealer for USD/ZAR and EUR/ZAR exposures arising from imported plant, overseas subcontractors, and project receivables denominated in foreign currencyCross-Currency Swaps (CCS) with an Authorised Dealer for medium-term funding or equipment-finance liabilities, converting foreign-currency debt service into ZAR or fixing exchange risk on a multi-year construction programmeVanilla FX Options purchased from an Authorised Dealer to hedge contingent tender and milestone exposures, particularly where a bid bond or performance guarantee is denominated in USDExchange-traded USD/ZAR currency futures on a JSE-licensed exchange, used only where the treasury mandate permits margined instruments and the exposure is evidenced by an underlying trade contractOffshore Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) only with prior FinSurv approval and only for a documented offshore ZAR exposure; NDFs are not a standard free instrument for South African resident companies because the ZAR is deliverable onshore and the use of an unauthorised NDF market may constitute breach of exchange control.
