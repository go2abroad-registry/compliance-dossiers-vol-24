Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official administrative portal is SARS eFiling (www.sarsefiling.co.za), with customs operations conducted through the SARS Customs client interface and the SAD500 electronic declaration channel.

E-government filing portal

A Proprietary Limited Company (Pty) Ltd is juristically created through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Upon CIPC incorporation, the entity is automatically registered for corporate income tax, and SARS issues a nine-digit Income Tax Reference Number (taxpayer identification number for income tax purposes). Foreign shareholders are not issued a separate TIN unless they have a South African source of income or are required to register in the Republic; a non-resident shareholder who is a natural person or foreign company that does not carry on business in South Africa is not ordinarily registered for income tax. For VAT, the entity must submit the VAT 101 application via eFiling once taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold, together with supporting constitutional documents, bank confirmation, and confirmation of the CIPC registration. For export operations, the company must additionally register as a customs client with SARS Customs and obtain a customs code enabling submission of export declarations. For payroll, the entity must register as an employer for PAYE, SDL, and UIF via the EMP 101 procedure. SARS registration is supported by certified copies of founding documents (Memorandum of Incorporation), the resolution appointing a registered SARS representative, and the completion of the SARS Registration, Amendments and Verification (RAV) workflow in eFiling. The procurement SOP therefore requires: (1) CIPC incorporation and automatic IT registration; (2) verification of the IT reference number on eFiling; (3) VAT 101 registration if turnover exceeds compulsory or voluntary thresholds; (4) customs client registration for export declarations; and (5) employer and dependent statutory registrations where relevant.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed as a separate entity under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 at the corporate income tax rate. Statutory elections available include: (i) the s24J election for financial instruments to adopt an accrual or mark-to-market basis of interest accounting; (ii) the valuation date value election for capital gains tax purposes for assets held prior to 1 October 2001; (iii) the group taxation elections under s45, s47, and s48 permitting intra-group asset disposals and share-for-share roll-over treatment; (iv) the s41 group contribution election; and (v) the s11D research and development allowance election approved by the Department of Science and Innovation. Hybrid financing and hybrid mismatch arrangements are statutorily recharacterised and denied under ss8E, 8F, 8FA, 8G, 19, and 23M, leaving no elective hybrid mismatch mechanism for a standard Pty Ltd taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration applies where taxable turnover exceeds R1,000,000 in any 12-month period (s23(1)). Voluntary registration is permitted where taxable turnover exceeds R50,000 in any 12-month period (s23(2)). Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African recipients are subject to a separate registration threshold of R50,000. Zero-rated export supplies count toward the registration threshold; exempt financial and certain residential property supplies do not.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are eligible for zero-rating under s11(1)(a) VAT Act where the South African supplier has delivered the goods to a non-resident recipient and the goods are exported from South Africa within the prescribed period (generally 90 days from the date of supply), supported by documentary proof of physical removal from the Republic. The electronic patient monitor (ECG telemetry hardware) must be consigned under a customs declared SAD500 export and physically dispatched to the non-resident buyeron an arm's-length sale. Services are eligible for zero-rating under s11(2)(k) VAT Act where the services are rendered to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time the services are rendered, the services are not supplied to a non-resident in respect of movable property situated in South Africa, and the services do not relate to immovable property in South Africa. Offshore calibration or after-sale software support services for the non-resident customer must satisfy the s11(2)(k) test, including the requirement that the services are physically consumed outside South Africa and are not for the direct benefit of a person in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 filed through SARS Customs, including the unique declaration number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing dispatch from a South African port/airport to the non-resident consignee
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the ECG patient monitors and confirming the foreign consignee
  • SARS export clearance stamp, customs release notification, or port-of-entry verification confirming physical removal from South Africa
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Acknowledgement / bank settlement report confirming receipt of export proceeds in a South African bank account in the required foreign currency
  • Freight and transport documents, insurance certificates, and contract documentation evidencing the pass of title to a non-resident buyer

SARS conducts targeted audit scrutiny of export zero-rating claims by cross-matching SAD500 customs declarations, shipping/airway records, SARS bank data, and eFiling VAT returns. Where the prescribed export documentation is not retained or the goods are not demonstrably exported, the output tax previously returned at 0% is clawed back together with the reversal of any input tax claimed on related purchases, plus statutory interest. Fraudulent or negligent zero-rating gives rise to additional tax under s60 VAT Act equal to 100% of the tax evaded or the tax that is subject to the wrong reflective accounting, compounded by understatement penalties imposed under s222 and s223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 of up to 200% of the shortfall in cases of intentional evasion, together with interest at the prescribed rate. Criminal prosecution may follow for deliberate tax evasion and false statements, with fines and imprisonment, and SARS may suspend the taxpayer's customs client code or tax clearance status, which materially disrupts future export liquidity and counterparty standing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade settlements, dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees, subject to documentary compliance and prescribed banking procedures. Capital account flows, including equity injections, shareholder loan advances, and distributions, remain under exchange control oversight, requiring appropriate purpose classification, reporting to FinSurv, and in certain cases prior approval. Profit remittance via dividends is permitted without prior approval provided that the company is in good standing with SARS, audited financial statements support the dividend, and the payment is made through an Authorized Dealer with the relevant balance-of-payments code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exchange Control Regulation 7 requires that export proceeds be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless an extension is granted by FinSurv. Once received, 100% of export receipts must be remitted to the exporter's South African banking account and converted to ZAR through an Authorized Dealer or retained in an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) for permitted working capital purposes. No automatic retention or split-ratio conversion is allowed for standard manufacturing exporters; any holding of foreign currency beyond the repatriation timeline requires express exchange control approval.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), exercising exchange control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, through an Authorized Dealer network comprising licensed commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed banks under the Banks Act and cannot hold domestic South African bank account numbers in ZAR without partnering with a locally licensed Authorized Dealer. They may be acceptable for ancillary payout concentration, receivable collection, or foreign-currency liquidity pooling outside South Africa, but are not substitutable for the SARB-regulated domestic banking layer required for capital injections, trade settlement, and ZAR FX conversion. Legal constraints include FICA client due diligence, exchange control reporting, and restrictions against holding resident ZAR balances without an Authorized Dealer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign shareholder funding for share capital must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the receiving South African Authorized Dealer bank using the SARB balance-of-payments purpose category for inward direct investment in equity capital. The remittance instruction must cite the share subscription agreement and, if requested, the shelf company or incorporation certificate. The receiving bank will debit the company's foreign-liability record and issue a Foreign Exchange Inward Transfer or credit advice evidencing the inflow; this document is critical for subsequent dividend remittances or shareholder loan servicing. No prior SARB approval is required for ordinary equity subscriptions, but the bank will conduct source-of-funds verification and ensure the purpose code reflects 'Direct Investment in South Africa – Equity Capital'. The company must then update its CIPC beneficial ownership register and issue shares in accordance with the Companies Act.Supplier settlements for imported components, calibration equipment, and raw materials will be executed principally through SWIFT MT103 transfers denominated in USD, EUR, GBP, or JPY, supported by invoices, shipping documents, customs declarations, and import permits where applicable. For high-value or open-account transactions, documentary or commercial letters of credit may be established through the Authorized Dealer bank, denominated in hard currency or ZAR, with reimbursement on SWIFT MT700/MT754 circulation. All outbound trade payments above the applicable threshold require the standard proof-of-import or service documentation to be retained by the bank for exchange control audit purposes.
