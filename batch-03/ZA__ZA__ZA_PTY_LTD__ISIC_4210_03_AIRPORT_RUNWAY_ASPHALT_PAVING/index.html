Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – all domestic and international taxpayer registrations are administered via the SARS eFiling portal (sarsefiling.co.za), complemented by the SARS Customs Modernisation digital single-window for import/export clearance.

E-government filing portal

A South African Proprietary Limited Company (Pty) Ltd is first incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), obtaining a unique company registration number. The company must then register for income tax, VAT, PAYE, UIF and SDL on SARS eFiling using the CIPC registration documents and the 'About Your Company' (BAC) questionnaire. A non-resident controlling party or shareholder files Form ITREG for non-resident taxpayer registration, and VAT registration is applied for on Form VAT101 under section 22 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991. Where a foreign-owned entity has no effective local management, SARS requires the designation of a local representative taxpayer or registered VAT agent. Upon acceptance, SARS issues an income tax reference number (the statutory TIN) and, where applicable, a separate VAT registration number with an effective date determined by SARS under section 23(4), which may be earlier than the registration date where taxable supplies commenced.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa applies mandatory entity-level taxation to incorporated companies; there is no US-style check-the-box or conduit election available. A (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxpayer at the standard corporate income tax rate of 27%. Available statutory elections affecting classification or liability include: small business corporation status under section 12E of the Income Tax Act (subject to a R20 million turnover cap and all-shareholder natural-person requirements), VAT group election under section 50 of the VAT Act, the representative taxpayer election for non-residents, section 24J time-apportionment elections for interest-bearing instruments, and the hybrid debt/equity and interest-deduction limitation rules under sections 8F/8FA and 23M of the Income Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are governed by mandatory statutory rules rather than elective classification. The default classification therefore stands as Entity_Level_Taxation without pass-through eligibility.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration: ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available on reaching ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is claimable under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act for goods supplied by the exporter to a non-resident or export operation where the goods are physically removed from South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply, or within the extended period granted by SARS under section 56(3); and under section 11(2)(l) for services supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside South Africa, provided the services are exported and the recipient is not a South African resident at the time the services are rendered. For a runway-paving contractor, zero-rating applies to the exported goods embedded in the construction supply and to offshore services rendered at a foreign airport site, provided the fixed-location/situs rules are satisfied and documentary proof of foreign use is retained. No zero-rating applies to on-site South African services or goods that remain in South Africa after the contract is performed. The exporter must retain a complete export evidence file, including the declaration required under VAT section 54 and the SARS export guide TP03-02-2012.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / SARS Customs Clearance Declaration) showing the customs exit stamp and itemised goods data
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reconciling to the customs declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an authorised dealer confirming receipt of the foreign currency export proceeds
  • Proof of foreign-currency bank credit, together with the freight and insurance certificate where applicable

South Africa does not currently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-API invoice-authorisation regime as operated in certain other jurisdictions. SARS requires that valid tax invoices contain the full statutory particulars under section 20(5) of the VAT Act and be issued within 21 days after the time of supply. VAT returns (VAT201) must be filed electronically through SARS eFiling under automated risk profiling. SARS has announced a phased PEPPOL-based e-invoicing mandate, but until that mandate is formally in force, electronically generated invoices in an accessible audit file are accepted. Exports are cleared through the SARS Customs Modernisation single-window using electronic SAD500 declarations, which serve as the operative customs clearance and fiscal audit trail.

SARS subjects zero-rated exports to document-level verification of the customs trail, the shipping evidence, the recipient's non-resident status, and the foreign-currency payment flow, including reconciliation to Exchange Control reporting. Where export conditions are not substantiated, the zero-rating is disallowed, the full 15% VAT becomes payable, and interest runs from the original tax-period due date under section 239 of the Tax Administration Act (TAA). Imposition of additional tax follows under section 60 of the VAT Act and sections 222–223 of the TAA at up to 200% of the shortfall. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract criminal liability under section 58 of the VAT Act and section 235 of the TAA, with consequences including customs forfeiture, trade-reprieve suspension, director disqualification, and retrospective clawback of refunds with penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a dual-tier exchange control framework. Current account transactions are generally freely convertible subject to documentary support and reporting thresholds; capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, require prior approval or must be executed through Authorized Dealers in accordance with SARB rulings. Profit remittances are permitted for non-residents via ordinary banking channels provided that the remitting entity lodges an annual compliance certificate, retains audited financial statements demonstrating distributable reserves, and validates payment of all relevant taxes (including Dividends Tax and Corporate Income Tax). Remittance of profits is not subject to a cap but must be routed through an Authorized Dealer with correct purpose codes and supporting board resolutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

In terms of Exchange Control Ruling 4.1, export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days of the date of shipment, unless an extension has been granted by FinSurv. The exporter must furnish the Authorized Dealer with the original export documents (bill of lading, commercial invoice, customs declaration) and a detailed receipt declaration. Where foreign currency earnings are to be retained abroad, prior approval is required, and if repatriated, the proceeds must be converted to South African Rand (ZAR) upon receipt unless a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) has been approved to hold the currency. The default mandatory conversion ratio is 100% conversion to ZAR except where an FCA retention allowance has been granted; in practice, no partial surrender ratio exists under the current exchange control dispensation.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility for offshore digital EMIs is rated 'Medium'. South African corporate clients may engage authorized non-resident electronic money institutions (EMIs) for treasury functions only if the EMI is formally licensed and supervised in its home jurisdiction, has a valid SARB/RMCD approval where relevant, and can provide transparent transaction monitoring and reporting interfaces. However, a cross-border EMI cannot serve as a primary settlement bank for domestic VAT, PAYE, and COD remittances, and the Company Payoff Account (COA) requirements in the FICA Act mandate that an account-holding institution must be a SARB-licensed bank. Legal constraints include the need to register a local branch or representative office to conduct financial services in South Africa, and the EMI must adhere to the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services Act (FAIS) if providing advice; otherwise, it may only offer pure money transfer services under an approved FinTech sandbox or E-Money Licence with a limited systemic footprint.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from the non-resident shareholder must be routed through an Authorized Dealer in South Africa via SWIFT with an appropriate purpose code (e.g., 'A0 – Equity Capital Investment' or 'PC 300' for inward transfers). The receiving bank will require an inward transfer request (Form A) or the new-exchange-control e-application (ExCon) containing the company's registration number, a copy of the shareholder resolution, and a certificate of incorporation or equivalent from the remitting jurisdiction. All funds must be denominated in a freely convertible currency (USD, EUR, GBP) and converted to ZAR at the market rate on the settlement date unless the company opens a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) to retain the injection. The SARB-licensed bank will also issue a BOP (Balance of Payments) declaration for the transaction, and the company must retain proof of the inward transfer for to foreign-investment reporting under the South African Reserve Bank's Financial Surveillance Guide.Trade settlements for supplier payments under the airport runway paving project (is: 'polymer-modified bitumen, aggregates, and plant hire') are cleared via the South African payment system using SWIFT MT103 for direct beneficiary transfers and commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) for conditional trade instruments. Outbound disbursements for imports must be effectuated within the allowed open-account settlement terms (90 days from shipment date) and require submission of an exchange control Form A for each payment application above ZAR 50,000. All outbound wires must include the country-specific purpose code (e.g., T – Trade Settlement) and the central bank tracking code. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5,000,000 aggregate annual value of affected international related-party transactions triggers the mandatory maintenance of a full Local File. A Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue exceeding ZAR 10,000,000,000. SARS may also exercise audit discretion below these thresholds where material pricing risk is identified.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply EU-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the Income Tax Act's General Anti-Avoidance Rules (Sections 80A–80L), controlled foreign company rules (Section 9D), thin capitalization provisions, and treaty abuse restrictions require genuine commercial substance. An airport runway asphalt paving company must maintain local staff, physical machinery, contract management, risk control, and financial oversight in South Africa; a foreign shell that lacks such substance will be denied treaty benefits and may be re-characterized for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalties under Tax Administration Act Sections 222–223: 25% for substantial understatement, 50% for failure to take reasonable care, 100% for gross negligence, and 125% for intentional tax evasion. Interest accrues at the prescribed rate. Criminal liability carries a fine of up to ZAR 2 million or imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in submitting a false return are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall and penalties. SARS may seek a civil judgment, apply for a director travel ban, attach personal assets, and institute fraud charges that can result in imprisonment.
Shell Company / Conduit Financing Abuse

Statutory sanction: SARS disregards the avoidance arrangement under GAAR (Sections 80A–80L) and re-computes taxable income; excessive interest deductions are disallowed under thin capitalization rules; transfer pricing adjustments under Section 31 carry penalties of up to 125% of the adjustment. Criminal sanctions may apply for fraudulent conduit schemes.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the Pty Ltd is merely a facade. Directors are personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties, may be disqualified as directors, and may be prohibited from leaving South Africa under the Tax Administration Act if a tax debt remains unpaid.
AML / Aviation Sector Payment Transparency

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention; criminal forfeiture of assets under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act (POCA); imprisonment for money-laundering offenses of up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, responsible managers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability where they knew or negligently allowed AML control failures. Courts can impose travel bans, freeze personal accounts, authorize extradition, and revoke the company's civil aviation contractor license.
