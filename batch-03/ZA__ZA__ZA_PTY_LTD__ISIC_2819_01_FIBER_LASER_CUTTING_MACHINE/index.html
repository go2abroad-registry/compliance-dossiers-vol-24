Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) - eFiling portal at https://efiling.sars.gov.za; customs administered by SARS Customs Division.

E-government filing portal

For a local (Pty) Ltd, incorporate via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), then apply to SARS for an income tax reference number using IT77 via eFiling or branch, with certified memorandum of incorporation, director IDs, and registered address; VAT registration via form VAT 101 is compulsory when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 over any 12-month period, and voluntary from ZAR 50,000; foreign-controlled entities may also register as VAT vendors if they conduct an enterprise in South Africa.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box pass-through election. Available statutory elections include: Small Business Corporation (SBC) status election under s 12E Income Tax Act; VAT grouping under s 50 VAT Act; 100% capital allowance election under s 12C for manufacturing assets; CFC high-tax exception election under s 9D(2A); and hybrid mismatch self-review/notification elections under s 8E-8EA and s 23M for cross-border payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory registration when taxable supplies exceed this in any 12 consecutive months; voluntary registration available from ZAR 50,000)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods supplied and physically removed from South Africa to any export destination in terms of s 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, provided the vendor retains prescribed export documentation as required by VAT Rule 18. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under s 11(2)(l) when not used for consumption in South Africa. The vendor must include these supplies on its VAT return, claim input tax on export-related costs, and maintain an audit trail of removal, customs status verification, and (for services) nexus assessment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 / DA440.01A) and SARS customs clearance records
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or Road Consignment Note evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank credit confirmation showing receipt of export proceeds in ZAR or foreign currency account
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sales contract or purchase order
  • Preference substantiation (Certificate of Origin) if claiming tariff preference or free trade area benefits

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in force. Electronic tax invoices are legally recognised under s 20 of the VAT Act; SARS is progressively implementing Pan-African and PEPPOL e-invoicing, with a proposed phased API-based clearance framework, but full national e-invoicing clearance remains voluntary as of the current assessment year.

SARS may disallow zero-rating and reassess at 15% VAT if export evidence such as customs declarations, bills of lading, or remittance proof is missing or insufficient. Penalties include a 10% per-month late payment penalty, an understatement penalty ranging from 10% to 150% depending on behaviour (up to 150% for intentional evasion), and interest on the underpaid VAT. Criminal prosecution can be instituted under s 58 of the VAT Act, with a fine of up to ZAR 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years. Prescription periods extend to 5 years for negligence and are unlimited for fraudulent misrepresentation, allowing full retrospective clawback of claimed input VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a managed-float ZAR with full current-account convertibility for trade and ordinary business payments; capital-account convertibility is liberalised but subject to exchange-control reporting and thresholds. Inward FDI (equity and intercompany shareholder loans) requires no prior FinSurv approval, only recording through an Authorized Dealer, a capital-import register entry, and SARB balance-of-payments reporting. Outward investment by a South African (Pty) Ltd is generally approved up to ZAR 10 billion per company per calendar year, conditional on SARS tax-clearance status and FinSurv circular limits; amounts above the threshold require a prior FinSurv application. Profit and dividend remittances to a non-resident shareholder are treated as current-account flows and are remittable without prior approval, but are operationally gated by: (i) proof of non-resident beneficial ownership and shareholding; (ii) SARS Dividend Tax compliance at 20% or a treaty-reduced rate; (iii) latest audited financial statements demonstrating legally distributable reserves; and (iv) settlement exclusively through an Authorized Dealer in a prescribed foreign currency. The practical profit-remittance hurdles are documentary and compliance-driven rather than convertibility refusal, with the structural rule that ZAR may not be transferred cross-border outside the Common Monetary Area (CMA) of South Africa, Lesotho, Namibia and Eswatini.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 10, all export transactions must be declared and full export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment. There is no partial-ratio surrender formula for manufactured goods such as fiber laser cutting machines; the effective surrender obligation is 100% - all proceeds must be repatriated into the exporter's account held with an Authorized Dealer and may not be retained offshore or in a non-approved foreign account. Export receipts may be credited to a FinSurv-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) in the exporter's name, but funds may remain in the FCA only for bona fide trade-cycle needs and must be converted to ZAR when applied to domestic payments or when surplus exceeds the approved retention parameters. Authorized Dealers conduct periodic FCA monitoring and SARB reporting; any deviation (late repatriation, under-invoicing, unreported offsets or unapproved offshore retention) triggers FinSurv penalty exposure and SARS cross-checks against SAD500 export declarations and transfer-pricing records.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting as agent of the National Treasury under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Exchange Control Regulations).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury rails. Offshore digital EMI and neo-bank accounts (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, Statrys, Mercury) are not Authorized Dealers under South African exchange control and cannot lawfully receive export-proceeds surrender, house capital imports, or execute regulated outbound remittances for a South African (Pty) Ltd. Use of an offshore EMI as the company's operating account is an AML/FICA red flag (unreported inward and outward flows) and may breach the Banks Act 1990 deposit-taking prohibition; SARS and FinSurv will treat diverted export proceeds as a compliance breach. EMIs that operate through a South African-regulated banking partner or hold an FSCA/SARB licence may be used for ancillary facilitation (virtual cards, payroll, low-value expense payments) only after Enhanced Due Diligence by the onshore relationship bank, but all balance-of-payments items and trade settlements must move through the SWIFT-based Authorized Dealer rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where cross-border connected-person transactions aggregate above ZAR 5,000,000 in a fiscal year; Master File mandatory where the multinational group has consolidated revenue exceeding ZAR 10,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a nominal ESR registration regime. Substance is tested through the s80A–s80M GAAR, s103(1) nullification of abnormal tax avoidance transactions, controlled foreign company rules, and treaty beneficial-ownership requirements. A Pty Ltd manufacturing fiber laser machines must demonstrate office, employees, operational decision-making, raw material procurement, and actual production activities in South Africa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty up to 200% of the shortfall for intentional tax evasion; interest at the prescribed rate; criminal prosecution under TAA with fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are personally liable for criminal restitution, fines, travel bans, and imprisonment; assets can be seized under asset forfeiture provisions.
Shell Company / Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the company, recharacterize transactions, apply up to 200% understatement penalties, impose dividend withholding tax on deemed distributions, and restrict treaty access.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability under the Companies Act for knowingly tolerating a facade; also personal criminal liability, travel restrictions, and potential deportation or extradition proceedings.
AML and Payment-Rail Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Proceedings under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act and Financial Intelligence Centre Act: fines up to substantial statutory amounts, seizure of criminal proceeds, and imprisonment up to 30 years for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted personally as principals or accomplices; penalties include asset forfeiture, freeze orders, travel bans, criminal fines, and long-term imprisonment.
