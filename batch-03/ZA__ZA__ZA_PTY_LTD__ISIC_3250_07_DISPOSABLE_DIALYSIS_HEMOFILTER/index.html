Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — national integrated revenue and customs authority. Official portal: www.sars.gov.za. E-filing platform: www.sarsefiling.co.za for income tax, VAT, PAYE, UIF and SDL. Customs export/import declarations are processed through the SARS Customs SADEX/EDI electronic clearance interface.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); SARS automatically issues the income tax reference number (TIN) upon incorporation. Step 2: Activate the SARS eFiling profile and submit the VAT 101 registration application; compulsory VAT registration applies when taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months; voluntary VAT registration is permitted where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 per 12-month period. Step 3: A foreign parent or non-resident vendor without a fixed place of business in South Africa must appoint a representative taxpayer under section 161 of the Tax Administration Act. Step 4: Apply to SARS Customs for an importer/exporter code to file SADEX/EDI export declarations. Step 5: Register for PAYE, UIF and SDL if the company employs staff. The statutory VAT ID is the 10-digit SARS vendor number issued upon successful VAT registration; the income tax TIN is the SARS reference number allocated at incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box regime; a (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxable at entity level as a resident company on worldwide income at the 27% corporate rate. Available elections: (i) VAT group registration under section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act (single-vendor treatment for two or more commonly controlled resident companies); (ii) voluntary VAT registration from ZAR 50,000 turnover; (iii) nomination of a representative taxpayer by a non-resident vendor; and (iv) reliance on the controlled-foreign-company high-tax exemption in section 9D(2A) where the offshore effective tax rate equals or exceeds 90% of the South African corporate rate. Hybrid debt and hybrid equity instruments are mandatorily recharacterised under sections 8F and 8FA of the Income Tax Act with no taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months. Voluntary registration: taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 50,000 per 12-month period. SARS may also compulsorily register a vendor under section 23 of the VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported disposable dialysis hemofilters is claimable under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the supplier supplies movable goods to a non-resident recipient, the goods are exported in the ordinary course of business, and prescribed documentary proof of physical removal from South Africa is retained. Per the SARS VAT Guide for Exporters (VAT 409), the goods must physically depart South Africa under a customs-cleared export declaration, export evidence should ordinarily be secured within 90 days from date of supply, and all supporting records must be retained for at least five years. Failure to substantiate physical export converts the supply to standard-rated VAT at 15%.

  • SARS Customs export declaration (SAD 500 / SADEX electronic bill of entry) showing export clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment to the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and VAT export invoice expressly marked 'Zero-rated export - section 11(1)(a) VAT Act'
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming foreign exchange proceeds credited to a South African bank account
  • Packing list, transport tracking records, and warehouse release documentation

South Africa does not currently impose a live real-time fiscal clearance mandate, API-based clearance requirement, or digital stamping obligation for VAT invoices or export transactions. SARS mandates prescribed VAT invoice content under section 20 of the VAT Act (vendor name and VAT number, invoice date, description, consideration, and tax amount). Electronic tax invoices are admissible where transmitted in a reliable and unalterable format in accordance with section 28 of the Tax Administration Act. SARS's published e-invoicing roadmap provides for phased mandatory e-invoicing (structured XML/e-invoice standards) from 2025–2029 for business-to-government and business-to-business transactions; exporters currently operate under document-based compliance and SARS's risk-based refund verification model.

SARS subjects export zero-rating claims to risk-based refund verification (eRefund/eRecon and export verification units); refunds may be suspended pending proof. If zero-rating is disallowed on audit, the exporter is assessed for 15% VAT on the export proceeds plus statutory interest under section 187 of the Tax Administration Act at the prescribed rate (repo rate plus 7 percentage points). Understatement penalties under section 222 escalate by degree of fault: 10% for a substantial understatement (shortfall exceeding the greater of 5% of tax payable or ZAR 1 million), 25% for no reasonable grounds, 50% for failure to take reasonable care, 100% for gross negligence, and 150% for intentional tax evasion. Deliberate false export claims trigger criminal prosecution under sections 234 and 235 of the Tax Administration Act and section 58 of the VAT Act, with fines, imprisonment of up to 5 years, and recovery of losses. Records must be retained for 5 years from the date of the VAT return; failure to retain documentation shifts the evidentiary burden to the taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is a floating, freely convertible currency for current account transactions (trade in goods, services, dividends, interest and royalties) subject to documentary verification through Authorised Dealers (ADs). Capital account transactions, including equity and intercompany loans from non-resident shareholders, are permitted with AD-assigned purpose codes and FinSurv informed reporting; no prior SARB approval is required where the inward flow is effectuated via an AD bank with an 'A-form' or 'MP103' declaration. Profit remittances are legally unrestricted provided the company holds a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS), the dividend withholding tax (20% or lower treaty rate) is deducted, and the AD bank obtains board resolutions plus audited financial statements reflecting distributable reserves. Practical hurdles include SARS verification of the manufacturing substance, transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany margins (OECD aligned), and the requirement that export receipts be cleared through a South African AD account within 30 days, which may delay the accumulation of distributable cash.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be received into the exporter's account at an Authorised Dealer (AD bank) in South Africa within 30 days from the date of shipment, as evidenced by the bill of lading or airway bill; extensions up to 12 months are obtainable through the AD with an acceptable commercial rationale, and anything beyond 12 months requires FinSurv approval. There is no mandatory percentage conversion to ZAR; exporters may credit 100% of proceeds to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held at an AD bank or convert at the market rate, but full repatriation of the invoice value is compulsory and any shortfall must be explained with documentary proof (e.g., claims, discounts, rejections). Export declarations must be lodged on the SAD 500 customs form, and all export transactions above the self-balancing limit require the AD to reconcile the payment received with the SARB/ECB20 electronic repository.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, in conjunction with the South African Revenue Service (SARS) customs and tax compliance framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW-MEDIUM, strictly ancillary and non-primary. Under the National Payment System Act, 1998 and the Banks Act, 1990, a foreign fintech digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, or other offshore e-money institutions) has no statutory licence to function as an Authorised Dealer in foreign exchange or to receipt export proceeds on behalf of a South African company. It cannot issue the required A-form/MP103 declarations or generate a SARB-recognised Certificate of Receipt. An EMI that has obtained a deposit-taking or payment-system licence in South Africa, or that clears through a local AD partner bank, may be used for low-value or ancillary collections, but the company must still maintain a fully regulated AD bank account as the legal clearing channel for export proceeds and capital injections. Given the medical-device trade cycle and the need for documentary letters of credit, the blueprint institutes a local AD bank as the sole liquidity anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and shareholder loan injections must be routed exclusively through the designated AD bank using SWIFT MT103/MT202 with the remitter's bank including full beneficiary information (ISO 20022 structured address), the SARB purpose code for direct investment equity capital or intercompany financing, and a completed A-form (equity) or MP103 (non-equity) online declaration registered with the AD. The AD bank verifies the source of funds, issues the SARB-compliant Certificate of Receipt, and places funds into the Capital Escrow Account; release to the operational ZAR account is permitted only after SAHPRA licence evidence is lodged and the bank confirms that no residual exchange-control conditions apply. Under the National Payment System Act, 1998 and the Banks Act, 1990, a foreign fintech digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, or other offshore e-money institutions) has no statutory licence to function as an Authorised Dealer in foreign exchange or to receipt export proceeds on behalf of a South African company. It cannot issue the required A-form/MP103 declarations or generate a SARB-recognised Certificate of Receipt. An EMI that has obtained a deposit-taking or payment-system licence in South Africa, or that clears through a local AD partner bank, may be used for low-value or ancillary collections, but the company must still maintain a fully regulated AD bank account as the legal clearing channel for export proceeds and capital injections. Given the medical-device trade cycle and the need for documentary letters of credit, the blueprint institutes a local AD bank as the sole liquidity anchor.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022, i.e. for years ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% for earlier years.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividend tax on the gross amount of any dividend paid by a resident company to a non-resident beneficial owner (s.64E of the ITA). Dividends paid to South African resident companies are generally exempt if the prescribed requirements are met.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 must be filed within 12 months of the company’s financial year-end, or earlier if SARS has stipulated an earlier date by public notice or through a filing profile on eFiling. The standard South African year of assessment runs 1 March to 28/29 February unless an approved substituted financial year is used.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate annual cross-border connected-person transactions exceed ZAR 5 million. Master File is required where the multinational group’s consolidated annual revenue is at least ZAR 10 billion. Country-by-Country Report filing applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least ZAR 12.5 billion (or EUR 750 million). SARS may require TP documentation below the Local File threshold where specific risk indicators exist (chronic losses, high intangibles, limited-risk distributor role).

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no separate ESR statute equivalent to offshore-centre economic substance regulations. Substance is instead enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule (s.80A–80L ITA), the specific anti-avoidance rule (s.103(2) ITA), the CFC attribution rules (s.9D), the arm’s-length recharacterisation rule (s.31 ITA), and beneficial-ownership clauses in DTAs. A local (Pty) Ltd must demonstrate real premises, qualified personnel, active management decisions in South Africa, and commercial capacity commensurate with its functions/assets/risks; a bare shell or ‘cash-box’ intermediary will be ignored or treated as a sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty under s.222 of up to 200% of the additional tax; late-payment penalty of 10% plus prescribed interest; criminal prosecution under TAA ss.236/237 with a fine up to ZAR 30 million and imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise a false return may be held personally and jointly liable under TAA s.158 and Companies Act s.77; civil restitution, seizure of assets, a travel ban on leaving South Africa, director disqualification, and imprisonment are available sanctions.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke the GAAR (s.80A–80L) to disregard or recharacterise the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, and reduce or withdraw the DTA withholding rate; a penalty of 75%–200% of the recharacterised tax may be added.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or implemented the sham arrangement may be joined as relevant persons in the assessment, face civil restitution for the unpaid tax, be prohibited from acting as directors under Companies Act s.162, and be subjected to travel bans during investigation.
AML / FIC Act Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanction under s.71 of the Financial Intelligence Centre Act of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention, or the greater of ZAR 10 million and 10% of annual turnover for repeat non-compliance; criminal prosecution under POCA can result in fines and imprisonment up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as management authorities and may be separately fined for negligent failure to report; asset forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition in money-laundering cases may be ordered, and custodial sentences may be imposed.
Exchange Control / Unapproved Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Contraventions of the Currency and Exchanges Act and Exchange Control Regulations attract fines and imprisonment up to 10 years, forfeiture of the foreign currency amount, and, where transfer pricing applies, a s.31 adjustment plus an understatement penalty of up to 200%.

Director personal liability: Directors or assisters who sign false payment instructions or false TP substantiation are personally liable to the Authorised Dealer and the South African Reserve Bank for the amount transferred; criminal liability, travel restrictions, and arrest at the point of departure may follow.
