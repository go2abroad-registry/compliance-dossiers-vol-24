Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); national portal: SARS eFiling (efiling.sars.gov.za; sars.gov.za); customs administration: SARS Customs and Excise unit, with SAD500 electronic goods declarations lodged via the SARS Customs portal.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); CIPC's automatic data feed creates the SARS income tax reference number (TIN). (2) Activate the company profile on SARS eFiling, submit the entity registration details, and appoint a public officer as the SARS 'tax representative'. (3) Register for VAT as a vendor through eFiling (VAT101 / online application): compulsory registration applies once taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000 of annual taxable supplies. (4) Obtain a SARS Customs client code / import-export code using the same TIN. (5) Register for PAYE, UIF and SDL if employees are engaged, and file the annual income tax return (ITR14). (6) For export proceeds, complete the F178 Export Exchange Control form with the SARB-authorised dealer bank maintaining the company's foreign currency account.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the residence-based Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; corporate rate is 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through or 'check-the-box' regime exists for a standard proprietary company, and distributions remain subject to 20% dividends tax at declaration. — (1) Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); CIPC's automatic data feed creates the SARS income tax reference number (TIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the residence-based Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; corporate rate is 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through or 'check-the-box' regime exists for a standard proprietary company, and distributions remain subject to 20% dividends tax at declaration.

Available election forms

(i) Small Business Corporation (SBC) election under s12E(4) of the Income Tax Act where annual turnover does not exceed ZAR 20 million and not more than 10% of gross income is investment income, giving access to the progressive corporate rate schedule; (ii) Micro-business Turnover Tax election (Sixth Schedule) for turnover up to ZAR 1 million, which replaces income tax, VAT and provisional tax - not recommended for an export manufacturer with material input VAT refunds; (iii) Voluntary VAT vendor registration election under s23(3) of the VAT Act from ZAR 50,000 annual taxable turnover; (iv) VAT cash/payments basis election under s15(2) for qualifying small vendors whose gross income does not exceed ZAR 2.5 million; (v) s11D research and development tax allowance election on approved expenditure (additional 50% deduction) conditional on Department of Science and Innovation / SARS pre-registration of the ultrasound console development programme; (vi) Hybrid debt and hybrid equity classification elections and documentation under s8E, s8F, s8(3)(d) and s23M for any profit-participating, convertible or preference-share financing to prevent interest disallowance; and (vii) Group relief and asset-for-share elections under s42-s45 for intra-group restructuring. No check-the-box entity classification election is available under South African law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period, actual or reasonably expected, under s23(1) VAT Act; voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 of annual taxable supplies under s23(3); non-resident electronic services suppliers register from ZAR 50,000 under s23(1A).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, goods are zero-rated where they are supplied to a non-resident purchaser, are consigned or delivered to an address in an export country or physical removal from South Africa is documented, and the goods are duly exported with SARS customs clearance. The exporting (Pty) Ltd must be the SAD500 declarant, the sale must be evidenced by commercial invoices and bank proof of receipt of foreign-currency proceeds, and the proceeds must be repatriated within the SARS/SARB prescribed period. Offshore installation, commissioning and after-sales engineering services for the Doppler consoles are zero-rated under s11(2)(l) (services physically rendered outside South Africa) and s11(2)(k) (services supplied to non-residents in respect of movable goods situated outside South Africa).

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 goods declaration electronically lodged via SARS Customs, incorporating the F178 Export Exchange Control form where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill / combined transport document issued in the exporting (Pty) Ltd's name evidencing removal of the ultrasound consoles from South Africa to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Authorised Dealer foreign exchange transaction advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in South Africa within the prescribed repatriation period
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin (for preferential SACU/EPA treatment where claimed) and the vendor's export proof schedule with customs release report

South Africa has not implemented a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control e-invoicing regime as at 2025. Mandatory obligations are: (i) issue a full VAT tax invoice under s20 of the VAT Act within 21 days of supply with prescribed particulars, including an express 'zero-rated - s11(1)(a)' indicator for export supplies; (ii) file VAT returns and SAD500 customs export declarations electronically through SARS eFiling and the SARS Customs system; (iii) retain supporting records for 5 years; and (iv) adopt structured e-invoicing architecture (UBL/XML) to prepare for SARS' announced e-invoicing and data-exchange roadmap.

SARS applies a documentary sufficiency test on export zero-rating; failure to prove physical export or timely foreign-currency receipt triggers reassessment of output VAT at 15% plus statutory interest under the Tax Administration Act (TAA). Understatement penalties scale from 25% (substantial understatement) to 50% (failure to take reasonable care), 75% (no reasonable grounds) and 150% for intentional tax evasion, rising to 200% for repeated default. Fraudulent schemes (fabricated bills of lading, sham SAD500 declarations, dummy exporter arrangements, circular foreign remittances or re-importation) attract additional tax up to 100% under s60 of the VAT Act, clawback of refunded input VAT with set-off and collection enforcement, and criminal prosecution under s58 of the VAT Act and s234 of the TAA, resulting in fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current-account purposes (trade in goods/services, interest, dividends, royalties) subject to documentary controls administered by ADs; capital-account transactions by residents (outward investment, foreign borrowings) require FinSurv or AD approval within specified thresholds. Non-resident capital in the form of equity or shareholder loans may enter without prior approval, but the inward investment must be registered with an AD. Profit/dividend remittances to non-residents are permissible provided the company holds a valid SARS tax clearance status, audited annual financial statements are filed, an exchange-control dividend declaration is made to the AD, and 20% dividends tax is withheld (reduced under applicable double-taxation agreements). ZAR legal-tender status extends only to the Common Monetary Area (South Africa, Namibia, Lesotho, Eswatini); offshore settlement is effected through the floating interbank market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CEM, export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of export/shipment, and must be credited to an account with a local AD (a ZAR account or a foreign currency account (FCA)). There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may retain 100% of proceeds in foreign currency for working capital, subject to reporting to the AD on the prescribed export claim/transaction form, supported by the SAD500 customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading and packing list. Advance export payments must be received within 30 days of the advance. Extension of the 180-day period requires FinSurv approval; non-repatriation is an offence under Exchange Control Regulation 12 (penalty up to ZAR 25,000 and/or treble the amount involved), and proceeds must be surrendered to the AD if not received with an approved extension.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under delegated authority of the Minister of Finance; day-to-day administration is delegated to Authorised Dealers (ADs), i.e., registered commercial banks, under the Currency and Exchanges Manual (CEM).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM as a supplementary treasury and FX-liquidity rail; LOW as the primary banking relationship. Legal constraints: under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, only licensed banks may accept deposits or maintain ZAR transaction accounts; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury, Currencycloud) cannot hold domestic ZAR accounts, cannot clear through the South African National Payment System (SAMOS/PASA), and must settle via a licensed AD bank. Cross-border EMI services are usable for outbound supplier payments, notional pooling and FX rate improvement, but all exchange-control reporting (CEM export claims, inward investment records) and FICA accountability must be administered by the local AD. Any payment service provider must comply with FICA (for accountable institutions) and SARB payment-system guidelines; a clear mandate that the EMI is a disbursement tool only is the viable structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the non-resident investor instructs its bank to transfer funds via SWIFT (MT103) to the AD bank account of ZA_PTY_LTD with the purpose code/reference described as 'Share Subscription – Inward Foreign Investment' or 'Shareholder Loan – Intercompany Debt'. No prior FinSurv approval is required for ordinary equity or debt; the AD records the inward investment and issues a reference/registration number under the CEM. On receipt, the company must file with the AD: board resolution, share subscription/loan agreement, register of shareholders (or creditor's subordination for loans), and any FIC report if the deposit falls under a cash threshold. SARS tax clearance must be current for the company to support future outward remittances, and all inward funds must be traceable by MT103 and credit advice, retained for at least five years for AML/CTF verification.Clearing rails for a medical-device exporter: (i) SWIFT MT103 / TT transfers for routine supplier payments for components, freight, customs duties and OEM deliverables; (ii) Commercial and standby Letters of Credit (MT700/MT707 issuance and MT798 amendments) for high-value consignments, particularly where the ZA exporter is the beneficiary of an import LC from distributors; (iii) documentary collections (CAD, DP/DA) as a cost-effective rail against bank-verified trade documents; (iv) local EFT (ACB/RTC) for ZAR-denominated domestic disbursements (e.g., software licences, professional fees, staff payroll). Every outward foreign payment requires the AD's exchange-control declaration (CEM/Form A data) with supporting documents (proforma invoice, purchase order, delivery evidence), and a valid SARS tax clearance certificate is required for non-trade payments (dividends, royalties, professional fees). Mandatory Return Breakdown

Within 12 months after the end of the company's year of assessment, which is ordinarily the approved financial year-end. SARS may prescribe an earlier filing date by public notice.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional estimate: by the last day of the sixth month of the year of assessment. Second provisional estimate: by the last day of the year of assessment. A top-up or closing statement is due within six months after the year-end where the final estimate is below the actual liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 1,000,000,000 (R1 billion) consolidated group revenue triggers the Master File and Local File documentation expectation. In addition, each cross-border related-party transaction or aggregate series of connected transactions exceeding ZAR 5,000,000 should be supported by contemporaneous local-file-quality transfer pricing documentation.

Economic substance scope: South Africa is not a zero-tax shell jurisdiction and does not apply the EU/OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, the resident (Pty) Ltd must demonstrate real economic substance: a registered local office, active local management, employees, bank accounts, procurement, production and risk-taking. SARS applies the arm's-length rule under section 31, the general anti-avoidance rules in sections 80A to 80L, section 103 simulation doctrine and treaty limitation-on-benefits clauses to disregard letterbox companies. A shell used to access treaty benefits or to defer dividends tax will be treated as an agent or conduit, and the economic owner may be assessed directly.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return Filing

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 10% to 200% of the shortfall under section 222 of the Tax Administration Act, plus compensatory interest at the prescribed rate. Criminal conviction for a false return may result in a fine of up to ZAR 40,000, imprisonment of up to five years, forfeiture of the evaded tax benefit and loss of assessed-loss deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false returns to be submitted face prosecution in their personal capacity. Conviction may lead to imprisonment, disqualification from serving as a director under section 69 of the Companies Act, civil liability for the tax debt, SARS civil summonses and exit-control or travel-restraint orders during the criminal process.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply a primary adjustment under section 31 of the Income Tax Act to increase taxable income to the arm's-length price, make a secondary adjustment recharacterising the unpaid amount as a loan or deemed transaction, charge interest, and assess an understatement penalty of up to 200%. The disguised arrangement may be restated for dividends tax and CFC attribution purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length transactions without documentation may be personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall under common-law negligence principles and the reckless-trading provisions of the Companies Act. Where the transfer pricing arrangement is a sham, directors can be prosecuted for fraud and the corporate veil may be pierced to recover taxes.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the corporate form on the basis of simulation or substance-over-form, allocate the income to the real controlling foreign entity, deny treaty benefits, disallow management-fee deductions, impose the 20% dividends tax on non-commercial distributions and assess interest and penalties. A sham company may also be wound up for unlawful conduct under the Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Where a company is a mere facade, a court may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors, shadow directors and beneficial owners jointly and severally liable for taxes and restitution. Individuals knowingly participating in the sham risk criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment and travel bans.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 imposes administrative sanctions of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention. SARS concurrently assesses tax, interest and understatement penalties. Money laundering offences under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act carry substantial fines and imprisonment, and the proceeds may be confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners are personally accountable for wilful blindness or failure to report suspicious transactions. Criminal liability attaches to individuals who structure payments to avoid reporting thresholds; travel bans, restraint orders and extradition requests may follow the same proceedings.
Dividend Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: SARS will assess the full 20% dividends withholding tax on the distribution, plus interest and understatement penalties. The South African company, as the withholding agent, remains liable regardless of whether the shareholder has been paid. Evasion may also trigger criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers who cause the company to declare or facilitate untaxed foreign distributions become jointly and severally liable for the unremitted dividends tax under section 179 of the Tax Administration Act. Where non-payment is fraudulent, imprisonment may follow, and the court may issue asset restraints and travel restrictions.
