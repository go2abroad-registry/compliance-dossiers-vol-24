Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of Samoa (MfR), being the national tax authority of the Independent State of Samoa, with public electronic services and portal access at https://www.revenue.gov.ws; the Ministry administers the Tax Administration Act 2012, the Income Tax Act 2015, and the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2016, while the Registrar of International Companies within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL) maintains company registration records for international companies.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company ('IC') obtains a Certificate of Incorporation bearing a unique IC registration number from the Registrar of International Companies (MCIL) upon incorporation under the International Companies Act 1987. Because the IC is statutorily tax-exempt, no Samoan TIN or VAGST identity number is required for purely offshore operations. If the IC nonetheless triggers domestic tax exposure by employing persons in Samoa (PAYE), importing goods (customs/VAGST), or conducting any taxable activity in Samoa, it must apply to the MfR for a TIN and, where annual taxable turnover meets the VAGST threshold, complete VAGST registration through the MfR registration desk or online portal. The foreign entity must maintain a licensed registered agent in Samoa and provide certified copies of its certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, board resolutions, and identity documents of directors and shareholders.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — a Samoa International Company is a non-resident offshore entity exempt from Samoan income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, VAGST, and stamp duty on all income, profits, gains, and transactions arising outside Samoa under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — a Samoa International Company is a non-resident offshore entity exempt from Samoan income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, VAGST, and stamp duty on all income, profits, gains, and transactions arising outside Samoa under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended). The IC is further prohibited from carrying on domestic business with Samoan residents and incurs only the annual government fee; no tax return or income tax registration obligation arises while the entity remains in compliance with its offshore-only purpose.

Available election forms

Samoa does not maintain a domestic 'check-the-box' entity classification election; the tax status of an IC is fixed by statute and is automatically exempt. Available statutory mechanisms include continuation into or out of Samoa, redomiciliation, voluntary election to be wound up or converted, and instrument-based governance elections under the International Companies Act 1987. For foreign tax planning, the IC may be subject to an entity classification election made under the tax law of another jurisdiction (e.g., U.S. Form 8832), and the exemption is not conditional on any Samoan filing. Samoa's domestic legislation does not provide a hybrid mismatch election regime applicable to ICs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 50,000 (compulsory registration applies to a supplier carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa whose taxable turnover in any 12-month period exceeds or is reasonably expected to exceed 50,000 Samoan Tala; voluntary registration is available for suppliers below the threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAGST-registered supplier may zero-rate: (i) exported goods in respect of which the supplier holds and retains customs evidence of physical export from Samoa within the prescribed time; (ii) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and that are not used, enjoyed, or consumed in Samoa; and (iii) supplies to prescribed international, diplomatic, or donor bodies. For the present IC, offshore FDA 510(k) and CE Mark regulatory consulting services are supplied to non-resident medical device manufacturers and are sourced entirely outside Samoa; the tax-exempt IC is outside the VAGST net altogether, so no VAGST is chargeable and no input tax refund arises because exempt offshore operations do not incur Samoan input VAT.

  • Samoa Customs export declaration (authorized electronic Customs Declaration/Export Entry lodged in the Customs Management System)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods from Samoa
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice from a licensed commercial bank in Samoa evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • VAGST-registered supplier's tax invoice and zero-rating substantiation file, including transport and insurance documents where applicable
  • Certificate of Origin, if preferential tariff treatment or treaty-based documentation is claimed

Samoa does not currently mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API-based digital stamping, or third-party invoice clearance. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue sequential tax invoices containing the prescribed particulars (supplier and recipient names and addresses, both parties' VAGST registration numbers, description of supply, taxable value, and VAGST charged) and must retain records in Samoa or in a form accessible electronically to the Ministry. Returns are lodged through the MfR's online filing system, and the Ministry may require electronic data extraction during audit.

The MfR may audit zero-rated supplies, deny refunds, and retrospectively claw back wrongfully claimed input credits or zero-rated output tax together with statutory interest. Under the Tax Administration Act 2012, shortfall penalties apply at 25% of the tax shortfall for failure to take reasonable care, 75% for reckless conduct, and 150% for intentional, concealed, or fraudulent misconduct; late payment attracts a recurring penalty of 1.5% per month or part of a month, in addition to interest. Fraudulent export zero-rating, false customs declarations, or fabricated outward remittance documentation may further result in criminal prosecution (fines and imprisonment), cancellation or suspension of VAGST registration, seizure of goods, and public disclosure of penalised taxpayers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is not freely convertible. Current-account convertibility for residents operates through CBS-licensed authorized dealers upon documentary verification, while resident capital-account transactions require CBS exchange-control approval. A Samoa International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 is statutorily exempt from all exchange-control restrictions pursuant to section 192 of that Act; the IC may therefore freely receive, hold, pay, and remit in any convertible currency (notably USD and EUR for FDA 510(k) and CE Mark consulting fees) without WST conversion obligations or central-bank capital-movement veto, subject only to AML/CFT verification at the receiving bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must repatriate export proceeds to an authorized dealer in Samoa within the statutory period of 30 days from receipt and offer 100% of the foreign currency for conversion into WST unless a specific exemption is granted by the CBS. International companies are expressly excluded from these surrender and conversion requirements by operation of section 192 of the International Companies Act 1987; the IC's consulting and regulatory-compliance fee receivables may consequently be retained offshore or onshore in USD/EUR and disbursed without surrender to the central bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa, constituted under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and administering the Exchange Control Regulations 1984 in conjunction with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) for offshore-company matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Samoa has no standalone electronic-money-institution (EMI) or non-bank payment-institution licensing regime, and offshore digital EMIs are not recognized as deposit-taking or settlement institutions eligible for CBS clearing access. Cross-border digital payouts and credit-card fee capture are permitted only through licensed commercial banks or regulated international card acquirers sponsored by a Samoan bank; an offshore EMI cannot independently hold client funds in Samoa or issue a local settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection is executed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's home bank to the IC's USD settlement account at Bank of South Pacific (Samoa) Limited via BSP's Australian correspondent, with the remittance narrative referencing 'IC SHARE SUBSCRIPTION WS_IC' and the IC registration number. The sending institution must apply the appropriate BPM6 purpose code for a direct-capital injection; no CBS exchange-control approval is required for ICs, but the receiving Samoan bank requires the completed source-of-funds dossier, an AML clearance certificate from the sending bank, and a funds-declaration form where the credit exceeds the bank's reporting threshold of WST 50,000 equivalent. A pro-forma invoice, share-allotment resolution, and board resolution authorizing the account opening must be retained to evidence the commercial substance of the capital flow.Outbound settlement for FDA 510(k) submission fees, European notified-body CE Mark testing costs, and subcontracted regulatory consultancy services is made by SWIFT MT103 and MT202 in USD/EUR from the IC's multi-currency account to the beneficiary's designated bank; for larger procurement of medical-device testing hardware, a documentary commercial letter of credit (irrevocable, confirmed by a top-tier European or US bank) is used with ICC UCP 600 terms and the IC's bank as issuing institution. Samoan authorized dealers transmit instructions through their US and Australian correspondent networks, and payment narratives must cite the underlying contract number and invoice reference to satisfy AML transaction-monitoring and the receiving beneficiary bank's compliance review. Credit-card fee intake from consulting clients is captured through a licensed card acquirer sponsored by a Samoan commercial bank, with settlement in USD and automatic conversion into the IC's operational account.USD/EUR deliverable FX forward contracts and rolling-forward agreements executed with an OECD-level offshore bank counterparty (London or New York) that accepts the Samoa IC's full KYC/EDD onboarding file, used to lock conversion costs between FDA 510(k) USD receivables and CE Mark EUR disbursements.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on WST/USD arranged through a licensed derivatives broker or an authorized-dealer bank outside Samoa for the limited purpose of netting incidental Samoan operating-currency exposure without creating onshore WST deliverability risk.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between USD and EUR with duration matching the expected FDA and CE Mark fee cycles, converting fixed-rate USD regulatory retainers into EUR streams for European notified-body payments.Plain-vanilla FX call and put options purchased on a zero-premium-budget-capped basis to protect against adverse EUR/USD movement on one-off high-value regulatory filing milestones, with short-option writing expressly excluded from treasury policy.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa Income Tax Act (Section 52A) requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for any controlled transaction where the aggregate annual value exceeds WST 2,000,000 or where exempt entities have intra-group services exceeding WST 1,000,000. There is no statutory requirement to prepare Local File or Master File for fully exempt International Companies unless the exemption is disclaimed or local-source income is earned

Economic substance scope: The International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) does not explicitly prescribe economic substance, but Samoa's Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AMLCFT) Act 2017 requires each IC to maintain a registered office, keep adequate accounting records, and demonstrate a legitimate commercial purpose. A company failing economic substance criteria may be deemed a 'shell' for tax evasion purposes, triggering loss of the IC exempt status and application of ordinary Samoan corporate income tax at 27%

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Loss of IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: Retroactive revocation of exempt status; assessment of unpaid corporate tax for three years plus penalties of up to 150% of the underpaid tax; late payment surcharge of 2% per month on outstanding amounts

Director personal liability: Every director is jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax. Willful evasion is a criminal offense carrying a fine not exceeding WST 200,000 and imprisonment not exceeding 7 years. A director may also be subject to a travel ban and personal property restraint pending resolution
Shell Company / Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: Striking the company off the International Companies Register; administrative penalty of WST 50,000–WST 150,000; confiscation of any assets claimed under the exempt IC status; mandatory outing in the Samoan corporate registry as a non-operational entity

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 5 years. If the shell structure is used to facilitate fraud, directors face civil liability for all debts incurred while the company lacked substance, plus criminal prosecution for false declarations with imprisonment up to 5 years
AML/CFT Non-Compliance in Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 500,000 for the company; capability to freeze bank accounts; with AML/CFT violations being subject to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit's enforcement action, including cancellation of the company's registered office license

Director personal liability: Directors face personal monetary penalties of WST 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years for enabling money laundering or terrorist financing. Travel restrictions are routinely imposed pending legal process
False Declaration to the Tax Authority

Statutory sanction: A strict-liability civil fine of WST 25,000 per declaration; criminal conviction for perjury in tax documents carries a fine of up to WST 100,000 plus imprisonment of up to 3 years; subsequent disallowance of all deductions and exemptions

Director personal liability: The tax authority can pierce the corporate veil and recover unpaid taxes directly from directors' assets. In addition to restitution and interest, directors can face a 5-year entry bar into Samoa and removal from the company register as an authorized officer
