Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Samoa International Company. Share capital may be denominated in any currency, commonly USD, and there is no requirement to deposit capital into an escrow or blocked account. The licensed registered agent must, however, verify the lawful source of subscription funds as part of its Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2007 obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Samoa Fire and Emergency Services Authority (SFESA) for the registered-office premises; building occupancy and structural load clearance from the Ministry of Works, Transport and Infrastructure (MWTI); and occupational health and safety compliance under the Labour and Employment Relations Act 2013 for any physical staff. For a purely offshore International Company, reliance on the registered agent's certified office premises satisfies these requirements.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Samoa International Company. Share capital may be denominated in any currency, commonly USD, and there is no requirement to deposit capital into an escrow or blocked account. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue, Government of Samoa (MfR), Apia, Samoa; online portal: www.revenue.gov.ws (Samoa Tax Administration System - STAS, enabling TIN issuance, VAGST registration, and e-filing of VAT returns). Incorporation and International Company (IC) licensing are administered separately by the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), a division of the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), via the Samoa Companies Office.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) is statutorily exempt from all Samoa taxes (income tax, VAGST, stamp duty, capital gains tax, and withholding taxes) on its international business, and therefore has no mandatory obligation to obtain a TIN from MfR. However, to recover VAGST on incidental local purchases (e.g., office rent, utilities, professional fees), the IC may voluntarily register: Step 1 - incorporate the IC through a SIFA-licensed registered agent: name clearance, lodgement of Memorandum and Articles of Association, Certificate of Incorporation, and payment of prescribed incorporation fee (approx. WST 500-1,500); Step 2 - receive the IC Registration Number from SIFA and the Certificate of Exemption; Step 3 - if desired, submit Form VAT-01 (Application for Registration) plus the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum & Articles, power of attorney from non-resident director, and registered agent confirmation letter to MfR, who will issue a TIN and eligible VAGST certificate; Step 4 - maintain no income tax registration; the IC remains outside the MfR income tax return net. For a domestic Samoa company (fallback structure), the SOP would be: company incorporation under Companies Act 2006, then TIN application via STAS, corporate income tax registration, PAYE registration, VAGST registration if annual taxable turnover exceeds WST 100,000, and a business license from the Samoa Tourism Authority or Ministry of Revenue where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (International Company). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (International Company). The IC is exempt from all income tax and duties under the International Companies Act 1987, section 180 (as amended): zero percent income tax on all offshore-derived income, including fees from civil infrastructure BIM/CAD services supplied to non-resident clients; exemption from VAGST on offshore supplies; exemption from stamp duty on all instruments and transactions; no withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees paid to non-residents; no capital gains tax; no annual income tax return filing obligation; exemption valid indefinitely while the IC remains compliant with SIFA annual renewal fees and does not breach the domestic trading prohibition (i.e., no business with Samoa residents, no ownership of local real property except a 30-year lease of office premises, no import/export of physical goods within Samoa).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Samoa law; entity classification follows statute, not election. A Samoa IC is automatically tax-exempt; no affirmative election is required. Optional elections available: (1) Domestic company re-domiciliation - an IC may elect under Part XIV of the International Companies Act to convert into a domestic company under the Companies Act 2006, voluntarily subjecting itself to 25% corporate income tax and VAGST in exchange for access to Samoa's Double Tax Agreements (currently limited to a network of fewer than 10 DTA partners, e.g., Australia, New Zealand, Norway, Thailand, UAE); (2) Voluntary VAGST registration - an IC providing offshore services may elect to register for VAGST at a turnover below the statutory threshold to claim input tax credits on local purchases; the election is subject to MfR approval and cannot be withdrawn for 24 months; (3) No hybrid mismatch, CFC, or controlled foreign company election is available; Samoa has not enacted OECD hybrid mismatch rules; (4) No group consolidation election exists; each IC is taxed independently and is separately exempt.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 in taxable supplies in any 12-month rolling period (mandatory registration threshold). Voluntary registration is available to any person making or intending to make taxable supplies, including an IC, with MfR consent. A Samoa International Company supplying offshore BIM/CAD services to non-residents is not subject to VAGST on that offshore supply because the place of supply rule in the VAGST Act 2003, section 9, treats services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa and whose benefit is received wholly outside Samoa as outside the scope of Samoa VAGST; accordingly, the IC has no mandatory VAGST registration requirement, and any such registration is only elective for input recovery purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under VAGST Act 2003, section 16(1)(a)-(h), zero-rating (0% VAGST) applies to: (a) exports of goods (provided the exporter retains proof of physical export and legal ownership passes to the non-resident); (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time services are performed and where the benefit is enjoyed entirely outside Samoa; (c) services directly related to land or goods situated outside Samoa; (d) internationally transported goods and passengers; (e) goods sold to approved non-resident diplomatic missions; and (f) supplies to prescribed international development organizations. For the civil infrastructure BIM/CAD and structural drafting service scope of ISIC 7110: the IC must evidence that the client is a non-resident developer, civil contractor, or engineering consultancy; the drafting software and servers may be located in Samoa but the intellectual output is delivered electronically to the offshore client; the contract must state that the place of supply is the client's jurisdiction; the service must not be connected to any site, building, or infrastructure asset located in Samoa; and the IC must maintain full technical evidence of delivery (server logs, email transmission records, cloud delivery confirmations). An IC does not need to claim zero-rating because its offshore income is wholly outside the scope of VAGST and tax-exempt at the entity level; the zero-rating provisions above apply principally to registered domestic Samoa exporters of similar engineering services, allowing them to recover input VAGST while charging 0% output tax.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, government-operated e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime is operational in Samoa as of the 2025 filing season. MfR operates an indirect, post-transaction control model: registered persons must file electronic VAT returns (Form VAT-200) through STAS and retain supporting tax invoices. Tax invoice requirements (VAGST Act 2003, section 24 and VAGST Regulations 2004): sequential numbering, supplier name and TIN, recipient name and TIN (if registered), date of supply, clear description, quantity and unit price, VAGST amount shown separately, total amount, and the words 'Tax Invoice'. E-invoicing in standard PDF/XML format is accepted; no digital signature, barcode, or real-time transmission is mandated. MfR is, however, developing an electronic fiscal device framework for tourism and retail sectors only, which does not affect offshore service exporters or ICs.

MfR audit powers under Tax Administration Act 2012 allow a 5-year review window from the VAT return due date, extendable to 10 years in cases of assumed fraud. Penalty framework: (1) late payment penalty of 1.5% per month on any VAGST shortfall; (2) shortfall penalty of 25% of the assessed tax for failure to take reasonable care, 75% for intentional disregard of the law, and 150% for fraudulent conduct; (3) statutory interest at the annual prescribed rate (currently 12%) compounded monthly; (4) clawback of any VAGST input credits claimed without proper zero-rated export substantiation, with retrospective effect to the date of the incorrect claim; (5) criminal sanction: a person who knowingly claims fraudulent export zero-rating is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding WST 100,000 or imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years, or both; (6) for an International Company specifically, the more serious clawback is the loss of exempt-zone status: if the IC is found to have derived any Samoa domestic source income, or to have traded with a Samoa resident, SIFA may deregister the IC and MfR will retrospectively impose 25% corporate income tax on all assessable income plus penalties and interest, with no statute of limitation for tax evasion. MfR and SIFA share a joint risk-based audit protocol targeting shell ICs that transact with Asia-Pacific engineering contractors; documentary insufficiency is presumptive evidence of a domestic supply reversing the export zero-rating or the tax exemption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains a managed-float Samoan Tala (WST) with limited formal convertibility governed by an exchange-control licensing regime. Current-account inflows, comprising revenues from exported CAD/BIM engineering services, are freely receivable through authorized dealers with documentary support; current-account outflows, including sub-contract design fees, software licensing and professional charges, are permitted against standard invoice evidence. Capital-account transactions require prior CBS approval or post-facto notification lodged through the onboarding bank. A Samoa International Company (IC) is treated as a non-resident entity for exchange-control purposes and may lawfully retain USD/EUR/NZD/AUD receipts in foreign-currency accounts at an authorized dealer; profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is processed only after verification of audited financial statements, a books-and-records declaration demonstrating substantive accounting, and settlement of any Samoan tax or statutory liability. The WST has no robust offshore deliverable market, so hedging and repatriation discipline rely on onshore forwards and multi-currency account management rather than deep NDF liquidity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline and conversion ratio: (i) resident-export businesses under the Exchange Control Act 1972 must repatriate all export receipts to a CBS-licensed bank within 90 days from the payment-due date or invoice date, whichever is earlier; (ii) the mandatory conversion ratio for resident exporters is 100% surrender to the authorized dealer in WST unless the CBS grants a Foreign Currency Retention Facility, under which approved exporters may retain up to 100% of USD proceeds for up to 12 months; (iii) the WS_IC, being a non-resident offshore International Company prohibited from trading with Samoan residents, is outside the strict mandatory-surrender discipline: it may retain the entirety of its CAD/BIM service receipts in a USD Foreign Currency Account at a licensed dealer, but must nonetheless clear all incoming SWIFT settlements through a Samoa-licensed bank for balance-of-payments statistical reporting and complete a 90-day internal repatriation reconciliation with the relationship manager; (iv) any USD-to-WST conversion executed to fund local statutory expenses must be transacted at the authorized dealer's prevailing market rate and declared on the CBS exchange-control return on a T+1 basis.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS; Samoan: Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa) — the sole monetary authority and prudential regulator of Samoa, established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; it administers the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977, operating through CBS-licensed Authorized Foreign Exchange Dealers, being exclusively the licensed commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

MEDIUM-LOW, restricted auxiliary rail only. No offshore EMI is licensed as an Authorized Foreign Exchange Dealer in Samoa; therefore Wise or other offshore digital EMI accounts cannot legally serve as the primary treasury repository or perform CBS-recognized FX conversion, and balances parked in an offshore EMI for more than 90 days create a technical exchange-control breach against the Samoa export-proceeds repatriation expectation. Viable use: a secondary collections rail for small-ticket CAD/BIM milestone invoices from dispersed international clients, with funds received in USD at Wise and swept by MT103 to the BSP USD Foreign Currency Account on a maximum T+3 net-liquidity cycle, each sweep matched to an invoice and reported to the bank as incoming service receipts. Legal constraints: EMI onboarding requires the WS_IC UBO passports, board authorization, and an executed terms-of-service acknowledging that Samoa exchange-control obligations apply; the EMI cannot hold WST; and the EMI must never be used for equity injections, dividend repatriation, or intercompany capital movements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: MEDIUM-LOW, restricted auxiliary rail only. No offshore EMI is licensed as an Authorized Foreign Exchange Dealer in Samoa; therefore Wise or other offshore digital EMI accounts cannot legally serve as the primary treasury repository or perform CBS-recognized FX conversion, and balances parked in an offshore EMI for more than 90 days create a technical exchange-control breach against the Samoa export-proceeds repatriation expectation. Viable use: a secondary collections rail for small-ticket CAD/BIM milestone invoices from dispersed international clients, with funds received in USD at Wise and swept by MT103 to the BSP USD Foreign Currency Account on a maximum T+3 net-liquidity cycle, each sweep matched to an invoice and reported to the bank as incoming service receipts.
