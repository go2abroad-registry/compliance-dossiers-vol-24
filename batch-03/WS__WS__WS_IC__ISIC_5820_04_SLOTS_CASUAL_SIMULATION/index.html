Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of Samoa (official online portal: 'Samoa Ministry of Revenue – Online Services / Aso Avanoa')

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities incorporated as Samoa International Companies (ICs) obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the Ministry for Revenue as part of the registration process administered through the Registrar of International Companies within the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). For VAGST purposes, a non-resident IC that carries on a taxable activity in Samoa (or makes taxable supplies such as digital gaming services to Samoan consumers) must register for VAGST using Form VAGST-REG through the Ministry for Revenue online portal. If the entity undertakes exclusively offshore business within the Tax_Exempt_Zone framework, no domestic income tax registration is required, but annual fees and license renewals are payable to SIFA.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Foreign entities incorporated as Samoa International Companies (ICs) obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the Ministry for Revenue as part of the registration process administered through the Registrar of International Companies within the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). For VAGST purposes, a non-resident IC that carries on a taxable activity in Samoa (or makes taxable supplies such as digital gaming services to Samoan consumers) must register for VAGST using Form VAGST-REG through the Ministry for Revenue online portal. If the entity undertakes exclusively offshore business within the Tax_Exempt_Zone framework, no domestic income tax registration is required, but annual fees and license renewals are payable to SIFA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime is available under Samoa law. The tax exemption for International Companies is statutory under Section 111 of the International Companies Act 1988 (Samoa), granting full exemption from all Samoan income tax, VAGST, withholding tax, and stamp duty for a period of 20 years (renewable). ICs may elect to be treated as ordinary resident companies for domestic taxation purposes only where local-source business is undertaken, but such an election is irreversible and rarely used.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) goods physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of payment or removal to a customs controlled area; and (b) services supplied to a non-resident customer who is outside Samoa and not in Samoa at the time of supply, provided the services are not directly connected with land or goods situated in Samoa. Offshore software publishing and social casino game services sold via Apple IAP or Google Play Billing to overseas players will qualify as zero-rated supply where the recipient is non-resident, the payment originates offshore, and the services are legally and economically rendered outside Samoa. Supporting evidence must demonstrate the place of supply, export destination, and foreign exchange settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSDEC) / Samoa Export Declaration Form CED
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement (for goods) or digital service delivery log (for SaaS/gaming services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank/PSP statement confirming offshore payment settlement

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is currently enforced in Samoa. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices containing prescribed particulars under the VAGST Act 2006 and Tax Administration Act 2012. Returns must be filed electronically via the Ministry for Revenue online portal (monthly or quarterly depending on turnover class) with summary declaration of electronic invoicing records, but no national clearance ledger is implemented.

Under the Tax Administration Act 2012 (Samoa), fraudulent zero-rating or invalid export claims attract shortfall penalties up to 150% of the VAGST payable, late-payment penalties, and statutory interest compounded from the due date. Deliberate falsification of export documentation carries criminal penalties including imprisonment up to 10 years and/or monetary fines up to 300 penalty units. The Ministry for Revenue may retrospectively claw back zero-rated claims and reassess VAGST at the standard 15% rate for a period of up to 7 years in cases of fraudulent or reckless misrepresentation. The underlying export proofs must be retained for at least 7 years; failure to maintain the prescribed documentation triggers automatic deregistration of the zero-rating benefit and assessment of full rate VAGST.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a unified exchange-rate system for the current account; the Samoan Tala (WST) is not fully convertible for capital transactions. The Tax-Exempt International Company (IC) is considered a non-resident entity for exchange-control purposes and may hold, use, and remit foreign-currency proceeds through CBS-approved Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs). Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are treated as a capital-account transaction requiring prior CBS approval under the Exchange Control Act 1976; however, an IC that has not engaged in domestic WST-denominated transactions and that maintains offshore treasury operations is generally granted approval automatically upon submission of audited financial statements and a tax-clearance certificate issued by the Samoa Ministry of Revenue. The practical hurdles include mandatory CBS Form OSI/OST declarations, per-transaction documentary review, and the absence of a fully developed offshore forward market in-country.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and Exchange Control Regulations, any exporter is required to realise the full export proceeds in foreign currency within three (3) months from the date of export and to offer those proceeds for sale to an authorized dealer (licensed Samoan commercial bank) within three (3) months of receipt. For digital publishing and simulated gaming services derived from offshore platforms (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing), the IC must file a quarterly CBS 'Foreign Currency Account Return' and apply for a standing retention waiver from the surrender obligation, on the grounds that the revenue constitutes offshore digital-service income and that no WST conversion is necessary because all suppliers and operating liabilities are settled in USD/EUR through the IC's designated FCA. In practice, CBS will approve such waivers subject to the IC maintaining separate non-resident status evidenced by the Registrar of International Companies certificate and having no local-resident trade counterparties. Without the waiver, all proceeds credited to the IC would be deemed export proceeds and would have to be converted to WST within the strict statutory timeline.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (legally viable with constraints). Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, MultiPass, and Currencycloud may serve as an ancillary settlement necklace for ad-network payables, contractor royalty payments, and subscription micro-collections; however, they are not licensed in Samoa and cannot open WST-denominated flows. The Exchange Control Act prohibits residents from routing payments through non-authorized dealers unless a CBS exemption for non-resident ICs applies. Since the target entity qualifies as a non-resident IC, it may lawfully maintain treasury relationships with foreign EMIs, but those EMIs must contractually agree to (i) avoid any Samoan resident counterparty, (ii) provide FATF-compliant sourcing evidence, and (iii) ensure that no inward or outward payment is denominated in WST. Hong Kong / Singapore EMIs with direct USD segregated settlement accounts through JP Morgan or Citibank are preferable and will materially reduce friction versus local Samoan clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound injection route is as follows: (1) The disseminating shareholder bank executes a SWIFT MT103 wire with proper ISO 20022 Purpose Code. For equity capital contributions, the required purpose code is 'CAP10 - Equity/Investment in equity capital' or APER/Samoan customs, and must quote the Samoa IC treasury profile reference and the specific CBS approval file number. (2) The inbound wire must designate the IC's Foreign Currency Account (USD or EUR) at ANZ Samoa or BSP, and must include the beneficiary line as 'BENEF: WS_IC -- International Company'. (3) The remitter shall supply an Electronic Transfer Advice, together with a copy of the executed share subscription agreement, board resolution approving the issuance, and the UBO declarations; the receiving Samoan bank will file CBS Form AIF and will not credit the account until the bank's AML department sends a 'clear display notice' and the CBS auto-approval window of 2 business days has elapsed. (4) Funds exceeding WST-equivalent 1,000,000 require approval of the CBS Financial Markets Unit via a signed a pre-approval application; the routes for interim treasury funding are bank-guaranteed cheque, Swift MT101 (transfers) or MT202 (interbank transfers) from the IO's sub-account at the same bank. (5) For early-stage operations, it is advisable to structure a convertible loan facility rather than immediate equity injection; if so, the purpose code becomes 'CL02 - Intercompany Foreign Direct Investment Loans' and the contract must include an arm's-length interest rate, a maturity profile, and a licence to remit to non-resident lenders.Outbound disbursements are grouped into three clearing tiers: (i) payables for cloud infrastructure, IP licensing, user-acquisition advertising, and distributed developer compensation—all settled via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) to beneficiary banks worldwide, with the corresponding beneficiary's name and local bank account number, referencing invoices and the framework agreement; (ii) inter-company management fees and contractor payments where the beneficiary is outside Samoa—settled via MT103 or MT202 (for interbank cover) executed by the IC's treasury; and (iii) commercial letters of credit—legally permissible but practically inapplicable to intangible digital publishing; where required for physical merchandise (such as a distributor-created console or gift-card residue), a 'Digi-LC' arrangement can be opened through ANZ Samoa with an issuing bank outside Samoa. For Apple IAP and Google Play Billing balances, the IC will route a daily/weekly settlement sweep from the App Store merchant account into its USD concentration account through the same MT103 rail. All outbound transactions above WST100,000 must be filed with the CBS on Form OST (Overseas Settlement of Transfer) and must retain the underlying invoice / contract as the documentary audit trail. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% for resident companies under the Income Tax Act 2012, as amended by the FY2020/21 Budget (27% prior to 1 July 2020). International Companies (IC), the target entity class, are wholly exempt from Samoa income tax by force of Section 200 of the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended), producing an effective Samoan CIT burden of 0% on exempt offshore income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for an exempt International Company: distributions of profits to non-resident shareholders are made free of any Samoan dividend withholding tax because the IC is wholly outside the Income Tax Act 2012. By contrast, a resident company under the Income Tax Act 2012 pays a final dividend withholding tax of 15% on gross distributions to non-resident shareholders (the IC exemption therefore acts as an incentive for the family-trust holding structure).

Due no later than the anniversary date of incorporation. The renewal fee covers the next 12-month period; a late renewal within 12 months is accepted with a surcharge, and non-renewal beyond 12 months results in automatic strike-off.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Samoa threshold (in WST) triggers Local File/Master File compilation; the Income Tax Act 2012 imposes the arm's length standard on all cross-border related-party transactions and the Commissioner may require contemporaneous TP documentation on demand (within 30 days). A prudent IC holding game IP should nonetheless prepare an OECD-aligned Local File when annual related-party flows exceed WST 2,000,000 (approx USD 700,000) to withstand audit, even though no TP filing is made in the exempt zone; China-side documentation thresholds (e.g., related-party transactions over RMB 100 million under Chinese SAT rules) must be separately observed by the Chinese beneficial owner.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to BVI/Cayman ESR; the IC regime relies on statutory ring-fencing: the International Companies Act 1987 prohibits the IC from carrying on business with Samoan residents, deriving Samoan-source income or owning Samoan land, and requires a licensed Apia registered agent, registered office and resident secretary. The OECD FHTP (BEPS Inclusive Framework) monitors Samoa's status; an IC should maintain board minutes, bank records and IP license agreements in Samoa and perform genuinely strategic functions (execution of board decisions, approval of IP sub-licenses, distribution resolutions) to establish substance and defuse DTA/GAAR challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false exempt-status declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2012 and Tax Administration Act 2012: penalty of 150% of the evaded tax plus default interest of 2% per month, administrative fine up to WST 100,000, and the Commissioner's power to estimate and garnish bank accounts at the Samoan settlement bank.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified a false Box 5 declaration are personally and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax; conviction carries imprisonment up to five years, director disqualification and a summary travel restriction: the Ministry of Revenue may issue a departure prohibition certificate preventing the director from leaving Samoa until the assessed tax and penalty are secured.
Shell-company / sham substance and corporate veil piercing

Statutory sanction: Registrar of International Companies may strike off the company and forfeit its assets after 12 months of non-renewal; on strike-off, all contracts (including Apple/Google developer agreements) are legally terminated, the IC is dissolved, and assets are vested in the Registrar; restoration requires a reinstatement fee and court approval in contested cases.

Director personal liability: Samoan courts will pierce the corporate veil: controlling shareholders and shadow directors are jointly liable for the company's debts, restitution of platform proceeds is ordered to affected parties and the Crown, and directors may be banned from incorporating or administering any Samoan entity for up to 10 years.
Anti-money laundering and sanctions breaches (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2018 (Samoa): corporate fines up to WST 2,000,000, confiscation of proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2007, and the Financial Intelligence Unit's power to freeze the IC's bank accounts and tipping-off penalties for the registered agent.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners are subject to criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering (and up to 7 years for financing of terrorism), personal asset forfeiture, international extradition to the requesting state, and cross-border travel bans issued at the request of the FIU or foreign enforcement authorities (e.g., the Australian/American authorities under the FATF network).
Unlicensed gaming / platform policy mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Under the Gambling Act 2008 (Samoa) only a sole Concessionaire may provide casino-style gaming in Samoa: an unauthorized IC faces fines up to WST 500,000 per offence, forfeiture of gaming equipment and platform assets, and shutdown orders; foreign regulators (e.g., UKGC, AGCO, Swedish SGA) may impose cumulative fines and block the app stores, while Apple and Google enforce clause-level termination of the developer account for unlawful gambling content.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability for operating unlawful gambling, personal fines, repatriation orders for funds received, travel bans from the licensing state, and extradition under mutual-assistance treaties; the family-trust settlor is exposed to clawback of distributed assets in the jurisdiction of the principal regulator.
