Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act zero-rates movable goods (ANC headphones and speakers, HS 8518.30/8518.50, ISIC 2640) physically exported from South Africa when supplied in the ordinary course of the enterprise, provided the supplier retains documentary proof of export, shipment occurs to a destination outside South Africa (including SACU member states for VAT purposes), and payment is received within the prescribed currency-repatriation period. Section 11(2)(l) zero-rates exported services such as engineering design, firmware licensing, destructive testing, and post-sale OEM support supplied to a non-resident, provided the services are not rendered within South Africa and are not connected to South African immovable property. The exporter must reflect the zero-rated export line and the SARS export reference on the VAT201 reconciliation.

SARS is implementing a phased e-invoicing mandate aligned with African Tax Administration Forum (ATAF) guidance; as of the current effective position, no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping code is yet in force. Binding current requirements include: (i) eFiling Web Interfile (API/CSV Push channel) for all VAT201 submissions; (ii) full tax-invoice content requirements under s20 VAT Act, including the supplier's SARS VAT number, invoice sequence number, and full description of goods; (iii) five-year digital archival integrity for all invoice datasets; (iv) mandatory electronic SAD500 customs export declarations integrated with SARS Customs; (v) automated risk-based digital validation and refund-hold protocols for first-time or high-value export claims.

The SARS VAT Refund Integrity Unit reconciles VAT201 zero-rated export lines against SAD500 customs data and bank settlement records using automated risk algorithms. Any claim lacking the full export documentation bundle is reversed by additional assessment with 10% late-payment penalty and statutory interest under s39 of the VAT Act and s187/s188 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract the s222 understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall and criminal prosecution carrying imprisonment of up to five years. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers SARB Exchange Control penalties and administrative fines under Regulation 8 of the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and may retroactively disqualify zero-rating where non-repatriation demonstrates non-arm's-length terms.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a two-stream convertibility regime under the SARB exchange control framework. Current account transactions flowing from genuine trade, manufacturing, royalty, and service contracts are effectively liberalised and are administered by SARB-licensed Authorised Dealers (commercial banks) without individual FinSurv approval, provided that the initiating entity furnishes a complete economic-purpose declaration and supporting documentation. Capital account transactions, including inward share subscriptions, shareholder sub-ordinated loans, external borrowing, and expansion CAPEX funded by non-residents, require formal risk classification under the Authorised Dealer's delegated authority, and larger or unusual capital flows may be referred to FinSurv for conditional approval. ZAR is convertible for bona fide operational purposes; no blanket surrender or investment-approval requirement burdens an ordinary manufacturing company, but outbound profit remittance is conditioned on: (i) accurate maintenance of the non-resident shareholding and external loan registers with the Authorised Dealer, (ii) payment of South African Dividends Tax at 20% or the applicable treaty-reduced rate, (iii) a board resolution confirming the distribution from legally distributable reserves, and (iv) issuance of a tax-compliance status confirmation by SARS where remittance exceeds the bank's discretion threshold. Capital reductions, redemption of shares, and repatriation of contributed capital are subject to stricter FinSurv screening and must be supported by valuations, solvency opinions, and prior Authorised Dealer sign-off.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a South African resident exporter of active-noise-cancelling headphones and smart-audio devices, the statutory base requirement is that the full export proceeds be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment stated on the export declaration. If the exporter does not hold an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA), 100% of the FOB value must be converted into ZAR upon receipt and credited to the local operating account. An FCA held with the Authorised Dealer permits the exporter to retain the foreign currency without forced conversion, but every credit and debit must be traceable to an underlying trade transaction and reported in the dealer's BOP/EC returns. The exporter must submit the bank copy of the customs export entry (SAD500), commercial invoice, packing list, and transport document to the Authorised Dealer within the time frame stipulated by the bank, ordinarily 30 days after the month of shipment. Deviations such as late payment, part-payment due to warranty/quality claims, extended credit terms, or the use of bonded warehousing require prior written approval from the Authorised Dealer or FinSurv. Non-compliance may trigger administrative exchange-control penalties, withdrawal of the exporter's simplified customs/forex facilitations, and negative tax-reporting consequences under the Customs and Excise Act.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act No. 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations of 1961
