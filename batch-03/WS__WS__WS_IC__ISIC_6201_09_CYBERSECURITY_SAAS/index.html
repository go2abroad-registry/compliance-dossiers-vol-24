International Companies Act 1987 of the Independent State of Samoa, as amended by subsequent amending legislation; applicable provisions on the formation of an International Company, registered office and licensed registered agent requirements, memorandum and articles of association, appointment of directors and members, and the statutory exemption of non-Samoan-source income from Samoan income tax.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or deposit account requirement; the subscriber may adopt a nominal authorized capital (commonly US$10,000) and allot shares without any mandatory cash deposit. Consideration for shares is fixed by the directors under the International Companies Act.

None of these clearances is triggered by the Company's remote cloud-based operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue, Government of Samoa; official tax and customs portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

International Companies (ICs) are not issued a Samoa TIN/VAT ID and are not required to register for taxes because they are statutorily tax-exempt. The standard procedure is: (i) incorporate the International Company under the International Companies Act 1987 with the Registrar of International Companies administered through the Samoa International Finance Authority; (ii) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and maintain a registered office in Samoa; (iii) rely on the automatic tax exemption in section 200 of the International Companies Act 1987; and (iv) if the foreign entity wishes to conduct onshore Samoa taxable activities, it must instead incorporate or register a Samoa domestic company, obtain a TIN from the Ministry for Revenue, and apply for VAGST registration where the WST 100,000 threshold is met or voluntary registration is desired.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime exists in Samoa. An International Company is automatically in the Tax_Exempt_Zone category and cannot elect to be treated as a domestic taxable company, pass-through entity, or hybrid entity under Samoan law. Any onshore taxable activity must be ring-fenced through a separate Samoa domestic company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (compulsory VAGST registration for any person carrying on a taxable activity where taxable supplies exceed WST 100,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available below this threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAGST Act, a zero-rated export supply requires that goods are exported from Samoa or that services are supplied to a non-resident person and are consumed or performed entirely outside Samoa. For cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration-testing SaaS, the supplier must demonstrate the customer is not a Samoan resident and that the benefit of the service is not enjoyed in Samoa. An International Company, however, is exempt from VAGST by statutory operation, so its export SaaS supplies fall outside the VAGST system entirely and no VAGST zero-rating refund mechanism is engaged.

  • Customs Export Declaration (required only for physical goods; not applicable to pure SaaS export)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (required only for physical goods; not applicable to pure SaaS export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice stated at 0% VAGST or tax-exempt status
  • Underlying SaaS subscription and penetration-testing engagement agreement
  • Proof of non-resident customer status (e.g., photocopy of passport, certificate of incorporation, or tax residence certificate)
  • Technical delivery and cloud usage evidence showing the service was delivered from offshore infrastructure and consumed outside Samoa

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in Samoa. Registered persons must issue valid VAGST tax invoices and retain supporting documentation; VAGST returns are filed electronically through the Ministry for Revenue portal. Cross-border SaaS export transactions are not subject to a Samoan tax-authority digital clearance requirement.

The Ministry for Revenue may audit export zero-rating and exemption claims, with extension periods for fraud or wilful misstatement. If documentary substantiation is incomplete or false, the claimed zero-rating is denied, the full 15% VAGST is assessed on the supply, refunds are clawed back, and late payment interest plus civil penalties may be imposed. Criminal fraudulent invoicing or false export documentation can result in fines, imprisonment, and retrospective reversal of the International Company's tax exemption where the exemption is used to conceal a domestically taxable activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is partially convertible rather than freely convertible. Current-account payments — trade in goods and services, SaaS subscription fees, operating expenses, and dividend remittances supported by audited distributable profits — are generally processed by authorised dealers (commercial banks) against standard documentary evidence without case-by-case CBS approval. Capital-account transactions, including direct cross-border investment, intercompany debt, treasury placements, and officer loans, require explicit CBS approval or a standing facility. A Samoa International Company (IC) is treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes and may hold a CBS-approved Foreign Currency Account (USD/EUR/AUD/NZD) at an authorised dealer; SaaS subscription collections can be retained in USD without conversion to WST, and conversion is required only for local Samoan operating costs. Profit-remittance hurdles are minimal where the bank holds the IC registration certificate, non-resident declaration, board resolution, and audited financial statements evidencing distributable reserves; the practical friction is a 3-to-10-business-day exchange-control verification window and the bank's right to require a CBS foreign-exchange application form for each dividend payout.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations impose three core rules: (i) a resident exporter must receive full export proceeds from foreign sales of goods or services within a statutory time limit of six months from the date of exportation or performance, unless the CBS extends the period; (ii) proceeds must be surrendered, i.e., offered to an authorised dealer in exchange for WST immediately upon receipt, which in normal banking practice means within 14 calendar days of credit; and (iii) the default mandatory conversion ratio is 100% for ordinary resident exporters without a standing Foreign Currency Account exemption. For a licensed International Company under the International Companies Act 1987, the CBS routinely grants standing approval for an IC-designated Foreign Currency Account, allowing 100% retention of export receipts in USD or another approved currency with no WST conversion; the effective mandatory surrender ratio is therefore 0% for ICs holding such standing approval. Where the IC has not secured FCA standing approval, the authorised dealer treats each receipt as a designated foreign-currency deposit and may require conversion to WST on the full amount. The IC must file export/T-form declarations with the authorised dealer on request, and the authorised dealer submits quarterly foreign-exchange returns to CBS.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa) — the sole monetary authority and exchange-control administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low — legally restricted. Samoa has no EMI or restricted-licence regime for offshore fintech banks; the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 and CBS payment-system rules reserve deposit-taking and money-remittance services to CBS-licensed authorised dealers. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Currencycloud cannot obtain settlement accounts or domestic clearing membership in Samoa, and using them as primary treasury rails would constitute an exchange-control breach, create concentration risk at the local clearing bank, and potentially trigger suspension of the IC's banking relationship. Such EMIs have supplementary viability only for low-value disbursements such as contractor payouts, provided each transaction is mirrored and transparently recorded in the authorised-dealer ledger for compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be directed to the IC's USD-denominated Foreign Currency Account at a CBS-authorised dealer, being Bank of Samoa, Samoa Commercial Bank, or BSP, using SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer). The remitter must state the shareholder legal name in Field 50K, the exact registered IC name in Field 59, and a remittance reference in Field 70 that exactly matches the share-register entry, e.g., 'SUB/2025/WS-IC/004 — SHARE CAPITAL'. Field 71A should be 'OUR' or 'SHA' on a conservative basis to eliminate cost ambiguity. For USD wires originating from the US Fedwire system, the beneficiary account must be the standard Samoa account number without IBAN, and the reference must include the token 'IC-WS'. The authorised dealer's compliance pack requires: a board resolution approving the subscription; an updated share register; an issued share certificate; the IC registration certificate; a CBS capital-inflow declaration where the injection exceeds the current-account threshold; and confirmation that the funds are not repatriated crypto-assets. Intercompany loans require a written facility agreement, CBS exchange-control approval, and arm's-length interest documentation to prevent recharacterisation as equity.Operational trade disbursements for the SaaS business, including cloud hosting, security tooling, subcontractor fees, and platform licences, are cleared through SWIFT MT103 from the IC's Foreign Currency Account in the original invoice currency, USD, EUR, or AUD, with no WST conversion. For institutional-scale procurement or where a vendor requires a bank guarantee of payment, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 through Bank of Samoa or SCB's international correspondent network is permissible; the LC must describe the service in ISIC 6201 terms, and the presentation must include a commercial invoice, a certificate of service performance for the SaaS milestone, and a certificate of origin where requested. SWIFT GPI tracking is strongly recommended to evidence final value-date credit for AML reconciliation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable to tax-exempt International Companies. Domestic transfer pricing rules apply to active Samoan taxpayers with related-party transactions and annual turnover exceeding WST 20 million; no separate Local File or Master File threshold has been gazetted.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations for International Companies. The tax-exempt status may be challenged under the general anti-avoidance rule if the company is effectively managed or controlled in Samoa or lacks demonstrable offshore economic purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 150% of unpaid tax, interest at 10% per annum, criminal fine up to WST 1 million, and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid taxes attributable to their direction or willful misconduct; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 7 years.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Strike-off, mandatory deregistration, forfeiture of assets, and penalty of WST 20,000 under the International Companies Act 1987 as amended.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 5 years, personally liable for winding-up debts, and banned from serving in any Samoan-registered entity.
AML Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WST 300,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018; freezing orders may be imposed on bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Officers face individual criminal liability, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from holding financial services licenses.
Regulatory Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late penalty of WST 200 plus WST 100 per day; strike-off after three months of default; restoration fees up to WST 2,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for restoration costs and, in cases of persistent default, subject to travel bans and temporary prohibition from serving as officers.
