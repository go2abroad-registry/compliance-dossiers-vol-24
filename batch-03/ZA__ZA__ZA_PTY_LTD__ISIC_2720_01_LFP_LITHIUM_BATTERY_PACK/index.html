Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – administered nationally with uniform application across all nine provinces; primary portals are SARS eFiling (www.sarsefiling.co.za) and the official SARS website (www.sars.gov.za); customs functions are embedded in SARS Customs and Excise, not a separate authority.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain a CIPC registration certificate; thereafter it must register as a taxpayer through SARS eFiling, appoint a Public Officer resident in South Africa (mandatory under s246 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (TAA)), and submit: the MOI and CIPC certificate, registered office address, directors' and shareholders' details, South African bank account confirmation, and details of business activities. SARS issues a 10-digit Income Tax Reference Number for corporate income tax (CIT) registration. VAT registration is a separate procedure: the VAT101 application (online or at a SARS branch) must be filed once taxable supplies exceed, or are reasonably expected to exceed, ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period or, if voluntary, from ZAR 50,000. SARS then issues a distinct VAT vendor reference number. If the battery pack assembly operation will have employees, the company must also register with SARS for PAYE, UIF and SDL. Non-resident ownership is fully permitted with no minimum capital; however, the company must be tax-resident in South Africa unless its place of effective management is outside South Africa and it is a resident of a Double Tax Agreement treaty country for treaty-tie-breaker purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – a South African incorporated (Pty) Ltd is a separate CIT taxpayer; normal tax at 27% applies to worldwide income for residents (Income Tax Act 58 of 1962); distributions are subject to 20% Dividends Tax; capital gains are included at an 80% inclusion rate (21.6% effective at the 27% rate). No look-through, fiscally transparent, or pass-through classification is available to a (Pty) Ltd. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a South African incorporated (Pty) Ltd is a separate CIT taxpayer; normal tax at 27% applies to worldwide income for residents (Income Tax Act 58 of 1962); distributions are subject to 20% Dividends Tax; capital gains are included at an 80% inclusion rate (21.6% effective at the 27% rate). No look-through, fiscally transparent, or pass-through classification is available to a (Pty) Ltd. Non-resident shareholders are not taxed on retained company profits in South Africa, but branch profits of non-resident companies are taxed at the same CIT rates and no dividend tax is payable on branch remittances.

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include: (1) Small Business Corporation (SBC) concessional rate schedule under s12E of the Income Tax Act for qualifying companies with annual turnover up to ZAR 20 million and all shareholders as natural persons; (2) VAT group registration election under s50 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991, allowing two or more connected companies to register as a single VAT group; (3) s11D research and development tax incentive election (150% deduction) with SARS pre-approval; (4) s12B / s12BA accelerated capital allowance elections for manufacturing assets and renewable energy / battery energy storage assets; (5) s24J loan-original-issue and s24C deferred-income elections. Hybrid mismatch rules under s8G–s8M of the Income Tax Act apply mandatorily and cannot be elective; no entity-level election can override the opaque taxation of a (Pty) Ltd.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies made in South Africa, measured over the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected over the following 12 months (s23(1) of the VAT Act). Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 in taxable supplies (s25(1)). Foreign suppliers of electronic/digital services to South African residents are compulsorily required to register when deriving more than ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period from such services. A manufacturing (Pty) Ltd importing lithium battery components and manufacturing LFP battery packs will invariably exceed the threshold and must register for VAT even if its sales are export-oriented, in order to recover input VAT on imported and domestic costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the supplier (the (Pty) Ltd) sells movable goods that are consigned or delivered by the supplier to an address in a foreign country, provided the goods are exported under customs control and the supplier obtains and retains documentary proof of export within the prescribed timeframe (SARS requires proof normally within 60 days from the time of supply; if goods have not left South Africa within 12 months, zero-rating may be denied). The supplier must be the exporter and the sale must be on terms that put the risk and title transfer offshore. Services physically rendered offshore to non-residents (e.g., engineering or commissioning services for energy storage installations) qualify for zero-rating under s11(2)(l) subject to documentary evidence. Input VAT incurred on battery cell imports, component purchases, and manufacturing overheads is fully creditable, enabling a refundable VAT position for an export-oriented LFP battery pack manufacturer.

  • Valid full tax invoice (s20 of the VAT Act, unless the export invoice complies with an exemption)
  • Customs export declaration SAD500 (electronic via SARS e-SAD) evidencing the goods left South African territory
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or road consignment note for SACU cross-border road exports) showing the foreign destination
  • Proof of physical removal including shipping instructions, delivery order, packing list and container tracking evidence
  • Commercial sales contract / purchase order from the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT / bank confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a South African authorised-dealer bank account

South Africa does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping mandate comparable to Latin American models. However: (1) VAT vendors must issue full tax invoices (s20 of the VAT Act), retaining sequentially numbered, duplicate, original copies for 5 years; (2) all VAT returns (VAT201) must be filed electronically via SARS eFiling or integrated EDI/API channels; (3) SARS is implementing the e-invoicing interoperability framework aligned to Peppol and has signalled phased mandatory e-invoicing for large businesses from 2026, with the Tax Administration Act requiring complete electronic record retention; (4) for exports, the SAD500 customs declaration must be lodged electronically via SARS Customs e-SAD prior to goods leaving South Africa, and cargo data is subject to customs clearance and risk-based inspection before departure.

SARS Customs and VAT auditors maintain focused export verification campaigns; a zero-rating claim will be reversed if any core document is missing or inconsistent. Consequences: (1) output VAT assessed at 15% on the full supply value plus interest at the prescribed rate from the original due date; (2) understatement penalties under s222/s223 of the TAA are graded from 10% (standard) to 200% (gross negligence / intentional tax evasion); (3) additional tax of up to double the evaded amount under s60A of the VAT Act may be imposed for fraudulent evasion; (4) retrospective clawback of all VAT refunds paid on the disputed export transactions plus reopening of earlier refund periods; SARS's normal assessment prescription is 5 years, but there is no prescription period where fraud, misrepresentation, or non-disclosure is established (s99 TAA); (5) criminal prosecution under s232/s233 of the TAA and the Customs and Excise Act for fraudulent export declarations, punishable by fines and imprisonment; (6) for export proceeds not repatriated within 180 days, the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) exchange-control regime treats the proceeds as a contravention of the Currency and Exchanges Act, exposing the company and its Public Officer to administrative penalties, denial of future export levy concessions, and potential blacklisting by authorised dealers.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions (trade in goods, services, dividends, interest, royalties) through an authorised dealer, subject to documentary verification, SARB Balance of Payments (BOP) coding, and the authorisations set out in the Currency and Exchanges Manual. Capital-account flows (equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, disinvestment of foreign direct investment, guarantees) are controlled: non-resident equity and loan capital may enter only through an authorised dealer and must be recorded on the SARB Foreign Investment Register, while outbound capital transactions (share buy-backs, loan repayments, disinvestment proceeds) require authorised-dealer approval and, in specified cases, FinSurv approval. Profit-remittance hurdles: dividend declarations must satisfy the solvency-and-liquidity test in Sections 4 and 46 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008; Dividends Tax at 20% must be withheld unless a Double Taxation Agreement reduces or exempts the rate; a SARS Tax Compliance Status or Tax Clearance Certificate may be required for the remittance; and the payment must be executed through an authorised dealer with the underlying inward foreign direct investment evidenced on the Foreign Investment Register.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of LiFePO4 battery packs must complete the SARS Customs SAD500 export declaration and must repatriate all export proceeds to South Africa within 30 days of the date of shipment (Exchange Control Regulations, reg 22A; SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, Exports chapter). A longer settlement period requires a commercially justified application to the authorised dealer; extensions beyond the dealer's discretion require FinSurv approval. No mandatory fixed percentage conversion into ZAR applies to export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign exchange in an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with an authorised dealer, but all proceeds must be received into the exporter's South African banking relationship within the 30-day window and must be supported by the export documentation set (SAD500, Bill of Lading/Airway Bill, commercial invoice, packing list) and, where applicable, ITAC export permits for controlled goods.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers. The Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) supervises AML/CFT obligations under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI/e-money institutions (for example EU/UK FCA-authorised firms) do not hold a South African banking licence and cannot independently hold ZAR settlement accounts or clear domestic payments as principals. Under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 and the SARB Payments System Framework, such providers must partner with a South African authorised dealer as agent or obtain registration as a third-party payment provider with the SARB National Payment System Department. Feasibility rating for treasury use: Low as the sole operating and clearing rail for ZAR; Medium as an ancillary multi-currency FX/receivables aggregator, provided all funds transit through a local authorised-dealer account and complete import/export documentation is maintained for SARS and SARB inspection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI/e-money institutions (for example EU/UK FCA-authorised firms) do not hold a South African banking licence and cannot independently hold ZAR settlement accounts or clear domestic payments as principals. Under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 and the SARB Payments System Framework, such providers must partner with a South African authorised dealer as agent or obtain registration as a third-party payment provider with the SARB National Payment System Department. Feasibility rating for treasury use: Low as the sole operating and clearing rail for ZAR; Medium as an ancillary multi-currency FX/receivables aggregator, provided all funds transit through a local authorised-dealer account and complete import/export documentation is maintained for SARS and SARB inspection.

Turnover: A (Pty) Ltd is required to undergo a full independent audit when its public interest score (PI score) under Companies Regulation 26 is 350 or more. The PI score is calculated as: (a) 1 point per average employee; (b) 1 point per ZAR 1 million of third-party liabilities; (c) 1 point per ZAR 1 million of turnover; and (d) 1 point per shareholder. A PI score of 100–349 mandates an independent review under ISRE 2400 by a registered accountant; a PI score below 100 confers exemption from both audit and independent review. The ISIC 2720 LFP battery pack assembly operation, with manufacturing turnover and trade creditor liabilities, will almost certainly exceed 100 points and likely approach or exceed 350 points, making at least an independent review compulsory and a full audit likely.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5 million or more in annual cross-border connected-person transactions triggers the need for contemporaneous Master File/Local File documentation in accordance with SARS TP Practice Note.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime. However, section 80A-L GAAR, section 31 transfer pricing rules, and CFC legislation require genuine economic substance (staff, office, equipment, risk, decision-making). Letterbox companies and treaty-driven conduits are vulnerable to anti-avoidance reassessments and denial of DTA relief under the Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to ZAR 2,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years under Tax Administration Act sections 234 and 235. Civil understatement penalty of up to 100%–200% of unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: SARS may pierce the corporate veil (TAA section 262) and hold directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes plus penalties. Directors can face travel bans, asset seizure, and imprisonment for wilful evasion.
Shell Company / Artificial Conduit

Statutory sanction: SARS may ignore the entity as a sham under GAAR, recharacterize transactions, and assess the real owner. Penalties can reach 200% of tax avoided. Treaty relief may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test of the MLI.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate a sham structure may be made personally liable for corporate tax debts and may be disqualified from acting as directors. Criminal prosecution for fraud and exchange-control evasion is possible.
AML / Exchange Control Violations

Statutory sanction: FIC Act contravention: fines up to ZAR 10,000,000 per offence. Currency and Exchanges Act breach: penalty on treble the amount involved and/or imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal fines, sequestration, travel bans, and imprisonment for wilful AML or exchange-control non-compliance. Senior management must implement and enforce an effective Risk and Compliance Programme (RCP).
