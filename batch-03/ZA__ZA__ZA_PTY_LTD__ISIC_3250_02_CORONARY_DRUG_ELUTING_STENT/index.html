Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs declaration processing through SARS Customs Connect/eCANCOR.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; CIPC data transfer to SARS automatically generates the provisional income tax reference number (ITR). 2) Appoint a Public Officer within one month under s246 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. 3) Register for VAT via form VAT101 within 21 days of taxable turnover exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available once turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000. 4) Register for PAYE/SDL/UIF (EMP101) where employees are engaged. 5) Register as a SARS customs client to obtain an exporter code before submitting SAD500 export declarations. 6) Non-resident parent or service entities must apply through the TRS (Tax Registration Status) process to obtain a non-resident tax reference number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a South African tax-resident (Pty) Ltd is subject to corporate income tax at 27% on worldwide income, capital gains tax at an 80% inclusion rate, and 20% dividends tax on distributions; no pass-through classification is available.

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box regime. Applicable elections include: VAT group registration (s50 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991); voluntary VAT registration (s23 VAT Act); input-tax de minimis apportionment under s17(1) VAT Act; controlled-foreign-company elections under s9D Income Tax Act; hybrid mismatch elections under s25H and interest limitation elections under s23M; and Research & Development allowance elections under s11D Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period (s23(1) VAT Act). Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 (s23(2) VAT Act). Non-resident vendors with a South African economic presence are likewise registrable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s11(1)(a) VAT Act where the vendor exports movable goods, including Class III drug-eluting coronary stents manufactured under ISIC 3250, within 90 days of the date of the tax invoice (or an extended period approved by SARS not exceeding 12 months), title passes to the non-resident buyer offshore, and the vendor retains documentary proof of export. Offshore services supplied to non-residents may qualify under s11(2)(l) where the beneficiary is outside South Africa at the time of supply, the services are not consumed in South Africa, and no direct connection exists to South African immovable or movable property.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) evidencing movement of goods out of South Africa
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 credit evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Full tax invoice complying with s20 VAT Act
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • SAHPRA export clearance or certificate for the Class III stents, where applicable

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently in force. Tax invoices must satisfy s20 VAT Act requirements and may be issued electronically provided the data is preserved intact and legible for at least five years. VAT returns (VAT201) are filed electronically through eFiling; customs export clearance is fully electronic via SARS Customs with SAD500 submission and release. SARS is developing e-invoicing interoperability, but no API-based real-time invoice authorization applies as of the current tax year.

SARS applies post-export refund integrity audits. Failure to export within the 90-day period or absence of valid documentation triggers a deemed standard-rated supply under s10(23) VAT Act, clawing back the zero rating at 15% output VAT with statutory interest. Understatement penalties under TAA ss221-223 range from 10% (standard) to 150% (intentional evasion) of the tax shortfall, potentially up to 200% for undisclosed reportable arrangements. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating can result in fines and/or imprisonment of up to five years (TAA s235), restitution orders, and revocation of the SARS exporter code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is a floating currency. Current-account transactions are fully convertible and may be settled freely through an Authorised Dealer with supporting documentation. Capital-account transactions remain subject to exchange-control surveillance, with inward direct investment (equity or shareholder loans) permitted without prior SARB approval but requiring AD registration via Form A and inward-transfer notification. Profit remittances (dividends) are permitted provided the AD receives an auditor-confirmed dividend resolution, a SARS tax-compliance status, and Foreign Investment/Portfolio Investment forms where applicable. Capital repatriation on liquidation requires proof of the original inflow and a FinSurv-approved disinvestment certificate. Non-resident ownership of a (Pty) Ltd is permitted, and there are no residuary restrictions on foreign shareholding percentages, subject only to FICA-driven source-of-funds verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate 100% of invoice value to South Africa within 180 calendar days from the date of shipment (extendable to 360 days with AD/FinSurv approval). The historical mandatory conversion of export proceeds into ZAR was abolished effective 1 January 2021; an exporter may now retain proceeds in a domestic Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or convert to ZAR at its discretion. Failure to timeously receive export proceeds exposes the exporter to administrative penalties under the Exchange Control Regulations (typically 0.5% per month of the outstanding sum) and may trigger suspension of the exporter's FCA facility.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) — Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) administering the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers; licensed commercial banks act as Authorised Dealers (ADs) under Section 9A of the Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) do not hold a South African banking licence or Authorised Dealer status and therefore cannot open ZAR-denominated accounts, issue resident bank guarantees, or settle through the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system. EMIs may only operate via licensed AD partnerships for cross-border payments. For a Class III coronary-stent manufacturer, EMIs are impractical for capital escrow, letter-of-credit issuance, statutory export repatriation, or FEC hedging; their permissible use is limited to low-value operational expenditures. Onboarding such an entity through an EMI would additionally trigger FICA enhanced due diligence without conferring regulated-deposit protection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder-loan transfers must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the company's USD FCA or ZAR operational account at the appointed Authorised Dealer. The payment must carry a purpose code denoting inward direct investment ('Equity Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan'), and the AD must receive a Form A / inward-transfer notification simultaneously. The AD verifies the source of funds, issues a unique inward-transfer reference, and records the transaction in the SARB Financial Surveillance system. Equity inflows above the applicable voluntary-notification threshold require FinSurv designation; net proceeds are to be held in the Capital Escrow Account until share certificates and the updated CIPC securities register are produced. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for all companies and close corporations (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; 28% for earlier years).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% Dividend Tax under sections 64EA-64N of the Income Tax Act; the South African company declaring the dividend must withhold 20% from the beneficial owner's payment and remit it to SARS within 30 days of the dividend declaration or payment, whichever is earlier.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 12 months after the end of the company's financial year (e.g., a 31 March year-end return is due no later than 31 March of the following calendar year); SARS may shorten this period by public notice for a class of taxpayers.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional: within 6 months of the start of the year of assessment. Second provisional: on or before the last day of the company's year of assessment. Third provisional (optional): within 6 months after year-end to reduce interest exposure.

IRP6 – Section 1 (First provisional estimate) Estimated total taxable income for the current year and 50% of the estimated normal tax payable. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory when the aggregate value of 'affected transactions' (cross-border transactions with connected persons) exceeds ZAR 50 million in a year of assessment, or when the company's gross receipts or turnover exceeds ZAR 100 million. A Country-by-Country (CbC) Report is required where the consolidated group revenue is at least ZAR 10 billion. SARS may call for TP documentation below these thresholds under sections 29/30 TAA and section 31 of the Income Tax Act.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no separate ESR statute, but economic substance is embedded in the Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules (section 9D), the Foreign Business Establishment (FBE) exemption, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, sections 80A-80L) and the section 103(1) anti-avoidance provisions. A taxpayer claiming treaty benefits or CFC relief must prove substantive activity: local premises, personnel, equipment, operational management, demonstrated risk-taking and genuine income-generating activities. For a Class III medical device manufacturer, the SAHPRA manufacturing licence, sterile manufacturing plant, R&D laboratory and registered pharmaceutical establishment are the core substance evidence; a 'letterbox' or conduit entity holding only IP and contracts with no local plant, qualified staff or regulatory licence will be recharacterised: interest and royalty deductions denied, CFC income imputed at 27%, dividends treated as deemed distributions, and the arrangement consolidated into a single taxpayer under section 80B.
