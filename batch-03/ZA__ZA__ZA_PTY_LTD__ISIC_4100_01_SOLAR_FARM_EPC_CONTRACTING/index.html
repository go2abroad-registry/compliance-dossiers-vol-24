Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current account transactions (goods, services, dividends, interest, royalties) with no quantitative ceiling on remittances, subject to documentary verification and domestic tax clearance. Capital account transactions (equity subscription, intercompany loans, disinvestment, hedging) operate under a liberalised but managed system: non-resident equity investment and subsequent capital repatriation are permitted without value-based approval, provided SARB Balance of Payments (BOP) reporting, non-resident declarations, and authorised-dealer records are completed. Practical hurdles include longer settlement processing due to documentary checks, profit remittances requiring a SARS Tax Clearance Certificate (COT) and an auditor-certified statement of distributable profits, and the ZAR being an emerging-market currency with moderate offshore liquidity and meaningful NDF depth.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa has abolished mandatory conversion ratios. Under the amended exchange control regulations (2021), all export proceeds for goods and services (including EPC engineering and construction services) must be received in the Republic within 180 days from shipment or service completion (240 days for capital goods). Proceeds may be retained in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with an Authorised Dealer and need only be surrendered to cover domestic rand expenditure. Exporters holding a General Export Permit (GEP) with Approved Exporter (AEO) status retain full retention privileges. Non-receipt within the statutory period requires FinSurv approval and documented remedial evidence, failing which penalties and suspension or withdrawal of the GEP may apply.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933, and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (R.1111), as amended. All cross-border operations are administered through SARB-licensed Authorised Dealers (registered commercial banks) pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW to MEDIUM as a primary treasury rail; HIGH only as a supplementary, low-value disbursement channel. Under the National Payment System Act, No. 78 of 1998, only SARB-designated system operators and Authorised Dealers can execute cross-border ZAR settlements, provide FX conversion, or issue letters of credit. Foreign EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) operate in South Africa solely through local bank partnerships and cannot access the SARB settlement system (SAMOS), cannot hold exchange control 'deemed approval' functions, and cannot clear EPC milestone payments or L/Cs. Recommended role: payment of small subcontractors and vendor invoices below ZAR 1 million monthly; all project milestone flows, large import settlements, and FX hedges must remain in the licensed banking structure.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Incomplete beneficial ownership disclosure (CIPC BO register not filed)

Root cause: Incomplete beneficial ownership disclosure (CIPC BO register not filed)

Defense evidence: Filed CIPC BO1/BO2 beneficial ownership register; notarised share register and shareholders' agreement; signed beneficial ownership declarations from all natural persons holding 5% or more; corporate structure chart confirming the ultimate natural person chain.
Non-resident directors without South African tax registration and local background screening

Root cause: Non-resident directors without South African tax registration and local background screening

Defense evidence: SARS income tax registration confirmation (ITREG), foreign tax residence certificates, police clearance certificates from the home jurisdiction, and South African Police Service (SAPS) clearance for the authorised signatory.
Opacity of source of funds for the inbound capital injection financing the EPC project

Root cause: Opacity of source of funds for the inbound capital injection financing the EPC project

Defense evidence: IFRS-audited financial statements of the foreign parent; board resolution approving the capital subscription; notarised intercompany loan and facility agreements; bank statements showing a three-month funding trail; signed EPC contract and milestone payment schedule evidencing commercial rationale.
Sanctions, negative media, or PEP screening hit on shareholders or directors

Root cause: Sanctions, negative media, or PEP screening hit on shareholders or directors

Defense evidence: World-Check/Refinitiv screening evidence pack; formal explanation letter; sanctions risk assessment memorandum confirming no UN/EU/OFAC designation; professional banker's reference letters for identified PEPs; source of wealth report prepared by a licensed compliance adviser.
Missing sector licensing for solar EPC contracting (construction and electrical installation)

Root cause: Missing sector licensing for solar EPC contracting (construction and electrical installation)

Defense evidence: CIDB (Construction Industry Development Board) grading certificate; Electrical Contractors Board of South Africa registration; Engineering Council of South Africa (ECSA) registration of the responsible professional engineer; DMRE registration for solar installations; annual SARS Tax Clearance Certificate; relevant Certificate of Compliance under the Electrical Installation Regulations.
Non-resident shareholding above 50% with no demonstrable local operational substance

Root cause: Non-resident shareholding above 50% with no demonstrable local operational substance

Defense evidence: Lease agreement for South African offices or project warehouse; payroll and PAYE registration for locally employed staff; environmental authorisation and grid connection application documents; minutes of local management meetings; confirmation letter from the company's South African auditor on operational substance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity injection: inbound SWIFT MT103 or MT202 referencing the SARB BOP 'direct investment' code per the Currency and Exchanges Manual, with the company registration number stated in field 70 or 72. Documentary pack: shareholder resolution, CIPC share allotment filing, share certificates, Non-Resident Declaration issued by the Authorised Dealer, and auditor evidence supporting the consideration for issued shares. Intercompany debt: inward loan requires a notarised loan agreement with arm's length interest and tenor, disclosure through the SARB e-FinSurv portal for loans above ZAR 5 million, and completion of the Authorised Dealer's BOP record. All incoming wires are credited only after the Authorised Dealer completes BOP verification, with rejection risks for missing purpose codes or incomplete backing documentation.PV module and inverter imports: irrevocable commercial letters of credit (MT700) issued by the South African Authorised Dealer, supported by invoices, bill of lading, packing lists, and customs entry (SADC GMR); direct supplier settlements executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 under SARB BOP trade codes. EPC client milestone receipts are received via project milestone wire transfers secured by the L/C or guarantee structure. Performance and advance payment guarantees are issued as standby L/Cs (MT760) on behalf of the EPC contractor. Domestic operating expenses, payroll, and subcontractor payments are cleared in ZAR through the operational account. Local import verification applies above specified thresholds, requiring proof of goods arrival before final supplier settlement.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FEC) - ZAR/USD, ZAR/EUR, and ZAR/CNY forwards with terms up to 12 months, permitted only against documented firm or anticipated underlying transactions (EPC import purchase orders and project milestone receivables); cancellation and rebooking allowed with the Authorised Dealer under SARB rules subject to post-trade documentation review.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) - ZAR versus USD/EUR interest rate swaps permissible for project-finance debt service hedging where the EPC contractor or project SPV has a matched asset-liability structure; requires board-approved hedge policy and SARB transaction recording.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) - offshore ZAR NDFs are permissible as economic hedges for the foreign sponsor, while the onshore EPC entity executes an equivalent deliverable FEC to satisfy the SARB underlying transaction requirement and avoid an unhedged offshore overlay position.Zero-Cost Collars and Vanilla Currency Options - permitted against confirmed contractual EPC exposure, with premiums settled through the Authorised Dealer; the bank must verify the underlying trade as a condition of granting option hedge treatment.
