Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), succeeding the Ministry of Revenue for all tax administration, with the Samoa Taxpayer Online Services portal (https://www.sra.gov.ws); customs matters are handled by the Samoa Customs Service under the Ministry of Revenue.

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 2017 is registered with the Registrar of International Companies at the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) and does not apply for a domestic TIN; the SIFA certificate of incorporation serves as the statutory tax identity. No VAGST registration is required or permitted for an IC because the entity is statutorily outside the scope of the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2008. If a TIN is needed solely for foreign bank onboarding or CRS/FATCA documentation, the IC may request a non-resident TIN from the SRA, which does not trigger any Samoan tax liability.

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box or flow-through election regime; the International Companies Act 2017 confers an automatic, non-waivable zero-tax status on International Companies, which are deemed non-resident and exempt from all Samoan income tax, withholding tax, stamp duty, and VAGST. No hybrid mismatch or entity classification election is available under Samoan law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 60,000 in taxable supplies over any consecutive 12-month period; mandatory VAGST registration applies to resident suppliers exceeding the threshold, but Samoan International Companies remain outside the VAGST net irrespective of turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For VAGST-registered entities, zero-rating applies to the direct export of goods evidenced by customs clearance and to services supplied to non-resident recipients where the benefit is enjoyed outside Samoa and the recipient is not a resident of Samoa. A Samoan International Company is not VAGST-registered and therefore cannot claim input credits or zero-rated refunds; its offshore advertising intermediation services are simply outside the Samoan indirect tax base.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration filed through ASYCUDA World with customs clearance endorsement
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of goods from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign consideration

No real-time electronic fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Samoa. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue standard tax invoices in paper or electronic format under the VAGST Act 2008, and e-filing is available through the SRA portal; however, no government API clearance or B2B continuous transaction control is imposed on International Companies.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims under the VAGST Act 2008 may result in clawback of all input credits, a late payment penalty of up to 25%, monthly default interest of 1%, additional penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded, fines up to SAT 100,000, and imprisonment up to seven years. For a Samoan International Company, any breach of the International Companies Act 2017, such as carrying on trade with Samoan residents without authorization, exposes the IC to loss of its tax-exempt status, retrospective assessment of all Samoan taxes, deregistration by SIFA, and director liability penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a regulated but partially convertible currency pegged to a weighted basket comprising the USD, EUR, JPY, AUD and NZD. Current-account payments are permitted for residents upon submission of an exchange-control declaration, whereas capital-account transfers require prior CBS approval. A Samoa International Company (IC) is expressly exempted from exchange control under Section 247 of the International Companies Act 1987: all IC subscriptions, receipts, and distributions may be effected in freely convertible foreign currency without CBS approval, and dividends or capital distributions to non-resident shareholders are not subject to profit-remittance approval or withholding tax. The practical hurdles are bank-level rather than statutory: the bank will require KYC evidence of beneficial ownership, economic substance documentation, and an invoice-backed purpose code before releasing an outward USD wire. The IC treasury ledger should be maintained 100% in USD because holding WST balances domestically may trigger domestic-income classification and dilute the exchange-control exemption.

Export proceeds surrender rules

General Samoa trading companies exporting goods must receive full payment within 90 days of shipment and surrender/convert 100% of the export proceeds into WST through a CBS-licensed dealer unless a written exemption is granted. This rule is disapplied to International Companies: a Samoa IC deriving programmatic advertising service revenue from offshore advertisers is not an exporter of goods, and under Section 247 of the International Companies Act 1987 it may retain USD advertising revenue in a USD-denominated Samoa bank account or offshore with no statutory repatriation, surrender, or conversion obligation. If the IC were to invoice a Samoa-resident advertiser and receive WST, the proceeds would fall into the domestic regime; therefore, the treasury policy must exclude domestic invoicing and keep all contracting parties non-resident.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS; Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), operating under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and administering exchange control under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977, with AML/CFT supervision under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2017 (as amended) and the KYC Rules 2018.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Samoa has no electronic-money institution or digital-bank licensing regime, and a Samoa International Company cannot passport an overseas EMI licence into the jurisdiction. Global EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, and similar multi-currency platforms) routinely decline entities incorporated in Pacific offshore centres, including Samoa, because their correspondent banks treat the jurisdiction as elevated-risk under FATF-style de-risking. The realistic use of an EMI for this structure is limited to a downstream disbursement rail for paying publishers and SSP counterparties without being the primary treasury concentration account; the core liquidity must sit in a CBS-licensed bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity is injected by SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's corporate or personal bank, with field 50K naming the remitter, field 59 naming the Samoa IC and its USD account, and field 70 referencing the purpose code DIRECT-INVEST-CAPITAL together with the IC certificate number. The Samoa receiving bank will require a Board Resolution authorising the Capital Escrow Account, a notarised share subscription letter, the source-of-funds declaration (audited accounts or 12 months of bank statements of the shareholder), and the CBS exchange-control declaration form (Form EX-1) for balance-of-payments statistical reporting even though the IC itself is exchange-control exempt. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No explicit annual threshold is published for ICs. For taxable companies, Samoa applies the arm's length principle under the Income Tax Act 2012; the Ministry for Revenue may require a transfer pricing study where related-party transactions exceed WST 2,000,000 in aggregate or where the transaction is judged to lack economic substance

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 requires an IC that carries on a relevant activity (including 'holding business' and 'service business' – an AI/SSP platform acting as a service or intermediary) to maintain substantial economic presence in Samoa: physical office or premises, qualified employees, sufficient operating expenditure, and core income-generating activities conducted in or from Samoa. A pure equity holding IC only needs an adequate registered office, staff for holding and managing its shares, and compliance records. The Tax Administration has power to impose penalties for failure; the benefit of IC tax exemption may be denied if economic substance is absent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misdeclaration of Samoan-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Exemption is void; 27% corporate income tax applied to undeclared Samoan-source income; penalty of 100% of the tax understated, late-payment interest of 2% per month, and criminal fine up to WST 100,000

Director personal liability: Directors and the tax agent are jointly and severally liable for unpaid penalties; travel bans may be issued by the District Court; imprisonment up to 2 years for intentional evasion
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of IC tax exemption; retrospective assessment on worldwide income at 27%; penalty of WST 50,000 for non-compliance with ESR; the Registrar may strike off the company

Director personal liability: Directors are liable to pay a personal penalty equivalent to 150% of the revenue earned through the shell arrangement; disqualification as director; cancellation of Samoa residency status
AML / Beneficial Ownership Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to WST 500,000; freezing orders over bank accounts; criminal fine up to WST 1,000,000 and imprisonment for 7 years under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable to criminal prosecution; assets may be confiscated; a failure to cooperate with the FIU triggers a travel ban and mandatory notification to Samoa's international law-enforcement partners
Unapproved Treaty Shopping / DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax shortfall must be paid immediately by the paying company; penalty of 120% of the WHT due; denial of all treaty benefits under the Limitation of Benefits article in Samoa's DTAs

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the tax declaration face personal fines up to WST 250,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 years for making false statements to the Ministry for Revenue
