Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; corporate registration via CIPC data sharing automatically issues income tax reference number

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation with CIPC, SARS automatically issues a company income tax reference number for resident companies. The foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must complete SARS eFiling registration to confirm particulars, then apply via the 'Register for VAT' channel (VAT 101 form) once turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed the R1 million compulsory threshold (or voluntary from R50,000). Separate registrations are required for PAYE, UIF, SDL (EMP 101) and Customs (SAD500 exporter code via SARS Customs Modernisation). A non-resident sole shareholder is permissible; the company is a tax resident if incorporated in South Africa, and must appoint a SARS-appointed public officer within one month of commencement of business.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident company (incorporated in ZA) taxed on worldwide income under s9 of Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; no pass-through; branch of non-resident subject to s9D CFC attribution if POEM outside ZA — Upon incorporation with CIPC, SARS automatically issues a company income tax reference number for resident companies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident company (incorporated in ZA) taxed on worldwide income under s9 of Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; no pass-through; branch of non-resident subject to s9D CFC attribution if POEM outside ZA

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election. Available elections: VAT group registration election under s50(1) of VAT Act 89 of 1991; Small Business Corporation (SBC) turnover election under s12E (R20 million turnover cap) for a 27% reduced corporate rate; s11D R&D tax incentive election (150% deduction); s12B(5) election to accelerate write-off of manufacturing plant and machinery; depreciation and capital allowance elections; Transfer Pricing six-month adjustment election; hybrid mismatch automatic adjustment (no election, mandatory); Turnover-based VAT accounting election (s15(2A)) if annual revenue below R50 million.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 compulsory annual taxable turnover threshold (any rolling 12-month period); voluntary registration at R50,000; branch/external company register if making taxable supplies in ZA and turnover exceeds threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under s11(1)(a) VAT Act for movable goods supplied to a non-resident (who is not a vendor, or is a vendor acquiring for enterprise purposes) if goods are exported from the Republic within 90 days of the time of supply and the supplier retains the prescribed documentary proof. Sterile examination nitrile gloves qualify as movable goods; medical/PPE classification does not alter zero-rating. Services connected to movable goods exported (s11(2)(d)) and services to non-residents (s11(2)(l)) are also zero-rated. All zero-rated supplies must be supported by export documentation; failure to export within 90 days triggers 15% VAT charge.

  • SARs Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 bill of entry) with customs clearance stamping
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing physical departure from a South African port/airport, endorsed to foreign consignee
  • Commercial invoice issued by the (Pty) Ltd supplier showing VAT number and zero-rating reason
  • Inward remittance certificate / proof of foreign currency payment received from the non-resident purchaser
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (SADC/EUR.1 where applicable)
  • Any contract, insurance, or transport documentation proving export within 90 days (required under Interpretation Note 30)

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance/stamping mandate currently in force. Tax invoices must comply with s20 VAT Act prescribed particulars (vendor name, VAT number, invoice number, date, description, quantity, consideration). SARS accepts e-invoices and EDI submissions; VAT returns filed electronically via eFiling or e@syFile Vendor. Customs export declarations (SAD500) and export clearance must be lodged electronically through the SARS Customs Modernisation platform (EDI or eFiling), with automatic risk-based selectivity but no continuous transactional digital stamping.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is a managed-float currency with operationally convertible current account transactions routed through SARB Authorised Dealers; capital account transactions remain administratively controlled. A South African (Pty) Ltd owned wholly or partly by non-residents may receive equity capital without minimum statutory capital, but the inward subscription must be reported by the receiving Authorised Dealer under the inward investment reporting framework and must satisfy FICA beneficial ownership verification. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permissible once the dividend is declared in accordance with the Companies Act solvency and liquidity test, South African dividend tax at 20% is withheld or a treaty exemption applies, and the paying bank obtains supporting board resolutions, SARS declarations, and non-resident shareholder tax status evidence. Liquidation proceeds and surplus distributions may require prior FinSurv approval where treated as capital repatriation rather than ordinary dividends.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SARB Exchange Control Regulations, export proceeds for goods shipped by a South African resident must be received in full within 180 days from date of shipment, unless a specific extension is granted. Upon receipt, 100% of the foreign currency proceeds must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer by conversion into ZAR unless the exporter holds a FinSurv-approved Foreign Currency Account permitting retention under defined conditions. The default statutory conversion ratio is therefore 100%; no private offshore settlement or indefinite retention of export earnings is allowed. Each export transaction must be supported by the SARS customs declaration (SAD500), transport documents, a client invoice, and proof of receipt/conversion submitted to the Authorised Dealer for compliance reporting.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, with domestic banking supervision conducted by the SARB Prudential Authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury banking layer for the South African entity. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not SARB Authorised Dealers and cannot lawfully provide ZAR current-account services or execute the mandatory SARB surrender of export proceeds on behalf of a South African exporter. Such platforms may be used only for ancillary inbound fee payments or payments to suppliers outside South Africa where the funds never touch the local ZAR clearing system; each use must still be reported by the South African entity to its Authorised Dealer and must avoid prohibited speculative FX activity. Any EMI operating in South Africa would need FSCA licensing and SARB financial surveillance approval, which makes full treasury substitution impracticable.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Non-resident ownership chain with layered intermediate incorporation vehicles, holding companies, or discretionary trusts masking the natural-person ultimate beneficial owner.

Defense evidence: CIPC-compliant register of beneficial ownership (CoR 35.2 or equivalent), full corporate organisation chart, certified certificate of incorporation for each chain entity, register of members, trust deed or written trust arrangement where applicable, passports of all ultimate natural persons, source-of-funds bank statements for the capital contribution, and board resolution approving the legal entity banking relationship.

Root cause: Regulatory sensitivity of the 'sterile medical device' product class, specifically whether the SAHPRA registration, sterilisation validation, and medical-device quality certifications match the export sales profile.

Defense evidence: SAHPRA medical device establishment licence or registration certificate for the Class II sterile nitrile examination glove, ISO 13485 certificate, ISO 11135/ISO 11137 sterilisation validation reports or batch release records, CE marking certificate or EU Notified Body certificate where exporting to regulated markets, FDA 510(k) listing where applicable, and current Good Manufacturing Practice evidence.
High-frequency export to multiple African and offshore buyers with divergent invoice pricing creates potential trade-based money laundering red flags.

Request for offshore Foreign Currency Accounts, structured FX hedging, or payment routing through correspondent banks in third countries triggers additional exchange control and sanctions scrutiny.

Root cause: Request for offshore Foreign Currency Accounts, structured FX hedging, or payment routing through correspondent banks in third countries triggers additional exchange control and sanctions scrutiny.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder remits subscription proceeds by SWIFT MT103 from its foreign corporate account to the South African corporate banking account held with an Authorised Dealer. The payment instruction must identify the named South African beneficiary entity, use a capital-receipt purpose code such as 'Equity Investment in South African Company' or SARB Balance of Payments reporting code for inward foreign direct investment, and include full remitter and beneficiary name details. The Authorised Dealer performs FICA checks, sanctions screening, and inward investment reporting to FinSurv, and will allocate a capital reference before releasing funds into the operational ZAR account through the SAMOS local-ZAR clearing system. A signed Share Subscription Agreement, board resolution, register of members, CIPC beneficial ownership update, and proof of source of funds must be retained. Non-resident intercompany loan capital is also permissible but must be reported to FinSurv through the Authorised Dealer, with loan agreements, interest-rate terms, and repayment schedules filed.Outbound settlement for imported raw materials, chemicals, packaging, and production machinery should be executed through Authorised Dealer rails: direct SWIFT MT103 payment against commercial documentation, documentary collection (D/P or D/A), or commercial letters of credit issued by the South African bank via SWIFT MT700 and advised/confirmed through a correspondent bank. For large contracted supplier purchases, an irrevocable confirmed commercial LC is preferred because it simultaneously protects the exporter and gives the issuing bank documented trade evidence. The Authorised Dealer requires the commercial invoice, transport documents, packing list, insurance certificate, SARS customs SAD500 import declaration, and, where applicable, evidence of customs duty payment. Advance payments are permitted only with supporting pro-forma invoices and bank-guarantee triggers; import settlement is ordinarily expected within 180 days of shipment and any rollover requires bank justification.Deliverable USD/ZAR and EUR/ZAR forward exchange contracts (FX forwards) with an SARB Authorised Dealer, executed only against documented current-account or approved capital-account underlying exposures.Cross-currency swaps between ZAR and the trade currency used to hedge both principal and interest cash flows on approved offshore funding or material import/export receivables.FX option collars and risk reversals used as a hedge on anticipated export revenue, provided the instrument neutralises the underlying currency risk and contains no speculative open position.Onshore ZAR non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only where expressly approved by FinSurv or accepted as an institutionally available instrument for qualifying non-resident counterparties; ordinary resident operating-entity hedging uses deliverable forwards instead.

Late/non-payment of provisional tax triggers interest under section 89quat of the Income Tax Act. Additionally, if the estimated taxable income is materially understated (i.e., less than 90% of actual), a 15% to 20% penalty may be imposed under paragraph 20 of the Fourth Schedule.
Form Section 2 Estimated taxable income for the current year - must be a reasonable estimate based on available financial information. Include only income subject to South African normal tax; exclude treaty-exempt income; disregard temporary timing differences in the absence of a SARS confirmation. Late/non-payment of provisional tax triggers interest under section 89quat of the Income Tax Act. Additionally, if the estimated taxable income is materially understated (i.e., less than 90% of actual), a 15% to 20% penalty may be imposed under paragraph 20 of the Fourth Schedule.
Form Section 4 Provisional tax payable - 27% of estimated taxable income less any applicable rebates; payments are made in two installments. Non-resident payors must pay provisional tax before any dividend or interest is distributed if the underlying earnings are South African-source and not fully subjected to withholding. Late/non-payment of provisional tax triggers interest under section 89quat of the Income Tax Act. Additionally, if the estimated taxable income is materially understated (i.e., less than 90% of actual), a 15% to 20% penalty may be imposed under paragraph 20 of the Fourth Schedule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Country-by-Country Report mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue ≥ ZAR 10 billion; Local File and Master File documentation expected where the aggregate value of cross-border connected-person transactions exceeds ZAR 5 million. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared for every cross-border transaction falling within those thresholds.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations, but the common-law 'substance over form' doctrine, the controlled foreign company rules (Section 9D), and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (Sections 80A-80L) of the Income Tax Act are applied. A domiciled shell company with minimal substance will be disregarded for residency and treaty-access purposes, and its income may be re-characterised as flowing to the real controlling foreign entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Section 235 of the Tax Administration Act, intentional tax evasion is a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to ZAR 1 million or imprisonment for up to five years. Section 234 imposes a separate penalty of up to two years' imprisonment for knowingly making false statements in a tax return.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed under Section 9 of the Tax Administration Act when a company's failure to pay tax results from negligence, fraud, or illegal acts committed with the director's knowledge or consent. Civil damages, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment apply on conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS will disregard the entity's separate existence under the substance-over-form doctrine; the income is re-attributed to the beneficial owner. An additional 'conduit-company' penalty of 100% of the tax evaded may be levied under Section 223, and administrative penalties and interest accrue from the due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who set up a shell entity to evade tax become jointly and severally liable for the entire tax deficiency, plus penalties and interest. A court may issue a personal travel ban and order restitution of assets transferred to offshore accounts.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / FICA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FICA imposes administrative penalties of up to ZAR 10 million per breach on a company; criminal sanctions include a fine of up to ZAR 100 million or imprisonment up to 15 years for wilful non-compliance and money laundering offences.

Director personal liability: Directors are presumed to be responsible for institutional AML failures; personal fines up to ZAR 10 million, civil disqualification from directorship, and extradition-compatible criminal charges can be pursued. Travel bans are routinely imposed pending investigation.
