Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Ministry for Revenue (MfR) — online portal: MfR i-Tax (https://www.revenue.gov.ws)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate as an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 via the Samoa Registrar of International & Foreign Companies (MCIL). Step 2: Apply for a Samoa Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) through the MfR i-Tax portal within 14 days of commencing any taxable activity if the IC employs local staff, leases local property, imports goods, or purchases taxable local supplies (Tax Administration Act 2016, s.67). Step 3: File the TIN application form together with Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles, and director/shareholder identity documents. Step 4: For VAGST, file Form VAGST-REG for compulsory registration once taxable turnover exceeds WST 220,000 in any 12-month period; voluntary registration is available below this threshold to recover input tax. Step 5: MfR issues a consolidated TIN certificate and VAGST registration number accessible via the i-Tax login. A pure offshore PR firm with no Samoan-source income, no local payroll, and no local procurement remains outside the income tax registration net because of the IC's statutory income tax exemption.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Samoan law. An IC is per se exempt from income tax for 20 years from incorporation (International Companies Act 1987). Continuing to hold valid IC status constitutes the operative tax-exempt election; the only elective mechanism is voluntary VAGST registration under s.11 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992 (as amended), which allows an offshore supplier to register to recover input VAT on local costs, subject to MfR approval. A company may alternatively convert its IC status to that of a domestic company under the International Companies Act 1987, thereby electing into standard entity-level taxation under the Income Tax Act 2012, but this terminates the exemption. No other classification elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 220,000 annual taxable turnover (compulsory threshold; voluntary registration permitted below threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for the 0% VAGST rate when physically exported from Samoa within 28 days (extension to 60 days possible) and the exporter retains proof of export. Services qualify for zero-rating when supplied under a written contract to a non-resident person who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and the services are not acquired for use in Samoa. For an International Company providing overseas PR and communication services exclusively to non-resident clients, supplies are zero-rated if VAGST-registered or outside the scope of Samoan VAGST if unregistered. Zero-rating claims require the supplier to hold export documentation, a written service contract, evidence of foreign exchange consideration, and proof of the client's non-resident status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World) endorsed by Samoa Customs Division
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods (for service exports: signed service agreement and delivery/performance records)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Central Bank of Samoa exchange control form evidencing repatriation of export proceeds in foreign currency

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or e-invoicing API mandate exists in Samoa. Registered taxpayers must file VAGST returns electronically via the MfR i-Tax portal on a monthly or quarterly basis as directed by the Commissioner. Customs matters are processed through ASYCUDA World for export declarations. There is no live-invoice clearance or continuous transaction control regime.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are subject to desk audit and post-clearance review by the MfR. Unsupported or fraudulent claims attract: (i) a shortfall penalty of 50% of the tax underpaid for failure to take reasonable care; (ii) a 150% penalty for intentional evasion; (iii) late-payment interest accruing at 2% per month from the due date; and (iv) criminal prosecution with a fine of up to WST 50,000 and/or up to 12 months' imprisonment for knowingly providing false or misleading information to the Commissioner (Tax Administration Act 2016, ss.68-70, 86). The Commissioner may retrospectively reassess and claw back any refund amount within a 4-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā is subject to a partially convertible, exchange-control-supervised regime. Current account transactions, including trade in goods, services remittances, and ordinary consulting retainers, are generally free of prior Central Bank approval, with delegated authority to licensed commercial banks for documentary verification. Capital account transactions remain controlled, requiring explicit Central Bank of Samoa approval for outbound direct investment, resident currency diversification, and certain non-resident financing repayments. For the Samoan International Company in the PR and communications sector, profit remittance is not structurally obstructed provided the entity maintains non-resident offshore status under the International Companies Act 2012; however, Samoa-resident subsidiaries must evidence tax clearance from the Ministry of Revenue and audited financial statements demonstrating distributable after-tax profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1984, exporters must repatriate all export proceeds within 90 days of shipment date. WST-denominated export receipts must be surrendered at 100% conversion to an authorized commercial bank or money changer. Foreign-currency receipt proceeds may be retained in a Samoan Foreign Currency Deposit account, but must be brought into Samoa through a licensed bank within the 90-day statutory period. Deviations or extended repayment terms require written Central Bank of Samoa approval supported by signed trade contracts, invoices, and a compliance declaration from the settlement bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Fa'aputuputū Tupe Tu'u a Sāmoa), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, exercising delegated exchange control supervision under the Exchange Control Act 1968 (Cap. 214) and the Exchange Control Regulations 1984.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Ramp Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for WST-denominated liquidity. Samoa does not license standalone electronic money institutions under the Financial Institutions Act 1996, and non-bank EMIs cannot legally offer deposit-taking or WST settlement. A prudentially regulated offshore EMI may be used solely as an upstream remittance or treasury-on-ramp rail for USD receipts, with final custody and clearing anchored in a licensed Samoan commercial bank or a high-quality international correspondent USD account. Any structure routing funds through an unlicensed offshore EMI without a domestic banking anchor triggers enhanced AML scrutiny and Central Bank of Samoa sanctions risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a compliant International Company under Section 199 of the International Companies Act 1987; Samoa's ordinary domestic corporate income tax rate is 25% under the Income Tax Act 2012, but this rate never applies to an exempt IC.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - dividends, capital distributions and liquidation proceeds paid by a Samoa International Company to non-resident shareholders are wholly exempt from Samoan withholding tax under Section 199 of the International Companies Act 1987; no dividend WHT is levied at source and no Samoan reporting or collection mechanism applies to outbound distributions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The first annual return is due within 12 months after the date of incorporation; subsequent returns are due within 12 months after the date of the most recent annual return, i.e., by each incorporation anniversary; a final return is required on deregistration or liquidation.

Deadline: · Frequency:

No statutory deadline; issued within 5-10 business days of a written application accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation and a copy of the latest annual return; valid for the calendar or fiscal year stated and reissued upon renewal.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold applies to Samoa International Companies because they sit outside the Samoan income tax net and are exempt from tax; no Local File or Master File is filed in Samoa. As an institutional best-practice and OECD-aligned risk benchmark, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (including an arm's length benchmarking study) should be prepared when annual gross cross-border related-party transactions - particularly intercompany recharges of media buying, global campaign production and management fees - reach WST 2,700,000 (approximately USD 1,000,000) per annum.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Samoa, as an OECD 'no or only nominal tax' jurisdiction, has committed to economic substance requirements for International Companies carrying on relevant activities, which include service centers, headquarters, distribution, financing, banking, insurance, shipping and IP holding. A global PR and communications firm is a 'service center' type business: the core income-generating activities - client contracting, campaign strategy, media negotiation, contract risk management, and invoice approval - must be genuinely conducted in Samoa through a physical office with full-time employees, local board meetings and contemporaneous decision-making records. Alternatively, the IC may rely on the foreign-resident-parent substance exemption by demonstrating that the relevant income is subject to a substantial foreign tax regime. Failure to meet these requirements triggers a civil penalty of up to WST 50,000 per infraction, rectification orders, strike-off for persistent non-compliance, spontaneous exchange of information with the shareholders' tax authorities, and classification of the IC as a shell entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Misuse of the Section 199 IC Tax Exemption

Statutory sanction: Automatic forfeiture of the zero-tax exemption; compulsory conversion to or re-registration as a domestic company; back-assessment of corporate income tax at 25% for all open years (up to 6 prior fiscal years); late payment interest at approximately 12% per annum; penalty of up to 150% of the tax shortfall; deregistration and prohibition from the IC register.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are jointly and severally liable for all unpaid tax and penalties; civil restitution orders for concealed profits; travel bans and asset freezing; criminal prosecution for tax evasion carrying a fine of up to USD 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 14 years; permanent disqualification from acting as a director of a Samoa company.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure (OECD ESR)

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalty of up to WST 50,000 per company per infraction; mandatory rectification notice; Registry strike-off for persistent default; referral to the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices; automatic exchange of information with the beneficial owner's home tax authority; termination of correspondent banking relations.

Director personal liability: Directors personally fined, disqualified from holding office and named in integrated information-exchange disclosures; travel bans pending rectification; in aggravated cases, civil liability to creditors and the estate for operating a sham offshore structure.
