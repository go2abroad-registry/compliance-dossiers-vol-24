Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); SARS eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; Customs operations via SARS Customs division (SAD500 lodgement).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the enterprise number. Step 2: Register for Income Tax and VAT through SARS eFiling using the enterprise number; SARS issues the Income Tax Reference Number (ITRN) and the VAT vendor number (SARS VAT 103 registration notice). Step 3: Register as an exporter with SARS Customs to obtain the customs client code for electronic SAD500 export declarations. Step 4: Foreign shareholders must be declared under the exchange control 'authorised dealer' framework; non-resident directors require SARS ITRN via the non-resident tax registration workflow and, where applicable, a local representative taxpayer appointment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The ZA (Pty) Ltd is a South African resident company under s.1 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, taxed on worldwide income at a 27% corporate income tax rate (assessment years commencing on/after 1 April 2022). No pass-through or transparent treatment is available to a (Pty) Ltd; capital gains are subject to an 80% inclusion rate; the company is a separate taxpayer from its shareholders.

Available election forms

Small Business Corporation (SBC) election under s.12E: progressive rates (0%–27%) applicable up to ZAR 550,000 taxable income for qualifying companies with gross income below ZAR 20 million. No US-style check-the-box election exists under South African law; residency is determined by incorporation or effective management. Hybrid mismatch rules apply through s.8E (hybrid debt instruments), s.8F/8FA (interest limitation), s.23M (hybrid instruments and hybrid entities), s.25H (conduit arrangements), and the Multi-Lateral Instrument (MLI) via the integrated tax treaty framework. Voluntary VAT registration election under s.25(1) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 is available where annual taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 but fall below the ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 annual taxable supplies (s.23(1) VAT Act); Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 annual taxable supplies (s.25(1) VAT Act); Non-resident electronic services suppliers: ZAR 1,000,000 threshold (s.23(1A) VAT Act).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s.11(1)(a) of the VAT Act for goods physically exported by the supplier (as exporter of record) where the goods are removed from South Africa within 90 days of the earlier of the invoice date or supply date and prescribed documentary proof is retained. Services zero-rating under s.11(2)(l) applies to services physically rendered wholly outside South Africa; s.11(2)(k) zero-rates services supplied to non-residents where the services are not 'consumed' in South Africa. Claimants must hold a valid customs export declaration (SAD500), commercial invoice, transport documentation, and proof of settlement in foreign currency or exported goods in compliance with South African Exchange Control Rulings and SARS Interpretation Note 30.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 Bill of Entry) lodged with SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice with full export particulars
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank settlement advice proving foreign currency repatriation
  • Proof of delivery/export (port exit stamp, freight forwarder cargo manifest, container tracking)
  • Payment evidence and underlying sales contract / purchase order

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or API stamping regime currently applies in South Africa. SARS has conducted soft-landing e-invoicing pilots (2023–2025) and announced phased adoption, but full CTC (continuous transaction control) clearance is not yet legislated. Tax invoices under s.20 of the VAT Act must be issued no later than 21 days from date of supply; electronic tax invoices are valid where the integrity of the original data is maintained through a reliable auditable trail. Exporters must file VAT returns via eFiling with supporting SAD500 data matched against SARS Customs records.

SARS audit wing: incorrect zero-rating triggers output VAT clawback (15%) plus interest from date of supply (s.187 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011). Failure to submit returns risks a penalty of up to 10% of unpaid VAT per month (s.60 VAT Act). Understatement penalties (s.223–226 TAA) range from 10% for substantial understatements (prejudicial intent absent) up to 150% for intentional tax evasion or gross negligence with no reasonable grounds for the tax position. Fraudulent zero-rating or fictitious export schemes constitute criminal tax evasion under s.235 TAA: on conviction, a fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years per count. Statutory assessment periods extend to 5 years for non-disclosure and are unlimited in cases of fraud, misrepresentation, or non-submission of returns (s.99 TAA).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa applies a dual-tier exchange-control regime. Current-account transactions, including import/export trade flows, service fees, royalties, dividends and interest, are fully convertible and may be effected by Authorised Dealers without prior SARB approval, provided the relevant transaction documents and SARS tax clearance are presented. Capital-account transactions—such as the inward issue of shares to non-residents, the repatriation of capital, external loans to residents, and direct investments by residents offshore—require either prior FinSurv approval or strict post-transaction registration through an Authorised Dealer. Profit remittance for a (Pty) Ltd is permitted once the distribution has been formally declared in accordance with the Companies Act 2008, the company has obtained a valid SARS tax clearance, and the non-resident shareholder's repatriation application (Form MP336(b)/MP920) is lodged by the bank; withholding tax on dividends (div tax) must be withheld and remitted to SARS. No general block exists on conversions, but any suspicious or unusual trade settlement may be held pending anti-money-laundering / FIC Act review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Section B.2 of the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, the exporter must ensure that proceeds for exported goods are received in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment. Unless the exporter holds an approved Foreign Currency Account (FFC/STC dispensation) or a Special Export Allowance, the foreign-currency proceeds must be sold into rands immediately upon receipt. There is no statutory mandatory minimum conversion ratio for general export proceeds; however, any surplus retained in an approved FFC may not be held in unapproved offshore accounts. Inward receipts must be fully accounted with reference to the original export invoice, bill of lading / air waybill, and customs export declaration (SAD500), and late receipts require a 'confirmation of export proceeds' letter to the bank.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the delegated exchange-control authority, with administrative enforcement by Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and payment-service providers are rated 'Low' as the primary banking rail for a South African resident operating entity. The SARB exchange-control regime requires all resident external account transactions and trade settlements to be handled by an Authorised Dealer (a South African bank with FEA licence). An offshore EMI may only be used as an ancillary collection gateway (e.g., for Kickstarter/Stripe settlement) if the company applies for an inward process with its own bank, and even then the EMI must be a FATF-licensed institution with adequate KYC. Such EMIs generally cannot open alternative banking accounts in ZAR, cannot issue LCs, and are not regulated by the FSCA as banks for SARB purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require the non-resident investor to instruct its overseas bank to send an SWIFT MT103 to the South African Authorised Dealer's correspondent, with the beneficiary being the (Pty) Ltd's capital escrow account and the payment reference quoting the full legal entity name, corporate registration number, and the SARB Balance of Payments code for 'Equity capital injection' (code 4101 / inward equity). For amounts above the Authorised Dealer's auto-approval threshold (usually ZAR 10 million cumulatively), the bank must be supplied with the completed 'Inward Capital Declaration' (form IED) and supporting share-allotment documentation. The capital receipt must be matched to a resolution of the board, a certified share subscription agreement, and the eventual filing of a capital letter / certificate (CS1 and CS6 issue) with CIPC. For non-resident loans exceeding ZAR 10 million, the SARB requires a prior application through the bank (financing approval via Form CM) while smaller loans may be processed as a registration subsequent to receipt. Funds may only be applied to the stated business purpose once final compliance approval is appended by the bank.All outbound payments to foreign suppliers of epoxy resin, polyhedral moulds, sharp-edge customisable dice blanks, packaging, or overseas print-runs must be effected via SWIFT MT103 (Producer-to-bank transfer) for standard value settlements, or via MT202 for nostro-level transfers if instructed by the bank. The payment instruction must incorporate the correct SARB import/period designation code (typically 'IM' — imports of goods) and cite the original invoice, shipping documents, and the harmonised system commodity code. Where the overseas vendor requires assurance of payment, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (UCP600) may be issued by the Authorised Dealer, with the LC being advised/confirmed by the vendor's bank; LED-free hand-cast dice are generally 'finished products' subject to customs entry upon import. For purchases from SACU partners (e.g., Namibia, Botswana, Lesotho, Eswatini), SADC payments may be routed via the central bank's regional payment settlement system in ZAR without SWIFT, but still require a bank-declared purpose. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: a South African company holding connected-person transactions (cross-border or domestic) with an aggregate gross consideration exceeding ZAR 100 million in a year of assessment must prepare and retain a SARS-compliant Local File. Master File threshold: the multinational group must prepare a Master File where the group's consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion. Country-by-Country Report (CbCR): the ultimate parent, or a surrogate parent entity in SA, must file a CbCR where the group's consolidated revenue is at least ZAR 10 billion. Additionally, SARS Practice Note 7 and the 2022 SARS Transfer Pricing Guide oblige taxpayers to retain transactional records (e.g., contracts, invoices, board minutes, pricing analyses) for any transaction exceeding ZAR 5 million (goods) or ZAR 1 million (services), even if a full Local File is not mandatory.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not impose a separate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime, but substance is a decisive factor in four areas: (1) tax residence and POEM — a company incorporated in SA is resident unless its POEM is outside SA (section 1, ITA); (2) CFC attribution — section 9D attributes passive income of a controlled foreign company to SA shareholders unless a 'foreign business establishment' exemption applies, which requires physical premises, employees, and management activity abroad; (3) transfer pricing and earnings stripping — section 31 requires that connected-person transactions be priced as if between independent parties and that the 'reality' of the arrangement reflect commercial substance, while section 23M limits interest deductions where the debt-to-equity ratio and anti-avoidance tests are not met; (4) general anti-avoidance — sections 80A–80L allow SARS to disregard a 'tainted arrangement' lacking commercial substance, with consequences including a 100% additional tax penalty. For the dice-manufacturing (Pty) Ltd, substance is demonstrated through on-the-ground manufacturing operations (moulding, curing, finishing), local employees, inventory holding in SA, and board decision-making in SA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 10% to 150% of the shortfall depending on conduct (Table 3, section 222, Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011); additional tax up to 200% of the understatement for intentional default (section 223 TAA); criminal conviction under section 235 TAA carries a fine and/or imprisonment not exceeding five years; under section 234 TAA, fraud in tax matters carries a penalty of up to five years' imprisonment; interest on all undercharged amounts at the prescribed rate.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed public officer are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes in respect of amounts required to be withheld or collected (PAYE, SDL, UIF, dividend withholding tax) under section 179 TAA if the failure to pay was due to the director's negligence or fault. Directors who knowingly sign false returns face criminal liability under section 235 TAA, civil restitution claims, disqualification under the Companies Act, and SARS may apply to the High Court under section 194 TAA for an order prohibiting the director from leaving South Africa where the outstanding tax debt is due and payable.
Lack of Economic Substance / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (sections 80A–80L ITA) and disregard or re-characterise the arrangement, requiring the transaction to be taxed as if it had not been entered into; a 100% additional tax may be imposed under section 80L; alternatively SARS may treat the entity as a controlled foreign company, attributing its income to SA shareholders at the 27% corporate rate; interest and penalties apply from the original due date; the company's tax clearance and eFiling profile may be suspended under sections 163 and 164 TAA.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implemented or continued a sham arrangement may be personally assessed as representatives under section 179 TAA; liable for civil damages for reckless trading under section 77 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008; subject to criminal prosecution for aiding a tax-avoidance scheme; and subject to exit restrictions (travel ban) under section 194 TAA pending payment of the reassessed liability.
Transfer Pricing and Withholding Tax Defaults

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment under section 31(2) re-determining the taxable income at arm's length; secondary adjustment under section 31(3) deeming an interest-bearing loan to the shareholder/connected person, triggering additional taxable interest; understatement penalties up to 150%; WHT arrear penalties and interest on unreported dividends/interest/royalties; criminal exposure for officers who intentionally failed to withhold under section 235 TAA.

Director personal liability: Directors of the withholding agent are personally and jointly liable for the unremitted dividend, interest, and royalty WHT under section 179 TAA; directors who approved the TP documentation or signed the return may face civil recoupment; deliberate disregard of SARS notices can lead to a section 194 departure-ban order and criminal indictment for incitement to tax offences under section 234 TAA.
AML / FICA / Payment-Rail Compliance

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) may yield administrative penalties (the FIC may impose fines of up to ZAR 10 million for repeated or severe non-compliance); money laundering under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998 is punishable by a fine and imprisonment of up to 30 years; failure to report a reportable transaction to the FIC carries further criminal exposure; SARS may also treat unreported funds as undeclared taxable income with a 200% additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly process or facilitate the laundering of proceeds through the company face personal criminal liability and imprisonment; directors are additionally liable under section 179 TAA for any tax underpaid as a consequence of unreported revenues; the Financial Intelligence Centre may apply to have the company and its directors interdicted from further business; travel bans under section 194 TAA may be sought by SARS where the tax arrears exceed the prescribed threshold.
