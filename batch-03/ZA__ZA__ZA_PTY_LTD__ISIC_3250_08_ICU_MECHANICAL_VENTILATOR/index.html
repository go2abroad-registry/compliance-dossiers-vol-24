Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); national eFiling portal at https://www.sars.gov.za and https://www.efiling.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A South African incorporated (Pty) Ltd must first be registered for income tax by appointing a public officer (bona fide South African resident) and submitting the SARS company registration particulars via eFiling or at a SARS branch; SARS issues a 10-digit income tax reference number. Simultaneously, VAT registration is obtained by filing the VAT 101 form through eFiling, requiring the MOI, registration certificate, board resolution appointing the public officer, proof of local registered address, South African bank account confirmation, and the foreign parent entity's tax identification number where applicable. Upon approval SARS issues a 10-digit VAT vendor number and the VAT 103 registration certificate. A non-resident shareholder or branch must appoint a local resident representative who becomes jointly and severally liable for the VAT obligations of the registered vendor. For the export of manufactured goods, the company must additionally register as a customs trader at the SARS Customs desk (exporter code 012) and may apply for Schedule 3 rebate registration or direct-export arrangements.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a locally incorporated (Pty) Ltd is a South African tax resident subject to normal corporate income tax on worldwide income at the standard rate of 27% (years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2023; 28% for earlier years). No pass-through status applies to a (Pty) Ltd; distributions are subject to Dividends Tax withheld at 20% (treaty-reduced where applicable). Territorial source-based taxation applies only to non-resident persons deriving South African-source income; no general tax-exempt zone exists in South Africa, although Special Economic Zones may confer reduced corporate rates and incentives to qualifying manufacturers. — A South African incorporated (Pty) Ltd must first be registered for income tax by appointing a public officer (bona fide South African resident) and submitting the SARS company registration particulars via eFiling or at a SARS branch; SARS issues a 10-digit income tax reference number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a locally incorporated (Pty) Ltd is a South African tax resident subject to normal corporate income tax on worldwide income at the standard rate of 27% (years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2023; 28% for earlier years). No pass-through status applies to a (Pty) Ltd; distributions are subject to Dividends Tax withheld at 20% (treaty-reduced where applicable). Territorial source-based taxation applies only to non-resident persons deriving South African-source income; no general tax-exempt zone exists in South Africa, although Special Economic Zones may confer reduced corporate rates and incentives to qualifying manufacturers.

Available election forms

South African law does not provide check-the-box entity classification elections. Statutory elections available to a qualifying manufacturing (Pty) Ltd include: (i) s12E election for small business corporation status with progressive rates where gross income does not exceed ZAR 20 million; (ii) s12C accelerated capital allowances for new manufacturing assets (40-20-20 profile or 50-30-20 for small business corporations); (iii) s12BA allowance for greenfield industrial projects; (iv) s12I industrial policy investment allowance on qualifying expansion; (v) s44 corporate-reorganisation rollover elections for asset-for-share transactions; (vi) VAT grouping election under s50 of the VAT Act for related companies; and (vii) s24J debt-issue elections. Hybrid mismatch ordering rules under s8F-s8L and s23A-s23N apply to cross-border financing structures and cannot be overridden by elective classification, and the controlled foreign company rules of s9D attribute certain passive income to resident shareholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration applies when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (s23(1) VAT Act); voluntary registration is available where taxable supplies exceeded ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12-month period (s23(3A) VAT Act). Foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services are subject to a matching ZAR 1,000,000 12-month threshold (s23(2A)), with mandatory registration required within 21 days after exceeding the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where (i) goods are supplied under an agreement of sale or sale on consignment; (ii) the supplier is the exporter of the goods; (iii) the goods are exported within 90 days after the date of supply (or within such longer period as the Commissioner may allow); and (iv) the supplier retains the prescribed documentary proof of export. Services directly rendered in respect of movable goods destined for export, including pre-shipment calibration and maintenance of ICU mechanical ventilators, qualify for zero-rating under s11(1)(k) where the supplier retains sufficient linkage evidence. Related freight, insurance, and international transport services are zero-rated under s11(1)(m). The zero-rated supply is reported in Box 2 of the VAT 201 return, and the exporter may claim an input tax refund on the related manufacturing costs in Box 1 subject to SARS verification.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (Form SAD500 series) submitted electronically via e-Customs and reflecting the registered exporter code
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch from a South African sea port or international airport to a destination outside South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Authorised Dealer foreign exchange funds-clarification record confirming receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency within the prescribed repatriation period
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and delivery or redelivery notes evidencing export within 90 days of the date of supply; where the 90-day period is exceeded, prior written approval of the SARS Commissioner is mandatory
  • Zero-rated tax invoice issued in the VAT 104 format, retained with the VAT record under s55 of the VAT Act for at least 5 years and produced on SARS audit demand

South Africa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or digital fiscal device approval for VAT invoices. A full VAT tax invoice must be issued within 21 days after the time of supply and contain the prescribed s20(4) VAT Act particulars, with the standardised SARS VAT 104 layout accepted for B2B transactions. SARS eFiling is the compulsory channel for submitting the VAT 201 return and for lodging customs export declarations via e-Customs (SAD500 series). SARS has introduced a voluntary e-invoicing interoperability framework for large enterprises and requires auditable electronic records in the audit file - South Africa (AF-SA) format upon request; no electronic clearance precondition applies to claiming export zero-rating.

Export zero-rating claims are audited by SARS Customs and the Large Business Centre against SAD500 departure logs, carrier manifests, and exchange-control records filed with the South African Reserve Bank. Where the export is not completed within 90 days, or the documentary chain is defective, SARS issues an additional assessment clawing back the full 15% VAT together with prescribed interest from the date of supply (s24(6) VAT Act). Under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, the understatement penalty ranges from 10% to 150% of the shortfall depending on conduct classification (s222-s223); intentional fraud attracts additional tax of up to double the shortfall and criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years (s234-s235 TAA). Directors and the appointed public officer face personal liability for VAT recovered but not remitted under s120(1)(b) VAT Act and s155 TAA. False export declarations additionally constitute customs offences under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964, exposing the exporter to penalties up to double the duty evaded, seizure of exported goods, and a jeopardy assessment with immediate asset attachment. The prescription period for SARS to raise a new or revised assessment is 5 years from the date of assessment of the original period, effective for periods commencing on or after 1 January 2023.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current-account trade and for all non-resident capital transactions; the convertibility restriction applies only to resident capital-account outflows. Current account: ADs may process import payments, service fees, royalties, commissions and income remittances without FinSurv approval, subject to FICA verification, the completion of the AD application form and supporting commercial documents. Capital account: non-resident injections by equity or shareholder loan are liberalised and require only AD notification, while resident outflows require AD-level approvals within statutory ceilings or FinSurv authorisation. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is freely permissible where (1) profits are evidenced by audited financial statements showing distributable reserves, (2) no loan avails or uncovered borrowings from the Common Monetary Area were used to fund the dividend, (3) the company holds a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN, (4) the AD receives a dividend declaration and indemnity, and (5) dividends tax (20%) is accounted for or reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty. For an ICU ventilator manufacturer with government receivables, profit remittance is routine upon completion of the trade file and audit trail, provided all export proceeds were repatriated within 180 days.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory regime: under the Financial Surveillance Regulations and the Currency and Exchanges Act, all export proceeds must be received into a South African AD bank account within 180 calendar days from the date of customs clearance/export. There is no mandatory conversion quota; the exporter may convert proceeds to ZAR at the market rate or retain the foreign currency in an authorised Foreign Currency Account (FCA), but full repatriation within the 180-day window is compulsory. Documentation: every shipment is declared to SARS Customs on the SAD500 export declaration with the applicable balance-of-payments goods code, and the AD closes the export file with the invoice, customs acknowledgement, transport documents and the inward SWIFT credit. Late receipt requires the AD to apply to FinSurv for approval with extenuating evidence; non-receipt triggers administrative penalties and may suspend FCA facilities. CMA countries (Lesotho, Namibia, Eswatini) are treated as domestic settlement flows and do not attract the 180-day rule.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Financial Surveillance Regulations, 2021, with day-to-day administration delegated to Authorised Dealers (ADs), being the licensed commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary treasury rail. SARB's regulatory perimeter requires cross-border current and capital payments to be cleared through AD banks; offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex or Currencycloud are not licensed as ADs and cannot discharge export-proceeds repatriation, balance-of-payments reporting or import-verification duties. A resident (Pty) Ltd holding unexplained offshore EMI balances would breach the Financial Surveillance Regulations unless the balance is a defined current-account receivable or an approved working-capital remittance under AD discretionary limits. Viable use: ancillary collection of low-value foreign e-commerce or tele-health fees, capped at de minimis amounts and swept periodically to the AD account; all government LC and tender proceeds must flow through tier-1 banks. Any material foreign-currency working capital held offshore must be structured through an approved Foreign Currency Account hosted by a South African AD.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be maintained where the aggregate value of cross-border connected-person transactions exceeds ZAR 5 million in a year of assessment. A Master File is required where the consolidated group revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion, and SARS must be able to access the documentation on request.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply a separate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but the economic substance doctrine is embedded in the Income Tax Act through the general anti-avoidance rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest-deduction restrictions, and the transfer-pricing arm's-length standard. A ventilator manufacturing (Pty) Ltd must demonstrate real production capacity, manufacturing staff, factory premises, procurement and inventory risk, and decision-making ability in South Africa. A shell intermediary inserted into the supply chain without substantive functions, assets, or risks is likely to be disregarded by SARS, with income re-attributed to the entity that actually performs the economic activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: SARS will make a primary transfer-pricing adjustment under section 31 of the Income Tax Act, reducing deductions or increasing gross income by the arm's-length shortfall. The adjustment attracts interest, a 10% late-payment penalty for unpaid tax, and an understatement penalty of up to 200% under the Tax Administration Act if the behavior is classified as intentional tax evasion or impermissible avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implemented or signed off on tax returns containing false transfer-pricing positions may face individual criminal liability under section 234 of the Tax Administration Act, with imprisonment of up to five years. Travel restrictions may be imposed as bail conditions once a criminal investigation or summons is pending.
Shell Company and Absence of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the transaction or recharacterize its substance under the general anti-avoidance rules of the Income Tax Act, allocate the taxable income to the entity that truly performs the manufacturing activity, disallow the intermediary's deductions, and impose understatement penalties of 100% to 200%.

Director personal liability: Directors of a company engaged in reckless trading or knowingly participating in a sham arrangement can be held personally liable for the company's tax and commercial debts under the Companies Act. The fraudulent or reckless use of a corporate entity may also result in criminal prosecution and imprisonment of up to 10 years.
Dividend Tax Evasion and Wrongful Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The company remains liable for the unpaid dividend tax plus interest and a 10% late-payment penalty. SARS may impose an understatement penalty of up to 200% and administrative penalties for failure to comply with record-keeping and declaration requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize a distribution without withholding tax can be jointly liable for the unpaid tax where the company cannot satisfy its obligations. Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act and the Companies Act is possible, with imprisonment, asset forfeiture, and travel restrictions as bail conditions.
AML, Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre may impose administrative fines of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention under the FIC Act. Tax liability may be increased through the anti-avoidance and transfer-pricing rules, and the underlying funds may be subject to freezing and forfeiture proceedings under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors of the accountable institution who knowingly or negligently permit laundering through the corporate structure face criminal liability, imprisonment of up to 15 years, and civil restoration claims. Travel bans and exit restrictions may be imposed by the court while criminal or forfeiture proceedings are pending.
