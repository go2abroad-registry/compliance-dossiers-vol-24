Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); online portal: SARS eFiling (www.sars.gov.za / secure.efiling.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity conducting a business in South Africa or having a place of effective management within South Africa must first obtain an income tax reference number from SARS. This requires submission of the CIPC (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission) registration certificate, memorandum of incorporation, list of directors and the appointed public officer, and proof of registered office and bank account details. The application is lodged through SARS eFiling after creating an eFiling profile. Following income tax registration, VAT registration is applied for using the VAT101 form (with accompanying supporting documents) either online or at a SARS branch, with the SARS vendor reference number (VAT number) issued once the entity meets the turnover threshold or alternatively applies voluntarily. For a non-resident supplier of imported or electronic services, SARS may register the supplier for VAT under the separate non-resident supplier regime.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a South African (Pty) Ltd is a separate legal taxpayer subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income at 27% (year of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023), with dividends subject to dividends withholding tax at shareholding level and no US-style pass-through status recognised. — A foreign-incorporated entity conducting a business in South Africa or having a place of effective management within South Africa must first obtain an income tax reference number from SARS. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a South African (Pty) Ltd is a separate legal taxpayer subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income at 27% (year of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023), with dividends subject to dividends withholding tax at shareholding level and no US-style pass-through status recognised.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under South African law. Statutory elections available include: (i) the participation exemption election in respect of foreign dividends and controlled foreign company (CFC) income under section 9D; (ii) headquarter company elections under sections 9I; (iii) certified emission reduction and innovation allowance elections under the Income Tax Act; (iv) hybrid mismatch rule elections under sections 23M to 23R (including designated instruments); and (v) VAT grouping election under section 50 of the VAT Act. Corporate restructuring roll-over relief, assessed-loss ring-fencing elections, and micro-business turnover elections are also available in specific circumstances.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration: ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies (actually received or invoiced) during a 12-month period; voluntary registration permitted from ZAR 50,000. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services (including digitised goods and services) are subject to a separate compulsory registration threshold of ZAR 50,000 in respect of supplies to South African recipients.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies where the supplier is a resident VAT vendor and the goods are physically exported from South Africa to a non-resident within the prescribed timeframes under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, or where services are supplied to a non-resident and performed outside South Africa, are physically supplied outside South Africa, or are directly connected to exported movable property, or constitute international transport services. The economic substance of the export must be evidenced by actual movement of goods or performance of services outside the Republic and payment in foreign currency. The zero-rating claim must be supported with retained documentary proof that the goods left South Africa and that the consideration was received in an amount in foreign currency within 180 days of the date of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) as required under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or road manifest proving physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-issued statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Copy of the agreement or contract with the non-resident customer
  • Certified evidence of payment and currency conversion, if applicable

South Africa does not presently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. SARS eFiling is the return submission portal, and SARS eInvoicing is an optional, standards-based electronic tax invoice exchange platform (Tax Invoice e-Invoice) designed to support automated reconciliation; it is not a clearance regime. Taxpayers must maintain complete, accurate records and ensure tax invoices contain all prescribed particulars under the VAT Act. Electronic tax invoices must retain their integrity, legibility, authentic origin, and be available for audit on demand; cryptography or equivalent controls are necessary if the integrity of the information cannot otherwise be demonstrated.

SARS conducts substantial audit scrutiny of export zero-rated claims. If export documentation is incomplete, fraudulent, or the foreign currency proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, SARS may reinstate the supply as standard-rated, issue an additional VAT assessment, and impose understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Understatement penalties for a substantial (less than 15%) or repeat understatement can reach up to 200% of the tax shortfall in the most serious case, plus interest on the outstanding amount. Intentional false statements and fraudulently obtained refunds expose the taxpayer to criminal prosecution, administrative monetary penalties, and potential VAT vendor deregistration, all without prejudice to exchange-control penalties for non-repatriation of export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is freely convertible on the current account for trade in goods and services, with no rationing or administrative quota. Non-resident capital account transfers are liberalised under SARB policy: inward direct foreign investment requires only a declaration through an Authorised Dealer and a SARS tax compliance certificate at the point of repatriation. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders may be made freely once a dividend is validly declared, provided the company has obtained a SARS dividend tax clearance, has filed any withholding tax obligations (20% final dividend tax, subject to treaty relief), and presents the underlying audited financial statements to the Authorised Dealer. There is no exchange control approval required merely for the payment of genuine dividends out of realised profits, but the disbursement must be effected through the regulated banking system in compliance with the Currencies and Exchanges Act. Proceeds of disposal of a local investment by a non-resident may be remitted back subject to proof that the investment was originally credited through an Authorised Dealer and that all South African taxes (CGT, dividend tax, interest) have been settled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SARB Exchange Control Manual and Circulars, an exporter must repatriate export proceeds of custom synthesised peptides into South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment (date of the Customs Bill of Entry / SAD500). Once credited, the proceeds must be offered for sale to an Authorised Dealer in ZAR within 30 days of receipt unless the exporter maintains a pre-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or a Specialised Foreign Currency Account with the Authorised Dealer, in which case the full 100% may be retained offshore/banking locally for future trade obligations. The de facto mandatory conversion ratio for proceeds received into a domestic ZAR account is 100% (net of any permitted banking fees); the authorised dealer applies the prevailing spot market rate. Exports of peptides require accurate commodity tariff classification and must not be perceived as a transfer of dual-use goods without proper supporting end-user and GMP documentation.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), the authorised Exchange Control Administrator under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as primary banking rails for a South African company. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, or Revolut are not authorised dealers in terms of the SARB exchange-control framework and are generally unable to offer ZAR domestic ACH clearing or to act as a local treasury account holder for compliance with South African exchange control. They may be used as auxiliary payment collection channels for invoices denominated in USD/EUR/GBP, but the funds must be settled through a local Authorised Dealer in order to satisfy the export proceeds repatriation timeline (180 days from shipment / 30 days from credit) and the Verification of Beneficial Ownership requirements under FICA. The bank-grade classification of the CMO laboratory as 'medium-risk enhanced due-diligence' reinforces that an offshore EMI cannot perform the file-and-recall confirmations and foreign currency surrender functions that SARB requires.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder capital contributions must be transferred by SWIFT wire (or MT103) into a dedicated capital escrow / share subscription account at an SARB Authorised Dealer in South Africa. The remittance instruction must carry the correct SARB Balance of Payments (BOP) purpose code for inward direct investment – generally '7 – Share capital, controlling branch / Disinvestment, as applicable', with the underlying narrative referencing the share allotment resolution and the subscriber's name. The Authorised Dealer requires the multilingual Notarial Fidelity (Form Q1) whichever is no longer mandatory for ordinary share issues, but requires a complete FATCA/CRS questionnaire, a CIPC certificate of incorporation, the resolution of the board issuing shares, and a SARS entity tax-compliance certificate. After receipt, the bank will verify that the wire matches the declared principal, will quarantine for source-of-funds checks (3-5 days), and will then credit the operating account; no further SARB approval is required for a bona fide inbound capital contribution, but the transaction must be recorded on the bank's BOP data system and the share issuance must be filed with CIPC within 30 days.Clearance for outbound trade settlements (payments for raw materials, lab consumables, and external peptide synthesis resins) must be executed through the SARB Authorised Dealer using the standard international payment instruction SWIFT MT103 for direct remittances. The authorised dealer requires the mandatory Form C/CD/IP/CC plus a SARS declaration and the corresponding trade documents: commercial invoice, packing list, carriage contract, and, for letters of credit, an irrevocable documentary credit opened by SWIFT MT700/MT707 with a conforming set of negotiable shipping documents. The company may also utilise an open-account settlement with its foreign suppliers by issuing an MT103 under the 'Goods' purpose code, supported by an import declaration (Form CC or the electronic duty approval). For recurring licensing and technical support fees, use the current-account purpose code 'Royalties / license fees' and submit the IP licence agreement, SARS tax clearance, and a transfer-pricing bench total (OECD ALP) memo. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A full statutory audit is mandatory where the Public Interest Score (PIS) is 500 or more; for all public companies and state-owned companies; or where the MOI or a regulator (e.g., pharmaceutical licensing authority for a licensed CDMO) requires it. PIS is scored annually per s.26 of the Companies Act (points for employees, third-party liabilities, turnover, shareholding spread, and subsidiary holdings). Private companies with PIS below 500 may instead obtain an independent review; owner-controlled companies with PIS below 350 may be exempt from both audit and review and use compilation-only statements.
