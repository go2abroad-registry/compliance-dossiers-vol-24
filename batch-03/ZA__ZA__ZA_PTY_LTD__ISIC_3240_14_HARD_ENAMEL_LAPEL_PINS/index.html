Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — National eFiling portal: www.sarsefiling.co.za (official SARS website: www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity commencing business in South Africa must first register as an external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain a CIPC registration number. Thereafter, the entity must register with SARS through the eFiling portal: create an eFiling profile, complete the income tax registration for an external company, and submit the CIPC certificate, memorandum of incorporation, proof of registered domestic address, and appointment of a South African resident representative. SARS issues an income tax reference number. VAT registration is additionally required under the Value-Added Tax Act where taxable supplies exceed the compulsory threshold; a non-resident vendor without a physical South African address must appoint a local resident agent for VAT purposes and file the prescribed SARS VAT registration form.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity commencing business in South Africa must first register as an external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain a CIPC registration number. Thereafter, the entity must register with SARS through the eFiling portal: create an eFiling profile, complete the income tax registration for an external company, and submit the CIPC certificate, memorandum of incorporation, proof of registered domestic address, and appointment of a South African resident representative. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows legal form and a private company ((Pty) Ltd) is automatically taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. No hybrid mismatch 'check-the-box' election is available; the hybrid mismatch rules under sections 25H and 25I operate as mandatory adjustments. Election-based mechanisms that do exist include: (i) Small Business Corporation relief under section 12E where all shareholders are natural persons and annual gross income does not exceed ZAR 20 million; and (ii) Micro Business Turnover Tax under the Sixth Schedule for qualifying businesses with annual turnover not exceeding ZAR 1 million.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period for compulsory registration; voluntary registration is permitted where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may be zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the supplier supplies goods by sale or consignment to a non-resident recipient and the goods are physically exported from South Africa to outside the country within the prescribed period. Services may be zero-rated under section 11(2) where the services are deemed to be supplied outside South Africa, including services supplied directly to non-residents where the services are not performed in South Africa, subject to enumerated statutory exceptions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SARS SAD500 form) reflecting actual date of export
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing consignment from South Africa to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in South Africa
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Transport and logistics evidence such as carrier manifests or cross-border road export permits where applicable

South Africa has not yet introduced a mandatory real-time electronic invoicing and fiscal clearance mandate. SARS permits paper and electronic tax invoices that comply with section 20 of the VAT Act and requires VAT returns to be filed through the eFiling portal. SARS has issued an e-invoicing framework for future standardisation, but no API clearance, real-time digital stamping, or mandatory government clearance of invoices is currently legislated.

SARS verifies zero-rated export claims through matching of SAD500 declarations, bank remittance flows, and transport documents. If a zero-rating claim is fraudulent or supporting proof is not retained, SARS will retrospectively claw back the zero-rating benefit and assess VAT at the standard 15% rate. Additional tax of up to 200% of the assessed tax may be imposed under section 223 of the Tax Administration Act, together with interest under section 167, administrative non-compliance penalties, and criminal prosecution for fraud under section 234, which may lead to fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions (merchandise exports, services, dividends, interest, royalties) without prior SARB approval, provided the cross-border flow is routed through a locally licensed Authorized Dealer with a valid exchange-control purpose code. Capital-account convertibility is materially liberalized for non-residents but retains a residency-based control layer: share capital, shareholder loans, profits, and liquidation proceeds may be repatriated upon submission of board resolutions, SARB/FinSurv notification forms, a SARS tax-clearance certificate (TCC) or SARS/Customs confirmation, and company financial records. Practical profit-remittance hurdles for ZA_PTY_LTD include (1) SARS matched-payment verification requiring the underlying dividend or profit stream to be declared and taxed, (2) audited financial statements proving distributable reserves, (3) scrutiny of a loop structure where the ultimate beneficial ownership resides offshore, and (4) the requirement that Etsy Direct Pay and Stripe marketplace proceeds first satisfy the 180-day export-repatriation rule before being transformed into distributable reserves.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 8 and FinSurv Circulars, 100% of export proceeds for merchandise (ISIC 3240 hard enamel lapel pins) must be received into South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment. There is no undifferentiated surrender-to-ZAR conversion ratio currently in force; the proceeds may be retained in an SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with a local Authorized Dealer, but the full gross invoice value must be repatriated onshore to the named exporter account no later than day 180, and any deduction from invoice value (agent commissions, trade discounts, warranty reserves) is limited to 5% without advance FinSurv approval. Digital marketplace sales (Etsy Direct Pay, Stripe) must be reconciled to the corresponding SARS SAD500 customs export declarations; where a marketplace aggregates receivables, a lawful collection-agency or customer-commissioner mandate must exist for the proceeds to qualify as direct export receipts under the repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administering exchange control under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (Government Notice R.1111, as amended), with operational delegation to Authorized Dealers (tier-1 commercial banks) and Authorized Dealers in foreign exchange with limited authority.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder equity injection: a single SWIFT MT103 from the originating foreign bank into the company's ZAR operational account or designated Capital Escrow account at the local Authorized Dealer, using exchange-control purpose code 350/351 (non-resident investment in a South African enterprise) and the narrative 'ZA_PTY_LTD share subscription'. The bank requires a board resolution, Companies Act Section 40/41 allotment documentation, the Form A / CFO declaration for non-resident participation, and SARS notification where the consideration exceeds reporting thresholds. Shareholder loans from non-residents require prior written approval under Exchange Control Regulation 8 where the facility exceeds ZAR 3 million per lender, a written arm's-length facility agreement, and registration on the Authorized Dealer loan register; amounts above the threshold require FinSurv consent. Their lawful utility for ZA_PTY_LTD is limited to acting as foreign-currency collection aggregators for Etsy/Stripe payouts, subject to a documented collection-agent mandate, followed by a monthly SWIFT MT103 sweep to the company's local tier-1 FCA or ZAR operational account and onward conversion through the Authorized Dealer. Without a contracted agency or customer-commissioner role, the local bank will red-flag third-party funding and potential exchange-control circumvention; using an EMI as an intermediate wallet also elevates FICA risk. The cleanest structural choice is direct Etsy Direct Pay and Stripe settlement into the local FCA, with the EMI used only where the marketplace cannot route to South Africa.
