VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of annual taxable supplies in Samoa

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of exported tangible goods and supplies of services to non-residents (or to a business carried on outside Samoa) are zero-rated if the direct benefit occurs overseas and the supplier retains documentary proof; an International Company may claim 0% VAGST only if it has registered voluntarily for VAGST and has an approved tax-exemption status for offshore transactions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDEC)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of offshore payment settlement
  • Samoa International Company tax exemption certificate

Samoa mandates electronic filing and payment through the Revenue Information Management System (RIMS); VAGST returns and tax clearance are submitted electronically, and registered businesses must use approved cash registers/invoicing systems that generate real-time fiscal audit records. No standalone national e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping platform is operative.

VAGST zero-rating claims are audited by the Ministry for Revenue with revocation of the export certificate, reassessment for full VAGST plus 50% penalty and late-payment interest, and criminal prosecution (fines up to WST 20,000 and imprisonment) for fraudulent export documentation or unrepatriated sale proceeds within the deadline.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates a managed float anchored to a NZD/AUD/USD basket; current-account payments for goods and services are convertible through licensed dealer banks as CBS delegates, whereas capital-account transactions (portfolio transfers, offshore lending, and resident dividend remittances) require prior CBS approval unless the transactor is expressly exempt. A Samoan International Company incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) holds non-resident status and is expressly exempt from the Exchange Control Act 1976; it therefore enjoys unrestricted current- and capital-account convertibility, may retain USD/EUR subscription revenue offshore indefinitely, and may remit dividends or repay shareholder debt without CBS licensing, provided it conducts no business with Samoan residents and executes no WST-denominated resident transactions. The practical friction point is bank-side: any Samoan licensed bank will still require an enhanced due-diligence file, a non-resident declaration, and a source-of-funds questionnaire before honouring conversion or outbound transfer instructions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters are required to repatriate export proceeds to a licensed Samoan bank within the period fixed by CBS notice (customarily 30 days from receipt) and to convert a prescribed ratio into WST (CBS practice historically up to 50%), with certification on CBS-prescribed forms and waiver or extension available on application. The subject International Company is carved out: it is non-resident, it exports invisible digital SaaS analytics services (ISIC 6201), and it is exempt from the Exchange Control Act 1976; accordingly, no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation certificate applies, and all Stripe/Paddle settlement revenue may be held offshore in USD or EUR without Samoan conversion.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Apia, established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; sole monetary authority with licensing and supervisory power over all Samoan domestic commercial banks, and the statutory administrator of the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Exchange Control Regulations (as amended), and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007; the CBS also hosts the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit for AML/CFT supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility for non-Samoan digital EMIs (Mercury, Relay, Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer) as the primary operating treasury where the IC presents a licensed-agent incumbency certificate, discloses the true beneficial owner, and evidences an operational MarTech SaaS footprint; Medium feasibility for EMI-provided treasury functions such as lending and structured FX; Low feasibility for any WST-denominated service because foreign EMIs hold no Samoan licence. Legal constraints: (i) Samoa IC statute preserves shareholder confidentiality and authorises nominee structures, but US OCC/Fed, UK FCA, and EU EBA KYC rules require transparent UBO disclosure, a source-of-funds narrative, and proof of substance (domain ownership, cloud billing, subscriber data, payroll); (ii) a zero-tax Pacific IC with dormant accounts is commonly declined, so the underlying Stripe/Paddle revenue trail must be documentary; (iii) foreign EMIs are neither registered nor supervised by the CBS, so only their home-jurisdiction licences govern settlement; (iv) the IC redomiciliation right under Samoan law is viewed favourably by EMIs for continuity risk. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (International Company full income tax exemption); Samoa domestic corporate income tax headline rate: 27%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for International Company distributions (exempt under the International Companies Act 1987, s.193). Samoa's general non-resident dividend withholding tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2007 is 15% for dividends paid by taxable domestic resident companies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — Samoa imposes no statutory Local File or Master File filing thresholds. The arm's length principle applies under the Income Tax Act 2007 to taxable resident companies, but the tax-exempt IC has no income tax transfer pricing filing duty. For ESR purposes, an IP-rich IC must retain contemporaneous evidence of intercompany pricing for SaaS/IP royalties and service fees, including contracts, comparability analysis, cost allocations, headcount, and expenditure in Samoa. Where Stripe or Paddle act as merchant of record or payment facilitator, the fees paid to them and the residual SaaS/IP revenue allocated to the IC must be documented as arm's length.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019, in force since 1 January 2020, require every IC undertaking a relevant activity — including Intellectual Property Business and Distribution and Service Centre Business — to demonstrate in Samoa: (a) core income-generating activities performed by qualified full-time employees; (b) adequate physical premises; (c) adequate operating expenditure in Samoa; and (d) board meetings and strategic management conducted in Samoa. A pure equity holding IC must maintain employees and premises adequate for holding the equity interest. The SEO content analytics SaaS tool constitutes an IP asset; income from its licensing, subscriptions, and analytics must be justified by economic substance in Samoa, failing which the IC will be deemed non-compliant and reported to international authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to WST 500,000 for failing the economic substance test; penalty up to WST 100,000 for failure to file the ESN; strike-off and dissolution by SIFA; revocation of the 20-year tax exemption; automatic exchange of information reporting to foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be fined up to WST 100,000, disqualified from acting as directors for up to 5 years, and, for fraudulent declarations, imprisoned up to 5 years; civil restitution may be ordered against directors who benefited from the non-compliant structure.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to WST 1,000,000; forfeiture and freezing orders under the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit framework; criminal money laundering conviction carries imprisonment up to 14 years; the IC may be struck off and its assets vested in the Government of Samoa.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved in money laundering face imprisonment up to 14 years, personal criminal fines, travel bans pending investigation, and confiscation of personal assets; directors may also be barred from holding office in any Samoa company.
Tax Evasion / Breach of Tax-Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the exemption with back assessment of income tax at 27%, VAGST at 15%, and interest; evasion penalties up to 150% of the tax shortfall; criminal fine up to WST 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false declarations or knowingly benefited from the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the assessed taxes and penalties; convicted directors face imprisonment up to 5 years and are disqualified from managing companies in Samoa.
Annual Fee Default / Administrative Strike-Off

Statutory sanction: Late penalty of WST 500; strike-off and dissolution after 90 days of default; assets of the struck-off IC automatically vest in the Government of Samoa; restoration requires arrears, reinstatement fees, and, if more than 12 months have elapsed, a court order.

Director personal liability: Upon strike-off, the corporate veil is pierced: directors and shareholders are personally liable for debts incurred after dissolution; transacting in the dissolved company's name is a criminal offense punishable by fine and imprisonment up to 2 years.
