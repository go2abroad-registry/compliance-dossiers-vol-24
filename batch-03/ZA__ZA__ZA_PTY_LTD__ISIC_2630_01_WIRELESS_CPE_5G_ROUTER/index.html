Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; SARS Customs and Excise also administers customs declarations, tariff classification and export documentation.

E-government filing portal

For a South African-registered Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd: (1) incorporate through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and obtain the company registration number and Memorandum of Incorporation; (2) register for income tax on SARS eFiling using the CIPC particulars and supporting corporate documents, obtaining a SARS income tax reference number; (3) apply separately for VAT registration as a vendor if the value of taxable supplies exceeds the statutory turnover threshold, or voluntarily if conducting an enterprise, obtaining a SARS VAT vendor number; (4) where goods are exported, register with SARS Customs for a customs code and file export declarations. Non-resident companies carrying on an enterprise or deriving South African-source income must similarly register through a SARS-recognized representative, agent or tax practitioner; registration is required before VAT zero-rating or input tax claims can be made.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African (Pty) Ltd is a resident company taxed on its worldwide income at entity level. Non-resident companies are subject to tax only on South African-source income attributable to a South African trade or a fixed place of business; resident shareholders may also be subject to controlled foreign company attribution rules. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African (Pty) Ltd is a resident company taxed on its worldwide income at entity level. Non-resident companies are subject to tax only on South African-source income attributable to a South African trade or a fixed place of business; resident shareholders may also be subject to controlled foreign company attribution rules. No pass-through treatment applies.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. Available statutory elections and mechanisms include: VAT group registration for connected companies under section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act; full group income-tax consolidation for a qualifying parent and 70%+ held subsidiaries under section 14 of the Income Tax Act; the headquarter-company regime under section 9I for qualifying non-resident-owned South African companies, which grants certain preferential source and exemption outcomes; and the application of controlled foreign company high-tax exemption based on foreign tax rate tests. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory and not elective; sections 8F, 8FA and related provisions recharacterize hybrid instruments and require resident taxpayers to align deductions with inclusion amounts.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration arises when taxable supplies (including zero-rated exports) exceed R1 million in any 12 consecutive months. Foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African recipients are subject to a R50,000 annual remuneration threshold for compulsory registration. Voluntary VAT registration is available below the threshold where a person carries on an enterprise, subject to SARS approval and any security or bonding requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is available on the supply of goods physically exported from South Africa to a foreign destination where the supplier is a VAT vendor, the sale is made under an agreement requiring export, the goods are removed from South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply (or such extended period as SARS may allow), no local consumption occurs, and contemporaneous proof of export is retained and produced to SARS. Export-related services may be zero-rated where the services are physically rendered outside South Africa, are supplied to a non-resident in connection with exported goods, or are supplied under a foreign contract meeting the statutory tests. Zero-rating is not automatic; the vendor must substantiate each claim before an input VAT refund is paid.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD500) lodged with SARS Customs and Excise
  • Bill of lading for sea freight, air waybill for airfreight, or road consignment manifest for cross-border transport
  • Original tax invoice or commercial invoice identifying the foreign purchaser and describing the exported wireless CPE equipment
  • Inward remittance certificate (IRC) issued by an authorized South African dealer, supported by SWIFT/TT wire transfer records evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Packing list, container tracking data or other contemporaneous logistics documentation proving physical removal from South Africa

South Africa has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, state-run e-invoicing API authorization, continuous transaction control regime or digital stamping obligation. SARS permits electronic tax invoicing where the content, authenticity and integrity of the invoice are preserved. A valid VAT invoice must state the vendor's name, address and VAT number; the invoice date; a description and quantity of goods or services; the consideration; the amount of VAT; and, for zero-rated exports, the prescribed export notation. VAT returns, customs declarations (SAD500) and supporting records are filed through SARS eFiling; SARS uses risk profiling, data matching and eAUDIT procedures to verify transactions but does not require pre-approval of ordinary B2B invoices.

SARS subjects export zero-rating claims to eAUDIT, customs risk profiling and VAT refund verification. If mandatory export documents are absent, false, or insufficient, the zero-rated supply is recharacterized as taxable at 15%, and the output VAT is clawed back with interest from the original return date. The Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 permits an understatement penalty of up to 100% of the tax shortfall, and criminal liability may arise for fraudulent export declarations or false customs documentation. The Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 further provides for fines, seizure and forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment for directors or persons responsible for false declarations. Repeat non-compliance may result in SARS declaring the entity seriously non-compliant and blocking VAT refunds or the renewal of customs and VAT registrations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is fully convertible for current account transactions including merchandise trade, services, interest, and dividends, subject to documentary oversight by authorised dealers. Capital account transactions remain regulated: inward foreign investment into a South African resident company is generally permitted through an authorised dealer without pre-approval, while outward transfers of capital, shareholder loans, and guarantees may trigger FinSurv declaration or approval thresholds. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to compliance with the South African Exchange Control Regulations, the Dividends Tax regime, and proof of underlying commercial substance; a non-resident shareholder may freely receive dividends through the SARB authorised banking channel after SARS tax clearance, with withholding tax applied at 20% or a reduced treaty rate where a Double Taxation Agreement applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers, all export proceeds from the physical export of goods must be received in South Africa within 180 days of the date of shipment, unless FinSurv approval for an extended period or retention in an external/foreign currency account is granted. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero: there is no statutory requirement to convert export receipts into ZAR, but the full value of the exported goods must be repatriated, properly recorded, and documented. Shipping documents, customs declarations, and bank inward payment advice must be retained as an audit trail; failure to repatriate on time is a criminal offence under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) – Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs as primary banking rails. Offshore electronic money institutions cannot clear ZAR payments or hold SARB-regulated corporate deposits without a South African banking licence served through an authorised dealer. A licensed local fintech may act as a payment gateway, but the underlying treasury and FX settlement must be executed through a tier-1 domestic bank; foreign EMIs may not, in practice, provide fully compliant export proceeds collection or supplier disbursement rails for a South African resident exporter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5 million aggregate per category of cross-border connected-party transactions (goods, services, IP, financing) requires disclosure on the ITR14 transfer-pricing schedule; a full OECD-aligned Local File/Master File documentation package must be kept and produced to SARS within 30 days of a request. CbCR notification and master-file obligations apply to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of ZAR 10 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no separate Economic Substance Regulations statute; however, economic substance is tested through (i) the place of effective management (POEM) residency rule, (ii) CFC attribution under section 9D, (iii) the headquarter company regime (s9I) requiring at least 50% non-resident group income, (iv) GAAR (s80A-s80L, s103), and (v) beneficial-ownership and principal-purpose conditions for DTA relief. A nominal shell distributor with no local staff, premises, or management functions is liable to POEM reclassification, transfer-pricing recharacterisation, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraud

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% of the shortfall for intentional tax evasion (TAA s223); criminal fine and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years for tax offences (TAA s234-s235); a period of 35 months of fixed monthly non-compliance penalties may run concurrently.

Director personal liability: A director who personally participates in or directs the fraud is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; SARS may attach personal assets, pursue civil restitution, impose travel bans in flagrant cases, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution, with imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell company / artificial substance arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the arrangement under GAAR or the POEM rules; denial of DTA relief; section 31 transfer-pricing adjustments with interest; understatement penalties of 25%-75%; cancellation of tax clearance and possible deregistration under the Companies Act 71 of 2008.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved dividends while the company was financially reckless face personal liability under s77 of the Companies Act; SARS can join directors to tax collection proceedings, obtain asset restraints, and prohibit departure from the Republic under the Tax Administration Act.
Transfer pricing mispricing

Statutory sanction: Section 31 primary adjustment (recomputing taxable income at arm's length), recalculated interest, and secondary recharacterisation of the excess as an imputed loan or deemed distribution; understatement penalties of 25% (substantial), 50% (failure to take reasonable care), 75% (gross negligence), or 150% (intentional evasion).

Director personal liability: The finance or tax director who signed false transfer-pricing certifications may be personally assessed for penalties and interest; directors can be held civilly liable for the company's resultant shortfall and criminal exposure exists for knowingly creating false documentation.
AML / illicit cross-border trade payments

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA) and the Prevention of Organised Crime Act: fines of up to ZAR 100 million or imprisonment up to 30 years for the most serious money laundering offences; FIC administrative penalties; transactional freeze orders and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and money-laundering reporting officers may be prosecuted personally and are subject to disqualification from acting as directors; personal travel bans and asset-freezing orders may be granted pending criminal proceedings; SARS will integrate tax evasion findings into the same prosecution.
