Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) via the eFiling portal (https://www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African Proprietary Limited company must first register as a taxpayer with SARS to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number. Registration is effected online through SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch using the company registration documents (Memorandum of Incorporation, Certificate of Incorporation, proof of registered office, and director identity documents). For VAT purposes, the company must separately complete a VAT 101 application and obtain a VAT registration number if taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed the compulsory threshold. SARS issues the same taxpayer reference number for income tax and VAT, although separate VAT registration is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned South African Proprietary Limited company must first register as a taxpayer with SARS to obtain an Income Tax Reference Number. Registration is effected online through SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch using the company registration documents (Memorandum of Incorporation, Certificate of Incorporation, proof of registered office, and director identity documents). For VAT purposes, the company must separately complete a VAT 101 application and obtain a VAT registration number if taxable supplies exceed or are likely to exceed the compulsory threshold. SARS issues the same taxpayer reference number for income tax and VAT, although separate VAT registration is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in South African law. A company is resident if incorporated in South Africa or if its place of effective management is in South Africa, with resulting worldwide taxation. Relevant statutory elections include the Headquarter Company regime (section 9I of the Income Tax Act), the election for a company to be treated as a small business corporation for reduced income tax rates, and the election to apply the VAT grouping rules under section 50 of the VAT Act for connected persons. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under section 23M may be applicable for cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration at R1,000,000 (one million South African Rand) of taxable supplies in a consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available at R50,000 annual taxable supplies for qualifying vendors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of movable goods supplied to a non-resident customer and exported from South Africa within a prescribed period, provided the vendor retains documentary proof of export and the supplier is responsible for the export. Zero-rating also applies to certain services supplied directly to non-residents under section 11(2)(k) of the VAT Act, including the transfer of intellectual property, rights, or information for use outside South Africa, where the services are not supplied to a person present in South Africa. Export zero-rating is not automatic; the vendor must satisfy SARS that the goods or services are exported and supplied to a non-resident.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) or electronic export declaration via SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign exchange receipt evidencing payment in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice / tax invoice issued under VAT Act section 20
  • Proof of export within the prescribed time period, including transport documentation and port/customs clearance stamps

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or government digital stamping. VAT vendors must issue electronic or paper tax invoices containing the prescribed particulars under section 20 of the VAT Act. SARS accepts eFiling for VAT returns and maintains an electronic declaration platform. No third-party API clearance or fiscal data lock is required before invoice issuance.

SARS applies rigorous audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims, requiring full documentary substantiation. Penalties for incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating include an additional tax of 200% of the tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, administrative understatement penalties ranging from 10% to 42.5%, interest on outstanding VAT, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion or fraud. Retrospective clawbacks may be raised where goods are not physically exported or documentary proof is deficient, with VAT assessed at the standard 15% rate plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand is largely convertible on current account, subject to reporting by authorised dealers, while capital account outflows by residents remain exchange-control restricted. Non-residents may repatriate dividends, branch profits, loan repayments and capital proceeds through an authorised dealer, provided the original inward transfer was correctly designated, applicable South African taxes have been settled, and a SARS Tax Compliance Status Pin has been obtained. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily documentary rather than quantitative: an audited proof of available distributable reserves, withholding tax accounting for dividends, and FinSurv reporting requirements apply. Inbound direct investment into a private company generally does not require prior SARB exchange control approval if effected through a registered bank under the inward listing reporting framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South African resident exporters must repatriate export proceeds into South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment unless an extended period has been approved by FinSurv. There is currently no fixed mandatory conversion ratio into rand; after repatriation, export proceeds may be credited to an approved foreign-currency account or converted into ZAR at the market rate. In practice, absent an approved foreign-currency account facility, the full export amount is credited and settled in rand. Exporters must retain supporting invoices, bills of lading, delivery notes and customs clearance evidence to demonstrate the inward payment matches booked export value. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline or to account for the proceeds triggers exchange-control penalties and possible audit intervention by FinSurv.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 and the Currency and Exchanges Act 9, 1933

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a substitute for a regulated South African bank account. Non-resident electronic money institutions and offshore digital EMIs are not licensed banks under the Banks Act 1990 and may not take ZAR deposits or hold settlement balances on behalf of the company. They may, however, be used as inbound payment aggregators or white-label payout channels if they partner with a licensed South African Payment Service Provider and comply with the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998. The primary treasury relationship should therefore remain with a SARB-authorised commercial bank; a non-resident EMI is permissible only for the so-called Platform_Pay collection layer, not for statutory liquidity or capital holdership.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward non-resident capital contributions for ZA_PTY_LTD must be routed through a SARB-authorised dealer via SWIFT MT103, using the receiving bank's prescribed Balance of Payments purpose code for direct investment equity capital. The receiving bank will verify the share allotment resolution, updated register of members, CIPC filing or intended filing, proof of source of funds, and the non-resident status of the investor. The authorised dealer then registers the inward transfer and issues an exchange-control or inward-listing reference for the transaction; this reference must be retained as the definitive compliance evidence for future dividend, interest, or capital repatriation. Shareholder loans must be supported by a loan agreement and are similarly recorded under the interchangeability? no, under the applicable FinSurv reporting category for non-resident accommodation.
