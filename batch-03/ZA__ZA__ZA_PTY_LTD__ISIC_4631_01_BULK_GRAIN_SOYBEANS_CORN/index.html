E-government filing portal

A foreign investor must first incorporate a (Pty) Ltd with CIPC (Business Registration: CoR 14.1; company number pattern YYYY/XXXXXX/07); CIPC automatically transmits company data to SARS, which issues the corporate Income Tax Reference Number within 2-5 business days. For VAT, the company registers via SARS eFiling on Form VAT101 within 21 days of exceeding the ZAR 1,000,000 annual taxable-turnover threshold; VAT ID is issued instantly on approval. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT under VAT Act s11(1)(a) where movable goods are supplied to a non-resident under a contract of sale/movement and are exported within 90 days (or extended period allowed by SARS); proof of export is mandatory. Services: 0% VAT under s11(2)(k) for services supplied to a non-resident where the services are not physically rendered in SA to an individual present in SA and are not directly related to land or goods situated in SA; vessel brokerage for offshore grain (soybeans/corn) consignments qualifies if the benefit of the service is enjoyed outside SA. Alternative: s11(2)(l) for services physically performed outside SA. The supply of export transportation/freight services may also qualify under s11(1)(b). Place-of-supply rules in s9(2) must be documented contemporaneously.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is freely convertible on the current account (trade, services, remittances) subject to documentary substantiation through an Authorised Dealer; capital account transactions are liberalised with FinSurv approval or verified reporting through the Authorised Dealer network. Non-resident equity investments below R10 million per calendar year can generally proceed via Form C and Funds Check reporting without prior FinSurv approval, while inward loan facilities above that amount or debt:equity gearing in excess of 3:1 require prior approval. Profit remittance (dividends) to non-resident shareholders is permitted without exchange-control restriction if the dividend is declared out of genuine audited profits, SARS tax compliance is evidenced, and the bank's e-FX / Form C declaration is completed truthfully; remittances out of artificially engineered reserves or in breach of the debt:equity guideline may be blocked. Operational convertibility is therefore a function of bank compliance and documentary integrity rather than statutory restriction, and grain-trade receipts (soybeans, yellow corn) in USD can be converted to ZAR immediately or retained in an approved foreign currency account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for bulk soybeans and yellow corn must be received in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment pursuant to Exchange Control Regulation 8. There is no mandatory ZAR conversion ratio: the exporter may credit the foreign currency to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer, but balances not applied to approved commercial purposes within the permitted holding period must be sold to an Authorised Dealer for ZAR. Each export shipment requires the completion of the electronic Form B (Exchange Control Export Declaration) together with the customs Single Administrative Document (SAD), commercial invoice, bill of lading, and proof of discharge/acceptance; the bank forwards the data to SARB FinSurv. Failure to repatriate within 180 days exposes the company to administrative penalties, cancellation of Authorised Dealer facilities, and criminal liability under the Currency and Exchanges Act.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1961 (as amended); regulated commercial banks operate as Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Corpay) are not viable as primary or standalone cross-border treasury rails for a South African Pty Ltd because SARB's Exchange Control Regulations permit foreign exchange and cross-border settlement only through locally registered Authorised Dealers (banks) connected to SAMOS (the national large-value settlement system) and the correspondent banking network. Offshore EMIs do not hold Authorised Dealer licences, cannot open ZAR nostro accounts, and cannot clear local ACH or payment streams for corporate accounts; they may be used only as auxiliary instruction-origination channels that ultimately settle through a tier-1 South African bank. Domestic neobanks and e-money institutions (TymeBank, Bank Zero) are viable for local ZAR liquidity but rely on partner banks for foreign exchange and cross-border clearing. Overall feasibility rating: LOW for standalone use; MEDIUM as a supplementary payment-origination rail once the tier-1 bank relationship is established.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inward FDI through the SARB authorised-dealer banking channel: (1) pre-open a Capital Escrow / Non-Resident Share Account at the tier-1 bank and file the electronic Form C via the bank's e-FX platform; (2) deliver the shareholder's funds by SWIFT MT103 wire with narration referencing 'Share capital subscription / equity injection' and the applicable ZA BoP purpose code (direct investment - equity, code 3300 series) or the bank's inward-remittance indicator for Foreign Direct Investment; (3) for cumulative calendar-year equity injections above R10 million, the bank must complete a Funds Check / inward transfer notice to SARB FinSurv before crediting the capital suspense account; (4) following receipt, allot shares in accordance with the Companies Act 71 of 2008, file the CSO share return with CIPC, and deliver to the bank the board resolution, share premium schedule (where applicable) and non-resident declaration within 21 days; (5) if any portion of the remittance cannot be capitalised, retain it in a due-to-non-resident creditor account under FinSurv thresholds and remit the surplus back through Form C / FC; shareholder loans in excess of the 3:1 debt:equity gearing limit require prior FinSurv approval, and all interest or fee remittances must be supported by SARS NTR03 tax certificates.Use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for USD/ZAR settlement of grain purchases, together with irrevocable and confirmed documentary letters of credit (UCP 600 / ISBP 745) for sight or usance payment against presentation of original multimodal or ocean bills of lading, GAFTA/FOSFA quality arbitration clauses, PPECB export certificates, insurance certificates and commercial invoices. For lower-risk SACU buyers, documentary collections (URC 522) or cash-against-documents (CAD) are acceptable substitutes that reduce LC costs. Domestic ZAR purchases from local silos settle via SAMOS (national large-value clearing) and the Automated Clearing Bureau (ACB) for bulk electronic payments. All outward remittances require the bank's e-FX declaration (Form A for import payments), positive SARS tax-clearance verification, sanctions screening of counterparties, vessels and ports, and, where applicable, the relevant import permit from DAFF/MAFISA for genetically-modified soy or corn. USD clearing through correspondent banks in New York (CHIPS/Fedwire) is facilitated by the Authorised Dealer's nostro arrangement under SARB authorisation.Forward Exchange Contract (FEC) with a SARB Authorised Dealer covering firm and reasonably anticipated export receipts and import payments of USD/EUR for bulk grain trades, tenor up to 24 months (note: onshore NDF is not permitted; FEC is the deliverable ZAR/USD instrument).Foreign currency option contracts (European or American puts and calls) and option combinations such as collars, participations and forward-plus strategies executed through the authorised dealer's dealing desk for hedging export and import price exposure.FX swaps and Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) executed with an Authorised Dealer bank to align ZAR/USD cash flows on trade finance facilities or shareholder loans; swaps with non-resident counterparties require FinSurv approval where not back-to-back hedges.USD/ZAR exchange-traded futures and options listed on the JSE (SAFEX/agri-currency derivatives), accessed through a licensed broker/clearing member and used strictly for hedging grain commodity price and currency risk with daily margin and cleared settlement.Composite hedging programmes combining FECs with commodity basis-risk instruments (e.g., CBoT vs SAFEX maize/soy spread swaps) through the authorised dealer or licensed commodity broking arm, subject to the prudent-hedge principle and prohibition on naked speculative positions.Rolling forward accounts and average-rate forward contracts for regular seasonal grain export flows where the company holds an active dealing mandate with the authorised dealer, enabling volumetric smoothing of FX volatility without speculative leverage.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Corpay) are not viable as primary or standalone cross-border treasury rails for a South African Pty Ltd because SARB's Exchange Control Regulations permit foreign exchange and cross-border settlement only through locally registered Authorised Dealers (banks) connected to SAMOS (the national large-value settlement system) and the correspondent banking network. Offshore EMIs do not hold Authorised Dealer licences, cannot open ZAR nostro accounts, and cannot clear local ACH or payment streams for corporate accounts; they may be used only as auxiliary instruction-origination channels that ultimately settle through a tier-1 South African bank. Domestic neobanks and e-money institutions (TymeBank, Bank Zero) are viable for local ZAR liquidity but rely on partner banks for foreign exchange and cross-border clearing. Overall feasibility rating: LOW for standalone use; MEDIUM as a supplementary payment-origination rail once the tier-1 bank relationship is established.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5,000,000 aggregate annual value of affected cross-border connected-person transactions; SARS may require documentation below this threshold where indicators of non-arm's-length pricing exist.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to low-tax jurisdictions, but substance is enforced through the s31 transfer-pricing rules, the CFC attribution rules in s9D, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in s80A-L. A grain broker holding physical office premises, local employees, warehouse contracts, chartering functions, and genuine risk management capacity in South Africa must evidence those functions; a passive commission-taker with no local decision-making authority cannot claim treaty residence benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under s223 of the Tax Administration Act, an understatement penalty of 100% applies for gross negligence and 200% for intentional tax evasion, plus compound interest. Criminal conviction under s235 TAA carries a fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years. SARS may obtain preservation orders and attach assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly prepare or cause to be filed a false return are personally liable for the tax shortfall under s234 TAA and may face civil restitution, travel bans, and extraterritorial asset seizures. Under the Companies Act, the corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as a facade to shield unlawful tax conduct.
Shell Company and Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the legal form under s80A-L GAAR, recharacterize the arrangement, deny DTA benefits, and impose a 200% understatement penalty under s223. Deemed adjustments may be treated as dividends subject to 20% dividends tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for company debts where the court finds reckless trading under s22 or s77 of the Companies Act. Penalties include disqualification from management and imprisonment in cases of fraud.
