Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

The foreign shareholder must first incorporate the South African (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), which issues a Registration Certificate and Enterprise Number. Thereafter the company must register with SARS via eFiling or a SARS branch to obtain (i) a Company Income Tax reference number, (ii) a VAT vendor number under section 23 of the VAT Act where taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed R1 million in any consecutive 12-month period, and (iii) PAYE and UIF registration if employing staff. Non-resident directors/shareholders must furnish certified copies of their passport, foreign incorporation documentation, and proof of residential address. A public officer resident in South Africa must be appointed and registered within one month of incorporation; all SARS correspondence and tax returns are issued against this registration profile.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A (Pty) Ltd is a resident legal person taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, subject to the current corporate rate of 27% for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2023; no pass-through election is available for a standard profit company. Available elections include: the section 11D research and development incentive election (approval-based additional allowance), the section 12E Small Business Corporation progressive-rate election (where all shares are held by natural persons and qualifying turnover thresholds are met), the section 14(1) VAT group registration election, and the section 20(1)(a) companies group taxation election for tax-neutral intra-group transfers. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under sections 8I–8K of the income tax Act are mandatory and not electable; where treaty relief or transfer pricing mutual agreement proceedings are pursued, formal elections under the applicable double tax agreement and SARS guidance are required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act applies to goods physically exported from South Africa (including export to SACU/SADC destinations) where the vendor dispatches or causes the dispatch of the goods through an authorised agent within the agreed terms, and retains acceptable proof of export. Instant rice noodles manufactured in South Africa and delivered to non-resident buyers outside South Africa qualify for the 0% rate; the exporter must simultaneously claim input tax credits on raw materials, packaging, and production inputs. Services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside South Africa may qualify under section 11(2)(l) where the services are not connected with movable or immovable property in South Africa. The zero-rating is denied if the goods are deemed by SARS to have been supplied for local consumption or if the prescribed export evidence is not retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 lodged through the SARS Customs/AES electronic system)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill / Consignment Note) evidencing physical export
  • Commercial Invoice covering full description, quantity, HS tariff classification, INCO terms and consideration paid
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 / bank credit advice confirming settlement to the South African bank account)
  • Proof of payment of freight and insurance (where applicable) applying the INCO rule used

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping of invoices. Electronic and paper tax invoices are both valid provided the invoice meets the content requirements of section 20 of the VAT Act (supplier name and address, vendor VAT number, invoice number, date, description and quantity of goods, price and VAT amount). SARS eFiling provides the electronic channel for VAT201 returns and supplementary data submissions. SARS has published a Draft Policy and Roadmap for mandatory e-invoicing aligned with the Peppol framework, which will phase in future API-based clearance; until such mandate is promulgated, no real-time API filing or digital signature is legally required.

SARS may assess additional VAT (15%), interest, and understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 if zero-rated exports are not substantiated: a substantial understatement penalty of 50% of the shortfall applies automatically, rising to 100% for intentional or grossly negligent conduct and up to 150% in case of fraud or repeated non-compliance. SARS retains a six-year audit window for non-disclosure and an unlimited window for fraud or deliberate misdeclaration. Where the 180-day foreign currency repatriation deadline is missed without prior SARS/Exchange Control approval, the export proceeds are subject to exchange control penalties, possible compulsory conversion liability, and the VAT zero-rating may be reversed. Directors and the public officer are jointly and severally liable for penalties and may face criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act and the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964, including imprisonment of up to five years and disqualification from VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest) for which banks have delegated authority. Capital-account transactions require explicit FinSurv approval or a standing dispensation. Repatriation of profits by a non-resident shareholder is permitted after SARS tax clearance (CS01) and certification that the company has no overdue local liabilities. Controls are being progressively liberalised under the 2021 Currency and Exchanges Guidelines for Authorised Dealers, but formal exchange-control approval remains necessary for inbound equity contributions above the general transactional limit and for any intra-group offshore treasury arrangement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment. Upon receipt, foreign-currency proceeds must be deposited with an authorised dealer and, unless an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or automatic retention dispensation applies, must be converted to ZAR within 30 days of receipt. The current automatic retention threshold is 30% of the export invoice value for trade finance purposes; higher retention or a long-term FCA requires FinSurv approval. Documentary proof of shipment and invoice value must be retained on Form DA550 and made available to the authorised dealer and SARS.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), together with the Prudential Authority for bank supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (Revolut, Wise, Airwallex) are not licensed authorised dealers in South Africa and cannot hold a ZAR corporate current account directly. They may be used only as supplementary FX intermediaries insofar as funds are cleated through a domestic FNB/Standard Bank account via SWIFT. Local fintech-connected neobanks (TymeBank, Bank Zero, Discovery Bank) provide transactional ZAR accounts but generally lack commercial trade finance capabilities such as LC issuance, import/export settlements and guarantee lines. Therefore, for a food manufacturer needing regular import of packaging and retort equipment, a tier-1 commercial bank is mandatory; EMIs are suitable only for low-value office expenses, not core treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Share Capital Contribution / Equity Participation' and a reference containing the unique beneficiary identifier 'ZA_PTY_LTD_SHARE_CAPITAL'. The receiving authorised dealer requires a FinSurv capital inflow application (Form FC/IC or the latest B.254) to be submitted before the funds land. The company must provide a signed board resolution, updated MOI, SARS tax clearance, and bank account verification letter. Where the inward remittance exceeds ZAR 10 million (or the approved headroom), FinSurv may issue a loan/commercial condition dispensation, therefore the bank must receive finSurv approval prior to crediting. The routed funds are non-yieldable and cannot be placed on an offshore term deposit without separate approval.For payments to overseas suppliers of retort meal packaging, spices, and processing equipment, the preferred rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 via the authorised dealer for remittances under USD or EUR, using transaction purpose code P1112 for food imports or P1002 for machinery, supported by original invoices; (2) irrevocable documentary letters of credit (LC) at sight or usance (60–120 days) for bulk raw materials and capital equipment, opened on the bank's issuance platform. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 must be filed by the last day of the 12th month after the company’s financial year-end. SARS may prescribe an earlier deadline by public notice, and failure to file by the due date may result in an estimated assessment being raised.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First IRP6 return and payment: within six months of the beginning of the year of assessment. Second IRP6 return and payment: by the last day of the year of assessment (i.e., financial year-end). The annual ITR14 filed within 12 months after year-end reconciles the provisional payments with the final assessed liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South African transfer-pricing rules require documentation to be retained and produced to SARS on request when controlled transactions exceed the SARS materiality thresholds: ZAR 5,000,000 for royalties, interest, service fees and similar charges; ZAR 10,000,000 for financial transactions (loans, guarantees, cash pooling); and ZAR 50,000,000 for other controlled transactions (including sale of goods). A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are mandatory for a South African constituent entity of a multinational enterprise group with consolidated group revenue equal to or exceeding ZAR 10,000,000,000. A Local File is required for any South African entity participating in controlled transactions above the materiality thresholds, with detailed economic analysis and comparability benchmarks.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to the UAE or Caribbean jurisdictions. Economic substance is instead enforced through: the place of effective management (POEM) test; controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution rules in Sections 9D of the Income Tax Act; transfer-pricing substance criteria under Section 31 (including the requirement that the structure embodies real commercial rationale); and the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in Section 103(1). A South African (Pty) Ltd manufacturing instant rice noodles is expected to demonstrate real production activity, fixed premises, manufacturing plant, inventory, employees with decision-making authority, and board meetings in South Africa, failing which the arrangement may be recharacterised, deductions denied, and treaties denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalties under Section 223 of the Tax Administration Act: 150% of the tax shortfall for intentional tax evasion and 200% for fraud; criminal conviction under Section 234 may result in a fine up to ZAR 500,000 or twice the prejudice to SARS and/or imprisonment up to 2 years per count; common-law fraud prosecution can attract substantially longer imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise or participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the company’s assessed tax under Section 159 of the TAA. SARS may obtain a civil restraint order, freeze personal bank accounts, impose a travel ban (via the High Court), and secure a criminal conviction resulting in disqualified directorship and imprisonment.
Reckless Trading and Unpaid SARS Debt

Statutory sanction: SARS may levy a 10% late-filing penalty and interest at 10.5% per annum, then institute liquidation. Under Section 77(3) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008, a director is liable for any loss arising from reckless trading; SARS may also apply Section 159 of the TAA to hold directors personally liable for the full company tax debt.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to civil restitution of the diverted funds, disqualification from managing companies for up to 10 years, and a formal travel ban preventing departure from South Africa unless SARS issues a tax-clearance exit certificate. In egregious cases, criminal prosecution for reckless trading or fraud leads to imprisonment and confiscation orders.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Under-Export Invoicing

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust gross income and taxable income under Section 31 of the Income Tax Act to reflect an arm’s-length price; secondary adjustments (deemed dividends or loans) may be imposed; understatement penalties of 75% for impermissible avoidance arrangements, 150% for intentional evasion, plus interest and potential CFC inclusion in the South African shareholder’s income.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved a non-arm’s-length transaction with knowledge of the financial prejudice to the company are personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall and penalty; SARS may pierce the corporate veil to recover from personal assets, and serious cases trigger a travel ban and criminal fraud indictment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard the corporate existence as a sham, reassess the beneficial owner, deny treaty relief, and impose the maximum 200% fraud penalty; the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission may deregister the company and disqualify directors; bank account restrictions and freezing orders can be applied by SARS.

Director personal liability: Directors face joint liability for the entire understated tax plus interest, civil restitution of distributed profits, automatic disqualification under the Companies Act, travel bans, and imprisonment for participation in a fraudulent scheme. The courts will lift the corporate veil where the company is used as a mere facade.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FIC Act) penalties: administrative fines up to ZAR 10,000,000; criminal penalties of imprisonment up to 15 years for natural persons involved in money laundering; SARS may also treat undeclared cash flows as unreported income with a 200% penalty and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the FIC Act fine if they failed to take reasonable steps to ensure compliance; personal foreign bank accounts may be frozen; a travel ban may be issued while the investigation is pending; conviction for money-laundering carries imprisonment and mandatory restitution of the laundered amounts.
