Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Samoa qualify for a 0% VAGST rate where the supplier establishes that the goods have physically left Samoa pursuant to a contract of sale, lease, or hire-purchase with a person who is outside Samoa at the time of supply. Certain services provided to a non-resident and enjoyed outside Samoa may be zero-rated. An International Company carrying on offshore business for non-residents is exempt from VAGST and income tax under the International Companies Act, thereby falling outside the domestic taxable supply regime.

  • Samoa Customs export declaration (approved and stamped)
  • Bill of lading or airway bill
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank transfer evidence
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export contract and foreign buyer documentation

No mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is currently in force. Registered persons are required to issue a valid VAGST invoice in paper or electronic form and to retain all sales, purchase, and export records for SRA audit.

Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to SRA audit scrutiny, retrospective clawback of any improperly allowed input tax credits, and payment of the full amount of unded VAGST. Penalties may include a civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded, late payment interest and penalties, and, for deliberate falsification of export documentation, criminal fines and imprisonment under the VAGST Act and the Tax Administration Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa's currency (Samoan Tālā, WST) is not freely convertible. Resident current-account transactions require documentary support through an authorized dealer; capital-account movements require CBS approval. An International Company (IC) registered under the International Companies Act 1987 is treated as non-resident for exchange control purposes: it may hold and operate foreign-currency accounts, switch currencies freely offshore, and remit profits/dividends without CBS approval. Onshore conversion to WST is only available via licensed commercial banks and may be limited by market liquidity. Profit remittance hurdles are effectively absent for ICs where all funds remain in foreign currency; dividends and distributions transmit cleanly via SWIFT MT103.

Export proceeds surrender rules

General Samoa surrender rule for resident exporters: export proceeds must be repatriated through a licensed bank within 30 days of receipt, and 100% of the foreign exchange must be sold to the authorized dealer unless a standing foreign-currency account exemption has been issued. International Companies are statutorily outside this regime—there is no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender timeline for IC export receipts. The SFO should direct all counterparties to remit directly to the IC's foreign-currency account at an authorized dealer in Samoa or abroad, clearly tagged as 'non-resident IC proceeds'.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) — Exchange Control Division, pursuant to the Exchange Control Act 1976 and delegated powers from the Minister of Finance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium. Samoa has no domestic EMI licensing or regulatory sandbox. A licensed foreign EMI (e.g., FCA-, MAS-, or EEA-authorised) may accept the IC for bank-to-bank transfer services if its KYC accepts offshore companies using nominee directors and family trusts. However, Samoan licensed banks tend to de-risk transfers originating from EMIs, and the Central Bank of Samoa does not extend any equivalence or passporting recognition. The SFO should restrict EMI usage to minor operational amounts and maintain a tier-1 commercial bank as the core clearing and custody hub.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File or Master File threshold is codified in Samoa domestic law. The Income Tax Act 2015 imports an arm's length standard for associated-party dealings, but the IRD has not published monetary documentation thresholds. A tax-exempt IC conducting private investment holding via custody and private banking has no transfer pricing documentation obligation.

Economic substance scope: Applicability of Economic Substance Regulations (ESR): Samoa's international company legislation has been aligned with OECD/EU substance standards. A Single Family Office engaged in pure passive holding of shares and securities must demonstrate adequate board management, physical registered office, and core income-generating activities in Samoa, or rely on delegation to a licensed Samoa corporate services provider. Nominee-only substance is a fatal deficiency and exposes the IC to penalty action and information exchange.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misdeclaration of Residency

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the IC as a Samoa resident company; assessment of corporate income tax at 25% on worldwide profits; statutory interest on underpaid tax; evasion penalties of up to 100% of the tax shortfall; criminal prosecution on indictment with substantial fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations are personally liable for penalties and tax arrears; the courts may pierce the corporate veil in fraud; directors may face travel bans, asset restraint orders, and imprisonment for fraud or perjury.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Registrar power to strike off the IC; administrative monetary penalties under the economic substance regime; escalation of the file to the Samoa competent authority for spontaneous exchange of information with foreign tax authorities; revocation of the tax exemption.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made personally accountable for penalty amounts; disqualification from acting as director for a defined statutory period; personal liability for restoration costs if the entity is struck off and later reinstated.
AML/CFT Breach and Concealment of Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Samoa's AML/CFT legislation; confiscation of proceeds; fines against the body corporate and individuals; administrative enforcement by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit and Registrar; removal of the licensed agent's approval.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability and imprisonment on conviction, civil confiscation of assets, disqualification from financial-sector directorships, and travel restrictions while investigations are pending.
Artificial Dividend Stripping / Round-Tripping

Statutory sanction: Samoa's general anti-avoidance rule may disregard the scheme to the extent it creates Samoan-source taint; the annual declaration will be rejected if it misrepresents activity; the case may be disclosed to treaty partners under the tax information exchange framework.

Director personal liability: Directors who architect or authorize the circular flow may be liable in the foreign parent jurisdiction for foreign tax penalties; within Samoa they risk personal sanction for filing false or misleading information and court-ordered restitution of redirected amounts.
