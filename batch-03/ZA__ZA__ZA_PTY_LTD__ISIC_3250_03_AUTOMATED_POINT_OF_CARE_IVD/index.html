SARS Customs Division handles export declarations; no sub-national tax authorities exist (unitary national fiscal system).

E-government filing portal

A (Pty) Ltd obtains its SARS tax reference number (TIN) via integrated CIPC-SARS registration: (1) incorporate with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) using CoR14.1/CoR14.3; no minimum capital is required and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted; (2) CIPC transmits the incorporation record to SARS, which issues a distinct company income tax reference number (8-digit numeric) and registers the company as a resident taxpayer; (3) activate eFiling, appoint a public officer (TAA s246) and, for non-resident controllers, a local representative taxpayer; submit IT77/ITA101 registration details and the registered address; (4) apply for VAT registration on form VAT101 (compulsory above ZAR 1,000,000 taxable turnover; voluntary from ZAR 50,000); (5) register for a Customs code (CUS) and the export-trade codes needed to file SAD500 electronic declarations; (6) register for PAYE/UIF/SDL if employees are engaged. Foreign companies without local incorporation that are nonetheless carrying on an enterprise apply via form IT77 and must designate a SARS representative; SARS may impose security under TAA s99.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a segmented convertibility system. Current account transactions (trade in goods and services, interest, royalties, and dividends) are generally freely convertible and may be settled by authorised dealers without prior approval, subject to documentary verification. Capital account transactions remain subject to exchange control approval or notification thresholds. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted provided that the company's shares are held under a valid non-resident declaration (Form NBR/CS) or approved foreign investment structure, the dividend is declared from distributable reserves after compliance with the Companies Act and income tax (secondary tax on companies / dividends tax), and the transfer is effected by an authorised dealer. Undue delay in remittance beyond 180 days for export proceeds and 365 days for certain service proceeds may create convertibility hurdles. Exchange control approval is also required for any capital transfers not explicitly authorised under the general permissions in the Exchange Control Manual.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South African exporters of manufacture goods are required to receive full payment within 180 days from the date of shipment. All export proceeds must be remitted to South Africa through an authorised dealer in foreign currency, and the authorised dealer is required to convert the proceeds into South African rand (ZAR) unless the exporter has obtained prior approval to retain foreign currency in an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA). There is no unilateral mandatory conversion ratio below 100% for standard export receipts; the full amount of the export consideration must be surrendered to the authorised dealer in exchange for rand. Exceptions require FinSurv approval and typically apply only to specific industries or approved investment structures. The exporter must retain evidence of shipment (bill of lading, airway bill), invoice, and proof of payment (SWIFT MT103) for five years for exchange control compliance.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and authorised dealers (commercial banks), under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations issued thereunder

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for unsupported offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs). South African exchange control regulations allow cross-border payment and settlement services to be provided principally by authorised dealers (banks) or authorised intermediaries. Off-shore EMIs cannot independently maintain customer foreign currency bank accounts or execute ZAR settlements without a local banking partner, and the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services Act (FAIS Act) plus FICA requirements effectively require a registered South African platform. An offshore EMI may be viable only if it enters a white-label partnership with a fully licensed South African authorised dealer and the EMI does not handle the ZAR clearing leg. Onboarding viability rating: Medium only under a bank-as-a-service arrangement with strict reporting to SARB.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unclear ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) chain involving a non-resident parent or nominee shareholders, especially where the shareholding is held via a discretionary trust or shell holding company without a clear economic substance.

Root cause: Unclear ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) chain involving a non-resident parent or nominee shareholders, especially where the shareholding is held via a discretionary trust or shell holding company without a clear economic substance.

Defense evidence: Provide annotated group structure chart with names, passport copies, tax numbers, and address verification of all natural UBOs holding above 25%; signed declaration of beneficial ownership; audited or management financial statements of each holding vehicle; confirmation from the parent company's registered agent or corporate secretary; and a memorandum explaining the share classes, voting rights, and economic entitlement.
High-risk medical device or IVD receivables flow mixed with intercompany royalties or intellectual property licensing, rendering the settlement currency and purpose ambiguous to the bank's internal AML transaction monitoring systems.

Root cause: High-risk medical device or IVD receivables flow mixed with intercompany royalties or intellectual property licensing, rendering the settlement currency and purpose ambiguous to the bank's internal AML transaction monitoring systems.

Defense evidence: Execute an intragroup services and licensing agreement with transfer pricing documentation (contemporaneous benchmarking report), issuance of original invoices with clear ISO 9362 bank identifier codes, provide the underlying purchase order / contract for each export shipment, and provide a reconciliation of the royalty calculation to the gross sales ledger. Add a detailed note to the SWIFT instruction identifying the VAT and customs tariff headings under ISIC 3250.
Sudden large inbound capital injection from a non-resident shareholder (e.g., equity subscription of above ZAR 30 million) within 60 days of account opening may trigger Foreign Exchange Control scrutiny and source-of-wealth verification.

Root cause: Sudden large inbound capital injection from a non-resident shareholder (e.g., equity subscription of above ZAR 30 million) within 60 days of account opening may trigger Foreign Exchange Control scrutiny and source-of-wealth verification.

Defense evidence: Submit a board resolution authorising the capital contribution, original share subscription agreement, proof of source of funds from the remitting bank (six months of bank statements and prior year audited financials of the shareholder), a declaration that the funds are not proceeds of tax evasion or sanctioned activity, and the duly completed Form FI (foreign investment declaration) for retention by the authorised dealer. Engage the bank's Global Markets desk to issue a release code before the inbound transfer.
Names of counterparties or incorporated addresses in jurisdictions subject to Financial Action Task Force (FATF) monitoring (e.g., certain African or offshore centres) may cause false-positive sanctions screening or PEP/PEP-related refusal of the commercial relationship.

Root cause: Names of counterparties or incorporated addresses in jurisdictions subject to Financial Action Task Force (FATF) monitoring (e.g., certain African or offshore centres) may cause false-positive sanctions screening or PEP/PEP-related refusal of the commercial relationship.

Defense evidence: Prepare a counterparty due diligence pack: independent corporate search and ultimate beneficial owner identification for each counterparty using a reputable business registry, confirm no UN, EU, US OFAC, or South African targeted financial sanctions listing, and provide a notarised certificate confirming the counterparty's identity. For jurisdictions with reduced transparency, obtain an audit trail of the trade transaction from purchase order to customs entry to establish legitimate commercial purpose.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection from a non-resident shareholder must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 if inter-entity settlement) to the company's ZAR capital account at an authorised dealer in South Africa. The remitter must use a purpose code consistent with the SARB Balance of Payments reporting, typically 'External Liabilities – Direct Investment' (equity) or the appropriate category under the South African Reserve Bank's Balance of Payments statistical classification; the bank will require the shareholder's full legal name, country of residence, tax identification and a completed Form FI to be submitted by the receiving authorised dealer to FinSurv if the total capital inflow exceeds the prescribed threshold. The funds must be declared as foreign capital in ZAR equivalent and cannot be recalled without exchange control approval. The company may simultaneously open a Capital Escrow Account if funds are held pending issue of share certificates or satisfaction of conditions precedent.Outbound trade settlements for IVD equipment and consumables are executed exclusively through the authorised dealer's electronic clearing system. The standard rail for non-documentary import payments and operating expenses is SWIFT MT103 with instructions referencing the invoice and import permit; the bank debits the company's ZAR account and purchases foreign currency at the agreed spot rate for same-day or value-forward settlement. For high-value or trans-boundary trade in medical devices where documentary compliance is required, a commercial letter of credit (L/C) is used: the issuing bank in South Africa transmits an MT700 series to the beneficiary's bank, with settlement upon presentation of compliant documents (shipping, quality certificates, ISO 13485 certificate, and prescriptions if applicable). All outbound payments must include the correct SARB Foreign Exchange Information System (FIS) code, support with the original invoice and transport documents, and remain within the enterprise's authorised dealer approval limits. Interest or royalty remittances are additionally subject to withholding tax compliance, and a migration of high-frequency low-value invoices to bank-integrated API or host-to-host channels is recommended to reduce friction.Forward exchange contracts (FX forwards) of up to 12 months for firm or anticipated trade commitments with no speculative intentNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for ZAR when transacting with an authorised dealer counterparty outside South Africa to hedge a non-rand base currency riskCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) with an authorised dealer to cover foreign currency funding risk on medium-term debtFX options (European) purchased from a licensed authorised dealer to hedge planned dividends or large contract settlementsVanilla currency swaps for trade-related cross-currency exposure, subject to SARB trade-related risk management guidelines
