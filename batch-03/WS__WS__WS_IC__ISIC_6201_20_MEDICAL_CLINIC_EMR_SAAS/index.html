Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), established under the Samoa Revenue Authority Act 2013 and overseen by the Ministry for Revenue; official e-Services portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned International Company (IC), the applicant first completes IC incorporation with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), appoints a licensed registered agent in Samoa, and files the certified charter, certificate of incorporation, and beneficial-owner particulars. Because an IC is a non-resident tax-exempt entity, it ordinarily requires no income-tax TIN; where a Samoan GST taxable activity is nevertheless derived, the entity must register online via the SRA portal or by Form SRA 200, supplying the incorporation documents, registered agent details, foreign owner identification, and projected turnover. The SRA issues a TIN within approximately 5 business days; GST registration is effective from the date the WST 80,000 threshold is met or exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election is available to Samoa International Companies. The principal statutory election is conversion into a domestic company under the International Companies Act 1988, which terminates the statutory 20-year tax exemption, and the related option to align the IC's accounting reference date for SRA administrative filings. Samoa's Income Tax Act 2018 and Tax Administration Act 2018 incorporate OECD BEPS-aligned hybrid mismatch and anti-double-non-taxation rules, so cross-border payments under the EMR SaaS model cannot rely on a mismatch election to achieve exemption in both Samoa and the recipient jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 80,000 (SAT 80,000) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; non-resident suppliers of remote services (including cloud-based EMR software) are caught by the same economic registration threshold for supplies made to Samoan-resident recipients

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible goods and 'international services' qualify for a 0% GST rate. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who is not in Samoa at the time of supply and the services are performed and contractually used entirely outside Samoa; this covers offshore EMR SaaS subscriptions, HIPAA cloud software licensing, and software-related support provided to foreign medical clinics. The supplier must retain evidence of the recipient's non-residence and of overseas use/benefit, and a GST-registered exporter may claim input-tax credits and refunds for GST-inclusive costs attributable to those zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SRA customs entry for export) endorsed by Samoa Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing the physical movement of exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) bank credit advice evidencing hard-currency receipt
  • Commercial invoice and signed SaaS/EMR service agreement identifying the non-resident client
  • Certificate/declaration of the overseas recipient's non-residence and use of the software outside Samoa

Samoa currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory e-invoicing platform, and no API/digital-stamping obligation. Registered persons must issue a prescribed GST tax invoice, in paper or electronic form, within 28 days of supply, and must file GST returns and remit GST electronically through the SRA e-Service portal at the frequencies (monthly, two-monthly, or six-monthly) directed by the Commissioner.

The SRA can reopen returns within 4 years (7 years for fraud or willful evasion), claw back the unremitted 15% GST with interest, and impose shortfall penalty tax of up to 150% for intentional evasion or 75% for gross negligence, plus monthly late-payment penalties. Deliberate falsification of export zero-rating documents attracts criminal prosecution with fines up to SAT 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years, and the responsible IC's registered agent may be sanctioned administratively under the International Companies Act 1988.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains exchange controls on resident Tālā accounts; the International Company (IC) is treated as non-resident for exchange control purposes, allowing free convertibility in foreign currency and unimpeded profit remittance offshore without CBS approval. WST is convertible for non-residents only through authorized dealers. Capital account transactions by non-resident ICs are unrestricted, while resident entities face partial restrictions requiring CBS authorization for certain inward/outward investment flows. Profit remittance for the EMR SaaS IC is not subject to dividend withholding tax or capital repatriation approval, provided the IC maintains non-resident status under the International Companies Act 1987.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must surrender 100% of export proceeds to an authorized bank in Samoa within 30 days of receipt, unless an exemption is granted by CBS. The WS IC, being non-resident, is not subject to surrender obligations for its foreign-currency EMR SaaS subscription receipts. If foreign currency is physically remitted into the IC's Samoan bank account, conversion to WST at prevailing exchange rates is permitted without mandatory surrender; however, transfers to local resident payees requiring WST may be subject to bank-level exchange control declarations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) – Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., those licensed under a reputable FCA, FinCEN, or other recognized regime) can be selectively used for receivables collection in USD/EUR via virtual IBAN facilities, especially for B2B SaaS billing from global clinics. However, Samoa does not license or recognize EMIs as banks, and any domestic WST disbursement or operational funding in Samoa must clear through a commercial bank authorized by CBS. Legal constraints: EMI accounts cannot hold WST without a local banking partner; significant outbound flows from an EMI to a Samoan IC may trigger enhanced AML review due to payment-intermediary risk. Recommended as a secondary card and subscription-convenience rail, not as a substitute for primary corporate banking.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — A Samoa International Company is exempt from corporate income tax under the International Companies Act 1987, as amended, and the Income Tax Act 2012. The exemption covers all income derived by the International Company, including offshore royalties, SaaS subscription revenue, interest, dividends, and capital gains.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by a Samoa International Company to non-resident shareholders under the statutory International Company exemption. No dividend withholding tax is deducted at source. If the International Company exemption is lost and the entity becomes a taxable Samoan domestic company, the statutory non-resident dividend withholding rate is 15%, subject to any applicable double tax agreement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer-pricing documentation threshold is publicly codified for a tax-exempt Samoa International Company. Because the company bears no income tax, no Local File or Master File must be filed with the Samoa Ministry of Revenue. However, where the International Company has intercompany transactions with related parties — including EMR software licenses, cloud hosting costs, management fees, or client receivables above WST 1,000,000 per annum — a contemporaneous arm's length benchmark study should be prepared to defend economic substance, pricing integrity, and the absence of a taxable Samoan permanent establishment.

Economic substance scope: Samoa applies economic substance rules to International Companies carrying on relevant activities, including intellectual property holding, distribution and service centers, finance and leasing, and pure holding company business. A Medical Clinic EMR SaaS entity holding registered software, trademarks, or data rights is likely to be characterized as an intellectual property business or a distribution/service business. The company must demonstrate in Samoa: active decision-making, qualified local employees or an outsourced local function proportionate to the activity, adequate operating expenditure, physical premises, and annual filing of an economic substance return. A pure equity holding company may satisfy a reduced substance test. Where economic substance is not satisfied, the exemption is not automatically a criminal violation, but the company becomes exposed to reassessment, penalties, cancellation, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Use of International Company Exemption

Statutory sanction: The Samoa Ministry of Revenue may disregard the tax-exempt International Company form, treat the Samoan activity as a permanent establishment, and assess the entire net revenue at the relevant domestic corporate income tax rate. In addition, the Tax Administration may impose an evader penalty of up to 200% of the tax avoided, late payment interest, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under the Income Tax Act 2012, carrying fines up to WST 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to seven years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto controllers who knowingly engineered the sham are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax, penalties, interest, and litigation costs. The Supreme Court of Samoa may pierce the corporate veil, impose travel bans, issue worldwide asset freezing orders, disqualify directors for a specified period, and order civil restitution to the Treasury or defrauded creditors.
Economic Substance Failure — IP Holding and SaaS Revenue

Statutory sanction: The Registrar or Ministry of Revenue may declare the International Company economically non-compliant, impose a fixed penalty plus a continuing daily penalty, remove the company from the International Company register, or strike off the company. Economic substance legislation regimes in Samoa allow cancellation of the company's certificate of incorporation and recovery of unpaid government fees with penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false economic substance declaration are exposed to civil penalties, disqualification, and criminal sanctions for false statements. In serious cases, directors may face a travel ban and personal restitution orders for sums distributed to shareholders while the company was insolvent or non-compliant.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Terrorism Financing Act 2018 of Samoa, a corporate entity may be fined up to WST 1,000,000, and natural persons up to WST 300,000, with imprisonment for up to five years. The Financial Intelligence Unit may also issue asset seizure orders, suspend bank accounts, and impose civil penalties for failure to verify customer identity and report suspicious activity.

Director personal liability: Directors, company secretaries, and resident agents can be personally prosecuted if they knew or recklessly ignored the concealment. Personal liability includes financial penalties, confiscation orders, travel bans, and a prohibition from acting as a director or agent in Samoa for a prescribed period.
Shell Company and Sham Transaction

Statutory sanction: A court may declare the International Company and its contracts to be shams, void the intercompany agreements, reassess income to the true economic owner, and impose civil fraud penalties. The Registrar may strike off the company for failing to maintain a principal place of business or accounting records. Criminal fraud penalties may apply with imprisonment for up to seven years under the Crimes Act 2013.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the company is used for fraud or evasion. Directors and shareholders can be ordered to repay all dividends and distributions received from the shell company, make restitution to customers or the tax authority, and are subject to travel bans and disqualification orders. In extreme cases, custodial sentences are imposed.
