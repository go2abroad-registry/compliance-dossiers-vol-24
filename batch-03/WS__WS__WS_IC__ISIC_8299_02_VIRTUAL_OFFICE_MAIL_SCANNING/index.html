Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa; statutory administrator of the Tax Administration Act 2012, Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST) Act 1993, and Income Tax Ordinance 1959 (as amended); online filing and registration portal: Tax Administration Portal (TAP) at https://tap.minrev.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

The International Company (IC), although income-tax-exempt, must obtain a Revenue Identity Number (RIN/TIN) for VAGST registration, customs transactions, and administrative dealings. SOP: (1) file Form RIN-01 via TAP or at the MfR APIA office; (2) attach the certified Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of International Companies under the International Companies Act 1987 (IC Act), notarized passport copies of directors/shareholders, and the registered office address certificate; (3) receive the RIN within 5 to 10 business days; (4) if taxable turnover reaches or exceeds WST 100,000, submit VAGST Form 01 to register for the indirect tax; (5) no local resident director, local physical office, or minimum capital is required for the tax-exempt IC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: Under section 198 of the IC Act 1987, the International Company is exempt from income tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty on all non-Samoan-source income and international activities, provided it has not elected to carry on business within Samoa. For VAGST purposes a separate nexus test applies: an IC supplying virtual office, mail scanning, and digital forwarding services exclusively to non-resident clients outside Samoa is treated as an offshore/exempt-zone supplier with no taxable nexus; if it makes taxable supplies connected to Samoa exceeding WST 100,000 annually, it must register and remit 15% VAGST.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style pass-through election exists in Samoan law. Statutory options: (a) an IC may serve written notice on the Registrar of International Companies electing to surrender its tax exemption and become taxable under the Income Tax Ordinance 1959 if it intends to carry on domestic business in Samoa; (b) voluntary VAGST registration is permitted under section 10 of the VAGST Act 1993 for suppliers below the WST 100,000 threshold; (c) hybrid mismatch protections under Part XI of the Tax Administration Act 2012 deny deductions, exemptions, and credits in cross-border arrangements producing double non-taxation, deduction/non-inclusion, or double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover (compulsory registration); voluntary registration available below WST 100,000. A non-resident IC with no Samoan nexus is outside the charge unless services are physically performed in or connected to Samoa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 14(2) and Schedule 1 of the VAGST Act 1993 applies to (a) services physically performed outside Samoa, and (b) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and not supplied in connection with land or goods in Samoa — the qualifying category for offshore virtual office, mail scanning, and digital forwarding services rendered to foreign clients. The supplier must hold documentary evidence of the client's non-resident status and offshore consumption. Purchase of local inputs (telephone lines, printing, courier dispatch) does not sever the offshore service characterization but disallows corresponding input tax credits unless a valid zero-rating substantiation file is maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED) lodged in ASYCUDA World — required for any physical mail or goods forwarding
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export of the physical mail/cargo from Samoa to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and Inward Remittance Certificate or bank remittance record confirming foreign-currency payment from the non-resident client

No real-time fiscal clearance, no government digital stamping, and no mandatory API e-invoicing clearance regime is in force. Requirements: (1) VAGST-registered persons must issue a valid tax invoice within 28 days of supply by paper or digital means, showing the supplier RIN, buyer RIN, description of supply, and the 15% tax component; (2) returns are filed electronically via TAP on a monthly cycle, with quarterly filing available to small taxpayers; (3) payment is by electronic funds transfer to the MfR collection account; (4) physical export declarations are lodged electronically through the ASYCUDA World single window; no cryptographic invoice clearance is required.

MfR audits export zero-rating claims against the CED, shipping records, service contracts, and remittance evidence. Under the Tax Administration Act 2012, tax shortfall penalties apply at 20% (failure to take reasonable care), 75% (failure to disclose a shortfall with intent to conceal), and 150% (evasion or intentional misstatement); input tax credits claimed on fraudulent zero-rated exports are clawed back retroactively with statutory interest; criminal conviction attracts a fine up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years, and directors of the International Company are jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan currency is the tala (WST), pegged in practice to a basket of major trading currencies and freely convertible through licensed authorized dealers. Samoa maintains current-account convertibility for legitimate trade, services, dividends, and interest payments; capital-account transactions, offshore borrowings, and inward investment flows require prior Central Bank approval unless covered by a standing exemption. International Companies registered as offshore entities and not carrying on domestic business are generally exempt from income tax and exchange-control restrictions, but any flow passing through a Samoan bank account remains subject to CBS anti-money-laundering and balance-of-payments reporting. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation from an International Company to a non-resident shareholder are treated as current-account items on presentation of audited or signed financial statements, board resolutions, tax-exemption confirmations, and shareholding evidence, and do not require Ministry of Revenue withholding clearance because the IC is tax-exempt.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Samoan domestic exporters, full export receipts must be repatriated to Samoa through a CBS-licensed authorized dealer within 90 days after the date of shipment or the availability of foreign exchange, whichever is later, and must be declared to the Central Bank for monitoring. In practice, authorized dealers receive the foreign currencies, credit the exporter's foreign-currency account, and apply a conversion ratio of up to 100% to WST only when needed for domestic disbursements; exporters wishing to retain foreign currency for approved working-capital or trade purposes must obtain a CBS-specific conversion waiver or retention authority. For an International Company providing virtual-office and mail-scanning services to non-resident clients, the physical export of goods from Samoa does not occur, so the classic surrender-of-goods export proceeds regime does not apply; nevertheless, offshore service receipts collected through a Samoan account are classified as invisible exports and fall under CBS balance-of-payments reporting rather than customs surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), operating as the exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977, with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) as the licensing, registration, and supervisory authority for International Companies (IC) and offshore service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Cross-border viability is LOW for purely offshore digital EMIs. Samoa does not issue electronic-money-institution (EMI) licenses and does not grant passporting rights to foreign EMIs. An International Company may lawfully use a third-country EMI that supports Samoan International Company ownership, but the EMI cannot clear into the Samoan banking system as a licensed bank and will not provide Samoan settlement account numbers. Any EMI used for client onboarding must remain ancillary to a principal bank account; if the EMI is treated by SIFA or CBS as an unlicensed deposit-taking institution, the IC or its service provider may face administrative sanctions. Recommended architecture is a principal Samoan or correspondent bank account at ANZ or BSP and an external EMI only for recurring credit-card and Stripe-style collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Samoan IC shall be routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the shareholder's account to the Samoan authorized dealer bank, using the purpose description 'IC equity subscription — International Company Act 1987' and the Samoan BOP capital-account code for direct investment / equity capitalization. The remittance must be accompanied by a board resolution approving the share allotment, the updated register of members, and a CBS declaration where the bank is required to report capital inflows. Where the shareholder funds are non-WST, the authorized dealer may convert at the prevailing market rate; the bank must retain the SWIFT remittance advice, legal opinion on non-resident ownership, and the IC's certificate of compliance to prevent freezing or delayed crediting. Use of an offshore EMI as an intermediary is discouraged because the bank will require evidence that the EMI is not a shell and that the ultimate origin of funds is visible in the SWIFT chain.Supplier settlements and trade disbursements should be executed via bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 for invoices under USD 250,000 or equivalent, and via MT103/MT202 cover combined with a commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for larger procurement, particularly when dealing with Asia-Pacific suppliers for paperless digital hardware or professional services. The outbound remittance must reference the service invoice, PO number, and IC business scope; payment purpose should be 'Service fees — virtual office, mail scanning and digital forwarding' or 'trade settlement under LC.' If the supplier requires guarantee, an SBLC or standby L/C can be issued on a secured basis against cash collateral or approved credit facility at ANZ or BSP. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Companies (wholly tax-exempt); the general Samoan resident corporate income tax rate of 27% applies only to domestic entities and does not apply to an International Company conducting business exclusively outside Samoa.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for an International Company: dividends distributed by a Samoa IC to non-resident shareholders are exempt from Samoan dividend withholding tax because the IC operates as a tax-exempt zone entity. The default Samoan domestic non-resident dividend withholding rate of 15% does not apply to ICs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold in Samoa Tala (WST) is published; enforcement operates through the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 and the general arm's length maintenance standard of the Income Tax Act. Practical compliance threshold: prepare and retain a complete Master File and Local File when annual related-party transactions exceed WST 5,000,000 (approx. USD 1.85 million), or whenever the IC renders IP-related, service, or resale activity to group entities.

Economic substance scope: The Samoa International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 apply. A virtual-office/mail-scanning activity serving related group entities is classified as a 'Distribution and Service Centre' and constitutes a relevant activity requiring demonstrable core income-generating activities in Samoa. The IC must maintain a physical office, at least one qualified full-time local employee managing the scanning and mail operations, local mail-processing equipment, and annual local expenditure proportionate to revenue. A pure mail-forwarding shell or reliance solely on the registered agent's facilities fails the doctrine and triggers enforcement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abusive Exploitation of IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: Immediate revocation of the IC tax exemption; reassessment of all Samoan-source income at the 27% corporate rate; civil penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded; monthly late-payment surcharges of 2%; criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 2012 with fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The Samoan courts will pierce the corporate veil where the IC is a facade. Directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid tax; convicted directors face travel bans preventing departure from Samoa, confiscation of personally held assets, and custodial sentences of up to 5 years.
Economic Substance Violation and Shell Company Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty up to WST 20,000 plus WST 100 daily default penalties under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019; mandatory strike-off; freeze of bank accounts tied to the IC; adverse reporting to foreign tax authorities through the Global Forum and TIEA framework.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance declarations are personally liable for the penalties without limitation; an adverse finding establishes cross-border travel and banking red flags, disqualification from any Samoa directorship, and possible seizure of personal proceeds traceable to the sham arrangement.
Anti-Money Laundering and CMRA Facility Abuse

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the CMRA and IC registrations; confiscation of mail records and digital files; corporate fines up to WST 500,000 under the Samoa Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism Act 2018; senior officers face imprisonment up to 14 years for laundering criminal proceeds through the mail-scanning pipeline.

Director personal liability: The anti-avoidance regime disregards the IC's separate personality where the scanning facility is a conduit for laundering funds; directors are liable for civil restitution of the full laundered amounts, are subject to immediate travel embargoes and blacklisting from all licensed activities in Samoa, and may be sentenced to up to 14 years' imprisonment.
Falsification of Registers and Destruction of Economic Records

Statutory sanction: Offence under the International Companies Act 1987 with fines up to WST 50,000; repeated non-compliance triggers compulsory winding up; reinstatement after strike-off carries an additional surcharge of WST 500.

Director personal liability: Directors bear strict liability for the accuracy of registered records; falsification results in court-ordered personal compensation to injured third parties, a 10-year ban from holding directorships in any Samoa entity, and imprisonment of up to 7 years subject to extradition arrangements.
