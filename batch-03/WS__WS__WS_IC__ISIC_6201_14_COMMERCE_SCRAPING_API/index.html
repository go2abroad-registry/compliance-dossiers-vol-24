Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue (MfR), Independent State of Samoa (Matagaluega o Tupe Mau a le Malo); official online services portal: revenue.gov.ws / MfR e-Services (TIN verification, VAGST returns, customs ASYCUDA World declarations).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned international company (IC) is incorporated under the International Companies Act 2007 via a Samoa-licensed registered agent; the Registrar of International Companies (Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour) issues the Certificate of Incorporation and the Ministry of Revenue automatically assigns the TIN. The IC obtains a Standard Government Fee Certificate in lieu of tax filing; no income tax registration or annual return is required. VAGST registration is not required where the IC conducts only offshore API/proxy services; in the exceptional case of a permitted domestic taxable activity, a VAGST business registration form must be filed with MfR and a local fixed establishment designated.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned international company (IC) is incorporated under the International Companies Act 2007 via a Samoa-licensed registered agent; the Registrar of International Companies (Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour) issues the Certificate of Incorporation and the Ministry of Revenue automatically assigns the TIN. The IC obtains a Standard Government Fee Certificate in lieu of tax filing; no income tax registration or annual return is required. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid entity election exists under Samoa domestic law; the IC's tax-exempt status is mandatory, non-elective, and cannot be waived by election. Samoa has not adopted OECD hybrid mismatch or top-up tax elective regimes for ICs; fiscal transparency is determined solely under the shareholder's foreign tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory registration); voluntary registration available for exporters and suppliers of zero-rated goods/services upon MfR application; economic nexus requirement enforced for any domestic taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAGST-registered exporter may zero-rate the export of goods or services where the goods are physically exported within the statutory period or the services are supplied to a non-resident for beneficial use wholly outside Samoa. A Samoan IC supplying API data pipelines, proxy gateways and scraping infrastructure to non-resident clients meets zero-rating criteria if the service is performed from Samoa infrastructure but the recipient, place of contract performance, and economic benefit are offshore; invoices must be issued to non-residents in foreign currency (commonly USD or USDC).

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged via ASYCUDA World (Samoa Customs / MfR)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice demonstrating foreign-currency receipt
  • Executed service agreement with the non-resident counterparty
  • Zero-rated VAGST tax invoice identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Technical evidence of offshore supply (API access logs, gateway server records, proxy usage reports)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or e-invoicing API mandate is in force in Samoa; taxpayers issue MfR-compliant paper/PDF invoices and file VAGST returns via the MfR e-Services portal. ASYCUDA World provides electronic customs declarations but does not operate a continuous invoice clearance function.

False or unsupported zero-rating claims are treated as tax shortfalls under the Tax Administration Act 2012 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 2003: MfR may claw back refunded VAGST with interest and impose shortfall penalties up to 200% for deliberate evasion, plus monthly late-payment penalties for overdue amounts. Knowingly forged customs documents, bogus bills of lading or fictitious remittance certificates expose directors to criminal fines and imprisonment, deregistration of the IC, and indefinite reopening of assessments where fraud is proven.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is not freely convertible. Current account payments are generally permitted through authorized-dealer commercial banks when supported by genuine underlying invoices, contracts, or audited financial statements. Capital account transactions, including the injection of foreign equity into an International Company, require prior CBS approval or an Exchange Control declaration routed through a licensed bank. Profit remittance hurdles include audited accounts, a CBS-specified foreign-remittance declaration, proof of good standing from the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), and an internal bank decision confirming that no ongoing Exchange Control obligation is breached. Although an International Company is tax-exempt, remittances are still subject to AML/CFT and wire-transfer compliance due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Samoa Exchange Control Regulations, resident exporters must repatriate export proceeds to an authorized-dealer bank in Samoa within 30 calendar days from the date of receipt. Mandatory conversion ratio is 100% into WST unless the Central Bank of Samoa grants a specific foreign-currency retention or exemption approval. For an International Company, a CBS exemption may be applied for through the local bank, but the default statutory timeline and conversion ratio remain the 30-day surrender rule and 100% conversion requirement. The authorized dealer must retain the export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading or service contract, and settlement proof for inspection.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), operating under the Exchange Control Act 1979, the Exchange Control Regulations 1980, and the Financial Institutions Act 1996

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Samoa does not have a standalone e-money institution or fintech EMI licensing framework. Offshore digital EMIs cannot obtain settlement accounts at CBS or access Samoan clearing rails. A foreign EMI would need a banking licence under the Financial Institutions Act 1996, physical presence in Samoa, a local compliance officer, and SIFA recognition to offer account services to an International Company. Cryptocurrency flows such as USDC are not native settlement instruments for WST and cannot be cleared through CBS; they must be converted through a licensed money remitter or foreign exchange dealer outside Samoa before entering the banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be wired by the overseas parent or investor through SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) to the Samoan authorized-dealer bank for credit to a dedicated Capital Escrow Account. The remittance instruction must quote the Samoan International Company's SIFA registration number, the CBS Exchange Control approval reference, and a purpose declaration corresponding to the BPM6 category of direct investment - equity capital. The receiving bank will verify the board resolution authorizing capital issuance, subscribed share schedule, source-of-funds statement, certificate of good standing, and the execution of the CBS Exchange Control form before releasing funds from escrow into the operational account. The foreign currency may be retained in a permissible USD denominated account only if the CBS retention approval has been granted; otherwise WST conversion is required at the prevailing authorized-dealer rate.Cross-border trade and licensing disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 payments for high-volume API licensing settlements, and Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT710) governed by UCP600 and ISBP 821 for larger contractual deliverables. All outbound transactions require an underlying commercial contract, invoice, and an authorized-dealer foreign-exchange declaration. Samoan banks route USD and third-currency payments through correspondent bank networks; WST is not an international clearing currency, so no direct offshore WST clearing exists. For crypto-denominated USDC disbursements, the International Company must first convert USDC to USD/EUR through a licensed exchange and then execute a compliant SWIFT payment to the beneficiary account; the bank will not clear USDC denominated direct settlements.Plain vanilla FX forwards booked with a CBS-licensed authorized dealer covering genuine committed trade, API licensing, or approved capital-account exposure.Cross-currency swaps for tenor-matched conversion of foreign-currency debt or equity proceeds, subject to prior CBS Exchange Control approval and full underlying documentation.Short-term FX swaps used to cover working-capital mismatches between USDC/USD receivables and WST or other settlement currencies.NDFs are not permissible for the WST onshore market because the Samoan tala remains deliverable through authorized dealers and CBS does not maintain a licensed non-deliverable forward framework.
