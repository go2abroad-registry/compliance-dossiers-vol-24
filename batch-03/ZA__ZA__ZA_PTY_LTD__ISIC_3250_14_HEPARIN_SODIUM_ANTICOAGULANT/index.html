Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Proprietary Limited Company; the Companies Act abolished par value and minimum issued capital requirements. Shares may be issued at a board-determined issue price, and the full consideration must be paid or secured prior to allotment under s. 40. No escrow bank account is statutorily mandated; however, subscription proceeds should be deposited into the company's authorised corporate bank account before allotment is recorded in the securities register.

Occupational: clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, including a Major Hazard Installation (MHI) risk assessment and permit from the Department of Employment and Labour where solvent storage exceeds threshold quantities, COIDA registration and workplace compliance inspection for laboratory and cleanroom zones; SAHPRA GMP inspection of the manufacturing suites is issued as a prerequisite to the operating permit.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — the national tax and customs administration of the Republic of South Africa, functioning under the National Treasury; digital services through the SARS eFiling portal (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za), the SARS MobiApp, and the SARS Customs client portal for import/export registration and SAD500 clearance; corporate law prerequisites administered by the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC).

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated (Pty) Ltd with permissible non-resident ownership: (1) Incorporate with CIPC and obtain the registration certificate and Memorandum of Incorporation; (2) appoint a resident 'public officer' (a natural person ordinarily resident in South Africa) to represent the company for all tax matters (s 241 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011); (3) register the company with SARS through eFiling using the Registration, Amendments and Verification (RAV) module to obtain the income tax reference number (TIN); (4) register for VAT in the same RAV process — compulsory within 21 days after month-end once taxable supplies in the preceding 12-month period exceed ZAR 1,000,000, and voluntary from ZAR 50,000; (5) obtain a SARS Customs trader (importer/exporter) client code before any export of heparin products; (6) if employees are engaged, register for PAYE, UIF and SDL; (7) SARS KYC documentation: certified CIPC certificate, MOI, identity documents/passports of directors, proof of registered office address, bank confirmation, financial statements or projections, and powers of attorney for tax practitioners; (8) non-resident shareholders should obtain non-resident income tax reference numbers from SARS to support dividends/interest withholding tax compliance and tax treaty relief claims; registration must be completed before trading commences, and each tax type carries its own effective date and filing cycle.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The 'Proprietary Limited Company' ((Pty) Ltd) is a separate legal and taxable person; corporate income tax at 27% (for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); dividends tax at 20% on shareholder distributions; capital gains inclusion rate of 80% for companies; no pass-through eligibility (unlike partnerships/trusts) and no check-the-box regime; Territorial_Scope: companies tax-resident in South Africa (incorporated or effectively managed in SA) are taxed on worldwide income with a foreign tax credit; the Pty Ltd is SA-resident by incorporation, while non-resident shareholders are taxed only on SA-source income (dividends, interest, royalties) via final withholding taxes; Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Special Economic Zone (SEZ) regime offering a reduced 15% corporate income tax rate and VAT/customs relief for approved SEZ enterprises is available only upon application and location within a designated SEZ — it is not a default classification for a standard Johannesburg-registered Pty Ltd.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or fiscal-transparency election exists in South Africa. Statutory elections and mechanisms relevant to the Pty Ltd structure include: (i) voluntary VAT registration election under s 23(2) VAT Act once taxable supply turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000 in any 12-month period; (ii) VAT group registration election under s 50(3) VAT Act for companies within a 70% 'group of companies' to file as a single VAT vendor; (iii) small business corporation election under s 12E Income Tax Act (progressive 0%/7%/21%/27% rates) available only where all shareholders are natural persons and income caps are met — generally unavailable for a corporate-owned active pharma extraction holding; (iv) s 11D research-and-development allowance election/procedure (150% deduction approved by SARS and the Department of Science and Innovation for qualifying pharmaceutical/chemical R&D); (v) hybrid instrument and interest-deduction limitation regimes (ss 8F, 8E, 23M Income Tax Act) operate by statutory recharacterization rather than taxpayer election; (vi) taxpayers may apply for private binding rulings and advance pricing agreements with SARS to confirm the consequences of any election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: total taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (s 23(1) VAT Act), with the registration application due within 21 days of month-end; voluntary registration: permitted where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period (s 23(2)); foreign suppliers of electronic services have a separate ZAR 50,000 compulsory threshold; small vendors below ZAR 1,000,000 may file VAT returns on a two-monthly cycle, but exporters of zero-rated goods should register promptly to recover input VAT on manufacturing, fractionation and extraction costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: The export supply of movable goods (heparin sodium API / low-molecular-weight heparin in bulk) is zero-rated under s 11(1)(a) VAT Act where (i) the goods are supplied to a non-resident customer, (ii) the goods are physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply (or such extended period as SARS may allow), (iii) the vendor retains the prescribed documentary proof (customs-cleared SAD500, transport document, foreign-currency payment evidence), and (iv) a valid s 20 tax invoice is issued and annotated 'zero-rated export'; Services: offshore regulatory, quality-control and technical services supplied to a non-resident for use or consumption wholly outside South Africa may be zero-rated under s 11(2)(l); special rules apply to SACU members, ships/aircraft stores, and supplies to foreign embassies; because heparin is a regulated pharmaceutical substance, the export must be covered by SAHPRA manufacturing/export authorization (and, on request, a Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product), and the goods must be declared at the port of exit; failure to export within 90 days invalidates the zero-rating and the supply becomes taxable at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) cleared by SARS Customs at the port of exit
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorized dealer (South African commercial bank) confirming receipt of export payment in foreign currency
  • Tax invoice compliant with s 20 VAT Act, denominated in foreign currency, and annotated 'zero-rated export'
  • Packing list and freight/insurance certificate covering the exported consignment
  • SAHPRA export authorization and Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product for the controlled heparin sodium API
  • Evidence of the overseas buyer's non-resident status (e.g., foreign certificate of incorporation, registered address proof)

SARS published the e-Invoicing Framework (June 2024) defining a phased mandatory e-invoicing rollout: compulsory e-invoicing for large enterprises is scheduled to commence from 1 January 2026, with cascading phases for medium and small vendors expected through 2027/2028; the framework mandates structured XML (UBL-based) e-invoices exchanged through accredited seller/buyer service providers and validated against the SARS eFiling and data-matching engines; South Africa does not currently operate a per-invoice real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamp model (unlike Brazil, Mexico or the UAE); until the mandate applies, vendors must issue tax invoices satisfying the content particulars of s 20 VAT Act (vendor VAT number, description, quantity, price, tax fraction, and the basis for zero-rating), retain electronic copies, and file VAT201 returns electronically on a monthly or bi-monthly cycle.

If zero-rating is claimed without physical export within 90 days, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 15% and SARS may raise an additional VAT assessment within the limitation periods (s 99 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011: 3 years generally; 5 years where the taxpayer failed to disclose all material facts; no time bar in cases of fraud or misrepresentation). Understatement penalties under ss 222–223 TAA escalate from 10% (substantial understatement) to 25% (failure to take reasonable care), 50% (no reasonable grounds), 75% (impermissible avoidance) and 150% (intentional tax evasion); consistent late or non-payment exposes the taxpayer to a monthly penalty of 10% of the tax due (s 210 TAA) capped at twelve months, plus interest at the SARS prescribed rate. Fraudulent export zero-rating schemes additionally attract criminal prosecution under ss 234–236 TAA (fine and/or imprisonment up to five years), public naming on the SARS 'name and shame' register under s 67(1) TAA, and SARB exchange-control enforcement for failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, including discretionary penalties, loss of export credit facilities, and suspension of customs clearance privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a controlled-currency regime with separate current and capital account treatment. Current-account transactions, including bona fide trade payments, dividends, interest, royalties and arm's-length service fees, are convertible through a SARB Authorised Dealer upon documentary verification and do not generally require prior exchange-control approval. Capital-account transactions, including inward equity investments, shareholder loans, intra-group finance and outward capital repatriation, are subject to FinSurv approval or delegated Authorised Dealer registration. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted only where SARS tax obligations are up to date, dividends withholding tax has been accounted for, and the company provides the Authorised Dealer with a valid tax-clearance certificate and documentary evidence of the underlying earnings. The ZAR is not fully externally convertible on the capital account, and large or unusual repatriations may require enhanced review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory percentage-surrender or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. However, under SARB exchange-control rules, 100% of export proceeds for goods shipped from South Africa must be received through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment. Export receipts may be credited to an approved foreign-currency account or a ZAR account, but must be reconciled against the supporting export documents, including the SAD500 customs declaration, bill of lading or airway bill, commercial invoice and bank receipt. Retention of funds in a foreign-currency account beyond the standard limits or beyond the 180-day repatriation timeline requires prior FinSurv approval; otherwise, the surplus must be disposed of as directed by the Authorised Dealer.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary account and settlement channel. South African exchange control requires export proceeds, trade disbursements and capital repatriation to flow through a SARB Authorised Dealer, which means a domestic commercial bank or a registered branch of a foreign bank with Authorised Dealer status. Offshore digital EMIs are not Authorised Dealers and cannot lawfully replace the domestic clearing leg or provide compliant ZAR settlement. A licensed payment facilitator or fintech may be used as an invoicing or collections utility, but all FX conversion, purpose-code validation and exchange-control reporting must be executed by the regulated banking partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital and shareholder loan injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the company's ZAR or approved foreign-currency account at a SARB Authorised Dealer. The inbound remittance instruction must state the applicable SARB purpose-of-payment code for direct equity investment or shareholder financing, the non-resident investor identity and the underlying corporate approval. The company must provide a board resolution, share allotment records or signed loan agreement, capital-introduction certificate and tax documentation so the Authorised Dealer can issue or record the foreign investment approval. Transactions exceeding the Authorised Dealer's delegated authority, or presenting debt-equity conversion features, require prior FinSurv approval.Outbound disbursements for heparin raw-material purchases and pharmaceutical trade obligations should use irrevocable documentary letters of credit (UCP 600) issued or confirmed by a South African Authorised Dealer and advised through a foreign correspondent bank, or direct SWIFT MT103 wires where open-account settlement is contractually agreed. Payment is released only against compliant documents, including the SAD500 import declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of origin and inspection certificate. The Authorised Dealer must record the SARB purpose code for import trade settlement, and any FX conversion must be matched to the underlying obligation.Forward exchange contracts (FECs) with a SARB Authorised Dealer on a deliverable ZAR-foreign-currency basis, limited to documented current-account trade exposure and not executed for speculative purposes.Cross-currency swaps, interest-rate swaps and basis swaps entered through a SARB Authorised Dealer or a registered offshore treasury counterparty, subject to FinSurv notification or approval where the hedged risk represents long-term capital exposure.OTC deliverable and non-deliverable currency options purchased through an Authorised Dealer for hedging forecast export receipts or import payables, with premiums settled from current-account funds and no open short-currency position.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on ZAR, available only with specific SARB FinSurv or Authorised Dealer authority, or executed by the offshore parent treasury on arm's-length terms with full intercompany documentation, and only where the hedge reduces genuine currency risk arising from cross-border heparin trade.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary account and settlement channel. South African exchange control requires export proceeds, trade disbursements and capital repatriation to flow through a SARB Authorised Dealer, which means a domestic commercial bank or a registered branch of a foreign bank with Authorised Dealer status. Offshore digital EMIs are not Authorised Dealers and cannot lawfully replace the domestic clearing leg or provide compliant ZAR settlement. A licensed payment facilitator or fintech may be used as an invoicing or collections utility, but all FX conversion, purpose-code validation and exchange-control reporting must be executed by the regulated banking partner.
