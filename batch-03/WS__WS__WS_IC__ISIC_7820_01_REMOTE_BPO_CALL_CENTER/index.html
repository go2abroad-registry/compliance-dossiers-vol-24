Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an International Company under the International Companies Act 1987. The subscribed capital is stated in the Memorandum, commonly USD 1.00 to USD 100.00; no escrow account or capital deposit is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue (MoR), Government of Samoa, Apia — official web portal at www.revenue.gov.ws; e-filing, TIN registration and VAT returns are transacted through the Mini-MOR online portal operated by the Commissioner of Revenue under the Tax Administration Act 2015.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Samoa International Company (IC): (1) a licensed registered agent incorporates the IC with the Samoa International Financial Services Authority (SIFSA) under the International Companies Act 1987 and obtains the Certificate of Incorporation, constitution and registered office; (2) the agent submits the SIFSA incorporation pack plus the TIN application (Form TIN-01, with passport copies and proof of address for shareholders and directors) to the Ministry of Revenue via the Mini-MOR portal or in person; (3) the Commissioner registers the IC and issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN), and, where the IC elects voluntary VAT registration for zero-rating of offshore services, a VAT registration certificate; (4) the foreign IC is not required to maintain a physical office, appoint local directors, or file Samoa tax returns for exempt income, but must keep its SIFSA license current and inform MoR of changes to registered agent, registered office, name or structure.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Samoa IC is an entity-level legal person under the International Companies Act 1987, but for income tax and VAT purposes it is a statutorily exempt person — while it does not carry on business in Samoa it is outside the 27% corporate income tax, outside VAT on offshore receipts, and free of dividend/interest/royalty withholding taxes and stamp duty. The classification is territorial: any Samoa-sourced trading, local payroll for Samoa-resident call center agents, or supply to a Samoa-resident buyer in the BPO value chain re-subjects the IC to full Samoa taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Samoa IC is an entity-level legal person under the International Companies Act 1987, but for income tax and VAT purposes it is a statutorily exempt person — while it does not carry on business in Samoa it is outside the 27% corporate income tax, outside VAT on offshore receipts, and free of dividend/interest/royalty withholding taxes and stamp duty. The classification is territorial: any Samoa-sourced trading, local payroll for Samoa-resident call center agents, or supply to a Samoa-resident buyer in the BPO value chain re-subjects the IC to full Samoa taxation. Pass-through treatment is not available; shareholders are treated as separate persons.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Samoa. The relevant statutory elections are: (1) International Companies Act 1987 conversion election — an IC may elect, by special resolution, to convert to a domestic company (opting into Samoa tax) and a domestic company may elect to convert into an IC (acquiring tax-exempt status), both subject to SIFSA approval; (2) Income Tax Act 2015 election for resident entities to be treated as fiscally transparent partnerships or trusts where the arrangement is genuine and the Commissioner consents; (3) elective voluntary VAT registration for offshore service providers below the SAT 100,000 threshold, which is the operative election used by remote BPO ICs to access zero-rating; (4) no hybrid mismatch election is available to ICs, and Samoa CFC/transfer-pricing rules apply only to Samoan-resident shareholders or connected domestic parties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 100,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (Samoan tala); mandatory registration for persons making or intending to make taxable supplies above SAT 100,000 in Samoa, voluntary registration below the threshold; an offshore BPO IC whose call-center and tele-sales services are performed for and consumed by non-residents has no Samoa taxable supply nexus and is not registrable except by voluntary election.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating applies to: (a) goods physically exported from Samoa under customs control; and (b) services supplied to a recipient who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and not present in Samoa when the service is performed (international/offshore services), including remote multilingual inbound helpdesk and outbound tele-sales rendered by a Samoa IC to foreign principals under a cross-border BPO agreement. Eligibility conditions: the IC must be a VAT-registered person (or elect voluntary registration), must invoice at 0%, must contract, deliver and invoice in a foreign currency, must evidence that the service is not for consumption in Samoa, and must repatriate the foreign-currency consideration within the statutory period; services consumed by a Samoa-resident entity, call monitoring performed on behalf of a domestic client, or agent payroll sourced inside Samoa will taint the zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World electronic export lodgment and customs endorsement) for any physical deliverables, media or equipment accompanying the services
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing shipment from a Samoa port or airport to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Master Services Agreement, work orders and call detail records (CDRs) demonstrating that the helpdesk/tele-sales service was performed for and delivered to the non-resident principal
  • Zero-rated tax invoices and a supplier export register reconciling each invoice to its dispatch, performance and remittance documentation

No real-time continuous-control e-invoicing or digital fiscal stamping mandate exists in Samoa as of the 2025 tax year. The binding requirements are: (i) registration, return filing and payment via the Mini-MOR e-government portal; (ii) issuance of a statutory tax invoice (supplier and recipient TINs, invoice date/number, description, VAT-exclusive price, VAT or 0% rate, and VAT amount); (iii) retention of underlying electronic records, CDR logs and bank remittance evidence for audit; and (iv) customs electronic export declarations via ASYCUDA World where physical goods movement is involved. The Minister of Revenue may by notice mandate certified digital invoicing or a clearance/authorization process for specific sectors, but none has been gazetted for the offshore BPO industry.

The Commissioner may verify zero-rated claims against ASYCUDA export records, bank statements, customs exit confirmations, subscriber CDRs and the BPO agent payroll. Failure to substantiate export/offshore status or to repatriate proceeds within 180 days reverses the 0% rate to 15% VAT; the resulting VAT shortfall attracts statutory interest plus a shortfall penalty of 50% of the tax avoided (20% where the failure was negligent and not intentional or reckless), and a 100% evasion penalty with criminal prosecution where fraud or deliberate falsification is established: on conviction a body corporate is liable to a fine up to SAT 200,000 and an individual to a fine up to SAT 100,000 or imprisonment up to two years, or both. Retrospective clawbacks can extend to net input VAT refunds previously paid and, for an IC, may trigger SIFSA decertification and loss of the International Company tax exemption where the audit establishes that an unapproved domestic activity was carried on.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is convertible for current-account transactions but is not freely deliverable offshore; resident capital-account transfers require CBS approval or execution through an authorized dealer. An International Company is a non-resident statutory construct: it must transact exclusively in foreign currency with non-resident counterparties and is effectively outside the CBS surrender and approval regime for offshore transactions. Dividend, capital, and profit repatriation from the IC's foreign-currency account is unrestricted provided the funds remain offshore; the practical hurdle is WST conversion for local statutory costs (registered agent, government fees, audit), which must be executed through a Samoan authorized dealer. The IC must not derive WST revenue from Samoan residents or it risks losing its non-resident status and triggering full exchange-control obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters under the Exchange Control Regulations, export proceeds must be received within six months of the date of export (services: within six months of the invoice date), and 100% of the foreign currency received must be offered for sale to an authorized dealer within two months of receipt, unless the CBS has approved a foreign-currency retention limit. Offshore International Companies are statutorily exempt from the surrender regime in respect of foreign-currency transactions with non-residents; therefore, BPO service fees invoiced in USD to offshore clients may be retained, not converted into WST, and may be freely deployed for offshore payroll and vendor settlements, provided the underlying contracts are with non-residents and the receipts are credited to the IC's designated USD account at a licensed Samoan bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Falepuna, Apia, established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984. CBS is the Exchange Control Administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977, and is the licensing and AML/CFT supervisor under the Financial Institutions Act 1996 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2007 (as amended). Corporate registration of the International Company (IC) is administered by the Registrar of International Companies within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour under the International Companies Act 1987 (ICA 1987).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for the Samoa-incorporated IC itself. Samoa has no digital-EMI or e-money licensing regime, and fintech platforms including Wise Business do not accept companies incorporated in Samoa as accountholders. The target rail 'Wise_B2B' is viable only as an inbound collection channel: BPO clients can initiate Wise payments to the IC's USD account at a Samoan bank using a unique payment reference. Outbound Wise/B2B disbursement capability would require a separately incorporated operating or treasury entity in a supported jurisdiction (e.g., New Zealand or Hong Kong), which introduces substance, transfer-pricing, and exchange-control exposure unless the Samoa IC is properly remunerated at arm's length with full intercompany documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the IC's USD account at ANZ Samoa or BSP Samoa; Wise B2B may be used if the subscribing shareholder is based in a Wise-supported jurisdiction. Payment fields: Field 23E 'INSD' and Field 70 must read '//INV/' followed by 'Share capital subscription of [N] shares per Board Resolution dated [DDMMMYYYY]', or '//INTC/' for a shareholder loan. Purpose codes: CAPT for capital injection; INTC for intercompany loan. No CBS approval is required for a foreign-currency credit to the IC's non-resident account, but the bank's compliance pack must include the board resolution, return of allotment filed with the Registrar of International Companies, updated share register, and a source-of-funds declaration for amounts above USD 100,000. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (standard resident company rate under the Income Tax Act 2015 (Samoa), as amended)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% final withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident Samoan company to non-resident shareholders (Income Tax Act 2015). International Companies: 0% — distributions by an exempt IC to non-resident shareholders are outside the Samoan WHT net.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 2,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions in a tax year (advisory threshold; Samoa has no formal Local File / Master File legislation, but the Income Tax Act 2015 arm's length rule and the OECD TP Guidelines are applied on audit; ICs with exclusively offshore-sourced income are outside the documentation obligation)

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations. Substance is nevertheless required to preserve the IC exemption and treaty access: (i) a licensed registered agent and Apia registered office (ICA 1987); (ii) board meetings or written resolutions minuted in Samoa; (iii) local accounting records reflecting the BPO's real staffing, billing and client contracts; and (iv) genuine offshore management. A pure booking-company structure with zero employees and no governance will be treated as a sham and reclassified as resident, exposing all profits to 27% CIT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Income Tax Act 2015 & Tax Administration Act 2012

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax, 50% uplift for deliberate misstatement, interest at 10% per annum, and criminal fine not exceeding WST 100,000; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing: directors who signed false returns are personally and jointly liable for the underpaid tax; travel objection notices may be issued preventing departure from Samoa until a tax clearance certificate is settled.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement under International Companies Act 1987

Statutory sanction: Strike-off and deregistration by the Registrar, fine up to WST 20,000, and loss of the ICA tax exemption with retroactive 27% CIT on accumulated profits.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for the company's unpaid taxes and third-party debts if the IC was a mere façade; disqualification from managing a company for up to 5 years.
AML/CFT Breach — Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (as amended) and UBO Filing Obligations

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WST 500,000 per breach for the company; the responsible officer faces a further fine up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; freezing of funds and revocation of the registered agent licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally indictable where wilful blindness or laundering of proceeds through the BPO payment rails is proven; civil confiscation and asset forfeiture apply.
Profiteering from Tax Havens — Treaty Override and Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of treaty relief and WHT exemption; recharacterisation of payments as taxable dividends at 15% WHT plus penalties of 25% and interest at 10% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors who structured the conduit arrangements face personal assessment for unpaid WHT, breach of directors' duties claims by creditors, and civil restitution orders.
