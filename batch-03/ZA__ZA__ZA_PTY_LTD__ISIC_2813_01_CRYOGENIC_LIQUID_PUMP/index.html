Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification election is available in South Africa. Statutory elections include: (1) Small Business Corporation status under Section 12E for qualifying private companies whose shareholders are individual natural persons and gross income ≤ ZAR 20 million; (2) Micro Business turnover tax election under Section 48 of the Income Tax Act where turnover ≤ ZAR 1 million; (3) VAT group election under Section 50 of the VAT Act for allowable grouping of domestic VAT vendors; (4) Section 24J elections for debt instruments. Hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 23M-23N of the Income Tax Act are mandatory and not electable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 mandatory registration threshold on taxable supplies over any 12-month period; ZAR 50,000 voluntary registration threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act if supplied to a non-resident and physically removed from South Africa to a foreign destination by the supplier or on the supplier's instructions in the ordinary course of the sale, with proof of export obtained and retained within 30 days of supply (or extended period approved by SARS). Services are zero-rated under Section 11(2)(l) where supplied to a non-resident and not utilized in South Africa. The zero-rating is unavailable for supplies to SACU residents where the goods are retained in the SACU customs area unless removed further to a non-SACU destination prior to consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged at the South African port of exit
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement out of South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/MT202 confirming foreign payment
  • Commercial Invoice and tax invoice
  • Packing List
  • Export permit or ITAC license if goods are controlled or restricted
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential duty rates are claimed)
  • Proof of non-resident status of customer (e.g. VAT registration certificate or contractor certificate)

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls. VAT vendors must file returns through SARS eFiling and issue substantive tax invoices; SARS accepts both paper and electronic tax invoices in XML or PDF. SARS is progressively implementing e-invoicing interoperability standards, but there is no API-based real-time clearance requirement at this stage.

SARS may raise additional assessments within six years for material non-disclosure and without time limit for fraud or misrepresentation; understatement penalties may extend to 200% of the tax shortfall under Section 223 of the Tax Administration Act; interest runs at the prescribed rate from the date the VAT refund is determined to be unpaid. Unsupported or fraudulent zero ratings are subject to full VAT clawback, revised assessments, potential disqualification from VAT registration, and criminal liability for directors under the Tax Administration Act and the Value-Added Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) operates as a managed-float currency with full current-account convertibility: trade settlements, service fees, royalties, dividends and branch remittances may be paid without prior SARB approval, subject to documentation and reporting through an authorised dealer (AD). Capital-account transactions remain regulated: inward direct investment (equity subscription) is freely receivable through an AD, but foreign shareholder loans, cross-border security, and onward capital transfers may require FinSurv approval or AD-delegated oversight. Profit remittance hurdles are practical: ordinary dividends to non-resident shareholders are permitted without exchange-control approval but attract a 20% Dividends Tax at source; repayment of intercompany loans and exit capital must be traced to inward-funding certificates, CIPC beneficial-ownership filings and FICA-verified source-of-funds documentation. In essence, ZAR is convertible for bona fide current-account purposes, with capital-account flows managed through the AD channel rather than open-ended offshore convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated in full into the South African banking system within 180 days from the earlier of the shipment date or the date of exportation, in accordance with the Currency and Exchanges Act and FinSurv Manuals; extensions are granted only by the authorised dealer for bona fide commercial reasons (e.g., consignment exports or extended payment terms). The mandatory surrender ratio is effectively 100%: every export receipt must be brought into the AD banking channel and either converted to ZAR at the market rate or credited to the exporter's Foreign Currency Account (FCA); there is no partial-conversion tolerance outside the FCA mechanism. The exporter must file the SAD500 customs declaration, retain the commercial invoice, Bill of Lading, proof of payment and bank credit entry, and present these to the AD upon demand; non-compliance triggers FinSurv penalty action, suspension of preferential AD facilities and potential exchange-control offence exposure.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), as the exchange-control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, with daily administration delegated to authorised dealers (tier-1 commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Substantive legal constraints prevent offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) from acting as the primary bank or authorised dealer for this (Pty) Ltd: they are not appointed ADs under the Exchange Control Regulations, cannot operate South African ZAR clearing, cannot issue FCA accounts, and are not members of the SAMOS RTGS system. Such platforms are treated as third-party payment providers requiring registration under the National Payment System Act, 1998 if they process payments in South Africa; without a local AD partnership, any inbound or outbound ZAR payment is non-compliant. Viability rating for an ISIC 2813 exporter with LC/SWIFT trade rails is LOW-MEDIUM: acceptable as a supplementary collections tool (distributor deposits, small supplier payments routed via SWIFT), provided the EMI's affiliate is licensed in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction and the company maintains the primary operating and FCA relationship with a tier-1 South African bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder's equity subscription must be routed exclusively through a South African authorised dealer: (i) the company passes a board resolution authorising the share issue and opens an inward-capital escrow or operational account; (ii) the shareholder remits USD, EUR or GBP via SWIFT MT103 to that AD account, citing the SARB balance-of-payments purpose code for 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' (inward FDI); (iii) the AD credits the proceeds to the company's Foreign Currency Account or converts to ZAR at the prevailing market rate; (iv) the AD issues a Certificate of Receipt of Inward Funds (the inward-funding certificate) evidencing the original capital contribution - this certificate is the mandatory prerequisite for all future dividend distributions, loan repayments and capital repatriation; (v) where the inward amount exceeds the AD's delegated limits or is combined with a non-resident shareholder loan above the threshold, FinSurv prior approval is required before crediting; and (vi) all subscription monies must be sourced from the shareholder's own bank account, with source-of-funds evidence captured on the FICA file, and the CIPC share register updated before remittance is accepted.Outbound disbursements for cryogenic-pump component imports and logistics are cleared through the following rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for open-account and supplier payments, executed by the AD only after the company presents the proforma invoice, purchase order, and the SAD500 customs declaration or proof of importation; (ii) commercial irrevocable Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) opened through the AD's trade-finance desk for high-value equipment, with a documentary pack comprising the commercial invoice, insurance certificate, Bill of Lading or airway bill, certificate of origin and inspection certificate; (iii) documentary collections (documents against payment or documents against acceptance) as a secondary rail for established suppliers; (iv) intracompany service and royalty disbursements via MT103 with the relevant BoP service code and transfer-pricing documentation attached; and (v) domestic ZAR legs cleared through SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement) as the national RTGS system. The AD will enforce the 180-day import-settlement rule and may require extended-payment approvals where the supply chain demands longer terms.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) concluded via a South African authorised dealer for committed foreign-currency trade payables and receivables; settlement is in ZAR on maturity and the underlying exposure must be evidenced by invoices, contracts or L/Cs.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not a permissible primary hedge for a resident (Pty) Ltd in the onshore market because the ZAR is a deliverable currency and offshore NDF books are restricted; equivalent economic cover is available through an onshore FEC with the AD.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) and plain-vanilla cross-currency swaps (CCS) for medium-term and long-term foreign-currency debt or intercompany loan exposures, with the AD or FinSurv approving the underlying capital-account transaction.Foreign-currency options (European-style) and zero-cost collar structures purchased from an authorised dealer for hedging anticipated import bids on LNG and cryogenic plant and equipment, with maturity matching the tender pipeline.FEC rollover and early-close facilities at the authorised dealer's prevailing market rates, documented as amendments to the original forward cover contract.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation is required when the aggregate value of cross-border affected transactions with connected persons exceeds ZAR 100,000,000 per tax year; Master File is required when the aggregated group cross-border related-party transactions exceed ZAR 1,000,000,000. Below these thresholds, contemporaneous documentation is still recommended to demonstrate arm’s-length pricing and to mitigate penalties.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as found in some offshore jurisdictions. However, the Income Tax Act, common law substance-over-form, and DTA beneficial ownership provisions impose an effective substance requirement. A company must maintain real business activity — such as office premises, payroll, decision-making, and supply-chain involvement — in South Africa. Without economic substance, tax avoidance transaction rules (s80A–s80L) or treaty shopping provisions may disallow benefits, and the company can be treated as a shell resulting in penalties and assessed tax adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under section 234 of the Tax Administration Act (No. 28 of 2011), a person convicted of tax evasion is liable to a fine of up to ZAR 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years. In addition, the full tax underpaid plus 200% understatement penalty may be assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise or collude in tax evasion could face joint and several liability for unpaid tax plus penalties. Criminal conviction can result in direct imprisonment of up to 10 years, travel bans, and disqualification from managing companies.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (s80A–s80L), SARS may disregard the company as a sham and impose a 75% understatement penalty. The company may be assessed as a domestic company on deemed worldwide income, and treaty benefits may be denied.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties arising from a re-characterised sham transaction (s80L and s160 of TAA). Personal liability for civil restitution can extend to all amounts unpaid, plus interest. Courts may issue a punitive costs order.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) imposes fines of up to ZAR 100 million or imprisonment up to 15 years for money laundering. The Prevention of Organised Crime Act (POCA) allows asset forfeiture. General banking license infractions can lead to payroll freezing.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate or fail to report suspicious transactions face criminal liability, fines, and imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 (s77) allowing restitution claims against directors for future liabilities.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the taxable income to an arm’s-length value, impose an additional tax of up to 100% of the adjustment, and levy an understatement penalty of 50% for substantial mispricing. Contemporaneous documentation missing attracts a uniform administrative penalty of up to ZAR 2,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation may be personally liable for the additional tax and penalty if they negligently or knowingly misrepresented facts. Personal liability can lead to sequestration of personal assets.
Dividend Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: The company is liable for the unwithheld amount, plus a 10% late payment penalty, and interest at the prescribed rate. In addition, SARS may recover the unpaid amount from the directors as an ordinary debt.

Director personal liability: Under s143(1) of the Tax Administration Act, directors are jointly and severally liable for the company’s unpaid dividend withholding tax if the company fails to meet its obligation. Civil recovery includes travel bans, attachment of personal bank accounts, and possible contempt of court imprisonment.
