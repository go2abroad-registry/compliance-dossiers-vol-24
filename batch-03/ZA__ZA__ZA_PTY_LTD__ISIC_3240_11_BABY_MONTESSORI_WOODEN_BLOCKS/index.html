Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; customs clearance portal: eCleg at https://ecleg.sars.gov.za. SARS is the sole national tax and customs authority for income tax, VAT, customs and excise, with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) handling incorporation pre-registration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned South African (Pty) Ltd: (1) incorporate with the CIPC, which immediately transmits a unique company registration number to SARS; (2) SARS automatically issues an income tax reference number to the new company; (3) the directors must activate the SARS eFiling profile and submit form RAV01 to register for VAT, PAYE, SDL, UIF and Customs Client status; (4) the company must appoint a public officer who is ordinarily resident in South Africa (Tax Administration Act 28/2011, s.26), failing which refunds and assessments are blocked; (5) VAT registration is compulsory once taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months (voluntary from ZAR 50,000). For a non-resident external company, the same RAV01 route is used after CIPC external company registration, supported by apostilled constitutional documents and proof of a local registered office; no court or ministerial approval is needed.

For a non-resident external company, the same RAV01 route is used after CIPC external company registration, supported by apostilled constitutional documents and proof of a local registered office; no court or ministerial approval is needed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South African law has no US-style check-the-box election: every incorporated (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed at entity level at 27% corporate income tax on worldwide income (rates applicable to years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023). Statutory elections that remain available include: (i) voluntary VAT vendor registration under s.23(2) of VAT Act 89/1991 where taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000; (ii) VAT grouping election under s.50(1) for related companies of which one holds at least 80% shares; (iii) Headquarter Company election under s.9I of the Income Tax Act, requiring prior SARS approval and yielding exemption for foreign-held dividends, interest and royalties; (iv) CFC participation exemption elections under ss.9D and 10(1)(h) for outbound foreign dividends; and (v) deemed advance tax rulings are available to confirm zero-rating of export supplies. Hybrid mismatch instruments are instead subject to mandatory anti-hybrid disallowance rules (ss.8E, 8EA, 8F and 8FA), with no elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory registration threshold in any consecutive 12-month period; ZAR 50,000 voluntary registration threshold; foreign suppliers of electronic services must register without regard to the threshold from their first month of exceeding ZAR 50,000 in annual taxable supply to South African recipients

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s.11(1)(a)-(e) of the VAT Act to the supply of movable goods (including Montessori beechwood blocks) where: (i) the supplier is a registered vendor; (ii) the goods are supplied to a non-resident (or to a resident for export under an export consignment); (iii) the goods are physically removed from South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply or within any written extension granted by SARS; (iv) the vendor retains prescribed proof of export; and (v) ownership in the goods passes to the recipient. Services are zero-rated only where physically rendered outside South Africa (s.11(2)(a)); services connected to the goods while in South Africa, or services rendered to residents, cannot be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500) submitted through SARS eCleg with the approved clearance/export entry number
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical removal of goods from South Africa to the non-resident customer's destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-issued certificate of receipt of foreign currency evidencing collection of export proceeds within the SARB financial-surveillance deadline
  • Zero-rated VAT tax invoice bearing an export endorsement and containing all s.20(1) VAT Act content fields
  • Packing list, delivery note, freight and insurance documents evidencing delivery at the non-resident's address
  • Documentary proof of the recipient's non-resident status, such as export order, contract or foreign address records

South Africa does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or API-based invoice stamping. VAT returns are filed electronically via SARS eFiling by the 25th day after the tax period; customs export declarations are cleared through the eCleg portal (SAD 500 declaration) with near-real-time electronic border release, but without an invoice-by-invoice government clearance. Tax invoices must meet the minimum content list in s.20(1) of the VAT Act and be issued within 21 days of the time of supply; s.9(1) applies the earlier of invoice date or payment date as time of supply. Since 2023, SARS promotes voluntary PEPPOL-compliant e-invoicing for procurement and business transactions; the scheme is available but not yet mandated, so no live fiscal stamp is required for exporting companies.

SARS will restore output VAT at 15% on any supply for which the required export documentation is incomplete or false, and may retrospectively reassess all related periods under the normal-assessment rules. Consequences include: (i) additional VAT of 15% on the deemed local supply; (ii) fixed 10% penalty under s.39 of the VAT Act for default without reasonable excuse; (iii) behaviour-based understatement penalties of 0% to 125% of the shortfall under ss.222-223 of the Tax Administration Act 28/2011, with the 125% tier reserved for intentional tax evasion; (iv) interest at the prescribed rate under s.187 of the Tax Administration Act (currently repo rate + 3%); (v) denial of input credits and future refunds pending compliance; and (vi) criminal prosecution for fraud under the VAT Act and Tax Administration Act with fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years in aggravated cases. SARS operates a dedicated refund-verification unit that independently validates each export refund against customs data, banking records and shipping manifests before payment, and will claw back refunds paid if later found to be unsupported.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is convertible for all current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services, and for genuine dividend, interest, royalty and management fee remittances, subject to SARS tax clearance on the recipient and withholding tax to be effected at the South African source. Capital and financial account transactions, including inbound equity investment and cross-border shareholder loans, require reporting to FinSurv through an authorised dealer, but are generally approved provided the company is in good standing, the CIPC share register is up to date, and no prohibited funding source is involved. Profit distributions must observe the company's solvency and liquidity test under the Companies Act, 2008 and the Dividends Tax rate of 20% is imposed at source on shareholders; no general blocked-profit regime exists for bona fide non-resident shareholders. The main practical hurdle for remittance is the submission of the final dividend declaration, a solvency and liquidity certificate, and a SARB/SARS compliance confirmation to the paying bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment. Unless a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or retention allowance has been approved by FinSurv or the authorised dealer, all such proceeds must be surrendered into the South African banking system and credited to the exporter's account in rand. In substance, the mandatory conversion ratio is 100% for unauthorised non-FCA arrangements, whereas an FCA permits controlled retention of the export currency for subsequent approved trade payments. The exporter must submit the original customs export declaration and proof of receipt to the authorised dealer; failure to repatriate within 180 days constitutes an exchange-control offence and may result in administrative penalties, suspension of access to trade facilitation systems, and criminal liability under the Currency and Exchanges Act.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), in terms of the Currency and Exchanges Act, 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, administered through authorised dealers (commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low, unless the EMI is a fully licenced South African bank or an authorised dealer in foreign exchange. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Mercury, or Payoneer cannot operate as the primary clearing rail for export proceeds because South African exchange control requires all trade receipts to be repatriated through an authorised dealer. They may be used only for ancillary pre-funding or marketing disbursements, never as the main revenue channel; routing export proceeds to an unrestricted offshore EMI would be viewed as a failure to repatriate and would trigger FinSurv penalties. Domestic digital adjuncts offered by licenced banks, including TymeBank as a licenced bank, may support retail-facing disbursement rails if they fully comply with FICA and use the bank's authorised-dealer framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Exchange-control authorisation is not required for a non-resident shareholder subscribing to ordinary shares in a South African proprietary limited company, but the authorised dealer must receive from the shareholder a completed non-resident declaration on SARB Form B2, together with the CIPC-allotted share certificate. The remitting bank must use a SWIFT MT103 with a clearly designated purpose code for direct investment, e.g. IMF Balance of Payments code for acquisition of equity capital in a South African resident enterprise. The inward funds are credited to a capital escrow account maintained by the authorised dealer; the company then allots shares within 30 days and files the return of allotment with CIPC. Any shareholder loan advanced separately must be evidenced by a formal loan agreement and reported on Form B1; the company's bankers must also verify that the capital injection does not pass through an unrestricted shell or personal wallet account.For imported beechwood raw material and non-toxic finishes, use SWIFT MT103 payment orders for telegraphic-transfer settlement, denominated in USD or EUR, with the supplier invoice, customs entry, and bill of lading submitted to the paying bank. For higher-value or documentary trade, use irrevocable commercial letters of credit transmitted via SWIFT MT700, with payment against presentation of shipping, inspection, and compliance certificates (EN71, ASTM F963, and the certificate of origin). All outbound trade payments must be declared on the importer's exchange-control returns and reconciled against the company's export proceeds by the authorised dealer. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold applies to the s31 connected-person rule; SARS administrative practice (Practice Note 7, 2023 TP Guide) uses ZAR 5,000,000 in annual cross-border connected transactions as the audit trigger for contemporaneous Local File documentation; a full Master File/Local File suite is expected where group consolidated revenue reaches ZAR 10 billion (CbC reporting threshold); smaller groups must still retain contemporaneous support for every connected transaction regardless of amount.

Economic substance scope: South Africa enforces substance through four cumulative doctrines: (1) residence: central management and place of effective management (POEM) in South Africa makes the company tax-resident and fully taxable on worldwide income; (2) Controlled Foreign Company rules (s9D) attribute passive income of offshore subsidiaries to South African shareholders, with participation exemption for genuine active manufacturing income; (3) s31 TP rules require functional, asset and risk analysis, rejecting loosely priced cross-border flows lacking actual employees and value-adding functions; (4) GAAR (s80A-L) invalidates arrangements where the sole or main purpose is tax avoidance without a real business purpose. The ZA_PTY_LTD's onshore beechwood turning, warehousing, quality control, and Montessori certification workforce constitute adequate economic substance; a letterbox company holding only Amazon and Stripe accounts would fail the substance doctrine, be denied treaty benefits, trigger 20% dividends WHT re-characterization, and face CFC attribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false statements

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 150% (intentional evasion) plus 100% additional tax on any TP adjustment; criminal conviction: fine equal to up to three times the tax amount evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years (s235 TAA; s104 Income Tax Act); interest runs from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors and those controlling company finances are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax, penalties and interest if they knowingly failed to pay (s159, s179 TAA); SARS may obtain a personal tax judgment, a domestic travel ban/withdrawal of a passport, liquidation of personal assets, and, upon fraud findings, imprisonment of responsible officers.
Transfer pricing and record-keeping failure

Statutory sanction: Adjustment by SARS to arm's length with 100% additional tax (s31(3) ITA), reduced to 50% only where adequate contemporaneous documentation exists; understatement penalty of 10%–150% may apply cumulatively; interest and 10% late-payment penalty.

Director personal liability: False TP documentation exposes directors to s235 criminal liability for false statements and to s159 TAA personal liability for the additional tax; a director signing the IRP6 estimate based on a non-arm's length position may face an estimation penalty and personal claim by SARS.
Shell company and abusive DTA/treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR voiding of the arrangement (s80A-L); re-characterization of distributions as dividends with 20% WHT; denial of dividend relief under LOB/PPT clauses; attribution of CFC passive income to South African shareholders under s9D; administrative penalties up to 150% plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for tax debts of a company that is a sham or facade (substance-over-form piercing), and for reckless trading under s77 Companies Act 71/2008, including civil restitution of amounts disbursed; SARS may seek freezing orders and residential/flexible travel bans.
Anti-money laundering and trade-based verification (FICA)

Statutory sanction: FIC Act administrative fines up to ZAR 100,000,000 for accountable institutions (and up to 15 years' imprisonment for natural persons for money laundering); SARS may also impose interpretations of criminal actions in respect of false trade documentation; forfeiture of the toys/equipment.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and company secretaries face personal fines under the FIC Act and potential imprisonment; directors' inability to demonstrate KYC/BO diligence creates criminal exposure and triggers cross-border travel restrictions while investigations are pending.
Failure to file and pay / provisional under-estimation

Statutory sanction: Monthly administrative penalties (ZAR 250–ZAR 16,000 per month per return, up to 35 months); 10% late-payment charge; estimation penalties of 10% or 20% of the difference (s162 TAA); prescribed-rate interest; SARS debt collection, instalment agreements requiring personal sureties, and forced liquidation of the Pty Ltd.

Director personal liability: Directors who were aware of the company's failure to pay taxes are personally liable under s159 TAA for all unpaid tax and interest; shareholders who extracted dividends while VAT/PAYE/CIT were unpaid are jointly liable under the Companies Act dividend solvency test and may face claw-back actions by the liquidator.
