Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — national tax and customs administration governing corporate income tax, VAT, customs duties and transfer pricing; SARS eFiling portal at www.sarsefiling.co.za, with headquarters at Lehae la SARS, 299 Bronkhorst Street, Brooklyn, Pretoria.

E-government filing portal

A South African (Pty) Ltd obtains its income tax reference number automatically through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC)–SARS integration at incorporation. Where separate activation is required, the appointed public officer must file the SARS eFiling registration application (Form REG-01 for income tax, Form VAT 101 for VAT), annexing the CIPC Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of registered office address, director identification copies and bank letterhead evidence of the company operating account. Compulsory VAT registration must be applied for within 21 days of exceeding, or reasonably expecting to exceed, the ZAR 1,000,000 taxable-supply threshold in any 12-month period; voluntary VAT registration is available at ZAR 50,000 of annual taxable turnover. SARS issues a unique 10-digit income tax reference number and, upon VAT approval, a distinct VAT vendor number, with the company required to maintain a registered public officer for all statutory correspondence.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a resident South African (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a separate legal entity at the 27% corporate income tax rate (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023), with worldwide income subject to tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a resident South African (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a separate legal entity at the 27% corporate income tax rate (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023), with worldwide income subject to tax. South African law provides no general pass-through entity classification comparable to a US LLC; limited liability companies are inherently taxable persons. Territorial scope is limited only in the case of non-resident companies carrying on business through a South African permanent establishment, where source-based taxation applies. A company incorporated in South Africa is automatically resident for tax purposes regardless of shareholder nationality.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box or elective pass-through classification regime; entity taxation status is fixed by statute and cannot be overridden by taxpayer election. Available statutory elections of practical relevance to an exporting manufacturer include: (i) VAT group registration under s50 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991, allowing linked entities to file a consolidated VAT return and apply zero-rated export treatment on an aggregated basis; (ii) assessed-loss group relief elections under s20(1)(aA) of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, subject to acquisition of at least 70% of the target shares for at least one full year; (iii) the headquarter-company regime under s9I of the Income Tax Act, electable subject to minimum qualifying service expenditure, shareholding and FDI criteria; (iv) corporate restructure rollover elections under s41–s47 of the Income Tax Act, including the s42 asset-for-share and s45 intra-group transaction elections; and (v) apportionment-basis elections for mixed-use expenditure. Hybrid mismatch and BEPS-aligned rules (including the s8E hybrid equity and s8F hybrid debt instrument provisions) apply automatically, are not elective, and limit deductions to the extent that mismatch outcomes arise from cross-border or domestic arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies made during the preceding 12 months, or reasonably expected to be made in the following 12 months. Voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12-month period. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African residents are separately captured under the ZAR 1,000,000 threshold for foreign vendor registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, the supply of movable tangible goods — including FPV racing drones, carbon-fiber airframes, brushless motors and component kits — qualifies for zero rating where the South African vendor supplies the goods in the course of exportation, the goods are physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply, and the vendor retains the prescribed documentary proof of export. Offshore services supplied to non-residents, such as remote tuning, telemetry analysis or bespoke design services, may qualify for zero rating under s11(2)(k)/s11(2)(l) only where the services are physically rendered outside South Africa or the benefit and use thereof is entirely outside the Republic. Local retail sales to South African consumers are standard-rated at 15%; the zero rate does not apply merely because payment is received in foreign currency or the buyer is a non-resident. The vendor must issue a fully compliant s20(4) tax invoice expressly stating '0% VAT — export supply' and retain the export evidence in its records.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD500 / Bill of Entry) lodged with SARS Customs, reflecting the FPV drones and components, applicable tariff classification, export value and exporter registration details
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing physical departure of the consignment from a South African port or airport and transfer to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice demonstrating receipt of the export payment in foreign currency and full repatriation to a South African bank account within the prescribed period
  • Tax invoice issued under s20(4) of the VAT Act stating the zero rate and containing vendor and buyer identification, invoice number, date, description, quantity, unit price, consideration and the zero-rated designation

South Africa operates no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital-stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) system. Electronic invoices are legally valid when compliant with the content requirements of s20(4) of the VAT Act, requiring the vendor name, trading address and VAT number, the recipient name, address and VAT/identity number, a serialized invoice number, invoice date, description and quantity of goods, unit price and consideration, and either the VAT amount or an express statement that the supply is zero-rated. VAT returns and customs declarations are submitted electronically through SARS eFiling and the SARS modern electronic data systems; the SARS Modernisation Programme anticipates future phased mandatory structured e-invoicing and enhanced automated transactional clearance, but no API-based pre-clearance or per-invoice approval is currently law.

SARS may require production of the complete export documentation bundle within 21 days of a verification request. Where the goods are not exported within 90 days or the documentary trail is incomplete, the zero-rated treatment is withdrawn and output VAT of 15% is assessed with interest from the original due date. The Tax Administration Act No. 28 of 2011 (s223 Table 3) imposes understatement penalties of 25% to 150% of the shortfall depending on behavior, with 150% available for intentional tax evasion and 100% for gross negligence; sections 59 and 60 of the VAT Act authorize additional penalty taxes and per-day non-compliance penalties. Deliberate fraudulent export zero-rating exposes the public officer, directors and any assisting advisors to criminal prosecution under s235 of the Tax Administration Act and the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964, including imprisonment and fines, and the normal three-year assessment prescription period is extended without limitation where fraud, misrepresentation or non-disclosure is established (s99(2) of the TAA).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible on current account under a managed-float exchange-rate regime. Non-residents may subscribe for ordinary shares in a ZA (Pty) Ltd without prior FinSurv approval, provided the subscription price is paid in foreign currency through a South African authorised dealer using a compliant inward-investment reporting route. Dividends and profits may be remitted to non-resident shareholders without exchange-control pre-approval where the shares were acquired with non-resident capital, subject to (i) 20% dividends tax withholding (or a treaty-reduced rate), (ii) the company's SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) being valid, and (iii) the authorised dealer receiving the signed board dividend declaration, dividend statement, tax receipt, and shareholding records. Repatriation of loan capital, intercompany balances, and proceeds of share redemptions remains subject to FinSurv threshold limits and procedural approvals; there are no exchange-control restrictions on bona fide current imports (FPV drone components, brushless motors, carbon fibre, electronics), but all ZAR/foreign-currency conversion must pass through an authorised dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exports must be paid for by the 180th day after the date of shipment (the statutory export payment period). The full invoice value must be repatriated to South Africa and paid into the exporter's authorised-dealer account; there is no fixed percentage mandatory conversion into ZAR if the exporter has been granted a Foreign Currency Account (FCA), but absent such approval the dealer will convert the receipts into ZAR at the prevailing market rate. Any export proceeds retained offshore, any payment not received within 180 days, and any under-invoicing or over-invoicing are reportable exchange-control contraventions. The exporter must maintain a shipment-level ledger with SAD500 export declarations, air waybills, commercial invoices, proof of insurance, and SWIFT or aggregator receipts, and must notify the authorised dealer immediately if collection exceeds the 180-day period.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting as the national exchange-control authority through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations (Currency and Exchanges Act, Act 9 of 1933); day-to-day foreign-exchange and cross-border transaction administration is delegated to licensed authorised dealers (South African commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Viability rating: LOW as the sole or primary treasury layer; MEDIUM as an ancillary collection aggregator. South African exchange-control law recognises only authorised dealers as lawful channels for crossing the ZAR/foreign-currency boundary; offshore EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Revolut Business) are not authorised dealers, cannot open local ZAR clearing accounts, cannot issue South African IBANs, and cannot discharge the 180-day export-proceeds or FICA reporting obligations. PayPal South Africa and Stripe (through local or partner acquiring) may be used only as front-end e-commerce collection rails; the resulting ZAR or foreign-currency settlement must be swept into the authorised-dealer bank current account and reflected in the export ledger. The blueprint is therefore: tier-1 SA bank account for legal clearing, PayPal and Stripe as sales rails, and a single approved FCA (if high foreign-currency volumes justify it) for receivables retention.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: (1) execute the share-subscription agreement, board allotment resolution, and update CIPC share register and beneficial-ownership record; (2) instruct the non-resident shareholder to pay in USD, EUR, or GBP via SWIFT MT103 directly to the (Pty) Ltd ZAR current account held at the South African authorised dealer, with the narrative 'Share subscription – inward investment' and the share or ledger reference; (3) submit to the bank the certified KYC pack of the subscribing entity and its UBOs, the board resolution, Memorandum of Incorporation, subscription agreement, and CIPC updated register; (4) the authorised dealer will convert the foreign currency into ZAR at the market rate, record the transaction as a non-resident capital inflow under its FinSurv-mandated reporting, and issue an inward-investment acknowledgement stating the ZAR equivalent and share allocation; (5) retain the acknowledgement for future profit or dividend remittance and for the company's equity accounting records. Funds introduced through non-authorised-dealer rails (EMIs or PSPs) or held offshore beyond the permitted FCA are de facto exchange-control contraventions, even if later converted in South Africa.Payments to overseas drone-component suppliers (carbon fibre, brushless motors, FPV flight controllers, battery cells) must settle through the authorised dealer using SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer or open account) or, for larger contracts, a UCP600-compliant documentary Letter of Credit issued on the company's behalf. The bank file must comprise the pro-forma commercial invoice, purchase order, customs SAD500 or bill-of-entry (DA185) evidencing the import, transport documentation (bill of lading or air waybill), and the correct exchange-control purpose code for imported goods; the credit must name the ultimate beneficiary-supplier with no shell intermediaries unless fully risk-controlled. Because PayPal and Stripe are front-end collection rails, all ZAR balances generated by domestic or third-country e-commerce must first be swept into the tier-1 bank current account; nothing may be paid out to overseas suppliers directly from PSP or EMI balances in foreign currency without first passing the authorised-dealer compliance review and the 180-day export-reconciliation obligation.Deliverable forward exchange contracts (FECs) with an authorised dealer, matched to identified trade cash flowsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/ZAR for hedging currency risk where physical settlement is unavailableCross-currency swaps (CCS) for restructuring USD-denominated supplier liabilities into ZARFX options or collars approved by the bank for hedging documented transactional exposure on exports and imports under FinSurv-compliant documentation

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: LOW as the sole or primary treasury layer; MEDIUM as an ancillary collection aggregator. South African exchange-control law recognises only authorised dealers as lawful channels for crossing the ZAR/foreign-currency boundary; offshore EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Revolut Business) are not authorised dealers, cannot open local ZAR clearing accounts, cannot issue South African IBANs, and cannot discharge the 180-day export-proceeds or FICA reporting obligations. PayPal South Africa and Stripe (through local or partner acquiring) may be used only as front-end e-commerce collection rails; the resulting ZAR or foreign-currency settlement must be swept into the authorised-dealer bank current account and reflected in the export ledger. The blueprint is therefore: tier-1 SA bank account for legal clearing, PayPal and Stripe as sales rails, and a single approved FCA (if high foreign-currency volumes justify it) for receivables retention.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required where aggregate cross-border connected-person transactions exceed ZAR 100 million in a year of assessment (SARS TP Regulations, GN 1021 of 30 October 2017); below this threshold, contemporaneous arm's length records must still be retained; Country-by-Country Report due where consolidated group revenue is at least ZAR 10 billion; the TP return (CIR 414) is disclosed in the ITR14 Annexure C irrespective of the file threshold where any cross-border related-party transaction exists

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE or BVI; however, substance is heavily policed through (i) the general anti-avoidance rules of s80A-L and s103(1) of the Income Tax Act, which permit SARS to disregard arrangements lacking bona fide commercial substance, (ii) the s1 place-of-effective-management test for tax residence, (iii) the controlled foreign company attribution rules of s9D for passive income of low-tax foreign subsidiaries, and (iv) the OECD Principal Purpose Test embedded in all South African DTAs; a shell Pty Ltd with mailbox presence and no real employees, assets or decision-making in South Africa would be recharacterised, its losses denied and its DTA treaty access denied

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under TAA s235: a fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years; where conduct is fraudulent and deceptive, s236 imposes a fine and/or imprisonment of up to ten years; SARS concurrently imposes an understatement penalty of up to 150% of the shortfall (s223) plus statutory interest from the due date.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who cause or knowingly permit the default are jointly and severally liable under s77 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008; the corporate veil is pierced under s20(9) for unconscionable abuse; SARS may obtain travel bans, asset preservation orders and winding-up applications under TAA s184 and s194.
Shell Company / Impermissible Tax Avoidance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: SARS disregards or recharacterises the arrangement under s80A-L and s103(1), recomputes taxable income on the substance of the transaction, denies the intended tax benefits, and imposes an understatement penalty of 100%-150% plus interest; the DTA claim is denied through the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Courts lift the corporate veil where the entity is an alter ego for the principals; directors are personally liable for misrepresentations made to SARS; repatriation restrictions and delinquency orders may be sought against directors; failure to file CbCR declarations and TP returns adds separate criminal exposure.
AML / FIC Act Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FIC Act administrative penalties under the 2022 Amendment framework rising to ZAR 50 million per serious contravention for larger institutions, plus daily penalties for continuing default; money laundering under POCA s1 attracts a fine of up to ZAR 100 million and/or imprisonment of up to 30 years; wilful failure to file an RCT or report a suspicious transaction carries imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors with compliance oversight are personally named in FIC penalty orders; conviction for money laundering triggers automatic disqualification from acting as directors; SARS and the FIC may jointly apply for asset forfeiture and travel bans under POCA and TAA.
Transfer Pricing and Arm's Length Failure

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts taxable income under s31 to the arm's length price and applies a secondary adjustment deeming the adjusted amount to be an interest-bearing loan at the prescribed rate (s31(2)(b)); understatement penalties of 20% to 150% of the shortfall plus interest and late-payment penalties are imposed.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and tax practitioners who knew or ought reasonably to have known of unsubstantiated pricing are jointly assessed for the understatement penalty under TAA s223(3); directors who signed misleading transfer pricing certificates may face civil restitution and criminal sanction.
Dividend Withholding Tax Default

Statutory sanction: The declaring company is personally liable for the uncollected dividend withholding tax together with an additional penalty equal to 100% of the uncollected amount (s64N of the Income Tax Act read with TAA s223); interest runs from the dividend payment date; ANCP applies for non-submission of the required return.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the unlawful dividend distribution are personally liable under Companies Act s77(3)(b); where the company is deregistered or insolvent, SARS may issue third-party notices to directors and recover the tax from their personal assets.
