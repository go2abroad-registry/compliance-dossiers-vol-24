Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), established under the South African Revenue Service Act 34 of 1997 and administering the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991, the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 and the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; principal compliance portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs clearance and trade data declarations via SARS Customs EDI and the Automated Customs Management platform.

E-government filing portal

A private (Pty) Ltd company incorporated under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 is automatically registered for income tax through the integrated CIPC-to-SARS company registration process; SARS issues a ten-digit Income Tax Reference Number within approximately 21 days of the CIPC incorporation filing (CoR 14.1/14.3). Foreign-owned companies with non-resident directors may proceed online at CIPC attaching the Memorandum of Incorporation, certified director identification and a registered business address; no minimum paid-up capital is required. Where a foreign company is not locally incorporated but carries on a taxable enterprise it must apply directly at a SARS branch under the foreign-entity tax reference application procedure, and where it has employees it must register as an employer for PAYE, UIF and Skills Development Levy before remuneration is paid. VAT registration is a separate SARSeFiling step: compulsory once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months, with voluntary registration allowed once ZAR 50,000 taxable turnover is achieved. The VAT 101 new-vendor application must include the CIPC registration certificate, bank confirmation of the company bank account, and FICA/Know-Your-Customer verification for directors; SARS may withhold registration until anti-money-laundering documentation is accepted. Following registration, provisional income tax returns and VAT201 returns must be filed electronically, and the entity should proceed with SARS-approved electronic invoicing integration as detailed below.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a (Pty) Ltd is a separate legal taxpayer subject to South African corporate income tax at 27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; there is no pass-through status for a company. Distributions to shareholders attract Dividend Tax at 20% withheld at source. A South African incorporated company is tax-resident in the Republic and taxed on worldwide income, including foreign-sourced trading profit, subject to the controlled foreign company rules in s9D of the Income Tax Act and applicable double-taxation agreements. The present cold-chain wholesale import/wholesale distribution model does not qualify as a tax-exempt zone or a designated Special Economic Zone unless the entity obtains the prescribed SEZ customs status, which is not assumed here.

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election. The available statutory elections relevant to this (Pty) Ltd are: (i) VAT grouping election under s50 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991, merging qualifying group companies into one VAT unit; (ii) the payment or cash basis of VAT accounting under s15(2A) VAT Act for vendors whose taxable turnover does not exceed ZAR 2.5 million; (iii) the turnover-based apportionment method election under s17(1) VAT Act for mixed exempt and standard-rated input VAT; (iv) the financial year-end election for income tax purposes (any permissible year end chosen at incorporation, changeable with SARS approval); (v) voluntary self-assessment election for imported services under s88 VAT Act; and (vi) for research and development expenditure, prior-approval registration under s11D of the Income Tax Act, which is not commercially applicable on the current facts. Hybrid mismatch and anti-avoidance measures under ss31 and 8E-8F of the Income Tax Act apply by operation of law; there is no voluntary hybrid or branch-equivalent election available to override those provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration arises when taxable supplies in any 12 consecutive months exceed ZAR 1,000,000 (s23(1) VAT Act); voluntary registration is available where taxable turnover has exceeded ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12 months (s23(3)). SARS may direct compulsory registration from an earlier date where an entity is expected to exceed the compulsory threshold. Independently of these thresholds, the importation of frozen beef carcasses and pelagic fish into the SACU customs territory is subject to import VAT at 15% under s13 VAT Act, calculated on the customs value plus duty payable plus a 10% uplift, and only a registered vendor can recover that import VAT as input tax; an unregistered importer therefore suffers a non-recoverable 15% cost, making VAT registration commercially algebraically indispensable for the cold-chain import model.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered vendor may apply the zero rate on export supplies under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where each of the following conditions is met: (i) goods are supplied to a non-resident of South Africa for export to an export country outside the South African customs territory; (ii) the vendor retains the prescribed documentary proof; (iii) the goods are actually exported within 90 days of the time of supply, failing which the supply is deemed taxable under s11(3); (iv) the consideration is paid in foreign currency or in rand under Exchange Control Regulations on terms approved by the Commissioner; and (v) the vendor holds a valid VAT registration certificate. Re-export of duty-paid imported frozen beef carcasses and pelagic fish under a cold-chain wholesale model qualifies for zero-rating where the goods leave the RSA customs area under a customs-supervised movement evidenced by the export declaration. Ancillary international freight, cold-chain handling and risk-insurance services supplied to non-resident principals are zero-rated under s11(2)(k) and s11(2)(l) VAT Act. Import VAT paid at the border remains fully creditable as input tax against zero-rated export turnover where the 90-day export conditions are substantiated; any excess input VAT is claimed by way of normal VAT201 refund, not by a separate export-refund mechanism.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or road consignment note (CMR) evidencing dispatch outside the SACU customs territory within 90 days of supply

SARS has adopted a phased Electronic Invoicing (EI) mandate under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 and the accompanying EI Rules published in the Government Gazette in December 2024. Covered VAT vendors must issue standard tax invoices as structured e-invoices in the prescribed JSON/XML data standard through a SARS-authorised Electronic Invoicing Service Provider (EISP), with invoice data transmitted to the SARS Electronic Invoicing System (EIS) at or immediately after invoice issuance. Implementation is phased: from 1 January 2025 for digitally-enabled and higher-turnover vendors designated by SARS, with broader roll-out to the remaining VAT vendor population through 2025 and 2026. South Africa does not impose Latin-American style fiscal-printer stamping or physical digital stamping hardware; however, the e-invoice dataset must reconcile with the SAD500 customs export import declaration trail and with the VAT201 return data. SARS may raise a notional assessment under s95 TAA where mandatory e-invoice transmission or full electronic filing is absent, and s210/s211 TAA penalties apply to non-payment or non-compliance.

Where goods are not exported within 90 days of supply, s11(3) VAT Act deems the supply taxable in the period in which the 90-day requirement was not met, and SARS retrospectively claws back the zero-rating, assessing VAT at 15% on the full consideration together with interest. In audit, SARS matches VAT201 zero-rated outputs against SAD500 customs declarations and shipping manifests; claims lacking the prescribed documentary bundle are disallowed under s55 and s16(3) VAT Act, with the associated import VAT input credits also reversed. Further consequences include: (a) an understatement penalty under s223 TAA 28 of 2011 of 150% of the tax shortfall for intentional tax evasion, 100% for a position with no reasonable grounds, or 50% for failure to take reasonable care; (b) an additional penalty of up to 200% of the unpaid tax under s210 TAA for failure to pay VAT; (c) interest under s187 TAA at the SARB repo rate plus three percentage points, adjusted and published by SARS; (d) criminal prosecution under s60 VAT Act (fine up to ZAR 25,000 and/or imprisonment up to two years) and under s234 TAA for false statements or fraudulent tax evasion (fine and/or imprisonment up to five years); and (e) forfeiture or additional customs penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 where goods are intercepted returning to the Republic without valid declaration, including measures against circular or fraudulent export schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a fundamentally convertible current account, but the capital account remains subject to exchange-control oversight. Day-to-day trade payments, import settlements, service fees, interest payments, and profit repatriations can be routed through an authorised dealer without individual SARB approval provided that complete documentary evidence is retained. Equity injections, shareholder loans, acquisitions, and certain offshore treasury arrangements require FinSurv approval, policy clearance, or an exemption. Dividend remittances by a South African (Pty) Ltd require SARS tax-checks, a withholding-tax directive, a corporate resolution, and proof that the distribution is lawful under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; in practice profit remittance is blocked where the company has unresolved tax returns, thin-capitalisation exposure, or exchange-control non-compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For any export receipts, the exporter must ensure that the full invoice value is repatriated to South Africa through an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless the dealer has granted a formal extension. The exporter is required to submit the bill of entry, commercial invoice, customs declaration, and evidence of receipt to the dealing bank. There is no open-ended fractional surrender percentage; the default statutory effect is that 100% of foreign exchange proceeds must be surrendered into the authorised dealer's ZAR account unless the exporter has been expressly permitted to maintain a foreign-currency account or to use offshore proceeds for permitted current-account purposes. Any retained foreign currency must be reported on the bank's exchange-control return, and unused working balances must be converted to ZAR on demand by the SARB or FinSurv.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), in conjunction with the National Treasury and the Minister of Finance under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Offshore digital EMI business models are not authorised dealers under SARB exchange-control regulations and may not lawfully operate principal ZAR clearing, deal in foreign exchange, or hold client funds in lieu of a South African banking licence. Such EMI platforms may be used only as ancillary payment-information interfaces, and all actual settlement must pass through a South African authorised dealer. Reliance on any unauthorised foreign EMI for trade settlements may expose the entity to exchange-control penalties, FIC Act accountability failures, and SARS audit risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be received via SWIFT MT103 or, where applicable, SWIFT MT202 COVER into the ZA entity's account held at an SARB authorised dealer. The payment narrative must identify the remitter as the subscribing shareholder and state 'NON-RESIDENT SHARE CAPITAL INJECTION / DIRECT INVESTMENT EQUITY' or the equivalent SARB balance-of-payments purpose-code wording. The bank will verify the corporate resolution, updated memorandum of incorporation, share-subscription agreement, SARS importer/exporter customs code, and source-of-funds documentation. For shareholder loans, a formal loan agreement and FinSurv policy position are required before the bank will credit the funds; loans must be subordinated if they are to support the working-capital solvency test for a revolving LC facility.Trade disbursements for frozen beef and seafood imports should be executed using SWIFT MT103 for open-account and telegraphic-transfer settlements and through SWIFT MT700/MT710/MT720 for documentary letters of credit. Offshore digital EMI business models are not authorised dealers under SARB exchange-control regulations and may not lawfully operate principal ZAR clearing, deal in foreign exchange, or hold client funds in lieu of a South African banking licence.
