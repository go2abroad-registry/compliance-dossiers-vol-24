Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; statutory corporate website: https://www.sars.gov.za; customs electronic declarations lodged via the SARS Customs EDI/DMS system

E-government filing portal

The CIPC-incorporated Proprietary Limited company obtains its income tax reference number (TIN) by completing SARS taxpayer registration through eFiling (form IT77 plus company particulars). Required supporting documents: CIPC registration certificate (CoR 14.3), Memorandum of Incorporation, minutes of directors appointments, CoR 21.1 registered office notice, identity or passport copies of directors (with permits for foreign directors), shareholder declarations, and a South African banking confirmation. The TIN is issued electronically upon acceptance; thereafter the company must register separately for VAT using form VAT101 once taxable supplies reach the compulsory threshold or an election is made at the voluntary threshold. Where the company employs staff, parallel registrations for PAYE, UIF and SDL are mandatory. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the (Pty) Ltd; no minimum capital is required, but a resident director and a local registered office are prerequisites.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; a (Pty) Ltd is invariably a separate income taxpayer. Relevant elections available to this entity: (1) VAT grouping under s.50 VAT Act, allowing the (Pty) Ltd and fellow South African group companies to be treated as a single VAT vendor; (2) income tax group relief under ss.41-47 Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, available only where a resident company holds 100% of the shares and all group members align financial year-ends; (3) assessed loss carry-forward elections under s.20(2A) Income Tax Act; (4) CFC attribution elections and the foreign business establishment exclusion under s.9D; and (5) hybrid interest and hybrid dividend instruments, which are not elective but are mandatorily re-characterised under ss.8E, 8EA, 8F, 23M and 25D pursuant to the OECD hybrid mismatch recommendations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month rolling period; voluntary registration at ZAR 50,000 of taxable supplies in a 12-month period. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services supplied to South African recipients are registrable under the separate electronic-services VAT regime without a monetary threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimable under s.11(1)(a) VAT Act for goods exported from South Africa and supplied to a non-resident (including medical gas pipeline and copper vacuum manifold components delivered to an offshore hospital project), provided the supplier retains the prescribed documentary proof and the goods are physically removed from the country within the SARS-prescribed export period (ordinarily 90 days after supply). Services are zero-rated under s.11(2)(k) where the services are physically performed outside South Africa, or under s.11(2)(l) where supplied to a non-resident not in South Africa at the time of supply and the services are not directly connected with land or movable property situated in South Africa. Installation of terminal manifolds and copper vacuum piping at a South African hospital is a supply directly connected with immovable property in South Africa and is standard-rated at 15%; only offshore-site installation services or the physical export of prefabricated modules would attract the 0% rate. The burden of proving export and non-resident supply rests on the vendor.

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration (SARS B/E) showing correct HS tariff classification of copper vacuum and oxygen manifold components and the destination country
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal of goods from South Africa within the prescribed export period
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identical to the customs declaration and to the VAT invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Signed declaration or contract evidencing the non-resident status of the buyer and the place of supply of any offshore installation services

VAT invoices must contain the particulars required by s.20 of the VAT Act; there is currently no mandatory real-time government clearance or digital stamping for ordinary VAT invoices. SARS has announced and is piloting a phased national e-invoicing framework (SARS eInvoicing), including pilots in the medical schemes and intermediary sectors, which will in due course introduce compulsory interoperable e-invoicing and structured e-invoice clearance. Currently mandatory electronic channels include: (i) VAT201 returns submitted through eFiling or the SARS API by registered vendors; (ii) customs import and export declarations transmitted electronically through the SARS Customs EDI system using the SAD500 series; (iii) electronic submission of vendor banking and contact data for refund processing; and (iv) automatic data exchanges covering third-party invoices, travel and medical scheme information integrated into the SARS risk engines.

SARS cross-references zero-rated export declarations with customs exit logs and bank forex repatriation data; the vendor must produce the complete documentation bundle within the SARS notice period (commonly 21 working days). Where the fiscal proof fails, SARS re-assesses output VAT at 15% on the full supply value, disallows inputs attributable to the export, and levies interest from the original due date at the quarterly-published SARS prescribed interest rate under s.187 Tax Administration Act No. 28 of 2011. An understatement penalty ranging from 25% to 200% of the tax shortfall applies by conduct category under ss.222-223 TAA (substantial understatement 25%, gross negligence 100%, intentional tax evasion 150% or 200%). Fraudulent export zero-rating further exposes the company and responsible directors to criminal prosecution under ss.232-235 TAA, including imprisonment of up to five years for tax evasion, and to joint and several liability for the corporate VAT debt under s.19(2) VAT Act; VAT refunds may be suspended pending the SARS compliance review under s.190 TAA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is a floating, freely convertible currency on the current account: invoiced hospital EPC receipts and imported equipment payments need no SARB permit, only full documentation. The capital account is managed: non-resident share subscriptions flow freely via Authorised Dealers, but shareholder loans exceeding ZAR 50 million require prior FinSurv approval, and SARS restricts interest deductions under the Section 31 thin-capitalisation debt:equity guideline of 3:1. Dividend/profit remittance to a non-resident shareholder is lawful after declaration, subject to 20% Dividends Tax (reduced to 5%-15% under most DTAs), proof of unencumbered equity funding, and filed audited annual financial statements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exchange Control Regulation 9 and FinSurv Guidelines require all export proceeds, including cross-border services such as SADC hospital medical-gas EPC work, to be received into the exporter's account at a South African Authorised Dealer within 30 days of shipment or service completion. The full 100% of foreign currency must then be surrendered by conversion into ZAR unless the company holds an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) permitting retention and reuse; the default conversion ratio is therefore 100%, and any deferral or exemption requires a documented FinSurv FCA approval.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the national exchange-control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations (R1111). Operational approval and reporting are delegated to licensed commercial banks (Authorised Dealers). For this ZA (Pty) Ltd, all inward equity, shareholder loans, trade payments, and profit repatriations must be executed through an Authorised Dealer and recorded on SARB/FinSurv systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a secondary FX corridor; Low as a primary bank. Non-bank electronic money institutions in South Africa are payment intermediaries, not deposit-taking banks: the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 prohibits them from offering current-account deposit services or SAMOS settlement finality, and they cannot issue commercial letters of credit or receive National Treasury BAS payments from public hospitals. Offshore digital EMIs may act as Authorised Dealers with Limited Authority for FX conversion and low-value cross-border transfers, but settlement must anchor in a fully FICA-compliant Tier-1 bank account. The entity should open at least one big-four bank account and layer EMIs only for cost optimisation of small corridor payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No explicit statutory Local File / Master File monetary threshold in South Africa. SARS expects contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for all cross-border related-party transactions; practical threshold is R5 million per related-party transaction category in a tax year. Large multinational groups (consolidated turnover exceeding approximately R750 million) should retain a Master File and Local File.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime as in offshore centres. Nevertheless, the general anti-avoidance rules (s 80A-L), controlled foreign company rules, and SARS transfer-pricing guidance require genuine commercial purpose, personnel, premises, and risk management. A shell Pty Ltd without substance will not qualify for treaty protections and may be recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: SARS may raise a corrected assessment; understatement penalty under s 222 TAA of 100%–200% of the shortfall for intentional tax evasion; criminal prosecution under s 235 TAA with fines up to R10 million and imprisonment up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in fraudulent filing may be criminally prosecuted, subjected to civil restitution orders, asset attachments, travel bans, and disqualification as company directors; SARS may invoke the corporate veil-piercing provisions of the Companies Act 71 of 2008.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply the general anti-avoidance rule (s 103) or GAAR (s 80A-L) to set aside the arrangement, recharacterise revenue, disallow treaty benefits, and impose a 100% understatement penalty; CIPC may deregister the company for being defunct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate tax where the company is a mere veil for fraud or illegal conduct; courts may impose civil restitution, travel restrictions, and personal fines.
Transfer-Pricing and Arm's-Length Mispricing

Statutory sanction: SARS will adjust taxable income to the arm's-length price, disallow excessive deductions, and apply interest from the original due date. Understatement penalties range from 25%–75% for reasonable-care failures to 100%–200% for gross intentional understatement.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons who sign materially incorrect returns may be personally assessed for penalties and interest, and directors who knowingly approve abusive TP structures may face personal criminal liability and extradition exposure.
AML and Exchange-Control Violation

Statutory sanction: The Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 provides fines up to R10 million and imprisonment up to 15 years for money-laundering and compliance failures; Exchange Control Regulations impose forfeiture of the unapproved amount and further monetary penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as accountable institutions or responsible persons may be prosecuted personally; SARS and SARB may seek asset seizure, personal fines, travel bans, and criminal conviction for unlawful transfer facilitation.
