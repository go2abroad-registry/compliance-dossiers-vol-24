Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Tax Administration under the Ministry of Revenue (MOR); online portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities incorporating as International Companies in Samoa must register through the Companies Registry under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended). The entity obtains a Certificate of Incorporation and must apply for a Samoa Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the Ministry of Revenue registration process. An International Company is treated as a tax-exempt offshore entity for income tax purposes; however, VAT registration is required if the entity makes taxable supplies in Samoa or exceeds the registration threshold. Required documents include certified passport copies of directors/shareholders, registered office address, certificate of incorporation, and details of intended activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa's International Companies Act allows an IC to apply for tax exemption status for offshore income, resulting in no income tax on qualifying foreign-sourced income. An IC may elect to be treated as a domestic company under the Companies Act 2001 and Income Tax Act if it carries on business in Samoa, which would subject it to standard corporate taxation. No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available; entities must rely on Samoa's general anti-avoidance rules and applicable tax treaty provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 per 12-month period in taxable supplies; overseas entities making digital supplies to Samoan consumers may be required to register if they exceed the threshold under Samoa's cross-border digital services rules

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export of goods where the goods are exported within 28 days of supply, and to internationally supplied services provided to non-residents who are outside Samoa at the time of supply. Telematics SaaS and fleet dispatch services qualify for zero-rating when supplied to non-resident recipients who are not in Samoa and do not use the services in Samoa, provided the supplier retains sufficient evidence. Services physically performed outside Samoa or directly connected with exported movable property may also qualify.

  • Samoa Customs Electronic Export Declaration or Customs Notice of Export
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice reflecting zero-rating
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement providing proof of offshore consideration received

Samoa does not currently mandate real-time e-invoicing, government fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. All VAT-registered persons must issue tax invoices with prescribed content including sequential numbering, seller TIN, buyer TIN, invoice date, description, and VAT amount; file VAT returns through the Ministry of Revenue portal; and retain records in a compliant format. No API-based pre-clearance is currently imposed.

The Ministry of Revenue may assess unreported VAT, impose late payment interest at the prescribed rate (commonly 2% per month or 24% per annum), and apply penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded for fraudulent zero-rating claims. Criminal prosecution applies for invoicing fraud or intentional misstatement, with fines not exceeding WST 50,000 for a body corporate and/or imprisonment for responsible officers. Zero-rated input tax credits may be retrospectively clawed back for up to 4 years where export substantiation is defective; the limitation period extends to 8 years in cases of fraud or wilful default.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains a managed-float foreign exchange regime, with the Samoan Tālā (WST) trade-weighted against the currencies of major trading partners. Current-account transactions are generally convertible through authorised commercial banks, subject to documentary support and CBS reporting thresholds; capital-account transfers — including direct investment inflows, inter-company loans, and dividend repatriation to non-resident shareholders — remain subject to CBS consent unless the entity qualifies for an express offshore exemption. A Samoan International Company (IC) that both (a) holds a valid international company certificate and (b) conducts its offshore activities exclusively through a licensed international-finance facility or a licensed international bank is deemed to operate outside Samoan resident exchange-control scope. Consequently, all foreign-currency balances, profit remittances, and proceeds from non-Samoan sources may be held and transferred freely, provided no movement of WST occurs onshore. In contrast, any onshore operational WST account used for local salaries, rent, or utility payments carries convertibility hurdles: funds must trace to CBS-sourced purchase of WST, and conversion back to foreign currency for external disbursement will trigger individual advance approval, hard-currency rationing risk, or mandatory documentary evidence of underlying services. Standard non-resident shareholder dividends are therefore structured through offshore foreign-currency settlement accounts antecedent to any WST leakage.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Samoan resident exporters of goods or services, the CBS requires export proceeds to be repatriated to a licensed Samoan commercial bank within 30 calendar days from shipment or invoicing date, and at least 50% of all export receipts must be surrendered into WST immediately upon credit, unless a formal CBS waiver is granted on a shipment-specific contract basis. However, a registered International Company engaged exclusively in offshore logistics-software and fleet-telematics services is expressly exempt from such surrender requirements, provided the SaaS receivables are charged to non-resident obligors through an offshore foreign-currency account and are not intermingled with any onshore Samoan revenue. In practice, the IC should still retain the underlying licensing agreements, invoices, and evidence of non-Samoan source to demonstrate non-residence in the event of a CBS audit; otherwise, a residual 50% surrender obligation could attach to any inadvertently remitted onshore proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) — Exchange Control Division, exercising powers under the Exchange Control Act 1975, the Exchange Control Regulations 1976 (as amended), and the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended). The CBS administers all resident and non-resident foreign exchange restrictions, issuance of exchange control approvals, and monetary policy oversight of licensed commercial banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low with material legal constraints. Samoa does not license standalone electronic-money institutions (EMIs), and the CBS strictly prohibits unlicensed payment service providers from holding Samoan customer tālā balances. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) may, however, be used as a purely treasury-adjacent collection rail for invoicing overseas SaaS customers if the EMI account is opened in a Western jurisdiction (e.g., UK, Singapore) under the IC’s legal name and does not route through any Samoan domestic banking node. The critical legal limitation is AML/KYC reciprocity: Samoan ICs with nominee shareholders or registered-agent-only substance tend to fail enhanced due diligence at offshore EMIs unless a physical director, full UBO disclosure, and audited financial statements are provided. Moreover, CBS foreign-exchange approval is not required for funds that never cross a WST settlement point. Recommended structure: maintain a non-Samoan EMI account solely for card-not-present recurring billing from fleets, then sweep net proceeds monthly via SWIFT to the BSP Samoa offshore USD clearing account, preserving a clean audit trail and avoiding Samoan exchange-control touchpoints.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder loan capital from the non-resident parent or investors must enter the Samoan IC through a licensed commercial bank using a SWIFT MT103 wire routed from the remitting jurisdiction to BSP's Samoa USD beneficiary clearing account (Apia, SWIFT BIC: BOSPSWSM; correspondent bank typically ANZ Australia or Citi New York). The wire must include (i) purpose code 'CB-101 – Direct Investment in Offshore International Company' as classified by the CBS Balance of Payments Manual, (ii) a copy of the IC's International Company Certificate and board resolution authorising share issuance, and (iii) for capital-above-WST500,000, a prior CBS offshore-capital notification or exemption-listing reference. Where subscription is in non-cash assets (cloud servers, IP licenses), an emailed valuation report from an independent expert must be attached to the payment instruction. Upon receipt, the bank will credit a Capital Escrow subclass flagged as non-WST, with no automatic conversion to tālā, enabling further outbound funding of SaaS operating expenses without exchange-control friction. Any subsequent onward lending from the IC to subsidiaries should be executed using an MT103 with purpose code 'CB-201 – Intercompany Loan' and a signed loan agreement; the CBS must receive supplemental notification if the loan tenor exceeds one year or the interest rate deviates from arm's-length range.For routine SaaS vendor invoices (hosting, telematics API, telecommunication capacity) and third-party contractor settlements, preferred clearing is SWIFT MT103 via BSP's offshore USD account, using real-time gross-settlement turnaround to New York/Auckland correspondents and business-day value dating. The payment instruction narrative must cite the underlying commercial invoice and the CBS service category 'Telecommunications and Information Services – Computer Software and License Fees'. For procurement of physical logistics-hardware (e.g., GPS units), when the counterparty requires bank-availed settlement, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by BSP under ICC UCP600 is acceptable for shipments from Hong Kong or Singapore, with presentation documentation including maritime/airway bill, commercial invoice, and insurance certificate. Samoan ICs may also use a non-Samoan fintech clearing account (e.g., Wise Business) for disbursements under WST 50,000 per wire, provided the funds never convert into WST and are swept monthly back to the BSP offshore account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a Samoa International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 tax-exemption regime; the general Samoa corporate income tax rate is 27% for resident domestic companies, for reference only.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions paid by a Samoa International Company to non-resident shareholders. Dividends, capital distributions, share premium returns, and liquidation proceeds are exempt from Samoan income tax and dividend WHT under the IC tax-exemption regime.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has no statutory transfer-pricing documentation threshold for an exempt International Company because the IC is outside the domestic CIT net. As a defensive compliance practice, maintain contemporaneous master-file and local-file-level analysis for intercompany SaaS licensing, data-access, and logistics-dispatch service transactions when annual covered revenue exceeds WST 1,000,000 or the equivalent in foreign currency, as foreign tax authorities will expect arm's-length returns on the group functions, assets, and risks.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime for International Companies. IC tax exemption is conditioned on the absence of Samoan-source business and on the entity being an offshore vehicle with a registered office and licensed registered agent in Samoa. If an IC is managed or controlled from Samoa, carries on Samoan-resident business, or acts as a conduit without commercial substance, the exemption can be revoked or disregarded. For the logistics-fleet dispatch SaaS structure, substance should be evidenced by board minutes, written intercompany service agreements, route-data licensing contracts, segregated customer payment flows, and decision-making records showing that the IC performs real offshore activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Exemption Abuse / Sham Offshore Company

Statutory sanction: Revocation of International Company status; retroactive assessment to Samoan corporate income tax at the 27% domestic rate on Samoan-source profits; late payment, default interest, and false-return penalties under the Tax Administration Act; and administrative strike-off of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly executed false declarations or directed diverted revenue through the IC can be held jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties where the corporate veil is pierced. The Samoa Ministry of Revenue or a foreign court may obtain freezing orders, asset-preservation orders, travel bans, and civil restitution against the directors; criminal tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Samoa Registrar may strike off the IC for failure to maintain proper records, registered agent, or annual confirmation. In foreign tax jurisdictions, the IC may be disregarded as a sham, with all revenue attributed to the true economic owner under substance-over-form, CFC, and common-law sham principles.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and parent-company directors can be removed and disqualified if they allow an empty corporate structure to fraudulently divert profits. Courts may impose personal liability for restitution of diverted proceeds, civil penalties, travel restrictions, and, in cases of fraudulent cross-border invoicing, extradition and criminal proceedings.
AML/CFT Violation and Money-Laundering Conduit

Statutory sanction: Offences under Samoa's anti-money-laundering and proceeds-of-crime legislation attract confiscation of property, monetary penalties, and imprisonment. The IC's licence may be suspended or revoked, and the licensed registered agent must file a suspicious-transaction report with the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and any designated AML compliance officer face personal criminal prosecution, fines, forfeiture of digital-asset balances, and travel bans. Civil asset-recovery procedures may extend to all Stripe, bank, and contract balances traceable to the suspicious logistics-dispatch payments.
False Statutory Declaration and Governance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act and supporting regulations, each false declaration or non-compliance is an offence punishable by fines, director disqualification, and court-ordered winding up. Unpaid annual fees accrue late-payment surcharges, and repeated non-compliance results in strike-off without prejudice to tax assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false statutory declarations commit a criminal offence and may be personally liable to compensate the company and creditors for resulting losses. Foreign insolvency practitioners and regulators may pursue the directors personally, seek mutual-assistance travel bans, and obtain orders restricting their ability to leave Samoa or the relevant enforcement jurisdiction.
