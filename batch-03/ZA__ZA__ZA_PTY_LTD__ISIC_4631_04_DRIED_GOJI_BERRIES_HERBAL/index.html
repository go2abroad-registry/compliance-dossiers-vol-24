Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity election. Statutory elective mechanisms available to a ZA (Pty) Ltd include: (i) CFC high-tax exclusion election under section 9D(9) of the Income Tax Act to relieve a controlled foreign company's income; (ii) VAT grouping election under section 70(3) of the VAT Act allowing a corporate group to be treated as a single vendor for indirect tax simplification; (iii) section 23H inventory valuation method elections; (iv) headquarter company election under section 9I for regional treasury/joint venture functions, subject to substantial shareholding and local substance tests; and (v) hybrid mismatch reporting elections under the Part IIBA rules, requiring alignment with OECD BEPS Action 2 and prescribed form reporting where a deduction/non-inclusion or double deduction outcome is intended. No 'pass-through' election is recognised for a South African incorporated company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration arises when the value of taxable supplies exceeds or is likely to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months (s 23 VAT Act). Voluntary registration is possible if taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months exceeded ZAR 50,000 (s 23(3)). For an exporter, zero-rated export supplies are includible in the ZAR 1 million turnover measurement, so mandatory vendor status will typically be reached earlier than for purely domestic micro-enterprises. The company should register immediately upon contract conclusion if projected export turnover reaches the threshold within 12 months; failure to register attracts penalties and retrospective VAT assessment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies under s 11(1)(a) and s 11(1)(c) of the VAT Act to the supply of goods that are physically exported from South Africa: (i) the vendor (ZA_PTY_LTD) supplies dried goji berries/herbal botanicals under a sale agreement; (ii) the goods are removed from the Republic by the vendor or buyer within 90 days of the time of supply (or as extended under s 11(4) for classed goods); (iii) the vendor retains prescribed export documentary proof; (iv) the goods are not returned to South Africa or consumed locally. An intermediate service export (wholesale trading/packaging facilitation) may qualify under s 11(2)(l) as an exported service to a non-resident recipient where the services are for use outside SA, but commodity-sale income is classified as goods export. The company must assert the zero rate at invoicing only after obtaining the customs export log number; VAT refund claims for excess input tax are then implemented by SARS on a monthly or two-monthly basis per the vendor's filing cycle.

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration (electronic SARS Customs release, containing goods description, HS classification, customs code and unique reference number; for goji berries use e.g. HS 1211 (lycium) / 0813 (dried fruit cross-check) depending on final classification)
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or multimodal transport document proving removal of goods from South Africa to the named export destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list signed by the supplier and matched to the customs declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT TT credit advice and Foreign Exchange Transfer Form evidencing receipt of export proceeds through authorised dealer channels
  • SARB Financial Surveillance export form (Form F / electronic export schedule) confirming currency repatriation obligation
  • Phytosanitary / health certification and certificate of origin (for botanical exports where applicable), retained as supporting proof for customs risk clearance

SARS does not currently impose continuous transaction control (CTC) real-time fiscal stamping as in Latin America, but requires compliant electronic tax invoices and eFiling submission. A VAT tax invoice must issue within 21 days of supply and contain prescribed particulars per s 20 VAT Act, including seller VAT number, invoice number, date, full description, consideration, and the VAT charged separately; for exports the invoice must reflect 'export' and zero-rating evidence. Customs clearance is mandatory and electronic: the SAD500 export declaration is transmitted through the SARS EDI/electronic customs system; the SARS Customs risk engine may require documentary scanning and digital clearance before goods move. Exporters must also report through the SARB Financial Surveillance electronic export system (Form F/export schedules) where relevant. Records (including online invoice files, customs clearance records, commercial transport documents) must be retained for at least five years and be available for digital inspection on SARS eFiling.

SARS may audit zero-rated export claims by matching the SAD500 declarations, bills of lading, remittance records and the VAT return; the vendor must retain full export 'proof pack' for five years. Understatement penalties under s 223-224 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 range up to 200% of the shortfall for intentional/ gross-negligence conduct; where zero-rating is wrongly claimed, SARS claws back the difference as output VAT at 15% plus interest (at prescription rates) with a possible 'additional tax' equal to the tax under s 191 VAT Act. Fraudulent export schemes attract criminal prosecution under s 234 VAT Act: fine up to ZAR 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years; SARS also publishes the names of offending vendors, can revoke the vendor's registration/exporter status, and issue jeopardy assessments to attach assets before a dispute is resolved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is a freely floating, market-convertible currency, but South Africa maintains a structural exchange-control environment. Current account transactions are fully liberalised and require only substantiating documentation routed through an Authorised Dealer. Capital account transactions (inward loans, share issues to non-residents, outbound investments) are subject to threshold-based reporting or prior FinSurv approval. Profit remittance (dividend distribution) to non-resident shareholders is generally executable without pre-approval provided: the investment qualifies as a direct investment (non-resident shareholding of at least 10%); the inward shareholding has been duly reported to FinSurv; the declaring company is 'financially self-financing'; and no impermissible domestic borrowing against the foreign capital (the 'local borrowing ratio' restriction) is in force. Dividends are additionally subject to the statutory 20% dividend withholding tax administered by SARS, with the net amount freely remittable through the Authorised Dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion ratio; South Africa abolished the fixed percentage surrender requirement. However, export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa through an Authorised Dealer strictly within 180 days from the date of shipment (invoice date or bill of lading date as contractually evidenced). Upon repatriation, the proceeds must be credited to an Ordinary ZAR Account or, with FinSurv approval, retained in a designated Foreign Currency Account (FCA). The foreign currency may be held in the FCA but cannot be invested offshore or on-lent without Authorised Dealer/FinSurv authorisation; any unconsumed balance must be sold into the ZAR market or applied to approved trade payments. Extension beyond the 180-day rule is available only via a formal FinSurv application supported by the underlying trade terms (e.g., open-account credit exceeding 180 days).

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), exercising delegated power under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, administered operationally through Authorised Dealers (registered commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury account; MEDIUM only as a supplementary receiving rail. Legal constraints are prohibitive for full reliance: offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not licensed by SARB and therefore cannot act as Authorised Dealers, cannot issue inward transfer advices satisfying SARB Balance-of-Payments reporting, and cannot complete the mandatory export-proceeds repatriation chain. The South African (Pty) Ltd remains statutorily obliged to route export receipts through a domestic Authorised Dealer within the 180-day window. EMIs may be used at the margin for buyer-side collections or embedded payments, but all proceeds must be swept to the primary domestic Authorised Dealer account before the SARB surrender/repatriation deadline. Any EMI arrangement must be disclosed to the onboarding bank to avoid anti-structuring AML triggers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident parent/shareholder funds enter the company's domestic ZAR operational account via SWIFT MT103 under the SARB Balance-of-Payments classification 'equity capital (inward direct investment)'. The inbound wire must include the correct BOP purpose code for equity investment in a resident company; the receiving Authorised Dealer files the prescribed inward investment report to SARB FinSurv within 30 days of receipt. The company must simultaneously allot shares and file the special resolution with CIPC within 30 days. No prior FinSurv approval is required for an equity injection. For shareholder loans, the funds must be reported as a foreign liability on the prescribed onshore reporting template; the interest rate and terms must be arm's length and the loan must not be used to finance domestic borrowing beyond the SARB local borrowing ratio. All supporting source-of-funds evidence (audited financial statements of the lender, signed equity subscription agreement, board resolution, and share certificate) must be retained and produced to the bank on demand.Outbound trade settlements are executed through Authorised Dealer clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for documentary telegraphic transfers to international suppliers for dried goji berry packaging, freight, and ancillary logistics; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) opened through the corporate bank for larger, high-value shipments from overseas suppliers where documentary security is required; (3) Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) as a cost-effective alternative for established counterparties. Every outbound payment must be matched against the underlying invoice, contract, SAD500 declaration, and logistics documents, and must be captured in the Authorised Dealer's SARB monthly balance-of-payments returns. Dividend remittances to the non-resident parent are paid via SWIFT MT103 under the BOP code for dividends/distributed profits, supported by the audited dividend declaration, resolution, and exchange-control reporting form completed through the bank; the 20% withholding tax must be remitted to SARS before the net dividend is released.Deliverable FX forwards (up to 12 months) with an Authorised Dealer against firm, committed export receivables or import payables, requiring underlying documentary proof of the exposureForeign currency options (purchased puts/calls) with an Authorised Dealer for premium-defined risk reduction, permissible where the underlying trade exposure is documented and matures within the option tenorCross-currency swaps for longer-dated structural exposures (e.g., shareholder loan repayment mismatch or multi-year supply contracts), executable only upon SARB FinSurv approval obtained through the Authorised DealerNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing an offshore ZAR or USD/CNY fix where onshore deliverable liquidity is insufficient, permitted for hedging commodity-driven receivables when the bank confirms structural eligibility and the instrument is aligned to a firm underlying transactionRolling/implied forwards on approved Foreign Currency Account balances, allowing the company to lock the future ZAR value of retained export proceeds without breaching the FCA holding rules

The South African (Pty) Ltd remains statutorily obliged to route export receipts through a domestic Authorised Dealer within the 180-day window. EMIs may be used at the margin for buyer-side collections or embedded payments, but all proceeds must be swept to the primary domestic Authorised Dealer account before the SARB surrender/repatriation deadline. Any EMI arrangement must be disclosed to the onboarding bank to avoid anti-structuring AML triggers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: SARS guideline expects a Local File where cross-border connected-party transactions exceed ZAR 5 million in aggregate per annum; Master File required for MNE groups meeting the CbC threshold (consolidated group revenue ≥ ZAR 10 billion); CbC Report (BEPS Action 13) for groups with consolidated revenue ≥ ZAR 10 billion; documentation must be provided within 21 days of SARS's request and in the prescribed format (SARS Transfer Pricing Guide; Master/Local File Guideline 2016).

Economic substance scope: South Africa imposes no separate ESR statute (no UAE-style registration). Instead, substance is enforced through: (i) s. 31 arm's length control requiring conduct and substance analysis; (ii) s. 31A Diverted Profits Tax (15% additional assessment on profits diverted via arrangements lacking economic substance, effective from years ending on or after 31 December 2021); (iii) GAAR under s. 80A-80L (treating tax avoidance arrangements with no commercial substance as void); (iv) s. 103 ITA general anti-avoidance; and (v) the Companies Act corruption/self-dealing provisions (s. 20(9) piercing the corporate veil).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: Under TAA s. 234-235, upon conviction: fine of up to ZAR 1 million and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; understatement penalties of up to 150% of the shortfall (s. 223); 10% monthly late-payment penalties up to 100%; interest at the prescribed rate; publication of taxpayer's name by SARS (s. 67).

Director personal liability: Directors who 'knowingly' participated in filing false returns or whose actions caused the company's tax default can be held personally liable for unpaid PAYE/other employees' taxes (s. 159-162 TAA); criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment; civil restitution in the amount of under-collected taxes and interest; travel bans under s. 194 TAA and attachment orders against personal assets.
Shell company / artificial profit diversion

Statutory sanction: SARS may impute arm's length profits to the SA Pty Ltd (s. 31), impose the 15% Diverted Profits Tax (s. 31A), apply GAAR invalidity (s. 80A), impose understatement penalties up to 150%, and levy interest; the intermediary's invoice deduction is disallowed in full.

Director personal liability: For directors who form or operate the shell: piercing the corporate veil under s. 20(9) Companies Act and s. 77(3) (reckless trading) may render directors jointly and severally liable for the company's entire diverted tax debt; SARS can obtain attachment orders, DTA exchange-of-information triggers, and in aggravated cases criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Transfer pricing mispricing

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the transaction to arm's length under s. 31; additional CGT? no — additional income tax at 27% on the adjustment plus interest at the prescribed rate; s. 223 understatement penalty in the 10%-150% band; foreign exchange and VAT implications for the exporter; simultaneous adjustment of the PE? not automatic.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign TP schedules or approve intercompany pricing while knowing the prices are non-arm's length and cause a material shortfall face personal civil liability for the shortfall under company law, personal tax debts through SARS's third-party liability provisions, and criminal liability in flagrant evasion cases—including travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years.
AML / cash & FIC non-compliance

Statutory sanction: FIC Act fines up to ZAR 10 million per contravention and imprisonment for directors who knowingly permit money laundering; Customs & Excise Act penalties for false export declarations (up to 100% of the value of goods); named on the FIC board's published list; forfeiture of proceeds under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors personally subject to criminal fines and imprisonment; no corporate veil protection for AML offences; travel bans and asset freezes; civil forfeiture of property involved in money laundering; SARS shares intelligence with the FIC and NPA for prosecution of directors.
Failure to withhold international taxes

Statutory sanction: The Pty Ltd is personally liable for the unremitted 20% dividends tax (s. 64N), 15% royalties WHT and 15% interest WHT, plus 10% monthly penalty up to 100% and interest at prescribed rates; SARS may collect directly from the company and offset any refunds; understatement penalties for repeated neglect (s. 223).

Director personal liability: Directors who are 'knowingly party' to a failure to withhold are jointly and severally liable for the WHT shortfall (s. 159 TAA with extension via s. 162 for employees' tax); travel bans (s. 194 TAA) and seizure of personal assets are available; criminal prosecution follows in case of intentional evasion.
