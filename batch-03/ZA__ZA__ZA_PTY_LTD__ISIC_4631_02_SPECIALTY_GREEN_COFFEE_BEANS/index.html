Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box pass-through regime. A (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person subject to normal tax at the entity level. Elective mechanisms available include VAT group registration (section 50 of the VAT Act) for wholly-owned groups, election for income tax deductibility of certain financial instruments, and the option to apply the South African residence basis of taxation if the entity is effectively managed in South Africa. No hybrid mismatch elections are separately available beyond the anti-avoidance provisions aligned with the OECD BEPS framework.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration permitted if supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in 12 months; registration may also be required for suppliers of imported services engaged in non-taxable activities if nexus criteria are met

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods are supplied by the vendor to a non-resident recipient, supplied under a sale or consignment, and exported from South Africa within 60 days (or further period approved by SARS) of the date of supply. Alternatively, under section 11(1)(b), a supply of movable goods is zero-rated where the goods are supplied to an exporter vendor and are exported within 90 days (or approved extended period) from the date of supply. The supplier must retain evidence showing the physical export and that the goods left the Republic. Services exported to non-residents may also be zero-rated if attributable to a non-resident location of supply and not for use in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500), Bill of Lading (or Airwaybill), Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency, signed commercial invoice, packing list, and a statement by the exporting agent confirming physical removal from South Africa

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. SARS accepts electronic tax invoices and requires electronic filing of VAT201 returns through eFiling or an approved software integrator. Tax invoices must contain prescribed particulars including supplier/recipient VAT numbers, description, date, price, and VAT charged. SARS is advancing an eInvoicing framework but there is currently no statutory obligation for pre-clearance or digital fiscal validation at the border.

SARS may audit zero-rated export supplies within a five-year prescription period. Failure to hold sufficient documentary proof results in the adjustment of VAT to 15% with additional tax, interest, and understatement penalties up to 100% of the shortfall depending on behaviour. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating claims attract criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act, fines, and imprisonment. Misdeclared exports can also be clawed back with retrospective effect from the date of supply, and exchange-control violations for late repatriation may trigger separate administrative penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a free-floating currency with full current-account convertibility for bona fide trade transactions: import payments for green coffee, freight, insurance, and incidental processing services may be routed without FinSurv pre-approval through any Authorized Dealer, subject only to standard documentary verification (SAD500 customs declaration, commercial invoice, contract, bill of lading). Capital-account convertibility is restricted: inward foreign direct investment into a South African (Pty) Ltd is freely permitted but must be declared to and recorded by the Authorized Dealer; outward direct investments, offshore bank accounts, and foreign borrowings by the local company require Authorized Dealer approval within the annual discretionary allowances (outward FDI allowance is ZAR 10 million per legal entity per calendar year) or FinSurv approval above those limits. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without SARB pre-approval provided the company holds verifiably distributable reserves evidenced by audited financial statements; the remittance is subject to the 20% dividend withholding tax under Section 64E-64H of the Income Tax Act, and in bank practice depends on the inward-foreign-investment reference being correctly recorded at the time of capital injection. Practical hurdles: post-FATF-grey-listing compliance is strict, bank onboarding timelines for non-resident-owned commodity traders are materially longer, any capital inflow not tagged with the inward-investment reference blocks future dividend and exit remittances, and prohibited loop structures (South African funds diverted offshore and returned as FDI) attract severe penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 6 read with the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual - Exporters, all export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 30 days of the date of shipment, unless the Authorized Dealer has approved extended payment terms up to 12 months, or FinSurv has approved terms beyond 12 months. On receipt, 100% of the foreign-currency export proceeds must be surrendered to an Authorized Dealer, meaning sold to the Authorized Dealer for ZAR at the ruling market rate; alternatively, if the exporter maintains a permitted Foreign Currency Account, the proceeds may be retained in that account strictly for permissible current-account trade disbursements such as payment to foreign suppliers, freight, insurance, and other trade-related services. There is no fractional mandatory conversion ratio: surrender is effectively 100% unless the Foreign Currency Account route is used, and the exporter must complete the Authorized Dealer's export declaration, retain shipment evidence (bill of lading, SAD500), and report outstanding export proceeds if payment is not received within the 30-day window. For ZA_PTY_LTD, this rule is operationally relevant only if the company re-exports green coffee to SADC/SACU markets via the SIRESS clearing system or undertakes a back-to-back container re-shipment; if the company imports only for local distribution, the corollary obligation is full compliance with import-payment timing and documentation rules enforced by the Authorized Dealer.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorized Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low for use as the primary treasury or clearing rail; Medium only as an ancillary receivables-collection tool that is swept daily into the Authorized Dealer. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Intergiro, BCB Group) do not hold South African Authorized Dealer licences; they cannot receive export-proceeds surrenders, cannot issue valid SARB balance-of-payments purpose codes, and cannot maintain the inward-foreign-investment reference required for future dividend remittances. Additionally, a South African-resident company is prohibited under Exchange Control Regulation 10 from holding any offshore bank or EMI account without prior SARB FinSurv approval. The compliant alternative is the Authorized Dealer's own multi-currency digital platform, which delivers comparable speed, upfront pricing, and API connectivity while preserving exchange-control legality.
