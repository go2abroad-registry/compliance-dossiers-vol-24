Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue, Government of Samoa (official portal: Samoa Ministry for Revenue Online Services / polasamoa.ws), operating the Taxpayer Registration System under the Tax Administration Act 2015 and Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2008.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity must engage a licensed Samoa registered agent who submits: (i) certified copy of the International Company Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour; (ii) Certificate of Registered Office; (iii) board resolution confirming the registered agent and local address; (iv) beneficial ownership declaration and AML/CFT identification for directors/shareholders; (v) completed Ministry for Revenue TIN application form (RDF-1). The application is filed online through the Samoa Ministry for Revenue e-services portal; the Ministry issues the TIN/VAGST registration certificate within 5-10 business days. For International Companies exercising the statutory exemption, the TIN is issued as a non-filing exempt entity on condition that the entity conducts no local business in Samoa.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident entity must engage a licensed Samoa registered agent who submits: (i) certified copy of the International Company Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour; (ii) Certificate of Registered Office; (iii) board resolution confirming the registered agent and local address; (iv) beneficial ownership declaration and AML/CFT identification for directors/shareholders; (v) completed Ministry for Revenue TIN application form (RDF-1). The application is filed online through the Samoa Ministry for Revenue e-services portal; the Ministry issues the TIN/VAGST registration certificate within 5-10 business days. For International Companies exercising the statutory exemption, the TIN is issued as a non-filing exempt entity on condition that the entity conducts no local business in Samoa.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election exists under Samoa law; an International Company is statutorily tax-exempt under the International Companies Act 1987. The entity may voluntarily elect VAGST registration solely if it makes taxable supplies within Samoa, but such election is inadvisable and generally not available to offshore SPAC vehicles. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable and anti-hybrid avoidance rules under the Tax Administration Act 2015 disapply any election used to create double non-taxation. A VAGST registration election, once made, binds the entity for 24 months and requires local tax filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 — annual taxable turnover threshold calculated over the preceding 12 months or expected next 12 months; compulsory registration applies to any person making taxable supplies in Samoa above this threshold. A pure offshore International Company has no Samoan nexus and no VAGST filing obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods qualify for 0% VAGST if the goods are exported within 28 days of invoice and supplied to a non-resident recipient outside Samoa. Offshore services, including SPAC investment management, escrow administration, and M&A advisory services supplied to non-residents, qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is located outside Samoa, the benefit of the service is consumed offshore, and the service contract contains a clear export declaration clause. Claim requires the supplier to hold contemporaneous evidence of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs Service, approved electronic declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Samoa
  • Commercial Invoice and export contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank statement evidencing payment from offshore
  • Certificate of Origin and insurance document where applicable

No real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing clearance mandate is currently implemented in Samoa. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue sequentially numbered tax invoices and retain them for 7 years. Monthly VAGST returns are filed electronically via the Ministry for Revenue e-services portal by the 25th day of the following month. No digital stamping or continuous transaction control system is imposed, but taxpayers must retain complete transactional data for audit retrieval.

The Ministry for Revenue may claw back zero-rating benefits and assess VAGST at 15% plus interest where documentation is incomplete. The Tax Administration Act 2015 imposes shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for intentional disregard, late payment penalties of 10% plus accruing interest, and a civil penalty of WST 200,000 for knowingly providing false export evidence. Fraudulent zero-rating claims are subjected to retrospective reassessment without limitation periods (up to 10 years in all other cases) and criminal prosecution remedies including fines and imprisonment for directors and registered agents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa's legal tender is the Samoan Tala (WST) under a managed-float exchange-rate regime anchored to a trade-weighted currency basket of major partners (chiefly AUD, NZD, USD). Current-account receipts and payments (visible trade, services, travel, dividends, interest) are substantively convertible: authorized dealers may process them without central-bank prior approval if supporting commercial documentation (invoice, contract, transfer-pricing file) is presented. Capital-account transactions (external borrowing, inbound/outbound FDI, purchase of foreign securities, intercompany loans, guarantees, and cross-border transfers not evidenced by current trade) remain approval-based for resident domestic persons under CBS guidelines. Profit remittance for resident Samoan companies is subject to (i) CBS exchange-control assent (Form EX/4), (ii) withholding-tax clearance and a tax-residency/compliance certificate from the Ministry of Revenue, and (iii) evidence that local statutory obligations are satisfied. By contrast, an International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987, as amended, is carved out of the Exchange Control Act: it may lawfully maintain foreign-currency accounts, borrow abroad, accept sponsor subscriptions, and remit distributions or redemption proceeds to non-residents without CBS prior approval, provided the IC conducts no Samoan-domestic business, transacts only with non-residents or fellow ICs/offshore entities, and preserves its offshore status with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). Dividends and capital-redemption payments from an IC carry no Samoan withholding tax, and the sole practical remittance hurdle is the bank's documentary verification of the distribution resolution, solvency/asset-maintenance statement, and the IC's certificate of good standing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1978 (Part III), resident exporters are subject to a mandatory 100% surrender/repatriation obligation: all export proceeds must be repatriated to an authorized dealer in Samoa within 30 days of the date of receipt in foreign currency, and converted to Samoan Tala (WST) unless the CBS has issued a general or specific retention exemption. Failure to surrender within the statutory window constitutes a contravention enforceable by penalty and potential denial of future FX facilities. The CBS routinely grants retention exemptions for (i) export proceeds held temporarily to discharge foreign-currency trade liabilities and (ii) entities classed as offshore/international companies that have no Samoan-source export earnings. A Samoa IC-SPAC has no statutory export-surrender exposure because its treasury inflows are subscription and escrow proceeds, not merchandise export earnings; nonetheless, any incidental foreign-currency receipt must be disclosed through the bank's exchange-control declaration (ED/EX series) to preserve the IC's compliance record.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), officially styled Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa; statutory foreign-exchange regulatory power is delegated by the Minister of Finance under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and administered under the Exchange Control Regulations 1978. The CBS licenses authorized dealers (commercial banks), supervises all foreign-exchange transactions, capital-flow approvals, currency surveillance, and balance-of-payments statistical reporting in Samoa.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for independent offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Revolut Business, Currenxie) and MEDIUM for regulated regional EMIs (Australia/NZ-licensed or APAC payment-service providers) that maintain physical correspondent clearing and accept Samoa-incorporated ICs only under enhanced due diligence. Legal constraints: (i) the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018 and Proceeds of Crime Act 2018 impose CDD, UBO verification, transaction monitoring, and record-keeping obligations on any payment service provider handling IC funds; (ii) pure offshore EMIs systematically reject WS-IC SPAC structures because the blank-check business model presents pooled third-party funds, no underlying trading history, and indeterminate future counterparties; (iii) U.S.-dollar clearing through EMIs is further restricted by the U.S. PATRIOT Act shell-bank prohibitions and correspondent-bank de-risking policies, which disqualify offshore companies lacking high-quality UBO and source-of-funds documentation. A WS-IC SPAC should therefore use a tier-1 Samoan licensed bank as its anchor treasury account and may use an EMI only for non-core disbursements (e.g., subscription administration or investor communication fees), never as the escrow depository.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Sponsor equity and IPO/trust proceeds must enter Samoa through licensed authorized-dealer channels: remit via SWIFT MT103 with charges 'OUR' to preserve gross value, using ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI (equity subscription) or SUB (subscription), with a remittance reference quoting the IC's name, the escrow/trust account identifier, and the subscription agreement number. Because the IC is exempt from Exchange Control Act prior-approval requirements, no CBS permit is required; however, the receiving bank must file a balance-of-payments notification (EX/IC series) with the CBS within five business days, attaching the board resolution authorizing capital acceptance, an extract of the register of members showing the sponsor subscription, and the bank's completed CDD file. Escrow proceeds from the public-offering trust may be released to the Samoan operating account only upon the trustee's written release confirmation and satisfaction of the business-combination deadline condition. All inbound wires should be pre-notified to the bank's compliance desk at least 48 hours in advance, with full originator structure details, to avoid a freeze triggered by 'unknown high-risk vehicle' flags.Outbound payments (target-company acquisition consideration, redeemable-share redemptions, sponsor fees, and operational disbursements) clear via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for wire amounts, with MT202/MT202COV cover instructions for the correspondent leg; commercial letters of credit governed by UCP600 are used for any goods-import settlement; and the escrow trustee's release order is mandatory before segregated trust funds are drawn. The bank requires: original or certified closing statement (share purchase agreement, business-combination closing sheet), board approval minutes, the trustee's release certificate, and a payment purpose text referencing acquisition/share-purchase consideration. Payments above the authorized-dealer reporting threshold (WST 500,000 or USD equivalent) are reported statistically by the bank to the CBS; the IC exemption does not waive statistical reporting or correspondent-bank screening. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 aggregate related-party transaction threshold (practical administrative threshold) above which contemporaneous Master File, Local File, and benchmarking reports are demanded; below this threshold a summary TP declaration in the annual return suffices.

Economic substance scope: Samoa applies Economic Substance Guidelines under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) and the OECD Harmful Tax Practices regime. A SPAC must carry on substantive management, hold board meetings with Samoan director quorum, maintain the escrow/reinvestment decisions in Samoa, and file an annual substance declaration; failures result in revocation of the exempt status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion & Abuse of International Company Exemption

Statutory sanction: Income tax assessment at 27% on concealed profits, penalty of 100% of tax evaded, civil fine of WST 500,000, and seizure of the SPAC escrow assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the shortfall under section 29 of the Samoa Tax Administration Act; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 7 years, travel ban, and personal disqualification as director.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of the International Company registration, freezing of all bank and escrow accounts, and administrative penalty of WST 250,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil and order directors to restore all escrow funds to investors; directors receive a 10-year ban on serving as officers of any Samoan company and are personally liable for unpaid corporate debts.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 2,000,000 under the Samoa Money Laundering and Prevention of Terrorism Financing Act; licence cancellation of the registered agent; freezing of escrow accounts and investor property.

Director personal liability: Directors, nominees and beneficial owners face imprisonment up to 15 years, personal fines, asset confiscation, and an Interpol red notice upon issuance of an arrest warrant.
Withholding Tax Evasion / Profit Repatriation Misinformation

Statutory sanction: WHT re-assessment at 20% statutory rate, a 25% non-disclosure penalty, 2% monthly interest, and disallowance of all related deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors as responsible officers are personally answerable for the unpaid WHT; intentional filing of false returns subjects each director to 3 years imprisonment and a personal fine equal to 50% of the evaded withholding tax.
