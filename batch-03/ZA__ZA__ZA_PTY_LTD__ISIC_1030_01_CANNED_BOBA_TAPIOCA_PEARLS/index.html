Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the (Pty) Ltd is a South African tax-resident company taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate applicable to years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023; South Africa imposes no provincial or municipal income tax and the entity is not eligible for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

South African law does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Statutory elections relevant to this manufacturing Pty Ltd include: (i) small business corporation status under section 12E of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, which provides graduated rates for qualifying entities with gross income not exceeding ZAR 20 million and all shareholders being natural persons; (ii) voluntary VAT registration under section 23(4) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 once taxable turnover reaches ZAR 50,000; (iii) group relief elections under section 41(6) for intra-group asset transfers; (iv) headquarter company election under section 9I for regional service operations; and (v) nomination of a public officer under section 232 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Hybrid mismatch provisions contained in sections 8F, 8G, 8GA and 23A of the Income Tax Act apply automatically and do not confer elective outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in taxable supplies during the preceding twelve months is the compulsory VAT registration threshold; voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000. A manufacturing exporter of canned boba tapioca pearls must register once the threshold is exceeded in order to claim input VAT refunds on exported zero-rated supplies; there is no separate threshold for exporters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the supplier sells and physically removes goods from South Africa to a non-resident destination within 90 days of the time of supply, or within an extended period approved by SARS, and retains the documentary proof prescribed by section 11(3). The (Pty) Ltd exporting canned boba tapioca pearls must issue a 0% VAT export invoice, record the customs export declaration number, and hold transport and payment documentation establishing that the goods left South Africa and were delivered to the foreign consignee; if these conditions are not satisfied, the supply is treated as standard-rated at 15%.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500/EX1 electronic freight declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a South African authorised dealer confirming receipt of foreign-currency payment
  • VAT 204 export invoice / commercial invoice bearing the export declaration reference
  • Packing list and container/weight documentation for the canned boba consignment
  • Food export certificate or phytosanitary certificate where required by the destination authority

South Africa does not currently mandate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing regime comparable to those in Latin America or Italy. VAT vendors must issue tax invoices that satisfy section 20 of the VAT Act and file VAT 201 returns electronically via SARS eFiling, typically monthly or bi-monthly. Export consignments are cleared electronically through the SARS eCustoms platform using SAD500/EX1 declarations, which are subject to automated risk-based processing, documentary verification, and physical inspection where warranted; SARS continues to consult on a centralised e-invoicing solution under its modernisation programme.

SARS may disallow zero-rating and raise a retrospective output-tax assessment at 15% plus interest where goods are not exported within the statutory 90-day window or the extended period granted by SARS. Underdeclared tax attracts an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax avoided in terms of sections 212 and 223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, together with interest. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute a criminal offence punishable by a fine and/or imprisonment of up to 10 years; VAT refunds may be suspended under section 15 of the VAT Act pending full substantiation, and the directors of the Pty Ltd may incur personal liability where the company is unable to pay the assessed VAT.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a managed currency regime with full convertibility on current account and administratively controlled convertibility on capital account. Trade payments, service fees, royalties, interest and profit remittances are freely convertible provided the underlying transaction is evidenced and, where applicable, supported by a SARS tax clearance or compliance status. Capital inflows (foreign equity subscription into a resident private company) are freely permitted and are registered with the authorised dealer; capital outflows by residents, foreign borrowing by residents, and portfolio flows remain subject to FinSurv approval thresholds and prescribed return-filing. The Rand (ZAR) is tradeable in the onshore interbank and forward markets; there is no separate official and parallel exchange rate and no blocked-rand mechanism for genuine commercial transactions. For a manufacturing exporter such as ZA_PTY_LTD, trading receipts enter the ZAR market through an authorised dealer with no mandatory conversion ratio, but every inward and outward transfer must be tagged with the appropriate SARB/StatsSA balance-of-payments purpose code and archived for a minimum of five years.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the South African Exchange Control Regulations (R.1111) read with the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers, export proceeds of ZA_PTY_LTD must be fully received and allotted to an authorised dealer in South Africa within 180 days from the date of export shipment, unless the dealer or FinSurv grants an extension for bona fide longer settlement terms. The exporter may retain 100% of the export receipts in an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with an authorised dealer for working capital or import requirements; there is currently no mandatory rand-conversion ratio, the former percentage-surrender obligation having been phased out in the 2021 SARB reforms. Each shipment must be supported by a commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, SAD500 customs declaration, and proof of receipt/payment, and must be reported electronically by the dealer on the CP/EX-1 return to FinSurv. Late receipt beyond 180 days exposes the exporter to an administrative penalty or requires a waiver application with documented justification; pre-shipment and post-shipment export financing also require dealer approval and must be repaid from the generated receipts.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting jointly with the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary clearing rail, Medium as a supplementary processor. South Africa does not issue standalone EMI licences under a distinct fintech passporting regime; non-bank payment and cross-border money movement fall under the SARB National Payment System Department (NPSD) and the Banks Act 94 of 1990. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Enterprise, Airwallex, Nium, Currencycloud, OFX) cannot independently hold ZAR settlement accounts, cannot issue e-money in South Africa without a registered bank sponsor, and are not authorised dealers for exchange-control purposes; they may only be used as technology/instruction utilities routing through a licensed South African authorised dealer or a licensed payment service provider. Trade settlements, 180-day export allotment, FCA maintenance and FX hedging must all pass through SARB-authorised dealer channels; using an unsponsored EMI for statutory receipts would create an exchange-control compliance breach.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign parent or shareholder equity subscription must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to ZA_PTY_LTD's ZAR or USD account at a SARB authorised dealer using the balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment equity capital increase (e.g., code 1B1/equity participation in a resident company), referencing the CIPC share allotment and the board resolution approving the issue. The authorised dealer will verify the source of funds, issue a certificate of inward transfer/capital import and record the shares as having been issued to non-residents; ZA_PTY_LTD must retain the endorsed share certificates and the dealer’s registration evidence because these will be required to support future dividend remittances or share disposals by the non-resident holder under SARB regulations. Shareholder loans are treated differently: they constitute foreign borrowing requiring dealer notification and, above the applicable FinSurv threshold, prior approval, and repayment is subject to interest withholding tax (15%, treaty-reduced). Once the capital is registered, it can be co-mingled only with the operational ZAR account if the dealer approves; alternatively it can be held in a capital escrow sub-account pending asset deployment and SARB reporting. No blocked-rand balance will arise for this ordinary production enterprise; consumers and suppliers may pay and be paid in ZAR at the prevailing market and forward rates.Supplier payments for imported tapioca starch, sweetener, packaging and aseptic consumables should be executed via SWIFT MT103 with a trade purpose code (e.g., import of goods/services or trade credit) through the authorised dealer, accompanied by the purchase order, commercial invoice, bill of lading, SAD500 where relevant, and proof of delivery retained for at least five years. Larger shipments and open-account credit lines should be supported by irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued by a South African authorised dealer under UCP 600 (sight or usance), or by documentary collections (D/P and D/A) at the discretion of the counterparty; the bank will finance the import cycle with pre-import and post-import trade loans booked in both ZAR and foreign currency. Export receipts must be recovered within 180 days of shipment and every export contract should therefore include clear payment tenor clauses; ZA_PTY_LTD can use a portion of its export FCA balance to prepay suppliers and thereby reduce conversion risk. There is no FX priority system allocating scarce currency to manufacturing imports; the ordinary interbank market clears all legitimate trade payments, and the bank's trade desk must pre-approve any unusual routing, back-to-back LC structures or consecutive third-party disbursements to satisfy the FIC's money-laundering and terrorist-financing reporting obligations.Deliverable FX forwards (DFF) concluded with an SARB authorised dealer against evidenced export receivables or import payables, with tenors generally up to 12 months and rollovers permitted while the underlying exposure remains.Vanilla bought FX options (calls and puts) on USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR and CNY/ZAR with physical settlement through the authorised dealer, used solely to hedge committed trade transaction exposure.FX swaps (near-leg and far-leg combinations, including tom-next, spot-forward and forward-forward) used to bridge short-term working-capital gaps among ZAR, USD, EUR and CNY, provided the liquidity mismatch is explained by invoiced trade transactions.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) associated with a FinSurv-registered foreign-currency trade finance or term loan facility, converting foreign-currency debt service into ZAR on a matched-maturity basis.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted onshore for USD/ZAR because the ZAR is a deliverable tradeable currency; NDF or offshore-settled hedges may be used only for illiquid third-currency crosses (e.g., USD/CNH or USD/ZAR in limited offshore centres) where the obligation is a genuine hedge and the onshore authorised dealer confirms that no onshore settlement or rollover occurs.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary clearing rail, Medium as a supplementary processor. South Africa does not issue standalone EMI licences under a distinct fintech passporting regime; non-bank payment and cross-border money movement fall under the SARB National Payment System Department (NPSD) and the Banks Act 94 of 1990. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Enterprise, Airwallex, Nium, Currencycloud, OFX) cannot independently hold ZAR settlement accounts, cannot issue e-money in South Africa without a registered bank sponsor, and are not authorised dealers for exchange-control purposes; they may only be used as technology/instruction utilities routing through a licensed South African authorised dealer or a licensed payment service provider. Trade settlements, 180-day export allotment, FCA maintenance and FX hedging must all pass through SARB-authorised dealer channels; using an unsponsored EMI for statutory receipts would create an exchange-control compliance breach.
