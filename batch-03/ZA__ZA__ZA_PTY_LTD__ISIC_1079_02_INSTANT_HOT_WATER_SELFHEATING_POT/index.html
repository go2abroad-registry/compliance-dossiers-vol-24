Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); mandatory digital portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za), supplemented by the SARS MobiApp and branch offices; customs matters administered via SARS Customs Desks and the SAD500 electronic cargo system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested (Pty) Ltd must first be incorporated under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), obtaining the CIPC registration number and COR14.1 incorporation certificate. Within 21 business days of incorporation, the entity must register with SARS for income tax, obtaining a 10-digit income tax reference number. VAT registration (45XXXXXXXXX format) is compulsory if annual taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 or is reasonably expected to exceed this threshold in a 12-month period; voluntary VAT registration is available from ZAR 50,000. PAYE registration is required upon first employing staff. A customs code (importer/exporter) must be procured through the SARS Customs eFiling portal before any export or import. Non-resident directors or shareholders without a SARS tax reference number must obtain a Non-Resident Tax Reference Number (NRTN); a public officer resident in South Africa must be appointed for the company. All applications are processed via eFiling after CIPC authentication; biometric verification may be required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in South Africa. Statutory elections available to a (Pty) Ltd include: (i) Small Business Corporation (SBC) status election under s12E of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 for qualifying closely-held companies (progressive rates below the 27% headline rate); (ii) Headquarter Company (HQ) election under s9I exempting foreign dividends, interest, royalties, and capital gains on foreign subsidiaries; (iii) corporate restructuring rollover elections under s41-47 (asset-for-share, intra-group transactions, amalgamation transactions, liquidation distributions); (iv) VAT grouping election under s50 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991 for connected persons to file consolidated VAT returns; (v) s11D research and development tax incentive election requiring pre-approval from the Department of Science and Innovation and SARS; (vi) hybrid mismatch mandatory adjustments under s8F, s8FA, and s23M (conduit debt and hybrid shares) with no elective disapplication.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies made in any 12-month period (s23(1)); voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000 (s23(2)) subject to SARS approval; non-resident suppliers of electronic services must register at ZAR 50,000; grouping threshold based on aggregate turnover of connected persons

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of movable goods under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the goods are supplied to the exporter's customer and physically removed from the Republic within 90 days of the date of supply, provided acceptable documentary proof of export is obtained. The self-heating instant food pots (ISIC 1079) qualify as movable goods. Services physically rendered to non-residents outside South Africa qualify under s11(2)(l); services supplied to non-residents relating to movable property outside South Africa qualify under s11(2)(a). Supplies to registered IDZ/SEZ enterprises under s11(1)(l) also qualify for zero-rating. The exporter must retain the tax invoice and export documentation for at least five years. Zero-rating is denied where goods are diverted for local use or where the export does not occur within the prescribed 90-day window unless SARS grants an extension under s11(3).

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) containing the unique Movement Reference Number (MRN) and SARS Customs release/stamp confirmation
  • Original Bill of Lading (marine), Air Waybill (air), Road Manifest (ZA-DW) (road), or Rail Consignment Note (rail) evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the SAD500 description, quantity, and value
  • Proof of export: SARS Customs export clearance report, e.g., Export Clearance Certificate or Message Acknowledgement Report (Message Type 331)
  • Inward Foreign Investment remittance certificate / Exchange Control Form (MP337 or bank BFX report) issued by an authorized dealer bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • VAT 264 declaration form or SAPO export certificate where postal/courier channels are used
  • Shipping instructions, insurance certificate, and letter of appointment of the clearing agent (where applicable)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API clearance regime is currently in force in South Africa. SARS mandates the issuance of a full VAT tax invoice within 21 days of supply (s20 of VAT Act), retained for audit. SARS is progressively implementing its E-Invoicing programme under the VAT Modernisation roadmap aligned with Peppol and SANS 17090-5 standards; a discussion paper has been issued and phased mandatory adoption is anticipated for large, medium, and small businesses, but no statutory enforcement date has been gazetted. Voluntary e-invoices are accepted provided authenticity, integrity, and readability are ensured through an auditable control framework; no SARS clearance token or stamp is required.

SARS may retrospectively claw back claimed zero-ratings and assess output VAT at 15% plus interest where export documentation is not produced within 21 days of request (s31 VAT Act), where goods do not leave South Africa within 90 days (s11(3) deeming provision), or where the tax invoice is invalid or incomplete. Fraudulent or negligent export zero-rating claims attract understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011: 10% (standard case), 25% (failure to take reasonable care), 50% (position lacking reasonable grounds), 75% (intentional tax evasion), and 150% (gross negligence), increased by 2x to a 200% cap for repeat offenses. Interest runs on outstanding amounts from the original due date. Criminal prosecution under s234 TAA and s59 of the VAT Act may result in fines up to ZAR 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to five years. VAT refund claims are suspended under s190 TAA pending completion of audit verification; exporters must maintain full documentary files for at least five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current-account trade transactions; capital-account flows remain subject to the exchange-control regime. Inbound direct equity for a non-financial (Pty) Ltd requires no prior FinSurv approval but must be reported by the receiving Authorised Dealer on the monthly Balance of Payments (BOP) return. Non-resident profit and dividend remittances are freely permitted upon presentation of audited financial statements, a board dividend declaration, and SARS tax clearance confirming dividends tax (DT) or applicable treaty withholding. Shareholder loans must be registered with the Authorised Dealer, bear arm's-length interest, and are ring-fenced from conversion to equity without a Form BI-1 application.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for self-heating food kits must be repatriated to South Africa or credited to a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with an Authorised Dealer within 180 days of shipment date, extendable to 365 days on documented commercial grounds. Since the 2021 Currency and Exchanges Manual reform, there is no mandatory conversion ratio into ZAR; the statutory obligation is repatriation, not surrender. The exporter must present the SARS Customs export declaration, transport document (bill of lading/airway bill), and commercial invoice to the Authorised Dealer to close the export file; 100% of proceeds may be retained offshore for future trade settlement, hedging, or reinvestment within the FCA framework.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and operating through SARB-appointed Authorised Dealers (commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - offshore digital EMIs (Payoneer, Wise, Currencycloud) are legally viable only as supplementary collection and disbursement rails, not as the primary operating bank, because all ZAR-denominated settlement must clear through a SARB-licensed Authorised Dealer and BOP reporting obligations cannot be delegated to an unlicensed EMI. Wise South Africa (Pty) Ltd holds an Authorised Dealer (with Restrictions) licence and may provide qualifying cross-border payment services; any EMI proposal must evidence a formal partnership with a Tier-1 SA bank for ZAR settlement, and EMIs are categorically unsuitable for the Capital Escrow Account mandate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All non-resident equity injections route via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the shareholder's offshore bank through correspondent banking to the Authorised Dealer in Johannesburg, using the BOP financial-account purpose code for 'Equity Capital' (BPM6). The bank must receive the share allotment resolution, updated CIPC share register, and the source-of-funds dossier before crediting the Capital Escrow Account, and must report the transaction on the SARB BOP return within the monthly reporting cycle. Subsequent conversion of shareholder loans to equity requires a Form BI-1 through the Authorised Dealer, while any repatriation of contributed capital requires FinSurv approval or emigration regime treatment and is therefore documented at onboarding.Import payables for exothermic chemical components and packaging settle by SWIFT MT103 direct transfer or irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued under UCP600, with commercial documents presented through the Authorised Dealer matched against SARS customs clearance. The ZAR domestic treasury leg settles across SAMOS (South African Multiple Option Settlement) for local supplier payments, while SADC-regional disbursements use SADC-RTGS in ZAR or USD where the counterparty bank is a SADC participant. Every outbound trade payment requires the invoice, transport document, and customs entry to support the dealer's import register; export receivables are collected via MT103 into the FCA and may be pre-hedged under the underlying trade exposure.Onshore deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) on USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR, and GBP/ZAR for hedging confirmed export receivables and import payables of the self-heating meal production cycle.Foreign-currency options (purchased puts and calls) offered by the Authorised Dealer for premium-capped exposure management on seasonal procurement and tender pricing.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps applied to long-term ZAR-denominated shareholder loans or term funding to convert liability economics into USD/EUR aligned with export revenue.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) permitted only for hedging currencies with no onshore ZAR deliverable liquidity pool (e.g., CNH/ZAR or INR/ZAR); the ZAR itself is not transacted as an NDF by South African residents because an operational onshore deliverable market exists and offshore ZAR NDFs require FinSurv approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File / Master File thresholds: A South African taxpayer must prepare full transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) where the aggregate value of connected-person cross-border transactions exceeds ZAR 100 million in the relevant year of assessment. Master File (global group documentation) is mandatory for a South African resident entity that is part of a multinational group whose consolidated revenue exceeds ZAR 1 billion. The Country-by-Country (CbC) report filing threshold is also ZAR 1 billion group revenue in the preceding financial year. Even below these thresholds, taxpayers must maintain adequate contemporaneous documentation to support arm's length pricing.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the style of the UAE or Bahrain. However, the Income Tax Act contains substantial anti-avoidance provisions (Sections 80A-L) that disregard transactions whose sole or main purpose is to secure a tax benefit and which lack commercial substance. A shell company with no physical presence, no employees, no active decision-making in South Africa, and no real operational substance is vulnerable to GAAR, denial of treaty benefits, and reclassification of transactions. SARS also examines whether a company has the 'substance' of a resident entity through its place of effective management and the attribution of profits to a permanent establishment under BEPS Action 7.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Under-Declaration / False Tax Return)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act, Section 235: a fine of up to R10 million and/or imprisonment for a period not exceeding 10 years. Additionally, SARS may assess a 100% understatement penalty on the shortfall, plus statutory interest from the due date of payment.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in or authorises the false return is guilty as principal offender and subject to the same criminal sanction. Under Section 159 of the TAA, directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts, including penalties and interest, if the failure to pay was caused by their negligence or intentional conduct. SARS may also obtain a court order restraining the director from traveling abroad until the outstanding tax is settled; in serious cases a director may be arrested and prosecuted, risking imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under GAAR (Sections 80A-L), SARS may disregard the shell company's separate existence and reallocate the taxable income to the controlling person or the actual place of effective management. This can lead to a full reassessment at the 27% rate with 100% understatement penalties and interest. The company is also denied DTA relief on dividends/interest/royalties due to lack of beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the taxes reassessed if they caused the company to act as a facade and the corporate veil is pierced under the Companies Act, Section 77 and common law. Directors may be interdicted from leaving the country and may face personal tax recovery where the company's assets are insufficient. Criminal liability arises if the arrangement is considered a fraudulent scheme.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement (Profit Shifting)

Statutory sanction: SARS is empowered to adjust the taxable income to reflect an arm's length price using secondary adjustments (deemed dividend, deemed capital contribution). If the taxpayer has not prepared contemporaneous TP documentation, the understatement penalty can be 100% of the tax shortfall (intentional disregard). Interest is charged at the statutory rate. Persistent non-compliance may result in civil audits and criminal prosecution under the TAA for material non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: A director who signs or authorises a tax return containing a TP adjustment without adequate basis is considered reckless. SARS can assess the personal liability of directors jointly and severally for the company's TP adjustment tax and penalties where the director was knowingly involved in tax avoidance. Directors could also be barred from departing South Africa pending full payment, and criminal penalties up to 10 years imprisonment apply in egregious cases.
AML / Financial Reporting Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FICA imposes administrative penalties of up to ZAR 10.25 million for serious non-compliance, and criminal fines of up to ZAR 100 million and imprisonment for up to 30 years in the case of repeated or wilful failure. The company's directors may also face a debarment from holding financial roles. SARS can also invoke the general reporting obligation to require disclosure of cash movements above a threshold.

Director personal liability: Under FICA, directors are criminally liable if they knowingly or negligently fail to manage AML obligations. Personal fines and imprisonment are possible. Directors who siphon off cash structures or permit money laundering can be made civilly liable to repay the proceeds. Travel bans may be imposed as part of money laundering prosecutions, and the court may seize personal assets of directors as proceeds of crime.
