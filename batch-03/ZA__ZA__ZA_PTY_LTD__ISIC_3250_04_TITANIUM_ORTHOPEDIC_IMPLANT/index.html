Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — www.sars.gov.za; eFiling portal: https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated (Pty) Ltd, the first step is registration with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) for a registration number. The CIPC registration number (prefixed with 'K' for companies) serves as the basis for subsequent SARS registration. The company representative (public officer) must register the entity for Income Tax, VAT (where relevant), and Payroll (PAYE) through SARS eFiling or by visiting a SARS branch. Required documents include the Memorandum of Incorporation, CIPC registration certificate, identity documents of directors/public officer, and supporting bank account details. SARS issues a unique Income Tax Reference Number (with a suffix unique (e.g., '98...')) and, where applicable, a VAT number (e.g., '49...'). The registration must be completed within 21 business days of incorporation or inception of business activities, and the public officer must be appointed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa applies a classical entity-level tax system: no check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a Pty Ltd. The entity is a separate legal and taxable person subject to corporate income tax at 27%. Available statutory elections relevant to a manufacturing entity include: (i) s11D claim for additional research and development deduction for approved projects; (ii) s12C accelerated depreciation on manufacturing assets (20% initial allowance plus 20% per annum over five years, or 12.5% per annum over eight years for assets used in a process of manufacture); (iii) s12E tax incentive for small business corporations (annual turnover below R20m); (iv) s24J timing elections for interest accrual; (v) VAT cash-basis or invoice-basis accounting election (turnover below R2.5m); and (vi) electronic filing mandates under Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Hybrid mismatch rules (s25J) suppress any preferential election.

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods under s11(1)(a) VAT Act 89 of 1991. The supplier must be a resident vendor; the goods must be exported by the supplier or the buyer under a sale in the ordinary course of business; the vendor must obtain and retain documentary proof of exportation within the required period. The export must be physically delivered outside South Africa within 90 days of the time of the supply, and the vendor must be in possession of, within 30 days of the date of exportation or longer as permitted by SARS, documentary proof that the goods were exported. For manufactured medical devices such as titanium orthopedic implants, zero-rating also applies to services directly connected with the goods. Exported goods are zero-rated also under s11(1)(b) where supplied to an exported purchaser, subject to conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 series)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill with proof of physical departure from South Africa
  • Commercial invoice / tax invoice referencing the export transaction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank receipt)
  • Proof of payment / telegram/pre-advice showing foreign currency consideration
  • Goods' passport / packing list demonstrating the ultimate destination

South Africa does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system (continuous transaction control). SARS mandates electronic filing of the VAT 201 return via eFiling; suppliers may issue electronic tax invoices provided the vendor stores a true reflection and the invoice satisfies s20(4) of the VAT Act. Suppliers and customers must retain electronic invoice records in original form for 5 years. SARS is progressively standardising e-invoicing with a forthcoming CTC model; meanwhile, high-risk vendors may be issued VAT registration certificates requiring enhanced e-submission. For exports, fully digital customs declarations are filed through the Customs Duty platform of SARS (Customs eFiling / SAD500). No government-issued digital stamp or invoice clearance token is applicable for standard B2B export manufacturing supplies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees, provided the transaction is routed through an Authorised Dealer and supported by the required documentary evidence. Capital account transactions, including inward equity subscriptions and non-resident shareholdings, are freely permitted for a South African (Pty) Ltd, but they must be reported through the AD using the prescribed FinSurv reporting forms, notably Form Q1/Q2 for inward investment and Form N for foreign holdings. Profit remittance hurdles are administrative and documentary rather than quantitative: the AD must be satisfied that the dividend is declared out of distributable reserves, the company holds a valid SARS tax compliance status, Dividends Tax of 20% is withheld, and the beneficial ownership and source-of-funds requirements under FICA are met. No exchange control approval is required for the repatriation of dividends by non-resident shareholders once the transaction has been reported and the AD has verified that the funds are not derived from a blocked, illegal, or unreported account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the South African Exchange Control Regulations and the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, all export proceeds in foreign currency must be received in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days of the date of shipment. Upon receipt, the proceeds must be surrendered to the AD and converted into ZAR within 30 days of the date of receipt, unless the exporter has obtained FinSurv approval to credit the proceeds to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA). Where an FCA facility is granted, the exporter may retain up to 100% of the export proceeds in foreign currency without an immediate conversion obligation; without such approval, the default surrender obligation is effectively 100% conversion to ZAR. Exporters are also required to comply with the Electronic Export Data (EDE) system administered by SARS Customs, to submit a written undertaking from the foreign buyer where required, and to ensure that any export on a consignment basis is authorised by FinSurv in advance. Invoicing may be in ZAR or foreign currency, but where invoices are denominated in ZAR, surrender rules apply only to the foreign-exchange component.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual. Foreign exchange activities are delegated by National Treasury to Authorised Dealers (ADs), which are commercial banks licenced to conduct exchange control business on behalf of FinSurv.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury rails. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank fintech providers are not licensed Authorised Dealers under the South African Exchange Control Regulations and therefore cannot lawfully clear export proceeds, surrender foreign currency, or hold FCA balances on behalf of a South African resident company. Such platforms may be used only as ancillary collection mechanisms for small-ticket commercial samples, marketing deposits, or overseas distributor reimbursements, provided the funds are subsequently upstreamed into a South African bank account via SWIFT MT103 through an Authorised Dealer. Legal constraints include FICA remote onboarding limits, SARB exchange control monitoring, and the requirement that all foreign currency conversions take place onshore through an AD. The recommended approach is to use a local AD as the sole regulated liquidity rail and to use fintech only for client-facing payment collection where the end settlement is automatically routed to the South African treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the non-resident shareholder must be received through an Authorised Dealer in South Africa via SWIFT MT103 or MT202, with field 70 (Remittance Information) clearly stating 'Inward equity subscription / share capital contribution for ZA Pty Ltd, ISIC 3250 medical device manufacturing.' The AD will credit the funds to a ring-fenced capital escrow or inward investment suspense account and will require completion of the prescribed FinSurv reporting, typically Form Q1, before the funds are transferred to the company's operational account. The remitting bank's narrative must identify the ultimate beneficial owner and the purpose code for an inward direct investment in equity; any discrepancy in the remitter name or missing attribution will cause the transfer to be held until documentary verification is complete. The AD will obtain the prevailing market spot rate if conversion to ZAR is requested, or will hold the foreign currency in the linked FCA pending the exporter's instruction. The issuing of shares is evidenced by a board resolution, shareholder consent, and CIPC return of allotments, and the AD must be provided with copies to enable FinSurv post-reporting verification.Outbound trade payments, including payments to overseas suppliers of titanium alloy powder, machining tools, and laboratory testing services, are settled by SWIFT MT103 from the South African AD, supported by invoices, purchase orders, bills of lading, and the SARS customs declaration (SAD500). For export sales to foreign hospitals and distributors, the preferred clearing rail is a commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600, with the South African exporter as beneficiary; proceeds are collected through the confirming bank and routed to the exporter's FCA or ZAR account via the AD. Document collection transactions under URC522 are also possible but carry greater settlement risk; the recommended framework is to use irrevocable LCs for large-ticket implant shipments and MT103 for recurring supplier payments. All outbound foreign payments are reported by the AD through the electronic movement of funds and trade surveillance systems, and the exporter must maintain a complete trail of contract, invoice, customs, and inspection evidence to support the underlying current account nature of each transaction.Forward Exchange Contract (FEC): USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR, and CNY/ZAR forward contracts entered into with an Authorised Dealer bank, fully covered by documented export receivables or import payables; standard tenor is up to 12 months, with longer tenors requiring prior FinSurv approval.Option Range or Collar: currency option collar structures struck with an Authorised Dealer to hedge a fixed or determinable future export receipt stream; permissible where the underlying exposure is evidenced by a signed export contract or purchase order.Cross-Currency Swap: USD/ZAR or EUR/ZAR cross-currency swap, permissible with prior FinSurv approval and a matched underlying such as a foreign-currency trade finance facility or a contracted export programme with a notional value linked to the company's financial projections.Foreign Currency Call or Put Options: deliverable options purchased through an Authorised Dealer for hedging offshore receivables or payables, subject to local payment of the premium and settlement through the AD.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF): not permissible onshore for South African residents; offshore NDFs are available only to non-resident counterparties hedging ZAR exposure and may not be used by this company as a resident treasury instrument, as doing so would circumvent the exchange control requirement to route transactions through a local Authorised Dealer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold. SARS requires sufficient transfer-pricing documentation for all cross-border controlled transactions. In practice, full Master File/Local File documentation is expected where cross-border related-party transactions exceed ZAR 5 million annually; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue above ZAR 12 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate standalone ESR. Substance is nevertheless enforced through the POEM test, transfer-pricing economic analysis, and anti-avoidance jurisprudence. Shell entities with no operational substance are liable to TP adjustments, GAAR sett-asides, and may be denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under ss.234-236 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; fine up to ZAR 2,000,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years, alternatively both. Administrative understatement penalty can be up to 200% of the tax shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors participating in or knowingly permitting evasion may be criminally prosecuted. On liquidation, SARS may impose director personal liability for unpaid tax under s.155 TAA if corporate assets have been stripped; travel bans may be issued pending criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may disregard transactions or apply arm's-length adjustments under s.31 ITA, plus 200% understatement penalty under s.223 TAA. The GAAR may set aside transactions and assess additional tax. Exchange-control penalties also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell entity can be held jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts if the entity lacks the funds to pay and directors caused the stripping. The corporate veil may be pierced under s.77 Companies Act; travel bans and asset restrictions can follow.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Failure

Statutory sanction: FICA administrative penalties up to ZAR 50 million for responsible parties. Money laundering offences under POCA carry imprisonment up to 30 years and fines. SARS may also impose a 200% understatement penalty on undisclosed income.

Director personal liability: Directors are 'responsible persons' under FICA s.332, and non-compliance leads to personal criminal accountability. Directors may be subject to travel bans, extradition requests, and personal asset forfeiture order under POCA.
