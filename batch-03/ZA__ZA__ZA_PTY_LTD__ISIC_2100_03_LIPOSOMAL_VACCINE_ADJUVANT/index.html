Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African (Pty) Ltd is a resident legal person taxed at the corporate level on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; the headline corporate income tax rate is currently 27%; dividend distributions are subject to 20% dividends tax; no pass-through or fiscal transparency treatment is available to a company. Non-resident ownership is permitted, and the entity will be within the scope of controlled foreign company attribution rules in respect of any non-resident subsidiaries.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box regime; companies are per se taxable entities. Available statutory elections and designations include: (i) Headquarter Company (HQC) designation under s9I for eligible regional holding companies; (ii) corporate restructure rollover elections (asset-for-share, amalgamation, intra-group transactions) under s45–s47; (iii) s11D research and development tax incentive approval, relevant to vaccine adjuvant R&D, and s12I industrial investment allowance; (iv) micro business turnover tax election under the Sixth Schedule if turnover thresholds are satisfied; (v) VAT voluntary registration election where turnover is below the compulsory threshold; and (vi) functional currency elections under s24I and CFC attribution elections under s9D. No election converts the (Pty) Ltd into a pass-through vehicle.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period, with registration required within 21 days of breach; voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports qualify for zero-rating under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the vendor supplies movable goods (including liposomal vaccine adjuvants and mRNA lipid emulsions) to a non-resident and: (i) the goods are removed from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply or within such extended period as SARS allows; (ii) the vendor retains prescribed documentary proof of export, including the customs export declaration and transport documents; (iii) payment is received in foreign currency through an authorised dealer unless formally exempted; and (iv) the goods are not used or made available in South Africa after removal. Services physically rendered outside South Africa, or services directly related to movable property outside the Republic, may be zero-rated under s11(2)(l). Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export supplies is refundable by SARS, subject to normal verification, set-off, and audit procedures.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 / ACE electronic export declaration) cleared by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical removal of the goods from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Receipt issued by an authorised dealer evidencing receipt of export proceeds in prescribed foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Vendor VAT tax invoice and proof of payment from the non-resident customer
  • Certificate of Origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed

No general real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is currently in force. SARS has issued a national e-invoicing framework (2024 discussion paper) and is conducting a phased pilot utilising a Peppol-aligned interoperability specification; once mandated, e-invoicing will be transmitted via approved service providers. In the interim, VAT vendors must issue full tax invoices within 21 days of supply containing all statutory particulars (vendor name and address, VAT registration number, invoice date, description, quantity, price, VAT amount, and applicable rate). VAT returns (VAT201) must be filed electronically via eFiling or API, and export declarations must be transmitted in real time through the Customs Clearance System (ACE). Large businesses may be required to provide transactional data electronically to SARS during audit.

Export zero-rating claims are scrutinised through SARS risk rules, cross-border data exchange, customs-payment reconciliation, and eFiling-based audits. If goods are not exported within 90 days or documentary proof is not maintained, the supply is deemed taxable at 15% and an output VAT adjustment must be made. Consequences include: (i) interest from the effective date of adjustment under the VAT Act; (ii) understatement penalties under ss222–223 of the Tax Administration Act ranging from 10% to 200% of the tax shortfall, with 200% for intentional tax evasion or customs fraud; (iii) criminal prosecution for false or fraudulent documentation, with fines up to ZAR 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years; and (iv) retrospective clawback assessments are not time-barred where fraud, misrepresentation, or non-disclosure is established, enabling SARS to reopen historic export refunds and recover VAT paid out, together with penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is a partially convertible currency. Current account payments (trade settlement, freight, insurance, service fees, royalties below defined thresholds) are freely transferable through Authorized Dealers (domestic banks) without FinSurv pre-approval, subject only to furnishing underlying documentation and a valid SARS tax-compliance status where required. Capital account transactions remain regulated: non-resident inward equity subscription is unrestricted and reportable; inward loans from non-residents require Authorized Dealer registration up to ZAR 50 million per calendar year per obligor and FinSurv approval above that threshold; outward direct investment, loans to foreign group companies, and repatriation of capital require FinSurv approval, tax clearance, and bank capture in the electronic exchange-control reporting system. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is legally unobstructed but operationally conditioned upon (i) withholding of 20% Dividends Tax under s64G of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, (ii) production of a SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN demonstrating good standing, and (iii) completion by the Authorized Dealer of the dividend transfer declaration (exchange-control Form related to income remittances). No blanket surrender tax or capital gains pre-clearance blocks apply to a compliant (Pty) Ltd, but the regulators expect evidentiary proof that the underlying share subscription proceeds were actually received into South Africa and credited to a SARB-designated inward investment account before any downstream dividend or capital transfer is permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must earn full proceeds within 180 calendar days from the date of shipment (bill of lading or airway bill). One hundred percent of export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa and credited either to a ZAR operational account or retained in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held at an Authorized Dealer; no mandatory conversion ratio to ZAR is presently imposed for general pharmaceutical exports, but FCA balances may be used only for permissible current-account payments and may not be lent, invested domestically, or used as collateral without prior approval. The exporter must file the prescribed monthly/reporting exchange-control returns (goods export register and FCA statements), retain the SAD500 customs declaration, commercial invoice, and proof of payment as audit evidence, and obtain an Authorized Dealer extension or FinSurv approval before the 180-day limit if the foreign debtor defaults. For the LNP/adjuvant manufacturing scope, a standing export master file covering contracts, purchase orders, and letters of credit must be lodged with the bank to prevent the automatic classification of overdue export proceeds as a contravention attracting interest, administrative penalties, and potential criminal liability under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), exercising delegated statutory authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and successive SARB Exchange Control Rulings. The National Payment System Department (NPSD) of SARB separately authorizes payment system operators under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium. South Africa does not recognise an 'Electronic Money Institution' (EMI) licence class; a non-bank payment provider must obtain a Third-Party Payment Provider (TPPP) or Payment System Operator authorisation from SARB NPSD under the National Payment System Act, while a full bank balance-sheet licence is required under the Banks Act 94 of 1990. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may only interface with the ZA entity through a licensed Authorized Dealer partner bank, cannot independently issue Documentary Letters of Credit, and cannot complete exchange-control BOP reporting in the bank's name. For the present LNP manufacturing entity, an offshore EMI is feasible only for low-value service disbursements and foreign contractor settlements, but is not an acceptable primary treasury rail because medium-term capital commitments, DC/LC issuance, FCA custody, and FinSurv reporting require a domestic Tier-1 bank relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders inject foreign currency via SWIFT MT103 to the ZA entity's FCA or ZAR account at the Authorized Dealer, quoting the bank's prescribed purpose-of-remittance code for inward direct investment (equity capital) and including the shareholder reference and subscription contract number in Field 70. Upon receipt, the bank will issue an inward investment advice/foreign-currency receipt certificate, register the capital introduction in the exchange-control inward investment register, and release the funds for ZAR conversion or FCA credit. The funds must be supported by a share subscription agreement, board-approved share allotment, CIPC share register update (including BEE issuer declarations where applicable), and, if structured as shareholder debt, a formal loan agreement captured in the bank's inward loan register; the ZAR50-million-per-calendar-year inward-loan threshold requires FinSurv pre-approval above that limit. The entire file must be made available to the bank's International Financial Services unit together with the SARS tax-compliance status to avoid credit blocks, and the company must preserve the full transactional trail for future Dividends Tax and capital-repatriation filings.Disbursements are executed through the Authorized Dealer over SWIFT using MT103 for direct wire settlements of raw-material imports, service fees, and freight, and MT700/MT707 for documentary Letters of Credit covering high-value LNP equipment and lipid/mRNA supply contracts. Each outward payment must reference the correct SARB balance-of-payments purpose code (trade goods, services, freight, or royalties), be accompanied by the underlying commercial invoice, customs entry, certificate of origin, and insurance document, and be supported in the bank's trade system before release; payments above exchange-control thresholds (including loan disbursements to foreign sub-entities or royalty remittances) additionally require FinSurv approval, SARS tax clearance, and approved concession letters. Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 50,000,000 in aggregate connected-party international transactions, or ZAR 5,000,000 per individual transaction with a connected person, triggers the mandatory preparation and retention of comprehensive transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File). In practice SARS may exercise its audit powers under section 31 of the Income Tax Act and request contemporaneous documentation even below these monetary thresholds where the arrangement lacks commercial substance or involves high-margin IP/contract manufacturing; a prudent LNP nanomedicine manufacturer should maintain a current TP policy regardless of thresholds because the cross-border movement of royalty-bearing IP and raw-mRNA materials is a high-risk category.

Economic substance scope: South Africa applies an all-encompassing economic substance doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) in sections 80A-80L of the Income Tax Act: an arrangement is 'impermissible' if its sole or main purpose was to obtain a tax benefit, if it is not entered into in a manner consistent with sound business practice, and if it was entered into by a party lacking real commercial substance (e.g., a shell company or conduit). SARS may disregard or re-characterise the arrangement, adjust the taxable income to the position that would have existed absent the avoidance, impose 75%-150% understatement penalties, and raise interest compounded at the prescribed rate. For the liposomal vaccine adjuvant manufacturer, the economic substance doctrine requires actual physical production capability in South Africa, dedicated scientific and technical staff, tangible research and development activity qualifying under section 11D, genuine assumption of manufacturing risk, and real service/lease contracts — not mere paper invoicing through a local (Pty) Ltd.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion & Fraudulent Return Submission

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under section 234 of the TAA: fine of up to R100,000 or twice the evaded tax, or imprisonment for a period not exceeding five years. In addition, a 150% understatement penalty under section 222 TAA plus interest on all unpaid tax at the prescribed rate; SARS may obtain a preservation order attaching the company's assets. A final judgment of tax evasion also triggers automatic disqualification from government tenders and licence revocation by SAHPRA for pharmaceutical manufacturing.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid company tax under section 155 TAA where the failure to pay is attributable to fraud, negligence or breach of fiduciary duty (a 10-day demand letter procedure applies). Directors may face a travel ban imposed by SARS under a court order, restraint orders under the prevention of organised crime provisions, and personal criminal liability with up to five years imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced where the (Pty) Ltd is used as a mere instrumentality for tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Exploitation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment under section 31 of the Income Tax Act realigning profits to arm's length; secondary adjustment deeming the excess payment to be a dividend or a loan on which notional interest at 8.25% (compounded) is charged; understatement penalty of 75% where the taxpayer showed no reasonable grounds for the position taken and 150% for intentional evasion; SARS may also disallow interest deductions under thin capitalisation provisions (section 23N and section 31(2)), resulting in full taxation of the imputed interest plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved non-arm's-length invoices face joint and several liability under section 155 TAA for the corporate tax shortfall including penalties accrued; officers may additionally be liable for reckless trading under section 77(3) of the Companies Act 71 of 2008, and in egregious cross-border fraud cases, criminal prosecution under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act with travel bans and asset freezes.
Shell Company / Impermissible Tax Avoidance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: GAAR under sections 80A-80L: the offending arrangement may be entirely disregarded and tax raised as if the arrangement never existed; a 'conduit arrangement' re-characterisation may assess the foreign principal directly; penalties of 75%-150% under section 222 TAA plus compulsory interest; SARS may raise a deemed dividend withholding charge on funds repatriated tainted by an avoidance arrangement, creating an additional 20% effective expense.

Director personal liability: Directors or shadow directors (including foreign de facto controllers) who participated in or authorised the avoidance scheme are jointly and severally liable with the company (section 155 TAA); where the corporate form is abused, South African courts will pierce the veil and hold the directors personally for all SARS claims; travel bans, criminal imprisonment up to five years, and disqualification orders may be sought in egregious GAAR cases.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance (High-Risk Licensed Activity)

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (as amended by the General Laws Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Measures Act, 2022), administrative fines of up to ZAR 10 million for each continuing contravention and imprisonment up to 5 years per contravention; SARS may impose additional 'third-party' penalties and freeze assets; a conviction triggers automatic FATF-enhanced-scrutiny status and may revoke the required pharmaceutical manufacturing licence of the SAHPRA/COVID special licence.

Director personal liability: Each director and senior officer who controlled day-to-day operations is personally criminally liable for any contravention; the court may impose direct imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine of up to ZAR 10 million; the corporate veil is pierced where the company was used as a sham for laundering proceeds, and the director may be subject to asset forfeiture, travel bans, and a ban on financial-sector employment.
Statutory Manufacturing Licence / Regulatory Perimeter Non-Compliance (Pharma & Customs)

Statutory sanction: Under the Medicines and Related Substances Act 101 of 1965 (section 73), contraventions can attract administrative fines of up to ZAR 500,000 per day and licence revocation; the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 imposes penalties of up to ten times the duty evaded and forfeiture of the goods; SARS can recover import VAT plus interest; criminal conviction also renders the company ineligible for public health procurement contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors, as 'officers in charge', are personally liable for regulatory fines and face criminal imprisonment up to 10 years under the Medicines Act; directors who knowingly furnished false customs declarations can be criminally prosecuted under the Customs Act with personal fines and imprisonment; commercial disputes over misrepresentation can pierce the corporate veil and convert personal assets toward restitution for SARS and state health authorities.
