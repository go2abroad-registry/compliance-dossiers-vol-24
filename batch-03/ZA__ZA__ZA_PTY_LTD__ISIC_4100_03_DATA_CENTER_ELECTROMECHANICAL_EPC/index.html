Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), 299 Bronkhorst Street, Nieuw Muckleneuk, Pretoria; official portal and eFiling platform at https://efiling.sars.gov.za; customs administration through the SARS Customs Division (SAD500 declaration system).

E-government filing portal

For a locally incorporated (Pty) Ltd, CIPC transmits incorporation data to SARS and SARS issues an income tax reference number (TIN) upon registration; the company must maintain active eFiling credentials and notify SARS of registered address and director changes. A foreign entity deriving South African-source income (e.g., through a permanent establishment for EPC works) must apply for a SARS income tax reference number using the prescribed SARS Registration (RA) forms via eFiling or a SARS branch office. VAT registration: compulsory registration is required within 21 days after the end of the month in which taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period or are reasonably expected to exceed that threshold; voluntary registration is available once turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000; foreign suppliers of electronic services must register where electronic services supplied to South African recipients exceed ZAR 50,000 in any 12-month period. The entity receives a distinct VAT registration number (10 digits, normally commencing with 4). A SARS Customs client code must also be obtained before lodging import or export declarations.

The entity receives a distinct VAT registration number (10 digits, normally commencing with 4). A SARS Customs client code must also be obtained before lodging import or export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident (Pty) Ltd is a separate taxable person subject to corporate income tax at 27% on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available; distributions to shareholders are subject to dividends tax at 20%; non-resident companies are taxed only on South African-source income (territorial nexus).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in South Africa; classification follows the entity's legal form. Available statutory elections include: (i) Headquarter Company election under s 9I of the Income Tax Act (ITA) for qualifying companies with foreign shareholders or foreign operations; (ii) intra-group restructuring rollover elections under s 45 ITA (asset-for-share and share-for-share) subject to anti-avoidance conditions; (iii) VAT group election under s 50 of the VAT Act allowing connected companies to be registered as a single VAT group; (iv) controlled foreign company (CFC) elections under s 9D ITA, including the high-taxed exclusion; and (v) hybrid mismatch rules operative from 1 January 2020 under the ITA, which automatically deny deductions or include income in hybrid mismatch arrangements and cannot be neutralised by taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period (actual or reasonably expected); voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 turnover; foreign electronic services suppliers: ZAR 50,000 of electronic services supplied to South African recipients.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to non-residents qualify for zero-rating under s 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, provided the goods are physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the tax invoice date and the vendor retains prescribed proof of export. Cross-border services supplied to non-residents qualify under s 11(2)(l) where the recipient is outside South Africa at the time of supply, the services are not supplied directly in connection with South African land or improvements, are not supplied in connection with movable property remaining in South Africa, and are not used or enjoyed in South Africa. Datacenter electromechanical EPC services performed at a South African site are standard-rated at 15% due to the land nexus; services performed for a foreign data-center site may qualify for zero-rating. Imported subcontracted services are subject to reverse-charge VAT under ss 7(1)(c) and 54. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export supplies is refundable under s 44(4) of the VAT Act, with prescribed interest if SARS fails to pay the refund within 21 business days.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank-validated payment confirmation)

South Africa does not operate real-time fiscal pre-clearance or digital stamping. SARS mandates electronic filing via eFiling and has issued the binding Standard for Electronic Invoicing (SEI), with voluntary early adoption preceding a phased mandatory application from 1 January 2026. SEI requires structured, machine-readable electronic tax invoices in compliant XML formats (UBL/CII), issuance through SARS-compliant software, five-year electronic archiving, and machine-to-machine/API transmission capability. No government clearance token is affixed, but every tax invoice must contain the vendor VAT number, invoice number, supply date, description, value of supply, and VAT amount to support input deductions.

SARS e-audit and risk-flagging algorithms scrutinise export declarations, bill-of-lading dates, proof of receipt and forex circularity. Failure to substantiate zero-rating within the required period triggers additional VAT at 15%, interest under the Tax Administration Act (TAA), and understatement penalties up to 200% of the shortfall in egregious cases (s 223 TAA), with criminal prosecution for deliberate fraudulent zero-rating. Refund claims may be suspended during verification; assessments can be reopened retrospectively within the TAA prescription framework, extended in non-disclosure cases and unlimited in cases of fraud or misrepresentation; false export documentation also attracts administrative penalties, forfeiture and perjury-based sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is convertible on the current account without prior SARB approval for genuine trade and service transactions, provided that supporting documentation is retained by an Authorised Dealer. Capital account transactions remain subject to exchange control approval thresholds and require engagement via Authorised Dealers or FinSurv for securities, loans, FDI, and certain holdings. Dividend and profit remittances to non-residents are permitted on production of an audited certificate or independent accountant verification that the dividends are declared out of distributable reserves and after satisfaction of the applicable dividends tax under the Income Tax Act. Practical hurdles include timely AML/KYC clearance, notification of large transactions through the Cross-Border Electronic Reporting system, and compliance with the 180-day repatriation timeline for trade proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment unless an extension is granted by an Authorised Dealer. There is no fixed statutory mandatory conversion percentage into ZAR; however, the proceeds must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer or credited to an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) in accordance with the SARB Exchange Control Manual. Funds held in an FCA may only be used for permissible trade and financial transactions and must be repatriated and converted if directed by FinSurv. The exporter must submit a Bill of Entry and proof of receipt to the bank within the prescribed period.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), the delegated Exchange Control authority for the Republic of South Africa

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs). Such EMIs are not licensed as South African banks and may not provide domestic current account services, but they can support auxiliary payment routing for cross-border disbursements if the primary liquidity remains with a SARB-authorised dealer bank. Use of a non-licensed EMI as the sole treasury conduit is legally constrained due to exchange control requirements. EMIs must be jurisdictionally licensed, must not hold ZAR-denominated balances on behalf of the South African entity in a manner constituting unauthorized banking, and must provide full transaction audit trails for exchange control verification.

High-value EPC trade counterparty risk: large advance payments to foreign equipment suppliers located in higher-risk jurisdictions

Defense evidence: A full procurement dossier for each major outbound disbursement containing the signed commercial contract, pro-forma invoices, HS codes, Incoterms, certificate of origin, export/import permits, equipment specifications, and a risk-based assessment of the supplier bank and jurisdiction. Provide insurance or guarantees where applicable (e.g., advance payment bond, performance guarantee) and correspondence evidencing the genuine arm's-length nature of the transaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loan injections must be channelled through a SARB-authorised dealer bank using designated cross-border wire protocols (SWIFT MT103 cover or MT202, depending on currency) and the correct purpose codes for foreign direct investment (e.g., code 500 FDI equity) or shareholder loans (code 501/510). The bank will require a foreign investment declaration, identity of the immediate and ultimate investor, share subscription documents (for equity), and loan agreements for debt funding. For amounts above the SARB reporting threshold, Form FDI or an electronic reporting through the Currency and Exchanges Manual will be triggered. Prior FinSurv approval may be required for certain inward capital structures involving financial assistance (e.g., loans from non-resident shareholders under section 45 of the Companies Act combined with exchange control rules).Outbound trade settlements for the EPC works are cleared through SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or via Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700 series) opened through a South African Authorised Dealer. For foreign suppliers requiring documentary security, a confirmed, irrevocable L/C issued by an international bank and advised through a South African authorised dealer is the preferred clearing rail. For domestic subcontractor payments in ZAR, utilise the local real-time gross settlement system (SAMOS) and Electronic Funds Transfer rails. All trade-related payments must be supported by appropriate customs declarations, supplier invoices, and transport documents to satisfy exchange control audit and SARB reporting.
