Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of Samoa (MFRS) - taxpayer registration and filing portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws (online TIN assignment and e-services portal)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) must obtain a Samoan Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by filing Form TIN-01 with the Ministry for Revenue. Required supporting documentation: Certificate of Incorporation or continuation certificate, Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered office and registered agent details, board resolution appointing a tax representative, passport or national ID copies of directors and shareholders, and proof of registered address in Samoa. Submission is made through the MFRS e-services portal or through a licensed registered agent. The TIN is normally issued within 1-3 business days. The same TIN is used for VAGST registration; an IC whose taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold must file Form VAGST-01 using the same TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - International Company automatically exempt from Samoan income tax on non-resident-source income under the International Companies Act 1987; territorial scope limited to Samoa-sourced income; no entity-level taxation for offshore business activities

Available election forms

N/A. Samoa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. The International Company tax exemption is automatic under the International Companies Act 1987 and may be evidenced by a tax exemption certificate issued by the Ministry for Revenue. No check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election is available to an International Company in Samoa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT$100,000 (WST 100,000) annual taxable turnover; mandatory registration applies to any person making or intending to make taxable supplies exceeding SAT$100,000 in any 12-month period, with registration effective from the date the threshold is exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: VAGST zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Samoa by the supplier and retained outside Samoa until the export is complete. Services: zero-rating applies to services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Samoa at the time of supply, where the services are not connected with land or goods situated in Samoa and are performed wholly outside Samoa or otherwise exported. Trade remedy defense and anti-dumping inquiry consulting services supplied to offshore principals qualify where the benefit and use of the services occur outside Samoa. The supplier must retain contemporaneous evidence of the offshore recipient and non-consumption of the services in Samoa; input tax credits may be claimed to the extent attributable to the zero-rated supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs Service electronic export entry/SGD)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export and destination outside Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Samoan commercial bank or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of foreign currency in Samoa
  • Tax invoice and signed contract evidencing the offshore nature of the recipient and the exact service description
  • Service completion report, correspondence logs, or work product demonstating performance outside Samoa

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API-based clearance mandate is currently in force in Samoa. VAGST-registered persons must issue valid tax invoices containing prescribed particulars (supplier name and TIN, recipient name and TIN, invoice date, description, quantity, VAGST-exclusive amount, VAGST amount, and total consideration), retain supporting records for at least 7 years, and file VAGST returns electronically through the MFRS e-services portal.

The Samoa Ministry for Revenue may audit zero-rating claims and require complete documentary proof. Failure to substantiate export status results in disallowance of zero-rating, immediate recovery of unpaid VAGST, interest, and late-payment penalties. False, misleading, or fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute an offence under the VAGST Act 2008 and the Tax Administration Act, punishable by monetary penalties of up to SAT$100,000 and/or imprisonment. Principals and directors may be jointly and severally liable. Retrospective clawback can extend to all wrongfully claimed input tax credits plus penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a managed-float exchange-rate regime for the Samoan Tala (WST). The CBS sets and announces the daily reference rate against a basket of principal trading-partner currencies (USD, AUD, NZD, EUR and JPY) and intervenes through authorised dealers to smooth volatility. Current-account payments are substantially liberalised: trade-in-services remittances (legal, consulting and trade-defense representation fees) are executed by authorised dealers on submission of the underlying contract, invoice and a standard CBS application form, without prior CBS approval in routine cases. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, external borrowing and portfolio flows, remain controlled and require CBS authorisation on a documented, case-by-case basis. A Samoa International Company (IC) is treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes and maintains only foreign-currency offshore accounts; its convertibility profile therefore approximates a free offshore account, with the practical hurdles being documentary proof of economic substance, full beneficial-ownership disclosure and absence of transactions with Samoan residents. Dividend and capital repatriation by a tax-exempt IC is free of Samoa withholding tax and free of mandatory WST conversion, provided the annual IC licence fee has been paid and the remittance is supported by the IC tax-exemption certificate, the authorised dealer's outward-remittance declaration, and compliance with the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations, proceeds of exports of goods must be collected within six (6) months from the date of final shipment and remitted through a bank in Samoa licensed as an authorised dealer, unless CBS grants a specific extension. The statutory credit ratio is the full 100 per cent of the invoice value; however, the exporter may hold the proceeds in a designated foreign-currency account with an authorised dealer without conversion into WST. For a Samoa International Company earning invisible earnings (professional fees for anti-dumping and trade-defense advisory services), the same six-month collection rule applies from the invoice date; the inward remittance is credited to the IC's multi-currency account and does not trigger any WST conversion because the entire operating currency is foreign. Any shortfall, delay, write-off or netting of receivables must be declared to both the authorised dealer and CBS, accompanied by evidence of the collection measures taken; non-compliance is an offence under the Exchange Control Act 1975.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; foreign-exchange administration is exercised under the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Exchange Control Regulations. The CBS alone issues exchange-control licences to authorised dealers (commercial banks), maintains the official WST exchange-rate basket against a trade-weighted set of partner currencies, and oversees the national AML/CFT supervision of banks through the Financial Intelligence Unit established under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Samoa has no domestic digital-bank or electronic-money-institution licensing regime, and most offshore UK, EU and Singapore EMIs will decline a Samoan International Company because of residual reputational concern linked to offshore IC structures and Samoa's historical presence on EU screening lists. The workable model is to maintain the primary treasury relationship with a CBS-licensed authorised dealer (BSP platform preferred) and to use an OECD-licensed EMI only as a supplementary receivables-collection wallet for fee income from low-risk clients in the EU, UK and Australasia. Legal constraints: the IC is prohibited from transacting with Samoan residents or using the WST domestic payment infrastructure, so the EMI must operate purely as a non-resident collection and disbursement point for foreign-currency flows routed onward to the Samoan bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contribution is routed by SWIFT MT103 with reference text 'IC Capital Contribution - [company name]', accompanied by the bank's inward-remittance declaration. The receiving authorised dealer credits the IC's multi-currency account after verifying: (i) the board resolution approving the share allotment or subordinated loan; (ii) an excerpt from the register of members or the executed loan agreement; (iii) source-of-funds evidence from the remitting shareholder; and (iv) sanctions screening of the remitter and all intermediary banks. Because the IC holds non-resident offshore status and no WST is involved, prior CBS authorisation is not required; the bank applies the standard statistical purpose code (current-account transfer / professional-services credit) for CBS reporting and files a threshold report with the Financial Intelligence Unit where applicable under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007. Settlement timing: same-day for local Samoan Tala balances; T+1 to T+2 for USD legs via United States correspondent banks; T+1 for EUR, AUD and GBP legs through the bank's European, Australian and UK correspondents respectively.Outbound payments for trade-defense work (foreign counsel retainers, expert-witness fees, WTO and anti-dumping authority filing fees, translation and certification costs) are settled via: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for individual beneficiaries, each matched to an underlying invoice retained on file for five years; (ii) MT202 COV for correspondent interbank reimbursement where a local intermediary is used; (iii) ACH (USD, EUR, GBP) for recurring low-value fees if offered by the host bank; and (iv) standby documentary credits (MT760, or commercial LC MT700 where a counsel or tribunal requires payment security), supported by a segregated cash escrow within the same bank. All disbursements are exclusively in foreign currency to non-resident beneficiaries; settlement in WST to Samoan residents is impermissible for an International Company without a specific CBS derogation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard resident company rate under the Income Tax Act 2015; 0% for an International Company (IC) qualifying for exemption under the International Companies Act.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% withholding tax on dividends paid by an exempt Samoa International Company to non-resident shareholders. For a resident Samoa company that is not an International Company, the statutory dividend withholding tax rate is 15% when paid to a non-resident.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Form ICA-AR must be submitted and the annual government licence fee paid no later than the anniversary of the IC's registration date. A grace period of 21 days is permitted, after which a late penalty accrues.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No transfer-pricing documentation threshold applies to a tax-exempt International Company because it has no Samoa taxable income. A resident Samoa company (not an IC) must prepare Local File and Master File documentation when annual related-party cross-border transactions exceed WST 1,000,000. An International Company that fails economic-substance requirements and is reclassified as a domestic resident company will become subject to the same threshold.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Samoa's International Companies (Economic Substance) Rules require an IC conducting any geographical core income-generating activity to maintain a real physical office in Samoa, employ individuals in Samoa, hold board meetings in Samoa at least once per fiscal year, and keep all relevant bank statements and contracts at the Samoa registered office. For trade-remedy defense consulting, the core activity is 'provision of management and consulting services' and the IC must demonstrate negotiation, staff, and expenditure decisions made in Samoa. An annual economic-substance declaration must be filed with the Registrar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Immediate revocation of IC status, retroactive taxation of all income, a monetary penalty equal to 200% of the tax evaded or WST 100,000 (whichever is greater), and imprisonment of up to 7 years for the responsible officer.

Director personal liability: The officer who signed the false declaration is jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, civil fines, and may be subject to asset freezing, travel bans, and extradition under Samoa's Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act.
Shell Company – Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Loss of tax exemption, mandatory payment of all accrued taxes plus 10% interest per annum, a fine of WST 50,000, and strike-off from the Samoa Registry. If the company was used to create bogus legal defenses, the offending entity may also be disqualified from appearing in foreign anti-dumping proceedings.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil and order directors to compensate the IC for misrepresentations to tax authorities and trade tribunals; directors can be personally ordered to restore all distributions made to shareholders, and non-resident directors may be banned from holding an appointment in Samoa.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018, the IC is liable to a fine of up to WST 25,000 per offence, and each defaulting officer is subject to a further fine and imprisonment for up to 5 years; the registered agent's licence may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are each personally liable for AML penalties; directors who knowingly structure ownership to evade sanctions or anti-circumvention findings may face personal prosecution, confiscation of profits, and restrictions on travelling to EU Member States or the forum country initiating the anti-dumping action.
Trade-Remedy Disinformation and Fraud

Statutory sanction: The Samoa IC and its principals may be fined up to 10% of the value of the foreign trade-remedy claim or WST 200,000, whichever is higher; the IC's licence is revoked for serious misconduct; and the registered agent may be disbarred from acting for any Samoa company.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to civil restitution and disgorgement of gains in the foreign anti-dumping jurisdiction, injunctive relief against future conduct, and criminal extradition if the foreign state demonstrates that the Samoa IC was used as a sham vehicle to conceal the true exporter.
