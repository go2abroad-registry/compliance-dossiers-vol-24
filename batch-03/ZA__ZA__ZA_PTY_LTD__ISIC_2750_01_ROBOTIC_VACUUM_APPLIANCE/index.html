Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) acting through the SARS eFiling portal at https://efiling.sars.gov.za; customs and export clearance functions are administered by the SARS Customs and Excise division under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Pty Ltd must: (1) incorporate under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and obtain the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) registration certificate; (2) appoint a resident public officer as required by section 27 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 and section 67 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (TAA); (3) register on SARS eFiling for a 10-digit income tax reference number (TIN) by submitting the CIPC certificate, memorandum of incorporation, shareholding and directorship particulars, and a South African registered office address; (4) register for VAT once taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period, or voluntarily when turnover exceeds R50,000; and (5) obtain a SARS Customs client code for the lodgement of export declarations. Foreign-entity documents executed abroad generally require apostille or consular legalisation before SARS will accept them, and the public officer must be a South African resident.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Pty Ltd must: (1) incorporate under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and obtain the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) registration certificate; (2) appoint a resident public officer as required by section 27 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 and section 67 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (TAA); (3) register on SARS eFiling for a 10-digit income tax reference number (TIN) by submitting the CIPC certificate, memorandum of incorporation, shareholding and directorship particulars, and a South African registered office address; (4) register for VAT once taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period, or voluntarily when turnover exceeds R50,000; and (5) obtain a SARS Customs client code for the lodgement of export declarations. Foreign-entity documents executed abroad generally require apostille or consular legalisation before SARS will accept them, and the public officer must be a South African resident.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box regime; the Pty Ltd is a resident company taxed at entity level on worldwide income at 27%. Available statutory elections and designations include the section 11D research-and-development 150% allowance, section 12B renewable-energy allowance, section 20A group assessed-loss transfer election, section 24J interest amortisation election, and section 45 intra-group rollover election subject to SARS filing requirements. Hybrid debt and hybrid equity instruments are recharacterised under sections 8F and 8FA respectively rather than being subject to elective classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: total taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in the preceding 12 months or are reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in the following 12 months (VAT Act section 23(1)); voluntary registration: taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in the preceding 12 months (VAT Act section 23(3)). SARS may require security for voluntary or non-resident registrants.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where a vendor supplies movable goods to a non-resident, the goods are physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the date of issue of the tax invoice, the vendor obtains and retains the documentary proof prescribed by SARS, and full payment is received within 12 months of the time of supply or such longer period as SARS may allow. Offshore services supplied to non-residents may be zero-rated under section 11(2) only where the services are not supplied in South Africa and do not fall within the statutory exclusions (for example services connected to South African immovable property). Input VAT attributable to export sales remains creditable, producing cash refunds on a credit VAT 201 return following SARS verification.

  • SARS customs export declaration (SAD500 / DA-40) cleared by SARS Customs
  • Full tax invoice and commercial invoice issued to the foreign recipient
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or other through-transport document proving physical export from South Africa
  • Authorised-dealer foreign exchange confirmation evidencing inward remittance of export proceeds
  • Packing list, insurance certificate and proof of delivery at the overseas destination, retained for five years

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in force. Full tax invoices meeting the content requirements of section 20 of the VAT Act must be issued within 21 days of supply, with simplified invoices permitted for supplies below R5,000. South Africa is progressively introducing a PEPPOL-standard e-invoicing framework, with SARS pilots underway and a future API-enabled real-time reporting path expected. VAT 201 returns are filed electronically via eFiling, and SARS applies its iExp export risk-analytics platform to verify zero-rated export claims. All VAT records must be retained for at least five years under section 55 of the TAA.

SARS verifies export zero-rating through the iExp system; failure to substantiate triggers de-zero-rating so that output VAT at 15% is assessed on the full consideration, together with interest at the SARS prescribed rate, understatement penalties of 10% to 150% of the tax shortfall under the TAA Schedule 2 behaviour categories, administrative non-compliance penalties for late or non-submission, and additional VAT penalties. SARS may reopen assessments for up to three years generally, five years in cases of non-disclosure, and without time limit in cases of fraud. Knowingly submitting false export declarations or tax invoices is a criminal offence under sections 234 and 235 of the TAA and the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964, punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to five years, and the TAA responsible-person provisions may extend recovery to directors and controlling persons who caused the failure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a managed-float regime for the fully deliverable South African rand (ZAR). Current-account transactions (trade payments, service fees, royalties, interest) are freely convertible and require only adequate trade documentation at the Authorised Dealer. Capital-account flows are regulated but liberalised: inward direct investment in a private (Pty) Ltd (equity subscription or shareholder loan) is permitted without prior FinSurv approval, subject to post-event BOP declaration through an Authorised Dealer. Profit repatriation and dividend remittance are permitted provided: (i) the original inward investment was correctly declared on the BOP forms and is traceable in the bank's records, (ii) the dividend is declared from audited after-tax distributable reserves, (iii) the 20% dividend tax (or a treaty-reduced or exempt rate per a SARS ruling) has been accounted for, and (iv) the company holds a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN. There are no material convertibility hurdles for a bona fide manufacturing exporter, but any non-resident parent remittance must be settled through a domestic Authorised Dealer and supported by a paper trail of the underlying investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All proceeds from export sales must be physically received in South Africa, or credited to an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held with a local Authorised Dealer, within 180 days from the date of shipment/export. Since 1 January 2022 the statutory requirement to surrender or convert a fixed percentage of export proceeds to ZAR has been abolished; a resident exporter may retain 100% of export earnings in a Foreign Currency Account and may deploy them for admissible outbound payments, provided the proceeds are 'banked' onshore within the 180-day window and the FCA is reconcilable to SA Customs SAD 500 declarations. The 'normal export price' rule remains in force: goods must be exported at a price no lower than the normal export value, and the proceeds must be received in a freely exchangeable currency. Any extension beyond 180 days requires a FinSurv approval application through the Authorised Dealer; non-compliance triggers cessation of export-banking facilities, prescribed reporting to FinSurv, and AML/CFT scrutiny. Export banking records, SAD 500 forms, bills of lading/airway bills, and proof of receipt must be retained for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; the National Treasury retains policy oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary account provider for the South African entity; MEDIUM-HIGH as a supplementary collections/disbursement rail, provided a domestic Authorised Dealer remains the settlement anchor. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer) are not recognised by SARB as Authorised Dealers and may not lawfully hold transactional deposits or execute cross-border settlement for a South African resident company without an FMX-compliant partnership with a licenced local institution; the SARB Exchange Control Manual requires all cross-border receipts and payments of a resident to flow through the domestic banking system and be declared by an Authorised Dealer. Local fintechs holding an Authorised Dealer with Limited Authority (ADLA) licence or operating under a National Payment System Act licence (e.g., Sureswipe, PayU, certain e-commerce payment aggregators) can provide regulated foreign-currency collection in partnership with the big-four banks; the company may use such rails for marketplace/e-commerce receipts (e.g., Amazon, Shopify, distributor card payments) but must repatriate balances to its ZA bank and comply with the 180-day export- or service-receipts rule.
