Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official tax filing and registration portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za); customs and excise declarations administered through the SARS Customs Modernisation Programme (SAD500 EDI/API platform).

E-government filing portal

For a South African Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd: (1) incorporate with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the unique company registration number; (2) upon CIPC registration, SARS automatically creates the income tax reference number and links the taxpayer profile to the CIPC data under the automated SARS-CIPC data exchange; (3) confirm/complete the company tax registration (form IT102 or eFiling 'Register a Company' workflow) with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation, identity documents of directors, and a beneficial ownership schedule required under the General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act; (4) register as a VAT vendor by submitting the VAT 101 application online via eFiling or at a SARS branch; SARS issues the VAT vendor number which is also the VAT return filing reference; (5) apply for customs client registration through the SARS Customs EDI/API system using the customs client code; foreign-owned entities must appoint a SARS-registered representative and provide proof of foreign bank account details and inward equity investment record, if applicable; (6) maintain all SARS reference numbers (income tax, VAT, customs code, PAYE/UIF if employing staff) on a single eFiling profile for mandatory electronic filing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the (Pty) Ltd is a South African tax-resident company taxed on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 at the standard corporate income tax rate of 27% for financial years ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through regime is available; dividends paid by the company are subject to a 20% Dividends Tax withheld at source by the company on behalf of shareholders. — For a South African Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd: (1) incorporate with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the unique company registration number; (2) upon CIPC registration, SARS automatically creates the income tax reference number and links the taxpayer profile to the CIPC data under the automated SARS-CIPC data exchange; (3) confirm/complete the company tax registration (form IT102 or eFiling 'Register a Company' workflow) with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation, identity documents of directors, and a beneficial ownership schedule required under the General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act; (4) register as a VAT vendor by submitting the VAT 101 application online via eFiling or at a SARS branch; SARS issues the VAT vendor number which is also the VAT return filing reference; (5) apply for customs client registration through the SARS Customs EDI/API system using the customs client code; foreign-owned entities must appoint a SARS-registered representative and provide proof of foreign bank account details and inward equity investment record, if applicable; (6) maintain all SARS reference numbers (income tax, VAT, customs code, PAYE/UIF if employing staff) on a single eFiling profile for mandatory electronic filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the (Pty) Ltd is a South African tax-resident company taxed on worldwide income under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 at the standard corporate income tax rate of 27% for financial years ending on or after 31 March 2023; no pass-through regime is available; dividends paid by the company are subject to a 20% Dividends Tax withheld at source by the company on behalf of shareholders.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' pass-through election. Available statutory mechanisms include: (a) voluntary VAT vendor registration under section 23(2) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in a 12-month period (or are reasonably expected to exceed that threshold); (b) compulsory VAT registration under section 23(1) once taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a 12-month period; (c) group VAT election under section 50(2) allowing a 75%+ associated group of companies to appoint one representative vendor for VAT purposes; (d) income tax group (consolidation) election under section 41(1) of the Income Tax Act for 75%+ owned and controlled resident companies, permitting the filing of a single corporate group tax return; (e) Headquarter Company election under section 9I for qualifying SA-resident service companies, granting exemption on foreign dividends, interest, royalties and service income; (f) micro-business turnover tax election under the Turnover Tax Schedule (Fourth Schedule to the Income Tax Act) for businesses with annual turnover below ZAR 1,000,000; (g) hybrid mismatch rules (sections 23N, 8FA and 8FB) impose mandatory adjustments rather than elective treatment, with limited anti-avoidance elections only via the SARS hybrid mismatch implementation guidelines.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (one million Rand): compulsory VAT registration threshold when the value of taxable supplies made in the course of any enterprise exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 within a consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12-month period or are reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 50,000 in the next 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods (including absorbable polyglycolic acid braided sutures and surgical suture needle assemblies) are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the goods are supplied under a sale or consignment agreement and are exported by the supplier within 90 days of the date of the tax invoice (or such longer period as SARS may allow on good cause shown). The supplier must retain documentary proof of export. Zero-rating of services applies under section 11(2)(l) where the services are supplied to a non-resident who is not in South Africa at the time of supply, and the services are not consumed in South Africa; for medical device manufacturers this covers ancillary logistics, overseas technical support and offshore clinical/evaluation services. No export zero-rating may be claimed unless the goods physically leave the Republic and the vendor retains full evidence of export; exports to SACU countries (Botswana, Namibia, Lesotho, Eswatini) from South Africa also qualify as exports for VAT purposes only when the goods leave the SACU area; intra-SACU sales may instead be treated as inter-state supplies with a 15% rate or rebate depending on the regional arrangement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SARS SAD500 / DA550 form submitted via the SARS Customs EDI/API platform)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill in the case of airfreight) evidencing on-board shipment from a South African port on or before the export date
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign currency receipt certificate issued by an authorised South African dealer bank confirming payment of the export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reflecting the same description, quantity and value as the customs declaration
  • Container number, seal number, vessel/flight name and port-of-loading manifest records
  • Proof of destination-country customs clearance or final delivery documentation (when requested by SARS as additional export evidence)

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of VAT invoices. Requirements: (a) every VAT vendor must issue a full tax invoice within 21 days of the time of supply containing the mandatory particulars under section 20(4) of the VAT Act (serial number, invoice date, name/address/VAT number of supplier, description of goods or services, quantity/volume, consideration, VAT amount); (b) electronic invoices (structured PDF/XML) are acceptable where the transmission method has been approved by SARS; (c) all VAT returns (VAT201), income tax returns (ITR14) and customs declarations are filed electronically via eFiling or EDI/API; customs export clearance is processed real-time through the SARS Customs Modernisation system using SAD500 batch or online messaging; (d) SARS has announced a phased e-invoicing and near-real-time data analytics programme in line with international standards, but no statutory API-based real-time e-invoicing mandate or digital stamping regime is currently in force for VAT invoices.

SARS conducts systemic audit and risk-rating of zero-rated export claims. If the goods are not physically exported within the 90-day statutory period (or approved extension), the supply becomes standard-rated and VAT at 15% becomes payable with interest calculated from the original time of supply. Fraudulent export zero-rating exposes the vendor to: (a) a revised assessment with an understatement penalty of up to 150% of the VAT understated depending on conduct (TAA sections 222-223); (b) an additional HIV/AIDS levy-type percentage penalty only where applicable, and periodic non-compliance penalties of up to ZAR 2,000 per item per month under section 210 TAA; (c) criminal prosecution under section 233 TAA with a fine of up to ZAR 500,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 10 years per count; (d) penalties and forfeiture of the full value of the goods under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 for false export declarations (penalties up to 100% of the goods value, plus customs duties otherwise payable); (e) retrospective clawback of input VAT previously claimed on the same goods and disallowance of the zero-rating; (f) where fraud, misrepresentation or gross negligence is established, the normal 3-year prescription on SARS assessments is removed, allowing SARS to raise assessments without a time limit and to charge interest (currently approximately 10% per annum, compounded) from the original due date until full payment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible on the current account through Authorised Dealers for trade in goods, services, royalties, and dividend/profit remittances, subject to FICA-compliant documentation, SARS tax-clearance verification, and accurate balance-of-payments reporting. Capital-account transactions (foreign equity injections, shareholder loans, and quasi-capital advances) do not require prior FinSurv pre-approval where routed through a registered Authorised Dealer, but all inward investment documentation and the applicable capital-import declaration must be filed within the dealer's statutory timelines. Profit remittance by a (Pty) Ltd is lawful where the following are in place: a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status Pin, audited or independently reviewed IFRS financial statements, a properly adopted dividend declaration, and the prescribed exchange-control notification submitted by the Authorised Dealer. The key hurdle is not convertibility per se but the need to demonstrate an underlying real manufacturing/trading substance and a clean source-of-funds trail, as SARB and the ADs apply a risk-based principle to combat round-tripping and recharacterisation of intercompany advances into deemed permanent equity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 6(1)(a), the export proceeds derived from the surgical suture needle assembly business must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment and must forthwith be sold or credited to the exporter's account with an Authorised Dealer. The statutory default is therefore a 100% surrender of export foreign currency into the domestic ZAR banking system unless SARB/FinSurv has expressly approved a Foreign Currency Retention Account; no automatic partial conversion ratio applies because the entire amount is repatriated to the AD. The company must ensure that SWIFT funds are directed to the designated AD nostro/current account, that the SARS customs declaration (SAD500) is aligned to the invoiced value, and that the full export documentary file (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, inspection certificate, and evidence of payment) is retained for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the Exchange Control Authority, together with the National Treasury of the Republic of South Africa. The controlling legal instruments are the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), the South African Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder, the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers (as updated), and the FIC Act, 2001 (Act 38 of 2001).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low_Viability for primary treasury clearing and liquidity concentration. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business, etc.) are not South African Authorised Dealers and are not integrated with the SARB Financial Surveillance reporting framework or the SAMOS settlement system. Retention of export proceeds in such offshore EMIs without a FinSurv foreign-currency approval would contravene Exchange Control Regulation 6(1)(a). Their permissible role in this blueprint is limited to a secondary low-value payment aggregation service for minor inbound receipts, on condition that the full balance is automatically swept on a daily cycle into the primary South African AD bank account for conversion and VAT/reporting alignment. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for intermediated bank credit) to the chosen Authorised Dealer, with the stated reference being the SARB-approved inward direct investment declaration/capital-import form and the entity's registration number. The AD screens the funds against exchange-control requirements, credits the ZAR-denominated free-capital suspense account at the SARB closing spot rate, and issues the formal Capital Import Certificate. The imported funds may be applied only to genuine operational and capital expenditure of the ISIC 3250 assembly activity; on-lending to residents, speculative investments, or rapid round-tripping would require prior FinSurv consent. Full supporting files—share subscription agreement, loan agreement, board resolution, bank statements, and SWIFT confirmations—must be retained for repatriation and future remittance of dividends/loan repayments.Trade settlements for raw-material inputs and distribution-channel payments are structured either as SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfers (TT/Wire) or as documentary Commercial Letters of Credit issued per UCP 600 for higher-value procured inputs (MT700/MT707 instruction chain). Each outward payment must be aligned to an underlying commercial invoice, proof of goods receipt or delivery, and the SARS customs entry; where vendor payments exceed the Authorised Dealer's automatic threshold, Form A (application to purchase foreign currency) or the AD's electronic foreign-payment declaration is lodged contemporaneously. The operational clearing backbone is the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system under the National Payment System Act, 1998, ensuring settlement finality in central-bank money.Onshore ZAR/USD and ZAR/EUR Forward Exchange Contract (FEC) with an Authorised Dealer, supported by committed export orders or confirmed LCs; maturity is matched to the underlying invoicing cycle and net settlement is subject to SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual Chapter 7 documentation standards.Vanilla FX options (call/put combinations) on rand crosses traded through a South African Authorised Dealer, used strictly to cap conversion volatility on contracted surgical consumable export receivables and pre-agreed raw-material imports.Cross-currency swap (CCS/CCX) linking USD/EUR-denominated capital expenditure debt service to the ZAR export revenue stream, including the associated interest-rate hedge, provided the swap is evidenced by the underlying loan agreement and notified to FinSurv through the Authorised Dealer.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) only where the resident counterparty demonstrates a legitimate restricted-currency settlement requirement and the AD confirms a genuine trade or debt-service underlying exposure; uncovered or speculative NDF positions are not permitted in the South African framework.
