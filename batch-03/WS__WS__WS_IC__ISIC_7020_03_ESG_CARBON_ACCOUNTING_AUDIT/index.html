Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. International Companies are statutorily treated as offshore tax-exempt entities under the International Companies Act 1987. No hybrid mismatch or fiscal unity elections are available in the Samoa International Companies regime; a company may elect to be redomiciled or converted to a domestic company, which removes the tax-exempt zone classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and certain offshore services are zero-rated under the VAGST Act 2003 where the supplier is a registered VAGST taxpayer, the goods are exported within 30 days of supply, and supporting evidence demonstrates the recipient is outside Samoa or the service is supplied to a non-resident and used entirely outside Samoa. International Companies carrying out offshore carbon accounting and brokerage services can zero-rate their service fees if all consideration is payable from outside Samoa and the services are consumed wholly outside Samoa.

  • Bill of Lading
  • Air Waybill (where applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is in force for International Companies operating under the exempt offshore regime. Standard commercial invoices are acceptable. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract recovery of uncollected VAGST with interest, a penalty of up to 100% of the tax shortfall, prosecution for tax fraud under the Tax Administration Act 2012, and cancellation of the company's VAGST registration. Directors and officers may face personal liability and fines or imprisonment where wilful evasion is proven. The Ministry of Revenue may issue post-audit assessments and retrospectively claw back any refunded VAGST or tax credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The WST is a managed-float currency with current-account transactions largely liberalized, while capital-account movements remain subject to CBS oversight through authorized dealers. A Samoa International Company (IC) regulated under the International Companies Act 1987 is expressly exempted from domestic exchange control restrictions when transacting in foreign currency, provided it does not conduct business with Samoan residents in WST. Profit remittance for an IC is unencumbered in the sense that the entity may hold and transfer foreign-currency proceeds freely through designated foreign-currency accounts; however, any incidental WST-denominated flow, local expenditure with a Samoan resident, or dividend remittance to a Samoan-resident party would trigger CBS conversion/approval requirements. The practical convertibility ceiling is therefore not the statute but the thin onshore interbank market, with USD being the dominant settlement currency and WST deliverability generally confined to domestic settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBS administrative practice, resident exporters must repatriate export proceeds to Samoa within 90 days of shipment and offer the proceeds for conversion to authorized dealers in accordance with minimum surrender ratios unless an exporter exemption is granted. A Samoa IC, however, is outside the resident export regime: foreign-currency receipts from carbon auditing and offset brokerage services are not subject to forced surrender or conversion timelines, provided the IC maintains its designated foreign-currency account with an authorized dealer and files the required exchange-control declaration forms evidencing its IC exempt status. Any recommended practice is to keep a documented intercompany or client agreement showing that all invoices are settled in USD or another freely convertible currency and that no goods or services are supplied to Samoan residents in WST.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa, established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, acting as the designated exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and Exchange Control Regulations 1976, in conjunction with the Minister of Finance for policy approvals; the CBS supervises all authorized dealers in foreign exchange and administers the Samoan Tālā (WST) managed floating exchange rate regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for a Samoa-licensed or Samoa-domiciled electronic money institution (EMI), because Samoa has no dedicated EMI licensing framework and the Offshore Banking Act 1987 contemplates conventional offshore bank licensing rather than digital EMI charters. An offshore EMI incorporated in a recognized jurisdiction (e.g., UK, EU, Singapore, or Hong Kong) may be engaged as a payment conduit for IC fund flows, but legal constraints arise from correspondent de-risking, APG mutual-evaluation scrutiny, and the CBS expectation that foreign-currency deposit-taking for Samoa ICs occurs through authorized dealers. The practical recommended structure is a Samoa IC maintaining a primary account at ANZ or BSP Samoa and a secondary segregated settlement account at a reputable non-Samoa EMI only for low-balance invoice collections, with monthly sweeps to the primary bank to maintain audit trail integrity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires subscription proceeds for IC shares or shareholder loans to be remitted by SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the investor's bank into the IC's designated USD account at ANZ or BSP Samoa via the Samoan bank's offshore USD correspondent (typically a New York or Australian clearing bank). The remittance must carry a precise purpose narrative such as 'Share Capital Subscription – Samoa International Company (WS/IC) – Carbon Advisory Services' or 'Shareholder Loan - ESG Audit Working Capital'; the beneficiary account name must exactly match the IC's registered name, and the Samoan bank will require a certificate of incorporation, shareholder resolution approving the issuance, and a capital verification letter from the registered agent or auditor. CBS exchange-control declaration forms (E1/FX1) must be lodged if any portion of the transfer is converted to WST; otherwise, the foreign-currency account is maintained without conversion approval. For any capital injection exceeding USD 500,000 per month, the bank may require a source-of-funds letter and board minutes confirming the purpose, with funds held in the IC's operational account until deployment to carbon offset purchases or audit deliverables.Clearing rails for outbound trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for immediate wire transfers covering carbon offset brokerage fees, subcontractor audit payments, and reimbursable client expenses; (2) Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) fundamentally limited in applicability because the underlying service-based business (ESG auditing and carbon offset brokerage) does not generate shipping documentation, although an LC may be issued on a revolving clean basis for verified carbon credit purchases where the seller demands payment security; (3) Promissory-note-backed settlement or documentary collections are available but not market standard. For all outbound rails, the Samoan bank will require full beneficiary bank details (BIC/IBAN), a supporting invoice or credit-purchase contract, and an FX declaration if conversion into WST is involved; the practical clearing corridor is the bank's correspondent in New York (USD) or Sydney/Auckland (AUD/NZD), with same-day value for USD wires and T+1 for AUD/NZD. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 as amended; the general domestic corporate tax rate for resident taxable companies is 27% under the Income Tax Act 2015.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends and distributions paid by a Samoa International Company to non-resident shareholders, because IC distributions are excluded from the Income Tax Act 2015 withholding regime. Dividends paid by a domestic Samoan resident company to non-residents are subject to a 15% statutory withholding rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the anniversary of the date of incorporation each year; a statutory grace period of 3 months applies before late penalties accrue. The annual government fee must accompany the return to avoid default.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The income year is the calendar year; the return and final self-assessment are due by 30 April of the following year. Provisional tax installments are payable on the last day of months 4, 7, 10, and 13 of the income year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None in Samoa: the Income Tax Act 2015 contains general transfer pricing provisions empowering the Commissioner to adjust non-arm's length international related-party transactions, but Samoa has not enacted OECD-aligned Local File or Master File documentation thresholds, nor a Country-by-Country reporting obligation. For the target IC, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is not required in Samoa; however, a foreign parent or investor will be required to apply its own transfer pricing and CbCR rules, so the IC should maintain pricing records for intercompany ESG audit fees and carbon credit brokerage commissions in line with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has not enacted a full Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to BVI or Cayman. The applicable substance doctrine derives from the International Companies Act 1987 as amended (including the 2020 amendments adopted following Samoa's alignment with OECD/EU requirements). An IC must maintain a registered office and licensed registered agent in Samoa, keep books and records at the registered office, and must not carry on business with Samoa residents. The amended regime requires an annual compliance certification that the IC maintains sufficient presence and activity consistent with its stated business. A pure letterbox carbon offset brokerage with no operational capacity is high risk: it may be re-characterized as carrying on business in Samoa (forfeiting the tax exemption) or treated as an unlawful shell under the harmful tax practices standard.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filings

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2015 and the Crimes Act 2013, conviction is punishable by a fine of up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 7 years. The revenue authority will assess tax on all concealed profits at 27%, add late-payment penalties and compound interest, and may revoke the IC exemption and strike off the company.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who sign false returns commit an indictable offense and are personally liable for the unpaid tax where the company is a sham or fraudulent trading is proven. Piercing the corporate veil exposes directors to civil restitution, asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.
Shell Company and Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act 1987 as amended, the penalties include late-fee surcharges, administrative strike-off after 3 months' notice, dissolution with forfeiture of assets to the Registrar, disqualification of directors from managing an IC for up to 5 years, and retroactive loss of zero-tax status.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified and fined for wrongful trading. If the shell is used to defeat foreign tax claims, Samoan courts may pierce the corporate veil on sham grounds, exposing directors to restitution orders, anti-dissipation injunctions, and personal liability for the avoided tax.
AML/CFT Violations in Carbon Offset Brokerage

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 as amended, money laundering offenses are punishable by imprisonment for up to 14 years and fines of up to WST 1,000,000 for individuals, with higher penalties for corporate bodies. Record-keeping failures attract separate fines, and assets involved in laundering are subject to confiscation and international freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly authorize or permit laundering are personally liable; the corporate veil is expressly pierced under the AML statute. Civil forfeiture may extend to bonuses and dividends derived from the scheme, criminal travel bans prevent departure from Samoa pending trial, and imprisonment applies on conviction.
Prohibited Local Activity and Loss of IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: The IC ceases to be tax-exempt: the Samoa Revenue Corporation assesses 27% corporate tax on all Samoan-source profits, plus penalties and interest. The company may be ordered to convert to a domestic company, and the Registrar may impose fines and strike-off for repeated contraventions.

Director personal liability: Directors become jointly and severally liable for the assessed corporate tax and penalties if they knowingly permitted prohibited local trading. Senior managers face personal fines and, on proof of fraudulent intent, imprisonment under the International Companies Act 1987 and the Income Tax Act 2015, with exit restrictions pending resolution.
