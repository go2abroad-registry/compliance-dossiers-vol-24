Competent corporate registry

Registrar of International and Foreign Companies, Companies and International Companies Registry, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), Apia, Samoa.

Governing statute

International Companies Act 1987 (No. 28 of 1987) as amended — ss. 7 (Application and Certificate of Incorporation), 9 (Registered Office), 10 (Registered Agent), 11 (Memorandum of Association), 16 (Shares); read with the International Companies Regulations 1991.

Formation / constitutional filings

Prescribed Application for Incorporation of an International Company submitted under s. 7 of the International Companies Act 1987, together with the executed Memorandum and Articles of Association.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the International Companies Act 1987, s. 16; shares may be issued at nominal value, and no escrow or capital deposit account is required for an International Company. A higher registration fee may apply where authorized capital exceeds US$100,000.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for the registered office business suite issued by the Samoa Fire & Emergency Services Authority (FESA), covering smoke detection, extinguishers and egress; structural load inspection is not required for non-industrial office occupancy but building occupancy clearances apply; workplace and occupational safety compliance is administered under the Labour and Employment Relations Act 2013 by the Labour Division, MCIL.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the International Companies Act 1987, s. 16; shares may be issued at nominal value, and no escrow or capital deposit account is required for an International Company. A higher registration fee may apply where authorized capital exceeds US$100,000.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
Unanimous resolution of the sole incorporator and founding shareholder adopting: (1) the subscription and allotment of 100 fully paid ordinary shares for US$1.00 each to the founding member, entered in the register of members; (2) the registered office at the licensed registered agent's address in Apia, Samoa, under Section 9 of the International Companies Act 1987; (3) the appointment of [Director Name] as Director and [Secretary Name] as Secretary, each to serve until removed by resolution; and (4) authorization for officers and the registered agent to open bank accounts, affix the common seal, and execute all filing matters as may be necessary to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and commence business.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no industrial establishment. For a physical administrative office in Samoa, the Trade and Industry Division, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL) administers general business licensing matters.

Category: Not applicable — an International Company formed under the International Companies Act 1987 may carry on film-copyright international distribution and licensing activities directly upon incorporation, without an operating or manufacturing licence.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Ministry of Revenue; online taxpayer portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

For an International Company (IC): (1) incorporate under the International Companies Act 1987 via the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA); (2) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and statutory company records; (3) if the IC makes taxable supplies connected to Samoa (i.e., domestic annual turnover equal or exceeding WST 60,000) or elects voluntary registration, submit the SRA taxpayer/VAGST registration application (Form VAGST-01 / SRA Online taxpayer registration) with certified charter, incorporation certificate, director and shareholder details, and the Samoa registered office address; (4) SRA issues a TIN/VAGST number upon approval. ICs whose supplies are exclusively offshore are exempt from income tax and are not required to hold a VAGST TIN unless they make local taxable supplies; voluntary registration is available to recover input credits.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — International Company is exempt from income tax and most local levies under the International Companies Act 1987 (Part XVII); territorial scope limits taxation to Samoa-source economic activity; no capital gains tax and no withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders. — For an International Company (IC): (1) incorporate under the International Companies Act 1987 via the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA); (2) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and statutory company records; (3) if the IC makes taxable supplies connected to Samoa (i.e., domestic annual turnover equal or exceeding WST 60,000) or elects voluntary registration, submit the SRA taxpayer/VAGST registration application (Form VAGST-01 / SRA Online taxpayer registration) with certified charter, incorporation certificate, director and shareholder details, and the Samoa registered office address; (4) SRA issues a TIN/VAGST number upon approval. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — International Company is exempt from income tax and most local levies under the International Companies Act 1987 (Part XVII); territorial scope limits taxation to Samoa-source economic activity; no capital gains tax and no withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists in Samoa; the IC tax exemption is automatic and non-elective. No hybrid mismatch election is available as Samoa has not adopted BEPS Action 2 hybrid rules. ICs may elect governance under the International Companies Act 1987 rather than the Companies Act 2006, and may elect to maintain accounts in any convertible currency, such as USD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 (Samoa Tala) of annual taxable supplies made within Samoa; foreign and offshore-only licensors have no domestic registration nexus

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (physical removal from Samoa) and supplies of services to persons carrying on business outside Samoa, where the services are performed and exploited outside Samoa, are zero-rated under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992. For film licensing and royalty income, the licensing supply must be made to a non-resident licensee, the copyright exploitation must occur outside Samoa, and the IC must retain documentary proof; pure royalties and licensing fees to offshore distributors qualify as zero-rated export supplies.

  • Not applicable to pure services: no Customs Export Declaration or Bill of Lading is required for a film-rights license; such documents would only be required if physical media is shipped out of Samoa
  • Fully executed film license or distribution agreement (ISIC 5911 scope) identifying licensee territory, titles, and royalty consideration
  • VAGST tax invoice or royalty statement referencing the zero-rated export supply
  • Bank SWIFT wire advice or inward remittance certificate evidencing payment from the offshore licensee
  • Certificate of Incorporation (International Company) and TIN (if registered)
  • Schedule of licensed territories and exploitation/usage report demonstrating offshore use

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory electronic invoicing API, and no digital stamping regime is currently enforced by SRA. Tax invoices must contain the prescribed particulars under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992 (supplier name/TIN, recipient details, description, and VAGST amount). VAGST returns must be filed electronically through the SRA online portal.

SRA may audit zero-rating claims and require the IC to substantiate the offshore supply. If substantiation fails, the supply is recharacterized as taxable at 15% VAGST with interest accruing from the original due date. The Tax Administration Act 2011 and the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992 provide for civil penalties, including up to 150% of the tax shortfall for willful evasion, retrospective clawbacks, and criminal liability for fraudulent claims, with fines up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years for responsible officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is partially convertible: current-account flows such as royalties, licence fees, and trade settlements are freely processible through licensed authorized dealers, whereas resident capital-account transactions require CBS approval. A non-resident Samoan International Company operating entirely in foreign currency benefits from a statutory exchange-control ring-fence, so profit remittance, dividend payments, and capital repatriation are executable in USD/EUR without Tala conversion or CBS liquidity gating; only an intentional conversion into Tala triggers central-bank exchange-control filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters, Samoa requires export proceeds to be received through an authorized dealer and, where commercial operations require Tala, the foreign currency must be surrendered and converted to WST within thirty (30) days of receipt; no export proceeds are exempt without CBS approval. Non-resident International Companies in film copyright licensing are outside this surrender regime: 100% of film-distribution licence-fee receipts may be retained in designated foreign-currency accounts, and no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed on IC treasury operations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), acting through the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Exchange Control Regulations 1980, and the Financial Institutions Act 1996 (as amended); licensed authorized dealers execute CBS-approved foreign-currency transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low — Samoa has no digital EMI or electronic-money-institution licensing framework, and the Financial Institutions Act 1996 prohibits unlicensed entities from taking deposits or providing payment services to Samoa-resident persons. Offshore EMIs cannot serve as a Samoan clearing rail for WST settlements and should be restricted to third-country USD/EUR receivables and payables outside the CBS perimeter; a licensed Samoan commercial bank remains mandatory as the primary account-and-liquidity spine.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: International Companies have no statutory Local File/Master File threshold because they are tax-exempt. Samoa's Income Tax Act 2012 arm's length rules apply to resident companies with cross-border related party transactions above WST 500,000 in annual aggregate turnover; a Master File is recommended when group turnover exceeds WST 50 million. In the IC context, SIFA's Economic Substance Form 1 requires annual disclosure of gross income from relevant activity and substance indicators in Samoa regardless of amount.

Economic substance scope: The Samoa Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as amended) apply to International Companies engaged in relevant activities. Independent film licensing is an 'IP business' / 'distribution and service centre' activity; the IC must be managed and controlled in Samoa, maintain a physical office and local employees, and make adequate local expenditures. A mere mailbox with no operational substance in Samoa is prohibited and can lead to enforcement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The company is re-characterized as a resident taxable entity; back taxes assessed at the 27% corporate rate with a 100% shortfall penalty and daily late-payment interest. Criminal fine up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for willful evasion under the Income Tax Act 2012.
