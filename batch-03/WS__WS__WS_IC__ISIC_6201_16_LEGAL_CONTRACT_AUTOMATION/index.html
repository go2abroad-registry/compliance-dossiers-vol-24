Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue of Samoa (Maota o Lafi o Tupe), via the Samoa Ministry of Revenue Taxpayer Portal at https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company must apply for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) using Ministry of Revenue Form TIN-001. The application requires the Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of International Companies, a certified copy of the director's passport, a letter of consent from the registered agent in Samoa, proof of registered office address, and the relevant registration fees. The Director of Revenue issues the TIN within 5–10 working days. The TIN is also the VAT identification number and is prerequisite for VAGST registration, customs operations, and tax return filings. A branch of a foreign entity must additionally submit a Certificate of Registration from the Companies Office and nominate an authorised local representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned International Company must apply for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) using Ministry of Revenue Form TIN-001. The application requires the Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of International Companies, a certified copy of the director's passport, a letter of consent from the registered agent in Samoa, proof of registered office address, and the relevant registration fees. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. A Samoa International Company is treated as a separate legal person liable to tax only as expressly provided under the International Companies Act 1987 and the Income Tax Ordinance, and it enjoys an exemption from Samoan income tax, withholding tax, and capital gains tax. The only practical election is the voluntary registration for Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST) when taxable supplies are made in Samoa or to Samoan residents, which is irrevocable for a minimum period under the VAGST Act. Samoa has no hybrid mismatch election mechanism; however, the Commissioner may reclassify a corporate resident under controlled foreign company or General Anti-Avoidance Rules provisions where a Samoa IC is used to gain inconsistent tax outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 50,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; exemption for an International Company that makes no taxable supplies in Samoa

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the VAGST Act for (i) commodities physically exported from Samoa under customs control, (ii) services supplied directly to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and where the benefit of the service is received outside Samoa, and (iii) the supply of goods or services connected to international transport. A Samoa International Company providing legal contract automation SaaS and e-signature authentication must document the non-resident status of the customer, the offshore place of effective use, and the absence of any Samoan consumption. Retention of the signed master SaaS agreement, IP geolocation records of customers, billing address details, payment remittances, and evidence that the customer did not execute contracts in Samoa is necessary to substantiate the zero rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence
  • Commercial invoice and signed service contract evidencing offshore delivery

Samoa has not yet implemented a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or an API-based e-invoicing system. A VAGST-registered person must issue serially numbered tax invoices containing the supplier's TIN, VAGST registration number, full description of supply, tax amount, and issue date. Electronic invoicing via approved software is accepted if the invoice can be reproduced and audited in readable form. Tax returns and payments are filed electronically through the Ministry of Revenue portal. No digital stamping or government pre-clearance of invoices is currently mandated.

The Ministry of Revenue has the authority to audit export zero-rating claims within the statutory timeframe and, if substantiation is insufficient or a transaction is deemed mischaracterised, may retrospectively assess VAGST at 15% on the full supply value, impose late payment penalties and interest, and apply a civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall in cases of fraudulent or wilful misdeclaration. A successful clawback can also result in the revocation of exporter zero-rating privileges, publication of the taxpayer's default status, and referral for criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act with fines of up to SAT 50,000 and imprisonment not exceeding 2 years for knowingly participating in an evasion scheme.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is not a fully convertible offshore currency. Current account payments related to trade in goods and services are generally permitted without CBS pre-approval, provided a licensed bank verifies underlying documentation. Capital account transfers, including shareholder loans, capital injections, and outward portfolio investments by residents, require CBS permission or a commercial bank's delegated approval. Profit remittance by an International Company (IC) is treated favourably where all revenue is derived from outside Samoa and the IC has no domestic business activity; remittances are typically processed through the local banking system subject to tax clearance and compliance with anti-money laundering requirements. The primary practical hurdle is the unavailability of a deep offshore WST market, so most cross-border treasury flows are denominated in USD or AUD and require concluding FX transactions with the licensed bank as principal.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa does not impose a blanket 100% surrender requirement on export proceeds for International Companies. However, all payments received in foreign currency by a Samoa IC conducting domestic business must be repatriated into Samoa through a licensed authorised dealer within 30 days of receipt. For a pure offshore IC engaged in cross-border LegalTech SaaS subscriptions, no mandatory conversion into WST is required provided the proceeds are booked offshore and not remitted into Samoa's domestic financial system. If the IC voluntarily credits USD proceeds to a local foreign-currency account, the bank will require the underlying SaaS contract and invoices for credit, and conversion into WST will only occur upon explicit instruction for a permitted transaction.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), acting as the sole exchange control authority under the Foreign Exchange Control Act and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007, with day-to-day supervisory and approval powers delegated to licensed commercial banks operating in Samoa.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low Feasibility. Samoa has no statutory licensing regime for digital banks, electronic money institutions (EMIs), or non-bank payment processors. Offshore digital EMIs such as Mercury, Airwallex, or Wise are not authorised to open WST-denominated accounts or provide direct Samoa IBAN/routing rails for CBS-regulated transactions. A Samoa IC can only use such EMIs as ancillary receivable-collection gateways for non-Samoa customers, but the EMIs commonly reject offshore company types with no local operating substance unless the IC provides a professional legal opinion, registered agent certification, and evidence of beneficial ownership. The primary operating account should therefore remain with a tier-1 Samoa licensed bank, with the offshore EMI treated merely as a sub-ledger for specific inbound card payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound capital injection route for the Samoa IC is a SWIFT MT103 remittance from the parent company or founding shareholders to the tier-1 Samoa bank's USD or WST account. The remittance must include a purpose code indicating 'Overseas Direct Investment – Paid-Up Ordinary Share Capital' or 'Shareholder Subordinated Loan' on the SWIFT cover. The bank will require a letter from the IC's registered agent confirming the subscribed share capital, a copy of the board resolution approving the capital call, and a share subscription certificate. For the early-stage LegalTech SaaS entity, financial projections showing the shareholder loan being used exclusively for cloud infrastructure and software development are advisable. Funds received in USD are credited to a USD-denominated account and may be converted to WST for local operating expenses only where properly evidenced.Outbound trade and operational disbursements are cleared using SWIFT MT103 for low-value subscription payments, vendor payments, and recurring software licensing fees. For higher-risk legal outsourcing or technology services above USD 10,000, commercial banks may require a copy of the underlying contract and a proforma invoice, and might refer the payment to a documentary letter of credit where the trade route involves a non-OECD counterparty. Real-time gross settlement is only available domestically in WST; for USD/AUD cross-border flows, the Samoa bank uses its correspondent network in Australia or the United States (usually via BSP or ANZ's offshore head office), resulting in 1-3 business day execution. The IC should designate a single correspondent bank to minimise intermediary credits, and should encode its Samoa entity ID in the sending field 72 of the MT103 to avoid reconciliation delays.Outright FX Forwards (up to 12 months tenor) with a licensed Samoa commercial bank, subject to CBS dealer limits and documentary evidence of an underlying trade or subscription receivable.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) referencing the WST against USD/AUD, executed with an offshore bank, since WST lacks robust onshore deliverable depth.Currency Swaps for shareholder loan principal and interest repayments, limited to swaps between foreign currencies (e.g., USD/AUD) without crossing into WST if the original loan is denominated in a foreign currency.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (only with bank approval and full collateralisation, accepted for capital call and project financing, but not typically used for SaaS treasury operations due to simplicity and regulatory documentation cost).

A remote director may sign electronically through a SIFA-approved filing agent; the licenced Samoa agent countersigns. Late filing penalty: WST 50 per month or part thereof, capped at WST 500; the Registrar may administratively strike off the IC after 3 months of default; reinstatement requires court order or payment of reinstatement fees. For continued default, the registered agent and directors may be jointly fined up to WST 1,000.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory local transfer-pricing file applies to an exempt IC because it has no Samoan taxable income. If an IC acquires Samoan-source activity or becomes a taxable local person, the Income Tax Act 2015 Transfer Pricing Rules require a Local File where annual related-party cross-border transactions exceed WST 5,000,000 or the transaction value exceeds WST 1,000,000 per connected party.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime for International Companies; however, the International Companies Act 1987 and SIFA administrative practice require an IC to maintain a Samoan registered office, a licensed registered agent, and to refrain from domestic Samoan business. For foreign tax residency purposes, an IC must demonstrate substantive business through board minutes, commercial contracts, accounting records and bank accounts. Lack of substance may cause a parent jurisdiction to recharacterise the IC as a CFC or as resident in that parent jurisdiction. SIFA guidance also requires an annual economic substance declaration attached to the annual return.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Sham Transactions

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2015, tax evasion attracts a penalty up to 200% of the tax shortfall, a criminal fine up to WST 100,000, and imprisonment up to 7 years. The court may order forfeiture of connected assets.

Director personal liability: Directors, registered agents and shadow officers are jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded tax; travel bans, asset restraining orders and director disqualification may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike off the IC under the International Companies Act 1987; strike-off penalties up to WST 500 apply, and assets held by the struck-off company may be forfeited to the Crown unless restored by court order.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for debts and obligations incurred after the striking-off if they knowingly continued trading; travel bans and personal fines up to WST 10,000 may follow.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: A body corporate may be fined up to WST 1,000,000; natural persons may be imprisoned up to 10 years; the court may order civil asset forfeiture and cancellation of the company registration.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners may be criminally prosecuted where they knowingly permitted money laundering; personal civil restitution, travel bans, and professional disqualification are available.
Fraudulent Filing and Forgery

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act 1987, a person who makes a false statement or forges documents commits an offence punishable by a fine up to WST 50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and agents who sign or authorise fraudulent filings are personally liable for penalties, restitution of third-party losses, and may be disqualified from acting as company directors or registered agents.
