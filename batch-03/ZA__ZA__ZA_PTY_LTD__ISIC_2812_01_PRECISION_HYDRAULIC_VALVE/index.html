Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official portal: www.sars.gov.za; eFiling platform: sarsefiling.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to secure the enterprise registration number. 2) Register for income tax via SARS eFiling or SARS branch using Form COR/201 to obtain the company's SARS Income Tax Reference Number (the statutory TIN for corporate taxpayers). 3) Apply separately for VAT registration under the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991; registration is compulsory when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period, and voluntary from ZAR 50,000. 4) Register as a SARS Customs client (customs code/TVN) for lodgement of export declarations (SAD500) and import bills of entry. 5) Register for PAYE, UIF and SDL if employees are engaged. 6) Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the Pty Ltd equity; no local participation requirement applies, but a South African bank confirmation is required to complete SARS income tax registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (residence-based taxation; a Pty Ltd incorporated or effectively managed in South Africa is taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate rate; no pass-through, partnership or check-the-box treatment is available for a company) — 1) Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to secure the enterprise registration number. 2) Register for income tax via SARS eFiling or SARS branch using Form COR/201 to obtain the company's SARS Income Tax Reference Number (the statutory TIN for corporate taxpayers). 3) Apply separately for VAT registration under the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991; registration is compulsory when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period, and voluntary from ZAR 50,000. 4) Register as a SARS Customs client (customs code/TVN) for lodgement of export declarations (SAD500) and import bills of entry. 5) Register for PAYE, UIF and SDL if employees are engaged. 6) Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the Pty Ltd equity; no local participation requirement applies, but a South African bank confirmation is required to complete SARS income tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (residence-based taxation; a Pty Ltd incorporated or effectively managed in South Africa is taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate rate; no pass-through, partnership or check-the-box treatment is available for a company)

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification regime exists. Key statutory elections and reliefs under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 include: CFC high-tax exemption election (s9D(2A)) where foreign tax paid equals or exceeds 75% of the South African tax that would otherwise be due; deemed-CFC election (s9D(9A)); corporate restructure roll-over elections under s44 (amalgamation), s42 (asset-for-share) and s45 (intra-group transfer); R&D allowance election under s11D (pre-approved by the Department of Science, Technology and Innovation); VAT payments-basis election under s15 of the VAT Act for vendors with taxable turnover below ZAR 2.5 million. Hybrid mismatch rules under ITA ss8E, 8F, 8FA, 23H and 23M operate mandatorily to deny double deductions or deduction-without-inclusion and are not subject to an elective carve-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 per annum of taxable supplies (compulsory threshold); voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000; non-resident suppliers of electronic services are subject to the same ZAR 1,000,000 threshold and must register under s54A

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act zero-rates movable goods (including precision hydraulic valves, HS 8481.20) supplied to a non-resident on the condition that the goods are exported from South Africa within 90 days of the date of supply, unless SARS grants an extension. The supplier must hold original documentation proving physical export. Section 11(2)(l) zero-rates services supplied to non-residents to the extent the services are not used or consumed in South Africa. Where export conditions are not met, the supply is standard-rated at 15%.

  • Validated SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged electronically through SARS e-SAD / PENGUIN customs system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch from a South African port or airport to the non-resident purchaser
  • Commercially-issued full tax invoice in the supplier's name reflecting the zero-rated supply and HS classification 8481.20
  • Packing list and container/shipment manifest corroborating the exported goods
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (Form R/C) or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds following the export
  • Relevant incoterms transport/insurance documentation and, where applicable, the original certificate of origin

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control mandate is currently in force. SARS requires a valid full tax invoice under s20 of the VAT Act for supplies exceeding ZAR 5,000 and an abridged VAT invoice for supplies between ZAR 50 and ZAR 5,000. Electronic invoices are accepted; SARS is progressively rolling out a national e-invoicing interoperability framework (SARS eFiling APIs and SARS Connect) but issuance remains non-cleared. Vendors must retain all invoices, export evidence and accounting records for at least 5 years for audit verification.

SARS applies strict audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. If the export bundle is incomplete or the goods are not exported within the statutory timeframe, SARS may raise an additional 15% VAT assessment on the supply, with interest under s39 of the VAT Act, and may invoke understatement penalties under s223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 graded at 10%, 25%, 50%, 75% and up to 150% of the shortfall for intentional evasion. In fraud cases, s60 of the VAT Act permits additional tax of up to double the evaded VAT, and prescription periods are extended: 3 years for standard assessments, 5 years for material non-disclosure and unlimited for fraudulent returns or non-registration. Criminal prosecution under s58 of the VAT Act and s234 of the TAA carries fines and/or imprisonment (up to 5 years for wilful VAT fraud). Separately, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers South African Reserve Bank exchange-control enforcement, including administrative penalties, compulsory repatriation orders and potential invalidation of the underlying export zero-rating.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current account transactions, including trade settlements and ordinary business remittances, subject to documentary verification and tax clearance where applicable; capital account transactions are regulated under a risk-based approval framework requiring Authorised Dealer authorisation or SARB FinSurv approval for non-ordinary or larger transactions; foreign direct investment capital injections into an approved (Pty) Ltd structure are permitted, as are offshore loan accounts, provided the inward listing is recorded and foreign funding complies with the SARB applied-origin rules; profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are generally permitted after satisfaction of arm's length pricing, transfer pricing documentation, and the submission of a tax clearance certificate from the South African Revenue Service (SARS) confirming the company's tax affairs are in order; dividend payments require that the company is in good standing with SARS and that the beneficial owner provides the requisite declaration where applicable; there are no residual exchange control restrictions on current account profit remittance for a South African resident company once FDI compliance, share register, and bank verification are completed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Africa maintains a no-formal-surrender exchange control regime for export proceeds: exporters are not required to convert hard-currency receipts into ZAR at a mandated ratio, nor to surrender export proceeds to the central bank; however, all export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa within 30 days after shipment (subject to extension by the Authorised Dealer in limited circumstances), must be received through an Authorised Dealer bank account, and must be documented with the relevant bill of entry, shipping documents, and proof of receipt; once received in a designated foreign currency account, funds may be retained in foreign currency for fees, commissions, or other approved trade-related expenses, but the monies must remain within the South African banking system unless an exemption or explicit approval is granted; transactional reporting (formerly Form A and related exchange control forms) is now largely digitised through the Authorised Dealer's electronic filing system in line with SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual, and any conversion to ZAR is optional at market rates.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), with delegated administration to Authorised Dealers (commercial banks with foreign exchange dealing licences) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations (1961); the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) regulates AML/CTF obligations under FIC Act 38 of 2001.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for primary operating account: offshore digital EMIs (e.g. Wise, Currenxie, MultiPass, Mercury for non-US entities) cannot serve as the sole transactional account for a South African operational (Pty) Ltd because SARB exchange control authorities require the South African entity's funds, its export proceeds, and its trade settlements to be channelled through Authorised Dealers licensed by SARB; South African bank account or an authorised branch is mandatory for ZAR-denominated operational cash management and for conversion of foreign currency receipts; offshore EMIs may only be used on a supplementary basis for minor pre-funded balances where no lending, no overdraft, and no South African-source receipts flow through the EMI; given the banking AML risk tier of Low_Risk_Standard for precision valve manufacturing, a niche cross-border fintech account could support procurement from foreign suppliers only where funds are transferred from a South African Authorised Dealer bank after the relevant SARB forms (e.g. detailed payment instruction for foreign cash payments) have been processed, but the corporate treasury anchor must remain a tier-1 domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital for the ZA (Pty) Ltd should be wired in the company's name as 'Share capital contribution — FDI' using SWIFT MT103 or via a centralised treasury in the parent's jurisdiction; the remitter must use the correct purpose code for inward FDI; the receiving Authorised Dealer issues the BOP/FI(N) filing and maintains the register of 'inward listing'; practical steps: (1) foreign shareholder remits funds from its own bank account (not from pooled accounts or third-party collection accounts unless fully explained) to the newly opened Standard Bank or FNB capital escrow operational account; (2) the bank applies an inward foreign investment verification and issues the required inward filing; (3) the company records the contribution as share capital or shareholder loan based on the Memorandum of Incorporation and Companies Act records; use of the ZAR or FX settlement currency is at the remitter's market-choice; the bank will require a certified board resolution specifying the exact amount, purpose code, and beneficiary bank account, an investor's declaration that no sub-participation exists, and a SARS tax clearance for the company if the capital is used to fund offshore imports; if the inbound amount exceeds ZAR 15 million in a 12-month period, the bank may require an EMP/FDI application and stricter documentary support; for trade-related inflows, the purpose codes used are typically 'share subscription proceeds' / 'equity capital' (code 4101 or 4102 depending on bank), but if a loan is contemplated, the correct code is 'loan receipts' (code 4201/4202) and the bank must register the loan; import payments require the prior submission of an original invoice, bill of lading, and SARS import number if capital is routed via trade credit.Outbound disbursements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for full-value correspondent transfers with proper structured purpose codes, and through Commercial Letters of Credit (CLCs) for large procurement transactions; Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 must be submitted within 12 months after the company's financial year-end. For a company with a 31 March year-end, the return is due by 31 March of the following year unless SARS prescribes an earlier date. Provisional tax payments are due at 6-month intervals, with the second provisional payment due within 6 months after the financial year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional payment is due within 6 months of the start of the year of assessment; second provisional payment is due within 6 months after the end of the year of assessment. A third top-up payment may be made within 6 months of the second payment to reduce interest exposure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 5,000,000 aggregate value of cross-border connected-person transactions in a financial year triggers the mandatory requirement to prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File). A Master File is expected for large multinational groups with substantial intercompany financing and IP transactions, and Country-by-Country Reporting applies where consolidated group revenue exceeds ZAR 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime. Economic substance is nevertheless embedded in the domestic law through the place-of-effective-management residence test, the Controlled Foreign Company attribution rules in section 9D of the Income Tax Act, the general anti-avoidance rule in section 103, and the requirement that an assessed loss is available only if the company carries on an active trade. A South African (Pty) Ltd must evidence real local decision-making, operational capacity, employment, and physical presence, or SARS may disregard the entity as a conduit and re-attribute its income to the ultimate parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under sections 234 and 235 of the Tax Administration Act, with fines up to ZAR 10 million and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil understatement penalties under section 223 can reach 150% of the tax shortfall for intentional avoidance, plus compound interest and a possible SARS reference for criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved or signed false returns are jointly and severally liable for the civil penalty under the Tax Administration Act. The court may order civil restitution, issue a travel ban under section 184, disqualify directors, and impose imprisonment on those found personally responsible for the evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS may invoke section 103 of the Income Tax Act to cancel an impermissible tax avoidance arrangement, re-characterise transactions, impose tax as if the arrangement had not taken place, and assess penalties up to 150% of the additional tax. Section 20A may further deny assessed loss set-off where the company is not actively trading.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for unpaid corporate tax if they voted for or facilitated the sham structure. The court may pierce the corporate veil, order personal restitution of dividends or management fees, and issue travel bans pending payment of the SARS debt.
Transfer Pricing and Cross-Border Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust the consideration to an arm's-length amount under section 31 of the Income Tax Act, impose a secondary adjustment treating excess payments as deemed dividends or interest, and levy understatement penalties from 25% to 150% depending on conduct. SARS may also impose record-keeping penalties under the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: The financial director and CEO may be held personally liable for the company's tax shortfall if they knowingly implemented or approved non-arm's-length prices. SARS can use civil recovery mechanisms, asset freezing orders, and travel bans where a director is about to leave the country with a substantial tax debt outstanding.
Dividend Withholding Tax / Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The company remains accountable for the unpaid dividend tax as the withholding agent. SARS collects the tax, interest, and administrative penalties under sections 210 to 215 of the Tax Administration Act. A false claim of treaty relief exposes the company to the full 20% tax plus penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of the company are jointly and severally liable for the dividends withholding tax obligation under section 64K of the Income Tax Act. Individuals who authorised the distribution may be pursued personally, and travel bans may be imposed under section 184 until the tax debt is settled.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Companies Act and the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, with criminal penalties of imprisonment up to 15 years for knowingly conducting a transaction that conceals the proceeds of unlawful activity or evades AML reporting obligations. SARS may also treat concealed offshore interests as an undisclosed source of funds attracting penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly submit false beneficial ownership declarations may be disqualified by the CIPC and held personally liable in civil proceedings. The National Prosecuting Authority may seize assets and prohibit directors from leaving South Africa while investigations or tax recovery actions are pending.
