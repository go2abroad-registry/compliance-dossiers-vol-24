Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling portal (https://www.sars.gov.za and https://efiling.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establish a South African income tax and VAT presence by registering with SARS; for a (Pty) Ltd, incorporation with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) generates an automatic provisional income tax reference number, but VAT registration requires a separate SARS Form VAT101, supported by Companies Act documents, proof of physical or fiscal address in South Africa, and identity/authorisation documents of directors; foreign-owned entities must appoint a local SARS representative / public officer and apply for an income tax reference number via SARS registration before transacting; threshold-based VAT registration is compulsory for taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in 12 months.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities establish a South African income tax and VAT presence by registering with SARS; for a (Pty) Ltd, incorporation with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) generates an automatic provisional income tax reference number, but VAT registration requires a separate SARS Form VAT101, supported by Companies Act documents, proof of physical or fiscal address in South Africa, and identity/authorisation documents of directors; foreign-owned entities must appoint a local SARS representative / public officer and apply for an income tax reference number via SARS registration before transacting; threshold-based VAT registration is compulsory for taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in 12 months.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box system exists. South Africa applies statutory corporate residence and controlled foreign company rules (Sections 9D and 25D of the Income Tax Act). Hybrid mismatch rules under the Taxation Laws Amendment Act 2020 apply OECD hybrid mismatch recommendations and may require elective ordering adjustments; election of payment methods or deemed supply options for VAT is available under Section 21(1)(b) VAT Act but no flow-through classification is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory turnover in 12-month rolling period; ZAR 50,000 voluntary threshold for non-resident suppliers; goods imported into South Africa are subject to VAT regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods qualify for zero-rating under Section 11(1)(a) if the goods are supplied to an exporter and exported within 12 months, or under Section 11(1)(c) where the supplier has proof that goods were delivered to, and removed from, South Africa within the prescribed period; costs/transport charges incidental to export (Section 11(1)(l)) and international services (Section 11(2)(l)) may also be zero-rated. The supplier must retain documentary proof of physical export and the foreign currency consideration.

  • SARS Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / DA550)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Truck Manifest
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of export (delivery/removal certificate)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange receipt from SARB-approved bank)

SARS has no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. Tax invoices must comply with Section 20 VAT Act and SARS Notice 1446/2009; electronic tax invoices are permitted using approved electronic data interchange (EDI) XML formats with SARS eFiling. Full electronic invoicing (Peppol-style) is under development but not yet mandatory for private companies. SARS may request digital records and API-enabled reporting for eFiling VAT201 submissions.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating is an offence under Tax Administration Act and VAT Act. SARS will disallow the zero-rating, assess 15% VAT plus 10% to 200% understatement penalty (Section 223 TAA), apply interest, and can pursue criminal prosecution for tax evasion; directors are personally liable for undeclared VAT/TAA civil penalties. Export claims must be supported by evidence for at least 5 years and may be clawed back retroactively if documentation fails.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services, subject to documentary compliance and authorised-dealer processing. Capital-account flows, such as equity injections, intercompany loans, and loan repayments, require exchange-control reporting or prior approval through FinSurv, particularly for non-resident direct investment. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are freely permitted on after-tax profits provided dividend tax has been declared to the South African Revenue Service (SARS), a tax clearance certificate is presented, and the beneficial ownership and anti-money-laundering screening requirements of the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, 2002 (FICA) are satisfied. Practical hurdles include enhanced source-of-funds verification, sanctions-list screening, beneficial-ownership registration at the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), and accurate classification of the cross-border payment under the SARB Balance of Payments (BOP) reporting framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment. Upon receipt, the exporter must surrender the foreign currency to an authorised dealer in exchange for ZAR at the prevailing market rate; the mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of the invoiced amount unless a specific exemption or foreign-currency account dispensation has been granted by the SARB FinSurv prior to the transaction. Any deviation from this rule, including delayed repatriation or unauthorised retention of export earnings offshore, constitutes a contravention of the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and may expose the company to statutory penalties and loss of authorised-dealer facilities. The entity must also submit the relevant export incentive declarations and retain supporting documents such as invoices, customs records, and bank settlement statements for audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and authorised dealers under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for purely offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs). Offshore EMIs licensed only in the United Kingdom or other EEA jurisdictions cannot provide ZAR-denominated deposit accounts or act as SARB authorised dealers. They may operate in South Africa under the National Payment System Act, 1998 as payment service providers, but only for initiation and collection of payments, not as a treasury clearing hub. The company may use such EMIs as a low-cost gateway for e-commerce receipts from Amazon Pay and Stripe, but funds must be swept daily to a local tier-1 commercial bank account to mitigate settlement risk and comply with exchange-control reporting obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from non-resident shareholders must be routed through an authorised dealer using the SWIFT MT103 message format with an unambiguous purpose code: BOP code 3000 for direct equity investment and BOP code 3700 for intercompany trade loans. The remittance instruction should name the South African bank and the dedicated Capital Escrow Account; the authorised dealer will log the transaction in the SARB Financial Surveillance Department file and issue an electronic import/export compliance reference. Prior approval under form MP921 is required if the investment is treated as a loan advance or private equity related to share capital. On receipt, the entity must reconcile the cash against the executed subscription agreement or loan agreement, retain the SWIFT confirmation and foreign-exchange settlement certificate, and submit a quarterly BOP report where applicable. The bank will also require the entity’s FICA declaration, proof of CIPC incorporation, valid board resolution, and tax clearance status before activation of the capital account.Outbound trade settlements for imported raw materials and exported wooden puzzles should be executed via SWIFT MT103 messages using standard trade purpose codes (BOP code 2000 for imports of goods and BOP code 1000 for exports of goods). For amounts exceeding USD 50,000 and for transactions involving letters of credit, the entity should use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (LC) confirmed by a top-tier international correspondent bank; the LC must be issued by the local authorised dealer and advised through the beneficiary’s bank. Direct payments for recurring trading partners may also be cleared through the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system at the national level, but physical cross-border clearance requires correspondent banking relationships. All outbound payments must be prefunded in ZAR or from the foreign-currency account, accompanied by a distribution-voucher, invoice, customs clearance, proof of delivery, and relevant regulatory declarations.Forward exchange contracts (FECs) on ZAR/USD/EUR/GBP with tenors of up to 24 months directly tied to trade receivables or payables and supported by underlying commercial documentationCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) executed with an authorised dealer to hedge non-ZAR loan exposure or term-export cashflowsEuropean plain-vanilla currency options on ZAR for protection against adverse exchange rate movements on forecasted dividend remittances or import costsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible domestically for ZAR resident entities because ZAR is fully convertible; NDFs may only be executed offshore by non-resident counterparties writing ZAR exposure, and such transactions do not form part of the domestic treasury plan.
