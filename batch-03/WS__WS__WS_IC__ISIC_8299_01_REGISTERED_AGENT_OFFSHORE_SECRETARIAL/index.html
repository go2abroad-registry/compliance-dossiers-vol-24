Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Apia, Samoa - integrated tax and customs administration; official portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws (eServices for TIN registration, VAT registration, monthly returns and customs declarations). The Registrar of International Companies within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL) administers company incorporation and annual returns; SRA issues all tax registrations and certificates.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - The licensed Samoa registered agent (ICSP) files the SRA TIN application (company TIN registration form) on behalf of the International Company (IC), attaching the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, director and shareholder register, notarized passport copies of all beneficial owners, and certified proof of the registered office in Samoa. Step 2 - SRA performs KYC/AML screening and issues the TIN, which is also used for CRS/FATCA reporting. Step 3 - The IC obtains a Certificate of Tax Exemption from SRA/Registrar confirming exemption under the International Companies Act 2018 and the Income Tax Act 2015; this certificate is required by international banks and foreign counterparties. Step 4 - The agent pays the annual government fee (USD 100) and files the exempt-company annual return with the Registrar; the IC files no income tax return because it derives no Samoan-source taxable income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: A Samoa International Company is a non-resident exempt entity for all Samoan direct and indirect taxes. Foreign-source income, including registered-agent, company-secretarial and corporate administration fees from offshore clients, is wholly exempt from income tax; no capital gains tax, no dividend, interest or royalty withholding taxes on offshore payments, no estate or gift duty, and no business license tax on offshore activities. The exemption is forfeited if the IC carries on a commercial business in Samoa; the International Companies Act 2018 prohibits domestic trading and permits only concessionary acts such as contracting with the Government of Samoa, holding director meetings, maintaining a bank account, and transacting securities dealings with other exempt offshore entities.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Samoa; the IC's exempt status is fixed by statute. Available statutory elections: (1) voluntary VAT registration under the VAT Act 2008 s.10 for supplies below the compulsory threshold, practically applicable only if the IC were to engage in permitted domestic supplies; (2) an election by the IC to convert to a Domestic Company under the International Companies Act 2018, thereby entering entity-level taxation at the resident corporate income tax rate (27% headline, 2015 Act) on worldwide income; (3) CRS/FATCA classification elections to be treated as a non-financial entity, an investment entity or a reporting financial institution; (4) transitional elections for foreign companies to redomicile into Samoa as International Companies. Samoa has not enacted domestic hybrid-mismatch rules; BEPS-aligned outcomes depend on treaty application and the Multilateral Convention.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WS$ 100,000 (SAT 100,000) of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period for a business carried on in Samoa (VAT Act 2008 s.9); voluntary registration is available below the threshold but is factually inapplicable to an exempt International Company deriving only offshore income.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (1) goods physically exported from Samoa, evidenced by removal within the statutory period; (2) exported services, including offshore secretarial, corporate administration and registered-agent services, supplied to a non-resident person who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and not directly connected with land in Samoa or with movable property in Samoa; (3) international transport, insurance, and supplies to diplomatic missions. Registered-agent services supplied by an International Company to another International Company qualify as exported services where the recipient is non-resident and there is no direct land nexus; the separately designated statutory 'maintenance of registered office' element carries a legal-seat nexus with Samoa and requires a documented non-resident-client declaration and legal analysis. Input VAT attributable to the exempt offshore business is not creditable unless the corresponding supply is properly zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged electronically via ASYCUDA World (SRA Customs)
  • Bill of Lading, Master or House Air Waybill, or postal dispatch evidence
  • Commercial invoice and VAT zero-rated tax invoice compliant with VAT Act 2008 s.18
  • Executed service agreement or contract evidencing the offshore service
  • Non-Resident Client Declaration or certificate of foreign residence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank advice proving receipt of export proceeds through a licensed Samoa commercial bank

No real-time electronic fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or e-invoicing API mandate exists in Samoa. SRA requires a VAT-compliant tax invoice containing the mandatory particulars under VAT Act 2008 s.18 (supplier name and TIN, recipient name and TIN, invoice date, serial number, description, quantity, taxable value, VAT amount or zero-rated indicator). Electronic invoices are accepted without fiscal-printer certification or government API integration. Customs declarations are lodged through the ASYCUDA World electronic single window.

SRA may reassess and claw back zero-rated input VAT credits and refunds within 4 years of the relevant tax period, with no limitation period for fraud or willful evasion (Taxes Administration Act 2012). Penalties: shortfall penalties of 25% for careless conduct, up to 50% for intentional understatement, and up to 100% or more for deliberate evasion; default interest applies at 2% per month, compounding; criminal prosecution of directors and registered agents carries fines of up to WS$ 200,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent zero-rating claims. The Registrar may additionally suspend or revoke the ICSP license upon false certification, and the client International Company may lose its Certificate of Tax Exemption retrospectively.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains a managed-float currency regime for the Samoan Tala (WST). Current-account payments such as trade, services, wages and remittances are generally permitted through authorised dealers with underlying documentation. Capital-account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment, foreign-currency borrowing, intercompany shareholder loans, cross-border guarantees and portfolio flows, are subject to prior Central Bank of Samoa approval or a post-transaction notification requirement. For a non-resident-owned Samoan International Company, the entity is exempt from Samoa income tax on foreign-sourced income, but an outward remittance of WST profits or dividends will be processed only after the authorised dealer verifies a board resolution, audited or management financial statements, an international company good-standing certificate, a beneficial-ownership declaration and confirmation that the ultimate recipient is not a sanctioned person. The practical hurdle is therefore not tax, but the enhanced due diligence required to establish source of funds and the absence of any Samoa exchange-control objection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1977 and Central Bank of Samoa notices, exporters must arrange for export proceeds to be repatriated to Samoa through an authorised dealer within 90 days from the date of export. The default statutory surrender obligation is 100% conversion of the foreign-currency export invoice value into WST, unless the exporter obtains a Central Bank of Samoa foreign-currency retention licence or operates as a tax-exempt International Company with an approved foreign-currency account. For ISIC 8299 registered-agent and offshore secretarial services, there is no physical export of goods; cross-border fee receivables are treated as current-account receipts, must be received by wire through a licensed authorised dealer, and the entity may retain or convert the foreign currency only with bank-recorded exchange-control reporting and source-of-funds documentation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and responsible for administering the Exchange Control Act 1977 and all Exchange Control Notices issued thereunder.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, supplementary corridor only. Legal constraints: Samoa does not license independent non-resident fintech electronic-money institutions. Any entity accepting deposits or issuing e-money in Samoa must be licensed by the Central Bank of Samoa under the Financial Institutions Act 2007 and the applicable AML/CFT regulations. Non-resident digital EMIs, such as Mercury, Revolut Business or Airwallex, may onboard a Samoa International Company as a remote corporate client, but they cannot provide a Samoa-local clearing account or hold client funds as a Samoa-regulated money-transfer business. Relying on such an EMI as the primary treasury account creates legal, sanctions and asset-isolation risks and will be treated as an adverse structural red flag by local regulated banks. Accordingly, foreign EMIs may be used only for low-volume cross-border professional-fee receipts, with full disclosure to the Samoa bank and registered agent, and must never be used for client money or regulatory capital.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas remitting bank to the Samoa authorised dealer bank. The beneficiary field must state the exact legal name of the Samoa International Company and include the International Companies Registry number in the beneficiary-reference line. The remitter must use the home-jurisdiction outward remittance purpose code for equity investment or capital transfer; for example, 'equity capital', 'capital transfer' or 'loan disbursement' as recognised by the sending bank's classification system. The Samoa receiving bank will pre-clear the wire against a board resolution authorising the capital call, the share subscription agreement, the share register and source-of-funds documentation. Wires incorrectly described as 'consultancy' or 'trade payments' will be rejected or frozen. Any shareholder loan exceeding USD 10,000 must be supported by a written facility agreement and reported to the Central Bank of Samoa exchange-control section through the authorised dealer within the prescribed notification window, or the company may face Exchange Control Act penalties.Outbound settlements from a Samoa International Company are cleared through the authorised dealer's correspondent banking network. For professional-fee invoices and general operational disbursements, the preferred rail is a SWIFT MT103 from the IC's USD or WST account, with the beneficiary account information, the relevant reference for the invoice and the exchange-control declaration if the amount exceeds the WST-equivalent threshold of USD 50,000. For goods-based trade settlements, the acceptable rail is an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 or MT720, with the Samoa authorised dealer acting as issuing or advising bank. USD settlements are cleared via CHIPS or Fedwire through New York correspondents; AUD and NZD settlements use local clearing systems; WST settlements are domestic and require a licensed authorised dealer transaction. The bank will require invoices, director resolutions and any applicable exchange-control declaration before execution. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Annual Return must be filed within 28 days after the anniversary of the date of incorporation. For a company incorporated on 1 January, the return is due by 28 January each year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A – International Companies are outside the scope of Samoa's Income Tax Act 2012 (which contains no specific transfer pricing rules but applies only to domestic taxable entities). However, the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2020 imposes substance filing thresholds. For an IC with gross annual income from relevant geographies exceeding SAT 1,000,000 (approx. USD 370,000), the company must file an Economic Substance Return and maintain a Local File-style record of core income-generating activities, including evidence of management, employees, premises, and expenditure in Samoa. Master File documentation is not statutorily mandated, but prudent practice for an IC in the registered agent/secretarial sector with cross-border related-party transactions is to prepare a thin-capitalization memorandum and a TP policy summary.

Economic substance scope: Yes. The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2020 (as amended) applies. A Samoa International Company must demonstrate economic substance in Samoa proportionate to its activity. For the registered agent and corporate secretarial business, the relevant activity is 'Headquarters and similar services' and 'Services to related parties'. The company must show: (a) a physical office in Samoa; (b) at least one employee in Samoa (can be a licensed registered agent's employee if acting as the company's secretary); (c) annual operating expenditure in Samoa (e.g. WST 20,000–50,000 depending on turnover); (d) board meetings held in Samoa at least once per year; and (e) records and corporate minutes stored in Samoa. Failure to satisfy ESR results in penalties and possible automatic strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Domestic Income Derivation

Statutory sanction: Loss of exempt status; retrospective taxation of all profits derived from Samoa at the 27% corporate income tax rate; interest on unpaid tax at 12% per annum; penalty of 200% of the tax evaded under the Income Tax Act 2012; criminal fine up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years for willful evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they signed the false declaration or knowingly permitted the company to engage in Samoan domestic business. Travel bans may be issued by the Samoan Ministry for Revenue, and directors may be subject to extradition under mutual assistance treaties. Imprisonment of up to 7 years applies to fraudulent tax evasion.
Shell Company / Empty Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2020, the Registrar may impose a penalty of WST 500 per day up to WST 100,000 for failure to meet economic substance. The company may be struck off the Register; the Court may order the winding-up of the company and forfeiture of assets located in Samoa. The tax-exempt status is revoked retroactively, and a 100% surcharge on any unpaid tax may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell IC may be held personally liable for the company's tax liabilities up to the amount of distributions or payments received from the company. The Samoan Court can issue a director disqualification order, a travel ban, and a restitution order requiring disgorgement of profits. In egregious cases, directors can be prosecuted for fraudulent trading (penalty up to 5 years' imprisonment).
AML/CFT Violation (Beneficial Ownership Concealment)

Statutory sanction: Penalties range from WST 50,000 to WST 250,000 for each breach. The registered agent's license may be suspended or revoked. The IC may be struck off. Individuals knowingly involved can face a fine of WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Any assets derived from criminal conduct are subject to civil forfeiture under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent face personal criminal liability for failing to comply with AML requirements. Civil penalties include restitution of all illicit gains, a travel ban, and a 10-year prohibition from acting as a director of any Samoa registered company. Imprisonment up to 5 years applies for money laundering offenses. The statutory presumption of directorial knowledge in AML cases shifts the burden of proof to the director.
Failure to File Annual Return and ESR Filing

Statutory sanction: Late filing fee of WST 200 plus WST 50 per month for the Annual Return. Independently, the ESR regime imposes a fixed penalty of WST 500 for initial non-filing, WST 1,000 for continuing non-filing, and WST 10,000 for knowingly filing a false ESR return, plus a daily default penalty of WST 100 per day. The Registrar may strike off the company after 6 months of default, resulting in the transfer of all the company's property to the Samoan Crown.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for all debts and liabilities of the company incurred after the date of default if the company continues to trade while struck off. Further, directors who sign a false ESR declaration face criminal penalties of a fine up to WST 50,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years. A civil restitution order may compel a director to reimburse the company for any tax advantage gained.
