Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue (MOR) of the Independent State of Samoa; integrated e-services portal: Matai Online – https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 2020 must engage its licensed registered agent to file Form TIN-02 (Application for Taxpayer Identification Number) with the Ministry of Revenue, attaching the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office address, and passport copies plus proof of identity for all directors and ultimate beneficial owners; MOR issues the TIN within approximately 3–5 business days upon verification. Where the IC derives all gross income offshore, no Samoan income tax registration is required, but a TIN is mandatory as a precondition for GST registration if the IC makes taxable supplies exceeding WST 100,000 in Samoa or elects voluntary GST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 2005.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Samoan domestic law. Relevant statutory elections: (1) voluntary GST registration under section 8(3) of the GST Act 2005 for suppliers below the WST 100,000 threshold; (2) written election by the IC to maintain non-resident tax status by keeping central management and control outside Samoa, thereby retaining full exemption from Samoa income tax on offshore income; (3) application to MOR for a binding tax ruling or certificate of exemption under the Tax Administration Act 2015 confirming tax-neutral zero-rating treatment; and (4) under the Limited Liability Companies Act 2018, members may elect member-level pass-through taxation, though such election is not available to the International Company form itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (Samoan Tala) in annual taxable turnover; imported services supplied by a non-resident to a GST-registered Samoan recipient are additionally subject to reverse-charge accounting at the 15% rate regardless of the recipient's turnover threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 12(1) of the GST Act 2005 to (a) goods physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of the supply, and (b) services supplied directly to a non-resident customer, for use and benefit wholly outside Samoa, where the supplier has no fixed establishment in Samoa from which the supply is made. Data-center colocation, bare-metal server hosting, and rack-leasing services sold to offshore tenants qualify as zero-rated exported services when invoiced in foreign currency, supported by an export-service declaration, and substantiated by a service contract showing offshore consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs export entry) evidencing the physical movement or re-export of data-center equipment and associated hardware
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export; for pure offshore services, a fully executed colocation service contract plus a service-delivery and usage report issued to the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorized commercial bank in Samoa confirming receipt and repatriation of the foreign-currency consideration

Not applicable: Samoa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice API integration, digital stamping, or government clearance portals. Suppliers must issue compliant tax invoices, retain underlying source documents for seven years, and file GST returns electronically via the MOR Matai online portal.

The Ministry of Revenue audits export zero-rating claims under the Tax Administration Act 2015. Shortfall penalties apply as follows: 20% for failure to take reasonable care, 40% for gross carelessness, and 150% for evasion with fraudulent intent; unpaid GST is immediately clawed back together with interest at 1% per month. Criminal conviction for GST fraud carries a fine of up to WST 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. In addition, MOR may reassess transfer pricing and adjust below-market colocation lease charges between related parties, triggering additional GST, penalties, and interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is partially convertible: current-account payments (services, interest, operating expenses) are processed by authorized dealers without prior CBS approval, whereas capital-account flows (equity injection, shareholder loans, dividend and profit remittance) require CBS authorization or an authorized-dealer declaration with supporting tax clearance and underlying contract documentation. A Samoa International Company (IC) is treated as non-resident for exchange-control and tax purposes; its data center colocation leasing income from non-resident tenants is offshore business income that may be retained in USD without mandatory conversion. Practical legal constraint: an IC is generally prohibited from carrying on business with Samoan residents under section 8 of the International Companies Act 1987, therefore the colocation assets must be physically located outside Samoa and all lease counterparties must be non-resident. Profit remittance to the foreign shareholder is permissible once the authorized-dealer filing is supported by audited financial statements; the restriction on resident business prevents the IC from using a Samoan domestic WST account as its primary revenue account for onshore leasing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1980, a resident exporter of goods or services must repatriate foreign currency proceeds to Samoa within 3 months from the date of receipt or shipment and must surrender 100% of such proceeds to an authorized dealer in exchange for WST unless the CBS has approved a Foreign Currency Retention Account (FCRA); retention accounts are subject to CBS policy caps and quarterly reconciliation. For the non-resident IC, the statutory surrender obligation does not attach to offshore receipts; however, if any export-proceeds credit is remitted onshore to a Samoan bank, the collecting authorized dealer will apply the 3-month surrender framework by default and convert surplus foreign currency into WST absent a prior CBS retention approval.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), acting as Exchange Control Administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1975 (as amended), the Exchange Control Regulations 1980, and supervisor of authorized dealers under the Financial Institutions Act 1996; CBS also operates the national clearing and settlement system and the Samoa Automated Transfer System for domestic WST clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility. Samoa does not recognize offshore Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licenses, and the Financial Institutions Act 1996 requires any money transmission or deposit-taking within or into Samoa to be conducted by a licensed bank or licensed money remitter; international EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Airwallex, Wise) cannot clear WST and cannot file CBS exchange-control reports. 25% standard corporate income tax rate for domestic companies; Samoa International Companies (ICs) are fully exempt on all income derived from outside Samoa.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 5,000,000 aggregate annual related-party cross-border dealings. Samoa has no codified Local File/Master File regime, but the Income Tax Act 2014 and Tax Administration Act 2014 impose contemporaneous arm's-length documentation and a 7-year record retention duty on all related-party international dealings.

Economic substance scope: OECD/EU-aligned Economic Substance Rules apply to Samoa International Companies. An IC deriving data-center colocation income must perform core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Samoa: physical Tier-3/4 server premises, locally engaged technical staff, local payroll and rental expenditure, and board meetings held in Samoa. An annual economic substance declaration must be filed with the Registrar of International Companies; failure to substantiate presence results in loss of the IC tax-exempt status and re-assessment at 25%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Misreporting Domestic Income as Exempt Offshore Income

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2014: shortfall penalty of 200% of the tax underpaid; evasion conviction carries a fine not exceeding WST 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years; default assessments are immediately enforceable.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors are jointly liable for unpaid tax and penalties; civil recovery orders, travel bans (warrant for non-removal), and imprisonment up to 7 years may be imposed.
Shell Company / Sham Structure - Zero Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: International Companies Act 2008: deregistration by the Registrar, WST 100 per day late fee on annual license arrears, and forfeiture of the IC exemption; strike-off can occur without court order after 3 months' continuous default.

Director personal liability: The Registrar may apply to the Court for a 5- to 7-year director disqualification; travel bans and asset confiscation apply; shareholders face clawback of upstreamed dividends.
AML Violation - Laundering Colocation Rental Flows

Statutory sanction: Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007: fine up to WST 1,000,000 for a body corporate; imprisonment up to 14 years for individuals; servers and rental proceeds are subject to confiscation and freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal AML prosecution, exclusion orders, travel bans, mutual legal assistance extradition (including to Australia and New Zealand), and civil liability for restitution of laundered proceeds.
Prohibited Domestic Business - Unlawful Samoa-Source Trading

Statutory sanction: International Companies Act 2008 and Income Tax Act 2014: automatic forfeiture of the IC exemption; re-assessment of 25% tax on all previously exempt profits within the 7-year limitation period; penalty of 50% of the omitted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for omitted corporate tax and withheld amounts; court-ordered civil restitution from personal assets; immigration stop-lists prevent departure before full settlement.
