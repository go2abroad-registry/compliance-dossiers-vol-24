Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (Samoa); official online portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a Samoa Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by filing an application with the Ministry for Revenue, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, constitution, registered office certificate, director and shareholder identification documents, and evidence of a registered local agent. For an International Company, registration is facilitated through the Samoa International Finance Authority (IFA), and the TIN is issued promptly upon completion of the streamlined formation procedure.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign entity must obtain a Samoa Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by filing an application with the Ministry for Revenue, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, constitution, registered office certificate, director and shareholder identification documents, and evidence of a registered local agent. For an International Company, registration is facilitated through the Samoa International Finance Authority (IFA), and the TIN is issued promptly upon completion of the streamlined formation procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

International Companies are, by default, tax-exempt under the International Companies Act 1987. Such an entity may elect to be taxed as an ordinary resident company under the Income Tax Ordinance 2012 by applying to the Ministry for Revenue; no check-the-box pass-through election is available. Hybrid mismatch elections are not expressly available under Samoa domestic law.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services from Samoa are zero-rated where the supplier is a registered person, the supply is made to a person who is outside Samoa, the title or ownership passes outside Samoa, and the goods or services are not consumed in Samoa. Royalty-free stock music licensing supplied to non-resident customers via digital platforms qualifies as offshore services if the recipient is outside Samoa, the payment is received in foreign currency, and the digital product is not used locally.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World printout or electronic summary
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Contract or license agreement evidencing the supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement crediting the export proceeds in a Samoa bank account
  • Tax Invoice issued under VAGST rules

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or government API clearance regime is currently in force for VAGST. Registered persons must issue proper VAGST invoices and retain supporting documentation. For customs purposes, ASYCUDA World electronic declarations are mandatory for imports and exports.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective assessment of VAGST at 15%, interest at the statutory rate, and late payment penalties of not less than 5% per month. The VAGST Act 2008 provides fines of up to WST 10,000 for false invoicing or refund claims. Under the Customs Act 2014, fraudulent export declarations may attract penalties of up to five times the evaded duty and imprisonment terms. The Ministry for Revenue may claw back any refunds erroneously paid, with penalties and dishonor fees.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a managed exchange-control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1976. The Samoan Tala (WST) is not freely convertible offshore. Current-account transactions, including royalty remittances, subscription fees, and operating disbursements, are processed by Authorized Dealers (licensed commercial banks) upon submission of the relevant Exchange Control Form (EC-3 / EC-4A) with supporting invoices and contracts, generally without quantitative caps. Capital-account transactions, such as share capital injections, shareholder loans, and liquidation or share-sale repatriations, require prior approval from the Central Bank of Samoa on a case-by-case basis. Dividend and profit remittance is permissible following submission of audited financial statements and confirmation of tax clearance with the Ministry of Revenue, subject to applicable withholding tax. Practical hurdles include modest offshore WST liquidity, a thin correspondent network, and extended Central Bank review timelines for non-standard capital transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export and cross-border service revenue earned by a Samoan International Company must be repatriated to Samoa through an Authorized Dealer within 30 days from the date of receipt or within 30 days from the date such proceeds become contractually due, whichever is earlier. The mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of repatriated foreign-currency receipts, which must be offered for conversion into WST on the interbank market unless the Central Bank of Samoa grants a foreign-currency retention account exemption. For royalty-free stock music licensing settlements received via Stripe and PayPal in USD, the treasury must route the funds into a licensed Samoan bank account within the statutory window; retaining export proceeds in offshore PSP wallets beyond the grace period constitutes a breach of the surrender obligation. Authorized Dealers verify the filing of EC-3 declarations for each receipt, and non-compliance may trigger monetary penalties, foreign-exchange disallowances, and suspension of banking privileges.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa) — Exchange Control Division

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: LOW — NOT VIABLE AS A SOLE CLEARING RAIL. Samoa has no statutory electronic money institution (EMI) licensing framework; non-bank digital money operators are not supervised by the Central Bank of Samoa and cannot access domestic clearing, exchange-control approvals, or WST settlement infrastructure. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise, Payoneer, Currenxie) cannot hold a Samoan domestic account, cannot fulfill the export-proceeds surrender obligation, and are not recognized as Authorized Dealers for EC-3 declaration purposes. Permissible use is limited to upstream collection conduits for marketplace payouts (Stripe, PayPal) in USD, subject to a strict treasury policy that sweeps all balances into the licensed Samoan bank account within the 30-day surrender timeline. The Samoan International Company may employ an EMI/PSP account solely as a payment-rail convenience; the licensed Samoan bank must remain the primary clearing counterparty and the sole venue for all FX conversion and outward remittances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial capital injection (issued share capital or shareholder loan) is routed via SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 wire from the shareholder's banking relationship to the Samoan International Company's account at an Authorized Dealer in Samoa. The incoming remittance is tagged under Exchange Control Form EC-3 (Category: Direct Investment / Capital Injection) supported by a board resolution, certificate of incorporation, register of shareholders, passport copies of the remitting shareholder, and bank statement evidencing source of funds. USD or AUD is the recommended routing currency to minimize conversion friction; WST settlement occurs automatically upon credit. Small equity injections of USD 1,000 to 50,000 are typically processed by the commercial bank under delegated authority, while larger injections, redeemable instruments, or those involving third-party funding require prior Central Bank of Samoa approval. Post-credit verification includes reconciliation of the remitter's name against the registered shareholder, confirmation of the exchange-control declaration, and an AML release from the bank's compliance unit before the funds may be utilized for operational purposes.Outbound royalty and licensing disbursements to global composers, artists, and licensors are executed through SWIFT MT103 (single-beneficiary instruction) or MT102 (batch payment file) using the clearing relationship of a Samoan Authorized Dealer such as BSP Samoa or ANZ Samoa. Every outbound payment requires Exchange Control Form EC-4A (Outward Remittance), the underlying music license agreement, and the corresponding invoice; the Samoan bank must appear as the ordering institution for all correspondent-bank transactions. Funds collected through Stripe/PayPal must first be swept into the Samoan operational account to maintain a clean clearing chain before any outward disbursements. Commercial Letters of Credit are inapplicable to digital music licensing because the transaction involves no physical trade in goods; a standby letter of credit may be used only for escrow-based arrangements with record-label counterparties. Aggregated weekly batching of residual royalty streaming is the recommended efficient rail, with USD-denominated disbursements retained where the counterparty is foreign and WST disbursements converted through the domestic interbank market.Onshore WST/USD spot and short-dated outright forwards offered by licensed Authorized Dealers (tenors generally up to 12 months) for domestic operational exposureOffshore WST/USD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) booked through Singapore or Hong Kong dealing desks, supported by the Samoan bank's correspondent relationship on a back-to-back covered basisCross-currency basis swaps executed through the international branch networks of BSP Samoa or ANZ Samoa for larger notional exposures, subject to Central Bank notification and documentary supportNatural hedging through approved dual-currency (USD/WST) retention accounts combined with treasury netting of royalty payables against subscription and licensing receivables to minimize realized FX conversion costs

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (exempt International Company status under the International Companies Act 1987, s. 192; ordinary Samoa resident corporate headlinerate is 27%, but an IC is outside the Samoa Income Tax Act for non-Samoa-source income)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions paid by an exempt International Company to non-resident shareholders; 15% statutory dividend withholding tax for dividends paid by an ordinary Samoa resident company to non-residents.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing is due within three months after the company's annual return date, which is the anniversary of incorporation. A company with a 31 December balance date must file no later than 31 March of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For ordinary companies, the corporate income tax return is due no later than six months after balance date. If the financial year ends 31 March, the filing deadline is 30 September.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A for an exempt IC; if the entity becomes a taxable resident operator, the Samoa TP documentation order applies where aggregate related-party transactions exceed WST 5,000,000 in an income year, or where intercompany royalties and licence fees exceed WST 1,000,000 in an income year.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has adopted Economic Substance Regulations applicable to International Companies carrying on license-holding or IP-rich business. A royalty-free stock music catalogue licensing operation must demonstrate real decision-making and core income-generating activity in Samoa: physical licensed office, locally engaged qualified staff, board meetings in Samoa, maintenance of accounting records in Samoa, and local expenditure commensurate with gross revenue. Failure to satisfy ESR leads to loss of the tax exemption, exchange of information reporting to the foreign partner, a fine up to SAT 100,000, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 1975 and Tax Administration Act 2013 assessment powers impose a 200% tax shortfall penalty plus 2% monthly interest; criminal conviction carries a fine up to SAT 100,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; the IC is struck off and its bank accounts may be frozen by court order.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors can be held jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax under the veil-piercing provisions where the company is merely a facade; travel restraints and name publication are available under Samoa's revenue recovery procedures.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations impose a fine up to SAT 100,000 for false declarations; the Registrar may strike off the IC and the foreign revenue authority receives automatic exchange-of-information notification; the catalogue and receivables may be subject to civil forfeiture as assets of a sham entity.

Director personal liability: Courts will lift the corporate veil to make directors personally liable for unpaid licence fees and third-party creditor claims; disqualification from managing any Samoa company for up to 10 years follows a finding of sham conduct.
AML / Beneficial Ownership and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 provides fines up to SAT 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years; all proceeds linked to the subscription revenue and Royalty asset accounts are subject to civil forfeiture; foreign sanctions violations trigger liquidation of the IC and denial of cross-border banking services.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are personally prosecutable without reliance on the corporate entity; personal travel bans are issuable by the Samoan High Court; international arrest warrants may be executed through mutual assistance between Samoa and the market countries.
Transfer Pricing Misalignment / Royalty Sweep

Statutory sanction: The Samoa Revenue Commissioner may disregard the arrangement and re-characterise the transactions at arm's length, assessing additional tax at 27% on the uplifted amount plus a 50% documentation penalty and 2% monthly interest; the IC is reclassified as a resident taxable company if control and management are found in Samoa.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the underpriced IP license are personally liable for the tax adjustment up to SAT 250,000; the company loses the exemption and the directors become subject to disqualification proceedings; extradition is possible where the affected foreign state deems the royalty sweep as substantive tax fraud.
