Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — not applicable to this entity form.

Available election forms

Section 141 of the International Companies Act 1987 permits an IC to file a formal election with the Registrar and the Ministry for Revenue to be treated either as (i) an ordinary resident domestic company (subject to full Samoan taxation on worldwide income) or (ii) a non-resident/foreign company (tax-exempt on offshore profits). Absent such election, an IC is deemed to be a non-resident company. This election is the functional equivalent of a 'check-the-box' mechanism under Samoan law and does not trigger hybrid mismatch or CFC attribution. Samoa's Income Tax Act 2015 contains no separate hybrid mismatch elections, no REIT/trust pass-through elections relevant to ICs, and no branch-registered CFC elections. The election must be documented by board resolution and filed with both the Registrar and MfR; revocation is permitted upon proof of changed management and control.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa holds IMF Article VIII status: current-account convertibility is free, so software license fees, royalties, subcontractor payments and registered-agent fees are payable offshore without CBS approval, subject only to bank-level documentary compliance (invoice, contract, purpose code). Capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, capital reorganization) require CBS exchange-control approval or authorized-dealer no-objection if any WST conversion or a resident Samoan bank account is involved; USD-denominated injections into the International Company's external (non-resident) account bypass that perimeter because the IC is non-resident under the Exchange Control framework. Profit-remittance hurdles for resident Samoan companies are dividend withholding tax at the statutory rate and income-tax clearance before remittance; the IC is exempt from Samoan income tax and dividend withholding, so profit remittances are executed by board resolution from the external account with no Samoan tax leakage. The WST is on a CBS-managed currency-basket peg; IC treasury operations should be USD functional and WST conversion limited to local agent fees and government levies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1970, Exchange Control Regulations 1971 and CBS Exchange Control Guideline No. 3 (Exports), export proceeds must be repatriated within 6 months of shipment (for services, within the contractually agreed settlement term) and offered to an authorized dealer within 30 days of receipt; 100% of receipts are to be surrendered into WST or, with CBS approval, held in a foreign-currency retention account. The International Company is a non-resident taxpayer and exchange-control person; receipts credited to its external USD account with an authorized dealer are not subject to WST surrender or any conversion ratio, and no exchange-control fee applies. If proceeds are accidentally routed into a resident WST account, the full surrender rule applies and a CBS retention approval is required to convert back into USD for offshore disbursement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Falefa, Apia, Samoa. CBS is the sole monetary authority, banking supervisor, exchange-control administrator and national payments-system overseer under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, the Exchange Control Act 1970, the Exchange Control Regulations 1971 and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2023. The Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit is the national FIU, and the Registrar of International and Foreign Companies within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour administers the International Companies Act 1987 registry.
