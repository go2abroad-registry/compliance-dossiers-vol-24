Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Apia; official online portal: www.revenue.gov.ws (MfR Online Filing / Taxpayer Portal). Customs functions are administered by the Customs Division of the same Ministry through the ASYCUDA World electronic single window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity (including a Samoa International Company) must lodge the MfR Application for Tax Identification Number (TIN) form at the Ministry for Revenue, accompanied by certified copies of the Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of International Companies, the company constitution, the registered office and registered agent appointment in Samoa, the directors' and ultimate beneficial owners' particulars, and (where applicable) the International Company Tax Exemption Certificate. The MfR issues the TIN within 3–5 business days after verification. For tax-exempt International Companies, the TIN is used principally for customs clearance, banking and SWIFT/escrow account administration; the entity is enrolled in a non-filing tax-exempt status unless it carries on domestic-source business in Samoa.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-incorporated entity (including a Samoa International Company) must lodge the MfR Application for Tax Identification Number (TIN) form at the Ministry for Revenue, accompanied by certified copies of the Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of International Companies, the company constitution, the registered office and registered agent appointment in Samoa, the directors' and ultimate beneficial owners' particulars, and (where applicable) the International Company Tax Exemption Certificate. The MfR issues the TIN within 3–5 business days after verification. For tax-exempt International Companies, the TIN is used principally for customs clearance, banking and SWIFT/escrow account administration; the entity is enrolled in a non-filing tax-exempt status unless it carries on domestic-source business in Samoa.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

A Samoa International Company is prima facie exempt from income tax, GST and stamp duty on offshore income, and may elect to obtain a formal Tax Exemption Certificate from the Ministry for Revenue under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended), renewable by payment of the annual government fee. Samoa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; entity tax classification follows statutory incorporation, central management and control, and permanent establishment concepts in the Income Tax Act 2015. Where cross-border hybrid mismatch arrangements are involved, Samoa applies the hybrid mismatch and CFC alignment rules introduced under the Tax Administration Act 2015 and subsequent BEPS (Action 2) amendments, and no elective deviation from those statutory classifications is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 20,000 (approx. USD 7,400) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; compulsory registration applies once that threshold is exceeded, with voluntary registration permitted below the threshold. Financial services, including accounts-receivable factoring, discounting and reverse-factoring credit facilities, are exempt supplies under the GST Act, so a pure trade-finance factoring entity does not charge GST on its fee/interest income and cannot claim input credits on costs attributable to exempt supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods zero-rated if they are physically exported from Samoa to a destination outside Samoa within the period prescribed by the Customs Act and GST Act, and the supplier retains evidence of export. Services, including cross-border factoring and other offshore services, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident person who is outside Samoa, the services are not supplied for use in Samoa, and the non-resident is not in Samoa at the time of supply. For accounts-receivable factoring, the service must be performed outside Samoa and rendered solely to the non-resident factor or seller of receivables; domestic collections or any connection to land or goods located in Samoa will re-characterize the supply as taxable or exempt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World acceptance and release certificate) evidencing physical export from Samoa
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to the non-resident buyer or consignee outside Samoa
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and underlying sale/factoring agreement showing the non-resident counterparty and foreign-currency consideration
  • Bank SWIFT MT103, inbound remittance voucher, or bank statement evidencing actual settlement in foreign exchange

No real-time government-approved e-invoicing, digital stamping or mandatory third-party fiscal clearance/API validation regime is in force in Samoa for domestic B2B or B2C invoices. GST-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices in the prescribed form and file consolidated GST returns electronically through the Ministry for Revenue e-Filing portal within the statutory filing period. For import and export transactions, Customs declarations are lodged and cleared electronically through ASYCUDA World, which operates as the fiscal clearance gateway and generates the Customs Export Declaration receipt relied upon for zero-rating and duty relief purposes.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to documentary verification by the Samoa Ministry for Revenue and Customs Division. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims attract recovery of the full GST not accounted for, plus a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax avoided (or 200% for knowingly fraudulent conduct), late-payment interest accruing daily, and possible criminal prosecution for tax evasion (fines and imprisonment for directors and responsible officers). The assessment period is never closed where fraud, wilful default or misrepresentation is proved, and the MfR may issue retrospective clawback assessments, cancel the exporter's registration, and impose a director/agent personal liability under the Tax Administration Act 2015 and the Customs Act 2014.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is not freely convertible offshore. Samoa maintains a centralised exchange-control framework administered by the CBS under the Exchange Control legislation. Current-account payments for bona fide trade and services are generally permitted through licensed banks; profit remittances require documentary substantiation of the underlying transaction, source of funds, and tax-exempt status. Capital-account transfers are subject to case-by-case CBS approval. International Companies (ICs) under the International Companies Act 1987 are treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes, and foreign-currency factoring receivables collected through approved external accounts are not subject to WST conversion. The principal practical remittance hurdles are: full identify-and-verify documentation of the ultimate beneficial owner; invoice assignment notices; debtor acknowledgment; and a certificate of tax exemption or no-objection from Samoa's tax authority.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to International Companies. Onshore exporters are expected to repatriate export proceeds within commercially reasonable periods and may retain foreign currency in CBS-approved foreign currency accounts; the CBS does not impose a fixed surrender timeline for offshore ICs. For a cross-border factoring IC, inward collections on purchased invoices are treated as trade-receivable settlements, not Samoan export proceeds, and may be held in external WST-free accounts without conversion. Any conversion from WST to foreign currency is executed at the prevailing CBS indicative rate through a licensed commercial bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Samoa has no dedicated electronic-money-institution or EMI licensing category; a digital bank or fintech EMI cannot independently obtain a banking licence from the CBS. Offshore EMIs may act only as technology introducers, software vendors, or payment facilitators to a licensed commercial bank, and cannot access local clearing rails or hold client funds directly. Legal constraints require all Samoan-dollar settlement and custody to pass through a bank licensed by the Central Bank of Samoa. Correspondent access is available through ANZ and BSP networks, but onboarding an unlicensed EMI to those rails is commercially impracticable without a local bank sponsor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan capital is routed via SWIFT MT103, fed through a licensed Samoa commercial bank as receiving bank. The transfer must include transparent purpose codes for foreign direct investment or intercompany financing, the ultimate beneficial owner, and the IC's legal entity identifier. The receiving bank will verify the transaction against the board resolution, capital contribution agreement, and the account name. Factoring-purchase funding from overseas funding facilities should be routed as trade-finance disbursements using a letter of credit, irrevocable credit facility, or inter-company loan agreement; the CBS exchange-control form and tax-exemption certificate are required for any onshore WST conversion.Outbound settlements from the Samoa IC's operational account are cleared using SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments, MT202 for interbank cover payments, and commercial letters of credit through ANZ's or BSP's trade finance desks. Payments to sellers under reverse-factoring arrangements may be structured as supplier advances using MT103 with the invoice-purchase agreement cited as the underlying trade contract. For high-value disbursements, Samoa banks generally require same-day settlement covering in correspondent U.S. dollars or Australian dollars, with the WST leg cleared through the CBS real-time gross settlement system. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard Samoan corporate income tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2015, effective for tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2017)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory non-resident dividend withholding tax is the headline rate under the Income Tax Act 2015 for distributions by a resident Samoan company; however, a tax-exempt International Company distributing profits derived from foreign-source factoring income to non-resident shareholders is not subject to any Samoan dividend withholding tax, and the 15% rate will not be applied to such distributions provided the IC maintains its exemption and files the ESR return.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six calendar months after the anniversary of incorporation, or within six months of the IC's elected accounting reference date if different from the anniversary; licence fee payment accompanies the Annual Return and is due on the same date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 12 months after the end of the IC's accounting period, and in any event no later than 12 months from the balance sheet date; a prudent filing date of 6 months after the year end is recommended to avoid regulator queries and to align the ESR with the Annual Return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa follows the OECD/G20 BEPS minimum standards for transfer pricing documentation. Although the Income Tax Act 2015 contains the statutory arm's-length rule, the coercive documentation threshold aligning with international practice is consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more (approximately WST 2.3 billion), which triggers Country-by-Country Reporting and Master File requirements; a practical Local File threshold of annual related-party cross-border factoring turnover of WST 2,000,000 is applied by the Samoa Revenue Authority in transfer pricing audits, requiring contemporaneous documentation of the discount rate, funding margin, and risk allocation between the Samoan IC and its foreign parent, lenders, or factoring counterparties.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply mandatorily to every Samoan International Company. Factoring and accounts receivable trade finance are characterised as a 'Financing and Leasing' relevant activity. To preserve the zero-rate tax exemption, the IC must demonstrate in Samoa: (i) board management and strategic control of the factoring business; (ii) proportionate physical presence by way of offices and plant; (iii) adequate full-time qualified employees in Samoa; (iv) the performance of core income-generating activities in Samoa, including underwriting debtor risk, approving invoice discounting limits, setting margins, and managing collections; and (v) an annual ESR return substantiating these elements. A purely passive or offshore-administered factor holding receivables without a real Samoan economic presence will fail the ESR test and be reassessed as a taxable shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction for tax evasion and fraud punishable by a fine of up to WST 1,000,000 and confiscation of the tax advantage; the Registrar may strike the IC off and treat the company as never validly registered; penalty equal to 200% of the unpaid annual licence fees and reassessment of up to 25% corporate income tax on all Samoan-source income; publication of the company on a public non-compliant list.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of divested profits and for any tax or penalties assessed after veil-piercing; the Court may issue travel bans and asset freezing orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act; directors face disqualification from managing any company in Samoa for up to 10 years and imprisonment of up to 7 years upon conviction.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fixed ESR penalty of WST 5,000 plus a daily penalty of WST 500 for continued non-compliance; automatic revocation of the tax-exempt certificate converting the IC to a taxable resident company subject to 25% tax on Samoan-source income; forced deregistration without discharge of liabilities; public listing of the company as a non-compliant International Company.

Director personal liability: Officers may be held personally liable for the tax assessed against the company after status revocation; directors' own assets may be attached to satisfy ESR penalties; a director seeking to leave Samoa during an investigation may be placed on an exit control order; responsible directors face criminal fines up to WST 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Unlicensed Financial Activity and AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to WST 500,000 per unlicensed transaction, forfeiture of all profits derived from the unlawful factoring activity, revocation of any ancillary banking or money-services licence; AML penalties under the Financial Intelligence Unit Act include monetary penalties up to WST 1,000,000 for systemic due-diligence failures and confiscation of laundered proceeds; the factoring entity can be wound up by court order.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and beneficial owners face individual criminal liability for operating an unlicensed financial business and for money-laundering predicates, with civil restitution claims brought by aggrieved parties and by the State; court-imposed travel restrictions and seizure of personal bank accounts are available if defendants are flight risks; conviction may result in imprisonment of up to 14 years.
Beneficial Ownership Register and Nominee Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Registrar penalty of WST 1,000 per default plus WST 100 per day of continuing violation; forced transparency order requiring the IC to furnish the identity of all shadow owners; involuntary strike-off of the company and refusal of the Registrar to accept any subsequent annual return; forfeiture of nominee-held receivables as the proceeds of unlawful concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors, the registered agent, and the undisclosed principal are jointly and severally liable for penalties imposed under the International Companies Act and the Financial Intelligence Unit Act; the court may pierce the corporate veil making the true beneficial owner personally liable for the factoring debts and for taxes; travel bans pending investigation and imprisonment of up to 3 years apply for deliberate concealment.
