Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at https://www.sars.gov.za / https://ecs.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African Proprietary Limited (Pty) Ltd must first incorporate through the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), obtain the CIPC registration number, then register with SARS via eFiling or at a SARS branch for an Income Tax reference number (Company Income Tax number) and, if applicable, a VAT Vendor number. The eFiling account must be activated using the company's registration details and the appointed SARS-recognised representative (public officer). Non-resident directors and shareholders are permitted, and no minimum capital is required, but all foreign shareholder payments must be declared on the annual Financial Reporting and SARS returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a resident Pty Ltd is taxed as a separate taxpayer on its worldwide taxable income with foreign tax credits relief; its resident status derives from incorporation in South Africa; non-residents and shareholders are subject to South African source taxation only, with dividends subject to Dividends Tax.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election is available. A qualifying small business may elect the Small Business Corporation (SBC) regime under section 12E of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, subject to shareholding/turnover thresholds. A qualifying micro business (turnover ≤ ZAR 1,000,000) may elect turnover tax under the Sixth Schedule. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 23M-23P of the Income Tax Act apply, denying deductions or imposing recharacterisation where structured arrangements lack economic substance and give rise to mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory VAT registration threshold based on total taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period); voluntary registration permitted from ZAR 50,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act provided the goods are exported from the Republic of South Africa under any customs agreement within the prescribed period and are accompanied by prescribed documentary proof. Export services supplied to non-residents qualify for zero-rating under section 11(2)(l) if the services are physically rendered wholly or partly outside South Africa, or if they are supplied to non-residents who are not present in South Africa at the time of supply and do not use the services in South Africa, subject to specific conditions and exceptions for services tainted by South African consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of Export (carrier's certificate or shipping order)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (demonstrating payment for the exported goods)

SARS mandates B2B electronic invoicing for VAT vendors using compliant XML formats, implemented in phases with mandatory compliance from 1 August 2025 for qualifying vendors. Tax invoices must contain specific SARS prescribed minimum content. There is no real-time transaction clearance or fiscal stamping; VAT returns (VAT201) are submitted electronically through eFiling with API integration options. SARS does not issue pre-clearance digital stamps, but requires full records of all electronic tax invoices and credit/debit notes to be retained.

SARS may conduct compliance audits and raise an additional VAT assessment plus interest where the documentary proof is incomplete or fraudulent. A false claim to zero-rating triggers an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall under section 223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, plus possible criminal prosecution for tax evasion and fraud, with imprisonment up to 10 years or a fine. Where an exporter fails to remit the foreign-currency proceeds within the required 180-day period, the VAT relief may be reversed and the original 15% output VAT becomes payable together with penalties on the outstanding amount.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR, ISO 4217 code ZAR) is a partially convertible currency. Current account transactions are liberalised and may be settled freely through an Authorised Dealer with valid supporting documentation. Capital account transactions remain subject to exchange control approval or retention permits, although the SARB has progressively relaxed selected controls. Non-resident shareholders may freely receive dividends on approved ordinary shares, provided that the investment was originally declared and funded through authorised channels and the distribution is covered by audited or reviewed financial statements and solvency and liquidity requirements under the Companies Act 71 of 2008. Profit remittance is permissible after applicable dividends tax (currently 20%) has been withheld and remitted to the South African Revenue Service (SARS). There is no direct restriction on conversion for current-account trade payments, but banks must independently verify the economic substance of each transaction to satisfy anti-money laundering and Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must obtain an exchange-control approval from an Authorised Dealer, receive the full export proceeds within 180 days from the date of physical shipment or the date of service completion, and surrender the foreign currency to an Authorised Dealer for conversion into rand. Foreign-currency proceeds must be disposed of immediately on receipt unless a specific retention account facility has been granted by SARB/FinSurv. Retention of export proceeds in foreign currency is allowed subject to a valid foreign-currency account approval; however, the underlying export transaction must be closed out and the proceeds fully accounted for in the prescribed form. Any overdue receipts, short shipments, or write-offs must be reported to the Authorised Dealer with a full supporting audit trail, and SARB/FinSurv may require the exporter to provide documentary proof of shipment, payment, and conversion.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which administers exchange controls under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933, together with the National Treasury as policy authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) and digital payment operators have a Medium viability rating for cross-border settlement, but they cannot act as a substitute for a South African Authorised Dealer. A foreign EMI without a South African banking licence cannot hold ZAR client funds in a local clearing account nor process ZAR-denominated EFTs through the South African national payment system (NPS). Such EMIs may only be used as a limited ancillary treasury gateway, provided they are not the sole repository of corporate cash and all conversions are ultimately executed through an onshore Authorised Dealer with SARB reporting capability. Legal constraints include FICA, the Banks Act 94 of 1990, the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998, and exchange control restrictions preventing the initial acquisition of an offshore EMI account without SARB/FinSurv approval if the balance is funded by South African resident controlled funds. In practice, the recommended structure is to use a Tier-1 South African bank as the primary treasury counterparty and a regulated international bank or EMoney institution only for non-ZAR current-account corridor liquidity where no South African Withholding or Dividends Tax leakage arises.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Foreign ownership with no demonstrable local economic substance or operating premises

Defense evidence: CIPC registration certificate and Memorandum of Incorporation, articles of association, certificate of beneficial ownership from an independent corporate service provider, signed and notarised board resolutions authorising banking, a full shareholder and director register, a South African physical business address lease agreement or serviced-office agreement, a detailed business plan describing the solid-state transformer manufacturing operations, and proof of intended local employment or customer contracts with South African offtakers.
Complex cross-shareholding or opaque beneficial ownership chain

Root cause: Complex cross-shareholding or opaque beneficial ownership chain

Defense evidence: Organisational structuring diagram showing every intermediate company to ultimate individual shareholder(s), certified passports of ultimate beneficial owners, tax returns or tax residence certificates of shareholders, independent legal opinion confirming source of funds, a full sanctions and politically exposed persons (PEP) screening report, and a written explanation of the commercial purpose of each layer in the ownership chain.
Inbound capital injection from a jurisdiction with perceived higher money-laundering risk or a non-bank offshore entity

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from a jurisdiction with perceived higher money-laundering risk or a non-bank offshore entity

Defense evidence: Signed share subscription or shareholder loan agreement, banking evidence of the disposal of funds from the remitting entity, audited financial statements or certified balance sheet of the remitting entity, confirmation of the ultimate beneficial ownership, a complete source-of-wealth questionnaire, and a tax clearance certificate where available from the remitting jurisdiction, plus a declaration that the funds are not derived from proceeds of crime or tax evasion.
Large dividend or intercompany loan repayment to non-residents in the absence of formal exchange control approval

Root cause: Large dividend or intercompany loan repayment to non-residents in the absence of formal exchange control approval

Defense evidence: SARB/FinSurv approval of the original inward capital transfer, the company's exchange-control file maintained by the Authorised Dealer, audited annual financial statements confirming free reserves, board-approved distribution resolution, proof of dividends tax withheld and paid to SARS, and a tax clearance certificate from SARS for the company.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscription or shareholder loan must be effected through a South African Authorised Dealer using SWIFT MT103 or MT202. The remitting bank must clearly identify the funding purpose; the corporate should pre-declare the investment under the SARB's electronic reporting system on Form NDB (Issued by Authorised Dealer) or Form A2 for non-resident investments. Appropriate purpose codes include: 'K1' for direct investment, 'K2' for portfolio investment, 'K3' for loans and advances to residents by non-residents, or 'K4' for other inbound capital transfers. For share capital, the company must receive the funds into a South African bank account designated as a Capital Import Receipts account; the Authorised Dealer will issue a Foreign Investment Approval letter and an inward transfer of funds (ITF) acknowledging the transaction. For loan funding, a formal loan agreement, satisfying the National Credit Act exemption for cross-border corporate loans, must be lodged with the bank. All inbound receipts must be accompanied by a signed source-of-funds declaration, evidence of corporate authorisation from the foreign lender, and a copy of the board resolution approving the funding. The funds will then be credited to the operational account for the acquisition of fixed assets, working capital, or research and development expenditure, subject to LRA and environmental compliance in the manufacturing business.Trade settlements for equipment procurement and third-party component imports should follow a dual-rail structure: (i) standard SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for small-value spot payments, against invoices, packing lists, and transport documentation; and (ii) irrevocable, conforming Documentary Letters of Credit (DC) issued by the South African bank at the instruction of the buyer, usually on UCP600 terms, for high-value capital goods and transformers requiring intermediary financing. EFT-based disbursements must use the appropriate current-account purpose code designated by the Authorised Dealer (e.g., 'M' for merchant trade, 'T' for transport, or the specific trade-services code for manufactured goods). For exports, the company should receive the settlement through its Authorised Dealer account and, where the foreign customer is providing advance payment, obtain an import/export form declaration and ensure open-account receivables are settled within 180 days. All outbound foreign-currency payments for imports require verification that the underlying goods are not on the prohibited/controlled list; the bank will require the original invoice, confirmed purchase order, bill of lading/air waybill, and, where appropriate, a Pre-Shipment Inspection Certificate from the South African Bureau of Standards (SABS) for electrical transformer components. The client should maintain a complete audit trail for every trade disbursement in line with FICA and the Customs and Excise Act, 1964.Forward exchange cover (FX forwards) entered into with a South African Authorised Dealer on the basis of a firm commercial commitment and for a maximum term not exceeding 12 months, renewable on rollover where the underlying exposure remains.Foreign currency options, including call and put options, purchased from an Authorised Dealer for hedging trade receivables and payables, subject to premium payment and underlying transactional exposure.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) between ZAR and foreign currency for matching the timing of outbound import settlements and inbound export receipts, provided the swap is executed with a party permitted by SARB/FinSurv.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing a foreign currency basket or the USD/ZAR exchange rate may be used where physical delivery of ZAR is impractical, but only through an authorised dealer that has received the appropriate SARB approval for such instruments.Commodity-linked hedging instruments, including plain-vanilla options and forwards on copper or electrical-grade aluminium inputs, are permissible only under the dealer's general market-risk discretion and subject to FICA/KYC screening and clear evidence of the underlying physical input requirement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

ITR14 must be filed within 12 months of the financial year-end unless an extension is granted by SARS. Provisional tax payments (IRP6) are due two times during the year of assessment: six months after the beginning of the year of assessment and at the end of the year of assessment, with a final payment due six months after the year end. The final normal tax liability is paid based on the assessment following the ITR14 filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million (approximately USD 5.3 million) for annual turnover to determine the need for a full Local File. Master File required where the multinational group has total consolidated revenue above ZAR 1 billion (approximately USD 53 million) and cross-border connected-person transactions above ZAR 50 million (approximately USD 2.6 million). All cross-border transactions between connected persons must be documented in a Local File if the total value exceeds ZAR 10 million (approximately USD 530,000).

Economic substance scope: The South African Income Tax Act and the Tax Administration Act incorporate the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines via Section 31. Economic Substance is assessed through the 'enterprise or substantially all of the activities' rule for treaty entitlement. The General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in Sections 80A-80L perates to nullify transactions without commercial substance. In addition, the Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules under Section 9D require real economic substance testing for foreign subsidiaries of South African parents. As a manufacturing entity, the company must demonstrate operational substance through manufacturing plant, employees, and decision-making in South Africa to claim treaty benefits or incentive allowances.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011)

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of up to 150% of the shortfall for intentional non-disclosure; criminal fine not exceeding ZAR 30,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years per offence; interest of prescribed rate (10.5%) applied; civil tax debt may be collected by SARS through attachment and liquidation proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts where they engaged in reckless trading or knowingly participated in fraudulent tax practices (s 77 TAA). Minister may issue a certificate of criminal non-compliance and impose a travel ban. Criminal prosecution of directors can lead to imprisonment up to 10 years for serious fraud convictions. The corporate veil is pierced to hold directors personally liable for the full amount of unpaid tax plus penalties.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply GAAR and re-characterise the transaction with 75% penalty for impermissible avoidance; the company may be struck off under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 (s 22(5)); the South African Revenue Service may aggregate transactions and compute tax as if the arrangement had not been entered into; penalties up to 150% for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors of shell companies face personal liability for the company's debts if the company trades recklessly or was used as a vehicle to evade tax (s 22(5) Companies Act). Travel bans may be imposed under the Maintenance and Enforcement of Taxes Act. Directors may be prosecuted for fraud and be imprisoned up to 10 years. The courts will pierce the corporate veil to recover unpaid taxes and penalties from the directors personally.
AML/CFT (Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to ZAR 10 million per contravention (s 45E FIC Act); the Financial Intelligence Centre may issue a compliance order, prohibit business activities, or refer the matter for criminal prosecution; criminal fine up to ZAR 100 million (s 46 FIC Act) and/or imprisonment up to 30 years for seriously contravening a material obligation; assets may be frozen under the Prevention of Organised Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly or negligently breach FIC obligations are personally liable to the fine of up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment of up to 15 years (or up to 30 years for serious breach). Travel bans may be imposed during criminal investigation or to enforce a restitution order. The corporate veil is pierced where the director was the controlling mind behind the contravention; personal civil liability is imposed for the amount of laundered funds.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing (s 31 of the Income Tax Act)

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the taxable income to reflect the arm's length price; additional tax of 150% for intentional mispricing under s 223 TAA; a penalty of 10% of the adjustment may apply; interest on the tax shortfall; the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer to demonstrate that the pricing was arm's length.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or authorised a false transfer pricing document may be prosecuted for tax fraud under s 234 TAA and face personal criminal liability; if the company is liquidated due to undercapitalisation, the director may be personally responsible for the unpaid tax debts under s 77; exit control (travel ban) may be imposed for failure to pay outstanding assessments.
