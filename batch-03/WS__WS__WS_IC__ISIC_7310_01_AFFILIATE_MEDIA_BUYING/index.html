Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Revenue of Samoa, Taxpayer Services Division, official portal at https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) is exempt from income tax under the International Companies Act 1987 and is not required to file income tax returns. To obtain an optional Tax Identification Number (TIN) for banking and cross-border commercial compliance, file Form TIN-1 with the Ministry of Revenue, attaching: (i) certificate of incorporation; (ii) registered office address certificate; (iii) copy of passport of each director/shareholder; (iv) certificate of good standing from the Registrar of International Companies. The TIN is issued within 5 business days; no tax liability attaches to the TIN.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — An International Company (IC) is exempt from income tax under the International Companies Act 1987 and is not required to file income tax returns. To obtain an optional Tax Identification Number (TIN) for banking and cross-border commercial compliance, file Form TIN-1 with the Ministry of Revenue, attaching: (i) certificate of incorporation; (ii) registered office address certificate; (iii) copy of passport of each director/shareholder; (iv) certificate of good standing from the Registrar of International Companies. The TIN is issued within 5 business days; no tax liability attaches to the TIN.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 60,000 per annum of taxable supplies (approximately USD 21,000); however, an IC engaged exclusively in offshore media buying is outside the VAGST net as the recipient is non-resident and no local supply occurs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of services (including digital advertising and affiliate lead generation) are zero-rated under the VAGST Act 2002 when: (a) the supply is made to a non-resident of Samoa; (b) the services are performed entirely outside Samoa or the benefit is enjoyed outside Samoa; and (c) the IC holds documentary evidence of the offshore nature of the transaction. The IC must obtain and retain the full evidentiary bundle for 7 years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (form CED-EX01) — required for any physical goods; for pure services, a signed service agreement and invoice evidencing offshore supply
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill — required only when physical advertising collateral is shipped; otherwise a written confirmation from the media platform that the service is delivered outside Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed Samoan commercial bank — required to prove foreign currency collection within the 180-day period

No real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping mandate exists in Samoa. VAGST invoices may be issued in paper or PDF format; export invoices must include the exporter's TIN, the foreign buyer's details, description of services, zero-rating notation, and invoice number.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims are prosecuted under the VAGST Act 2002 and the Criminal Justice Act 2016. Penalties include: (i) retrospective assessment for 5 years; (ii) a civil penalty of 200% of the tax evaded; (iii) interest at the statutory rate from the due date; (iv) clawback of any refund paid, plus monetary penalties; and (v) for natural persons, imprisonment up to 5 years. The Ministry of Revenue may also cancel the IC's TIN and impose a disqualification order on directors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoa Tala is convertible for current-account transactions through authorized dealers without CBS pre-approval, while resident capital-account transactions require CBS consent. The WS International Company is statutorily non-resident for exchange-control purposes and is exempted from the Exchange Control Act 1975 in respect of all foreign-currency operations conducted outside Samoa. Accordingly, USD/EUR advertising-commission receivables, media-buying disbursements, intercompany funding, and profit remittances to offshore beneficial owners may be settled freely without CBS approval, without mandatory conversion into WST, and without any Samoan withholding or surrender obligation. Practical convertibility constraints arise only where the IC deliberately opens a WST-denominated account or settles local Tala vendor payables; such conversions are executed by the licensed bank at the CBS-managed interbank rate against standard source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident Samoan trading entities, foreign-currency export proceeds must be offered for sale to an authorized dealer within 30 days of receipt and surrendered into WST at the prevailing CBS reference rate unless a documented working-capital dispensation is granted. Because the WS_IC is a non-resident international company exempt from the Exchange Control Act, its inbound affiliate revenue streams are outside the statutory surrender regime and no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender timeline applies. Where the IC voluntarily converts foreign currency into WST, the authorized dealer records the conversion in its foreign-exchange register, but no compulsory conversion percentage is imposed on the offshore earnings.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Falefa, Apia, is the sole monetary authority and the administrator of the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1986. CBS licenses and supervises all commercial banks as foreign-exchange authorized dealers, administers the Samoa Tala (WST) managed-float exchange-rate policy, and acts as the AML/CFT supervisory authority for banks under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2018 and the CBS AML/CFT Rules 2020. The Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) co-regulates the Samoa International Company under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended through the International Companies (Amendment) Act 2021) for corporate registration, beneficial-ownership, annual-return, and economic-substance compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Direct onboarding of the Samoa IC to offshore digital EMIs is rated LOW. Mercury does not accept Samoa-domiciled international companies in its standard onboarding queue, Wise does not currently support Samoa as a business-account hold-and-send corridor, and Airwallex/Cross River-type platforms generally restrict Pacific offshore corporations. Legal constraints are not prohibitive because the IC's exchange-control exemption permits offshore account holding; however, reliance on an unregulated EMI as the primary treasury layer breaks the CBS-authorized-dealer chain and materially elevates correspondent and sanctions screening. The compliant architecture is to retain a CBS-licensed Samoan commercial bank as the legal funding source and, only where US ACH acceptance is required, interpose a US single-member LLC (check-the-box disregarded entity) owned by the IC, established with a US registered agent, EIN, IRS Form 5472 annual information return, transfer-pricing/intercompany funding memos, and no US market-facing activities to avoid creating a taxable US permanent establishment or effectively connected income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable for International Companies (no income tax liability); Samoa Revenue may nevertheless issue a specific documentary notice where connected-party transactions exceed WST 500,000 in a financial year and the exempt status is challenged. A taxable Samoan resident company preparing a Master File will require contemporaneous Local File documentation at moderate transaction levels and in all cases where the payer jurisdiction requires worldwide documentation.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to International Companies carrying on relevant activities. The ESR require the company to maintain a physical office in Samoa, qualified employees and adequate expenditure, core income-generating activities and management meetings in Samoa, and full accounting records in Samoa. For an ISIC 7310 affiliate/media-buying company, the material relevant activity is a distribution and service centre if it renders services to related parties, or IP holding if it owns and licenses advertising technology. A pure passive holding exemption does not apply if campaign management, vendor contracting, media buying, and affiliate payout decisions are actually made outside Samoa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unfiled Samoan-Source Advertising Income

Statutory sanction: Retroactive assessment at the 27% corporate income tax rate plus a fraud penalty of 100% of the shortfall and overdue interest; criminal liability for tax evasion exposes the company to a fine not exceeding WST 50,000 and imprisonment of officers for up to seven years; the Registrar will cancel IC status.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced because the IC is used as a facade for Samoan taxable business; directors and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax, penalties, and costs; court-imposed travel bans prevent departure from Samoa until civil restitution is made.
Shell Company / ESR Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Failure to satisfy an ESR substance notice results in an administrative penalty up to WST 100,000, disqualification from further business as an International Company, and mandatory cancellation from the International Companies Register; a separate fine of up to WST 10,000 is imposed for each false substance declaration filed with the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for ESR penalties if they knowingly certified false declarations; resignations or share transfers made within 12 months before the ESR review are disregarded; directors receive a travel restriction order and may be disqualified for ten years from managing any Samoa International Company.
Money Laundering / Fraudulent Affiliate Traffic Monetisation

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Samoa Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act 2007 carries a corporate fine not exceeding WST 500,000, confiscation of all proceeds linked to the fraudulent advertising scheme, and freezing orders on the IC bank accounts; failure to file a suspicious transaction report with the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit triggers an additional daily fine until compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors implicated in the scheme face personal criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to ten years, asset confiscation orders, and travel bans; banks will freeze the directors' personal bank accounts, and the court may order civil restitution to defrauded advertisers before any corporate liquidation is accepted.
