Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Pure virtual offices are not permissible. Every International Company must continuously maintain a physical registered office in Samoa at the premises of a SIFA-licensed registered agent, and must keep at that office the register of members, register of directors, and any corporate seal. Remote conduct of the ISIC 7120 factory-inspection business from abroad (e.g., China-based operations) is permissible, while the statutory seat and corporate records remain in Apia. No dedicated Samoan statutory licence is required for offshore ISIC 7120 activities; the mandatory endorsements are the SIFA incorporation certificate and the licensed registered agent. If the company later establishes a physical branch or employs staff in Samoa, a foreign investment certificate under the Foreign Investment Act 2000 and registration with the Samoa Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour become mandatory.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa, operating under the Tax Administration Act 2012 and the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2007; official e-services portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Submit the prescribed Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) application to the Commissioner of the Ministry for Revenue, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, the charter/memorandum and articles of association, registered office address in Samoa, names and addresses of all directors and shareholders, and certified passport copies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: An International Company incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax, dividend withholding tax, capital gains duty, stamp duty, and repatriation tax on all international business income and transactions for 20 years from the date of incorporation, as evidenced by a Tax Exemption Certificate obtainable from the Registrar. The exemption is confined to the IC's offshore business; any unauthorized domestic Samoan-source business would fall within the taxing net of the Income Tax Act 2012. Territorial scope is therefore limited to offshore operations, and the entity is not a tax resident subject to net-basis taxation for its exempt international activities.

Available election forms

Samoa does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime and has no domestic hybrid mismatch election. Available elections and mechanisms are: (a) application to the Registrar for a Tax Exemption Certificate under the International Companies Act 1987, evidencing the IC's automatic 20-year tax-exempt status; (b) optional voluntary registration for VAGST under the VAGST Act 2007 where the compulsory threshold is not exceeded; (c) conversion between International Company, Foreign Company, and Local Company status by registrar application, which prospectively changes the entity's tax classification; and (d) the IC may elect to obtain a ruling from the Ministry for Revenue on the treatment of a specific international service transaction, although no elective entity-classification or hybrid rules exist.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (Samoan Tala) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period. Supplies of inspection, testing, and quality-control services by an IC to non-resident principals for use outside Samoa are zero-rated or out of scope and therefore do not count toward the registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) goods physically exported from Samoa within the statutory timeframe and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident for use entirely outside Samoa, as provided under the VAGST Act 2007. For an on-site factory pre-shipment quality-control and inspection agency, the service must be rendered to an offshore principal, the principal must be a non-resident, the inspection/testing reports must not be used in Samoa, and the supply must not relate to land or goods sold in Samoa. The supplier must hold a signed contract with the overseas operator, detailed work orders, inspection and test certificates, and evidence of payment in foreign currency to justify the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration / electronic export entry lodged with the Samoa Customs and cleared for any goods or sample shipments leaving Samoa
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical removal of the goods or samples from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in Samoa
  • Commercial invoice and the signed service contract with the offshore principal
  • Inspection/test reports, conformity certificates, and audit working papers identifying the foreign importer and the inspected supplier

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping system is currently in force in Samoa. Taxpayers must retain and, on request, produce compliant tax invoices containing the supplier's name and TIN, sequential invoice number, invoice date, description of the supply, quantity, price, and the VAGST amount. Returns are filed electronically or in paper form through the Ministry for Revenue e-Services portal. Samoa's digital tax roadmap contemplates phased enhancement of electronic filing and data-matching, but no operational machine-readable clearance or fiscal stamping mandate applies to service exporters.

The Commissioner of the Ministry for Revenue may audit export zero-rating claims and compel full production of export documentation, contracts, and remittance records. Where zero-rated treatment is applied without adequate substantiation, or where export documents are false or misleading, the Commissioner may retrospectively claw back the 15% VAGST plus statutory interest, and may impose civil penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Act 2012. Fraudulent evasion or falsification of customs/export records may additionally result in criminal prosecution with fines and/or imprisonment, and the Central Bank of Samoa may impose exchange-control sanctions for failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tālā (WST) is not freely convertible outside Samoa. The current account is largely liberalized for trade and service payments, while the capital account remains controlled. Profit remittances by International Companies (ICs) are permitted under CBS exchange control policy provided the company holds a tax exemption certificate or tax clearance, submits audited financial statements, and the remittance is processed through an authorized dealer after a bona fide declaration. Dividend and branch remittances may require CBS approval for amounts above specified thresholds; unauthorized offshore transfers are not permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds from goods and services, including inspection and testing fees, must be repatriated to a Samoa-licensed commercial bank within 180 days from the date of invoice, bill of lading, or service completion, whichever is determinable. Unless an individual retention exemption is issued by CBS, 100% of the WST equivalent of export receipts must be surrendered to an authorized dealer for conversion into WST at the prevailing market rate. Retention in foreign currency is only permitted for designated offshore operating accounts with prior CBS approval.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low – Samoa does not recognize offshore digital EMI or virtual-account providers as authorized dealers under the Exchange Control Act. Any non-resident fintech/EMI account (e.g., Wise Business) can be used only for low-value ancillary operational disbursements and cannot lawfully receive export proceeds, capital injection, or settle Samoan trade invoices. 27% statutory corporate income tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2012; a licensed International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt on all offshore-source income, resulting in a 0% effective rate for non-Samoan commercial inspection, testing and QC audit fees.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for qualifying International Company distributions (statutory exemption under the International Companies Act 1987); 15% final dividend WHT applies to dividends paid by an ordinary Samoan-taxable company to non-resident shareholders, reducible to 5% under certain DTAs for substantial corporate shareholdings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within six months after the IC's financial year-end (i.e., 30 June for a 31 December year-end); the annual government renewal fee must also be settled at the certificate anniversary to maintain good standing.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within six months after the end of the income year (i.e., by 30 June for a 31 December balance date); any residual final tax payment is due at the same time.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has no codified Local File/Master File monetary thresholds; the Commissioner may require transfer-pricing documentation where the IC derives Samoan-source income or where related-party arrangements distort the Australian or New Zealand tax base. For a tax-exempt offshore IC, prudent contemporaneous practice is to maintain arm's-length benchmarking and intercompany agreements whenever annual related-party inspection and QC fees reach WST 800,000 (approximately USD 300,000).

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2022, issued under the International Companies Act 1987, impose an economic substance test on ICs carrying on 'relevant activities', expressly including a 'distribution and service centre' business. A commercial inspection, testing, and QC agency billing offshore clients must demonstrate in Samoa: (i) active conduct of core income-generating activities managed and directed from Samoa; (ii) adequate physical office, qualified staff, and operating expenditure proportionate to income; and (iii) timely filing of the annual ESR declaration with SIFA. Material mismatch between substance and revenue triggers penalties, suspension of the tax-exemption certificate, deregistration, and automatic exchange of information with foreign tax authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2012, evading tax triggers a shortfall penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax, a monetary penalty of up to WST 20,000, and imprisonment of up to 5 years; the Registrar of International Companies may additionally deregister the IC and revoke its licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and offshore beneficial owners who sign, cause, or procure false declarations are personally criminally liable; the corporate veil is pierced for civil restitution where the entity is a pure nominee; courts may impose travel bans, asset-freezing orders, confiscation, and director disqualification.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2022 and SIFA enforcement directives, penalties include fines per offence, daily default penalties, suspension of the IC's zero-rate tax exemption effective retroactively, forced deregistration, and adverse exchange-of-information reports to OECD member states.

Director personal liability: Directors who permit the IC to operate without economic substance are jointly and severally liable for regulatory fines; personal disqualification from directorships, liability to restore diverted client inspection fees as equitable compensation, and criminal referral where offshore trust assets are treated as personal assets.
AML/CFT Deficiency and Suspicious Wire Activity

Statutory sanction: Under the Samoan AML and Proceeds of Crime Act 2007 and SIFA directives, the IC faces deregistration, monetary penalties, confiscation of proceeds, and blacklisting from international payment corridors; convicted individuals are liable to imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and resident agents are independently prosecutable; they face custodial sentences, confiscation orders, personal travel bans, and permanent debarment from the Samoan international finance sector; courts will disregard the corporate form to enforce restitution against the directing mind.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2012, the payer must withhold and remit; failure attracts a penalty equal to the unremitted tax, interest at the prescribed rate, criminal fines, and assessment of the IC as statutory agent of the overseas payee for collection of the foreign entity's tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately fail to withhold after notice from the Revenue Authority become personally liable for the uncollected tax as collection officers of the Crown; aggravated cases carry imprisonment of up to 5 years, confiscation of equivalent assets, and prohibition on leaving Samoa.
