Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); online portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sars.gov.za and https://efiling.sars.gov.za).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), a South African (Pty) Ltd receives a registration number and must apply to SARS for an income tax reference number via eFiling or the SARS branch office. For VAT purposes, a taxable enterprise with annual taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1 million must complete the VAT registration application (VAT 101 form) electronically or physically. Non-resident foreign entities deriving South African-source income or conducting taxable activities in South Africa must register with SARS as non-resident taxpayers, obtain a VAT reference number if turnover thresholds are met, and are required to appoint a SARS-approved local representative or tax agent for service of notices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Upon incorporation with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), a South African (Pty) Ltd receives a registration number and must apply to SARS for an income tax reference number via eFiling or the SARS branch office. For VAT purposes, a taxable enterprise with annual taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1 million must complete the VAT registration application (VAT 101 form) electronically or physically. Non-resident foreign entities deriving South African-source income or conducting taxable activities in South Africa must register with SARS as non-resident taxpayers, obtain a VAT reference number if turnover thresholds are met, and are required to appoint a SARS-approved local representative or tax agent for service of notices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in South African law; entity classification is fixed by the Companies Act and Income Tax Act. Statutory elections include: VAT grouping under section 50 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991, income tax group taxation under section 41 of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 for groups with a common 65% ownership, and asset-for-share roll-over elections under the Eighth Schedule. Hybrid mismatch rules in respect of hybrid instruments and hybrid entities operate mandatorily and do not provide elective carve-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 in annual taxable supplies (compulsory VAT registration); voluntary registration is permitted where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where they are supplied to a non-resident person not present in South Africa at the time of supply and are exported from South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply, provided the supplier retains satisfactory documentary proof of export. Services are zero-rated under section 11(2) where the services are physically rendered outside South Africa, or are supplied directly to non-resident persons for purposes of their export trade or under other statutory exemptions, including international transport and movable goods shipped abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 / Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Repatriation Confirmation from the receiving bank
  • Commercial or tax invoice with zero-rating endorsement and packing list

South Africa does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance mandate or digital stamping on commercial invoices. SARS operates an electronic filing and assessment system (eFiling) and an e-invoicing interoperability pilot ('eInvoice'). Customs export declarations and import clearances must be lodged electronically via SARS Customs' EDI/Web service using the SAD500 declaration; customs risk assessment and release are processed in real time without a physical stamp.

SARS may retrospectively disallow the 0% VAT rate and claw back output VAT plus interest where goods are not exported within 90 days, documentary proof is incomplete or false, or services do not qualify as exported supplies. Penalties under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 include understatement penalties of up to 75% of the shortfall, additional VAT of up to 200% for deliberate tax evasion, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating. Export proceeds are subject to South African Reserve Bank financial surveillance rules requiring repatriation within 180 days, with potential administrative penalties and exchange-control sanctions for non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a managed convertibility regime. Current account payments for trade, services, interest, and royalties are generally free and may be settled through an authorised dealer without prior FinSurv approval, subject to documentary verification and applicable SARS tax compliance checks. Capital account transactions are more controlled, but ordinary inward equity investment into a South African (Pty) Ltd does not require prior exchange control approval where the subscription is made in compliance with the Companies Act, the share register is updated, and the inward capital is reported through the authorised dealer. Profit repatriation is permissible but must be supported by a duly declared dividend, audited or independently reviewed financial evidence, SARS tax clearance, and resolution of any local tax obligations. The rand is not freely externally convertible; all foreign exchange operations must be channelled through an authorised dealer or an approved Foreign Currency Account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must repatriate the full export proceeds to South Africa. Payment for exported goods must be received within 180 days from the date of shipment. Foreign currency proceeds must be surrendered to an authorised dealer within 30 days of receipt, unless the exporter maintains a FinSurv-approved Foreign Currency Account. Where such an account is approved, export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency only for approved trade and working capital purposes, and any residual unused balance must ultimately be converted to rand. The effective mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of net export proceeds, reduced only by allowable externalisation through an approved Foreign Currency Account structure.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers. Cross-border treasury activity must be effected through SARB-appointed authorised dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-moderate feasibility as a supplemental treasury rail, but not viable as a primary account. Offshore digital EMIs are not South African banks and do not hold authorised dealer status under the Exchange Control Regulations. They cannot lawfully maintain a domestic ZAR clearing account, cannot provide South African domiciled documentary LC facilities, and cannot service the statutory export proceeds surrender obligation. Such EMIs may support low-value USD/EUR collection or disbursement flows through a licensed sponsoring bank, but all substantive liquidity, LC, capital, and FX transactions must be executed through a South African authorised dealer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan funding must be routed through the company's account at a South African authorised dealer using SWIFT MT103 or equivalent ISO 20022 credit transfer. The remittance must reference the company name and the applicable capital, share subscription, or intercompany loan purpose. For an ordinary equity subscription, no prior FinSurv approval is required, but the authorised dealer must verify the share allotment, the updated share register, CIPC beneficial ownership filings, board resolutions, source of funds, and the natural-person identity of the ultimate beneficial owner. The foreign currency is settled to ZAR on the same value date at the prevailing market rate through the authorised dealer's market desk. The BOP reporting classification should reflect inward direct investment equity capital; shareholder loans should be separately classified as intercompany trade or capital loans and reported to the SARB through the authorised dealer. A capital escrow account may be used to ring-fence the proceeds until all corporate legalities and share allotment formalities are completed.Outbound payments for imported PCBA components, tooling, logistics, and contract manufacturing overheads are cleared using SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open-account telegraphic transfers and SWIFT MT700/MT707 documentary credit messages for commercial letters of credit subject to UCP 600. Each disbursement must be supported by a documented underlying trade transaction, including invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of origin, customs entry, inspection certificate, and, where applicable, transfer pricing documentation. Foreign currency disbursements may be effected from an approved Foreign Currency Account or purchased through the authorised dealer spot market with a clear trade purpose. ZAR leg settlement for domestic costs and local VAT payments occurs through SAMOS or BankservAfrica clearing. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (Income Tax Act section 5(1); 28% applied to earlier years)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on every dividend declared by the Pty Ltd, imposed under section 64E of the Income Tax Act; the company acts as the withholding agent and must pay the tax to SARS no later than the month-end following the dividend declaration, failing which SARS assesses the unwithheld amount against the company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File transfer pricing documentation must be prepared and submitted where the value of cross-border connected-person transactions in the year of assessment exceeds ZAR 100 million; the Country-by-Country Report obligation arises for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding ZAR 10 billion. Below the ZAR 100 million threshold, transfer pricing records must still be maintained and produced on SARS request within the statutory documentation timetable of the Tax Administration Act.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; the operative doctrines are the general anti-avoidance rule of section 103 of the Income Tax Act and the common-law substance-over-form principle. A resident Pty Ltd is taxed on worldwide income and residence follows the place of effective management (POEM). A PCBA contract-manufacturing entity that lacks a substantive local factory, operational employees and genuine South African management can have its deductions disallowed and its profits re-attributed to the true economic owner; controlled foreign company rules in section 9D further attribute undistributed foreign accumulation to resident shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False ITR14 Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil understatement penalty of 150% of the understated tax under section 222 Category 4 (intentional tax evasion); prescribed-rate interest on the unpaid tax; automatic assessment with a 100% penalty where gross negligence is proved; criminal prosecution under sections 234-235 of the Tax Administration Act carrying a fine determined by the court and imprisonment up to 2 years for a first offence and up to 5 years for a subsequent offence; SARS may issue a warrant of seizure and reopen the assessment indefinitely in fraud cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorised the false return are jointly and severally liable under sections 159 and 160 of the Tax Administration Act for the company's unpaid tax; SARS may obtain a preservation order over personal bank accounts, residential property and travel documents; on conviction, the corporate veil is pierced for civil restitution and the director is disqualified from managing a company under the Companies Act.
