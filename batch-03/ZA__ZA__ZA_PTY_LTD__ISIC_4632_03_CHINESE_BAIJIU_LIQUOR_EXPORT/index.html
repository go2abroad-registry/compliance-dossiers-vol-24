Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – unified national tax, customs and excise authority; central portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za) for income tax, VAT and customs administration, with SARS e@syFile for import/export declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), which auto-links the CIPC enterprise number to SARS and creates the Income Tax reference number. Step 2: Complete SARS eFiling registration via PIN activation, uploading the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum of Incorporation, directors' identification and registered office proof. Step 3: For VAT, file Form VAT101 with SARS eFiling or at a SARS branch within 21 days after exceeding the compulsory R1,000,000 turnover threshold in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is available at or above R50,000 annual taxable turnover. Step 4: Register as a SARS Customs client (Form DA185) to obtain a customs code for lodging SAD500 export declarations; if claiming excise duty rebate or drawback on exported baijiu, register as a licensed excise client or under customs warehouse supervision. Step 5: Appoint a resident public officer (required for registration compliance); for non-resident directors, file the relevant SARS power-of-attorney and non-resident representation forms.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a resident proprietary limited company taxed on worldwide income at 27% corporate income tax (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023); no pass-through treatment; distributions subject to 20% Dividends Tax at shareholder level; double tax relief available under South Africa's tax treaty network.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; entity classification is determined by the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and the residence test in s1 Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Available elections include: (i) headquarter company election under s9I to ring-fence foreign dividends, interest and CFC income; (ii) VAT group registration under s50 VAT Act 89 of 1991 to consolidate affiliated vendors; (iii) voluntary VAT registration at R50,000 annual turnover; (iv) CFC effective-tax-rate exemption election under s9D(9A); and (v) mandatory self-assessment and documentation of hybrid mismatch outcomes under ss23M–23T Income Tax Act (no elective mechanism).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 compulsory registration threshold (taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period); voluntary registration from R50,000 annual taxable supplies; registration application must be lodged within 21 days after the compulsory threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the export of movable goods (baijiu inventory) under s11(1)(a) VAT Act, provided the vendor exports the goods in the ordinary course of enterprise operations and obtains and retains the prescribed documentary proof within the statutory timeframes as set out in s54 VAT Act and the SARS VAT 404 Guide. The goods must physically leave South Africa (outside the SACU customs area) within 90 days of the date of the tax invoice or supply. Zero-rating also covers separately charged freight and insurance for the export leg (s11(1)(b)) and certain s11(2)(k) services supplied to non-residents. Exported excisable spirits are subject to excise duty suspension or rebate/drawback under the Customs and Excise Act where duty-paid stock is exported under customs supervision; domestic sales, duty-free shop sales and supplies to SACU destinations remain taxable or dutiable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) cleared via SARS Customs e@syFile evidencing exit of goods from the SACU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) showing the consignee, port of discharge and physical movement of the baijiu cargo
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list describing the goods under HS 2208.90 with quantities, alcohol strength and export value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 (or Letter of Credit settlement evidence) showing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency from the non-resident buyer
  • Proof of excise duty rebate/drawback claim under ss40–41 Customs and Excise Act where duty-paid stock is exported, including the relevant excise control documents

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or API-based transaction clearance mandate is currently in force. Vendors must issue SARS-compliant electronic tax invoices under s20 VAT Act 89 of 1991 (full tax invoice for supplies exceeding R5,000; abridged invoice for R5,000 or less) and dispatch them within 21 days of the supply. VAT returns (VAT201) are filed electronically via eFiling; SARS has published an e-invoicing adoption strategy and pilot programme, but compliance remains return-based rather than invoice-cleared. Export clearance is mandatory through SARS Customs e@syFile using the SAD500 declaration, with electronic cargo-reporting via the SARS border APIs.

SARS risk-engines and high-risk verification teams target alcohol exporters showing high-value VAT refunds, mismatched SAD500 versus VAT201 data, circular trade patterns or missing proof-of-export documentation. Zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back, with interest at the prescribed rate, if the vendor fails to retain prescribed documentary proof for five years. Civil penalties include understatement penalties of 10%–150% depending on the degree of conduct under s223 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, and additional tax up to 200% of the VAT amount under s60 VAT Act. Criminal liability arises for fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims under ss234–235 Tax Administration Act (fine and/or imprisonment), common-law fraud or corruption prosecution, and SARS may pursue personal liability of directors/office-bearers where VAT debts arise from deliberate non-compliance in insolvency scenarios (ss179–183 TAA).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is free-floating and fully convertible for current-account transactions, including trade settlement. The capital account is open but compliance-gated: non-residents may remit equity subscription proceeds freely through an Authorised Dealer, but share allotment triggers post-transaction reporting (share certificates, auditor's certificate, SARB inward-investment coding). Dividend and profit repatriation to non-residents is permitted only if the underlying share capital was introduced through the local banking system, the distribution is covered by audited distributable reserves, and a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status (TCS) is presented. Loop structures and round-tripping through South African-funded offshore entities are prohibited; repatriation will be blocked where the foreign investor's capital is traceable to South African sources without prior FinSurv approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate full export proceeds to South Africa within 180 days of the date of shipment, evidenced by the customs declaration Form DA185/SAD500. The default mandatory conversion ratio upon repatriation is effectively 100% into rand: the full foreign-currency amount must be surrendered to the Authorised Dealer and credited in ZAR to the exporter's resident account unless the exporter holds a SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA), in which case the retained foreign currency is ring-fenced for approved obligations. There is no partial statutory conversion derogation; any three-party conversion or collection-agent routing requires prior corporate approval from FinSurv.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the national exchange-control authority, under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933), the Exchange Control Regulations, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual issued to Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed Authorised Dealers in South Africa and cannot lawfully clear ZAR or repatriate trade proceeds on behalf of the (Pty) Ltd; export proceeds must flow through a resident Authorised Dealer account within 180 days. Limited utility remains for collecting CNY/USD from foreign buyers in a non-resident EMI and wire-transferring onward to the South African bank, but this reintroduces third-party-settlement and source-of-funds red flags. The compliance-pressure and documentary-cost profile makes direct Tier-1 banking the durable and recommended structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity subscription: foreign shareholders remit via SWIFT MT103 instructing the originating bank to credit the (Pty) Ltd's capital escrow account at the South African Authorised Dealer. The purpose field must state 'FDI - share capital subscription' with reference to the Subscription Agreement and allotment date; the beneficiary line must carry the company's exact legal name and a distinctive 'Capital Injection' account number. The remitter must be the subscribing investor itself (no third-party funding); the bank retains board minutes allotting shares, a Companies Act 2008 solvency-and-liquidity statement, auditor's post-allotment certificate, signed share certificates, and the source-of-wealth pack. Foreign loan injections used for inventory require intragroup loan registration with SARB and must follow an arm's-length loan agreement; unauthorised excess proceeds are characterised as blocked capital and are subject to surrender.For settlement to Chinese baijiu producers and suppliers, the (Pty) Ltd will deploy: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers in USD or offshore CNY (CNH) for open-account purchases, governed by the underlying commercial invoice and packing list; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the South African Tier-1 bank to the Chinese beneficiary under UCP 600 (at-sight or 90-180-day usance) covering the import leg, with the bank's customs declaration code evidencing the cargo; (iii) documentary collection (D/P or D/A) as a fallback for smaller SACU buyers; and (iv) a pooled liquidity structure in ZAR/USD where export sale proceeds are swept daily into the trade account to fund downstream LC margin calls without breaching the 180-day export bill repatriation rule.Deliverable forex forwards (ZAR/USD, ZAR/CNH, ZAR/EUR) with Authorised Dealers under ISDA 2002/2006 Forms, covering genuine commercial exposure for up to 12 months with underlying invoice documentation.FX swaps and roll-over swaps to bridge mismatched payout dates in the import-export cycle and reposition ZAR/USD liquidity without speculative open positions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on CNH/USD or offshore CNY legs, permitted where onshore CNH convertibility is limited or the correspondent bank requires a synthetic hedge, subject to SARB FinSurv approval of the underlying trade contract.Cross-currency swaps (ZAR/USD and USD/CNH) with the primary relationship bank for structurally matched long-term trade-finance facilities, requiring an ISDA master agreement, credit support annex, and documented economic-risk rationale.
