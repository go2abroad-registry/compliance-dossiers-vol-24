E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime. The resident Pty Ltd is taxed at entity level on worldwide income (corporate rate 27%; capital gains included at 80%). Statutory elections relevant to classification and cross-border structure include: (i) the Headquarter Company election (section 9J, Income Tax Act 58 of 1962) which exempts certain foreign dividends, interest, royalties and disposal gains; (ii) the CFC high-tax exemption election (section 9D(2A)) for offshore subsidiaries; (iii) hybrid debt/equity recharacterisation rules under sections 8F/8G operate automatically without an election; (iv) the R&D allowance election under section 11D; (v) VAT cash-accounting election for qualifying vendors. Hybrid-mismatch outcomes are governed by the statutory definition of 'domestic reverse hybrid' and the taxpayer's shareholding composition.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 (R1 million) of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period, or where it can reasonably be expected that the threshold will be reached (VAT Act section 23(1)); voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12-month period (section 23(2)); non-resident suppliers of electronic services are required to register for VAT without regard to any turnover threshold (section 23(4))

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where the commercial agricultural full-spectrum LED fixtures are supplied under a sale, rental, hire-purchase or consignment arrangement and are physically exported from South Africa within the period determined by the Commissioner (practically within 90 days from the date of supply), by the supplier or by the purchaser acting on the supplier's behalf; the vendor must hold and retain the prescribed documentary proof. Services: zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) where the services are supplied to a non-resident, are not in connection with land or immovable property in South Africa, are not 'deemed to be supplied in South Africa' by the deeming rules, and the consideration is paid in foreign currency into the vendor's South African bank account. Input tax on goods and services used in producing export zero-rated LED fixtures is fully creditable (VAT Act sections 16 and 17).

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500) lodged electronically and endorsed by SARS Customs evidencing the outward movement of the goods
  • Commercial invoice / tax invoice complying with VAT Act section 20
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill or multimodal transport document evidencing export from South Africa to the foreign consignee
  • Inward remittance certificate / authorised-dealer bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in a South African bank account

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently mandated. Tax invoices must meet the substantive requirements of VAT Act section 20 and, for eFiling users, SARS requires the prescribed XML/PDF electronic invoice data formats; VAT201 returns, customs export declarations (SAD 500) and related trade documents are transmitted electronically through the SARS eFiling portal and the Customs Modernisation API. SARS is implementing a phased e-invoicing and digital-reporting programme with pilots expected from 2025; until the mandate comes into force, there is no statutory requirement for third-party fiscal clearance of each invoice.

SARS cross-checks zero-rating claims against customs export records, bills of lading, freight-forwarder data and bank forex receipts; incomplete or inconsistent documentation causes the 0% rate to be clawed back and the supply to be assessed at 15% output VAT from the original tax period. Interest at the prescribed rate (repo plus 700 basis points) runs under section 187 of the Tax Administration Act, and understatement penalties under section 222 escalate to 25% (substantial understatement), 50% (failure to take reasonable care), 100% (gross negligence) and 150% (intentional tax evasion), with criminal liability (fine or imprisonment up to two years) for false statements under section 235 of the TAA. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the applicable 180-day window (standard SARB benchmark being 90 days from shipment unless an extension is granted under the Currency and Exchanges Manual) exposes the exporter to exchange-control penalties, a deemed-loan recharacterisation of the outstanding amount, and potential disqualification of future zero-rating claims; directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the VAT debt under sections 159-160 of the TAA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current-account convertibility is fully liberalised: ZAR is a SWIFT-settled, freely convertible currency for trade, services, dividends, interest and royalties, transacted through Authorised Dealers without prior SARB approval, subject to FICA verification and SARS tax clearance. Capital-account transactions (FDI equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, offshore guarantees, acquisition financing and hedging) require either prior approval by SARB FinSurv or execution through an Authorised Dealer in accordance with the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers, using the prescribed digital reporting portal (Consolidated Reporting System / CRS). Profit remittance hurdles are limited to a compliant board dividend resolution, form MP336(a), a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status Pin, and a Non-Resident Declaration (NRD) where applicable; escrow-funded capital contributions must be held at an approved AD pending completion of FICA verification and exchange-control compliance. Financing structures using offshore holding-company loops are scrutinised for indirect offshore interest or 'loop structure' restrictions under paragraph A.13 of the Manual and should be cleared in advance by FinSurv.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds for goods shipped from South Africa must be received onshore within 180 days from date of shipment (or within 180 days from the contractual due date where the AD has granted a banks-approved extension up to an additional 180 days). There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may retain 100% of proceeds in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or convert freely to ZAR, but 100% of proceeds must be repatriated and reported in South Africa via Form ED cross-referenced to the SARS Customs export declaration (SAD 500), with the corresponding exchange-control number appearing on the bank's inward payment advice. Exporters must submit a monthly Export Debtors Report for any funded receivables outstanding beyond the default period, and any shortfall not received within the prescribed grace period triggers a FinSurv default report requiring the bank to notify the regulatory authority.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which administers the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933), with day-to-day delegation to SARB Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange (ADs) for routine current-account trade transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM – OFFSHORE EMI PARTNERED MODELS ONLY. Non-bank EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Platform) may provide multi-currency wallets, international payment rails and FX execution, but they are not SARB Authorised Dealers and cannot open domestic ZAR clearing accounts or hold exchange-control escrow balances onshore without routing through a participating Tier-1 AD. Onboarding of ZA_PTY_LTD on such platforms is viable for outbound supplier payments and inbound receipts in USD/EUR/CNY, yet every repatriation into ZAR and every inward FDI equity injection must ultimately clear through a licensed AD with an attached purpose code and FinSurv reporting; the offshore EMI must therefore be interposed after the primary domestic banking relationship is live, and the EMI's South African remittance partner must be pre-approved to avoid FICA-driven payment blockages.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Stage the FDI through a SARB-regulated channel: (i) open the ZAR Operating and Capital Escrow accounts with an AD; (ii) transfer the equity consideration via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for cover) to the AD's nostro account for credit to the escrow with payment narrative 'FDI Equities – purpose code 1121 – ZA_PTY_LTD', including the bank's inward foreign investment declaration number once issued; (iii) simultaneously file the inward share-issue report (Form E1/MP336(a) as required) and Non-Resident Declaration (NRD) through the AD; (iv) provide the bank with the SARS tax compliance pin and board resolution; (v) upon FICA verification completion, the AD transfers funds from escrow to the operating account, the company issues shares to the non-resident investor and the AD forwards the finalised inward capital report to FinSurv within the statutory reporting window.
