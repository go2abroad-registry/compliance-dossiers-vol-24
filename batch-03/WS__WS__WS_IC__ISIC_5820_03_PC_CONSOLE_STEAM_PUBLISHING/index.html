Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR) of the Independent State of Samoa, successor to the Samoa Tax Office/Department of Inland Revenue, operating jointly with the Samoa Customs Service and the Central Bank of Samoa for exchange control. National e-portal: www.revenue.gov.ws; taxpayer e-services filed electronically via the Samoa Tax Administration System (TAS).

E-government filing portal

A Samoa International Company (IC) is incorporated by the Registrar of International Companies under the International Companies Act; the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) issues the Certificate of Incorporation and the statutory Tax Exemption Certificate. For tax registry purposes, the IC obtains a TIN by filing the MfR TIN application form (Form TIN-01) with: (i) certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation; (ii) Memorandum and Articles of Association; (iii) register of directors and shareholders; and (iv) a statutory declaration confirming non-residence and absence of Samoa-source income. The MfR will not register the IC for income tax or VAGST provided the IC confines its activity to exempt international business; the IC instead pays the annual International Company government license fee to SIFA. If the IC subsequently commences a taxable activity in Samoa, the TIN is activated for income tax and VAGST registration. A non-resident foreign entity without a Samoan presence may obtain a TIN for inbound payment processing, but cannot be voluntarily VAGST-registered absent a taxable activity in Samoa.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Samoan domestic law; the IC's exempt status is statutory and evidenced by the Tax Exemption Certificate issued by the Registrar of International Companies. Available elections: (i) application for the exemption certificate at incorporation; (ii) election to convert/redomicile from IC status to a domestic company under the Companies Act 2001, thereby becoming subject to entity-level company income tax (standard corporate rate 27%) and VAGST at 15% on taxable supplies; (iii) election by a foreign corporation to register a Samoan branch, taxed at the entity level on branch profits; and (iv) voluntary VAGST registration below the statutory threshold at the Commissioner's discretion, only where a taxable activity exists. An exempt IC cannot elect pass-through treatment in Samoa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 200,000 of annual taxable supplies in Samoa; voluntary registration below the threshold is at the Commissioner's discretion; exporters of zero-rated supplies may register to claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the VAGST Act 1992 to: (a) goods physically exported from Samoa; (b) services supplied to a non-resident of Samoa who is outside Samoa at the time of supply, provided the services are not performed in connection with land or goods situated in Samoa; and (c) international transport and directly related services. For the Steam/PlayStation digital publishing model, the IC's platform publishing, distribution, and licensing services supplied to Valve Corporation or Sony Interactive Entertainment — both non-resident counterparties — qualify as exported services and, where VAGST registration is held, are assessed at 0% with entitlement to input tax credits. The exemption is lost where the services are rendered to a Samoa resident, are consumed in Samoa, or relate to Samoan real property. The IC must document the transaction as an export of intangible services evidenced by platform payout statements, executed publishing agreements, and inward remittance records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-Customs export entry) where any physical goods are part of the transaction
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export, or a digitally executed service delivery certificate issued by the Valve/PlayStation platform for services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice from a licensed Samoan bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice together with Steam Partner or PlayStation Store payout statement
  • Packing list, purchase order, and insurance certificate (where goods are concerned)
  • Executed publishing/licensing agreement with Valve Corporation or Sony Interactive Entertainment evidencing the non-resident counterparty and export nature of the supply
  • Certificate of incorporation or tax residence certificate of the offshore counterparty substantiating non-resident status

Samoa does not operate a real-time invoice clearance or e-invoicing API regime. The mandatory regime comprises: (i) use of MfR-approved Electronic Tax Registers (ETR) and fiscal cash registers/POS software for businesses making taxable supplies to consumers; (ii) mandatory electronic filing of VAGST returns and e-payment through the Tax Administration System (TAS); (iii) no electronic tax stamping of export invoices; and (iv) retention of the full statutory documentation bundle for export zero-rating claims. An exempt IC with no VAGST taxable activity in Samoa is outside the ETR/TAS filing net.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to audit by the Ministry for Revenue and Samoa Customs; the burden of proving export rests on the supplier. Where zero-rating is claimed without the prescribed documentation, or where refunds are obtained fraudulently — including circular fund flows, falsified remittance certificates, or supplies routed to a Samoa-resident related party — the Commissioner may: (i) assess VAGST at 15% on the supply; (ii) impose a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded, plus late-payment interest at the statutory rate; (iii) claw back refunds for up to six years (with the limitation period extended in cases of fraud); (iv) initiate criminal prosecution, with fines of up to WST 5,000 and/or imprisonment of up to five years on conviction; and (v) revoke the IC's Tax Exemption Certificate, resulting in deregistration by SIFA and the loss of tax-exempt zone status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) is convertible on current-account transactions; the Samoa International Company (IC) is treated as a non-resident and is exempt from exchange-control approval for foreign-currency operating flows. Capital-account injections into the IC are freely transferable in USD, EUR, or GBP, provided the funds are sourced from non-resident shareholders and the remittance is identified as direct investment equity. Profit remittances are not subject to Samoan withholding tax; the only practical hurdles are bank-level verification of audited financial statements, a board resolution authorizing distribution, and, where conversion to WST is attempted, execution at a CBS-approved bank exchange rate. No dividend-blocking or compulsory local reinvestment rule applies to an IC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters, CBS policy requires export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days and, unless a foreign-currency retention facility is granted, sold to a licensed Dealer in foreign exchange at no more than 100% conversion into WST. The target IC is outside the resident surrender regime: Steam/PlayStation platform receipts may be collected and retained in USD/EUR with 0% mandatory conversion, and no Samoan foreign-exchange surrender certificate is triggered for non-resident banking flows.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) – Foreign Exchange Division

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium; offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as EU/UK payment licence holders are not licensed or passported by CBS and cannot provide a Samoan settlement address. They are viable only as a collection overlay for low-value Steam/PlayStation receipts if the EMI maintains a licensed money-service business in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction and sweeps funds to the Samoan licensed bank within 48 hours. Legal constraints: no EMI may hold or claim to hold a Samoa banking licence, all account-opening questionnaires must affirm that the ultimate settlement cushion is held at a CBS-regulated bank, and the EMI’s licence, audited financials, and business-continuity/disaster-recovery plan must be presented as part of the mother-bank KYC file.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions, including platform payouts, intercompany licensing, and management fees, reaching or exceeding WST 600,000 per annum (approximately USD 220,000) require a contemporaneous transfer pricing study applying the arm's-length principle; the Commissioner may also require a Samoa transfer pricing policy, master file, or local file within 30 days' notice where aggregate related-party turnover exceeds WST 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has adopted OECD-aligned economic substance regulations with effect for International Companies undertaking 'relevant activities' (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, IP holding, distribution, and service centres). Pure digital game publishing via Steam or PlayStation is not in the ESR relevant-activity list; nevertheless, the International Company must demonstrate minimum substance: a registered office in Samoa, a licensed resident registered agent, a maintained beneficial ownership register, and at least one annual board meeting (wherever held) with minutes filed at the registered office. Failure to demonstrate substance exposes the IC to striking-off and the loss of the tax exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 100% of the tax evaded plus late payment interest at 1.5% per month; criminal conviction carries a fine up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years per offence; deregistration of the International Company.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed a false declaration or knowingly caused the company to evade tax are personally and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; courts may impose civil restitution, a travel ban with passport surrender, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company and Nominee Abuse

Statutory sanction: Strike-off and deregistration under the International Companies Act 1987; default fines of WST 500 to WST 5,000 plus WST 100 per day of continued default; re-registration refused until all arrears and penalties are paid.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced on grounds of sham and fraudulent trading; directors, including shadow directors and nominee shareholders, are jointly liable for all company debts, and an asset confiscation order may attach to their personal property.
AML/CFT Breach and Suspicious Transactions

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2018, a body corporate may be fined up to WST 500,000; an individual may be imprisoned up to 14 years; the IC and associated bank accounts are subject to freezing orders and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability, disqualification from acting as company officers, travel bans, and confiscation of proceeds; professional directors of nominee companies face revocation of their Samoa trust and company service provider licences.
Loss of International Company Exemption

Statutory sanction: Retroactive revocation of the International Company tax exemption; the company is assessed to 27% corporate income tax on all income for the full year of disqualification, plus a 20% late filing penalty and 1.5% monthly interest; the IC may be struck off and excluded from the International Companies register.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's back taxes where the disqualification resulted from their own acts or omissions; Samoan courts may issue a personal tax certificate clearance requirement before any director or shareholder may leave Samoa, and may impose a civil restitution order against personal assets.
