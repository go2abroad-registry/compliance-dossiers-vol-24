Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of the Independent State of Samoa; online portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company applying for a TIN/VAGST ID must file the prescribed TIN application with the Ministry for Revenue, attaching: (a) certificate of incorporation and certificate of good standing under the International Companies Act 1987; (b) details of the registered office and locally appointed registered agent/secretary; (c) certified copies of constitutional documents and the statutory beneficial ownership register; and (d) tax identification data of the foreign parent and merchant counterparties. After verification, the Ministry issues a TIN within approximately five working days. If the International Company makes taxable supplies in Samoa, a separate VAGST registration is required, obligating it to charge, report, and pay VAGST and to retain full records for at least seven years. For wholly offshore payment-acquiring and clearing operations, the International Company should also obtain a written tax-exemption certificate confirming its exempt status; no minimum capital is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - A Samoa International Company is exempt from Samoan income tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, and withholding taxes on offshore income and distributions. Its non-resident owners and cross-border payment-gateway operations are generally outside the territorial tax net, provided no taxable activity is conducted in Samoa. VAGST applies only to taxable supplies connected with Samoa.

Available election forms

There is no US-style check-the-box election and no general hybrid-mismatch election in Samoa. The principal election for the International Company is voluntary VAGST registration where annual taxable turnover does not exceed the WST 100,000 threshold; registration is compulsory above that threshold. The International Company may also elect to document and preserve its exempt status by filing annual statutory returns with the Registrar and, where appropriate, seeking a Ministry for Revenue exemption ruling for the offshore payment-processing services. No group fiscal unity, branch-transfer, or loss-relief election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (approximately USD 35,500) in annual taxable turnover; registration is compulsory once supplies of goods or services in Samoa exceed that threshold, and voluntary registration is permitted below it. A non-resident offshore payment-service provider with no Samoan taxable supplies is not required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Samoa and to services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa where the benefit of the services is not enjoyed in Samoa. Payment-gateway, merchant acquiring, clearing, and settlement services supplied from a Samoa International Company exclusively to non-resident merchants qualify for zero-rating if properly contracted, invoiced to a non-resident, performed offshore, and evidenced by foreign-currency settlement through Visa/Mastercard rails or Fedwire. Financial services that are exempt in the domestic VAGST regime will not produce zero-rated output tax unless the specific service is accepted as an exported taxable service; in either case, the exporter should substantiate the offshore character of the supply to protect input credits and refund positions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World export entry) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing shipment from Samoa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a licensed Samoan commercial bank
  • Signed service agreement and commercial invoice with the non-resident merchant
  • SWIFT MT103, Fedwire settlement receipt, or Visa/Mastercard settlement statement linking consideration to the invoice

Samoa does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, digital stamping, or an API-connected fiscal reporting engine at the point of invoicing. Customs export declarations are processed through ASYCUDA World, and VAGST returns are filed electronically through the Ministry for Revenue portal. Taxable suppliers must issue invoices and credit notes containing supplier name, TIN/VAGST ID, date, description of supply, consideration, and VAGST calculated. Electronic records must be retained for tax audit.

The Ministry for Revenue may scrutinise zero-rated export claims and require the above documents, bank statements, and the registered agent's or payment-service provider's transaction ledger within 180 days of supply. Failure to substantiate export status, or the use of an artificial structure to convert exempt or offshore revenue into a refund, will result in assessment of VAGST at 15% on the full consideration, interest, a tax-shortfall penalty of up to 150%, clawback of refunds, revocation of the International Company's exemption for Samoan-source activity, and possible director liability under the Tax Administration Act 2012 and the International Companies Act 1987. Criminal prosecution is available in cases of fraudulent zero-rating or false documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a managed non-fully convertible currency; the CBS operates an exchange-control regime under which all non-resident payments require CBS approval or delegation to authorized dealers. Current-account items (trade, services, dividends derived from resident sources) are generally approvable; capital-account flows (equity injections, loan repayments, liquidation proceeds) require prior CBS consent. An International Company (IC) carrying on an offshore payment-gateway business exclusively in foreign currency and without Samoa-resident counterparties faces no material convertibility hurdle: it may receive and retain USD in a CBS-approved foreign-currency account and remit profits offshore without enforced conversion to WST; only funds payable to Samoa residents or converted to WST for local expenses trigger the standard surrender and reporting duties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters of goods must collect proceeds within 180 days of shipment and convert 100% to WST through an authorized dealer unless a CBS retention approval is held; service exporters (including payment-processing fee earners) must repatriate proceeds within 180 days of invoice date and likewise surrender or hold under an approved foreign-currency account. The WS_IC is treated as a non-resident IC for exchange-control purposes: its USD fee income may be retained in a designated BSP Samoa foreign-currency account under standing CBS general permission for international companies, subject to quarterly authorized-dealer reporting; conversion of USD to WST for local disbursements is reported by the bank and requires no separate CBS license for amounts below the authorized-dealer threshold.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS; Samoan: Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), Exchange Control Division. Governing instruments: Exchange Control Act 1976; Financial Institutions Act 1996; Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (and Regulations 2009); National Payment System Act 2018.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium onshore; Medium offshore. Samoa has no standalone e-money or EMI licensing regime; CBS regulates remittance and payment businesses indirectly under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 and the National Payment System Act 2018. No offshore digital EMI (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud-type platforms) may lawfully open a customer-facing Samoa branch or custody client funds without CBS licensing. The legally cleanest structure is: the Samoa IC is licensed or registered as a money-remittance/payment business with CBS and holds its settlement and treasury accounts directly with BSP Samoa, optionally using a licensed NZ/HK/SG EMI purely as disbursement middleware with BSP's informed consent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Samoa International Company with controlled transactions exceeding WST 15 million per annum (approximately US$5.6 million) is required to prepare a Local File and contribute data to a Master File. An exemption applies for ICs whose gross offshore revenue is below WST 12 million and whose related-party payments are priced using a group policy with full benchmarking.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations (2020) require International Companies deriving income from 'relevant activities' to maintain substantial economic presence in Samoa. Although payment processing is not currently within the enumerated relevant activities, the cross-border payment gateway structure must demonstrate adequate physical presence, active management, and minimum qualified employees in Samoa to avoid being recharacterised as a shell entity. A summary economic substance declaration must be filed annually by the IC's registered agent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax, daily interest at 2% per month, immediate revocation of the IC tax exemption, and prosecution under Section 111 of the Samoa Income Tax Act 2015. First offence: fine up to WST 1 million; second offence: imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors can be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt if they authorised the evasion. Criminal conviction triggers automatic disqualification from managing any Samoa company, a travel ban pending full restitution, and seizure of personal assets used in the fraud.
Shell Company (Offshore Abusive Arrangement)

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations, a financial penalty of up to US$500,000 plus compulsory strike-off from the International Companies Register. Tax authorities may deem the entity tax resident in a foreign jurisdiction, eliminating Samoan treaty access and triggering back-taxes for five years.

Director personal liability: Registered agents and directors face fines of WST 250,000 and up to 5 years' imprisonment for knowingly facilitating a sham. Directors may also be compelled to personally indemnify third-party payment processors for regulatory penalties imposed by Visa, Mastercard, or the Samoa Central Bank.
AML / CFT Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines up to US$1 million per breach, immediate suspension of the licence, confiscation of unexplained transaction proceeds, and criminal trial under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2017. The FSA may blacklist the IC, preventing any form of interbank clearing or access to Visa/Mastercard networks.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors are personally subject to penalties up to WST 300,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years for willful blindness to money laundering. The corporate veil is pierced if anti-money laundering compliance is shown to be a sham; personal bank accounts and foreign passport assets may be frozen under an international mutual legal assistance order.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: A 40% administrative penalty on the uncollected withholding tax plus interest at 2% per month from the payment date. The tax remains due even if never collected from the recipient, and the payer loses entitlement to deduct the expense where withholding is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause a corporate distribution without withholding become jointly and severally liable for the full unpaid WHT and penalties. In serious repeat cases, a director may be banned from travelling to Australia and New Zealand through a tax debt recovery mechanism.
