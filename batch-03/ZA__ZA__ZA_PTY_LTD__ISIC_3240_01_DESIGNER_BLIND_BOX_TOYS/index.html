Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); integrated portal: SARS eFiling at https://www.sars.gov.za / https://efiling.sars.gov.za; corporate formation portal: Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); customs administration: SARS Customs & Excise.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-investor-owned Pty Ltd: (1) incorporate through CIPC and obtain the CIPC registration certificate; (2) register for income tax on SARS eFiling to obtain the SARS Income Tax Reference Number; (3) where taxable supplies exceed or will exceed ZAR 1,000,000 per rolling 12-month period, register as a VAT vendor by lodging VAT201 through eFiling; voluntary registration is permitted once annual taxable turnover reaches ZAR 50,000; (4) apply for a SARS Customs Client/Exporter Code and, if trading goods, an import/export customs code; (5) register for PAYE, UIF and SDL when employees are hired. A foreign non-resident entity not incorporated in South Africa that makes taxable supplies in the country may apply directly for VAT vendor status by submitting VAT201 to SARS, naming a responsible representative in South Africa and furnishing proof of a commercial establishment or continuing agency.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a separate legal entity on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (2024/25 tax period); dividends tax at 20% is collected on distributions to shareholders; there is no pass-through, transparent, or partnership-style tax classification for a Pty Ltd. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed as a separate legal entity on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate of 27% (2024/25 tax period); dividends tax at 20% is collected on distributions to shareholders; there is no pass-through, transparent, or partnership-style tax classification for a Pty Ltd. Controlled foreign company rules under s.9D of the Income Tax Act attribute certain passive income to resident shareholders.

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide a check-the-box or free-form single-member LLC election. Statutory elections and classifications include: headquarter company status under s.2(1) and ss.15B-15E for qualifying international corporate services companies; the s.9D high-tax exemption election for controlled foreign companies; s.45 group restructuring rollover relief election; s.23M/s.23N interest limitation elections; s.11I export-related deductions and special allowances; and hybrid mismatch designation rules under s.25H requiring correlative adjustments rather than elective pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration when taxable turnovers exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000 per annum for enterprises carrying on a continuing business; non-resident suppliers of electronic services to South African residents are obliged to register for VAT irrespective of the ZAR 1,000,000 threshold, subject to s.23(2)(e) of the VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under s.11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where a vendor supplies goods and the vendor (or another person on the vendor's behalf) removes them from South Africa within 90 days of supply, under a customs procedure, and the vendor retains prescribed documentary proof of export. Services may be zero-rated where they are physically performed outside South Africa, supplied on temporarily exported movable goods, or supplied to a non-resident when the benefit of the services is consumed outside South Africa under the enduring s.11(2) categories, including internet- and e-commerce-delivered services meeting the 'exported service' test.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500), Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate, Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Proof of Export (Cargo Report / customs outturn), and any documentary evidence required by SARS Customs Rule 60/70 evidencing physical exit of goods from South Africa.

South Africa has not enacted mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or fiscal device integration for ordinary goods manufacturers. SARS mandates VAT invoices compliant with s.20 of the VAT Act, requires VAT returns and payments via the eFiling platform, and maintains an audit-ready electronic file framework (e-audit, SFI). SARS may utilise API-based inspection during electronic verification, but no national continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance model is currently in force.

SARS scrutinises export zero-rating claims retrospectively; failure to provide the prescribed export proof triggers reassessment at the standard 15% VAT rate, interest at the prevailing SARS rate, a 10% late-payment penalty, and an understatement penalty of up to 200% under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Where fraudulent or reckless documentation is presented, SARS may claw back refunds with retroactive effect and the exporter faces criminal fines up to ZAR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years, with personal director liability available under s.61 of the Tax Administration Act for failure to comply or for facilitating a wrongful refund.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade payments, service fees, and ordinary operating remittances, subject to documentation requirements and anti-money laundering (AML) review. Capital account transactions are exchange-control constrained; inward direct investment in the form of equity or shareholder loans may be repatriated after approval or compliance with the approved settlement route. Profit remittance by way of dividends to non-resident shareholders is permitted without FinSurv prior approval provided the dividend is declared from after-tax distributable reserves, the shareholder is recorded in the company register, and the applicable 20% dividends tax under the Income Tax Act No. 58 of 1962 is withheld and remitted to the South African Revenue Service (SARS). Branch profits and liquidation proceeds are repatriable through authorised dealers upon production of the required tax clearance and non-resident endorsement documentation. Practical hurdles include: proving the non-resident nature of the shareholder, evidencing the original source of inward capital via a capital import certificate, and ensuring the company's tax affairs are up to date.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from designer blind box toys must be received within 180 days from the date of shipment, as required by SARB Financial Surveillance Instruction No. 3 of 2018. The full foreign currency amount must be surrendered to an authorised dealer, meaning it must be sold for ZAR within 30 days of receipt, unless the exporter has been granted a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) allowing retention of foreign currency. The surrender ratio is 100% of export proceeds in the absence of an FCA. The exporter must submit a Single Administration and Logging Declaration (SALD) for each export shipment, and proof of payment must be retained for audit by SARB FinSurv and SARS. Export proceeds cannot be netted against imports or used for offshore payments without FinSurv approval.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated under the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933, as amended, and SARB Financial Surveillance Instructions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (constrained). South Africa does not operate an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category comparable to the UK or EU. Offshore digital EMIs such as Payoneer, Airwallex, and Currencycloud can function as multi-currency collection and disbursement vehicles for the designer toy business, especially for USD/EUR/GBP proceeds from Shopify Payments and Stripe. However, they may not lawfully open domestic ZAR settlement accounts, access the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system, or issue local South African IBANs. Therefore, offshore EMIs cannot act as the primary treasury clearing rail; they serve only as supplementary foreign-currency conduits. A tier-1 local bank remains mandatory for all ZAR-denominated liquidity, statutory tax payments, dividend remittances, and capital account flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder equity or loan capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 through a South African authorised dealer (tier-1 commercial bank). The instructing non-resident bank must include the ZA_PTY_LTD full legal name, the CIPC registration number, and the SARB-required payment purpose code: 'EM' for equity capital subscriptions or 'Loan/A' for intercompany shareholder loans. The receiving bank will issue a capital import certificate only after the USD/EUR/CNY funds are received and converted into ZAR. The company must retain the board resolution and the executed subscription or loan agreement. Use of payment aggregation platforms, cryptocurrency exchanges, or fintech wallets for the capital injection is non-compliant and may result in FinSurv penalties.Trade disbursements to overseas suppliers, plastic pellet vendors, rotocasting contractors, airbrush paint suppliers, and packaging partners will be executed through the authorised dealer on SWIFT MT103 payment instructions, with purpose code '220' (merchandise trade payable) or '320' (services). For high-value or first-time cross-border contracts, a commercial letter of credit may be issued via SWIFT MT700 to the beneficiary's bank to mitigate non-delivery risk. Cash-against-documents (CAD) payment terms are acceptable for repeat suppliers below the bank's materiality threshold. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax, a final withholding tax imposed on dividends paid by South African resident companies to their shareholders. Non-resident shareholders may benefit from an exemption or reduced DTA rate only where the beneficial-ownership and substance conditions are satisfied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File discipline applies when affected transactions exceed ZAR 5 million in aggregate or any individual affected transaction exceeds ZAR 5 million, and enhanced CbCR/Master File reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion. SARS may request transfer-pricing documentation for any affected transaction irrespective of materiality where there is a risk of non-arm's-length pricing.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime for ordinary private companies. However, substance is enforced through controlled-foreign-company rules under section 9D, the general anti-avoidance rules under section 103, transfer-pricing rules under section 31, and the OECD multilateral-instrument Principal Purpose Test for treaty relief. A company engaged in the manufacture and sale of designer toys must demonstrate real local activity, operational premises, employees, business risks, and substantive decision-making in South Africa to prevent recharacterisation as a shell or conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Unreported Digital Sales

Statutory sanction: SARS may issue an assessment on undeclared sales plus an understatement penalty of up to 150% and interest under section 187 of the Tax Administration Act. Criminal prosecution may result in fines or imprisonment of up to five years for wilful tax evasion.

Director personal liability: A director may be treated as a representative taxpayer and held jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt where the company cannot pay as a result of fraud, gross negligence, or reckless trading. SARS may seek civil recovery from personal assets and may apply for a departure prohibition or travel ban where the director is a flight risk.
Transfer-Pricing Mispricing and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust taxable income to the arm's-length price under section 31, impose a secondary adjustment treating the excess as a deemed dividend, add an understatement penalty of up to 150%, and charge interest on the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length circular payments may be liable under section 77 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 for knowingly carrying on business recklessly. If the company is stripped of assets and placed in liquidation to avoid tax, directors can face civil restitution, attachment of personal assets, and criminal accountability.
Shell Company and Treaty-Shopping Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS will deny treaty relief under the Principal Purpose Test and reapply domestic withholding rates: 20% on dividends and 15% on royalties and interest. An understatement penalty of 100% to 150% may be imposed, together with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or misleading TRC declarations or beneficial-ownership declarations commit a criminal offense. Civil recovery may be pursued against shareholders and directors for distributions made with intent to defraud SARS, and travel bans may be imposed while the tax debt is under investigation.
Disguised Profit Repatriation and Dividends-Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: SARS may recharacterise the payment as a dividend and collect dividends tax at 20% from the company. The shortfall is subject to understatement penalties of 50% to 150% and interest from the original payment date.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial shareholders may be jointly and severally liable for uncollected dividends tax where the company is unable to pay. A court may pierce the corporate veil, order restitution of the distributed amounts, and impose personal liability where the arrangement was a deliberate scheme to evade tax.
