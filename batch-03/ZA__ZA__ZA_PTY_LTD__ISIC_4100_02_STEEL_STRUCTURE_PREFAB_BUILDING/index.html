Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Proprietary Limited Company; a nominal share capital (e.g. 100 Non-Par Value shares) suffices. No escrow account is statutorily mandated; funds are placed at the company's free disposal unless contractually restricted by bank or project finance covenants.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the municipal fire department in terms of SANS 10400 Part B (fire protection) and NFPA codes; structural load inspection certificates per SANS 10162 (steel structural design) and SANS 10400; occupational health and safety clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, including baseline risk assessment, fall protection plan, first-aid kit, incident reporting, and construction regulation compliance (Regs 8-10).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Proprietary Limited Company; a nominal share capital (e.g. 100 Non-Par Value shares) suffices. No escrow account is statutorily mandated; funds are placed at the company's free disposal unless contractually restricted by bank or project finance covenants.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — national tax and customs administration; primary digital portals are SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za) and the SARS Customs Declarations platform for SAD 500 lodgements.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African (Pty) Ltd is incorporated under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Upon incorporation, CIPC transmits company data to SARS and an Income Tax Reference Number is issued automatically. The company must then (1) verify its SARS tax profile by uploading certified incorporation documents (Memorandum of Incorporation, COR14.1A, CIPC registration certificate) through eFiling or a SARS branch; (2) appoint a public officer resident in South Africa as required by s.246 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; (3) register for Value-Added Tax using the VAT101 form via eFiling once taxable supplies exceed R1,000,000 in any 12-month period, or voluntarily from R50,000 (s.23 VAT Act 89 of 1991), with a separate VAT registration number issued upon approval; (4) register as an employer for PAYE/SDL/UIF using the EMP101e form where employees are appointed; and (5) obtain a SARS Customs Client Registration Number for export declarations. Non-resident directors and shareholders must disclose their foreign tax identification numbers or provide a certificate of non-resident tax status where a foreign TIN is unavailable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned South African (Pty) Ltd is incorporated under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Upon incorporation, CIPC transmits company data to SARS and an Income Tax Reference Number is issued automatically. Non-resident directors and shareholders must disclose their foreign tax identification numbers or provide a certificate of non-resident tax status where a foreign TIN is unavailable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; classification follows legal form, so a (Pty) Ltd is a resident company taxed at entity level at the 27% corporate income tax rate applicable to years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023. Available statutory mechanisms include: (a) the s.12E small business corporation election where all shareholders are natural persons; (b) s.24J debt instrument accrual elections; (c) s.11D research and development allowance election requiring SARS pre-approval; (d) s.23F/23G group assessment elections for controlled group companies with common shareholding of at least 75%; and (e) anti-hybrid recharacterization mechanics under s.8E, s.8F, s.8FA, s.23K and s.23L that neutralize hybrid debt, hybrid equity and hybrid finance mismatches, including any elective treatment attached to those instruments, without conferring a general entity classification election on foreign investors.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration where taxable supplies exceed or can reasonably be expected to exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (s.23(1)); voluntary registration permitted where taxable supplies exceed R50,000 in a 12-month period (s.23(2)); foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African residents are subject to a separate non-resident electronic services VAT registration regime effective 1 April 2019 without a local turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of steel structure goods under s.11(1)(a) of the VAT Act applies where: (i) goods are supplied under an agreement of sale to a non-resident recipient; (ii) the goods are exported from the Republic within 90 days of the date of supply, or within an extended period allowed by SARS ruling; and (iii) the vendor retains the documentary proof of export required by s.11(3). Offshore engineering and design services qualify for 0% VAT under s.11(2)(l) where physically rendered outside South Africa, or under s.11(2)(k) where supplied to a non-resident, provided the services are not excluded — in particular, services connected to immovable property situated in South Africa remain standard-rated, and prefabricated steel building kits physically exported as goods are best covered under s.11(1)(a) rather than the services provisions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500) lodged electronically via SARS Customs and endorsed as cleared for export
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export movement of the prefabricated steel units from a South African port to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, signed sales order and EPC contract evidencing the supply to the non-resident
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds into a South African foreign exchange account
  • Packing list, certificate of origin and marine cargo insurance certificate (where applicable)

South Africa presently does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC) or digital stamping for VAT invoices. A valid tax invoice must satisfy the content requirements of s.20 of the VAT Act and be issued within 21 days of the time of supply. SARS accepts electronic tax invoices and requires eFiling submission of VAT201 returns. The SARS eInvoicing pilot and a broader digitalization roadmap aligned with PEPPOL-style standardized e-invoice exchange are voluntary under the prevailing compliance regime; however, all customs export declarations (SAD 500) must be lodged electronically through the SARS Customs system, with API integration available to registered customs clients.

SARS audit strategies target zero-rated export claims intensively. Where documentary proof is defective or the goods are not physically exported within the prescribed period, the supply is standard-rated at 15% and the VAT is assessed as a deemed supply under s.8(6) of the VAT Act, with interest from the date the tax should have been declared. Understatement penalties under s.222-223 of the Tax Administration Act range from 10% to 150% of the shortfall depending on conduct, and prescribed-rate interest (presently the repurchase rate plus 7 per cent) is charged under s.187 TAA. Administrative non-compliance penalties of up to R10,000 per month per return may be imposed under s.255 TAA. Fraudulent zero-rating arrangements — including phantom exports, over-invoicing, forged SAD 500 declarations or false bills of lading — attract additional tax of up to 200% of the tax evaded under s.59(1) of the VAT Act, criminal prosecution with possible imprisonment, and parallel exchange-control sanctions under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 where export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a bifurcated regime. Current account transactions (trade in goods and services, interest, royalties, and operational remittances) are freely convertible without prior SARB approval, provided an Authorized Dealer receives a completed Form TD001/TD002 and the supporting contract; the proceeds are then settled at the market ZAR exchange rate. Capital account transactions are highly regulated: inward foreign direct investment (share capital) is permissible under general permission, but must be reported via the SARB Foreign Investment (Form CFI) framework through the receiving Authorized Dealer; outward investment, financial assistance, and loans by residents to non-residents require FinSurv approval. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful and uncapped subject to (i) audited distributable reserves, (ii) completed SARB Form DTF 212 (beneficial owner declaration for dividends), (iii) valid SARS Tax Compliance Status, and (iv) dividend withholding tax at 20% for dividends declared on or after 1 January 2025 (reduced to 5%–15% under an applicable Double Taxation Agreement). Structural hurdles include the Immovable Property (Restrictions) Act 12 of 2004 for any land-linked real estate investment and mandatory beneficial ownership disclosure under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 and the General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act 22 of 2022.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under exchange control Regulation 7 read with FinSurv guidelines, full export proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa through an Authorized Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into ZAR: an exporter may retain 100% of the repatriated foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) at an Authorized Dealer or convert it at the prevailing market rate. Each export must be supported by a customs Bill of Entry (EX/ SAD 500 series) declaring the proceeds-repatriation obligation, and all commercial shipping documents must contain the standard exchange control clause to that effect. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window triggers: (i) breach notices from the Authorized Dealer and SARB, (ii) penalty interest on the unremitted amount, (iii) suspension or deregistration of the importer/exporter code with SARS, and (iv) potential criminal prosecution under the Currency and Exchanges Act. The exporter must also maintain a 5-year trade register evidencing shipment and payment records for SARB/SARS inspection.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), administering the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, as amended. FinSurv delegates day-to-day enforcement to Authorized Dealers (commercial banks) and Authorized Brokers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., FCA- or EU-licensed) holds no statutory standing to conduct South African banking business under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, and has no direct access to the SARB-operated South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) clearing system. Such an EMI may operate only through a white-label/correspondent arrangement with a SARB Authorized Dealer, or by obtaining a restricted banking licence from SARB - a materially costly and time-intensive process. Additional constraints include FICA section 21 operator obligations, FATCA and CRS reporting, and FinSurv policy that all ZAR cross-border flows must pass through an Authorized Dealer. Accordingly, for this steel-structure EPC treasury the recommended architecture is a primary domestic corporate account with SWIFT MT103/MT700 rails, not an offshore EMI. Feasibility rating: Low-Medium.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for a non-resident parent injecting equity into the (Pty) Ltd: (1) instruct the remitting bank to dispatch a SWIFT MT103 with the equity-investment purpose code and clear reference to the Share Subscription Agreement; (2) the receiving Authorized Dealer credits the company's ZAR account or Foreign Currency Account only after obtaining the completed SARB Form CFI (foreign investment inflow), board resolution of the issuer, updated CIPC share register, and, for in-kind contributions, an independent valuation; (3) the Authorized Dealer performs OFAC/EU/UN sanctions screening and FATCA/CRS self-certification before crediting; (4) the CFI report is submitted by the Authorized Dealer to FinSurv within 30 days of credit; (5) funds may remain in USD/EUR/GBP within the FCA or be converted to ZAR at the prevailing dealing rate; (6) any subsequent shareholder loan or financial-assistance inflow not structured as equity requires prior FinSurv approval under Regulation 3; (7) escrow-release instructions must be accompanied by the underlying EPC purchase orders to support the commercial purpose of the injected capital.Clearing rails permitted for trade settlement: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for direct import payments to steel suppliers and subcontractors, supported by Form TD001/TD002, commercial invoice, bill of lading or AWB, customs entry, and inspection certificates; (ii) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit issued by the Authorized Dealer in SWIFT MT700/MT710 format, subject to UCP 600 documentary checking, covering raw steel and prefabricated components; (iii) Standby LCs and performance guarantees via SWIFT MT760 for EPC milestone obligations; (iv) ZAR-denominated payments to domestic subcontractors settled through the SARB SAMOS RTGS system; (v) all outflows must correspond to a genuine economic transaction, be submitted to the bank within 30 days of drawdown for exchange-control reporting, and avoid sanctioned persons in the steel supply chain. Where advance payment exceeds the normal trade cycle, the bank requires the underlying contract and may require a refund guarantee.Deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FEC): bilateral ZAR-USD / ZAR-EUR / ZAR-GBP forwards booked with an Authorized Dealer against confirmed import or export invoices; standard tenor up to 12 months without special FinSurv approval; longer tenors require documented underlying exposure and SARB notice.Cross-Currency Swaps and Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (USD-ZAR, EUR-ZAR): permitted for treasury balance-sheet hedging with a demonstrated underlying loan or trade liability, executed through an Authorized Dealer with SARB notification and full documentation of the economic purpose.European-Style Vanilla Currency Options (call/put on ZAR): purchasable through an Authorized Dealer to hedge confirmed EPC bid or supply exposures; resident entities may not write options without prior FinSurv approval.Offshore Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) in ZAR: available to non-resident counterparties (parent hedging subsidiary exposure) in the offshore market; onshore resident corporates may access NDFs only in exceptional cases approved by the Authorized Dealer/SARB where no deliverable-market alternative exists.
