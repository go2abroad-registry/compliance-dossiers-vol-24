Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); national e-portal: SARS eFiling (https://www.efiling.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity deriving South African source income or carrying on an enterprise in South Africa must first be registered as a taxpayer with SARS. The practical sequence is: (1) incorporate at the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) or register the foreign company as an external company; (2) obtain an income tax reference number through SARS eFiling or by appointment at a SARS branch, using the relevant non-resident registration forms and supporting constitutional documents; (3) if taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in a consecutive 12-month period, apply for VAT vendor registration within 21 days of reaching the threshold; (4) obtain the statutory tax clearance status / vendor number relevant to the activity. SARS accepts registration through a registered tax practitioner or via direct eFiling non-resident taxpayer portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification regime. A private company (Pty Ltd) is taxed at entity level as a 'taxpayer' under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Relevant statutory elections include: VAT grouping of two or more persons as one vendor under section 50 of the Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991; corporate reorganisation rollover relief elections under sections 45, 47, 48 and 55 of the Income Tax Act; CFC inclusion elections; and hybrid mismatch arrangements subject to specific anti-avoidance provisions, with no free election to disregard the domestic corporate entity for tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 taxable supplies in a consecutive 12-month period, measured on a rolling basis; registration compulsory within 21 days once the threshold is reached or can reasonably be expected to be reached.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export and supply of goods physically removed from South Africa to a non-resident are zero-rated under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act if goods are exported under customs supervision and the supplier retains documentary proof of export. Related services physically rendered for the use of a non-resident outside South Africa, and supplies of certain movable goods supplied to non-residents for export, may also qualify for zero rating. The taxpayer must be a registered vendor and must hold documentary evidence before claiming zero-rating on the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500 / SARS Customs entry with export clearance approval)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing physical export movement from South Africa
  • Commercial invoice / tax invoice with the zero-rating basis stated
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or authorised dealer foreign currency receipt statement
  • Shipping records, packing list, and proof of delivery to the non-resident consignee

There is no mandatory real-time national electronic invoice clearance or digital stamping framework in South Africa. VAT vendors must issue full South African tax invoices meeting section 20 VAT Act content requirements; returns and reconciliations are submitted via SARS eFiling with prescribed deadlines. SARS may require electronic communication and automated data reporting for registered vendors, but there is no continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance API requirement in force.

Export zero-rating claims are selected for higher-risk SARS verification where export evidence is incomplete or the 180-day foreign-currency remittance condition is not met. If documentary proof is absent or the goods were not physically exported, SARS will claw back the VAT at 15% and may impose a section 222 understatement penalty under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 of up to 125% of the shortfall, plus statutory interest and late/non-payment penalties. Deliberate submission of false or fraudulent export declarations may lead to criminal prosecution, monetary penalties, and in the case of exchange-control non-repatriation, additional SARB penalties and possible disqualification of preferential export treatment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR, ZAR code 710) is classified as a managed-float currency with full current-account convertibility for merchandise trade, service fees, royalties, dividends and capital gains repatriation, subject to prescribed documentary vetting by an Authorized Dealer. Capital-account convertibility is restricted: inward foreign direct investment requires the submission of a Financial Liability Statement and the allocation of a 'non-resident' nodal reference by the receiving Authorized Dealer, while outward capital flows (loan redemptions, share buybacks, disinvestment) require FinSurv approval, SARS tax clearance certification, and the filing of Forms A1/A2 where applicable. Profit remittance (dividends) to non-resident shareholders is permitted without FinSurv pre-approval only if the company has a valid SARS Statement of Assets and Liabilities / Income and Capital Gains Tax clearance (tax compliance status PIN) and the dividend distribution is effected by an Authorized Dealer with appropriate balance-of-payments coding; withholding tax on dividends (20% now 10% subject to double taxation treaty relief) must be deducted and remitted to SARS before the net proceeds leave South Africa.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SARS/SARB exchange control dispensation, all export proceeds from the manufacture and sale of sodium-ion battery cells must be repatriated to South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment unless an extension is granted by an Authorized Dealer or FinSurv (circulars allow 360 days for capital goods and project-based exports on application). There is no fixed statutory percentage conversion ratio; however, 100% of export value must be submitted for conversion or credited to a SARS/SARB-approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) maintained with an Authorized Dealer, with any material deviation from declared value requiring a pricing audit and supporting commercial documentation (invoice, packing list, bill of lading, customs SAD500 entry). For settlement, export proceeds may be retained in a specified FCA only for legitimate working-capital purposes, but any utilization must comply with the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual and be reported through the Authorized Dealer's exchange-control returns.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), which administers exchange control under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations issued thereunder; regulation is delegated operationally to Authorized Dealers in foreign exchange (the commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Feasibility / Supplementary Rail Only: Offshore digital financial services providers (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may lawfully service the entity for inbound collections and outbound settlement in non-ZAR currencies, but they are not licensed Authorized Dealers in foreign exchange under SARB rules, cannot maintain a ZAR domestic account with SAMOS settlement access, and cannot legally facilitate the conversion or repatriation of export proceeds in ZAR unless they partner with a locally licensed Authorized Dealer. Consequently, such EMIs are a valid supplementary clearing channel for multi-currency receipts in USD/EUR for working-capital efficiency, but the primary banking relationship, statutory reporting, nodal allocation, LC issuing and tax-linked forex approvals must remain with a Tier-1 South African commercial bank; using an unlicensed EMI as principal rail creates a regulatory violation risk under the Financial Sector Regulation Act and exchange control regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires an international SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the beneficiary account of ZA_PTY_LTD held with the South African Authorized Dealer, with Field 50K (ordering customer), Field 57A (South African receiving bank with SARB authorization) and Field 70/72 (remittance information) quoting the BOP purpose code for inward direct investment in equity (Balance of Payments code FX2/EQ or the bank-specific Form A1 inward-share allocation). The shareholder must transmit a signed Financial Liability Statement (Form FLS) or written instruction confirming the funds are a capital contribution; the receiving bank will allocate a unique non-resident nodal reference and issue the company an Excon 'inward capital' acknowledgement; funds are credited to a Capital Escrow Account (non-resident nodar) and can only be released for operational expenditure once the bank verifies the share allotment resolution, the SARS entity tax PIN, and the absence of funds from a sanctioned or high-risk jurisdiction. Loans instead of equity are discouraged: a cross-border shareholder loan requires FinSurv approval and SARS debt-to-equity thin-capitalization assessment before interest and principal may be serviced.Trade settlements for imported anode/cathode materials, electrolyte and cell components are cleared through direct SWIFT MT103 payments from the operational ZAR/USD account for approved invoices under US$100,000, and through irrevocable confirmed Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) for bulk procurement over USD 100,000 or for first-transaction counterparties. For export receivables, the entity must obtain 100% of proceeds within 180 days; payment may be received by wire transfer, by documentary collection (D/P, D/A), or by sight/term LC issued in the company's favor; all receipts must be allocated to the Authorized Dealer's export control system and disposed of within the prescribed timeline. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 12 months after the end of the company's financial year / year of assessment; SARS may prescribe an earlier eFiling deadline by annual tax-season notice. For a 31 March year-end, the filing season is typically announced around August of the following year. Late filing triggers an estimated assessment under s95 TAA.

Deadline: · Frequency:

First provisional estimate and payment due within six months of the start of the year of assessment; second provisional estimate and payment due on or before the last day of the financial year; a third voluntary 'top-up' estimate may be submitted after financial statements are finalised, subject to SARS-prescribed due dates.
