Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa; online portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

International Companies are incorporated through the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). Because an IC enjoys statutory tax exemption, it is not required to obtain a TIN or VAGST registration for foreign-source operations. A taxpayer identification number is required only if the IC triggers Samoa-source activity (e.g., local payroll, local premises), in which case the entity files the MfR 'Application for Taxpayer Registration' (Form R1) accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, resolution appointing a local tax agent, and identity documents for the ultimate beneficial owner. If the IC voluntarily seeks VAGST registration, it must additionally file Form VAGST-01 and provide a business plan, projected turnover, and local service address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Samoan law. The IC exemption is automatic under Part IX of the International Companies Act 1987 and does not require application. The IC may voluntarily elect to apply for VAGST registration, thereby assuming VAT obligations while remaining exempt from income tax and stamp duty. Alternatively, the IC may pass a special resolution to convert to a domestic company, which would subject it to ordinary territorial taxation; no de minimis or hybrid election is available prior to such conversion.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover in Samoa (compulsory VAGST registration); voluntary registration permitted below threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supply of services to a non-resident recipient is zero-rated under the VAGST Act 2008 where the benefit of the services is wholly received outside Samoa, including PCT patent prosecution, international trademark registration, and related IP advisory services. Goods physically exported from Samoa are also zero-rated. A VAGST-registered IC may claim zero-rating only upon retaining evidence of the foreign recipient status, offshore service performance, and direct connection to the export supply; wholly exempt ICs that are not VAGST-registered cannot claim refunds because no output VAGST is chargeable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Samoan commercial bank
  • Executed service agreement showing the offshore recipient and scope of IP prosecution work
  • Detailed tax invoice referencing VAGST zero-rating

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in Samoa. VAGST reporting is periodic through the MfR online filing portal; taxpayers must retain paper or electronic invoices for audit purposes, but invoices need no government pre-approval or digital stamp.

The MfR can audit zero-rated claims for up to 5 years from the end of the relevant tax period. A fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claim triggers a retroactive assessment at 15% VAGST, an additional penalty generally up to 150% of the tax shortfall, overdue interest at market rates compounded, and disqualification from the voluntary registration regime. Criminal prosecution is possible under the VAGST Act 2008 for intentional evasion, with fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years for responsible officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is freely convertible for current-account transactions such as patent filing fees, associate compensation, and intellectual-property office disbursements, subject to the customary declaration routed through an authorized dealer. Capital-account convertibility is liberalized for non-residents. A Samoa International Company (IC) is classified as non-resident for exchange-control purposes; therefore inward equity contributions, dividends, return of capital, and offshore fee receipts are remittable without CBS prior approval and without mandatory WST conversion. The IC is tax-exempt under the International Companies Act 1987, with no withholding tax on outbound dividends or service fees, and no capital-account surplus restriction. The practical friction is not legal convertibility but correspondent-bank de-risking: WST-denominated transactions above the CBS reporting threshold (as prescribed from time to time) require confirmation documentation through the presenting authorized dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa's Exchange Control Regulations 1980 oblige resident traders to repatriate and surrender export and service proceeds to an authorized dealer within six months of export. A Samoa IC, being non-resident for exchange control, is exempt from surrender and conversion mandates: patent-prosecution fees and royalty receipts may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, HKD, SGD, or NZD accounts, whether held at a CBS-licensed bank or an offshore bank, with no obligation to convert into WST.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), located at Apia, acting as the sole Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1977 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1980. CBS licenses and supervises all authorized foreign-exchange dealers (commercial banks) under the Financial Institutions Act 1996 and administers the national AML/CFT framework established by the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act 2007.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route is a segregated USD share-capital wire from the UBO's own bank account via SWIFT MT103 to the IC's escrow or operational account at ANZ Bank Samoa, BSP Samoa, or the IC's authorized offshore banking hub (e.g., DBS Hong Kong or OCBC Singapore). Remittance Information (Field 70) must state 'WS_IC SHARE CAPITAL — INTERNATIONAL COMPANIES ACT 1987 — [CIN]'; the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity Investment) is quoted in Field 72, while subsequent fee receipts use 'SERV'. The verification bundle comprises the board resolution, share certificate, registered-agent certificate of incumbency, and CRS/FATCA self-certification. The receiving authorized dealer files any CBS-prescribed monthly exchange-control return if the flow exceeds the applicable reporting threshold; the IC's non-resident status removes CBS pre-approval. No WST conversion is performed; injected capital remains in USD and is deployed solely toward patent-office disbursements and associate fees.Clearing rail of first resort is the Official_Patent_Office_Wire: SWIFT MT103 in USD to WIPO's designated banking channel at UBS Switzerland AG, Geneva (SWIFT UBSWCHZH80A), quoting the WIPO USD account and the PCT application number in Field 70; WIPO additionally accepts settlement initiated through the e-PCT system. USPTO fees are cleared by ACH/Pay.gov electronic funds transfer rather than SWIFT. The second rail is Wise for foreign-associate firms and smaller IP offices, executing via local clearing systems (Faster Payments, SEPA, US ACH) with purpose code 'SERV' and invoice references. Commercial letters of credit are not recommended: LC mechanics add documentation risk, delay, and cost disproportionate to fee-for-service remittances typically below USD 100,000. Treasury discipline requires operational accounts held in USD or EUR matching WIPO fee currency, with residual WST balances swept to USD monthly through the authorized dealer's CBS-confirmed conversion.Outright USD/EUR forward contract with an authorized dealer (ANZ Bank Samoa or BSP Samoa), maximum 12-month tenor, requiring a documented underlying current-account receivable or payable exposure such as committed EUR-denominated WIPO annuity obligations; plain-vanilla deliverable structure only.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) on WST referenced to the CBS currency basket: permissible in principle under the Exchange Control Act 1977, but not actively quoted by CBS-authorized dealers; practicability is Low, and the IC should instead execute a cross-currency forward (EUR/USD or GBP/USD) in the jurisdiction of the offshore funding bank.Currency swap between USD and EUR executed with the offshore funding bank (e.g., DBS, OCBC, or ANZ New Zealand), permissible for matching long-horizon EUR-denominated euro-PCT annuity streams and requiring full ISDA/CSA collateral documentation and a demonstrated hedge-effectiveness memorandum.Speculative FX averaging or leveraged derivative contracts are not permissible; the IC's treasury policy restricts hedging to named patent-office invoices and committed associate-fee liabilities.

Within 6 months after the end of the fiscal year (standard fiscal year ends 31 December; non-resident ICs may elect an alternate fiscal year with MfR approval). Filing is mandatory even for ICs benefiting from the 0% tax-exempt regime to evidence non-resident status.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 10,000,000 (approx. USD 3.5 million) in annual gross income from related-party cross-border transactions; a Local File is required when related-party transactions exceed WST 5,000,000. Master File required when the multinational group’s consolidated revenue exceeds WST 50,000,000. An International Company engaged in IP patent prosecution and trademark registration service must maintain contemporaneous documentation where the remuneration paid to affiliates (management fees, service fees, royalty streams) falls within these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: The International Business Act 2022 (IBA 2022) and the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2020 require a licensed International Company carrying on a relevant activity — including intellectual property holding, licensing and ancillary IP service activity — to demonstrate substantial economic presence in Samoa. The company must maintain at least one physical office in Samoa, employ qualified local staff (directors and/or officers) engaged in the core business, and undertake core income-generating activities (e.g., contract negotiation, patent prosecution management, invoicing and record-keeping) in Samoa, or satisfy an outsourcing arrangement through a Samoan licensed service provider. An IC claiming tax exemption under the 0% regime but lacking local substance is treated as a tax-resident defaulting entity and may be reclassified as taxable at 27% from the date of incorporation, plus penalties and interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 200% of the tax evaded plus interest at 12% per annum, and criminal fine up to WST 500,000 (or double the tax evaded, whichever is greater) upon conviction under the Tax Administration Act 2021.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties under the Corporate Veil Piercing provisions of the Companies Act 2013 and Tax Administration Act 2021. Directors face travel bans, asset freezing orders, and a maximum term of 10 years’ imprisonment where fraud or deliberate concealment is proven.
Shell Company (Economic Substance Failure)

Statutory sanction: Under Regulation 9 of the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2020, the Registrar may strike the company off the International Companies Register, impose an administrative fine of WST 50,000, and reassess all prior years’ income at 27% with a penalty of 100% of the additional tax. The company may also be blacklisted and banned from licensing in Samoa.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly caused or permitted the IC to operate without economic substance are personally liable for the reassessed tax and fines. The court may impose disqualification from acting as directors in Samoa for 10 years, travel bans, and a fine of WST 250,000 or imprisonment for up to 7 years.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 200,000 under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2021; the Registrar may suspend the IC’s license, the patent agency license may be revoked, and any transaction with a sanctioned party results in asset seizure and a criminal fine of WST 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines of WST 100,000 each, up to 5 years’ imprisonment, and cancellation of their professional licenses. Beneficial owners who aid concealment are jointly liable and may face extradition under Samoan mutual legal assistance treaties.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment by MfR increasing taxable income to the arm’s-length level plus a penalty of 40% of the additional tax and interest; failure to produce documentation attracts a fixed penalty of WST 25,000 per document per year. The pay-as-you-earn (PAYE) and VAT obligations may also be reassessed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved non-arm’s-length pricing may be declared personal guarantors of the adjusted tax, with a court order for personal restitution of up to 3 years’ profit shifted, a travel ban, and disqualification as a director for 5 years. Criminal prosecution may follow where the mispricing constitutes a perjury in signed statutory declarations.
