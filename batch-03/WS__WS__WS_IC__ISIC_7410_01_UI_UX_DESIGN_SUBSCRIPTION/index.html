Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of Samoa (Matagaluega o Tupe Maua), Apia, Samoa; operating the Samoa Taxpayer Registration System and the Ministry for Revenue e-Services portal for taxpayer registration, filing, and compliance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity first incorporates an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 through the Samoa International Financial Services Centre (SIFSC) and the Registrar of Companies. Upon incorporation, the IC, acting through its licensed registered agent in Samoa, applies to the Ministry for Revenue for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) using the prescribed registration forms and supporting documents, which include the certificate of incorporation, charter and memorandum/articles of association, particulars of directors and shareholders, the registered agent's licence, and a declaration of offshore business activity. Registration is submitted via the Ministry's e-Services portal or a district revenue office; upon verification a TIN certificate is issued, and the entity is classified in the revenue system as a tax-exempt international company. The same TIN is used for all direct and indirect tax interactions, including any VAGST registration if the IC later makes taxable supplies in Samoa.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign entity first incorporates an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 through the Samoa International Financial Services Centre (SIFSC) and the Registrar of Companies. Upon incorporation, the IC, acting through its licensed registered agent in Samoa, applies to the Ministry for Revenue for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) using the prescribed registration forms and supporting documents, which include the certificate of incorporation, charter and memorandum/articles of association, particulars of directors and shareholders, the registered agent's licence, and a declaration of offshore business activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa does not provide a United States-style check-the-box election, nor does it maintain hybrid mismatch election regimes applicable to international companies. Under the International Companies Act 1987, an IC is exempt by default from Samoan income tax and from Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST) on offshore income and offshore transactions. The only statutory 'election' in practice is the voluntary surrender of International Company status, after which the entity becomes an ordinary domestic company under the Companies Act 2006 and falls within the normal Samoan tax net; no hybrid mismatch or pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 150,000 annual taxable turnover (compulsory registration threshold under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 1991; voluntary registration may be permitted below the threshold, and offshore supplies by exempt international companies do not count toward the threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (VAGST at 0%) applies to: (a) goods physically exported from Samoa under a customs export declaration; (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is not present in Samoa at the time of supply; and (c) certain international transport, freight, and directly related services. For the target International Company supplying UI/UX design-as-a-service subscriptions to non-resident clients, the offshore digital services qualify for zero-rating where the supplier has a Samoa VAGST registration and the place-of-supply rule is satisfied, or fall wholly outside Samoan VAGST where no Samoan nexus is established. International companies are presumptively exempt for offshore business activities, and a formal zero-rating claim is required only where the IC also makes taxable supplies in Samoa or elects to hold a VAGST registration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs single-entry declaration / CusDec equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (carrier-issued proof of physical export, where goods are involved)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 evidence of payment received from the non-resident customer
  • Commercial Invoice and VAGST Tax Invoice stating the zero-rated nature of the supply
  • Service Agreement / subscription contract evidencing the non-resident recipient and offshore place of supply
  • Client onboarding records confirming non-resident status and absence from Samoa at time of supply

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or government-authorised digital stamping regime is currently in force in Samoa. A VAGST-registered supplier must issue a compliant tax invoice within 28 days of supply, retain source records for audit, and file periodic VAGST returns through the Ministry for Revenue e-Services portal. API-based pre-clearance or real-time tax authority validation of invoices has not been implemented for the standard business environment.

The Ministry for Revenue may reassess zero-rated or exempt offshore supplies within the statutory limitation period and claw back VAGST together with late payment penalties (commonly 2% per month) and interest where the export trail is incomplete, the recipient is found to be Samoan-resident, or the place-of-supply test is failed. Claims supported by false documentation attract civil shortfall penalties of up to 150% of the tax shortfall for fraudulent or reckless behaviour, additional penalty-unit fines for failure to keep records under the Tax Administration Act 2012, and, where prosecuted, criminal fines and terms of imprisonment as prescribed for tax evasion. An International Company that breaches the offshore-declaration conditions also risks revocation of its SIFSC/international status and loss of the associated tax-exempt privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a managed and only partially convertible currency. Current-account payments for trade in services are processed by authorized dealers on sight of underlying invoices and tax clearances, while capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend remittances) require CBS consent when routed through the onshore WST clearing system. The WS_IC, as a non-resident International Company earning design-subscription revenue abroad, can neutralize practical convertibility hurdles by holding perpetual USD receivables in an approved foreign-currency account at BSP Samoa and by avoiding WST conversion; profit remittance is then executed directly in USD via MT103 without CBS clearance, supported by the statutory income-tax exemption in the International Companies Act. Any incidental WST conversion for local expenses requires service invoices and an authorized-dealer declaration, with an approval lead time of roughly 3 to 7 business days.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, export receipts must generally be offered to an authorized dealer and converted into WST within one month of receipt unless CBS grants foreign-currency retention; current CBS practice permits retention of proceeds denominated in the same foreign currency as the underlying invoiced receivable, subject to periodic extension, and no mandatory percentage conversion applies to pure service exporters such as digital design subscription agencies. The surrender obligation is functionally inapplicable to the WS_IC because it is non-resident, tax-exempt, and earns foreign-source income settled entirely outside Samoa; retained USD collections never enter the onshore WST interbank market, and the entity should hold a standing retention exemption filed by its registered agent, supported by an annual non-resident attestation covering offshore board management, absence of local payroll, and offshore settlement patterns.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, which administers the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973; the CBS licenses authorized dealers (commercial banks) for all onshore foreign-exchange transactions and retains approval authority over capital-account movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with strict legal conditioning. Stripe does not maintain a Samoa-licensed settlement entity, and Wise Business does not offer WST-denominated local clearing, so both rails are usable only as auxiliary collection and payout layers. The WS_IC should contract Stripe_Billing through a Merchant of Record in a Stripe-supported jurisdiction (US, IE, or SG) acting as a disclosed agent, and Wise should be limited to sub-USD 5,000 contractor disbursements where the beneficiary holds a local payment rail. The EMI account must be in the name of the WS_IC, operated as a T+1 auto-sweep to the BSP Samoa USD operating account, staffed with no local employees, and restricted to non-Samoan-source counterparties to avoid creating a permanent establishment or foreign tax-residency nexus. Direct exclusive reliance on an offshore EMI as the principal depository is not viable because it would sever the correspondent clearing chain, trigger CRS/FATCA filing exposure in the EMI jurisdiction, and impair the registered agent's economic-substance attestation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory local-file threshold specific to International Companies has been gazetted; the arm's length standard applies to all related-party cross-border transactions. Prudent administrative threshold: maintain a contemporaneous Local File where annual related-party transactions exceed WST 1,000,000, and prepare a Master File where the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds WST 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies Act 2007, as amended by Samoa's economic substance legislation, requires every IC to maintain a registered agent, registered office, accounting records, and an adequate economic presence appropriate to its activities. ICs carrying on enumerated 'relevant activities' — including banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing, leasing, headquarters, IP holding, distribution, and service centres — must demonstrate real substance in Samoa through physical premises, locally engaged employees, and core decision-making in Samoa. A pure UI/UX design subscription agency (ISIC 7410 / NAICS 541430) with foreign clients and no Samoan-source income is not an enumerated relevant activity; however, the IC must avoid holding passive IP or intangibles that could be recharacterized as an IP holding business and must maintain tangible commercial substance commensurate with its design-as-a-service functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing (Income Tax Act 2012)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus statutory interest; criminal liability: fine up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; forfeiture of proceeds under asset recovery powers.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize false filings are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax, penalties, and interest; the corporate veil is pierced for knowing participation; the court may impose personal bankruptcy, departure prohibition orders, and extradition for serious tax crime.
Shell Company Abuse and Sham Substance (General Anti-Avoidance Rule and International Companies Act 2007)

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the arrangement under Samoa's general anti-avoidance rule, voiding the IC tax exemption; reassessment of profits at 27% with penalties up to 200%; strike-off of the IC; fines for false declarations under the International Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents who certify false substance declarations are personally liable for the resulting tax liability, restitution of diverted profits, disqualification as company officers, and potential imprisonment for fraud; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation.
AML/CFT and Financial Sanctions Breach (Samoa AML/CFT Act 2018 and International Companies Act Beneficial Ownership Regime)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties up to WST 500,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; freezing of corporate assets; revocation of the IC registration; prohibition from using the Samoan financial system; regulatory fines against the registered agent.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and compliance personnel who knowingly participate, facilitate, or fail to report are personally subject to criminal conviction, confiscation of benefits, travel bans, and extradition risk; the corporate veil is disregarded for deliberate AML violations.
Annual Return and Annual Fee Non-Compliance; Trading While Struck Off (International Companies Act 2007)

Statutory sanction: Compounding late fees, automatic strike-off from the Register, restoration fee of WST 300 plus all arrears; trading while struck off is a statutory offence attracting fines; each transaction in a struck-off IC is invalid for tax exemption purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors who trade through a struck-off IC are jointly and severally liable for all debts and contractual obligations incurred during the struck-off period; the court may order restitution to third-party creditors, impose disqualification orders, and issue travel bans to compel restitution.
