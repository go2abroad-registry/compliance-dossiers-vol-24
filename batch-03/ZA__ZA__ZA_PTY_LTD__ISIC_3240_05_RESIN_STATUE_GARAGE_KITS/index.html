Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; customs administration through SARS Customs EDI (SAD500 declarations).

E-government filing portal

The Pty Ltd obtains an Income Tax Reference Number automatically at CIPC incorporation, but must finalise registration via eFiling by submitting the company's registered MOI, CIPC Certificate of Incorporation (CoR 14.3), identity documents of directors and beneficial owners (non-residents provide apostilled police clearance and passports), registered office proof, and financial year-end election. VAT vendor registration via VAT101 is compulsory once taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period (likely due to export sales), and voluntary from ZAR 50,000. Foreign shareholders must complete SARS IT144 limited-interest declarations, and a SARS-recognised local representative taxpayer must be appointed under s157 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 (TAA).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — South Africa-resident Pty Ltd taxed on worldwide income at 27% corporate income tax (s5 Income Tax Act 58 of 1962); dividends subject to 20% dividend withholding tax unless treaty-reduced. No pass-through or territorial regime is available; no SEZ or tax-exempt-zone status applies to this business.

Available election forms

No check-the-box equivalent exists in South African law. Available statutory elections: (i) Small Business Corporation graduated CIT election under s12E (eligible only if all shareholders are natural persons and turnover is below ZAR 20 million); (ii) voluntary VAT vendor registration under s23(4) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991; (iii) choice of financial year-end; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under Part IIA TAA apply mandatorily with ordering rules rather than elections; (v) binding private ruling from SARS for interpretation certainty; (vi) s11D R&D tax allowance election (not applicable to routine polyresin casting).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months (s23(1)); voluntary registration: ZAR 50,000 rolling 12-month threshold (s23(4)); no threshold for non-resident suppliers of electronic services (s23(5)).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies under s11(1)(a) VAT Act where movable goods (resin statues) are supplied to a non-resident who is not a VAT vendor and the goods are physically exported within 90 days of the time of supply. Delivery to a local clearing agent for export, or a direct export by the vendor, also qualifies if SARS-prescribed documentation is retained. Input VAT on resin, moulding materials, packaging, and logistics is fully creditable, and excess input VAT is refundable through VAT201/VAT264 claims subject to verification of export evidence. Freight, insurance, and cross-border movement services directly linked to exports are zero-rated under s11(2)(u); services rendered to non-residents for use wholly outside South Africa are zero-rated under s11(2)(l).

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration (electronic SARS Customs EDI acknowledgment and exit-date stamp)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (carrier-issued proof of physical departure from South Africa)
  • Commercial invoice (seller to non-resident buyer evidencing price, Incoterms, and 0% rate)
  • Packing list and container/cargo tally
  • Bank Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds through an authorised dealer
  • Exchange Control Authorisation / export permit record (Currency and Exchanges Manual D.1.3)
  • Warehouse, terminal, or port release note confirming shipment departure

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital fiscal-signature mandate is currently enacted; SARS issued an e-invoicing policy paper in 2023 proposing a future phased clearance-based model. Existing obligations: all corporate vendors must file VAT201 returns and pay electronically via eFiling/e@syFile; export consignments are cleared through SARS Customs EDI (SAD500) with automated risk profiling and digital release at the port of exit; tax invoices must satisfy s20 VAT Act content requirements (JSON/PDF permitted without SARS pre-approval).

SARS matches SAD500 customs data, bank remittance records, and VAT201 returns and routinely audits export zero-rating claims. If the documentation bundle is incomplete or not retained for five years, SARS deems the supply taxable at 15% and assesses the output VAT. For incorrect zero-rating without fraudulent intent, s60(2) VAT Act imposes additional tax equal to the VAT that would have been chargeable at the standard rate (15/115 of the price); non-payment triggers TAA understatement penalties ranging from 50% (substantial understatement) to 150% (intentional tax evasion) of the shortfall, plus compounding interest under ss187–190 TAA (repo rate plus 9% per annum). Fraudulent export claims — false SAD500 filings, fabricated bills of lading, or circular remittances — expose directors to criminal prosecution under s58 VAT Act and ss234–235 TAA carrying fines and/or imprisonment up to 5 years for intentional evasion, alongside SARS powers to suspend refunds, freeze bank accounts, and recover personally from directors under wilful-default doctrines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is a managed-float currency with full current-account convertibility for trade, services, dividends, interest and royalties, administered by Authorised Dealers against documentary proof. Capital-account inflows (equity and shareholder loans) are freely convertible once reported on the inward investment declaration (Form G), which is the critical pre-requisite for any future outward remittance of capital, dividends, interest or loan repayments. The principal profit-remittance hurdles are NOT conversion limits but: (i) absence of a completed Form G at entry; (ii) SARS tax clearance and Dividends Tax at 20% (treaty-reduced where applicable); and (iii) FICA-verified banking records evidencing the source of non-resident funds. Non-resident wholly-owned ownership of a (Pty) Ltd is permitted without exchange-control approval at incorporation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: export proceeds must be received in the Republic within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless FinSurv grants an extension through the Authorised Dealer. Mandatory conversion: there is no percentage quota payable to the SARB, but the effective surrender ratio is 100% - all export proceeds received in convertible currency must be sold to the Authorised Dealer for ZAR immediately upon receipt, unless the exporter holds an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA), in which case the proceeds may remain in foreign currency for a maximum of 180 days after shipment, after which conversion to ZAR is compulsory. Retention beyond 180 days, or any offshore accumulation of export receipts outside an Authorised Dealer FCA, constitutes an exchange-control contravention. For the ISIC 3240 e-commerce sales channel, each export must be recorded on the electronic e-form (export confirmation) with SARS Customs SAD500 entries to evidence shipment, value and date.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933; daily administration is delegated to Authorised Dealers in foreign exchange (licensed commercial banks) through the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low - Restricted. South African exchange-control rules require all foreign-currency receipts of a resident company to be routed to, and held with, an Authorised Dealer. Offshore EMIs without a South African banking licence (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Mercury, Currencycloud) cannot legally serve as the primary treasury or settlement account for ZA_PTY_LTD; holding corporate funds offshore with such providers breaches the Currency and Exchanges Act. They may function only as upstream card-acquiring aggregates where settlement is immediately swept in ZAR to the local operational account, with full transaction-level reporting to the bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for companies with years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax on dividends paid by a South African resident company, subject to statutory exemptions and treaty relief. A dividend to a South African resident company may qualify for an exemption if the statutory requirements are met. Certain other exempt recipients and transactions may qualify under the Income Tax Act.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ITR14 is generally due within 12 months after the company's financial year-end. The first provisional tax payment is generally due six months after the commencement of the year of assessment, and the second is due by the last day of the year of assessment. A voluntary third provisional payment may generally be made within six months after year-end. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: South Africa has no general safe-harbour threshold below which section 31 does not apply. Cross-border transactions between connected persons must be arm's length regardless of amount. A formal Local File and Master File obligation generally arises where the applicable South African threshold and multinational-group conditions are met, commonly including aggregate potentially affected transactions exceeding R100 million for the relevant period. A Country-by-Country Report generally applies to qualifying multinational enterprise groups meeting the prescribed consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly R10 billion for South African reporting purposes. Records may be required below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance remains relevant through tax residence, permanent-establishment analysis, beneficial ownership, treaty anti-abuse provisions, the general anti-avoidance rules, controlled foreign company rules, section 31 transfer pricing and the requirement that deductible expenditure be incurred in the production of income. SARS may challenge arrangements lacking commercial purpose, personnel, decision-making, operational capacity, risk control or evidence of services actually performed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties, understatement penalties of up to 200% of the tax shortfall, statutory interest, recovery of tax and potential criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act. Conviction may result in a fine or imprisonment, with the applicable maximum depending on the specific offense.

Director personal liability: Directors and the public officer may face criminal liability for personal participation, authorization or knowing assistance. Civil recovery is ordinarily against the company, but personal liability may arise under tax, company, fiduciary-duty, fraud, reckless-trading or other applicable laws. Imprisonment is possible for convicted individuals; a travel ban is not an automatic tax sanction but may arise from a court order or immigration process in appropriate proceedings.
Failure to Account for Employees' Tax or Other Collected Taxes

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid amount, interest, administrative penalties, understatement penalties and possible criminal prosecution. SARS may pursue responsible persons where the statutory requirements for personal recovery or offense liability are satisfied.

Director personal liability: A director, public officer or responsible representative may incur personal exposure for wilful or negligent conduct, breach of fiduciary duty, fraud, reckless trading or statutory contraventions. Imprisonment may follow conviction; personal liability is fact-dependent and not automatic solely because the person is a director.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Profit Diversion

Statutory sanction: SARS may make a section 31 primary or corresponding adjustment, impose additional taxable income, interest and understatement penalties, and impose documentation-related compliance consequences. Withholding-tax exposure may arise on interest, royalties, dividends or service payments.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure for knowingly approving sham transactions, fraud, reckless trading or breach of fiduciary duties. The company remains the primary taxpayer; veil piercing requires an applicable legal basis and is not automatic.
Shell Company, Sham Transaction or General Anti-Avoidance

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply the general anti-avoidance rules, disregard or recharacterize the arrangement, deny deductions or treaty benefits, assess additional tax and impose interest and understatement penalties. Fraudulent conduct may result in criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil, regulatory or criminal exposure if they knowingly participate in an abusive arrangement, make false declarations or breach fiduciary duties. Imprisonment is possible on conviction. A travel ban requires a separate lawful basis and is not an automatic consequence of a GAAR assessment.
Companies Act Reckless or Fraudulent Trading

Statutory sanction: Court orders may include compensation, repayment, disqualification or other relief. The Companies Act may impose criminal sanctions for specified contraventions, and common-law fraud or insolvency consequences may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may become personally liable for company debts or losses where statutory or common-law requirements are met, particularly for reckless or fraudulent conduct. Civil restitution and disqualification are possible; imprisonment depends on the specific offense and conviction.
Financial Intelligence Centre Act and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanctions may include a reprimand, remediation direction, restriction, suspension or withdrawal of authorization where relevant, and substantial monetary penalties. Criminal prosecution may apply to intentional or knowing statutory violations.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, compliance officers and employees may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation or failure to comply. The entity and individuals may face fines and imprisonment under the applicable FIC Act provisions. Asset restraint or forfeiture may occur under proceeds-of-crime legislation; travel restrictions require a separate lawful order.
Prohibited Foreign Exchange or Profit-Repatriation Conduct

Statutory sanction: The South African Reserve Bank or authorized dealer may require corrective action, block or reverse transactions, impose exchange-control consequences and refer conduct for prosecution. SARS may assess withholding tax, additional income tax, interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unauthorized distributions, breach of fiduciary duties, tax offenses, fraud or unlawful financial assistance. Civil recovery and restitution are possible; imprisonment depends on the offense and conviction.
Recordkeeping and Obstruction of SARS

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties, adverse audit adjustments, denial of deductions, understatement penalties, interest and criminal sanctions for obstruction or failure to comply with a lawful request.

Director personal liability: The responsible representative and persons who destroy, falsify or conceal records may face personal criminal exposure and civil consequences. Imprisonment is possible on conviction; a travel ban is not automatic and requires separate legal authority.
