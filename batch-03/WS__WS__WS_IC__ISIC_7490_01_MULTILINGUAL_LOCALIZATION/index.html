Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Ministry of Revenue; official e-services portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws (SRA e-Tax Portal for TIN registration, VAGST filings, and certificates).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Samoa International Company (IC) is incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 with the Registrar of International Companies at the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). SIFA transmits incorporation data to the SRA for automatic issuance of a Tax Identification Number (TIN). Alternatively, the IC may file a direct TIN application form (Form TIN-01) with the SRA, submitting certified copies of the Certificate of Incorporation, constitutional documents, directors'/shareholders' register, and a registered agent's undertaking in Samoa. The TIN is typically issued within 2-5 business days. The IC is not required to obtain a VAGST registration number if its activities are confined to offshore commercial activity outside Samoa; however, local banking, payroll, or administrative presence may require TIN enrollment.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone. The IC receives statutory exemption from Samoa income tax, income tax on distributions, VAGST, and stamp duty in respect of all international business activities, contracts, and transactions entered into outside Samoa. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. The IC receives statutory exemption from Samoa income tax, income tax on distributions, VAGST, and stamp duty in respect of all international business activities, contracts, and transactions entered into outside Samoa. Such exemption applies as long as the IC does not carry on business with Samoan residents or own Samoan land, and no beneficial interest in the IC is held by a Samoan resident.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' entity classification election exists in Samoa (no pass-through election into the ordinary tax regime is permitted for an IC). Elections are limited to (i) the constitutional election to issue shares with or without par value, (ii) dividend declaration elections under the International Companies Act which are tax-exempt, and (iii) the election to apply Samoa's exchange control or the International Companies (Foreign Exchange) Exemption order for foreign currency transactions; hybrid mismatch treatments under the Tax Administration Act 2012 are unavailable to ICs because the entity is wholly tax-transparent in Samoa by virtue of its statutory exemption, so no anti-hybrid or CFC inclusion arises at the Samoan domestic level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 per annum (registration is mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds WST 100,000). An International Company carrying on exclusively offshore business in multilingual localization is statutorily exempt and outside the VAGST registration net; if the IC were to make any taxable supplies within Samoa (e.g., local studio rental or domestic post-production), the WST 100,000 threshold would apply and VAGST would be chargeable on that domestic portion only.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of services are zero-rated under Section 7 and Schedule 2 of the VAGST Act 1998 where the services are supplied to a recipient who is a non-resident, are exported or consumed outside Samoa, and the supplier retains documentary evidence of the offshore delivery. Multilingual translation, localization, dubbing, and media transcription services rendered to offshore clients (non-resident agencies, studios, or content platforms) qualify for 0% VAT provided the IC does not itself perform the work from a physical Samoan studio used for domestic supplies and does not supply to a Samosan resident. Services must not be in connection with land or goods situated in Samoa at the time of supply. The IC should obtain a written declaration from the client confirming non-resident status and offshore end-use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form CED-1) filed via the Samoa Customs electronic single window for any physical deliverable (e.g., mastered media, subtitling files on physical media)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export (where goods/media are shipped internationally)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Conversion Record issued by a licensed Samoan bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Signed international service contract between the IC and the non-resident client
  • Digital delivery evidence (upload/download logs, e-mail transmittals, server log extracts, content delivery network reports) for fully electronic localization deliverables
  • Client acceptance certificate or end-client confirmation of offshore use of the localized media

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based pre-clearance regime is currently enacted in Samoa. The SRA operates a post-audit compliance model; registered persons must issue tax invoices in a prescribed format containing supplier name/TIN, buyer details, description, supplier and buyer VAGST registration numbers, date, consideration, and VAGST amount (shown separately). E-invoicing is voluntary, but the SRA encourages electronic records; non-resident IC exporters should maintain all invoices, delivery dockets, and digital service delivery logs electronically to substantiate zero-rated export treatment. The SRA may require electronic filing of VAGST returns and attachment of invoice summaries; no third-party clearance provider is mandated.

The SRA conducts risk-based audits of zero-rated export claims for translation and digital media services, scrutinizing the nexus between the contracted service, the offshore recipient, and the remittance trail. Under the Tax Administration Act 2012, incorrect zero-rating claims attract a shortfall penalty of 25% for failure to take reasonable care, 75% for reckless behavior, 150% for intentional or fraudulent evasion, plus a late-payment penalty of 2% per month (capped at 150% of the unpaid VAGST). The Commissioner may retrospectively claw back refunds and adjust assessments within four years of the assessment date (unlimited in cases of fraud or willful misrepresentation). Criminal prosecution for tax evasion carries fines up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to seven years; directors, officers, and the registered agent are jointly and severally liable where the exporter knowingly supplies false or misleading export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is convertible for bona fide current-account items through Authorized Dealers, but the capital account is regulated: outward capital transfers by residents require Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) approval. A Samoa International Company (IC) is classified as non-resident for exchange-control purposes and may hold foreign-currency accounts without surrender obligations; however, any WST-denominated transaction touching a Samoa resident (e.g., local contractors or onshore overhead paid into Tala accounts) is exchange-controlled and requires a CBS declaration or prior approval. Profit remittance by the IC is free of income tax and without non-resident withholding, but must be executed through the IC's designated foreign-currency account to avoid onshore conversion constraints.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must collect full invoice proceeds within the six-month statutory window prescribed under the Exchange Control Act 1974 and credit them to an Authorized Dealer; absent a CBS retention limit, proceeds must be converted into WST. The IC, as a non-resident exporter of professional localization services, is exempt from the surrender and conversion obligation; invoiced USD/EUR/AUD receipts for translation and media-dubbing services may be retained indefinitely in its approved foreign-currency account, with the bank reporting year-end balances to the CBS.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; exercises exchange control under the Exchange Control Act 1974 and Exchange Control Regulations 1985, with delegated supervision of Authorized Dealers and the Financial Intelligence Unit for AML/CFT matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

LOW-MEDIUM feasibility rating for Wise B2B and Payoneer. Neither entity is licensed by the Central Bank of Samoa; they operate as offshore e-money and payout aggregators. Legal constraints: (i) the IC may lawfully hold offshore e-money balances; (ii) repatriation to a Samoa FCA is treated as an inbound remittance requiring source-of-funds evidence; (iii) EMIs cannot directly debit a local WST clearing account, so contractor payments must be made to offshore recipients; (iv) the IC should maintain a licensed Authorized Dealer account as the master treasury vault and use EMI rails only for small-batch outbound contractor payouts.

0% for dividends paid by an International Company to non-resident shareholders (IC dividends are outside the Samoan WHT net); domestic statutory rate of 15% applies only where the paying company derives Samoan-source income

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 0 - Samoa has no codified Local File or Master File thresholds; contemporaneous benchmarking is nonetheless recommended for any related-party cross-border flows exceeding WST 1,000,000 equivalent, and the SRA may request documentation on audit under the general anti-avoidance provisions

Economic substance scope: Applies through the International Companies Act 2009 s.219 and MCIL administrative policy: the IC must not carry on business in Samoa and must demonstrate real economic substance in its outside-Samoa operations. For a localization agency this means foreign-office premises, foreign employed linguists/dubbing staff, foreign-controlled bank accounts (Wise/Payoneer), and client-facing substance overseas. Failure to maintain the declaration of non-domestic business triggers withdrawal of exempt status, full 27% taxation, and adverse treatment as a tax haven shell

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Domestic Business Concealment

Statutory sanction: Retroactive assessment for up to 5 open years; 150% evasion penalty on the tax shortfall; monthly interest at 1.5%; deregistration of the IC; publication of the company and directors on the SRA defaulters list

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil restitution for taxes and penalties; travel bans and exit controls under the Tax Administration Act; imprisonment up to 7 years for wilful evasion; disqualification as directors of any Samoa company
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Strike-off and forfeiture of assets to the Crown upon deregistration; loss of limited-liability protection; fines up to WST 100,000 under the International Companies Act 2009; refusal of the annual renewal

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil for all company debts; directors liable to creditors for contracts entered during the non-compliant period; exclusion from future Samoan licence schemes; travel bans pending investigation
AML/CFT Violations by Vehicle Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 500,000 for the corporate body; freezing orders over all bank and payment-rail balances; mandatory cancellation of the IC registration; reporting to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent are jointly and severally liable for AML penalties if involved in the breach; imprisonment up to 10 years for money laundering; restraint orders and travel bans during the investigation
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Reassessment on arms's length terms; a 25% substantial-valuation penalty on any TP adjustment exceeding WST 50,000; denial of interest deductions for excessive debt; potential criminal referral if conduct is fraudulent

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the transfer pricing documentation face joint liability for the deficiency if the IC is struck off; civil claims by minority shareholders; professional disqualification in Samoa and treaty-partner jurisdictions
