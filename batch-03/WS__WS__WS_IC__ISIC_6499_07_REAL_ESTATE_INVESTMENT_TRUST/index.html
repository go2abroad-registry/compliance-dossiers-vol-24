Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA) under the Ministry for Revenue, Government of Samoa; online portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity not carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa is not generally required to obtain a TIN or VAGST ID. An International Company (IC) may obtain a Certificate of Tax Exemption from the Samoa Revenue Authority by submitting Form EX-01, the certificate of incorporation, registered office certificate, and a statutory declaration confirming non-resident and non-domestic business activities. If the IC were to engage in any taxable activity in Samoa (which its constitutive statute ordinarily prohibits), it must file Form TIN-01 with the SRA and will receive a TIN within 5 business days; VAGST registration is triggered once the turnover threshold is met.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: An International Company registered under Samoa's International Companies Act 2001 is automatically exempt from Samoan income tax, Value Added Goods and Services Tax (VAGST), and stamp duty on all activities, provided it does not carry on business with Samoan residents, own Samoan land, or otherwise engage in domestic taxable activities.

Available election forms

Samoa does not operate a check-the-box or fiscal classification election. An IC is tax-exempt by default; it may only become subject to ordinary taxation by converting into a domestic company under Samoa's Companies Act, which requires formal deregistration as an IC and approval from the Registrar of International Companies and the Ministry for Revenue. No hybrid mismatch elections are available under Samoan tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 50,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period (mandatory VAGST registration threshold for persons carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated where the goods are actually exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply and the supplier retains proof of export. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a person who is outside Samoa and who is not present in Samoa at the time of supply, as well as directly associated with exported goods; international transport services and supplies under approved incentive schemes are also eligible. The supplier must maintain contemporaneous commercial and shipping documentation demonstrating the goods left Samoa or the service was provided to a non-resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

There is no current statutory requirement for real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping. Taxpayers must issue serially numbered tax invoices containing the supplier's VAGST registration number, date, description, GST amount, and total consideration. Electronic invoicing may be used voluntarily, but the Samoa Revenue Authority does not operate a government clearance portal.

The Samoa Revenue Authority may retrospectively claw back zero-rated VAGST where export substantiation is incomplete or false, and may impose civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded plus monthly interest at 1% of the outstanding amount. Knowingly making a false declaration to obtain zero-rating or a refund is a criminal offence punishable by a fine not exceeding SAT 10,000 and/or up to three years' imprisonment. Records must be retained for a minimum of five years from the later of supply date or export date. Anti-money laundering agencies may separately enforce record-keeping and foreign exchange-related requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains a managed-float national currency (WST, ISO 4217 code 604). Current-account convertibility is substantially liberalized: an authorized dealer may pay genuine trade, service, and income remittances against a documented underlying transaction without prior Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) approval. Capital-account convertibility remains restricted: inbound equity subscriptions, loan drawdowns, capital repatriation, and portfolio investments must be processed through a CBS-licensed authorized dealer and are subject to declaration, economic-substance scrutiny, and, in specified cases, CBS prior approval. The subject International Company (IC) is statutorily treated as non-resident, placing it outside the domestic exchange-control perimeter: it may lawfully maintain and operate USD, NZD, AUD, and EUR accounts, hold rental proceeds offshore, and distribute dividends to non-resident investors without forced conversion into tala. Profit remittance hurdles for this REIT IC are limited to: (i) the requirement that any Samoan-domiciled distribution be executed by an authorized dealer; (ii) a board resolution and audited earning evidence demonstrating derivation from non-resident real estate business; (iii) a Ministry of Revenue tax clearance if the IC maintains any Samoan-source registration or activity; and (iv) compliance with Samoa AML/CFT Act 2007 screening of the beneficiary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1976, every Samoan-resident exporter must obtain payment of the full export invoice value within six months from the date of shipment and surrender 100% of the foreign-currency proceeds to an authorized dealer within seven days of receipt; conversion into WST is mandatory at the prevailing market rate unless a general or specific exemption is granted by the Central Bank of Samoa. For this REIT IC, the obligation is not triggered by core activities because rental income from offshore real estate does not constitute a Samoan export, and the IC non-resident status exempts it from the Exchange Control Act. If the IC ever derives Samoan-source proceeds (e.g., management fees from a Samoan-resident obligor), statutory compliance would require full repatriation within the six-month window, 100% surrender, and a CBS exemption letter to preserve foreign-currency treatment.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1976; offshore international financial services are co-supervised by the Samoa International Financial Authority (SIFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (U.K. FCA-authorised, Gibraltar- or Bermuda-licensed e-money institutions) are legally usable by the non-resident IC for ancillary, low-value flows, but they cannot function as the primary treasury rail. Legal constraints: (i) Samoa Financial Institutions Act 2014 and AML/CFT Act 2007 criminalize unlicensed money-service business touching Samoan residents, so an EMI must be restricted strictly to non-resident counterparties; (ii) the authorized-dealer bank will not accept EMI account statements as proof of capital clearance or dividend provenance; (iii) EMIs do not provide commercial letters of credit, term-loan disbursement, or custody services required by a REIT; and (iv) global correspondent de-risking makes EMI accounts subject to abrupt suspension of dividend-related payment runs. The blueprint therefore contemplates a single tier-1 commercial bank as the principal clearing house and one fiat-backed EMI reserved solely for incidental expenses not exceeding USD 50,000 per payment.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for an International Company (IC) de jure tax-exempt under Section 114 of the International Companies Act 1987; 27% standard corporate rate under the Income Tax Act 2012 applies only if the IC loses its exempt status by carrying on business in Samoa or becoming Samoan-resident.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends and capital distributions paid by a tax-exempt International Company (explicit statutory exemption in Section 114 of the International Companies Act 1987). If the payer were an ordinary Samoan-resident company, the statutory dividend withholding tax rate for non-resident shareholders is 15% under the Income Tax Act 2012.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local-currency threshold applies to an exempt International Company because it is outside the Samoan income tax base. For an ordinary taxable Samoan-resident company, the Income Tax Act 2012 (Sections 40–42) requires related-party cross-border transactions to be priced at arm's length; Samoa does not prescribe a statutory Local/Master File threshold, but the Commissioner may order contemporaneous TP documentation where annual related-party turnover exceeds WST 5,000,000 or where a transaction involves a low-tax jurisdiction. An IC that holds foreign real estate through a related SPV should nonetheless maintain an internal transfer pricing memorandum to support management fees, debt financing terms, and property acquisition/sale prices in the event of an economic substance review.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Samoa, as a no-or-only-nominal-tax jurisdiction under the OECD/G20 BEPS Action 5 and EU listing processes, must demonstrate that the IC regime is not harmful only where ICs engaged in geographically mobile services (headquarters, distribution, services, financing, leasing, banking, insurance, shipping, and DPP activities) maintain substantial economic presence. A Samoan IC cannot rely solely on its registered office and agent. A REIT IC must evidence, where managed from Samoa: physical premises, qualified local employees or delegates, recurrent board minutes and strategic decisions in Samoa, and expenditure commensurate with income. A purely passive IC holding foreign income-generating real estate, with no Samoan management activity, records, or employees, is treated as low-risk for ESR purposes but remains subject to the Samaritan AML/BO recordkeeping obligations of the licensed resident agent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Forfeiture of IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: Forfeiture of the tax exemption; assessment as an ordinary resident company at 27% on Samoan-source income; penalty of at least 20% of the tax shortfall plus 2% monthly compound interest; deregistration of the International Company certificate by the Registrar; cancellation of the licensed agent's authority to service the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly liable for taxes collected or withheld and not remitted (trust fund doctrine); civil restitution for diverted corporate assets; the Supreme Court may impose travel bans and asset freezes pending investigation; criminal tax evasion is punishable by up to 7 years' imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: OECD FHTP and EU listing consequences for Samoa; the Ministry may impose a Significant Economic Presence filing and penalties; the Registrar may strike off the IC under Part X; the Financial Services Authority may fine the licensed agent up to WST 500,000 for servicing shell entities without substance review; the anti-money laundering supervisor may issue a direction requiring divestment.

Director personal liability: Directors of a struck-off IC may be disqualified from acting as directors of any Samoan company for up to 5 years; personal fines up to WST 100,000 for causing the entity to breach substance conditions; the court may impose a travel ban pending the completion of an FIU/Registrar investigation.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Corporate fine up to WST 500,000 for money-laundering offences; the Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze bank accounts, suspend the resident agent's license, and the Registrar may strike off the IC; confiscation orders may extend to the underlying real estate portfolio held through the IC.

Director personal liability: Individual directors and officers are liable to imprisonment of up to 14 years and personal fines of up to WST 200,000; personal civil liability for dishonest assistance in laundering rental proceeds; court-ordered travel bans and passport surrender during FIU and criminal proceedings.
Failure to File Annual Return / Pay Annual Fee

Statutory sanction: Flat late-filing penalty of USD 100; after 3 months of continuous default, automatic strike-off from the International Companies Register with forfeiture of assets situated in Samoa; reinstatement only by court order or Registrar's discretion upon payment of all arrears plus a USD 50 reinstatement fee.

Director personal liability: Directors of a struck-off IC are jointly liable for unpaid annual fees up to USD 500; the Registrar may prohibit the directors from incorporating a new IC until arrears are settled; prolonged default supports a director disqualification order enforced through a travel restriction.
False Statements / Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to WST 5,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years under the International Companies Act 1987; the Registrar may strike off the company on just and equitable grounds; the Ministry for Revenue may issue a restitution order for any resulting tax loss.

Director personal liability: Full personal criminal liability of the signing director; civil restitution of any tax avoided; prohibition from holding office in any Samoan company for up to 10 years; the Supreme Court may issue a travel ban and asset freeze pending trial.
Unlicensed Collective Investment Scheme / REIT Regulatory Breach

Statutory sanction: Cease-and-desist order from the FSA; administrative fine of up to WST 250,000; the underlying property portfolio may be placed under a receiver; subscription agreements may be voidable; the directors must refund investor monies within a court-supervised timetable.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for all investor losses; criminal liability for unauthorized securities dealing with imprisonment of up to 5 years; the FSA may seek a personal asset freeze and travel ban to secure restitution and prevent dissipation of investor funds.
