Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); online filing portal: SARS eFiling (https://efiling.sars.gov.za) and corporate website (www.sars.gov.za)

E-government filing portal

A South African incorporated (Pty) Ltd is automatically registered for Income Tax with SARS upon incorporation through the CIPC-SARS data integration and receives an Income Tax Reference Number (ITRN); the company must appoint a public officer (s 246 TAA) within one month of commencing business and activate an eFiling profile. VAT registration is a separate step effected via VAT 101 (local vendor) or VAT 102 (foreign vendor/non-resident) submitted through eFiling or a SARS branch, supported by the CIPC registration certificate, memorandum of incorporation, proof of registered address, directors' identity documents, public officer appointment, and a bank confirmation letter. A foreign company operating through a South African permanent establishment must first register as an 'external company' with CIPC under the Companies Act and then apply to SARS via eFiling for an income tax number and, where required, a VAT number, using the SARS registration form and board resolutions appointing a local public officer. SARS issues a Tax Compliance Status (TCS) PIN linked to the ITRN, which is required for customs, tender and intergovernmental purposes. Compulsory VAT registration applies within 21 days of exceeding R1,000,000 taxable turnover in any 12-month period; foreign suppliers of electronic services to South African residents must register once annual consideration exceeds R50,000.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed at entity level at the 27% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income under the classical system (no pass-through; profits distributed as dividends subject to 20% dividends tax). An external company (foreign-incorporated) carrying on business through a South African branch is taxed at 27% on South African-source branch income, with a 15% branch remittance tax on unremitted branch profits. Territorial scope: residents taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits under s 6quat; non-residents taxed only on South African-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed at entity level at the 27% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income under the classical system (no pass-through; profits distributed as dividends subject to 20% dividends tax). An external company (foreign-incorporated) carrying on business through a South African branch is taxed at 27% on South African-source branch income, with a 15% branch remittance tax on unremitted branch profits. Territorial scope: residents taxed on worldwide income with foreign tax credits under s 6quat; non-residents taxed only on South African-source income. No tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

South Africa has no check-the-box or similar entity classification election. Available statutory elections/regimes: (i) voluntary VAT vendor election (s 23(2) VAT Act) from R50,000 taxable turnover; (ii) cash/payments basis of VAT accounting for qualifying vendors with taxable turnover up to R2,500,000 (s 15 VAT Act); (iii) micro business turnover tax election under the Sixth Schedule to the Income Tax Act for entities with annual turnover up to R1,000,000; (iv) group roll-over elections under s 45 (intra-group transfers at tax value) and s 47 (liquidation distributions); (v) CFC foreign business establishment exclusions/elections under s 9D of the Income Tax Act; and (vi) s 18A public benefit organisation approvals. Hybrid debt/equity and hybrid interest mismatch rules (s 8E, s 8F/8FA) are applied by statute with no taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: R1,000,000 (one million rand) actual or expected taxable turnover in any 12-month period; voluntary registration: R50,000; non-resident electronic services suppliers: R50,000 per annum

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies under s 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act: the goods (here, high-speed aseptic beverage bottling automated lines, ISIC 2825) must be supplied to a non-resident and physically exported from South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply, or within such longer period as SARS may allow under s 11(3). Services are zero-rated under s 11(2)(l) when physically rendered outside South Africa or supplied to non-residents, subject to exclusions for services connected to land or movable property situated in South Africa and services supplied to a resident for consumption in South Africa. The exporter must prove actual removal from the South African customs territory with conserved export documentation; refund of related input tax becomes available only when the zero-rated export is demonstrated and the VAT 201 return is filed.

  • Customs Export Declaration - SARS SAD500 (with release/exit confirmation)
  • Commercial invoice (and pro forma invoice if applicable) stating zero-rated export supply
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Road Consignment Note evidencing physical removal abroad
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice proving receipt of foreign currency payment
  • Contract of sale or purchase order from the non-resident buyer
  • Packing list and SACU/SADC certificate of origin (where applicable)
  • Marine/inland transit insurance certificate and container tracking evidence
  • SARS VAT 201 schedule with supporting zero-rating audit file retained for 5 years (s 55 VAT Act)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is currently in force. SARS mandates electronic tax return filing via eFiling, and customs exports are declared electronically through SARCC Customs EDI/SAD500, but ordinary VAT tax invoices are not subject to pre-clearance or API fiscal stamping. Tax invoices must meet s 20 VAT Act requirements: full tax invoices for supplies above R5,000 and abbreviated invoices for supplies up to R5,000. SARS issued a draft e-Invoicing Framework (2024) signalling phased adoption of a standardised e-invoicing model aligned to OECD digital VAT guidance; mandatory national e-invoicing has not yet been legislated.

SARS applies high-risk audit profiling to export zero-rating claims. If export is not substantiated within the statutory period (including the 90-day export condition), the zero-rating fails, output tax is re-assessed with interest at the prescribed rate, and input tax is clawed back under adjustment provisions. Fraudulent or grossly negligent claims attract additional tax of up to 200% under s 59(2) of the VAT Act; understatement penalties under s 223 of the Tax Administration Act range from 10% to 150% of the shortfall depending on behaviour; criminal prosecution may result in fines up to R2,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years (TAA s 234; VAT Act s 59A). Directors and the public officer are jointly and severally liable for VAT debts as representative taxpayers (TAA ss 179-181, 180). Goods diverted to local use after zero-rating are treated as a deemed taxable supply, triggering retrospective output tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a dual-tier convertibility framework. Current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, certain royalties and intra-group fees) are generally free from exchange-control approval, with only documentary proof and balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting required. Capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, disposal of local assets, and outward investment) remain subject to structured controls: inward foreign investment is freely permitted but must be reported through an SARB Authorised Dealer and captured via Form MP225 / loop-structure declarations; outward capital outflows and profit remittances above prescribed thresholds require FinSurv notification or approval. Dividends payable to non-resident shareholders are freely remittable provided the underlying investment was declared and the profit was earned from ordinary business activities; no capital account conversion permission is required for current-account income remittances, but foreign-exchange transactions must be undertaken through an authorised dealer with prescribed exchange-control documentation, including a tax clearance or CUSTOMS/SARS import-export declaration record where relevant.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received within 12 months from the date of shipment unless official extension has been granted by an Authorised Dealer or FinSurv in exceptional circumstances. All payments in respect of exported goods must be routed through an Authorised Dealer in South Africa unless an exporter has been granted specific authority to maintain a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) abroad. South African exporters may retain 100% of their export proceeds in a local FCA; however, the proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa within the applicable period and, to the extent not retained in an approved FCA, must be converted into South African rand (ZAR) by the Authorised Dealer on receipt. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively nil for entities holding a permitted FCA, but any proceeds not retained under an approved facility are subject to the authorised dealer's default 'surrender' practice of converting the received foreign currency to rand on crediting the exporter's operational account. Non-compliance exposes the exporter to administrative penalties, suspension of customs clearance, and exchange-control sanctions.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and the Exchange Control Division under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a standalone treasury channel, but legally constrained. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) cannot independently hold SARB-licensed exchange-control capacity in South Africa. A foreign EMI may operate only as a non-regulated payment facilitator through a South African Authorised Dealer partnership or by acquiring a financial technology licence from the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) together with SARB FinSurv authorisation. For an export-orientated (Pty) Ltd, the most viable use of an EMI is limited to pre-payment collection in foreign currencies; all settlement, conversion and regulated cross-border flows must still clear through the domestic authorised branch of a Schedule 1 commercial bank. Mandatory AML/KYC under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) cannot be fully delegated to an offshore EMI. Therefore, the recommended rating is Medium only where a full 'Banking-as-a-Service' arrangement is underwritten by a South African Schedule 2 bank; otherwise, rating is Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and debt capital injections from non-resident shareholders must be routed via an SARB Authorised Dealer (i.e., a Schedule 1 bank in South Africa) using SWIFT MT103/MT202 transfers with the exact remitter name, remitter bank, and transaction purpose code. For an equity injection, the purpose code is 'Equity Capital – Direct Investment (Form MP225)' and the bank must be provided with the certificate of incorporation, resolution of directors, a share subscription agreement, and evidence of the inward transfer in convertible currency. For shareholder loan capital, the loan agreement, market-rate evidence, and a declaration under exchange-control 'loan' category are required. The authorised dealer will issue a 'BOP Inquiry' number for statistical recording, and after the inward settlement the capital is credited to the company's ZAR account. If the injection exceeds R50 million authorisation threshold (or any amended threshold), prior FinSurv approval may be needed. In all cases, the (Pty) Ltd must maintain statutory records of the source and purpose of incoming funds for five years.Trade settlements for aseptic bottling line components and service fees are executed through the corporate operational account via SWIFT MT103 for direct transfer of imported capital equipment and through commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700 series) for deferred payment transactions. For exports of packaging machines to SACU and third countries, the exporter may request the importer's bank to issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit, confirmed by a South African bank, with payment at sight or usance tenor. Alternatively, documents against payment (D/P) and documents against acceptance (D/A) collection procedures through correspondent banks are permitted, using a Bill of Exchange and a full set of shipping documents. All outbound payments must be supported by a SARB-compliant declaration form (Form T105 or e-DEC for imports/exports), a SARS customs import file number, and, for any disbursement exceeding the effective published limit, a tax compliance status pin from SARS.
