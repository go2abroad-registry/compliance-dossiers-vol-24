Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a South African (Pty) Ltd is a resident company (resident by incorporation or effective management in South Africa) taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate, with no pass-through eligibility; non-resident branches taxed only on South African-source income; no sub-national income tax applies as South Africa is a unitary national jurisdiction.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; entity classification follows legal form under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Available elections include: (i) Headquarter Company election under section 9I, allowing qualifying resident HQ companies to elect exempt treatment of certain foreign dividends, interest, royalties and foreign service fees; (ii) intra-group asset-transfer rollover election under section 45; (iii) amalgamation rollover election under section 42; (iv) voluntary VAT registration election where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12-month period; hybrid mismatch rules (sections 8E and 25D) apply mandatorily with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration where taxable supplies exceeded ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months or are reasonably expected to exceed that amount; voluntary registration available where taxable supplies exceeded ZAR 50,000 in the preceding 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under Item 1 of Schedule 1 to the VAT Act when supplied on sale or consignment to a non-resident and removed from the Republic within 90 days of supply; services are zero-rated under Item 2 when supplied to a non-resident who is not physically present in South Africa at the time of supply, provided the services are not connected with land, improvements, or movable property situated in South Africa and are not otherwise excluded; SCADA integration and technical services performed offshore for foreign clients qualify under the general service-export zero-rating, subject to documentary proof of export and receipt of payment in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) cleared by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical removal of goods from South Africa
  • Inward Payment Certificate or bank SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds

SARS is phasing in mandatory e-invoicing from 2025/2026 for vendors above specified turnover thresholds; e-invoices must conform to the SARS E-Invoicing standard (UBL 2.1 with Peppol interoperability) and be submitted through SARS-accredited service providers and SARS Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). No real-time transactional fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in force; VAT returns (VAT201) and export schedules are filed electronically through SARS eFiling, and SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) XML data must be retained and produced on SARS request.

SARS cross-verifies zero-rating claims against customs SAD500 data, eFiling VAT201 schedules, and bank foreign-exchange receipts; export documentation must be retained for five years (section 55 VAT Act). Incorrect zero-rating results in additional VAT assessments, a 10% penalty and statutory interest (section 39(1)-(2) VAT Act); understatement penalties of up to 200% may be imposed for gross negligence or intentional evasion (section 223 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011); fraudulent export schemes attract criminal prosecution (section 58 VAT Act; section 235 Tax Administration Act), retrospective clawback of input VAT deductions, and potential personal liability of directors who knowingly participate in or benefit from the scheme.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a free-floating currency, but South Africa operates a controlled exchange system under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations administered by the SARB Financial Surveillance Department. Current account items, including trade settlements, service fees, interest and ordinary operating disbursements, may be processed through an Authorised Dealer without prior SARB approval, subject to documentary substantiation and compliance with the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2002 (FICA). Capital account items, including intercompany loans, share subscriptions, dividend distributions and disinvestment proceeds, require formal reporting to the Authorised Dealer and, in certain cases, approval from FinSurv. Dividend and profit remittances by non-resident shareholders are permitted without a statutory percentage cap, but require: (i) a valid Tax Compliance Status PIN issued by the South African Revenue Service (SARS); (ii) an audited solvency and liquidity assessment; (iii) a board resolution approving the distribution; and (iv) beneficial ownership disclosure of the recipient. Exchange control hurdles arise primarily from documentary delays, transfer-pricing scrutiny and extended timelines for capital repatriation where inward FDI was not properly recorded on a Form C.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received by the exporter in South Africa through an Authorised Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment, inclusive of the vessel or aircraft departure date, unless an extension is granted by FinSurv or the Authorised Dealer. There is no mandatory statutory ratio requiring conversion into rand, because the surrender requirement for export receipts has been abolished; instead, the proceeds must be repatriated to a South African banking account and may be retained in foreign currency only if the exporter holds an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) within the Authorised Dealer. The exporter must complete the Export Declaration Form (Form 900 / GIC 200) before or at shipment, and the Authorised Dealer must report the receipt of proceeds via the relevant exchange-control reporting system. Failure to repatriate within the 180-day window may result in a post-shipment audit, a written directive to return the proceeds, and administrative penalties under the Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited viability as the primary treasury rail. South African domestic ZAR clearing must occur through the national payment system administered under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 and the SARB National Payment System Department; an offshore Electronic Money Institution (EMI) is not an Authorised Dealer in foreign exchange and cannot provide SWIFT MT103 messages carrying correct exchange-control reporting references, import declarations (Form A) or export proceeds verifications (Form 900). An offshore EMI may be used as a supplementary collection account for EUR/USD receivables or for pre-funding international suppliers, but the proceeds must eventually be repatriated to a domestic Authorised Dealer account to close the exchange-control record. Legal constraints include FICA licensing restrictions, capital-flow reporting gaps, and lack of access to the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) clearing system. Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary liquidity tool; Low as a sole operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from a non-resident shareholder must be routed by international wire (SWIFT MT103) to the company's designated Authorised Dealer (tier-1 bank) in South Africa. The payment must clearly identify the remitter as a non-resident and include the corporate entity's legal name, CIPC registration number, and a reference to the underlying share subscription or loan agreement. The Authorised Dealer will require, before crediting the account: (i) a board resolution of the (Pty) Ltd approving the issuance of shares or acceptance of the loan; (ii) a copy of the signed share subscription / loan facility agreement; (iii) a completed Form C for foreign direct investment in equity where the company must preserve the right of future disinvestment/repatriation; (iv) a SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN; and (v) beneficial ownership declarations for the inbound remitter. The funds may be credited in foreign currency to an approved Foreign Currency Account or converted to ZAR; proceeds must not originate from a bank barred by South African sanctions. SARB balance-of-payments purpose codes should distinguish 'equity capital in South Africa' for share capital and 'loan finance to enterprise' for intercompany debt; the Authorised Dealer will allocate the correct BOP code and retain the documentation for exchange-control verification.The company may settle foreign trade invoices through: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers of import payments; (ii) SWIFT MT202 for interbank cover payments; (iii) Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) for larger procurement contracts, with payment against presentation of transport and inspection documents; and (iv) SWIFT MT707 for LC amendments. For physical imports of SCADA panels, industrial computers and cabling, the company must lodge an Import Declaration Form (Form A) with the Authorised Dealer, provide the pro-forma commercial invoice, and ensure that the import is classified correctly under the South African Tariff Schedule. For software licences and digital automation services, a service-payment instruction with a written services agreement and SARS-compliant invoice is sufficient; the Authorised Dealer will apply the appropriate current-account transfer code. All outbound payments require the bank to request underlying documentation and, if the beneficiary is classified as high-risk, an enhanced due diligence questionnaire.
