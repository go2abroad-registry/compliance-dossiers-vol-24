Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); online portal: SARS eFiling (www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must obtain a South African income tax reference number and VAT number by registering as a taxpayer through SARS eFiling or a branch office. The registration procedure involves: (i) applying for an income tax number using the SARS Registration Form (RAF01) or via eFiling under 'Register a Taxpayer'; (ii) submitting certified copies of the foreign company's incorporation documents, a valid South African branch registration (where applicable), and the directors' or public officer's identification; (iii) registering for VAT using VAT101 if taxable supplies exceed R1 million or voluntarily if above R50,000; and (iv) obtaining a Customs Code through the SARS Customs system for export/import activity. Foreign companies may appoint a local representative taxpayer or tax practitioner to manage the process electronically through eFiling.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must obtain a South African income tax reference number and VAT number by registering as a taxpayer through SARS eFiling or a branch office. The registration procedure involves: (i) applying for an income tax number using the SARS Registration Form (RAF01) or via eFiling under 'Register a Taxpayer'; (ii) submitting certified copies of the foreign company's incorporation documents, a valid South African branch registration (where applicable), and the directors' or public officer's identification; (iii) registering for VAT using VAT101 if taxable supplies exceed R1 million or voluntarily if above R50,000; and (iv) obtaining a Customs Code through the SARS Customs system for export/import activity. Foreign companies may appoint a local representative taxpayer or tax practitioner to manage the process electronically through eFiling.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not offer a US-style check-the-box election. Election mechanisms relevant to a proprietary limited company include: (i) Small Business Corporation (SBC) election under section 12E of the Income Tax Act for qualifying shareholders; (ii) group tax registration for VAT under section 50(3) of the VAT Act for multiple companies in a group; (iii) the election of a micro business tax regime under the Sixth Schedule for turnover not exceeding R1 million; (iv) the hybrid mismatch election rules under sections 8E, 8EA and 23K which allow alignment of tax treatment with the counterpart jurisdiction; and (v) the 'considered acquired' capital gain election for intra-group asset transfers under paragraph 65 of the Eighth Schedule.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 in taxable supplies during any 12-month period (compulsory registration); voluntary registration permitted from R50,000 per year. A foreign entity supplying imported or locally delivered EV charging hardware must register for VAT from the date when taxable supplies exceed the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A vendor may zero-rate exports of goods under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act when the goods are physically removed from South Africa to a destination of another country within 90 days of the supply, and the supplier retains documentary proof of export. Zero-rating of services is available under section 11(2)(l) for services supplied to non-residents when the services directly relate to moveable goods located outside South Africa, or are performed outside South Africa. The claimant must be a registered VAT vendor, have received export consideration in foreign currency or via approved payment rails, and maintain evidence that the goods left the Republic.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD 500) accepted by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading (for sea freight) or Air Waybill (for air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer statement proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Proof of physical removal from the South African customs controlled area

South Africa has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance electronic invoicing system as of the current regime. SARS requires tax invoices meeting the conditions of section 20 of the VAT Act, issued within 21 days of the supply. SARS is progressively introducing a mandatory e-invoicing interoperability model via the eFiling platform, with API-based submissions for Value-Added Tax returns (VAT201) and customs declarations. No digital stamping of invoices is currently imposed; however, taxpayers must maintain audit-ready electronic records and use SARS-approved electronic billing systems where invoices are transmitted electronically.

SARS may scrutinise claims for zero-rated export supplies by verifying the customs declaration and foreign exchange repatriation records. Where export documentation is found to be fraudulent or substantively deficient, the full 15% VAT is reclaimed with interest, and the taxpayer becomes liable to an understatement penalty under the Tax Administration Act ranging from 10% to 50% of the shortfall, plus an additional tax up to 200% in cases of deliberate non-disclosure or intentional tax evasion. Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act and the Financial Intelligence Centre Act may also apply for fraudulent export invoices, and retrospective clawback assessment may be issued within the five-year prescription period, extended indefinitely for fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a partially convertible currency regime. Current account transactions, including trade settlements, service payments, interest, royalties, and dividends, are freely remittable through an Authorised Dealer upon production of the required supporting documents. Capital account transactions executed by South African residents remain subject to FinSurv approval or Authorised Dealer authorisation, while inward non-resident capital investment is generally permitted without prior Exchange Control approval but must be reported and properly documented. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted after compliance with exchange control formalities, South African dividend tax at 20% or lower under a relevant double tax agreement, and beneficial ownership declarations. Practical remittance hurdles include the requirement to obtain a South African Revenue Service tax compliance status, submission of documentary proof of original inward capital transfer for capital repatriations, and the burden of satisfying the receiving bank's risk-based source-of-funds and anti-money laundering review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under South African exchange control regulations, all export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of export shipment and must be credited to a South African bank account or a designated foreign currency account held with an Authorised Dealer. There is no mandatory percentage conversion of export receipts into South African rand; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in an approved foreign currency account or convert it to rand at the prevailing market rate. The exporter must provide the bank with the customs export declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, and evidence of receipt of payment. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory 180-day timeline is a contravention of the Exchange Control Regulations and may result in penalties, adverse customs ratings, and exchange control enforcement action.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, with delegated authority to Authorised Dealers and Authorised Brokers licensed under the Currency and Exchanges Manual.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

The viability of offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) as a primary treasury and clearing rail is LOW. South Africa does not issue an electronic money institution licence equivalent to the EU EMI passport, and only SARB-licensed banks appointed as Authorised Dealers can lawfully maintain corporate ZAR accounts and process cross-border payments. An offshore EMI may be used solely as a payment-orchestration interface or FX quotation layer, but final settlement must be routed through a South African bank account held by the resident entity. Relying on an unlicensed foreign EMI for corporate cash custody or for executing trade settlements may breach the Financial Sector Regulation Act and the National Payment System Act, and would materially impair audit trail and exchange control compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed by SWIFT MT103 with clear sender-to-recipient information identifying the transfer as equity subscription or shareholder loan funding for ZA_PTY_LTD. The remitting bank must include a valid payment purpose code corresponding to inward Foreign Direct Investment or intercompany financing. The South African beneficiary bank will verify the underlying board resolution, the executed share subscription or loan agreement, the company certificate of incorporation, the CIPC beneficial ownership filing and the FATCA/CRS self-certification before crediting the ZAR operational account or designated capital escrow account. The bank must record the inward transfer for exchange control and balance-of-payment purposes.
