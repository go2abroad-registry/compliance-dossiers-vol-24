Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Independent State of Samoa; e-Services via TAXMI portal at https://www.ministryforrevenue.gov.ws; customs processing via UNCTAD ASYCUDA World single window.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Obtain a Samoa International Company (IC) incorporation certificate and Certificate of Good Standing from the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL) / Registrar of International Companies. Step 2 — Register for a TIN with MfR through the TAXMI portal before first customs declaration or taxable activity; complete the Taxpayer Registration Form (TIN application), and upload certified copies of: Certificate of Incorporation, Articles of Association, directors'/shareholders'/UBO passports, proof of registered office in Samoa, and, where applicable, foreign-company registration certificate. Step 3 — MfR validates the application and issues a single multi-role TIN (income tax + VAGST). Step 4 — Where the IC physically imports goods or carries on any local taxable activity, the IC must also register for VAGST and customs (Importer Code) within 14 days. Step 5 — Foreign entities without a Samoan physical presence may obtain a TIN through their authorized tax agent only for VAGST/customs purposes; no income tax registration is required because the IC is statutorily exempt and non-resident.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Samoa IC is exempt from Samoan income tax, capital gains, dividends, and stamp duties under the International Companies Act 1987, provided it does not carry on business in Samoa, has no Samoan-source income, and central management and control is offshore. For indirect taxes, the IC remains subject to VAGST and customs duty only on goods/services physically imported into or supplied within Samoa.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or comparable entity-classification election exists under Samoan law. Available mechanisms: (a) voluntary VAGST registration under the VAGST Act 2003 to recover input tax on exported AEO audit services; (b) election to be treated as an ordinary Samoan domestic company by notifying the Registrar and changing the charter, thereby accepting Samoan tax residence and worldwide taxation; (c) election to re-domicile as a foreign company or foreign-company status under the ICA so that tax-exempt status continues; (d) hybrid mismatch disclosures/structured arrangement elections under the Income Tax Act 2012 where the IC participates in cross-border hybrid instruments; and (e) annual election between the 'zero-rating' and 'exemption' treatment of exported services, documented in the VAGST return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 50,000 annual taxable turnover (compulsory); voluntary registration permitted; non-resident suppliers without a Samoan fixed establishment are generally outside the compulsory registration net except for imported-services reverse-charge obligations on local recipients.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated where the IC supplies goods and removes them from Samoa within 28 days; proof of physical export required. Services (incl. ISIC 7020 AEO certification, supply-chain security audits): zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident client, the services are not performed for a person in Samoa, and the benefit is not enjoyed in Samoa; management/consultancy services to offshore principals qualify. The IC must hold contemporaneous evidence that the recipient is non-resident (e.g., foreign registration, invoiced to offshore address) and that no domestic nexus exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World CED / Export Entry)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean/Master/Straight) or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export contract and purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing payment from the offshore client
  • Certificate of Origin (where applicable)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based CTC, or digital stamping mandate in Samoa. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue sequential tax invoices (paper or electronic) with prescribed particulars; all exports/imports must be declared electronically in ASYCUDA World for customs clearance; e-invoices must be retained for 7 years and produced on MfR demand.

MfR may audit any zero-rated export transaction for up to 7 years; missing or false export documents trigger clawback of the VAGST input credits/refunds previously granted, plus shortfall penalties of 25% (lack of reasonable care), 75% (intentional disregard), or 150% (fraudulent behavior), and criminal sanctions (fines up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years); deliberate creation of artificial export documentation disqualifies the IC from AEO status and triggers joint and several liability of directors/trustees.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains a partially convertible currency regime built on a CBS-managed float of the Samoan Tala (WST) against a trade-weighted basket of partner currencies. Current-account items, including AEO certification audit fees, professional management-consulting fees, training fees and reimbursable travel disbursements, are freely convertible for resident payers upon production of invoices and the underlying engagement contracts; CBS pre-approval is not required for bona fide trade in services. Capital-account items, including direct equity injections, project finance, shareholder loans and intercompany balances above WST 200,000 equivalent, require prior CBS application through an authorised dealer only where the funds are booked to a Samoan resident onshore entity. The WS_IC, being a Samoa International Company, is statutorily deemed non-resident for exchange-control purposes under the International Companies Act 1987, provided it is not managed and controlled from Samoa; consequently, its foreign-currency offshore operating and settlement accounts may be credited, held and disbursed in USD, EUR or AUD without CBS conversion approval. Profit-remittance hurdles arise primarily at the resident-subcontractor or resident-branch layer: dividend and branch-remittance payments to non-residents are subject to a 15% Samoan withholding tax, reduced where a tax treaty exemption or the IC tax-exempt certificate applies; the remitting bank requires audited financial statements, a tax-clearance certificate from the Samoa Revenue Authority and a board dividend resolution. Distributions by the WS_IC itself to non-resident shareholders constitute non-Samoan-source income and attract no Samoan tax provided the IC derives no Samoan-source business activity and is not managed and controlled from Samoa.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1982, a Samoan resident exporter must repatriate all export and service proceeds to Samoa and offer the foreign currency to an authorised dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment for goods or from the date of invoice or completion certificate for services; the full amount is surrendered through the dealer into WST unless the CBS grants a specific retention or offset licence. Samoa does not impose a percentage-based forced-conversion floor analogous to certain Asian exchange-control regimes. For the WS_IC, the non-resident deeming provision disapplies the 180-day surrender and forced-conversion obligations so long as the international company is not managed and controlled from Samoa; export proceeds may therefore be retained offshore in the IC's USD settlement account, applied directly to pay overseas AEO security-audit subcontractors and software vendors, and only net margins remitted as dividends under the IC's constitutional documents. The CBS nonetheless requires all foreign-currency dealings to be routed through an authorised dealer for statistical reporting, and the registered agent must maintain a statutory record of the IC's foreign-currency receipts for annual filing with the Registrar of International and Foreign Companies.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa); statutory mandate under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and exclusive exchange-control administration under the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Exchange Control Regulations 1982 and the Financial Institutions Act 1996; the Financial Intelligence Unit operates within the CBS pursuant to the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2018.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: LOW (viability 2/10). Samoa has no electronic-money-institution or digital-bank licensing category; the Financial Institutions Act 1996 reserves deposit-taking and payment facilitation to banks licensed by the CBS. Wise, although designated as a target rail, does not support business onboarding for Samoan-registered entities and maintains no Samoa-authorised institution; the WS_IC therefore cannot lawfully maintain its treasury solely on a Wise account. An unlicensed offshore EMI account held in the IC's name would violate CBS exchange-control expectations because foreign-currency accounts of deemed-non-resident international companies must be held with authorised dealers or a recognised overseas regulated bank, not with e-money licensees. The compliant architecture is a USD corporate account at ANZ or BSP as the bank of record, with Wise or comparable fintech products permitted only for low-value reimbursable travel and incidental disbursements connected to overseas AEO audit field work, and never as a treasury concentration point.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires the foreign parent or investor's bank to transmit a SWIFT MT103 crediting the WS_IC's USD account at the Samoan authorised dealer, quoting the beneficiary account number, BIC of the Samoan bank and a CBS-recognised purpose code; for direct equity the remittance narrative maps to the CBS balance-of-payments direct-investment equity sub-item equivalent to IMF BPM6 category FA.612, while for ordinary AEO service advances the payment narrative cites services income under management consulting (ISIC 7020) with the corresponding current-account service code. The bank executes sanctions screening and, for amounts exceeding WST 200,000 equivalent, submits the CBS large foreign-exchange transaction report under the Exchange Control Regulations; CBS prior approval is not required because the WS_IC is a deemed non-resident. The documentary bundle retained on file comprises the board resolution approving the capital injection, the share-subscription or shareholder-loan agreement, the registered agent's funds-receipt letter and a counsel-confirmed ownership-chain declaration.The primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for fee settlements to AEO subcontractors, software and training vendors, cleared through ANZ or BSP correspondent banks in Australia, New Zealand or the United States with full beneficiary BIC disclosure to satisfy Financial Intelligence Unit payment-transparency rules. Where supply-chain-audit hardware such as RFID readers, inspection kits, ISO 28000 or C-TPAT test equipment is procured from non-resident vendors, the permissible trade rail is a Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 with a 30 to 90-day usance tenor under Incoterms 2020. Local Tala disbursements clear through the CBS-operated Samoan Interbank Payment System, and every outbound service payment must be supported by an underlying invoice, an engagement order and, where Samoan-source income arises, tax-compliant proof; outbound service settlements above USD 10,000 require a CBS current-transaction declaration filed by the authorised dealer.Deliverable spot and outright forward contracts, up to 12 months, available only through CBS-licensed authorised dealers and supported by the underlying AEO audit-fee receivable or payable; forwards must be matched to a specific engagement contract and may not be speculative.Natural hedging and matched-book arrangements: maintaining a USD receivable and USD payable matched book with a working-capital buffer of at least two months of net fee flow, formally approved by the relationship bank as the acceptable risk-mitigation strategy.Cross-currency swaps: not available in the domestic Samoan market; may be executed by a parent treasury outside Samoa strictly in the parent's own name with no Samoan-resident risk booked and fully documented in the group treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): not permissible for WST, as the Tala is not an offshore-deliverable currency and the CBS treats offshore WST NDF contracts as speculative capital-account transactions outside authorised-dealer capacity.Currency options: permitted only on a bought basis, never written, executed via the parent's international bank under a board-approved treasury authority with premiums funded from operational cash flows and underlying audit revenue exposure documented.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax rate for resident companies (reduced from 27% effective for income years commencing on or after 1 July 2021); International Companies (ICs) are exempt from income tax on offshore business income and pay only the annual license fee

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Final withholding tax on dividends: 15% for resident corporate shareholders, 25% for non-resident shareholders; dividends paid by an International Company to non-residents are generally exempt from Samoan withholding tax under the IC tax-exempt regime

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions per income year triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation; Master File is required where the multinational group's global turnover exceeds WST 100,000,000; below-threshold taxpayers must keep base arm's-length records

Economic substance scope: No separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are enacted for Samoa ICs; nevertheless, the International Companies Act 2007 requires the IC to be managed and controlled from outside Samoa, prohibits transactions with Samoan residents, and requires maintenance of registered records demonstrating offshore activity; a shell IC used as a conduit for supply-chain audit fees without substantive offshore operations may be recharacterized and lose tax-exempt status

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 150% of the short-paid tax plus compounded late-payment interest; criminal indictment under the Income Tax Act 2012 and Tax Administration Act 2012 with fines up to WST 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; the Commissioner may impose travel bans and asset-freezing orders pending investigation; conviction can lead to imprisonment and disqualification from acting as director
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the structure by SRA; revocation of IC tax-exempt status retroactively; inclusion of diverted income in the taxable base of the beneficial owner; administrative penalties up to WST 100,000

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil on proven fraud; civil restitution of diverted corporate funds; director disqualification for up to 10 years; personal criminal exposure where the sham is used to facilitate offshore tax evasion
AML / Counter-Financing of Terrorism

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 2,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act 2018; confiscation orders for proceeds of crime; revocation of any AML aconsent or financial services licence

Director personal liability: Directors individually liable for AML compliance failures; travel bans and asset-restraint orders can be imposed during investigation; extradition exposure exists for laundering or terrorist-financing offences
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment to the taxpayer's taxable income; penalty of up to 40% of any tax understatement; interest at the statutory rate; fixed documentation-default penalties may be assessed for each non-compliant controlled transaction

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing declarations face personal penalty assessments; on proof of intentional misconduct, imprisonment and disqualification orders may follow, and the corporate veil may be pierced for restitution
