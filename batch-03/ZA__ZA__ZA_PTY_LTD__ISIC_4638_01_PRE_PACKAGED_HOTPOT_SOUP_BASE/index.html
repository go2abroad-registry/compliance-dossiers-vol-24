Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A non-resident company with a South African business presence must register for income tax and VAT with SARS through the SARS eFiling or branch process, submitting: Certificate of Incorporation or equivalent registration document, Memorandum of Incorporation, resolution appointing a local representative taxpayer, proof of physical address, bank details, and financial projections. SARS issues an Income Tax Reference Number and, where applicable, a VAT Vendor Number. Registration must occur within 21 days of commencing business, or before obtaining a VAT licence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A private company is taxed at entity level under the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, with no elective pass-through classification. Statutory elections relevant to the transaction include: debt-equity election for hybrid instruments under section 8F/8FA, the election to treat certain foreign payments as exempt under section 10(1)(h), the election for s 11D research and development allowances, the averaging election for small businesses under Schedule 2, and the VAT grouping election under section 50(1) of the VAT Act 89 of 1991 for related entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (one million South African Rand) compulsory VAT registration threshold measured by taxable supplies in a 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted where taxable supplies exceed R50,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1)(a)(i) of the VAT Act, a supply of movable goods qualifies for 0% VAT where the supplier has physically exported the goods from South Africa within 90 days from the applicable time of supply and retains the prescribed documentary evidence that the goods left the Republic. For services, section 11(2)(a) zero-rating applies to services supplied to a non-resident who is outside South Africa at the time of supply, provided the services are not connected to movable property in South Africa, real estate in South Africa, or services rendered to a South African resident, and are consumed for an offshore purpose.

  • Customs Single Administrative Document export declaration (SAD500) endorsed by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading / export airway waybill or other internationally recognized transport document evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an authorised foreign-exchange dealer, confirming receipt of export proceeds in South Africa in the mandated foreign currency within 180 days

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is currently operative. A vendor must issue a full tax invoice in accordance with sections 20(4) and 20(5) of the VAT Act, containing vendor name, VAT registration number, date, supply description, and consideration. SARS has announced a phased Peppol-compliant e-invoicing standard for future implementation, but as of the current tax period PDF/Excel invoices electronically submitted through eFiling remain acceptable substantiation.

SARS may reverse the zero-rating and raise output VAT at 15% if the required export documentation is not produced within the prescribed period, together with understatement penalties from 10% to 125% of the additional tax liability under the Tax Administration Act, interest, and a 10% per month late-payment penalty capped at 30%. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute a serious criminal tax offence under sections 234–236 of the Tax Administration Act, subject to unlimited fines and imprisonment up to five years; directors of a (Pty) Ltd may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid VAT under VAT Act section 16(3).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a managed float with selective exchange control. The current account is substantially convertible: exporters and importers may effect trade payments through authorised dealers without prior SARB approval, subject to documentary and reporting requirements. The capital account remains partially controlled: inward foreign direct investment into a South African resident, including equity subscription in a (Pty) Ltd, is freely allowed and does not require prior SARB approval but must be reported post-receipt through the receiving authorised dealer. Outward capital transfers by residents require FinSurv approval, and non-resident liquidation proceeds may be remitted after verification that all tax liabilities have been settled. Profit remittance hurdles for non-resident shareholders: dividends may be repatriated only if they qualify as external dividends, meaning they must be declared from the company's distributable reserves arising from its own operations, must not be funded by local borrowings, must be supported by an audited balance sheet and an accountant's verification letter, and must be reported through the authorised dealer with evidence of the original inward investment. The South African dividends withholding tax of 15% applies, but exchange control itself does not impose an additional charge.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 6, all proceeds from exported goods must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment. The full proceeds must be sold to an authorised dealer and converted to ZAR unless the exporter has obtained approval to maintain a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or has been granted a specific concession to retain foreign currency. The default mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% of export proceeds into ZAR; with an approved FCA, up to 100% may be retained in the FCA and used for approved purposes, but the 180-day repatriation requirement still applies and the FCA must be operated within SARB guidelines. Failure to receive the proceeds within the prescribed period constitutes a contravention of exchange control regulations.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and its network of Authorised Dealers (commercial banks) under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed Authorised Dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations and cannot lawfully hold ZAR or independently perform settlement for a South African resident exporter. Their viability is restricted to ancillary services such as quoting indicators, advisory analytics, or partnering with a domestic regulated bank to provide the actual clearing rails. Direct cross-border settlement for export proceeds must occur through a South African authorised dealer to satisfy repatriation and reporting obligations; therefore, reliance solely on an offshore EMI is not a compliant treasury solution.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Transactions with counterparties in high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions

Root cause: Transactions with counterparties in high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Provide bank references and sanctions screening reports for each counterparty; ownership structure charts and registration extracts for counterparty entities; underlying commercial contracts, invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations; and a signed undertaking that the company will not transact with sanctioned individuals or jurisdictions.
Complex, multi-layered beneficial ownership or an offshore holding structure above the South African entity

Root cause: Complex, multi-layered beneficial ownership or an offshore holding structure above the South African entity

Defense evidence: Submit a group structure chart down to each ultimate individual shareholder holding more than 25% with certified identity documents and proof of address; provide legal entity registry extracts for all intermediate entities; explain the commercial rationale for the offshore holding; and execute a formal beneficial ownership declaration granting the bank access to corporate records.
Unusually large or frequent cross-border payments not aligned with declared business turnover or the ISIC 4638 wholesale activity

Root cause: Unusually large or frequent cross-border payments not aligned with declared business turnover or the ISIC 4638 wholesale activity

Defense evidence: Maintain a documented treasury policy with activity limits and expected counterparties; provide monthly aged debtor ledgers, forecast reports, and reconciliations linking each transaction to an underlying export sale; include customs-cleared documentary evidence (SAD500 export declarations) and bank credit notes supporting the payment trail.
Capital injection from a non-resident shareholder in an amount disproportionate to the company's declared business plan

Root cause: Capital injection from a non-resident shareholder in an amount disproportionate to the company's declared business plan

Defense evidence: Provide the share subscription agreement and board resolution authorising the capital injection; source of funds letter from the remitting bank; audited financial statements or wealth evidence of the shareholder; the company's business plan and projected cash flows demonstrating genuine trade funding; and confirmation of any required exchange control declaration.
Request to open a Foreign Currency Account without a clear hard-currency export receivable stream

Root cause: Request to open a Foreign Currency Account without a clear hard-currency export receivable stream

Defense evidence: Provide a 12-month export forecast, up-to-date sales contracts or purchase orders from foreign customers, and a formal motivation citing cash-flow hedging needs; confirm that all FCA credits will originate from eligible export proceeds and that the account will be swept to ZAR in compliance with the 180-day repatriation rule.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be received through a South African authorised dealer bank. A non-resident equity subscription is freely permitted, but the authorised dealer must report the inward transfer to SARB FinSurv on the applicable balance of payments (BOP) and exchange control reporting systems. The remitting overseas bank should use SWIFT MT103 with payment purpose code 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION' or 'LODGEMENT OF CAPITAL'; the receiving South African bank converts the foreign currency to ZAR at the prevailing spot rate unless a pre-approved Foreign Currency Account is used. The supporting package must include the board resolution, share subscription agreement, source-of-funds evidence from the remitting bank, and completed FICA onboarding documents. The authorised dealer will issue a certificate of inward transfer which is required for future outward remittances such as dividends or capital liquidation proceeds.Outbound trade disbursements are executed through authorised dealer banks using SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign-currency payments to overseas suppliers, service providers, and logistics companies. For larger trade obligations or to mitigate counterparty risk, the company may use a commercial letter of credit (import or export LC) with SWIFT MT700 issued by the South African bank in favour of the foreign beneficiary, and MT707 for amendments. Domestic ZAR legs of any disbursement are cleared through the South African Multiple Option Settlement (SAMOS) system. Each outward payment must carry an appropriate BOP purpose code and be supported by underlying invoices, contracts, customs declarations, and where applicable, exchange control approval documents.Forward exchange contracts (FECs) on USD/ZAR with an authorised dealer, available up to 12 months for bona fide trade transactions with a documented underlying receivable or payableNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/ZAR, permissible when executed with a South African authorised dealer for hedging of non-deliverable currency flows, subject to FinSurv approval for maturities beyond 12 monthsCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) between ZAR and USD, permissible for long-term capital hedging or foreign-currency debt, with prior FinSurv approval and a board resolution evidencing the underlying exposureForeign Currency Account (FCA) natural hedging via retention of export proceeds in USD for eligible trade receivables, subject to SARB approval and the FCA operating limits
