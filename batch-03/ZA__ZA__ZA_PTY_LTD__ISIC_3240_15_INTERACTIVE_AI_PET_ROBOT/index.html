Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from South Africa in the ordinary course of business and evidenced by a customs declaration qualify for 0% VAT, provided payment is received in full within 180 days and the goods are exported no later than 180 days after the date of supply; certain services physically performed outside South Africa to non-residents may also be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

VAT returns must be submitted electronically via SARS eFiling; South Africa has adopted an e-invoicing framework aligned with the Tax Administration Act, but there is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; prescribed VAT invoices must contain the SARS vendor VAT number, transaction date, consideration, and VAT amount.

SARS may re-assess VAT upward, impose additional tax up to 200% of the tax evaded, penalties and interest under the Tax Administration Act, and criminal liability in cases where zero-rating is claimed without physical export or without receipt of consideration within the statutory period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is freely convertible for residents and non-residents within the current account under the Currency and Exchanges Manual administered by FinSurv. Current account transactions—trade payments, service fees, royalties, and interest—are generally accommodated through authorised dealers without SARB approval, subject to supporting documentation. Capital account transactions (equity subscription, foreign loans, loan repayments, portfolio inflows, and outward foreign investments) are restricted and require either authorised-dealer approval or SARB approval for larger or non-standard transactions. Profit remittances in the form of dividends to non-resident shareholders are permitted provided the company declares dividends out of distributable reserves from an after-tax base, has complied with shareholders’ agreements and Companies Act requirements, settles the 20% dividends tax applicable to non-resident shareholders, and provides the authorised dealer with a Completed Dividend Access/Remittance application including the share register and non-resident status proof. The key practical hurdle for a wholly or partially foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd is establishing and maintaining its non-resident status with the authorised dealer and ensuring the underlying inward share capital was formally reported through the authorised dealer’s records on SARB’s reporting system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must receive payment for export proceeds within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless an extended period has been granted by the authorised dealer or SARB. Upon receipt, proceeds must be surrendered to an authorised dealer in South Africa within 30 days, either by selling the foreign currency to the dealer for rand or by crediting it to a SARB-approved foreign currency account, which is itself held with an authorised dealer. There is no fixed mandatory percentage conversion for exports; all export proceeds arising from export of goods must be paid into a South African bank account or approved foreign currency account, and the exporter must demonstrate that the full export value has been realised in acceptable foreign currency, with the relevant export documentation retained for 5 years. This regime requires the entity to maintain export bills, airway bills/bills of lading, customs declarations, and proof of receipt to satisfy authorised-dealer audit checks.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), with authorised dealer commercial banks as delegated exchange-control administrators

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million aggregate annual international transactions across all connected or controlled-party dealings, or ZAR 5 million per individual international transaction for which the taxpayer may be called to justify pricing; Master File and Local File must be maintained and submitted within the statutory record-keeping window if the transaction value crosses the materiality threshold.

Economic substance scope: South Africa has not enacted a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to that of UAE; however, section 80A–80L (General Anti-Avoidance Rules), section 31 (Transfer Pricing), and controlled foreign company (CFC) rules under section 9D require a South African resident or controlling entity to maintain commercial substance and genuine principal-purpose documentation before treaty relief or tax benefits will be honoured. Pure shell or conduit entities without substance are subject to reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, and the imposition of penalty interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under section 234 and 235 of the Tax Administration Act, civil penalty up to 100% of the additional tax, interest charged at the prescribed rate, and criminal liability on conviction carrying a fine up to R10 million or imprisonment not exceeding 15 years, or both.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid corporate tax where they were a driving force in the evasion, civil restitution orders freeze personal assets, and travel bans under the Preservation of Administration Act may be imposed pending criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance rules (ss80A–80L) permit SARS to disregard the shell scheme, re-characterise the transaction and impose the full tax payable plus 75% to 100% adjustment penalties under s223/224; failure to maintain minimum corporate substance under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act may also attract administrative fines up to R5 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed transfer pricing or intercompany invoices without counterparty performance are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall plus interest; the corporate veil may be pierced under common law where the company is a façade for fraud.
AML / FICA / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, non-compliance attracts an administrative penalty of up to R10 million for each contravention; failure to report a reportable transaction is a criminal offence carrying fine up to R100 million, imprisonment up to 15 years, or both.

Director personal liability: Directors of the accountable institution (the ZA Pty Ltd) may be criminally prosecuted individually for repeated failure to implement AML controls, receive prohibition orders from being a director, and, on conviction, serve a personal prison term of up to 5 years in addition to the corporate fine.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Understatement

Statutory sanction: SARS may adjust the deemed arm's length price, assess additional tax at 27%, levy an understatement penalty of 25% (substantial understatement), 50% (if failure to exercise reasonable care), 75% (if intentional) or 150% (if gross negligence), and impose public disclosure under SARS's Annual Report of Tax Offenders.

Director personal liability: A director who signed off on a transfer pricing report known to be false or who refused to maintain the required documentation bears personal liability for the additional tax and penalties; under section 160 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008, a court may declare him delinquent and prohibit him from directing any company for up to 5 years.
