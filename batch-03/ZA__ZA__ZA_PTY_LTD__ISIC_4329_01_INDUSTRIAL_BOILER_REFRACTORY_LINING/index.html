Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A South African (Pty) Ltd is a tax resident company (incorporated and subject to domestic legal form under the Companies Act 71 of 2008) taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate for tax years ending on or after 31 March 2023; capital gains included at an 80% inclusion rate (effective 21.6%); distributions subject to 20% dividends tax; no pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily entity-level taxed. Elective regimes available: (i) Small Business Corporation (s12E) progressive rates of 0%, 7%, 21%, 27% on taxable income up to ZAR 550,000, available only where all shareholders are natural persons and no non-resident company holds more than 5% shareholding; (ii) Micro Business Turnover Tax (s48A-s48F) up to ZAR 1,000,000 turnover; (iii) VAT group registration (s50 VAT Act) for 51% related entities; (iv) Corporate restructure roll-over elections under s45, s46 and s47 Income Tax Act; (v) s11D research and development allowance election upon prior SARS/DTI approval; (vi) hybrid mismatch neutralisation elections under s12N effective 1 January 2024 aligned with OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory registration threshold for annual taxable supplies, s23(1) VAT Act); ZAR 50,000 (voluntary registration threshold, s24(1) VAT Act).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to a non-SACU 'export country' qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier exports the goods (s11(1)(a) VAT Act) or the buyer/agent exports them within 90 days of supply (s11(1)(c)); services supplied to non-residents not present in South Africa at the time of supply qualify under s11(2)(k) where not directly connected with land or movable property in South Africa. Claims require retention of export shipment evidence and extension of zero-rated status with the statutory reason stated on the tax invoice; SACU member state supplies (Botswana, Lesotho, Eswatini, Namibia) do not qualify unless special exclusions apply.

  • SAD500 Customs Export Declaration (approved via SARS Customs EDI/eFiling or freight forwarder consolidation report)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export to a non-SACU destination
  • Original commercial invoice (zero-rated, quoting s11(1)(a) or s11(1)(c) of the VAT Act)
  • Packing list and cargo manifest matching goods description, weights, and quantities
  • International transport/supply contract and proof of delivery (POD)
  • Proof of inward payment: SWIFT MT103 / foreign currency remittance advice (inward foreign investment allowance or modernised RCA record)
  • Non-resident customer identity documentation and destination confirmation

SARS has not yet implemented mandatory real-time fiscal clearance/CTC with digital stamping; however, the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 and VAT Act s20 mandate complete original tax invoices for all standard-rated and zero-rated supplies, issued within 21 days of the supply date and retained for 5 years. Electronic tax invoices (PDF/XML) are accepted where integrity and accessibility standards under SARS Interpretation Note 53 are met. Customs obligations are fully electronic via SARS Customs EDI/eFiling, requiring electronic filing of SAD500 declarations, and a multi-year mandatory e-invoicing programme is included in the SARS digital roadmap.

Where zero-rating cannot be substantiated or goods are diverted inside South Africa, SARS invokes the s16(3)(c) VAT Act clawback, raising additional VAT (output tax) with interest at the prescribed rate (s28 VAT Act / s187 TAA). Understatement penalties under s222/s223 TAA escalate from 10% (no culpable conduct) to 25% (not substantially in the dark), 50% (substantial understatement), 75% (lack of reasonable care) and 150% (intentional tax evasion); fraudulent export claims are further subject to criminal prosecution under s234 TAA (fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years for repeat offenders). Failure to timeously repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers SARB Exchange Control default reporting, administrative financial penalties, and criminal sanctions under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is classified as a designated currency with full convertibility on the current account, including trade-related payments, dividends, interest, and royalties, subject to documentation review by an Authorised Dealer (AD). Capital account transactions are regulated under the South African Exchange Control Regulations: non-resident direct equity investments are freely convertible inward, but liberalisation requires formal reporting through an Authorised Dealer, and exits, adverse dividend flows, or repatriation of capital may require FinSurv statistical declarations. Profit remittance is permitted without prior SARB approval provided SARS tax clearance status verifies that the distribution is from taxed local profits and the corporate law requirements (Companies Act, Memorandum of Incorporation, board resolution) are complied with. However, the burden lies on the South African entity to evidence the underlying transaction, maintain proper BOP codes, and retain all source documents for six years. Cross-border funding through foreign shareholder loans is also permitted, but must be declared and may be subject to thin capitalisation and treasury anti-abuse scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under South African Exchange Control Regulations, export proceeds for goods shipped from South Africa must be received in full within 180 days of the date of shipment unless an extension is approved (maximum practical extension is 365 days in limited circumstances). All foreign currency export proceeds must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer within 30 days of receipt and converted into ZAR at the prevailing market rate, unless the exporter has obtained an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or special exemption. The mandatory conversion ratio for ordinary trade receipts is effectively 100% of the export invoice amount, net of permitted deductions (commission, freight, insurance). The Authorised Dealer is required to obtain the relevant export documentation (customs AD480 bill of entry, commercial invoice, proof of shipment, sale contract) and to verify that the remitting bank and payment tenor are consistent with Exchange Control Circulars.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), Exchange Control Division

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM (2.5/5) as a standalone treasury rail. Offshore Electronic Money Institution (EMI) accounts are generally not recognised as Authorised Dealer bank accounts under South African exchange control and cannot serve as the principal receiving account for direct inward investments, export proceeds, or mandatory SARB reporting. They may be used as ancillary collection/payment tools for low-value disbursements, but they create income tax, VAT, and marketable securities reporting friction, and the SARB Foreign Exchange Guidelines discourage routing of cross-border trade through third-party payment processors not holding a South African bank licence. Any use of an offshore EMI for employee expense reimbursement or operational petty cash must be fully reconciled in the company's ZAR books and must not obscure the statutory BOP reporting trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing of an inbound foreign equity subscription or shareholder loan to ZA_PTY_LTD is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent/remitter's bank to the Account with Bank. The payment message must carry a clear remittance reference to the underlying share subscription or loan agreement and the South African Balance of Payments code (BOP code) for direct investment (e.g., FA 3001 for direct investment equity, or FA 3010 for direct investment loans where applicable). The Authorised Dealer will verify the purpose, check the beneficiary's FICA/KYC file, confirm SARS TCS status, and obtain the appropriate Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) reporting; typical documentary evidence includes the board resolution approving allotment of shares, the completed Form ABC (Advance Receipt of Funds), capital subscription agreement, investor due diligence pack, and where the foreign shareholder is a company, the certified extract of incumbency and ownership certificate. The bank will then credit the operational/capital escrow account in ZAR at the prevailing spot rate and produce a Foreign Exchange Transaction Advice (FETA) and a BOP return for SARB. Any currency conversion must occur only with an Authorised Dealer, and any intended use of the funds for project expenses must be supported by contractual invoices.Outbound disbursements for refractory lining projects, subcontractor procurement, and equipment purchases are cleared primarily through the ZAR domestic clearing system (SAMOS - South African Multiple Option Settlement) for local payments, while cross-border trade settlements use SWIFT MT103s for outright supplier payments and documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) under UCP 600 for import or performance guarantee structures. Each cross-border USD/EUR/GBP payment must be accompanied by the relevant BOP code (e.g., 3020 for imports, 3001 for dividend repatriation), the underlying contract, commercial invoice, and verification of SARB Form A or equivalent declaration. Where funds are in a foreign currency account, the Authorised Dealer may process direct foreign-currency settlement without conversion, provided the outflow is coded and approved. Payments to high-risk or sanctions-affected jurisdictions are blocked unless a successful advisory from the bank's sanctions team is received.
