Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of the Independent State of Samoa (formerly Samoa Tax Administration); taxpayer portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws; customs matters co-administered by the Samoa Customs Service under the Ministry for Revenue.

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is automatically ring-fenced and tax-exempt; no domestic TIN is required for offshore business. A TIN is obtained from the Ministry for Revenue only where the IC voluntarily registers for VAGST (annual turnover threshold WST 30,000), appoints resident employees (PAYE), or holds Samoan-source assets. Procedure: submit a completed IR-01 taxpayer registration/company form, certified copy of the IC certificate of incorporation and charter, registered-office consent, and director/secretary particulars; the Ministry issues the TIN/VAGST registration within five working days. The offshore exemption is not conditioned on obtaining a TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elective classification exists under Samoan law. The IC automatically receives ring-fenced offshore exemption under the International Companies Act 1987, s. 132 (no income tax, capital gains tax, estate or gift tax, stamp duty, or exchange-control obligations). The only available election is voluntary VAGST registration under the Goods and Services Tax Act 2003 for domestic-source supplies, which does not impair the IC's income-tax exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration where annual taxable turnover exceeds WST 30,000, or where taxable supplies are made through a registered branch in Samoa; voluntary registration is available for smaller suppliers. International Companies carrying on exclusively offshore activities are not required to register and are outside the VAGST net.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero rate applies to goods physically exported from Samoa within 30 days of supply (direct exports) and to offshore services supplied to non-residents, including fund-management, buyout, advisory, and syndication services rendered by a private-equity SPV to non-Samoan funds and investors. The IC's offshore equity-buyout activities are outside the scope of VAGST. Where the IC is VAGST-registered, input VAGST on Samoan-sourced acquisition costs (e.g., legal and registered-office fees) is refundable through the standard refund mechanism upon production of export/offshore-supply evidence and filing of a nil or credit return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec) electronically lodged and cleared by the Samoa Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation
  • Commercial invoice and the underlying acquisition/sale agreement for the buyout transaction
  • VAGST tax invoice and export invoice issued by the IC (if VAGST-registered)
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing payment by the non-resident buyer
  • Insurance certificate and, where required, certificate of origin

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime is in force. Registered suppliers must issue serially-numbered tax invoices showing supplier and recipient names, TINs, description, VAGST-exclusive amount, VAGST amount, and date; export invoices must include the export entry reference. All invoices and underlying records must be retained for the statutory record-keeping period under the Tax Administration Act 2015.

The Ministry for Revenue may reassess and claw back any VAGST refund or zero-rated treatment where export documentation or the inward remittance is absent. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating attracts a shortfall penalty of up to 100% (aggravated for concealment), late-payment interest, and criminal sanctions for wilful evasion, including fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment; directors and the licensed resident agent may be jointly and severally liable. Repatriation of export proceeds must be evidenced within 180 days of export; otherwise the zero-rating is reversed and the resulting tax and penalties are immediately recoverable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is convertible for current-account transactions under a CBS-managed float; resident capital-account outflows require exchange-control approval through authorized dealers. A Samoa International Company is statutorily treated as non-resident and exempted from exchange-control restrictions on transactions settled entirely in foreign currency, so capital injection, profit remittance, and dividend distributions face no CBS approval hurdle. The only practical constraint on profit remittance is correspondent-bank availability and completion of the bank's outward-remittance form when WST conversion is needed; no tax-clearance certificate is required because the IC holds a statutory income-tax exemption under the International Companies Act 1987.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must repatriate proceeds within 180 days of shipment and credit or surrender them to an authorized dealer in WST. International Companies are expressly carved out: foreign-currency receipts are exempt from any surrender or conversion obligation, and the CBS grants a standing exemption upon application by the licensed registered agent. The PE SPV will denominate all contributions, loans, and target payables in USD, thereby never triggering the surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), acting through its Exchange Control Division under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977; licensed commercial banks operate as authorized foreign exchange dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Major electronic money institutions and payment fintechs routinely decline Samoan International Companies because their correspondent-risk matrices classify the jurisdiction as an offshore financial center with elevated velocity risk. Where an offshore EMI (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) does accept a Samoa IC, it demands the licensed registered-agent confirmation, fund-level financials or administrator confirmation, and perpetual beneficial-ownership disclosure; the EMI cannot access WST domestic clearing because the Central Bank of Samoa has not licensed any local EMI. The EMI rail is strictly auxiliary for this SPV: small professional-fee payments only, never syndicated-loan proceeds or acquisition escrow.

Late filing causes monetary penalty assessments by the Registry (generally WST 100 per month or part thereof), suspension of the certificate of good standing, and potential strike-off and deregistration if arrears remain unpaid for a statutory period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory local-file or master-file threshold applies to a tax-exempt International Company. For resident companies subject to Samoa income tax, contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is expected for controlled transactions exceeding WST 500,000 in aggregate value; OECD TP principles are persuasive under Samoa general anti-avoidance rules.

Economic substance scope: An International Company must maintain its character as an offshore investment holding vehicle: it cannot carry on business in Samoa and must preserve a registered agent, minutes of meetings and registers. Samoa applies the OECD/Global Forum economic-substance expectations to offshore entities; a pure equity-holding PE SPV is generally not subject to separate Economic Substance Regulations filing in Samoa if it has no Samoan source income. However, if the SPV is deemed managed and controlled in Samoa, the claim of non-residence could be challenged and tax exemption revoked.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Back-assessment at the 27% corporate rate, penalty up to 150% of the undercharged tax, default interest, and criminal prosecution; statutory fines plus imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalty; personal civil restitution orders, travel restrictions, and imprisonment can be imposed.
Shell Company / Sham Transaction

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act, the Court may declare the company defunct, order deregistration and strike-off, forfeit its assets, and impose monetary penalties on the company and those in control.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil leads to director and shareholder personal liability for third-party debts and tort claims; disqualification from acting as a director and loss of capacity to hold a Samoan registered agent licence.
AML / Financial Crime Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with Samoa AML/CFT laws attracts fines of up to WST 1,000,000, freezing and forfeiture orders, and imprisonment of responsible officers for up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be arrested, prevented from leaving Samoa, and held liable for civil restitution to victims; corporate veil protection is expressly lifted for money-laundering offences.
