Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) — administered through the SARS eFiling portal (efiling.sars.gov.za); customs and excise matters are handled by the SARS Customs Division.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first be incorporated or registered as an external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain a company registration number. The entity then applies via SARS eFiling or a branch office for income tax registration using the applicable SARS registration form (e.g., IT77A for companies), receiving a 10-digit income tax reference number. For Value-Added Tax (VAT), the entity must submit a VAT 101 application to SARS and, once registered under the VAT Act, is issued a unique vendor VAT number. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services may follow the simplified SARS electronic services registration process where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at entity level, while a non-resident branch is taxed on South African-source income. — A foreign entity must first be incorporated or registered as an external company with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain a company registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — a South African resident (Pty) Ltd is taxed on worldwide income at entity level, while a non-resident branch is taxed on South African-source income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under South African tax law. A proprietary limited company is always treated as a separate taxpayer for income tax, VAT, and dividends tax. Controlled foreign company (CFC) rules under section 9D of the Income Tax Act, thin-capitalisation rules, and anti-hybrid-mismatch provisions (including sections 8E and 23H) apply without an elective classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period. Voluntary registration: taxable supplies exceeding ZAR 50,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A South African VAT vendor may apply the 0% rate to exported collectible trading cards if the supply is made to a non-resident customer and the goods are physically removed from South Africa and delivered outside the Republic within the period prescribed by the VAT Act. The vendor must obtain and retain prescribed documentary proof of export, including customs endorsements and transport documentation. Services physically rendered offshore or supplied to non-residents under qualifying conditions may also be zero-rated. For goods, the supplier must not process, further produce, or use the goods locally after the export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 form) as accepted by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing physical export and overseas delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation showing receipt of export proceeds in a South African bank account

South Africa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance of transaction data with SARS. Electronic tax invoices are accepted provided they comply with section 20 of the VAT Act and preserve the authenticity and integrity of content. SARS eFiling is used for VAT returns; electronic tax invoices and underlying records must be retained for the statutory period (generally five years).

SARS applies strict audit scrutiny to zero-rated export claims. If the required export documentation is incomplete, unavailable, or fraudulent, the zero-rating is disallowed and the supply is taxed at the standard 15% VAT rate. SARS may raise an additional VAT assessment with statutory interest and impose an understatement penalty of up to 150% depending on the taxpayer's conduct under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. The submission of false export evidence or tax evasion may also trigger criminal prosecution and personal liability for directors or representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is quasi-convertible. Current-account transactions are freely transferable where supported by genuine underlying commercial documents and SARS tax compliance. Profit remittances in the form of dividends to non-resident shareholders are permitted without SARB approval provided the company satisfies the Companies Act solvency and liquidity test, the dividend is funded from current profits, dividend tax is withheld, and a SARS tax clearance statement (TAX C) is presented to the authorised dealer. Interest, royalties, and technical service fees on current account may also be remitted, subject to withholding taxes and SARS tax clearance. Capital-account convertibility is controlled: inward direct equity investment by non-residents into ordinary shares does not require prior SARB approval, but outward capital repatriation, share buy-backs, capital reductions, inbound shareholder loans, guarantees, and cross-border hedging arrangements require authorised-dealer reporting and, in certain cases, prior FinSurv approval. 'Loop structure' arrangements and round-tripping of funds are prohibited unless approved by FinSurv.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For export of goods, including collectible trading cards, the exporter must ensure full payment is received no later than 180 days from the date of shipment. All export proceeds received in foreign currency must be brought into South Africa and credited to a South African authorised-dealer account within 30 days of receipt. The mandatory default conversion is 100% of the foreign currency receipt into ZAR at the ruling market rate, unless the exporter has been granted an Exporter's Foreign Currency Account (EFCA) by an authorised dealer under FinSurv guidelines. Supporting evidence includes the SAD500 customs export declaration, commercial invoice, proof of shipment, bill of lading or airway bill, and the remittance instruction. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory timeline constitutes an exchange-control violation and triggers SARB/FinSurv corrective and punitive measures.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, and the Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, subject to critical legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs and payment fintechs are not authorised dealers for South African exchange-control purposes and cannot provide a primary ZAR settlement rail for export receipts. A resident (Pty) Ltd directing export card-sale proceeds into an offshore EMI account creates a technical surrender breach unless funds are immediately repatriated through an authorised dealer and supported by export documentation. Such EMIs may be used only as a foreign-currency collection sub-rail or as an e-commerce gateway aggregator, provided settlement is ultimately swept by SWIFT MT103 to the South African bank account and all underlying Stripe/Authorize_Net orders are reconciled. Any entity holding merchant funds in South Africa must comply with the National Payment System Act and FIC registration requirements; a foreign EMI without such licensing cannot act as a standalone local clearing house.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for non-resident shareholder capital injection: (i) obtain board approval for the share allotment and update the MOI and CIPC share register; (ii) the non-resident shareholder remits funds from its own verified foreign bank account via SWIFT MT103 using remittance reference 'Direct investment - equity subscription' or the authorised dealer's prescribed Balance of Payments purpose code for equity capital; third-party aggregation of investment funds is prohibited without full KYC and source-of-funds verification; (iii) the receiving South African authorised dealer issues an Inward Transfer of Funds confirmation and reports the transaction to SARB/FinSurv for balance-of-payments recording; (iv) funds are held in a designated Capital Escrow Account or operational account, and the bank releases funds into the operating treasury upon production of the share subscription agreement, proof of CIPC filing, and a board resolution; (v) no prior SARB approval is required for ordinary inward share subscription by a non-resident, but shareholder loan capital must be documented by a loan agreement and is reportable to the authorised dealer. For every capital inflow, the company must preserve the SWIFT MT103/MT202 record, the inward transfer certificate, the board resolution, and the final CIPC securities register for a minimum of five years.Clearing rails for operational and procurement disbursements: (i) direct supplier payments for imported raw material, holographic cold foil, security substrates, and printing consumables are made through the South African bank as outward SWIFT MT103 transfers, accompanied by the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill, and SAD500 customs import documentation;
