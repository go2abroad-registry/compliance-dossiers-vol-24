Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Government of Samoa; official portal and e-services gateway: https://www.sra.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the International Company (IC) with the Samoa International Companies Registry, appointing a licensed registered agent and registered office in Samoa. 2. Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation; the IC is automatically a non-resident exempt person under the International Companies Act 1987. 3. Apply to the Samoa Revenue Authority for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) only if a Samoa tax presence is created; submission requires the prescribed TIN application form, certificate of incorporation, registered-agent confirmation, and beneficial-owner identification. 4. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election exist in Samoa. An International Company automatically receives exempt status and may elect to convert to a domestic Samoa company, which would subject it to ordinary Samoa income tax and VAGST on Samoa-source activity. A non-residence certificate may be obtained from the Samoa Ministry for Revenue to evidence tax-exempt status for foreign counterparties.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Samoa's VAGST Act 2008, zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Samoa and to services supplied to a non-resident recipient where the benefit and use of the services occur outside Samoa. For the AI subtitle transcription and voice cloning studio, the following conditions must be met: the contract is with an overseas studio, platform, or rights holder; the deliverables are digitally transmitted outside Samoa; payment is received from outside Samoa; and no Samoan audience or local market exploitation is made available. Services auxiliary to exported goods may also be zero-rated if the direct benefit is linked to the exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for physical goods; for pure digital services, a Declaration of Export of Services is used)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for physical media; not required for pure digital delivery)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/credit advice proving foreign exchange receipt from abroad
  • Signed service contract with the non-resident client
  • Digital delivery log, download/use record, and technical service report demonstrating offshore consumption
  • Zero-rated VAGST export tax invoice

No mandatory real-time electronic invoicing clearance or digital stamping regime is currently imposed by Samoa. Businesses may issue paper or electronic VAGST tax invoices; the SRA does not require API-based fiscal clearance. Standard invoice content must show the supplier's name, TIN, recipient details, description, date, VAGST-exclusive amount, VAGST amount, and total. Electronic records must be retained and accessible for audit in Samoa.

The Samoa Revenue Authority may audit export zero-rating claims. If export or foreign-consumption evidence is deficient, the SRA will reverse the zero rating and assess VAGST at 15% on the supply value, with statutory interest. Civil penalties vary by culpability: a shortfall penalty for failure to take reasonable care, and a higher penalty for intentional evasion or fraud. Fraudulent export documentation may also trigger criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks are governed by the Tax Administration Act 2012, with extended periods for fraudulent claims.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Samoa has no gazetted statutory Local File / Master File monetary threshold; the SRA may apply the arm's length principle and the general anti-avoidance rule to related-party transactions under the Income Tax Act 1974 and Tax Administration Act 2012. Prudent compliance practice: prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File style) where annual connected-party turnover in the AI subtitle and dubbing value chain exceeds WST 1,000,000 or where large IP royalty or licence payments are made to other group entities.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies Act 2007 and the related Economic Substance Rules require Samoan licensed ICs to demonstrate substantial local economic presence when conducting relevant activities (including financing, leasing, distribution and service centres, IP holding, trust and fiduciary services). The IC must maintain a registered office and licensed agent in Samoa, keep adequate accounting records locally, and have proportionate physical premises, resident employees, local board meetings and expenditure. A passive shell used merely as a tax conduit will be denied the tax exemption and reclassified as taxable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Income and Withholding Tax Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty tax up to 200% of the tax shortfall plus late payment interest; criminal fines and imprisonment up to 7 years; any tax advantage and exemption is disgorged retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who caused or knowingly aided the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; travel bans and imprisonment apply on conviction; the corporate veil is pierced where the IC is a mere facade.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the IC's tax-exempt status, re-assessment at the 27% corporate rate, denial of treaty benefits, penalties up to WST 100,000 for false substance declarations, and deregistration from the International Companies Register.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil recovery from personal assets, disqualification from acting as directors of any Samoan licensed entity, and blocking notices restricting international travel where the Registrar determines reckless or fraudulent use of the IC.
AML / Beneficial Ownership and Counterparty Due Diligence Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act, companies face monetary penalties and the licensed agent may be fined or deregistered; non-compliant ICs are liable to submission of transaction monitoring reports and confiscation of funds linked to money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for systemic AML failures, with imprisonment, travel bans and permanent debarment from management of regulated Samoan entities.
Transfer Pricing / Cross-Border Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: SRA re-characterisation of the transaction, disallowance of excessive deductions, shortfall penalties of up to 50%, imposition of interest on the corrected liability, and cancellation of treaty or exemption benefits where the recipient is not the true beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the related-party agreements may be assessed personally for the corrected tax shortfall and, where the conduct is deliberate, prosecuted for tax default with imprisonment and travel restrictions.
