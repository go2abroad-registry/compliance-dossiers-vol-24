Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MFR), Government of Samoa; MFR eServices taxpayer portal at https://www.mfr.gov.ws. The Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) administers incorporation, licensing, and annual government fees for International Companies (ICs) under the International Companies Act 1987 (ICA 1987).

E-government filing portal

A foreign beneficial owner must engage a SIFA-licensed registered agent (a Samoa trust or corporate management company) to incorporate the IC. The agent files the Memorandum and Articles of Association; SIFA issues a Certificate of Incorporation with a company registration number and annual fee schedule (minimum USD 200 for authorized capital up to USD 10,000; maximum USD 2,000). The agent then applies to MFR for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) through the electronic Taxpayer Registration System (eTRS), submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, registered agent consent, beneficial ownership declaration, and foreign address of directors. The TIN is required for bank account opening, GST registration, and any customs transactions. Because the IC is exempt from income tax under ICA 1987 s.191 and does not derive Samoa-source assessable income, no corporate income tax registration is required beyond the TIN; however, a Foreign Investment Certificate under the Foreign Investment Act 2000 may be needed if the IC engages in any local or licensed activity in Samoa other than purely offshore advisory business. All filings with SIFA and MFR are transacted through the registered agent due to the non-resident nature of the directors and shareholders.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: A Samoa IC is a resident-incorporated limited company but is statutorily exempt from Samoa income tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, and estate duty on all income, assets, and transactions, provided it does not carry on business in Samoa with Samoa residents or own Samoa real property. The IC is not a pass-through entity for tax purposes; it is an entity-level exempt company. Its non-resident shareholders receive dividends free of Samoa withholding tax, and no branch profit tax applies. Foreign-source fees, including study-abroad placement commissions and immigration/golden-visa consultancy income earned exclusively from non-resident clients, fall within the offshore exemption zone and are subject to 0% taxation in Samoa.

Available election forms

Under the ICA 1987 and the Tax Administration Act 2012, the IC may: (1) elect in writing to be treated as a non-resident company for income tax purposes, reinforcing the offshore exemption; (2) apply to MFR for a binding tax ruling confirming the absence of a Samoa permanent establishment and the zero-rating of offshore services; (3) elect not to register for GST where all supplies are zero-rated exports and input credits are immaterial; and (4) elect to continue or redomicile to another jurisdiction under ICA 1987 s.230 as a defensive restructuring measure. Samoa has not enacted OECD hybrid-mismatch rules or a US-style check-the-box election; the general anti-avoidance provisions in the Tax Administration Act 2012 (ss.64-66) would disallow any arrangement whose sole purpose is to use the IC to defeat the tax law of a treaty or non-treaty jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (approximately USD 36,000) in annual taxable supplies over any rolling 12-month period, as prescribed in section 14 of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1992; non-resident suppliers of remote/digital services to Samoa consumers are separately registrable under the cross-border services rules. A Samoa IC whose only supplies are zero-rated export services to non-residents may apply for exemption from GST registration; the registered agent should confirm the current threshold with MFR, as it is subject to ministerial adjustment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An IC may claim 0% GST and income-tax exemption on fees derived from (1) the direct export of goods from Samoa; (2) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time the services are performed and who does not consume the services in Samoa; and (3) advisory services in the ISIC 7810 scope, namely international student placement and immigration/golden-visa consultancy, provided the IC has no Samoa-resident client, no Samoa office employees, and no local asset usage. The escrow-account and wire-transfer payment rails must evidence settlement by non-resident principals. Input VAT on local agent fees, legal fees, and bank charges is creditable against any output VAT; where zero-rated outputs exceed taxable outputs, the IC may claim a cash refund from MFR within 60 days of the quarterly return, subject to document verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SGD 1 form) for any physical document or package shipped outside Samoa
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement out of Samoa (if applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Samoa commercial bank evidencing receipt of client fees from abroad
  • Executed service agreement and zero-rated tax invoices identifying the non-resident client, the offshore service description, and the stated ISIC 7810 activity
  • SIFA Certificate of Good Standing and MFR confirmation letter attesting the IC's tax-exempt status
  • Beneficial ownership statement and AML/KYC file for the escrow payment account

Samoa does not currently impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing mandate. GST-registered ICs must file quarterly GST returns electronically through MFR eServices by the last day of the month following each quarter. Tax invoices must contain the supplier's name and TIN, invoice number and date, description of the zero-rated or taxable supply, GST amount, and total consideration. For physical document shipments, customs data must be transmitted through the ASYCUDA World customs system; for pure service exports (study-abroad placement and visa advisory), no customs clearance is required and the contractual invoice package serves as the fiscal control record. MFR reserves the right to require e-invoicing data in CSV/PDF format upon audit demand.

MFR conducts desk and field audits of zero-rating claims; failure to substantiate the offshore nature of services within the 180-day repatriation window triggers retrospective clawback of all GST refunds plus statutory late-payment interest (approximately 10% per annum). Fraudulent or reckless claims attract shortfall penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2012: 20% base penalty for mere error, 75% for gross negligence, and 150% for intentional evasion, alongside criminal prosecution of the directors and the registered agent with fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years for falsifying export documents. The Central Bank of Samoa may additionally impose exchange-control penalties for unrepatriated export earnings, and SIFA may strike off the IC and revoke the registered agent's license where the IC is used to facilitate fraudulent visa or tax avoidance schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a managed-float regime pegged to an undisclosed weighted basket of partner currencies dominated by NZD, AUD, and USD. Current-account transactions for trade in services, including education, study-abroad placement, and immigration advisory fees, are freely convertible through authorized dealer banks without central bank pre-approval. Capital-account movements, including foreign-currency borrowing by Samoan residents, require Exchange Control approval. An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is statutorily treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes: it may maintain foreign-currency deposits with authorized dealers, freely remit dividends, advisory fees, and liquidation proceeds abroad, and operate without converting receipts into WST. Profit and capital remittance from a Samoan IC to non-resident shareholders or ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs) is not subject to Samoan withholding tax, provided the IC's income-tax exemption certificate remains valid and the bank receives standard evidential filings: certificate of good standing, board resolution authorizing the payment, and, for dividend distributions, a directors' solvency declaration registered with the Registrar of International Companies. Residual practical impediments arise only from the correspondent-bank compliance layer of the receiving bank jurisdiction, not from Samoan exchange-control measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Samoan resident trading entities, the Exchange Control Regulations 1984 and CBS practice require foreign-currency export proceeds to be declared, repatriated through an authorized dealer, and either credited to a CBS-approved foreign-currency account or converted into WST, with the standard export declaration form referencing a 30-day realization timeline from shipment. A mandatory 100% conversion ratio applies only where proceeds are brought into WST as a matter of CBS direction, not as an automatic rule. International Companies are outside the resident exchange-control net: no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to an IC's foreign-currency receipts. For the ISIC 7810 study-abroad and immigration-visa consultancy scope, the 'export' consists of cross-border professional services; such fees may be credited directly to the IC's foreign-currency account at an authorized dealer without conversion to WST. WST conversion is undertaken only to fund specific local operating liabilities in Samoa, and the IC should maintain a dual-currency structure to avoid unnecessary bid-offer spreads.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), Exchange Control Department, acting under the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Exchange Control Regulations 1984, and the Financial Institutions Act 1996.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail. Samoa has no electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime; the Financial Institutions Act 1996 restricts deposit-taking and money-transmission activities to licensed banks and licensed money-changers, preventing any offshore digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) from legally operating a Samoa-domiciled settlement account or sponsoring a Samoan IC as a direct participant. Offshore EMIs may be used only as an ancillary inbound collection channel for client fees from foreign jurisdictions, with the IC thereafter executing a weekly consolidation sweep to its BSP Samoan account. Legal constraints include: (i) EMI client funds held in a foreign pooled account are not client-money protected under Samoan law; (ii) Samoan licensed banks will reject outbound instructions sourced from an unlicensed EMI due to liability and sanctions-screening concerns; and (iii) the IC's own AML/CFT obligations under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 require look-through evidence of the underlying payor, which foreign EMIs often do not provide at the required granularity. Recommended architecture: BSP Samoa as the master treasury account; a licensed New Zealand or Australian bank as the collection and disbursement hub for the global Chinese/other client base; and an offshore EMI only for low-value, non-recurring receipt niches not exceeding 20% of aggregate monthly receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the shareholder's bank to the IC's foreign-currency account at BSP Samoa, using remittance information field 70 identifying 'CAPT' (capital contribution / share subscription) under the ISO 20022 or SWIFT purpose conventions. The receiving bank requires the following verification pack: (i) board resolution authorizing the share issue and identifying the bank account; (ii) updated register of members evidencing the subscribing shareholder; (iii) beneficial-ownership declaration for the UBO; and (iv) source-of-wealth summary for the shareholder, notarized in the jurisdiction of origin. For funds destined to be used as intercompany working capital rather than share capital, the routing is identical but purpose-field 'SLOA' (shareholder loan) must be displayed, and an executed loan agreement with a commercial interest rate must be pre-filed to avoid transfer-pricing or thin-capitalization challenge in the shareholder's home jurisdiction. Rail 2 - government and embassy visa-fee payments: direct SWIFT MT103 to the official fee-collection accounts of the relevant immigration authority or authorized embassy, with the application number in the payment narrative to ensure correct allocation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail. Samoa has no electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime; the Financial Institutions Act 1996 restricts deposit-taking and money-transmission activities to licensed banks and licensed money-changers, preventing any offshore digital EMI (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) from legally operating a Samoa-domiciled settlement account or sponsoring a Samoan IC as a direct participant. Offshore EMIs may be used only as an ancillary inbound collection channel for client fees from foreign jurisdictions, with the IC thereafter executing a weekly consolidation sweep to its BSP Samoan account. Legal constraints include: (i) EMI client funds held in a foreign pooled account are not client-money protected under Samoan law; (ii) Samoan licensed banks will reject outbound instructions sourced from an unlicensed EMI due to liability and sanctions-screening concerns; and (iii) the IC's own AML/CFT obligations under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 require look-through evidence of the underlying payor, which foreign EMIs often do not provide at the required granularity. Recommended architecture: BSP Samoa as the master treasury account; a licensed New Zealand or Australian bank as the collection and disbursement hub for the global Chinese/other client base; and an offshore EMI only for low-value, non-recurring receipt niches not exceeding 20% of aggregate monthly receipts.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for an International Company (IC) on income derived from business conducted wholly outside Samoa under the International Companies Act 1987, s. 223; 25% standard corporate income tax (CIT) rate under the Income Tax Act 2015 for any resident company deriving income from a Samoan source.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% final withholding tax on dividends or distributions paid by an International Company out of exempt offshore profits; 15% final withholding tax (FWT) under the Income Tax Act 2015 on dividends paid by a resident Samoan company to a non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory threshold in Samoan Tala (WST) is legislated for mandatory Local File or Master File preparation. The Commissioner for Inland Revenue may demand contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for any cross-border related-party transaction without a de minimis threshold. For an ISIC 7810 study-abroad and immigration-visa consultancy, any management fee, commission, or referral fee paid to a Chinese or Hong Kong affiliate should be documented as a matter of practice where the annual value of related-party transactions exceeds WST 500,000.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 and the SIFA Economic Substance Guidance apply to International Companies engaged in relevant activities. The IC must be managed and controlled in Samoa, maintain adequate physical premises and qualified employees in Samoa, and incur proportionate local expenditure. For a study-abroad and immigration-visa advisory entity, the relevant activity is typically characterized as a 'distribution and service centre' or 'service business' where services are rendered to foreign related parties; where the IC only serves unrelated offshore clients, it must nonetheless file an Economic Substance Declaration and demonstrate genuine presence, or retain its IC tax exemption under the International Companies Act 1987, s. 223.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abusive Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of hidden income at the 25% CIT rate; shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded; compounding late payment interest at 2% per month; deregistration of the International Company and loss of the s. 223 exemption; criminal prosecution for tax evasion carrying fines and custodial terms.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors risk personal liability where the corporate form is a mere facade; courts may pierce the corporate veil to collect unpaid tax and penalties; directors convicted of tax evasion face imprisonment and travel bans imposed by the Samoan courts at the MfR's request.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the International Companies Act 1987 and the Economic Substance Regulations 2019; strike-off and deregistration by the Registrar; revocation of the IC tax exemption with retroactive taxation at 25% CIT, including compensatory interest and penalties; exclusion from the international financial centre register.

Director personal liability: Nominee and executive directors may be disqualified from holding office in licensed Samoan entities, fined personally, and held civilly liable for the resulting tax liabilities; the SIFA registrar may issue removal orders and referral to the Attorney General for criminal proceedings.
AML/CFT Violations (Medium-Risk Enhanced Banking Tier)

Statutory sanction: Offences under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2018: corporate fines up to WST 1,000,000 per offence; natural persons subject to fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years; forfeiture of funds; revocation of the IC's banking and SIFA licensing privileges.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and nominated officers are personally prosecutable for AML failures; personal asset freezing and confiscation orders may be issued; convicted directors face imprisonment and prohibition from managing or controlling any financial or corporate service business in Samoa.
Unlicensed Immigration Advisory and Ultra Vires Domestic Trading

Statutory sanction: Licensing penalties and consumer-protection sanctions; cancellation of the IC registration; the fees derived from prohibited onshore activity treated as Samoan-source income and taxed at 25% CIT plus penalties; the Registrar may refuse reinstatement and publicize the violation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized unlicensed domestic advisory activity face fines and prosecution for acting without a required license; civil restitution to affected clients may be ordered; international immigration authorities may impose adverse travel and visa consequences on directors engaged in fraudulent immigration advisory practices.
