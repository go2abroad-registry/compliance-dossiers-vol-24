Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) - national tax and customs authority; official electronic portal: SARS eFiling at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; corporate registration interface: Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) at https://www.cipc.co.za

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the (Pty) Ltd with CIPC to obtain the company registration number, which is automatically populated by SARS as the Income Tax reference number. Step 2: Activate the company profile on SARS eFiling, appoint a SARS Public Officer (a South African resident), and update registered address, directors, and banking details. Step 3: Apply for a Tax Compliance Status (TCS) and confirm provisional taxpayer status. Step 4: For VAT, submit the VAT101 online via eFiling attaching the certified CIPC registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, directors' identity/passport copies, and bank confirmation letter; SARS issues a separate VAT vendor number with an effective registration date and filing cycle. Step 5: For import/export activities, register as an importer/exporter with SARS Customs to obtain a customs code and activate the customs eFiling module; additional licensing from the South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) is mandatory for biosimilar manufacturing and export of monoclonal antibody products. Non-resident parent companies without a South African permanent establishment are not required to register for income tax unless deriving South African-source income under the Income Tax Act, but the resident subsidiary remains fully registrable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box system; tax classification is statutory rather than elective. Relevant statutory elections available to a (Pty) Ltd include: (i) election as a Headquarter Company under section 9I of the Income Tax Act to ring-fence foreign-source dividends, interest, royalties, and CFC income for qualifying South African groups; (ii) joint election under section 8F(7) and section 8FA to reclassify hybrid debt or hybrid share instruments between debt and equity to neutralise hybrid mismatch outcomes; (iii) section 12C/12E capital allowance elections for manufacturing assets, subject to small business corporation turnover limits; and (iv) VAT group registration election under section 50 of the VAT Act allowing two or more registered vendors in a group to be treated as a single vendor.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when taxable supplies exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period; voluntary registration is available where taxable supplies exceed ZAR 50,000 in any 12-month period; municipal/parastatal supplies and certain deemed supplies may trigger registration earlier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act for goods exported by the vendor in the ordinary course of enterprise, provided the goods are physically removed from South Africa within 60 days of the date of supply (or despatched within 60 days and exported within a longer period as SARS allows). For services, section 11(2) zero-rates services supplied to a non-resident where the services are not used or consumed in South Africa and the non-resident is not in South Africa at the time of supply. A biosimilar manufacturer exporting monoclonal antibody products must hold a valid supply agreement with a non-resident purchaser, retain complete export documentation, prove physical export via customs records, and report zero-rated supplies separately on VAT201 Schedule 1. Any refund claim arising from persistent zero-rated export trading is subject to SARS VAT refund verification and may be delayed pending audit clearance.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD500/EX1) capturing the VAT vendor name, VAT registration number, HS code 3002 (monoclonal antibodies), and export particulars
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement of the goods out of South Africa to the non-resident customer
  • Commercial invoice (full tax invoice as defined in section 20 of the VAT Act) cross-referenced to the VAT201 Schedule 1 export listing
  • Proof of foreign payment: SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation or bank inward foreign transfer voucher / Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing settlement by the non-resident buyer
  • SAHPRA export permit / batch release certificate for the biosimilar biological product
  • Packing list, signed delivery receipt, weight certificate, and cold-chain temperature log as applicable
  • Forex conversion declaration where payment is denominated in a foreign currency

South Africa does not mandate continuous transaction controls (CTC), real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. However, SARS mandates electronic filing of VAT201 returns and schedules through eFiling using the official XML/PDF templates. Tax invoices must satisfy the full tax invoice requirements of section 20 of the VAT Act, and for exports must cross-reference the SAD500 customs declaration and export schedule. Vendors with turnover exceeding ZAR 10 million are additionally obligated to submit electronic turnover, purchase, and financial data to SARS under section 25H of the Tax Administration Act and the SARS Data Submission framework; this supports SARS auto-assessment and risk profiling but does not constitute transactional fiscal clearance.

SARS and the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) jointly scrutinise export zero-rating claims by cross-matching VAT export schedules against the customs SAD500 register and bank inward payment records. Failure to export goods within the statutory period or to obtain and preserve the required documentation results in the reversal of zero-rating and assessment of output VAT at 15% plus interest. Additional tax under section 60 of the VAT Act may be imposed up to 200% of the VAT evaded where non-compliance involves intent. Understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act range from 10% (substantial understatement) to 25% (failure to take reasonable care), 50% (no reasonable grounds), 100% (gross negligence), and 150% (intentional tax evasion). Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export claims may result in fines up to ZAR 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. Exchange control violations, including failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days without SARB approval, render the exporter liable to pay the un-repatriated amount into the National Revenue Fund, with administrative penalties of up to 40% of the contravention amount and potential suspension of the exporter's customs registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is fully convertible on current account (trade, non-trade services, dividends, interest and royalties) insofar as bona fide transactions are routed through a SARB-appointed Authorised Dealer with supporting documentary evidence and no prior FinSurv approval is required. Capital account flows (equity injections, shareholder loans, acquisition of offshore assets or offshore bank accounts) remain subject to the general permission or specific prior approval of the Financial Surveillance Department. Profit remittances by way of dividend to non-resident shareholders are lawfully executable without prior exchange control approval only where the underlying share subscription was formally recorded as inward foreign capital with an Authorised Dealer and assigned an inward investment N-code; the remitting bank further requires a SARS tax clearance status PIN and payment of dividend tax at 20%. All remittances sourced from unlisted domestic credit or unauthorised local leverage are blocked at the payer bank, creating a significant treasury hurdle for the seed-capital and plant-fit-out phase of a biosimilar manufacturing entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulation 7(2), every South African exporter must procure that the full export proceeds (100% of the invoiced value) are remitted into South Africa within 180 days from the date of export shipment, unless an extended period has been expressly approved in writing by the Authorised Dealer or FinSurv. Unless a Foreign Currency Account has been approved under Regulation 8A, 100% of the export receipts must be surrendered and/or converted into rand through the Authorised Dealer at the prevailing market rate; there is no optional retention or fixed partial-conversion ratio. The Company must match each export physical movement with a customs bill of entry and close out the export obligation through the bank's electronic trade reporting platform; advance-payment, documentary collection, open-account and letter-of-credit settlements are all permissible for monoclonal-antibody consignments provided the 180-day repatriation timeline is observed.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) — Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961, as amended

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (including Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud and similar) are not SARB-authorised Authorised Dealers under the Exchange Control Regulations and cannot lawfully act as the primary cross-border clearing venue for South African corporate treasury flows. Export proceeds may not be collected directly onto an offshore EMI without FinSurv approval of a Foreign Currency Account and must still be repatriated through a domestic Authorised Dealer within the 180-day window. For a high-risk Special-Licence biopharma manufacturer, the EMI route is operationally impractical and exposes the company to exchange-control penalties, so the EMI channel should be limited to ancillary supplier pay-out support while all statutory trade payments and receipts execute through regulated South African AD banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must execute the inward remittance via SWIFT MT103 for direct transfers, or MT202 (with MT103 cover) where an intermediary bank is used, crediting the South African Authorised Dealer bank account of the Pty Ltd. The wire narrative must reference the full legal name of the company, the share-subscription or loan agreement, and the inward FDI purpose code (equity capital subscription or shareholder loan). The Authorised Dealer will register the inward flow under the South African balance-of-payments framework and issue an inward foreign investment N-reference number, which becomes the critical escrow marker for future dividend or loan repayments. Funds are held in the Capital Escrow Account or operational account and converted to ZAR at the ruling market rate unless the Company has obtained approval to maintain a Foreign Currency Account. For shareholder loans, the executed loan agreement (with tenor, interest rate, repayment and default provisions) must be lodged with the AD before funds are released; ongoing repatriation capacity is preserved by booking each inflow as a foreign liability on the AD's reporting platform.Import settlements for bioreactor consumables, chromatography resins and raw API media shall be effected via SWIFT MT103 for high-volume disbursements below the AD's enhanced-due-diligence threshold, each accompanied by the underlying invoice, import bill of entry and tax clearance; larger or strategically critical procurement executes through confirmed Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700/MT707 and negotiated under UCP 600, with USD or EUR clearing through New York or Frankfurt correspondent banks.
