Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - the Samoa International Company is statutorily classified as an offshore non-resident company wholly exempt from Samoan income tax, address tax, VAGST, and stamp duty on operations conducted outside Samoa (International Companies Act 1987, s.220; Income Tax Act 2016, s.4; VAGST Act 2003).

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Samoa. The IC's exempt classification is fixed by statute and evidenced by a SIFA/SRA Tax Exemption Certificate. Available statutory elections: (i) voluntary VAGST registration under VAGST Act 2003, s.8, even below the WST 80,000 threshold; (ii) elective continuance/redomiciliation out of Samoa or conversion into a resident domestic company under the Companies Act 2006, which terminates the exemption; (iii) binding ruling applications to the Commissioner under the Tax Administration Act 2016 on whether a foreign-issued instrument is debt or equity; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act 2016 apply mandatorily to deny double non-taxation, with no elective carve-out for ICs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 80,000 (Samoan Tala) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period, or expected next-12-months taxable turnover; all persons carrying on a taxable activity in Samoa crossing the threshold must register (VAGST Act 2003, s.8).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the VAGST Act 2003, Second Schedule: (a) goods physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply, evidenced by customs documentation; (b) services supplied to a non-resident person, or services supplied in relation to goods situated outside Samoa, provided the services are not performed on land or moveable property in Samoa and the place of effective use and enjoyment is outside Samoa; (c) digital supplies of virtual event admission and tokenized/NFT tickets to non-resident consumers, including metaverse-distributed tickets paid via Stripe or USDC, qualify as offshore services at 0% where the recipient is non-resident and the performance platform is outside Samoa. Input VAT attributable to such zero-rated supplies is refundable through the quarterly VAGST return.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Samoan bank or authorized foreign-exchange dealer evidencing crediting or conversion of export proceeds
  • VAGST tax invoice and commercial invoice addressed to the non-resident purchaser

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or mandatory digital stamping regime is in force in Samoa. Registered persons must issue sequentially numbered VAGST tax invoices with the statutory particulars (name, TIN, date, description, VAGST-exclusive amount, tax, total) under VAGST Act 2003, s.23, and retain records within Samoa for 5 years under the Tax Administration Act 2016. Digital ticketing transactions (Stripe receipts, USDC smart-contract logs) are accepted as commercial evidence; no SRA fiscal seal or e-invoice clearance is required for metaverse ticket sales.

SRA audit windows of 4 years (7 years where omission exceeds 25% of income; 10 years for fraud) allow retrospective rejection of export zero-rated treatment, clawback of refunds, and 2%-per-month late-payment interest under the Tax Administration Act 2016. Shortfall penalties escalate at 25% (voluntary disclosure), 75% (intentional disregard), and 150% (fraudulent evasion). Willful VAGST evasion is criminally punishable under VAGST Act 2003, s.45 by a fine up to WST 2,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years; false export declarations additionally attract Customs Act 2014 penalties, and exchange-control violations under Central Bank of Samoa regulations can suspend the IC's banking rails. A fraudulent zero-rating claim may also trigger SIFA revocation of the International Company's exempt status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa maintains a regulated exchange-control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and associated CBS directives. The Samoan Tālā (WST) is partially convertible for current-account transactions, with no major restrictions on trade-related payments, royalties, or service fees, provided supporting documentation is filed with authorized foreign-exchange dealers. Capital-account transactions and profit remittances by onshore resident entities require prior CBS approval and are subject to documentary review. However, a Samoa International Company (WS_IC) beneficially owned by non-residents and transacting solely in foreign currency is treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes. Such an entity may hold foreign-currency bank accounts and repatriate profits or dividends offshore without CBS conversion approval, provided that no WST-denominated transactions occur within the domestic economy. The virtual concert/metaverse ticketing activity (ISIC 7110) is an export of digital services, and therefore proceeds received in USD, USDC, or other convertible currencies are freely retained in offshore settlement accounts. Material hurdles to profit remittance are limited to standard tax-exempt status confirmation (International Companies Act 2012, Section 130), KYC/AML compliance, and the completion of CBS Form X-20 when a domestic WST account is used for incidental expenses. No capital-gains tax, withholding tax, or exchange-control surcharge is imposed on distributions to non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Exchange Control Act 1976 does not impose any fixed statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds earned by non-resident international companies. Resident exporters must surrender export proceeds to a licensed bank within 90 days of shipment, but WS_IC entities are exempt because they are classified as non-resident under the Exchange Control (International Companies) Exemption Notice 1988. For the virtual concert business, all proceeds from ticket sales, NFT/utility-token sales, and metaverse merchandise are treated as foreign-currency export receipts. The company may hold these receipts indefinitely in Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) at commercial banks in Samoa or off-shore. If the IC voluntarily credits WST-denominated accounts, the exchange-control regime applies to the conversion, and the acquiring bank must obtain CBS approval for credits exceeding WST 500,000 per transaction. Recommended practice is to maintain 100% foreign-currency treasury positions with no conversion to WST, thereby eliminating surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Faletaoi, Apia, Samoa

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Samoa does not recognize offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) as licensed financial institutions. Only CBS-licensed commercial banks may provide payment and settlement services inside Samoa. The WS_IC may lawfully use foreign-based EMIs (e.g., Stripe, Airwallex) for receiving digital ticketing proceeds, but those EMIs do not hold a Samoan banking licence and cannot open a Samoan BIC/IBAN. Legal constraints: (1) the IC must not route payments through an EMI to a Samoan resident counterparty in violation of the Financial Institutions Act 2011; (2) crypto-fiat conversions executed by an EMI are permissible only if the EMI has valid anti-money-laundering controls under its home jurisdiction; (3) the Central Bank of Samoa does not provide a sandbox or passporting regime for foreign EMIs; (4) for settlement into the Samoan banking system, the IC must ultimately sweep funds to a CBS-licensed commercial bank. Recommended architecture: use Stripe (Asia-Pacific entity) for card acquiring, settle USD via SWIFT MT103 into a BSP Samoa FCA, and hold USDC in a non-custodial wallet or regulated custodian for metaverse disbursements.

Legal constraints: (1) the IC must not route payments through an EMI to a Samoan resident counterparty in violation of the Financial Institutions Act 2011; (2) crypto-fiat conversions executed by an EMI are permissible only if the EMI has valid anti-money-laundering controls under its home jurisdiction; (3) the Central Bank of Samoa does not provide a sandbox or passporting regime for foreign EMIs; (4) for settlement into the Samoan banking system, the IC must ultimately sweep funds to a CBS-licensed commercial bank. Recommended architecture: use Stripe (Asia-Pacific entity) for card acquiring, settle USD via SWIFT MT103 into a BSP Samoa FCA, and hold USDC in a non-custodial wallet or regulated custodian for metaverse disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for resident companies under the Income Tax Act 2012; 0% for an International Company (IC) by virtue of section 212 of the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended), which creates a complete and permanent statutory exemption from Samoan income tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty in respect of the IC, its operations, and its property.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% for dividends paid by a Samoan resident company under the Income Tax Act 2012; 0% for dividends declared by an International Company, because the section 212 exemption applies both to the IC and to its distributions, making profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders entirely free of Samoan withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None - Samoa has not enacted Local File or Master File documentation thresholds; the Income Tax Act 2012 contains only a general arm's length norm and general anti-avoidance provisions, which are inapplicable to a tax-exempt International Company, and no Samoan transfer pricing return is prescribed.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Act 2021 requires an International Company carrying on a 'relevant activity' - including headquarters, distribution and service centre, pure equity holding, IP holding, financing and leasing, shipping, banking, insurance, or fund management - to demonstrate economic substance in Samoa: (i) board meetings and strategic direction exercised in Samoa; (ii) Samoan-resident employees proportionate to the core income-generating activities; (iii) adequate operating expenditure incurred in Samoa; and (iv) an annual economic substance notification filed with SIFA. A metaverse IP-holding or service-coordinating IC must satisfy this test in Samoa, not merely in the beneficial owner's home jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil shortfall penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus statutory late-payment interest; criminal conviction for individuals: fine up to SAT$50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; for companies: fine up to SAT$100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorise false statements face personal criminal prosecution, civil restitution orders for the full tax shortfall, departure prohibition orders, disqualification, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Penalty not exceeding SAT$50,000 under the Economic Substance Act 2021; administrative strike-off and deregistration for non-payment of fees; upon deregistration, the IC's contracts, including digital ticket tokenisation and concert hosting agreements, may become unenforceable, and the tax exemption may be retrospectively withdrawn for the default period.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil: directors and controlling shareholders become jointly and severally liable for debts contracted during the shell period, face director disqualification orders, and may be barred from re-entry to Samoa.
AML/CFT Failures (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: On indictment: imprisonment up to 14 years and/or a fine up to SAT$1,000,000; on summary conviction: imprisonment up to 3 years and/or a fine up to SAT$200,000; tainted assets, including virtual assets and cryptocurrency, are subject to confiscation orders.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge or reckless disregard face personal criminal liability, asset freezing and restraint orders, international extradition exposure, and travel bans; AML statutory fines are not dischargeable through corporate winding-up.
Trading While Struck Off

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under the International Companies Act: fine up to SAT$50,000 plus a daily continuing default penalty of SAT$500; all contracts entered into while struck off, including ticket sales, hosting agreements, and token smart contracts, are unenforceable against the company.

Director personal liability: Each director who participates in post-strike-off trading is personally liable without limitation for the company's debts and obligations arising in that period and may be subject to disqualification orders and civil recovery proceedings.
