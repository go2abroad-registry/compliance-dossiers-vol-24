E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a South African income tax reference number and VAT registration through SARS eFiling. Required supporting documents include CIPC company registration certificate, Memorandum of Incorporation, foreign passport/national ID of directors, proof of physical address, bank account details, and a letter appointing a registered representative taxpayer in South Africa. Default tax classification

South Africa does not operate a check-the-box system. A (Pty) Ltd is mandatorily taxed at entity level as a resident company. Limited elections are available for small businesses under Turnover Tax (s6 Income Tax Act) and for group relief arrangements; hybrid mismatch rules under s25G and s25H restrict certain cross-border structured transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of matcha powder qualifies for zero-rating under Section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act when goods are supplied to a non-resident, are exported from South Africa within a prescribed period (generally 60 days from date of supply unless extended), and documentary proof of physical export is retained. Related offshore services are zero-rated under Section 11(2)(k) where supplied to non-residents for direct export purposes.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

South Africa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. VAT invoices must contain SARS-specified particulars and be issued within 21 days of supply. E-invoicing is permitted under the SARS eFiling/E-invoice framework using standard XML/UBL formats, and taxpayers must retain transactional data and support an electronic data interchange for SARS audit requests.

SARS conducts post-clearance and desk audits requiring verification of actual physical export, customs declarations, transport documents, and foreign-currency payment receipts. Failure to substantiate zero-rating triggers a VAT assessment at 15% on the export value, plus understatement penalties (up to 200%) and prescribed interest. Fraudulent or deliberately false zero-rating claims constitute a criminal offence punishable by fines, imprisonment, and retrospective clawback of all attributed VAT refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand is fully convertible for all bona fide current-account transactions, including the importation of organic matcha powder (HS 0902.10), freight, insurance, commissions and service fees, subject to documentary verification by an authorised dealer under the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers (CMAD). Capital-account convertibility is managed: inward foreign equity investments by non-residents are freely permitted and require reporting (not pre-approval) through the authorised dealer's Balance of Payments (BOP) system; repayment of shareholder loans below the FinSurv materiality threshold (ZAR 100 million equivalent) is permitted on registration, while larger borrowings require prior FinSurv approval. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders require no FinSurv approval provided the underlying equity was sourced from genuinely foreign capital, the company possesses a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status Pin, audited or independently reviewed financial statements are submitted, the dividend was declared in compliance with the Companies Act, and South African dividends tax at 20% (or lower treaty rate on a valid DTA declaration) is withheld and paid to SARS. The company must also maintain OECD-compliant transfer-pricing documentation for any intra-group payables to prevent thin-capitalisation or recharacterisation adjustments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Circular 09/2021 and the CMAD export chapter, proceeds from exports of goods must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment; the former mandatory 50% conversion of export proceeds into rand is abolished, and the effective surrender ratio is 0% provided 100% of each receipt is repatriated into a South African account or an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) maintained with an authorised dealer. The exporter must submit an exporting-form-validated SAD500 Customs export declaration, obtain a SARS Customs client code and Export Clearing Number, quote the bill of lading or airway bill, and close out each shipment within the 180-day window; failure to do so creates an Unremitted Export Proceeds (UEP) flag, requiring FinSurv extension approval, and can trigger suspension of the company's import/export trade facility with the bank.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) as the Exchange Control authority under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), with AML/CFT supervision by the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) and prudential regulation by the SARB-subsidiary Prudential Authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Onshore neobanks such as TymeBank and Bank Zero are licensed banks under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 but do not presently offer documentary collections, letters of credit, SARB BOP registration or FCA-based treasury rails suitable for a cross-border matcha importer. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Payoneer) are neither licensed banks nor authorised dealers in South Africa; under the Banks Act and the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 they cannot open an account in the name of a South African company at their own balance sheet and cannot perform Exchange Control reporting or FICA CDD on behalf of the regulated bank. Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury rail; Medium as a supplementary collection or supplier-disbursement side rail for low-value payments in USD/EUR/JPY corridors, provided every transaction is mirrored in the primary bank's KYC file and the regulated bank remains the source of BOP/FinSurv reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholder equity injection: the shareholder must remit from its own foreign bank account via SWIFT MT103, with ordering-customer field 50K matching the name on the shareholder register and field 72 stating 'Inward Foreign Investment - Equity Subscription'; the funds are credited to the (Pty) Ltd operational ZAR or multi-currency account at the authorised dealer, which captures the receipt in SARB's Balance of Payments system (the former Form N modality, now electronically reported) and issues a BOP transaction reference for the company's records. The company must file the Companies Act allotment return, keep the share subscription agreement, board resolution and payment instruction, and ensure no round-tripping or loop structure exists in which South African funds leave and re-enter as purported foreign capital. Shareholder loans follow the same routing and are registered as BOP loans; loans above ZAR 100 million equivalent require prior FinSurv approval and a signed facility agreement with market-conforming interest rates to avoid thin-capitalisation. A Capital Escrow Account is not statutorily required for pure equity, but is recommended at Absa or Standard Bank where proceeds must be held pending supplier contract effectiveness or Customs registration.Import disbursements for matcha: the company presents the supplier invoice, insurance document and transport evidence (airway bill or bill of lading) and, for customs-cleared shipments, the SAD500 Bill of Entry; the authorised dealer converts ZAR to JPY, CNY or USD and releases payment via SWIFT MT103 through the correspondent network. For deferred supplier terms, a commercial letter of credit is established by the South African issuing bank via SWIFT MT700, with settlement against compliant presentation (commercial invoice, packing list, B/L, organic certificates) under SWIFT MT202 COV bank-to-bank reimbursement, while the ZAR funding leg is settled through SARB's SAMOS real-time gross settlement system. All import payments must be completed within 180 days of shipment; late settlement triggers an 'Unpaid Import' (UPI) report to FinSurv and may require prior approval. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold is prescribed in the Income Tax Act or Tax Administration Act. SARS requires taxpayers to prepare and retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) for all cross-border transactions with connected persons. In SARS administrative guidance, a full transfer-pricing analysis has historically been expected for affected transactions exceeding R5 million in annual value, but in all cases the documentation must be sufficient to demonstrate that the pricing is consistent with the arm's length principle.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as found in the UAE or Cayman Islands. However, economic substance is central to the tax residence determination (place of effective management) and to the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in section 80A-L of the Income Tax Act. A company that is formally a South African resident but lacks genuine commercial activity, full-time employees, local decision-making, or real business premises may be re-characterised as a shell or conduit arrangement. SARS will then disregard the legal form, allocate income to the jurisdiction where economic activities are performed, and deny treaty benefits. The same anti-avoidance principles, combined with controlled foreign company (CFC) attribution rules in section 9D, prevent passive income from being shifted to low-tax jurisdictions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty under section 104 of the Income Tax Act: a fine of up to R10,000 and/or imprisonment for a period not exceeding five years. In addition, SARS may impose an administrative understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall plus statutory interest. The individual and the company can be prosecuted simultaneously.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or authorise tax evasion may be prosecuted as co-principals; they may also be held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax debt under section 237 of the Tax Administration Act, which includes civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply the general anti-avoidance rule (section 80A-L) to disregard the transaction, reallocate the income to the shareholder or foreign parent, deny treaty benefits, and impose an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the tax assessed. The company may also lose its tax residence status if its place of effective management is outside South Africa.

Director personal liability: Directors who established or operated the shell arrangement may be personally liable for the additional tax, penalties, and interest. Courts may impose a civil order for restitution equal to the tax shortfall, issue travel bans, and in egregious cases, attach the directors' personal assets.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) 38 of 2001, a person who fails to comply with the reporting and record-keeping duties may be liable for a fine of up to R10 million and/or imprisonment for up to 15 years. Corporate penalties may also be imposed alongside individual liability.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who were aware of or condoned the failure to report, or who facilitated the laundering of trade proceeds, face direct criminal liability, including imprisonment, travel bans, extradition, and personal fines. Civil liability may also include compensation to affected parties.
Transfer Pricing (Non-Arm's Length Pricing)

Statutory sanction: SARS will adjust the taxable income to be consistent with the arm's length standard. An understatement penalty of up to 200% may be imposed depending on the taxpayer's conduct, plus prescribed interest on the additional tax from the original due date. Repeated non-compliance may be treated as fraudulent and lead to criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve significant related-party transactions and sign off on tax returns may be personally liable for the assessed shortfall if found negligent or reckless. Personal liability may include payment of the full additional tax, penalties, and interest, and in cases of deliberate fraud, imprisonment.
Non-Filing and Payment Defaults

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties of up to R250 per month for each month of non-compliance, capped at R4,000 per tax year. Late payment of tax attracts a 10% late-payment penalty plus statutory interest at the prescribed rate. SARS may block tax clearance, attach bank accounts, seize assets, or apply for the company to be wound up.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts under section 237 of the Tax Administration Act if the company is liquidated due to negligence or if they are involved in the failure to file or pay. A court may also suspend directors' ability to act in management, impose travel bans, and in egregious cases order imprisonment.
