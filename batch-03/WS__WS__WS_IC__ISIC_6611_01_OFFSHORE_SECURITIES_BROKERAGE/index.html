Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Independent State of Samoa; official e-services portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A Samoa International Company (IC) is formed under the International Companies Act 1987 through a licensed registered agent with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). The tax identification procedure consists of filing with SRA the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office certificate, and a statutory declaration of exclusive offshore business, accompanied by the prescribed Tax Exemption Certificate application. SRA issues the IC's Tax Exemption Certificate and, if requested for banking or FATCA/CRS purposes, a non-resident TIN. The procedure does not create a VAGST registration; VAGST registration is opened only when the IC commences a taxable Samoan activity or elects voluntary registration for export zero-rating.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone: the IC is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, stamp duty, and estate duty on its worldwide offshore business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the IC is statutorily exempt from Samoan income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, stamp duty, and estate duty on its worldwide offshore business. It is not a pass-through entity and is not subject to ordinary Samoan entity-level tax; the exemption is preserved by the annual declaration and payment of the International Company registration fee to SIFA.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available in Samoa. An IC may elect to convert into a domestic company under the Companies Act 2001, thereby becoming a Samoa tax resident and losing the International Companies Act tax exemption; the election must be notified to both SIFA and SRA. Hybrid mismatch elections do not exist in Samoa, but the IC may obtain a binding tax ruling from SRA confirming the VAGST export zero-rating treatment of principal brokerage, agency brokerage, and introducing-broker fee structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 50,000 (Samoan Tala/SAT) of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; an offshore International Company supplying securities brokerage services exclusively to non-residents does not meet this nexus and is not registrable unless it voluntarily registers for zero-rated export supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAGST Act 2007, supplies of goods or services are zero-rated when they are exported or supplied to a person outside Samoa and the benefits of the supply are wholly enjoyed outside Samoa. Offshore securities brokerage services—order execution, custody, settlement, and CFDs/derivatives dealing—are supplied and consumed outside Samoa and therefore fall outside the Samoa VAGST net; if the IC is voluntarily registered, these offshore commissions and fees are zero-rated. The IC must demonstrate that the client is non-resident, the onboarding did not occur through any Samoa branch, and no brokerage service was performed for a Samoa-resident person.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs prescribed form) — required for physical exports; for brokerage, the functional equivalent is the client's electronic order ticket and broker execution record.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — required for physical exports; not applicable to offshore securities services.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation evidencing receipt of cleared brokerage proceeds from the non-resident client.
  • Executed client agreement, KYC file, and monthly brokerage statements evidencing the offshore place of supply and non-resident beneficiary.
  • SRA Tax Exemption Certificate and, if voluntary VAGST registration was obtained, the VAGST registration certificate supporting the zero-rating position.

Samoa does not currently impose mandatory real-time e-invoicing, digital stamping, or API transaction-clearance for tax purposes. Registered VAGST suppliers must issue a tax invoice carrying the supplier's VAGST registration number, customer details, date, description, consideration, and VAGST amount. SRA's portal provides electronic filing and payment only; there is no pre-clearance approval loop for brokerage invoices.

SRA can audit zero-rating and exemption claims for up to the statutory reassessment period, with an extended period in cases of fraudulent or negligent conduct. A false zero-rating or fabricated export claim triggers immediate refund clawback, assessment of VAGST at 15%, statutory interest, civil shortfall penalties (which may reach 150% of the tax evaded for fraudulent conduct), and criminal prosecution of the IC and its officers. SIFA may also retrospectively revoke the IC's tax-exempt status if it is found that the brokerage was not genuinely offshore, causing all historical income to be taxed with penalties from the date of incorporation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a surveillance-based exchange control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1984. The Samoan tālā (WST) is not fully convertible; current account transactions are generally permitted through authorised dealers, whereas capital account transactions require prior CBS approval. Non-resident International Companies enjoy exemptions in respect of external accounts and foreign currency holdings, but profit remittance to shareholders triggers documentary review for tax clearance and CBS notification. Brokerage client funds and operational disbursements must be routed through authorised banks to avoid unauthorised capital transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must repatriate export proceeds within 180 days of shipment and offer 100% of foreign currency earnings for conversion to WST within 7 days of receipt. Non-resident International Companies are not subject to compulsory surrender provided receipts are credited to an approved External Account; if any Samoa-origin export proceeds are credited to a domestic account, 100% must be surrendered within 7 days. For brokerage services, service export fees from overseas clients are treated as current receipts and may be retained in the IC's external account.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Samoa has no dedicated EMI licensing framework, and non-resident digital banks are not supervised by CBS for domestic account opening. Offshore EMIs are generally unsuitable as principal clearing accounts for a Samoa International Company engaged in retail securities brokerage because of higher AML/CFT scrutiny and the sectoral requirement to hold client money through a regulated bank under CBS supervision.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% effective on all foreign-source and exempt income of an International Company (IC); 27% statutory corporate income tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2013-2018 applies only to Samoa-source income or deemed domestic-source income of an IC that breaches its offshore conditions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends distributed by a qualifying exempt International Company to non-resident shareholders where the profits are foreign-source and the distribution is made after the IC exemption certificate is in force; 15% domestic non-resident dividend withholding tax under the Income Tax Act applies where the IC is deemed to have Samoa-source profits or trades with residents

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 annual aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (approximately USD 370,000 under official WST/USD 2.60 band) triggers the obligation to prepare and maintain arm's-length benchmark documentation, a local substance file and board-approved transfer pricing policy

Economic substance scope: Samoa's International Companies (Economic Substance) Rules 2021-2023 impose a full economic substance test on ICs carrying relevant activities, expressly including securities brokerage, trading, and financial intermediation. The IC must meet all of: (a) physical staff in Samoa appropriate to the brokerage volume; (b) premises and managerial presence proportionate to core income-generating activities; (c) minimum operating expenditure in Samoa; (d) no reliance on shell activities or nominee managers. Outsourcing may shift CIGAs outside Samoa but cannot defeat the substance standard unless controlled and disclosed. Failure of the economic substance test does not merely trigger a penalty; it exposes the IC to taxation on its entire global income at 27%, revocation of the IC certificate, and re-characterization of the entity as a shell for AML purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through false exemption declaration

Statutory sanction: Deemed domestic-source income taxed at 27% with a late payment surcharge of 5% per month and penal interest of 2% per month; evasion penalty equal to 100% of the omitted tax; filing of backdated returns for up to 6 years; international company certificate revoked and entity struck off

Director personal liability: Directors and the licensed registered agent are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; the court may issue a travel ban, restraint orders over personal and family assets, and imprisonment of directors for up to 7 years on indictable tax evasion
Shell company lacking economic substance (ESR)

Statutory sanction: Automatic ESR failure: penalty WST 20,000 plus WST 1,000 daily continuous default and WST 100,000 for false statements; the IC loses its tax exemption, is liable to 27% CIT on worldwide profits, and may be struck from the International Companies Register with restoration only after regulatory scrutiny

Director personal liability: Directors serve a disqualification order of up to 5 years across all Samoa companies; the liquidation costs and obligations fall on the directors' personal estates; officers are personally liable for penalty claims where the IC was operated as a facade
Unlicensed financial services and AML breach

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed financial business fine up to WST 500,000; money laundering forfeiture of all client and corporate assets held on account; per-count criminal fine up to WST 200,000; license ineligibility and permanent registry strike-off

Director personal liability: Directors face imprisonment of 5 to 10 years, confiscation of brokerage profits, restitution orders for defrauded retail clients, and extradition exposure where counterpart financial regulators issue mutual legal assistance requests
Transfer pricing and related-party profit diversion

Statutory sanction: The Samoa Revenue Corporation substitutes an arm's-length price and increases taxable income; penalty of up to 75% of the transfer pricing adjustment, plus interest from the original due date; the practice is treated as an act of evasion if documentation is fabricated

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or signed the related-party arrangements are liable for the aggregate unpaid penalty where the IC is insolvent; senior officers may be charged with filing false statements; court may appoint a receiver over the sister entity for recovery
Beneficial ownership concealment and false registry filings

Statutory sanction: AML regime sanctions in addition to tax penalties: fines up to WST 300,000 for failure to maintain a beneficial ownership register; automatic disclosure of the concealed controller to the Samoa Revenue Corporation and exchange of information under CRS/AEOI; IC certificate voided from inception

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents face WST 100,000 personal fines and imprisonment up to 3 years; the concealment is treated as fraudulent insolvency and supports cross-border money-laundering extradition; directors are personally ordered to repatriate illicit client funds
