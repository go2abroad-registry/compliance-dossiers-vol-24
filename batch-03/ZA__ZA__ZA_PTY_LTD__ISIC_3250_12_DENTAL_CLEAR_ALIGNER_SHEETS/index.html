Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za; SARS Customs via the Customs and Excise Automated System (e.g., SAD 500 lodgement).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned South African (Pty) Ltd or non-resident company must: (1) appoint a public officer resident in South Africa; (2) register for income tax through SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using Form IT-TRAC/registration application, submitting the notarised certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation, foreign company registration extract, and proof of physical address; (3) obtain an Income Tax Reference Number (ITREF) for the company; (4) if taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed the VAT threshold, lodge Form VAT 101 together with constitutional documents to obtain a VAT number; (5) register as an exporter with SARS Customs to obtain a Customs Code (DA 185 application) for export clearances of dental aligner sheets; (6) submit the relevant exchange control forms (e.g., Form NEP) to an authorised dealer in respect of export proceeds. Processing time is typically 1-3 business days for the ITREF and up to 21 business days for VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned South African (Pty) Ltd or non-resident company must: (1) appoint a public officer resident in South Africa; (2) register for income tax through SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using Form IT-TRAC/registration application, submitting the notarised certificate of incorporation, memorandum of incorporation, foreign company registration extract, and proof of physical address; (3) obtain an Income Tax Reference Number (ITREF) for the company; (4) if taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed the VAT threshold, lodge Form VAT 101 together with constitutional documents to obtain a VAT number; (5) register as an exporter with SARS Customs to obtain a Customs Code (DA 185 application) for export clearances of dental aligner sheets; (6) submit the relevant exchange control forms (e.g., Form NEP) to an authorised dealer in respect of export proceeds. Processing time is typically 1-3 business days for the ITREF and up to 21 business days for VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South African law does not provide a 'check-the-box' pass-through election for a (Pty) Ltd; entity-level income tax applies mandatorily at 27% (years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023). Available elections and regimes relevant to a manufacturing (Pty) Ltd include: (i) VAT group registration election under section 50 of the VAT Act permitting commonly controlled companies to be treated as a single vendor; (ii) section 11D research and development allowance election (150% deduction for qualifying R&D expenditure); (iii) section 12R Special Economic Zone (SEZ) election granting a reduced 15% corporate tax rate for qualifying manufacturing operations located in a designated SEZ; (iv) section 9I headquarter company election for certain foreign-dividend and interest flows; (v) hybrid instrument classification is mandatory under sections 8F and 8M of the Income Tax Act and cannot be altered by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 (actual or reasonably expected taxable turnover for any 12-month period); voluntary registration threshold: ZAR 50,000. Non-resident suppliers of imported services or goods supplied in South Africa are subject to reverse-charge rules and may have an obligation to register regardless of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 11(1)(a)(i) of the VAT Act applies where the company supplies goods (including multilayer thermoplastic dental aligner sheets classifiable under HS 3920/3921 or as medical devices in accordance with SARS tariff rulings) to a person who is not a resident of South Africa and the goods are exported from South Africa within 90 days of the time of supply (as prescribed by SARS Interpretation Note 30). Alternatively, zero-rating may apply where the supplier is a vendor and the goods are delivered by the vendor to a licensed customs and excise exporter for direct export. For services, section 11(2)(l) zero-rates services supplied to non-residents who are outside South Africa at the time of supply, unless the services are physically rendered in South Africa or are directly connected to immovable property or movable property situated in South Africa. The exporter must obtain and retain documentary proof of export, including the SAD 500 declaration, transport documents, and payment evidence, before any refund is paid. Foreign exchange proceeds must be repatriated to South Africa through an authorised dealer; the standard statutory requirement is receipt within 30 days from the date of export, and any extension requires prior approval from the South African Reserve Bank or an authorised dealer.

  • SAD 500 Customs Export Declaration (electronic SARS Customs clearance)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Waybill
  • Certificate of Origin (SACU format) or EUR.1 Movement Certificate where applicable
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Export Form (NEP) and Inward Remittance Certificate or bank advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds

South Africa does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime of the Brazilian NF-e type. SARS eFiling is the mandated digital channel for filing VAT 201 returns, and SARS Customs requires electronic lodgement of the SAD 500 export declaration. SARS has announced a phased adoption of a national e-invoicing framework aligned with the PEPPOL standard; however, e-invoicing remains voluntary as of the current effective date. Where e-invoices are used, they must satisfy the full or abridged tax invoice requirements of section 20 of the VAT Act, including the vendor's VAT number, invoice date, description of goods, quantity, and consideration; there is no digital stamp, tax clearance token, or server-side authorisation requirement for invoice validity. SARS retains the right to validate invoice data and cross-match export declarations, bills of lading, and bank receipts.

SARS may re-open a zero-rated export claim within the five-year prescription period; if the goods are not physically exported within 90 days of the time of supply, or the requisite documentary proof is not produced, the supply is deemed taxable at 15% and the net VAT becomes due with interest. Additional tax under section 60 of the VAT Act may be imposed at up to 200% of the VAT shortfall where the incorrect zero-rating was made knowingly or without reasonable care. The Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 imposes understatement penalties ranging from 10% (reasonable care) to 150% (intentional tax evasion) of the shortfall under section 223. Criminal liability arises under section 234 of the Tax Administration Act for fraudulent or materially false export declarations, carrying fines or imprisonment up to ten years. SARS also operates a dedicated export verification unit that scrutinises high-value medical device exports; any clawback will include statutory interest at the prescribed rate per section 76 of the VAT Act, and repeat offenders face enhanced audit ratings and possible real-time clearance monitoring.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

ZAR is fully convertible on the current account: bona fide trade payments, service fees, royalties, interest, dividends and branch profits may be remitted through an Authorised Dealer without prior SARB approval, subject to documentary verification and SARS tax compliance (profit remittance additionally requires satisfaction of the Companies Act solvency/liquidity test and accounting for the 20% dividends tax, or a treaty-reduced rate). The capital account is selectively convertible: inward direct investment and qualifying inward loans require no prior approval but must be reported and recorded as inward-listed capital so that future dividend and capital repatriation rights are preserved; outbound capital transfers by residents are restricted and require FinSurv approval or use of a permitted Authorised Dealer channel. ZAR is not internationalised: offshore ZAR deposit and ZAR NDF markets are available only to non-resident counterparties, and onshore entities must settle exclusively through Authorised Dealers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated into the South African banking system within 180 days from the date of shipment, as applied by FinSurv through the Authorised Dealer community. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: 100% of the declared export value may be credited to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or Export Retention Account (ERA) held with an Authorised Dealer, and only proceeds not retained in such accounts must be sold for ZAR. The exporter must submit the commercial invoice, transport documents, customs SAD500 export declaration and proof of receipt to the Authorised Dealer, which tracks the 180-day cycle on the SARB Excon system and requires prior approval for any extension.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) — Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; operational authority is delegated to Authorised Dealers (registered banks) and Authorised Dealers in Foreign Exchange with Limited Authority.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is required for all cross-border connected-person transactions regardless of a single monetary threshold; the SARS Transfer Pricing Disclosure Return (IT2) becomes mandatory when the taxpayer's annual gross receipts equal or exceed ZAR 100 million; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of ZAR 10 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to those in the UAE or the Cayman Islands; instead, economic substance is an embedded statutory and judicial principle enforced through the general anti-avoidance provisions (ss 80A–80L of the Income Tax Act), the controlled foreign company attribution rules (s 9D), and transfer pricing accurate delineation principles. A Pty Ltd manufacturing dental clear aligners must demonstrate real operational substance — physical factory premises, engaged employees, actual raw-material purchasing, and decision-making functions in South Africa — to support its tax residency, treaty benefits, and deductibility of cross-border payments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: SARS issues a revised assessment with the omitted tax, interest at the prescribed rate, and an understatement penalty of up to 200% of the shortfall. Criminal conviction under section 235 of the Tax Administration Act exposes the company and participating officers to a fine of up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment of up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the fraud may be treated as responsible persons under section 169 of the Tax Administration Act and be held jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax; asset restraint orders, travel bans, and disqualification from directorship are statutory consequences.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: SARS adjusts the taxable income to reflect arm's-length conditions, disallows the excessive deduction, imposes interest, and levies an understatement penalty determined by grade — up to 200% for intentional or highly culpable misstatements. Failure to submit the IT2 return and TP documentation triggers administrative non-compliance penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or knowingly ignored the mispricing can be cited as responsible persons for the additional corporate tax; in cases of gross negligence, they face personal liability, disqualification, and civil recovery proceedings in the High Court.
Shell Company and Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS cancels the tax benefit under the general anti-avoidance rule (section 80B), recomputes tax as if the arrangement had not been entered into, and adds an understatement penalty of up to 200% plus interest; treaty benefits are denied under the principal purposes test.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil where the arrangement was a sham, holding shareholders and directors personally liable for the corporate tax debt; directors also face civil restitution claims for breach of fiduciary duty and possible travel bans under the Tax Administration Act.
Persistent Non-Filing and Default Estimation

Statutory sanction: SARS raises a default assessment based on estimated taxable income; late filing penalties accumulate monthly (10% per month of assessed tax, capped at 100%); interest compounds; criminal prosecution may follow for habitual non-compliance under section 234 of the Tax Administration Act.

Director personal liability: Directors may be issued with personal liability notices for outstanding PAYE and provisional tax debts where the company has ceased trading or been liquidated without settlement; directors can be prohibited from leaving South Africa until the debt is resolved or security is provided.
AML and Financial Intelligence Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with the Financial Intelligence Centre Act (FICA) carries administrative penalties of up to ZAR 10 million per contravention and criminal liability: imprisonment of up to 15 years and/or fines for responsible officers. SARS may also invoke anti-money-laundering audit powers to assess hidden income with penalties up to 200%.

Director personal liability: Directors, as accountable-institution management, are personally liable for FICA failures attributable to negligence or willful blindness; upon conviction they face imprisonment, travel bans, prohibitions on directing any financial business, and civil restitution for any laundered funds.
