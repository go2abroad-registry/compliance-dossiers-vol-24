Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Ministry of Finance; online portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or non-resident company must register with the SRA through the Business and Taxpayer Registration Unit on the official IR-1 Business Registration form, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered agent certificate, constitutional documents or trust deed, and valid identification of directors/trustees. The SRA issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and, where taxable supplies in Samoa are made, a VAGST registration number within approximately 10 business days. International Companies that do not undertake taxable activities in Samoa are statutorily exempt and are not ordinarily required to obtain a TIN solely for offshore operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply, and to services provided to non-resident customers supplied and used entirely outside Samoa, provided the supplier is a registered VAGST person and retains documentary evidence proving the exportation or offshore consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Samoa has no real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate. Taxpayers must issue VAGST invoices, retain source documents, and file monthly VAGST returns electronically through the SRA online portal.

SRA audits zero-rated export claims; fraudulent or excessive zero-rating claims attract a shortfall penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded, late-payment penalties and interest, retrospective clawback of input credits, and criminal prosecution including fines and imprisonment. Directors and trustees may be held jointly and severally liable for such penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a non-internationally traded, managed currency pegged to a basket of major trading-partner currencies; practical convertibility is delivered solely through CBS-licensed authorised dealers and their correspondent/nostro networks. Current-account payments (imports, services, fiduciary fees, dividends) are available on an expedited basis with documentary retention, while capital-account movements (capital injections, onward loans, portfolio flows, and capital repatriation) require prior CBS exchange-control approval or a standing exemption certificate. Profit remittance from a tax-exempt International Company is permitted once the entity holds a tax-goodstanding/offshore-status certificate; the principal practical hurdle is WST conversion, not dividend legality. The recommended treasury posture is therefore a fully foreign-currency (USD/EUR) structure that transits no WST clearing account and keeps all onshore exposure de minimis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Samoa's exchange-control regulations, a resident exporter must repatriate export receipts to a CBS-licensed authorised dealer in Samoa no later than 90 days from the date of shipment and must surrender the full 100% of the WST-equivalent proceeds to the dealer unless the CBS has granted a foreign-currency retention account licence or a directed exemption. An International Company operating as a non-resident offshore fiduciary falls outside the surrender mandate where its banking relationship is expressly designated 'non-resident' and no goods pass through Samoan customs or the domestic clearing rail; however, any trust income that is remitted into a WST-denominated resident account will be captured as ordinary exchange receipts and subjected to the 90-day reporting and conversion discipline. Treasury policy must therefore maintain a CBS-acknowledged standing non-resident exemption reference and prohibit inadvertent WST crediting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa) — the Foreign Exchange Administration with statutory authority for banking licensing, monetary policy, and exchange-control administration under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and the Financial Institutions Act 1996.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File or Master File regime has been codified for tax-exempt International Companies; Samoa's Income Tax Act 2015 applies the OECD arm's length principle to resident taxpayers generally. Administrative benchmark: WS$1,000,000 in annual cross-border related-party dealings triggers a requirement to maintain contemporaneous documentation if the exemption is challenged. An Economic Substance Declaration is mandatory for all International Companies regardless of transaction value.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2021 impose an economic substance test on Samoa International Companies carrying on 'relevant activities'. Fiduciary administration of irrevocable offshore spendthrift trusts is characterised as fund management or headquarters/service centre activity. The company must demonstrate a physical office in Samoa, locally resident directors or managers with adequate expertise, sufficient number of qualified local employees, adequate local operating expenditure, and core income-generating activities conducted in Samoa. Substance declarations are shared with OECD and EU authorities under the framework on harmful tax practices.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / sham trust and offshore concealment

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the entire structure's income at the 27% corporate rate, penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, late payment surcharges, and formal strike-off of the International Company; the trust assets may be subjected to tracing orders and anti-avoidance rulings under the Income Tax Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of diverted trust assets, subject to travel bans while investigations are pending, and may face imprisonment of up to seven years for fraudulent disposition.
Shell company and absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WS$100,000 plus a daily penalty of WS$1,000 for continued non-compliance; deregistration without prejudice to creditors; automatic exchange of information with the tax residence jurisdiction of the ultimate beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution of penalties, disqualification of directors for up to five years, exclusion orders against foreign directors, revocation of the registered agent's licence, and personal liability of directors for unpaid penalties.
AML / KYC deficiencies and unlicensed trust administration

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Financing of Terrorism Act 2018, fines up to WS$1,000,000 for legal persons, confiscation of trust property and corporate assets, revocation of licences, and a prohibition on carrying on regulated financial activities.

Director personal liability: Directors and trust officers are criminally liable when involvement is wilful; imprisonment of up to seven years for money laundering and up to fourteen years for terrorism financing; civil restitution orders and travel bans for foreign principals.
Non-filing and misdeclaration of annual returns and economic substance declarations

Statutory sanction: Automatic dissolution under Section 243 of the International Companies Act 1988; restoration requires payment of all government fees in arrears plus restoration costs; information is shared with OECD and EU authorities and the company may be permanently excluded from the preferential tax regime.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for government fees in arrears and for fraudulent trading where the company continued to transact after strike-off; disqualification orders, travel bans, and criminal fines apply to the directors and the licensed agent.
