Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Ministry of Revenue, Apia, Samoa; online taxpayer portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity must first engage a licensed registered agent in Samoa. For an International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987, the certificate of incorporation issued by the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) constitutes the primary tax-exempt charter. The IC must also complete SRA Form TIN-01 to obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN/IRD number) for banking, customs, and employment purposes. The application must be lodged through the registered agent, who verifies the certificate of incorporation, registered office address, director/shareholder particulars, and the agent's practising licence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available to a Samoa International Company; the entity is mandatorily exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax on outbound payments under Section 198 of the International Companies Act 1987. If the IC carries on any local non-offshore commercial activity, it may voluntarily register for GST under the Goods and Services Tax Act 2001, but such registration does not affect the corporate income tax exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (annual taxable turnover threshold for mandatory registration)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated for GST purposes when the goods are exported out of Samoa within 28 days of the date of supply. International services (including offshore administrative services supplied to a non-resident ESOP employer) are zero-rated where the recipient is outside Samoa, the services are performed entirely outside Samoa, and the intellectual or practical benefit of the services does not arise in Samoa. The Samoa International Company tax-exempt status is preserved for the ESOP trust's undisputed offshore revenue; GST zero-rating applies only to any locally registered taxable activities.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill with evidence of physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT payment evidence from the non-resident settlor or employer
  • Commercial invoice or service contract describing the offshore nature of the ESOP administration services

Samoa does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government API integration, or digital stamping for e-invoices. SRA-accepted invoices may be paper-based or standard electronic invoices; registered persons must file monthly GST returns and remit GST through the SRA online portal.

The SRA may audit any claim of zero-rating for up to 5 years after the annual return is filed. If the exporter fails to substantiate the export or offshore-service supply, the SRA will assess the unpaid GST at 15%, impose a late payment penalty of 20% per annum, and charge monthly interest at the prescribed rate. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract criminal prosecution under the Revenue Administration Act 2016, fines up to WST 100,000, imprisonment up to 7 years, and full clawback of input tax credits, with personal liability extended to directors and registered agents of the International Company.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is a currency subject to exchange control. Current account transactions (trade, services, interest, dividends) are generally permitted for residents / approved non-resident entities subject to documentary verification and Authorized Dealer oversight. Capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, portfolio inflows, loans, guarantees) require the specific approval of the Central Bank of Samoa, unless an express exemption applies. International Companies (ICs) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 enjoy automatic exchange control approval for transactions involving persons resident outside Samoa, subject to statutory returns and the absence of a Samoan-source business or local beneficial ownership. Profit and capital remittance by an IC to non-residents is free from the usual exchange control application provided the remitting bank verifies the IC's statutory registers and audited or management accounts evidencing available profits or capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 2010, a Samoan exporter is legally obligated to surrender export proceeds through an Authorized Dealer within 30 days of receipt and in accordance with the dealer's notification and conversion instructions; at least 100% of the applicable export proceeds are normally required to be repatriated into Samoa and converted into WST unless a specific exemption is granted. For an International Company not engaged in domestic export trade in Samoa, the surrender rule is not triggered; proceeds for offshore services or trust activities are treated as offshore receipts and may remain in a designated foreign-currency account with an Authorized Dealer or an offshore licensed bank with CBS oversight.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa) acting pursuant to the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, the Exchange Control Act 2009, and the Exchange Control Regulations 2010.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) options are of limited viability for a Samoa International Company. Samoa does not currently issue EMI licenses to pure digital non-bank deposit takers; an offshore EMI would only be permitted to facilitate payments if it holds a banking or money remittance license from the Central Bank of Samoa or operates as a non-deposit-taking remittance agent subject to validation by an Authorized Dealer. Most global EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) do not domicile Samoa entities, and their services may be restricted or impose currency conversion margins. Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. A licensed Samoan commercial bank remains the mandatory anchor for trust/escrow accounts and for maintaining a stable AUD/USD/WST clearing channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Samoa International Company trust should be routed exclusively via SWIFT-based bank transfers from the parent employer or shareholders to the trust's account at an Authorized Dealer in Samoa. The wire must clearly state a purpose code such as 'Investment into Employee Ownership Trust' or 'Capital Contribution for ESOP Award Funding' in the pending / SWIFT 72Field narrative. Supporting documentation (board resolutions, ESOP plan, subscription agreements) must be lodged with the beneficiary bank prior to the wire, because Samoa banks treat any inward transfer above WST 20,000 or USD 10,000 as a balance-of-payments event. The beneficiary bank must also submit a TEL / balance-of-payments reporting disclosure to the Central Bank of Samoa within five business days. For non-resident funding in USD, AUD, EUR or JPY, the receiving Samoa bank may automatically convert into WST unless the trust has a previously authorized foreign-currency account. If the trust intends to hold and subsequently disburse in the same foreign currency, the trust must obtain an exchange control authorization at account opening (in practice, ICs are summarily approved).Permitted outbound clearing rails comprise: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for payroll disbursements to employee bank accounts outside Samoa, using the proper beneficiary bank codes and a narrative for employee compensation (e.g., '/ESOP/ salary / award proceeds'); (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) or Documentary Collection where the ESOP trust purchases equity or settles share transfers through a correspondent bank; (iii) local Samoan NZ clearing or bilateral netting for any on-island employee payments (via BSP or ANZ Samoa's domestic clearing gateway), and (iv) third-party payment aggregators only for small-value recurring awards, provided the aggregator holds an appropriate license in the country of the beneficiary. All outbound transfers above USD 10,000 are subject to CBS reporting and require the trust to provide a tax registration or legal basis verifying the distribution is not a prohibited dividend under the International Companies Act. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions; a Samoan Local File and Master File are expected to be maintained once this threshold is exceeded. Thin-capitalisation debt-to-equity safe harbour is set at 1.5:1 for related-party loans.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not enact UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the International Companies Act 1987 and SIFCA supervisory practice impose mandatory substance indicators: a licensed registered agent in Samoa, a physical registered office, board and trustee minutes maintained at the office, a statutory beneficial ownership register, and records evidencing real ESOP plan administration. Absent such substance, the Registrar may strike off the IC and the entity cannot credibly invoke treaty benefits under Samoa's OECD Global Forum commitments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Misapplication of the International Company Exemption

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Act 2012: shortfall penalty of 200% of the evaded tax (minimum WST 5,000); criminal fine up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years; the Commissioner may reassess for 10 prior income years and issue garnishee orders against Samoan bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and the licensed agent who knowingly participate in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties and interest; the Commissioner may issue a travel prohibition certificate preventing departure from Samoa; upon criminal conviction personal imprisonment applies and the agent's licence is revoked by SIFCA.
Shell Company and Abusive Offshore Structuring

Statutory sanction: International Companies Act 1987: late filing penalty WST 500; strike-off by the Registrar after 90 days of continued default; assets of the struck-off IC vest in the Public Trustee and forfeit to the Samoan Government; reinstatement costs WST 2,500 and is barred after one year unless legitimate substance is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors of a struck-off IC are personally liable for all debts and obligations incurred after strike-off; the Registrar may disqualify directors from managing any Samoan company for up to 5 years; the facilitating registered agent's licence is suspended and subject to cancellation.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act 2018: civil penalty up to WST 1,000,000 per continuing breach; SIFCA licence revocation; freezing and forfeiture orders may extend to trust assets; the IC cannot be used to circumvent transaction-reporting thresholds.

Director personal liability: Directors, trustees and plan administrators are personally subject to the AML/CFT regime; deliberate money-laundering through the trust carries imprisonment up to 10 years and confiscation of trust property and personal assets; travel bans may be imposed at the FIU's request.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2015 transfer pricing rules: the Commissioner adjusts the consideration to arm's length, recharacterises debt as equity where the 1.5:1 debt-to-equity safe harbour is breached, and assesses a 25% shortfall penalty plus 2% monthly interest; the general anti-avoidance rule can void the arrangement and extend the reassessment period to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the non-arm's-length pricing are jointly assessed for the additional tax; the general anti-avoidance rule makes the scheme void against the Ministry for Revenue; director travel bans and disqualification may follow where the adjustment is concealed.
