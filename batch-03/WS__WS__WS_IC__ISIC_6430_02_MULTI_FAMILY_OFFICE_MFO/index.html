Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa; taxpayer e-services portal 'TAP' (Taxpayer Access Portal) at https://www.revenue.gov.ws; International Company registry administered by the Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL) at https://www.mcil.gov.ws.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 with the MCIL Registrar through a licensed Samoa registered agent, filing a charter restricted to offshore business and appointing a registered office in Apia; no minimum capital is required and 100% foreign ownership with non-resident directors is permitted. Step 2: Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and company registration number from the Registrar. Step 3: File the exemption application with the Ministry for Revenue (MfR) annexing the Certificate of Incorporation and the offshore-objects charter; the MfR issues a Certificate of Tax Exemption confirming that the IC is outside the scope of Samoan income tax (including the 27% domestic corporate rate) on all non-Samoa-source income, gains and capital. Step 4: The IC is not required to hold an active income-tax TIN because it derives no Samoan-source income; a TIN and VAGST registration are required only if the IC makes taxable supplies in Samoa, which is prohibited for ICs in the ordinary course of offshore business. Step 5: If the IC voluntarily engages in zero-rated export activity in Samoa, it may apply to the MfR for VAGST registration to recover input tax, obtaining a separate VAGST TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Samoa's domestic law; ICs instead rely on statutory object-based exemption. Available elections: (i) the IC charter's objects clause electing offshore-only activity, which engages the automatic tax immunity in s.202 of the International Companies Act 1987; (ii) a formal application for an Exemption Certificate under s.203 of that Act confirming immunity from income, stamp, land and municipal duties and from the Exchange Control Act 1976; (iii) voluntary VAGST registration for a person making only zero-rated supplies (VAGST Act 2012, s.9); and (iv) no election is available to convert the IC into a Samoa-resident taxable company, because domestic trading is statutorily barred for ICs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (VAGST Act 2012, s.7); voluntary registration is available for persons making exclusively zero-rated supplies, e.g. exporters; an International Company confined to offshore business normally falls below this threshold and has no Samoan VAGST nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAGST Act 2012, s.16 zero-rates: (a) exports of goods physically removed from Samoa by the supplier within 28 days of the time of supply; (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and physically performed entirely outside Samoa; (c) services supplied in connection with land or moveable goods situated outside Samoa; and (d) international freight and passenger transport. A Samoa International Company earning discretionary portfolio-advisory fees from non-resident multi-family clients supplies offshore services that are outside the Samoan VAGST base entirely; should the IC procure goods or local services in Samoa for export, zero-rating is available only with a statutory declaration, corresponding export documentation, and evidence that the 28-day export window has been met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic declaration filed with Samoa Customs Division via ASYCUDA World) in the IC's name
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Airway Bill (air freight) evidencing physical export of the goods from Samoa within 28 days of the time of supply
  • Commercial Invoice / VAGST Tax Invoice with prescribed content under VAGST Act 2012, s.20, stating the zero rate
  • VAGST return and zero-rating schedule filed with the Ministry for Revenue, together with the exporter's statutory declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed Samoa commercial bank, or SWIFT MT103 payment evidence, proving repatriation of export proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin and packing/weight lists, where preferential tariff treatment or customs verification requires them

Samoa has no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based clearance mandate, and no digital stamping regime. The statutory compliance obligations are: (i) e-filing of VAGST returns and payments through the MfR Taxpayer Access Portal (TAP); (ii) issuance of prescribed tax invoices under VAGST Act 2012, s.20 showing supplier TIN, recipient TIN for B2B supplies, date, description, consideration, and applicable VAGST status; (iii) seven-year retention of accounting and invoice records; and (iv) electronic filing of export and import declarations through the ASYCUDA World customs clearance platform operated by the Samoa Customs Division.

The Ministry for Revenue audits zero-rated claims by cross-matching ASYCUDA World export declarations with bank-reported foreign exchange receipts, Inward Remittance Certificates, and SWIFT payment trails. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating triggers: default assessments recovering the full 15% VAGST, an accuracy/shortfall penalty of up to 50% of the evaded tax (up to 75% where evasion is intentional), late-payment penalties, and statutory use-of-money interest; refunds obtained by false statements are clawed back retroactively as a debt due with interest. Criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 2012 can add fines of up to WST 40,000 and imprisonment, with joint and several liability of directors, and the MfR may also cancel the IC's tax exemption certificate and strike the IC from the International Companies register for serious non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is not freely convertible on the capital account. Current-account transactions (trade in services, operational costs, reasonable management fees) are permitted with supporting vouchers. Capital inflows and outflows, including intercompany funding and profit remittances, require CBS approval for amounts exceeding WST 500,000 and are assessed case-by-case. International Companies (ICs) may maintain fully foreign-currency-denominated accounts in USD, EUR, AUD, and NZD; funds held and settled exclusively in those currencies are freely repatriable without conversion to WST, provided no WST-denominated transactions occur. Profit remittance hurdles: an IC must file a Foreign Exchange Remittance Application, audited annual financial statements (where relevant), a tax clearance from the Ministry of Revenue if triggered, and a board resolution authorising distribution. There is no withholding tax on outbound dividends from an IC, but a 10% levy may apply if the IC is not a licensed financial entity under SIFA.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1975 and the Exchange Control Regulations, the timeline for export receipts is 210 days from shipment date; the mandatory conversion ratio is 100% for domestic exporters unless the Central Bank grants an open foreign-currency retention account. For International Companies with a SIFA special license, there is no mandatory surrender when the receipt is in a currency other than WST and is not part of the domestic export economy. ICs must still submit an annual FX Conversion Certificate and obtain CBS permission for any deposit into a local WST account exceeding WST 100,000 in a single month.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) - Apia, Independent State of Samoa; exchange control administration is shared with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) for offshore International Company treasury operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. No licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or virtual banking licence exists in Samoa. Offshore EMIs (Revolut, Wise, Mercury, Airwallex) do not offer onboarding for Samoa-domiciled International Companies because of the absence of a correspondent banking relationship in WST or USD and the elevated money-laundering risk attached to multi-family office structures. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not authorised depository institutions under the Financial Institutions Act 2011; (ii) a funds transfer from an EMI can be rejected at the Samoan receiving bank without an underlying documentary declaration; (iii) use of a Samoan IC with a licensed SIFA trust/family office cannot be supported by an unregulated EMI under FATF Recommendation 15. The only viable treasury rails are SWIFT interbank transfers through a licensed commercial bank in Samoa.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for resident domestic companies; 0% effective rate for an International Company (IC) under section 200 of the International Companies Act 1987.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 1974 for dividends paid by a resident company; reduced to 0% where the payor is an International Company because ICA s.200 exempts IC distributions from all withholding obligations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Samoan Local File or Master File threshold is prescribed in WST; Samoa has not enacted a dedicated transfer-pricing documentation statute. The OECD/G20 BEPS country-by-country filing threshold applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million (approximately WST 1.2 billion). A standalone multi-family office IC is below any actionable threshold and is not required to prepare transfer-pricing documentation; however, any related-party fee (management fee or performance fee) paid to or received from an associate must satisfy the arm's length principle under the general anti-avoidance provisions of the Income Tax Act 1974 if challenged.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2020 apply to every IC, including a multi-family office. Discretionary portfolio advisory for multiple private families falls within the 'fund management' relevant activity (and possibly 'headquarters' if the IC coordinates the family group). The IC must be directed and managed in Samoa, maintain adequate physical presence (premises, employees, and expenditure), file the annual ESR-1, and retain a substance file. Failure to satisfy the test negates the regulatory benefit of the exemption and triggers the penalties described above. A pure tax-exempt shell without substance will be treated as a red flag by SIFA, the FATF/EU listings process, and foreign tax authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Concealment of Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 1974: fine up to 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalty assessments; SIFA will strike the IC off the register and cancel the ICA s.200 exemption; the file may be referred to the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit (SFIU) for anti-money-laundering action.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors and the registered agent jointly and severally liable for the unpaid foreign or Samoan tax and penalties; the Ministry of Revenue may issue travel bans preventing directors from leaving Samoa; directors may be disqualified and, upon conviction, imprisoned. Professional nominee directors face licence revocation by SIFA.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of WST 50,000, daily continuing penalty of WST 1,000, mandatory striking-off and dissolution by the Registrar of International Companies; re-registration barred for 5 years; referral to the EU/OECD harmful-tax-practices process increases the jurisdiction's risk rating.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false economic substance declarations are personally liable for the WST 50,000 penalty and restitution; the court may impose director disqualification and a travel prohibition; in flagrant cases, directors face fraud charges carrying imprisonment of up to 7 years under Samoa's Crimes Act 2013.
AML / CTF / Unlicensed Financial Services

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Financing of Terrorism Act 2007 (as amended): fines up to WST 1,000,000 for a body corporate; freezing and confiscation of assets; revocation of the international company's licence and a mandatory cease-and-desist order; the entity is listed on the public sanctions register.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are criminally liable for money laundering (imprisonment up to 14 years) and for operating an unlicensed financial business (imprisonment up to 5 years); personal civil liability for client losses and restitution; travel bans and asset freezing orders may be imposed pending prosecution; the SFIU may also refer directors to foreign authorities for coordinated action.
