E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the International Companies Act 2020 (or register a foreign branch under the Companies Act 2001 if onshore activity is intended). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box, look-through, or hybrid-mismatch election; classification follows legal form. The International Company is a separate legal personality automatically placed in a statutory 20-year tax-exempt zone (renewable) under section 210 of the International Companies Act 2020, so no election is required to obtain exemption. Available elective mechanisms: (a) voluntary VAGST registration under section 10 of the GST Act 2003 to recover input tax, which is counter-indicated for a pure offshore ad-revenue business; (b) an onshore election recorded in the IC memorandum to carry on domestic trade, which submits the IC to standard corporate income tax and VAGST on Samoa-source supplies; (c) VAGST group registration under section 15 of the GST Act 2003 for related Samoan-registered entities; (d) designation of reporting currency and share-capital terms in the constitution; and (e) settlement elections under the International Trusts Act 2016. OECD BEPS Action 2 and CFC rules have not been enacted in Samoa; shareholders resident in high-tax jurisdictions remain exposed to their home country anti-deferral regimes on the IC passive income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration applies to any person carrying on a taxable activity if the value of taxable supplies in Samoa reaches or exceeds WST 50,000 (approx. USD 18,500) in a 12-month rolling period; voluntary registration is available below this threshold. The threshold does not create nexus for an International Company whose ad-monetization services are supplied wholly to non-resident ad networks (AdMob, AppLovin) with no fixed establishment in Samoa; the statutory nexus test is the supply of goods or services in Samoa, and offshore software, advertising, and gaming revenue falls outside the VAGST net. A registered entity files monthly or quarterly VAGST returns through the IRIS portal.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The GST Act 2003 zero-rates the following supplies relevant to an ISIC 5820 hyper-casual gaming publisher: (a) exports of goods, provided the goods leave Samoa within 28 days of delivery to the carrier; (b) services performed physically outside Samoa; (c) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and where the services are not connected with land or goods in Samoa; and (d) services in connection with goods for export or international transport. Programmatic supply of advertising inventory and user-acquisition services for hyper-casual games to AdMob (Google Ireland) and AppLovin (Luxembourg and United States) qualifies under category (c): the recipient is non-resident, the service is the delivery of digital ad impressions to audiences outside Samoa, and consideration is remitted in USD from offshore ad networks. To sustain zero-rating, the IC must document: the executed ad-network contract; the non-resident status of the counterparty; per-period payout statements from the AdMob and AppLovin dashboards; and matching bank receipts. If the IC has no Samoan taxable supplies, it is not registrable and no VAGST return arises; where a VAGST registration is nonetheless held, the same documentation supports an input-credit refund, generally payable by the MfR within 60 days of a complete application.

The MfR accepts paper or electronic invoices provided they contain the statutory particulars in the GST Act 2003: supplier name and TIN, recipient identity, sequential invoice number, invoice date, description, tax-exclusive price, VAGST amount and rate, or the zero-rating statutory reference. The MfR may audit export zero-rating claims for up to 7 years after the relevant return period under the Tax Administration Act 2020. Substantiation is strictly documentary: contract, non-resident status certificate, payout dashboard, and Inward Remittance Certificate; proceeds not repatriated and matched within 180 days from the zero-rated invoice date are rebuttably presumed to be non-export revenue. Consequences of a failed or fraudulent zero-rating claim: (i) output VAGST is recaptured at the standard rate (currently 10%) from the original invoice date, with associated input credits denied and statutory interest at 12% per annum; (ii) a shortfall penalty of 50% to 150% of the tax understated depending on carelessness or willfulness; (iii) a late-filing penalty of WST 200 per month per return; (iv) criminal prosecution for fraudulent declaration with fines up to WST 10,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; and (v) for an International Company, revocation of the 20-year tax-exemption status and potential deregistration by the Registrar of International Companies, with retrospective clawback of all exempted taxes for the audit period. The MfR applies substance-over-form and transfer-pricing analysis where shell-company indicators are present and may recharacterize a related-party ad-network payer to defeat artificial zero-rating arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

WST is a partially convertible, managed-float currency. Current-account transactions (ad-tech receipts, platform commissions, server costs, user-acquisition spend) are processed by Authorized Dealers against underlying invoices/contracts without CBS pre-approval. Capital-account transactions — share-capital injection, shareholder loans, repayment of intercompany debt, and dividend outflow — require CBS prior approval or a standing dealer exemption. An International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 is tax-exempt on foreign-source gaming income, but profit remittance is still a controlled capital movement: CBS requires audited financials and a Samoa Revenue Authority clearance for large dividend wires (typically above WST 200,000). Non-resident owners may retain earnings in a CBS-approved USD FCA; conversion to WST is priced at the CBS daily reference rate plus a dealer spread. There is no statutory queueing or dual-rate mechanism; convertibility is transaction-specific and evidence-driven.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CBS exchange-control policy requires export proceeds — including digital-services receipts from hyper-casual game advertising (AdMob/AppLovin) — to be repatriated to a licensed bank in Samoa within 90 days from the invoice/payment date. Unless a CBS-approved FCA is in force, the mandatory surrender ratio is 100%: the dealer converts USD to WST at the prevailing rate upon receipt. Where an FCA is approved, proceeds may be retained in USD to match USD-linked ad-tech payables, subject to quarterly CBS reconciliation and a documented retention justification. Any extension, offset, or netting of receivables requires a prior CBS exemption; late receipt beyond 90 days must be disclosed and may attract administrative review and monetary penalty under the Exchange Control Act.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS); primary regulator for monetary policy, foreign exchange administration, and banking supervision under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, the Exchange Control Act 1973, and the Financial Institutions Act 1996. CBS licenses commercial banks as Authorized Dealers and administers exchange-control permissions, capital-movement approvals, and dealer margins. The Samoan Tālā (WST) is the only legal tender; USD is accepted only via approved Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs).

Secondary rail: irrevocable, confirmed Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued by ANZ/BSP Samoa and advised through an Australian or Chinese confirming bank for outsourced game-development blocks above USD 250,000. All outbound wires must reference the underlying contract and the pre-approved payee registry; monthly outbound flows above WST 200,000 require the dealer to submit CBS transfer documentation. Sanctioned-jurisdiction payees are prohibited; AdTech traffic sourced from OFAC/UN-sanctioned geographies must be contractually excluded from the monetization stack.Deliverable WST/USD FX forward (tenor up to 12 months) under ISDA/CSA with ANZ Bank Samoa or BSP Samoa; requires an underlying eligible current-account exposure and CBS 'no-objection' for tenors exceeding 12 months.USD/WST cross-currency swap executed through the dealer bank's offshore booking centre; permitted for structural hedging of the initial capital injection and long-term licensing liabilities, subject to CBS prior approval.Offshore USD/WST non-deliverable forward (NDF) cash-settled in USD with a Singapore or Hong Kong bank; acceptable as a hedge for USD-denominated AdMob/AppLovin receivables and classified as a non-capital transaction not requiring CBS approval, provided no WST is delivered at maturity.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Restricted. Samoa does not maintain an e-money or fintech-sandbox licensing regime; non-bank payment service providers require CBS licensing under the Financial Institutions Act 1996, and offshore EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not Authorized Dealers. An EMI may function only as an inbound USD aggregation wallet between AdMob/AppLovin and the Samoan bank account; it cannot perform WST conversion, and the 'double-hop' of funds may trigger enhanced AML review. Recommended approach: use an EMI for the first 90 days of operations only, then route payouts directly to the ANZ/BSP USD FCA; any retained EMI balance must be covered by a tri-party acknowledgment executed with the IC's Registered Agent.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% (standard Samoan corporate income tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2012; International Companies are exempt from this rate on foreign-source income)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory non-resident dividend withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 2012. Dividends paid by an International Company out of exempt foreign-source income are outside the Samoan withholding net (0%), provided the IC derives no income from Samoan sources.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the end of the income year (income year is the calendar year; deadline 31 March). An extension of up to 3 months may be granted by the Ministry of Revenue on written application.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 (approximately USD 360,000): aggregate related-party revenue or costs in an income year triggers mandatory Local File documentation. Master File documentation is required where the multinational group has consolidated turnover exceeding WST 100,000,000. Contemporaneous TP documentation is strongly recommended below the threshold for IP-heavy hyper-casual game publishers.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 apply. A hyper-casual gaming IC deriving advertising revenue through AdMob and AppLovin engages in the relevant activity of intellectual property holding and the provision of headquarters/advertising monetization services. The IC must maintain a physical registered office and registered agent in Samoa, employ adequate qualified personnel physically present in Samoa, hold board meetings and make strategic decisions in Samoa, and perform its core income-generating activities in Samoa, including counterparty contracting, advertising inventory pricing, and revenue governance. Non-compliance triggers ESR penalties, automatic exchange-of-information reporting, and loss of the IC tax exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Abuse of the International Company Exemption

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment at 27% corporate tax on the entire income; shortfall penalty of 150% plus interest at 1.5% per month; criminal fine up to WST 100,000; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the IC to misdeclare are personally liable for the unpaid tax, statutory penalties, and audit costs. Courts may pierce the corporate veil to order restitution of distributions and may impose travel bans pending final assessment.
Economic Substance - Shell Company (ESR Failure)

Statutory sanction: ESR civil penalty up to WST 100,000 per relevant activity; suspension of the IC tax residence status and final deregistration by the Registrar of International Companies for continued non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Samoa-resident registered agent are jointly liable for ESR penalties. Personal travel bans and restrictions on leaving Samoa may be imposed while the substance infringement is investigated.
AML - Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Body corporate fine up to WST 2,000,000 per incident; disqualification of the registered agent; asset seizure and bank account freezing ordered by the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Individual money-laundering offences are punishable by imprisonment up to 14 years. Directors are exposed to civil restitution claims by the Financial Intelligence Unit and confiscation of proceeds derived from the concealed gaming revenue.
Transfer Pricing - Non-Arm's-Length Advertising Monetization

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment by the Ministry of Revenue to the arm's-length price; reclassification of undeclared profits as deemed dividends subject to 15% withholding tax; penalty of 100% of the adjusted tax amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or instruct distorted intercompany agreements are jointly and severally liable with the IC for the adjustment and penalties, and may face criminal sanctions for false statements made in tax filings.
