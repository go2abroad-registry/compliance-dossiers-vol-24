Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa; official portal www.revenue.gov.ws; Customs Division within MfR administers import/export controls; Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) is the corporate registry for International Companies

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company (IC) with SIFA under the International Companies Act 1987 through a licensed registered agent/trustee in Apia; SIFA issues the Certificate of Incorporation. Step 2: File the TIN application with MfR on the official registration form, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office consent, director/shareholder particulars, and a declaration of offshore business scope (ISIC 6201 SaaS); the Commissioner of Revenue assigns the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). Step 3: Apply to MfR for a Certificate of Tax Exemption confirming the statutory exemption under ICA 1987 s.232 from income tax, VAGST, stamp duty, and all other national taxes; this certificate is provided to banks and counterparties. Step 4: No statutory business license is required for offshore SaaS activity; the IC must maintain its registered agent and file annual returns with SIFA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists under Samoan law. An International Company receives an automatic, non-revocable statutory tax exemption under ICA 1987 s.232 (as amended), conditional on carrying on business exclusively outside Samoa and deriving no Samoan-source income. No hybrid mismatch, CFC, or equivalent elections are available. Voluntary VAGST registration is not required because the IC is statutorily exempt from VAGST; the Certificate of Exemption is the operative evidence of tax status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 aggregate taxable turnover in any 12-month period triggers compulsory registration under the VAGST Act 2003; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. An International Company is exempt from VAGST regardless of turnover and is not required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAGST Act 2003, exports of goods and services supplied for the direct benefit of a non-resident outside Samoa are zero-rated. For PropTech/real-estate SaaS (ISIC 6201), the criteria are: (a) the customer is a non-resident not using the software in Samoa; (b) fees are received in convertible foreign currency; (c) the contract and invoice identify the place of supply as offshore. The International Company holds an absolute statutory exemption from VAGST under ICA 1987 s.232, making zero-rating a secondary safe-harbour rather than a primary obligation; refund claims are available only to registered persons and are not necessary for exempt ICs.

  • Customs export declaration (Export Entry/SAD) or deemed-export statutory declaration for non-tangible digital services
  • Executed SaaS subscription agreement or commercial invoice naming the non-resident counterparty and stating the offshore place of supply
  • Bill of lading or airway bill where incidental physical media/hardware is exported
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT payment evidence from the non-resident payer
  • Certificate of Exemption under International Companies Act 1987 s.232
  • Digital delivery logs or usage reports evidencing customer utilisation outside Samoa for audit substantiation

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate operates in Samoa. VAGST-registered persons must file returns electronically via the MfR online portal by the 28th day following the taxable period. Invoices (paper or electronic) must contain the supplier TIN, recipient name, date, description, consideration, and VAGST amount, with a 5-year record-retention requirement. International Companies are outside the VAGST net and issue non-VAGST commercial invoices.

MfR may audit under the Tax Administration Act 2022 with a general assessment window of 5 years, extended to 10 years where fraud or gross negligence is established. Fraudulent zero-rating or export refund claims attract a shortfall penalty of 100% of the tax avoided plus compounding monthly interest at 2%, criminal fines up to WST 100,000 or the tax amount, and imprisonment of directors/officers on conviction. An International Company that breaches the ring-fencing condition by carrying on business with Samoan residents or deriving Samoan-source income automatically forfeits the s.232 exemption, exposing the entity to retrospective re-assessment of income tax (27%), VAGST (15%), and stamp duty with penalty interest; SIFA may also strike off the IC and sanction the licensed registered agent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is not freely convertible outside Samoa. The Exchange Control Act 1976 establishes exchange control administered by the Central Bank of Samoa, and all commercial banks act as authorized dealers. Current account transactions are generally processed on presentation of underlying invoices and supporting documents. Capital account transfers, equity injections, cross-border loans, and dividend or profit remittances require exchange control approval and, where applicable, tax clearance certificates. International Companies are treated as non-resident persons for most exchange control purposes and may maintain foreign currency accounts, but any conversion into WST or transfer to Samoa resident persons must be routed through an authorized dealer and recorded by CBS.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate export proceeds into Samoa through an authorized dealer within the statutory period, normally 90 days from the date of shipment. Unless a specific exemption is granted by the Central Bank of Samoa, 100% of the export receipt must be offered for conversion into WST or deposited into an approved foreign currency account on terms stipulated by the authorized dealer. International Companies conducting exempt offshore business may apply for a standing exemption to retain export earnings in foreign currency, but the exporter must still file exchange control declarations and provide a banker's receipt evidencing inward remittance and conversion for any onshore or export transaction requiring WST settlement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMI and payment offerings such as Wise, Stripe, or a third-country electronic money institution cannot serve as the primary settlement account for Samoan treasury purposes because they are not licensed banks under the Central Bank of Samoa Act and cannot operate domestic WST clearing rails. A foreign EMI or payment facilitator may provide collection and disbursement services offshore, but all FX conversion, exchange control reporting, and Samoan standpoint payments must be routed through a CBS-licensed authorized dealer. Where the EMI is used for front-end SaaS subscription and landlord collection, the local bank remains the account of record for regulatory capital, escrow segregation, and FX approval. The arrangement must also be assessed under the Samoa Money Laundering Prevention Act, with the EMI treated as a third-party payment processor rather than a domestic financial institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan funds must be routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the remitter's licensed bank directly to the International Company's account at a CBS-authorized dealer. The payment reason or purpose code on the wire must clearly state 'equity injection', 'capital contribution', or 'shareholder loan - credit to capital account' as applicable; the beneficiary reference should contain the Samoa International Company registration number (IC-####-WS) and the unique board resolution reference. The local bank will hold the receipt in a capital escrow folder pending completion of source of funds verification, exchange control approval, and KYC release. For amounts above CBS threshold levels, the corporate customer must submit the CBS exchange control application form, a board resolution approving the capital injection, and evidence of the shareholder's capacity to fund. The bank will issue a foreign exchange purchase confirmation or inward credit advice, and the funds will be recorded in the books as issued share capital or director/subordinated shareholder loan.Outbound supplier, landlord, and SaaS settlement payments are cleared through the domestic authorized dealer using SWIFT MT103 for individual wire payments, with MT202 used only for interbank/treasury transfers. For documentary trade settlements, a commercial letter of credit (LC) is the required rail, with the presenting beneficiary supplying invoice, packing list, ocean/airway bill, insurance certificate, and certificate of origin as applicable. USD-denominated disbursements to third-party payment processors such as Stripe may be made through U.S. correspondent bank clearing (ACH direct, Fedwire, or SWIFT to Stripe's settlement bank) only after the local bank has performed sanctioned jurisdiction screening and issued a foreign exchange approval reference or conversion instruction. Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold and no Local File/Master File mandate under Samoa law; however, the Economic Substance (International Companies) Regulations 2019 impose ESR-1 mandatory substance reporting for all relevant-activity ICs regardless of revenue level; TP documentation (intercompany agreements, pricing methodology, benchmark analysis) should be maintained for all intercompany SaaS royalty and service transactions to survive foreign CFC and substance audits

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (International Companies) Regulations 2019 apply: an IC deriving income from relevant activities (including IP holding, financing, distribution, or provision of services such as the PropTech SaaS platform) must file Form ESR-1 with SIFA annually, maintain adequate qualified staff and physical premises in Samoa, incur sufficient local operating expenditure, and demonstrate that core income-generating activities are managed and conducted in Samoa; failure attracts monetary penalties, strike-off, and exchange of information with treaty partners

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company / absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty up to WST 10,000 for first offence and WST 20,000 for subsequent offences; compulsory disclosure of beneficial ownership to foreign tax authorities; strike-off and retroactive loss of the s.198 tax exemption

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face imprisonment up to 5 years; foreign CFC and anti-avoidance rules may attribute the PropTech platform income directly to the founders in their home jurisdiction
Prohibited domestic business activity

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WST 25,000; deregistration; the s.198 exemption is voided retroactively for the offending period and the IC becomes taxable at the 27% domestic rate on all Samoan-source income

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax (27%), penalties, and interest; repeat offenders face disqualification from acting as directors in Samoa
AML / beneficial ownership non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: Corporate fine up to WST 100,000; freezing of bank accounts; termination of Stripe Billing and ACH Direct payment rail access by U.S.-regulated processors; mandatory AML de-risking
