Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue of the Independent State of Samoa (Matagaluega o Tupe Maua), online portal: Ministry for Revenue Online Services at https://www.revenue.gov.ws (successor to the former Samoa Revenue Authority; tax administration unified at national level with no sub-national tax authorities).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity must be incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 (ICA 1987) through a licensed Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) registered agent, obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation and, where sought, a Certificate of Tax Exemption under ICA 1987. The entity then files a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) application with the Ministry for Revenue using Form IRD-001 (Application for Registration), accompanied by: (1) certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation; (2) Memorandum and Articles of Association; (3) SIFA-issued good standing certificate; (4) certified passport copies of directors/shareholders; (5) registered agent details and Samoa registered office address; and (6) a non-resident status declaration. The TIN is typically issued within 3 to 5 business days. If the International Company (IC) expects to make taxable supplies with a Samoa nexus exceeding SAT 100,000 in any consecutive 12-month period, the same form triggers compulsory Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration; the IC may also voluntarily register for GST to recover input tax on local administrative costs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. An International Company incorporated under the ICA 1987 is a non-resident, non-domestic entity subject to entity-level tax exemption: it is exempt from Samoa income tax, withholding tax, stamp duty, and all other national taxes on its international business income and transactions, provided it does not carry on business with Samoa residents, own Samoa land (except under a licensed international scheme), or derive income from Samoa sources. For GST purposes the IC remains within the territorial scope only for supplies connected with Samoa; offshore SaaS exports are treated as exported services.

Available election forms

Samoa has no US-style check-the-box or look-through election for ICs. Available statutory mechanisms: (a) ICA 1987 s.195 election: an IC may apply to the Minister for Revenue to be treated as a resident company for purposes of a double taxation agreement, thereby becoming subject to Samoa income tax on its worldwide income to the extent required for treaty benefits; (b) GST voluntary registration election under Goods and Services Tax Act 2017 (GST Act 2017) s.16, available to any person making taxable supplies regardless of turnover; (c) conversion election under ICA 1987 Part VIII to re-domicile as a domestic Samoa company, which terminates the Tax_Exempt_Zone status; (d) no hybrid mismatch election is prescribed under Samoa law, and the Income Tax Ordinance 2015 applies a general anti-avoidance rule and principal purpose test (with OECD BEPS-aligned rules) to deny treaty or exemption benefits where there is an abusive hybrid arrangement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

SAT 100,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period (mandatory registration); voluntary registration is available below the threshold; non-resident International Companies are only within the registration net for supplies with a Samoa nexus, and exported services do not count toward the threshold unless the IC is registered voluntarily.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% GST) is available under GST Act 2017 for the export of goods and the export of services. For a bulk transactional email API and newsletter hosting platform, the relevant criterion is the 'exported services' rule: the services are supplied to a recipient who is outside Samoa and not in Samoa at the time of supply; the services are not directly connected with land or goods situated in Samoa; and the services are not supplied to a person carrying on a business in Samoa to the extent the supply relates to that Samoa business. Offshore recipients of email infrastructure services therefore qualify for the 0% rate, enabling the IC to claim input tax credits/refunds on Samoa-sourced purchases (agent fees, local office costs, hosting expenses in Samoa) attributable to the zero-rated exports. If the IC is not GST-registered, exports fall outside the scope of Samoa GST with no refund entitlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec/EEZI) validated by the Samoa Customs Service — applicable to tangible goods; for pure email SaaS services no customs declaration is required and a written declaration of service export is used
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods (not applicable to services; replaced by service delivery records and API/session logs)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit advice from a licensed Samoa commercial bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and executed customer contract/order form identifying the non-resident recipient, description of email/newsletter hosting services, and currency of settlement
  • Recipient non-residency proof: certificate of residence/tax residence, jurisdiction of incorporation, and IP/domain registration data corroborating offshore consumption of the email infrastructure services

No real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API-based continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime operates in Samoa. Tax invoices may be issued in paper or electronic format; a GST-registered supplier must issue a tax invoice within 28 days of a taxable supply, and an electronic invoicing system may be used only with prior approval of the Commissioner of the Ministry for Revenue. In practice, email-infrastructure SaaS providers use standard billing systems (e.g., Stripe Billing) without statutory data integration obligations.

The Commissioner may audit exporters within the statutory record-keeping period (5 years) and issue a default assessment or raise a retrospective GST clawback where zero-rating is not supported by contemporaneous export documentation. Fraudulent or reckless zero-rating claims attract: (a) a tax shortfall penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded or a fixed penalty not exceeding SAT 50,000 for companies, at the Commissioner's discretion; (b) late payment interest at a statutory rate (CBS reference rate plus margin, currently yielding approximately 10% p.a.) running from the original due date; (c) criminal liability including fines up to SAT 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years for knowing misrepresentation or aiding GST evasion; and (d) suspension or cancellation of GST registration and loss of input credit entitlement. All export substantiation documents must be retained for 5 years, and failure to repatriate export proceeds within 90 days without Central Bank of Samoa approval may additionally trigger exchange control penalties and de-registration of the exporter.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, guaranteeing unrestricted current-account payments and transfers without the need for Central Bank approval. The national currency is the Samoan tala (WST), managed through a trade-weighted basket of NZD, AUD, USD, and JPY. Onshore resident entities remain subject to certain capital-account exchange controls and documentary requirements. However, an International Company (IC) registered under the International Companies Act 1987 is statutorily designated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes. Accordingly, the IC may maintain all treasury assets in USD, EUR, or other global currencies; may make dividend, loan, and liquidation remittances to non-resident shareholders without WST conversion; and is not subject to the onshore profit-remittance licensing procedure. Practical profit-remittance hurdles are minimal: banks will require an up-to-date Certificate of Good Standing, a tax exemption certificate, and a board resolution authorizing the remittance for amounts exceeding USD 10,000 or equivalent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident Samoan onshore exporters, the CBS Exchange Control Regulations impose a mandatory surrender rule: 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency must be offered for sale to an authorized commercial bank and converted into Samoan tala within 90 days of receipt unless a formal exemption certificate is issued by the CBS. International Companies (ICs) are expressly carved out from this regime due to their non-resident status under the Exchange Control Act. Consequently, all export receipts and recurring subscription revenue derived from the email newsletter platform (e.g., wholesale Stripe Billing payouts) may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency with no mandatory conversion ratio and no forced deposit into a Samoan tala account.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) and the Ministry of Finance Exchange Control Division, operating under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1972

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-Low. The Central Bank of Samoa licenses electronic money institutions under the Non-Bank Financial Institutions Act 2019, but only where a physical presence or a sponsored in-country agent is established. Fully remote offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) can provide auxiliary US/UK/EU payment collection in the same settlement vertical, but are not authorized to clear payments in Samoan tala and are subject to the CBS's inbound cross-border payment monitoring. For this commercial model, the recommended legal structure is to retain Stripe Billing as the primary card-acquiring rail, with payouts settled into the IC's USD account at a Samoa tier-1 bank. Any offshore EMI used only as an interim collection pool should maintain balances below the local licensing threshold and should not be represented to customers as the official treasury account holder.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be routed using a compliant SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the shareholder's bank to the IC's USD-denominated account at a Samoa licensed bank. The remittance instruction must include a clear purpose code, typically referenced as 'Equity capital for the purchase of issued shares' (Samoa BOP classification code S100), and must cite the board resolution date approving the share issuance in field 70 of the MT103. The receiving bank will require a board resolution, an updated shareholder register, and the completed 'Customer Source of Funds Declaration' to evidence the lawful origin of the funds. Because the IC is non-resident for exchange-control purposes, the capital injection remains in USD and is not converted into WST; however, the bank is obligated to report the transaction to the CBS for balance-of-payments statistics. If the capital injection transits through an offshore fintech EMI, the EMI's name must match the shareholder name exactly; otherwise the bank will reject the credit for compliance review.For operational trade disbursements the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103, used for cloud-infrastructure vendor payments (AWS, DigitalOcean), SaaS licensing fees, professional advisory invoices, and dividend distributions. Each outbound MT103 must be encoded with the correct beneficiary bank identifier (BIC), an IBAN or US ABA account number where applicable, and a statement in field 70 including the invoice and commercial contract reference. For low-value monthly disbursements, an automatic clearing house (ACH) tokenized payment within a United States treasury pool may be linked to the same Samoa bank account, provided the bank has an executed ACH relationship with its US correspondent. Commercial letters of credit (LC) are not necessary for this software business model, but a standby letter of credit (SBLC) can be established at the tier-1 Samoan bank to secure a material datacenter or hardware supply agreement if and when capital expenditure increases; documentary collections (D/P and D/A) may be used for single large-scale hardware purchases, though market practice favors straightforward SWIFT wires for SaaS disbursements.Outright forward foreign exchange contracts (FX forwards) on USD/WST and USD/EUR with an authorized Samoan commercial bankNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing the Samoan tala where offshore liquidity is insufficient or where the underlying exposure is an accounting-only translation riskCross-currency swaps (CCS) for intercompany treasury structures involving long-term USD to EUR or USD to AUD funding arrangementsEuropean-style foreign exchange options (plain vanilla calls and puts) transacted under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with bank counterparties rated at or above Standard & Poor's A-FX swaps for short-term operational rollover between USD, EUR, and WST once the Samoan bank confirms pricing under a global treasury limit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Company (IC) — s.195 International Companies Act 1987 grants full exemption; general Samoa corporate income tax rate is 25% for resident trading companies (Income Tax Act 1974).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — dividends, distributions, and deemed distributions paid by an International Company to non-resident or resident shareholders are statutorily exempt (s.195 ICA 1987). For comparison, Samoa's general statutory dividend WHT on a resident trading company is 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No WST-denominated threshold is enacted: Samoa has not adopted OECD Local File / Master File or CbCR filing legislation for International Companies. The general anti-avoidance rule in the Income Tax Act 1974 permits the Commissioner to recharacterize non-arm's-length related-party pricing. All intercompany contracts (email platform licence fees, API infrastructure charges, management fees) must therefore be supported by documented arm's-length pricing and contemporary evidence of Samoa substance.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2021 and its 2022 Regulations subject an IC to Economic Substance Requirements when it carries on a 'relevant activity' — including Headquarters, Distribution and Service Centre, Financing, and IP Holding — from Samoa. A Bulk Transactional Email API and Newsletter Hosting Platform (ISIC 6201, NAICS 518210) supplying related parties will be scrutinized as a service-centre / headquarters activity; the IC must evidence: (i) physical premises in Samoa; (ii) adequate full-time employees; (iii) proportionate local operating expenditure; and (iv) board meetings directed and managed in Samoa. A passive foreign-owned IC serving unrelated global customers must still maintain substance-consistent records but faces a lower factual ESR burden.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abuse of IC Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at the 25% corporate rate on deemed Samoa profits, 25% penalty tax, 2% per month late-payment interest, a 5-year back-duty window, and a fine of up to WST 100,000 under the Income Tax Act 1974.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the certified unpaid tax (s.132 ITA 1974); fraudulent evasion carries imprisonment up to 7 years; the court may issue travel bans and freeze personal assets pending prosecution.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WST 1,000,000 under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2021; persistent violation for 12+ months triggers compulsory strike-off by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: each director is personally liable for the fine; making a false substance declaration is an indictable offense punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years; travel bans and asset preservation orders commonly accompany enforcement.
AML/CFT and Fraud-Facilitation Breach (Phishing Infrastructure)

Statutory sanction: Under the Samoa AML/CFT Act 2007: corporate fine up to WST 2,000,000; money-laundering conviction: imprisonment up to 10 years and asset confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2007; failure to report a suspicious transaction: fine up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are prosecuted as natural persons — imprisonment up to 10 years for laundering proceeds derived from fraud; personal assets restrained and forfeited; FIU-issued travel bans apply from the moment an investigation is opened.
Fraudulent Trading / Wrongful Insolvency

Statutory sanction: The court may order the director to make unlimited personal contributions to the company's assets; fraudulent trading carries a fine of up to WST 500,000 under the International Companies Act 1987 and applicable company-law provisions.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution of all misapplied funds, extension of judgment liability to shadow directors of the parent entity, disqualification as a director for up to 10 years, and imprisonment up to 5 years for fraudulent trading.
