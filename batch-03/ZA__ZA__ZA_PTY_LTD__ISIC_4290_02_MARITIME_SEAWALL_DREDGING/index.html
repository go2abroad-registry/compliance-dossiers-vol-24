UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=5% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Companies Act 71 of 2008, section 56 (beneficial interest and beneficial ownership register), as amended by: (i) the General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act 22 of 2022, which came into force on 19 December 2022 and made beneficial ownership registration with CIPC mandatory effective 24 May 2023; (ii) the Companies Amendment Act 16 of 2024 (assented 26 July 2024); read with the Beneficial Ownership Regulations, 2023 (GN 755 in Government Gazette 48662 of 24 May 2023). The statutory UBO disclosure threshold under South African law is 5% (direct or indirect ownership of shares, voting rights, or control), which is stricter than the 25% FATF minimum benchmark; the 25% reference is therefore not the operative threshold in South Africa. Additional AML/CFT statutes: Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 (FICA), as amended by the Financial Intelligence Centre Amendment Act 11 of 2008 and the General Laws Amendment Act 22 of 2022; Protection of Constitutional Democracy against Terrorist and Related Activities Act 33 of 2004 (POCDATARA); Prevention of Organised Crime Act 121 of 1998; and FIC directives and guidance notes on accountable institutions, PEPs, and beneficial ownership information.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permitted in principle: section 23 of the Companies Act requires every company to maintain a registered office in South Africa that is accessible to the public, plus a postal address; the registered office may be the office of the incorporating attorney or a licensed registered-office service provider, provided a physical South African street address exists at which service of process can be accepted. However, for a marine dredging and seawall engineering construction entity (ISIC 4290) seeking port authority contracts, SARS tax-residence substance, SAMSA maritime licensing, and FICA-compliant banking, a dedicated physical office is strongly recommended; a virtual-only office is acceptable for incorporation but is assessed as an elevated substance and licensing risk once the entity tenders for Transnet National Ports Authority or Department of Public Works projects.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); eFiling portal at https://www.sars.gov.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity with a South African economic presence must apply for income tax registration on SARS eFiling, obtain an Income Tax Reference Number and, if applicable, a VAT number. Non-resident companies must register within 21 business days after ceasing to be a foreign resident for tax purposes or upon forming a fixed domestic permanent establishment. Registration is effected through a SARS-accredited tax practitioner or directly via eFiling with completion of the relevant registration forms, submission of constitutive documents, a power of attorney for resident representatives, and evidence of local address.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime is available for Proprietary Limited companies in South Africa. Certain elections are available under the VAT Act, including the deemed input tax election on second-hand goods and the VAT grouping election for connected persons. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of movable goods exported from South Africa under contractual terms placing the exporter in the general export trade, provided the exporter retains proof of export. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) where the services are physically rendered wholly or partly outside South Africa (or to certain offshore shipping/mining activities), and the supplier retains documentary proof of the non-resident status. Marine dredging services for sea-wall and breakwater works qualify as exported services if the contract is with a non-resident principal and the supply is not connected to any South African fixed property or movable property situated in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) stamped by SARS Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export and destination
  • Foreign inward remittance certificate or bank statement proving receipt of export proceeds in full and within 180 days
  • Original VAT 264 export claim schedule and contractual documentation supporting offshore supply

South Africa does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. VAT invoices are governed by section 20 of the VAT Act; electronic tax invoices are permissible if the system produces a printable or electronically retrievable format and ensures authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility. Any e-invoicing standard issued by SARS is interoperable with the existing eFiling and e@syFile channels, but no continuous API clearance is mandated.

SARS conducts risk-based audits on export zero-rated claims. Where export proof or foreign remittance is absent or false, the supplier is liable to output VAT at 15% retrospectively, plus statutory interest. Section 60 of the VAT Act imposes a penalty of up to 100% of the unpaid tax for deliberate evasion, and section 235 of the Tax Administration Act adds criminal sanctions, including fines and imprisonment up to five years for fraudulent zero-rating claims. Misdeclaration penalties range from 10% to 150% of the shortfall depending on prejudice to SARS.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The rand (ZAR) is a floating currency with full current-account convertibility: service and maritime EPC receipts (port-authority milestone payments) may be received in any convertible currency and sold to an authorised dealer without prior FinSurv approval, subject to documentary verification. Capital-account flows are managed: inward direct equity investment by non-residents into a (Pty) Ltd is permitted without exchange-control approval where routed through an authorised dealer and declared to SARS on the Inward Foreign Investment declaration (INV); outward capital flows by South African residents (loans, guarantees, equity participation, dividend remittances) remain restricted and require either FinSurv approval, utilisation of a corporate outward-investment dispensation (currently capped at ZAR 1 billion per transaction per group; larger amounts require FinSurv approval), or compliance with specific dispensations. Profit-remittance hurdle: dividend payments to non-resident shareholders are freely transferable once the company holds (i) a SARS tax-clearance certificate in good standing, (ii) an audited dividend declaration signed by the board, (iii) the inward-investment reference/record established at receipt of the share subscription, and (iv) a FICA-completed remittance instruction; no separate SARB ruling is needed. Dividend withholding tax and, for interest-bearing shareholder loans, the SARS section 23N/23M interest-limitation and thin-capitalisation rules are the fiscal checkpoints, so the capital injection should be structured as equity or an arm's-length loan with contemporaneous documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export repatriation: all export service receipts, including dredging milestones, must be received in South Africa within 180 days from the date of service billing/completion (or date of shipment for goods). On receipt, foreign currency must be disposed of within 30 days by sale to an authorised dealer for rand, or credited to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) with an authorised dealer under the applicable FCA limits. No mandatory conversion ratio applies: exporters may lawfully retain up to 100% of export proceeds in an FCA under the emerging/small-enterprise regime, or utilise FinSurv-approved retention for larger groups. SARS/FinSurv require the underlying export declaration (SAD500 for goods) or service-export contract, a receipt advice, and a complete exchange-control audit trail. Where a port authority pays in extended milestone terms exceeding 180 days, a prospective application for extended payment terms must be submitted to the authorised dealer/FinSurv with the EPC schedule and a certified debt statement; an unapproved overdue receivable constitutes an exchange-control contravention.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through its Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (as amended). FinSurv delegates day-to-day administration to Authorised Dealers (Tier-1 South African banks) and Authorised Dealers in Foreign Exchange with limited authority; the Prudential Authority (PA) of SARB supervises banking AML/CFT conduct and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) administers FICA reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: C- (not viable as a primary treasury rail). Offshore digital EMIs and cross-border payment fintechs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, WorldFirst and similar) hold no South African banking licence and are not authorised dealers in foreign exchange; a resident (Pty) Ltd is prohibited by the Exchange Control Regulations from maintaining a foreign-currency clearing account outside the authorised-dealer system. Such EMIs may be used only as a marginal receivables-agglomeration utility, and even then balances must be swept into the South African operational account via SWIFT MT103 with a correct purpose code. The FIC Act and the National Payment System Act, 1998 subject an EMI to local payment-intermediary licensing if it touches the domestic clearing system. Using an unlicensed EMI for inward/outward trade settlement would create FICA non-compliance, expose the company to sanctions-screening gaps, and trigger adverse FinSurv findings in the annual exchange-control audit. Recommended position: avoid EMIs for the primary account; if unavoidable, maintain a low-balance collection-only relationship with daily zero-balancing sweeps to the Tier-1 bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Protocol for non-resident equity contribution: (1) prior board resolution approving an allotment of shares or a shareholder-loan drawdown; (2) execution of the share-subscription or loan agreement; (3) wiring instruction from the non-resident bank via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the (Pty) Ltd account at the South African authorised dealer, with purpose code 'EQUI' (equity) or 'LOAN' (shareholder loan) and the unique reference ZA__ZA__ZA_PTY_LTD__ISIC_4290_02_MARITIME_SEAWALL_DREDGING in field 70; (4) the receiving bank verifies FICA/KYC, the share-charge and inward-investment declaration, then credits the ZAR account (or USD/EUR FCA) and issues a notice of inward transfer; (5) within 30 days, the company files the SARS Inward Foreign Investment (INV) declaration (the bank may e-file on its behalf) to obtain the inward-investment reference used for future dividend remittances; (6) within 31 days after allotment, a CIPC share-allotment return is filed; (7) exchange-control compliance: no FinSurv pre-approval is required for inward equity, but the transaction must be recorded on the authorised dealer's electronic exchange-control register and in the company's annual financial statements. Where the injection is a shareholder loan, the loan agreement, drawdown, interest schedule and repayment terms must be captured in the FinSurv loan file and satisfy SARS section 23N/23M interest-limitation rules. The bank will reject wires carrying generic purpose codes or missing documentary support, and may quarantine funds pending sanctions screening and beneficial-ownership verification.Primary clearing rails for offshore EPC disbursements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for high-value USD/EUR supplier and sub-contractor payments (tug hire, fuel/bunkering, environmental monitoring, port tariffs), each covered by a completed exchange-control instruction with underlying pro-forma invoice and contract, executed through the bank's online treasury and linked to an approved forward exchange contract; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (irrevocable, often confirmed) issued by the South African bank for the import of capital assets (trailing-suction hopper dredgers, cutter heads, marine plant spares), with L/C terms against the ocean bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, insurance certificate and surveyor's certificate; (3) Import collections (D/P, D/A) and Bank Payment Undertakings (BPU) for mid-value procurement, plus the bank's digital trade platform; (4) within the Common Monetary Area (South Africa, Namibia, Lesotho, eSwatini), rand disbursements settle through BankServAfrica without exchange-control documentation; to non-CMA SACU/COMESA partners (e.g., Mozambique, DRC), USD or ZAR payments use the MT103 rail with full documentary support; (5) all outward disbursements must match the declared business purpose and prior export-receipt obligations: the bank will reconcile the company's trade cycle, verify the applicable SARS tax-clearance certificates, and escalate related-party or high-value payments to the FinSurv desk. Record-keeping: retain each MT103/MT202 advice, payment application, underlying invoice/contract, SAD500 for goods and all L/C documentation for at least five years for FinSurv and FIC audit purposes.Onshore deliverable Forward Exchange Contracts (FECs) with authorised dealers: tenure 7-365 days, fully documented underlying exposure (dredging milestone receivables or import payables), governed by an approved FX Risk Management Mandate and bank Master FEC Agreement; the standard instrument for converting USD/EUR milestone receipts into ZAR.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) with a South African authorised dealer under an ISDA/CSA or bank FX master agreement, matched to the EUR/USD capex and milestone cash-flow profile; requires FinSurv/authorised-dealer approvals, named counterparty limits, and a clear non-speculative hedging statement.OTC FX options and zero-cost collars (vanilla, fully deliverable ZAR/USD or ZAR/EUR) purchased from the authorised dealer or its licenced international affiliate, permissible solely against a demonstrable current-account exposure, with the premium documented as treasury expenditure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for South African residents onshore; the offshore ZAR NDF market is limited to non-residents, therefore the company must hedge exclusively through deliverable FECs, CCS and vanilla options onshore.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: C- (not viable as a primary treasury rail). Offshore digital EMIs and cross-border payment fintechs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, WorldFirst and similar) hold no South African banking licence and are not authorised dealers in foreign exchange; a resident (Pty) Ltd is prohibited by the Exchange Control Regulations from maintaining a foreign-currency clearing account outside the authorised-dealer system. Such EMIs may be used only as a marginal receivables-agglomeration utility, and even then balances must be swept into the South African operational account via SWIFT MT103 with a correct purpose code. The FIC Act and the National Payment System Act, 1998 subject an EMI to local payment-intermediary licensing if it touches the domestic clearing system. Using an unlicensed EMI for inward/outward trade settlement would create FICA non-compliance, expose the company to sanctions-screening gaps, and trigger adverse FinSurv findings in the annual exchange-control audit. Recommended position: avoid EMIs for the primary account; if unavoidable, maintain a low-balance collection-only relationship with daily zero-balancing sweeps to the Tier-1 bank account.
