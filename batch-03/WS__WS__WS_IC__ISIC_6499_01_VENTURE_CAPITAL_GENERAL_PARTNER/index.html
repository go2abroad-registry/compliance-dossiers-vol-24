Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Government of Samoa; official portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws (e-filing through the Digital Revenue System on the TaXChange platform)

E-government filing portal

Incorporation of the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 2008 must be effected through a licensed sponsor/registered agent of the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). After incorporation, apply to the Commissioner of the Ministry for Revenue for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using Form TIN-01, submitting the certificate of incorporation, registered office certificate, agent consent, and know-your-customer documentation. Thereafter, file the annual declaration with SIFA (not an income tax return) and obtain the Certificate of Exemption from Taxes under Section 111 of the International Companies Act 2008; the exemption certificate confirms that no Samoa income tax or VAGST applies to offshore business activities and that the IC is outside the domestic tax net.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Incorporation of the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 2008 must be effected through a licensed sponsor/registered agent of the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). After incorporation, apply to the Commissioner of the Ministry for Revenue for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using Form TIN-01, submitting the certificate of incorporation, registered office certificate, agent consent, and know-your-customer documentation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. An IC's exempt status is mandatory rather than elective; a licensed IC engaging in business with Samoan residents or deriving Samoa-source income without an approved waiver automatically becomes taxable on that income under the Income Tax Act 2012 and the VAGST Act 2003. The functional election is the statutory conversion between IC form (tax-exempt offshore) and domestic company form (fully taxable), permitted by the International Companies Act 2008.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 120,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; an IC supplying services exclusively to non-residents is outside the VAGST net and may still voluntarily register to claim input credits against zero-rated export supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under the VAGST Act 2003 to exports of goods removed from Samoa and to exported services supplied to a non-resident recipient for benefit wholly outside Samoa. A Venture Capital General Partner may therefore zero-rate management fees, carried-interest structuring, advisory, and administrative services supplied to an offshore fund for non-resident limited partners; the IC tax exemption certificate independently removes VAGST regardless of registration status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (preliminary customs bill of entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Master Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing FX repatriation through a licensed bank
  • Commercial invoice together with the executed offshore fund and GP services agreement identifying the non-resident recipient

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital signature mandate exists in Samoa. VAGST-registered suppliers must issue standard tax invoices showing the supplier TIN and VAGST registration number, and file periodic returns electronically through the MfR Digital Revenue System.

The Commissioner may reassess within 4 years, extended to 7 years for fraud or wilful misrepresentation. Invalid zero-rating triggers VAGST at 15% on the full consideration, late-payment penalties of 2% per month, and a shortfall penalty up to 150% of the tax shortfall for evasion or intentional misstatement. Criminal exposure includes fines up to WST 20,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years per offence under the VAGST Act 2003 and the Tax Administration Act 2012; input tax credits are retrospectively clawed back with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoa tala (WST) is subject to a managed float with periodic CBS intervention; the currency is not freely convertible offshore, and the domestic interbank market is thin. Current-account transactions, including profit remittances, dividend distributions, management fees, and service payments, are processed freely by Authorized Dealers upon submission of bona fide documentary evidence. Capital-account transactions, including inward equity investment, loan injections, capital repayments, and portfolio transfers, require prior CBS authorization or an Authorized Dealer exercise of delegated authority, with full disclosure of the beneficial owner. For International Companies (ICs) designated as non-resident, foreign-currency accounts are permitted and remittances to non-resident investors are treated as current-account flows if supported by audited financial statements, board resolutions, and the relevant exchange-control declaration. Profit remittance is structurally unimpeded for a tax-exempt IC, but the practical hurdle is the banks' insistence on proving the underlying fund economics and the absence of a 'shell' purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Regulations 1979, resident exporters must receive full payment for exported goods within six months of the date of exportation and must surrender any foreign-currency export proceeds to an Authorized Dealer within 30 days of receipt, with the statutory default being 100% conversion of qualifying export receipts into WST. General exemptions exist for certain service exporters, reinvoicing centers, and entities granted non-resident status. An International Company acting as a Venture Capital GP has no local physical export proceeds; management fees and carried-interest receipts from non-resident fund investors are classified as invisible earnings and are outside the surrender rule, provided the IC maintains its non-resident designation and files the applicable CBS balance-of-payments statistical returns through its Authorized Dealer.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, acting as the monetary authority and foreign exchange regulator under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1979 (as amended). CBS exercises delegated control through licensed Authorized Dealers, which are all CBS-licensed commercial banks. The Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) separately regulates the International Company registry and the registered agent network, but has no foreign exchange competence.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW — structurally constrained. Samoa has no fintech EMI or digital-bank licensing category; the CBS recognizes only licensed banks as Authorized Dealers and no foreign EMI authorization confers clearing or settlement privileges in Samoa. An offshore EMI cannot act as the primary treasury rail for the IC because Samoan Authorized Dealers will reject funding and receipt flows through an unlicensed third-country payment institution absent a correspondent banking relationship, and the IC's own AML/KYC stack must ultimately sit inside a CBS-licensed institution. EMIs are acceptable only as a supplementary pass-through for LP bridge payments if the EMI maintains a pooled settlement account with a CBS-licensed correspondent and the IC preserves a direct SWIFT MT103 audit trail; in practice this elevates false-positive sanctions hits and adds settlement delay.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

LP capital calls are settled by SWIFT MT103 from each limited partner to the IC's USD operational account held with a CBS-licensed Authorized Dealer (BSP or ANZ Samoa), with MT202 cover for intermediary banks where required. Every incoming wire must include the IC's exact legal name, a unique drawdown reference number, and a coded narrative tied to the capital-call notice; the receiving bank will require the signed Capital Call Notice, a completed CBS balance-of-payments/exchange-control declaration, and an LP source-of-funds attestation for amounts above the applicable reporting threshold. Funds are swept into a segregated Capital Escrow Account to isolate uncalled commitments from operating liquidity; any WST conversion is executed at prevailing CBS interbank rates and is permissible for non-resident accounts without additional surrender obligations. Same-day availability applies after sanctions and PEP screening, and a pre-advice MT799 message is recommended to accelerate clearing. All MT103 records, investor registers, and drawdown documents must be retained for a minimum of seven years for CBS inspection.Outbound disbursements to portfolio companies and service providers route primarily through SWIFT MT103 core wires quoting the portfolio-company identifier and board-approved drawdown authorization; MT202 cover payments apply when the beneficiary bank operates a nested account structure. For equity and convertible-note subscriptions, banks require the LPA valuation memo, the investee's KYC pack, and a board resolution confirming the investment. Commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are available but atypical for venture-stage disbursements; they are used only where a portfolio company requires documentary trade settlement and the issuing bank confirms the LC structure as a permissible outbound rail. Management-fee sweeps and carried-interest distributions to non-resident principals are processed as current-account remittances with the IC's tax-exemption certificate attached and the applicable exchange-control form completed. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Company profits under section 197 of the International Companies Act 1987; general resident-company headline rate is 27% under the Samoa Income Tax Act 2012.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends and other distributions paid by an International Company, by virtue of section 197 of the International Companies Act 1987. The general Samoan statutory withholding tax rate on dividends paid by a taxable resident company to a non-resident is 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local-file or master-file obligation applies to a tax-exempt International Company because it has no Samoan assessable income. For a taxable Samoan company, contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is triggered when annual related-party cross-border transactions exceed WST 500,000 or when the taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds WST 5,000,000. Tax-exempt ICs still need to maintain transaction records sufficient to demonstrate arm's-length charges for management fees, carried interest allocations, and expense reimbursements if challenged.

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not impose separate Economic Substance Regulations comparable to BVI or Cayman Islands rules. Nevertheless, an International Company must maintain a valid registered office and licensed registered agent in Samoa, keep adequate records, and refrain from domestic Samoan business. The exemption under section 197 of the International Companies Act 1987 will be denied or retroactively revoked if the entity is managed and controlled from Samoa, earns Samoa-source income, or is used as a shell to avoid taxes that would otherwise be payable by the underlying fund or its principals.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under the Samoa Income Tax Act 2012 the tax authority may assess evaded tax at 27%, impose a civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded, and initiate criminal prosecution carrying fines up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Assets connected to the evasion are subject to freezing, seizure, and forfeiture.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the IC is a mere facade for fraud; directors, the general partner, and individual promoters are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of diverted profits and may face travel bans, director disqualification, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike the IC off the register, the International Finance Authority may suspend or revoke the licensed agent, and the tax exemption may be disallowed with retrospective effect, causing all profits of the venture capital GP to be taxed at 27%. Public register disclosure of the shell status may also be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow managers are personally liable for liabilities incurred through fraudulent trading; courts can order civil restitution of fees extracted, issue travel bans pending investigation, and impose imprisonment where the deficient substance is part of a deliberate tax evasion scheme.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance by Venture Capital GP

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 and supporting AML/CFT Rules, the body corporate is liable to fines up to WST 1,000,000, cancellation of the financial services licence, confiscation of proceeds, and public reprimand; individuals knowingly involved face imprisonment up to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the GP's natural-person signatories may be prosecuted personally, subjected to civil asset confiscation, restricted from international travel, and made personally liable for funds lost to money laundering schemes.
Unlicensed and Prohibited Onshore Activity

Statutory sanction: The International Companies Act 1987 exemption ceases immediately; the company is treated as a resident taxable company at 27%; unlicensed financial service activity is illegal and subject to fines, licence revocation, winding-up, and voidance of affected contracts. The Ministry for Revenue may assess additional penalties and interest on unreported income.

Director personal liability: Directors and persons knowingly engaged in the prohibited onshore business cannot rely on limited liability; they are jointly and severally liable for all company debts and third-party losses, and may be subject to civil restitution orders, travel bans, and imprisonment for carrying on unlicensed financial services.
