Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is not permissible as the statutory registered office of a Samoa International Company. The company must maintain a physical registered office in Samoa, which is normally the physical suite of the licensed registered agent, for service of process and statutory records. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Ministry for Revenue (MoR); official e-services portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

For a Samoa International Company (IC), no TIN/VAGST ID is required for offshore business because the IC enjoys a statutory tax-exempt zone classification. The standard procedure is for the registered agent to apply to the MoR for a Certificate of Tax Exemption using the agent authorization and the IC's Certificate of Incorporation; the MoR issues a tax-exempt certificate within 5 working days. If the IC carries on taxable activities in Samoa (e.g., local litigation support), it must apply for a TIN via Form IR-01 to the MoR Registrar, attaching the registered agent letter, director's passport copy, and certificate of registered office, and will then be issued a tax identifier and VAGST registration eligibility.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election exists in Samoa. Under the International Companies Act 1988, a foreign-owned IC may elect to maintain tax-exempt status by limiting its business to offshore activities; any decision to become a domestic company under the Companies Act 2001 or to be treated as a resident company for income tax purposes terminates the exempt status. Such election must be made in writing to the Registrar of International Companies and to the MoR Commercial Tax Division and is irrevocable for the remaining tax year.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 of taxable supplies in Samoa during any consecutive 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods and to services supplied to a non-resident client who is outside Samoa at the time of supply and who receives the economic benefit outside Samoa. Cross-border commercial litigation and arbitration services provided from the Samoa IC to overseas principals are zero-rated if no part of the services is performed in Samoa, no land or goods in Samoa are involved, and the fees are paid from non-Samoan accounts. The supplier must obtain and preserve documentary evidence within 90 days after supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, API mandate, or digital stamping is currently in force. Taxpayers may issue electronic or paper invoices; VAGST returns must be filed online through the MoR e-services portal. The MoR does not require pre-approval of invoice numbering, but all invoices must contain the supplier's TIN, the words 'Tax Invoice', the date, description, GST amount, and tax-exclusive value.

If zero-rating is claimed without the required export documentation or foreign inward remittance within 180 days, the supply is re-characterized as taxable at 15%, and a late assessment may be raised. Penalties include a tax shortfall penalty of 100% of the understated VAGST, a monthly late-payment penalty of 1.5% (minimum WST 100), and an 8% per annum interest charge. Fraudulent or negligent zero-rating claims are subject to civil penalties up to WST 100,000 and criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 2004, with imprisonment up to three years; MoR may claw back refunds for six years and suspend the IC's tax-exempt certificate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tālā (WST) is partially convertible. Current account transactions, including trade payments and ordinary profit repatriation by non-resident license holders, are generally permitted subject to CBS-approved exchange control forms and supporting documentation. Capital account transactions, especially capital injections, overseas investments, and offshore borrowings by resident entities, require prior CBS approval or an authorized dealer's exchange control compliance. For International Company structures, the Foreign Sales Corporation and International Companies Acts provide exemptions, but practical convertibility is administered through commercial banks as authorized dealers. Profit and capital outflows for non-resident shareholders are permitted upon proof of taxed/exempt status, audited financials, and absence of domestic liabilities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa requires exporters to repatriate export proceeds to Samoa within 90 days of shipment unless a specific exemption is granted by the Central Bank of Samoa. The proceeds must be offered for conversion into WST through an authorized dealer unless the exporter holds an approved foreign currency retention account. Conversion of retained foreign currency above the statutory limit is subject to CBS approval. For International Companies whose export activities are carried on outside Samoa, the surrender rule may be relaxed, but the default statutory timeline and mandatory conversion ratio remain 100% unless a CBS exemption or foreign currency account authorization is obtained.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), also designated as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984, with the Minister of Finance retaining residual exchange control powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Samoa is not a recognized market for offshore digital electronic money institutions. The Central Bank of Samoa does not issue EMI licenses, and foreign fintech EMIs are not recognized as authorized dealers for WST clearing or exchange control purposes. An International Company may maintain a non-resident EMI account for auxiliary fiat collection, but it cannot serve as the primary IOLTA trust or settlement rail for Samoan-regulated litigation finance. Legal constraints include absence of an EMI passporting regime, CBS restrictions on foreign exchange transactions without an authorized dealer, and the Samoan Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2019 imposing record-keeping and monitoring obligations on local custodians. A licensed offshore bank under the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA) is more viable than a generic offshore EMI but still lacks domestic clearing access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's regulated bank to the Samoa International Company's WST or USD operating account at an authorized dealer. The wire instruction must state the purpose code 'Capital Injection / Direct Investment' and identify the legal entity as 'WS_IC (International Company No. XXX)'. The bank will require the CBS Exchange Control form for capital transactions, a board resolution authorizing the contribution, and a certificate from the registered agent verifying that the entity is an International Company exempt from minimum capital requirements. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Companies (IC) under the International Companies Act 1988 (as amended); standard resident corporate rate is 27% under the Income Tax Act 2015.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for International Companies. Standard Samoa domestic dividend withholding tax is 15% under the Income Tax Act 2015, but this is wholly disapplied for IC distributions under the International Companies Act exemption.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No transfer pricing documentation thresholds are specifically gazetted for tax-exempt International Companies. As a prudent compliance standard, related-party cross-border transactions exceeding WST 5,000,000 in aggregate per fiscal year should be covered by contemporaneous local file and master file documentation, consistent with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines incorporated by the Samoa Income Tax Act 2015.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under the International Companies Amendment Act 2021 require every International Company carrying on a relevant activity (banking, financing, leasing, fund management, headquarters, holding, intellectual property, distribution, and service center) to demonstrate adequate substance in Samoa. This includes physical presence, qualified local employees, local board meetings, and substantial operational expenditure. Exempt companies must maintain a simple holding-company substance declaration. The Registrar of International Companies screens filings; non-compliance carries automatic penalties and possible deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imposition of the full corporate income tax rate of 27%, plus interest and a penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall under the Samoa Income Tax Act 2015. The IC's exempt status may be retroactively revoked and the company may be struck off the International Companies Register.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false declarations face criminal liability under the Crimes Act 2013, with penalties including imprisonment up to 7 years, fines up to WST 250,000, and a travel ban. Civil restitution orders may pierce the corporate veil, holding directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Mandatory penalty of WST 250,000, deregistration of the International Company, and forfeiture of assets under the Economic Substance Regulations. The Minister of Revenue may also impose annual substance reporting requirements and deny benefits to the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents are personally liable for substances declarations; false certification attracts fines and up to 3 years' imprisonment. A court may order directorial disqualification and a travel ban for failure to cooperate with the Ministry of Revenue.
Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Offences under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2021 (Samoa) carry penalties up to WST 1,000,000 for entities and WST 500,000 for individuals, plus asset seizure and freezing orders. The International Company may be wound up by the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and registered agents are personally exposed to criminal prosecution. Convictions may result in up to 10 years' imprisonment, personal travel bans, and extension of restitution orders beyond corporate assets to personal property obtained from the illicit activity.
