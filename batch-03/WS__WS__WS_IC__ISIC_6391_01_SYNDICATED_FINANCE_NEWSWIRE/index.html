Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), Ministry for Revenue; taxpayer portal: https://www.revenue.gov.ws; offshore entity registrar: Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), https://www.sifa.ws

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity must first incorporate as an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 through a licensed offshore service provider; the IC files its charter with the Registrar under SIFA/MCIL, then applies to the Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA) for a Tax Identification Number (TIN). Compulsory VAGST registration applies only where taxable supplies in Samoa exceed WST 100,000 annual turnover; a pure offshore IC deriving exclusively foreign-source income ordinarily obtains a TIN as a tax-exempt registrant without VAGST registration, for banking and AML compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Statutorily exempt International Company under the International Companies Act 1987; offshore-source income, distributions and capital gains exempt from Samoa income tax and withholding tax; subject only to annual government licence fees)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Samoa ICs are statutorily tax-exempt regardless of ownership or management location. Elections available: (i) IC status election under International Companies Act 1987; (ii) VAGST cash-basis or simplified accounting election for qualifying small suppliers; (iii) treaty tie-breaker and hybrid mismatch adjustments under Samoa's double tax agreements and OECD/G20 BEPS Inclusive Framework commitments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover (approx. USD 37,000); compulsory registration for persons making taxable supplies in Samoa above the threshold; offshore ICs supplying only exempt or exported services remain registrable on an optional basis

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to physical exports of goods, international transport, and specified exported services supplied to non-resident recipients for use wholly outside Samoa. The syndicated finance newswire/API subscription feed qualifies as an exported service where the subscriber is a non-resident financial institution and the service benefit is wholly consumed offshore; evidence of delivery, payment and foreign use must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec) evidencing physical export and customs clearance
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / electronic delivery log for cross-border goods or service transmission
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from a licensed Samoa commercial bank evidencing receipt of export consideration in convertible foreign currency
  • Export service contract and API delivery logs demonstrating delivery of the syndicated finance newswire feed to non-resident subscribers

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing mandate currently applies in Samoa. The SRA requires standard tax invoices, an electronic VAGST return filed via the SRA online portal, and retention of underlying records; government-approved real-time clearance e-invoicing has not been enacted.

False or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger retrospective clawback of input VAGST credits, penalties up to 150% of the tax evaded, monetary fines up to WST 100,000 per offence, and/or imprisonment under the VAGST Act 2004 and Tax Administration Act 2012; SRA audit may re-characterise zero-rating where the non-resident recipient, place of supply, or economic substance tests are not satisfied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tālā (WST) is not freely convertible offshore. Current-account payments and profit remittances are generally approved upon submission of documentary evidence through an authorized dealer (licensed commercial bank). Capital-account transactions, including inbound debt/equity transfers and outward investment, are subject to prior CBS approval unless the entity is a non-resident International Company (IC). An IC registered under the International Companies Act 1987 operates as a tax-exempt non-resident enterprise, may maintain fully foreign-currency-denominated accounts, and may remit profits/dividends offshore without local withholding or exchange-control approval, provided no Samoan-source income is derived and the company remains in good standing with the Registrar of International Companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters, proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa through an authorized dealer within 180 days of the date of export; 100% of the export receipts in foreign currency must be surrendered to the dealer in exchange for WST within 30 days of receipt, unless CBS has granted a specific retention allowance or exemption. For a non-resident International Company, the surrender obligation is not triggered because the IC is outside the exchange-control 'specified person' classification and cannot conduct business with Samoan residents; all export-derived foreign currency from non-resident counterparties is held freely in designated offshore accounts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), acting as the foreign exchange administration under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977 (as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for domestic WST clearing and regulated payments; Medium only as a supplementary non-WST routing channel. Samoa does not currently maintain a licensed EMI category, and any entity holding WST deposits or issuing local-currency payment instruments is required to hold a banking licence under the Financial Institutions Act 2016. Offshore digital EMIs cannot be direct participants in Samoa Interchange Settlement (SIS) or CBS clearing infrastructure; they may be used solely for receiving and disbursing USD/EUR/GBP proceeds through a correspondent bank, provided the EMI does not solicit Samoan-resident clients and all sampled funds are transmitted to a licensed Samoan bank for domestic conversion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold is codified in Samoa's Income Tax Act 2012. Prudent compliance practice: maintain a contemporaneous Local File where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed WST 1,000,000, and a Master File where group-wide turnover exceeds WST 5,000,000; the MfR retains the right to require documentation at lower values where evasion indicators exist.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) modelled on BVI or Isle of Man. However, the 0% IC regime and treaty access require demonstrable substance: a SIFSA-licensed agent, a physical registered office in Samoa, board minutes adopted in Samoa, a Samoan bank account, and substantive offshore management control over the syndicated finance feed. The General Anti-Avoidance Rule in the Tax Administration Act 2012 permits the MfR to disregard any entity or transaction lacking economic substance and to re-characterize a bare shell as taxable in Samoa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Evasion penalty of up to 100% of the tax shortfall (minimum WST 5,000), additional fines of up to WST 50,000, and imprisonment for up to 5 years under the Tax Administration Act 2012; confiscation of evaded amounts plus monthly interest surcharges

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of false returns are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the shortfall; the Commissioner may issue travel bans preventing departure from Samoa until all outstanding tax liabilities are secured or satisfied.
Shell Company and Abuse of IC Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Forfeiture of the International Company tax exemption; retroactive taxation at 27% on all income derived from the date of offending activity; back-payment of the annual government fee with monthly penalties; and possible strike-off of the company registration under the International Companies Act 1987

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the IC is found to be a sham or façade; directors become personally liable for unpaid company taxes and creditor claims; restraint orders, travel bans, and disqualification from managing any Samoa-registered company apply.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines up to WST 500,000 and officer fines up to WST 100,000; forfeiture of the proceeds of crime; deregistration of the International Company; adverse regulatory findings recorded by SIFSA and the Central Bank of Samoa

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability with imprisonment up to 14 years, personal asset freezing orders, confiscation of interests, and statutory travel bans pending the conclusion of AML investigations.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Commissioner's transfer pricing adjustment under the Income Tax Act 2012, an accuracy penalty of 20% of the adjustment amount, plus monthly interest at 1% on the underpaid tax; repeat offenses escalate to the 100% evasion penalty

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or approve sham pricing arrangements are jointly and severally liable for the diverted profits and civil restitution; serious cases trigger non-resident director travel bans and contempt of court sanctions.
