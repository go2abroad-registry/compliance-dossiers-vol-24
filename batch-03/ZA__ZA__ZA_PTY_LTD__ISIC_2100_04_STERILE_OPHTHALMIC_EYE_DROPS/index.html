Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); electronic filing and registration portal: SARS eFiling (www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity or a local company with foreign ownership must first register as a taxpayer through SARS eFiling or a SARS branch using Form IT77 (Company Income Tax registration) accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of physical address, and banking details. SARS assigns an Income Tax Reference Number; if taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed the VAT registration threshold, the entity must separately submit a VAT registration application (Form VAT101) to obtain a Vendor Number. Appointing a SARS-representative is mandatory for non-resident entities without a fixed place of business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Proprietary Limited Company is a separate South African tax resident subject to normal tax on worldwide income, without a US-style check-the-box flow-through regime.

Available election forms

South Africa does not have a check-the-box election. Pty Ltd is automatically taxed at entity level at the corporate rate of 27%. Specific statutory elections exist in the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, including s 20B (trading stock valuation), s 24 (accrual/expenditure timing), s 24J (interest accrual), s 23H (prepayments), and s 25H anti-hybrid rules that recharacterise cross-border instruments to neutralise hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

R1,000,000 (total taxable supplies made in South Africa during any 12-month period); voluntary registration permitted from R50,000 (regulation-prescribed threshold, subject to SARS discretion).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for zero-rating under s 11(1)(a) VAT Act if the exporter supplies movable goods pursuant to a sale or consignment and the goods are exported from South Africa to a foreign country by the supplier or the purchaser at the time of supply. Services qualify as exported services under s 11(2)(k) if the supply is performed outside South Africa, or is supplied directly to a non-resident who is not in South Africa and the services are not connected to fixed property or consumables in South Africa. For medical and pharmaceutical products, an export permit from the South African Health Products Regulatory Authority (SAHPRA) may be required, but zero-rating itself is not conditional on SAHPRA registration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) lodged via SARS Customs eFiling / EDI
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing goods leaving South Africa
  • Commercial Invoice / Tax Invoice bearing zero-rating rationale
  • Evidence of payment receipt from purchaser (SWIFT or Letter of Credit settlement)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorised dealer in foreign exchange where applicable
  • Underlying supply agreement and packing list
  • SAHPRA export certificate or permit where the product is listed or requires notification

No mandatory real-time government pre-clearance or digital stamping regime exists in South Africa. Tax invoices must satisfy the prescriptive requirements of s 20(4) VAT Act and be issued within 21 days of the supply; SARS eFiling and e@syFile are used for VAT returns and payments, not for invoice-level clearance. Electronic invoices and records are accepted if integrity and audit-trail conditions are met.

SARS may deny the zero-rating if the exporter fails to provide sufficient evidence of physical export and receipt of proceeds; the VAT becomes payable at 15% with interest (s 40) and an understatement penalty of up to 100% of the shortfall under s 223 Tax Administration Act depending on conduct (no reasonable care, substantial understatement, or intentional tax evasion). Fraudulent export claims attract criminal prosecution under the Tax Administration Act and Customs and Excise Act, retrospective assessments limited by law but generally five years, or longer in cases of fraud, and potential suspension of the entity's vendor registration or customs facilitation status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current-account payments (imports, exports, dividends, interest, royalties, and services) without prior SARB approval, provided normal trade/corporate documentation and SARS records are maintained. Capital-account flows are subject to a capital flow management framework: inbound non-resident equity subscription into a ZA (Pty) Ltd is lawful at free-market rates once effected through, and recorded by, an Authorized Dealer; offshore foreign-currency accounts, outward portfolio investment, and loans to non-residents generally require FinSurv approval or an applicable AD delegation. Profit remittance is not exchange-control restricted once the inward investment record and foreign-liability reporting are complete; the operative remittance hurdles are taxation-based, principally the 20% dividends tax on non-resident shareholders (treaty relief available), dividends/interest final withholding, SARS tax clearance status, transfer pricing, and CFC rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds from sterile ophthalmic products must be received onshore through a South African Authorized Dealer within 30 days from the date of export; the AD may grant a longer period on documented commercial justification. One hundred percent of the invoice value must be repatriated into the domestic banking system; there is no fixed statutory ZAR conversion ratio, but any foreign currency must be deposited into an AD-controlled account (including an approved Foreign Currency Account) and made freely available for conversion to ZAR at market rates. Each consignment requires the applicable SARS export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading/air waybill, customs record, and supporting quality documentation for the ophthalmic product; over-invoicing, under-invoicing, and offshore retention beyond AD-approved arrangements are exchange-control offenses.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and Authorized Dealers (major commercial banks) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961; the South African Revenue Service (SARS) administers customs, export declaration validation, and tax-clearance prerequisites.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Verto) are not licensed as South African banks and cannot lawfully act as the primary receipt and clearing account for an SARB-regulated resident exporter; receiving export proceeds directly into an unlicensed offshore EMI is incompatible with the 30-day repatriation rule and the Authorized Dealer framework. EMIs are commercially viable only as ancillary payment-orchestration, multi-currency conversion, and MT103 origination layers where the settlement owner and reporting hub remains a local AD account. Any offshore account opened by the resident company requires FinSurv approval or explicit AD facilitation; otherwise, the account is treated as an unauthorized exchange-control exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the non-resident shareholder issues a SWIFT MT103 directly to the company's South African Authorized Dealer account with a purpose designation of Direct Investment in South Africa – subscription for ordinary shares in a resident (Pty) Ltd; the AD applies the SARB FinSurv capital-liabilities transaction code for direct investment in share capital. The shareholder must provide the AD with the share allotment resolution, shareholder agreement, company registration particulars, and SARS tax-clearance evidence. Funds are staged in a restricted Capital-Staging/Provisional account until allotment documentation is lodged, after which the funds are released to the operating account in ZAR at spot or held in an FCA with AD consent. Verification requires reconciliation among the SWIFT reference, shares register, Companies Act filings, the AD foreign-liability record, and the company's annual FinSurv reporting.Settlement for imported APIs, polymer tubing, blow-fill-seal consumables, and plant equipment is executed via: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the AD account against verified commercial invoices, packing lists, and transport documents; and (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit (UCP 600/eUCP) issued by the AD in favor of foreign suppliers, on sight or deferred-payment terms, with inspection, insurance, and pharma-quality certificates. For SADC/SACU sales, export collections may use documentary collections (URC 522) or letters of credit received through the AD's confirmation desk. All outbound payments must identify the underlying current-account trade purpose and must not be structured as advanced settlements absent a proforma invoice and supporting SARS customs data.Deliverable forward exchange contracts (ZAR deliverable forwards) with a licensed South African Authorized Dealer in USD/ZAR, EUR/ZAR, and CNY/ZAR, tenors matched to approved underlying export/import cashflows.Vanilla deliverable FX options (puts/calls on ZAR) for the same underliers, documented under ISDA/SACMA master agreements, non-speculative, and approved by the Authorized Dealer.Cross-currency swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-term plant financing and refinancing of the ophthalmic manufacturing facility, subject to FinSurv underlying-debt authorization and reporting.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only as an exception for currency crosses lacking an onshore deliverable ZAR market, such as receivables denominated in certain non-convertible Sub-Saharan African currencies, each NDF tied to a confirmed trade or loan exposure and pre-approved by the Authorized Dealer; uncovered speculative NDF positions are prohibited.
