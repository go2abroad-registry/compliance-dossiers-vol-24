Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), administered through the SARS eFiling portal (www.sars.gov.za; portal.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and must appoint a public officer. The company then registers with SARS through eFiling to obtain its Income Tax Reference Number (tax reference number). If taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed ZAR 1,000,000 in any 12-month period, VAT registration is compulsory and must be completed within 21 days of the trigger; voluntary VAT registration is available once taxable supplies reach ZAR 50,000. Where employees are engaged, PAYE, UIF and SDL registrations are mandatory. A customs code is required for importing construction equipment, and a Tax Compliance Status (TCS) pin is generally required for government and utility procurement contracts.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a South African incorporated Pty Ltd is a resident company taxed at 27% corporate income tax on its worldwide income; no pass-through treatment applies. A non-resident company carrying on business through a local permanent establishment or 'external company' is taxed on South African-source income at the same headline rate. — A foreign-owned (Pty) Ltd must first be incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and must appoint a public officer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a South African incorporated Pty Ltd is a resident company taxed at 27% corporate income tax on its worldwide income; no pass-through treatment applies. A non-resident company carrying on business through a local permanent establishment or 'external company' is taxed on South African-source income at the same headline rate.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a 'check-the-box' classification election; a Pty Ltd is mandatorily taxed as an entity. Available statutory elections include: (i) micro-business turnover tax under the Sixth Schedule to the Income Tax Act for eligible businesses with annual turnover not exceeding ZAR 1,000,000; (ii) voluntary VAT registration once taxable supplies reach ZAR 50,000; (iii) roll-over relief elections under sections 42-44 (asset-for-share, share-for-share, amalgamation) and section 45 (intragroup transactions), subject to section 41 requirements; and (iv) certain employee share-incentive elections. Hybrid mismatch rules under sections 8E, 8EA and 23N-23R apply compulsorily as anti-avoidance overrides and cannot be disapplied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZAR 1,000,000 (compulsory registration where taxable supplies in any 12-consecutive-month period exceed, or are reasonably expected to exceed, ZAR 1,000,000); ZAR 50,000 (voluntary registration threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where movable goods are supplied by a registered VAT vendor and exported under customs control within 90 days before or after the time of supply, provided the prescribed export documentation is retained. Export of services qualifies zero-rated under section 11(2)(l) where the service is rendered to a non-resident who is absent from South Africa at the time of supply and the service is not connected with land, minerals, or on-site construction work in South Africa and is not otherwise consumed in South Africa. Offshore engineering, design, and project-management services for a foreign buyer may therefore qualify; onshore desalination plant civil, mechanical, and electrical construction works are standard-rated.

  • SAD500 customs export declaration (customs clearance certificate)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Full VAT tax invoice issued by the vendor
  • Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate or authorised-dealer foreign-exchange receipt evidencing payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Export contract, purchase order, and inspection certificate
  • SARS eFiling VAT201 return with supporting export schedule

South Africa currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing API regime. VAT vendors must issue and retain full or abridged tax invoices under section 20 of the VAT Act, produce them on SARS request, and file VAT returns electronically via SARS eFiling. SARS has consulted on an e-invoicing clearinghouse model, but no mandatory electronic clearance obligation is yet in force.

SARS may disallow the zero-rating where the export evidence bundle is incomplete or where the goods are not timeously exported, and may retrospectively claw back the output VAT, the refunded amount, and any VAT refund interest. Under the Tax Administration Act, an understatement penalty of between 10% and 150% of the shortfall may be imposed depending on conduct, with interest at the prescribed rate. Fraudulent export declarations attract criminal prosecution, monetary penalties, and imprisonment; non-repatriation of export proceeds within the 180-day exchange-control deadline may also trigger separate financial penalties and permit SARS to reopen assessments without time limitation where fraud or intentional evasion is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is a lightly managed and externally convertible currency within a statutory exchange-control framework. Current-account transactions, including trade, services, and normal profit remittances, are freely permitted and processed through authorised dealers without SARB pre-approval, provided that the underlying transaction is documented and within the applicable prudential limits. Capital-account transactions, including equity capital injections from non-residents and foreign currency borrowings, may be processed through a domestic authorised dealer without prior SARB approval, but the funds must be reported on the appropriate inward transfer application and allocated to the corporate's capital or loan register. Profit remittances and dividends by a South African resident company to a non-resident shareholder are lawful when: (i) the dividends are declared out of distributable reserves; (ii) the company is in good standing under the Companies Act, 2008; (iii) no unresolved tax obligations exist; and (iv) a valid tax clearance certificate is produced when the remittance exceeds the authorised-dealer threshold. FinSurv approval remains required for certain capital disposals, intra-group re-domiciliations, and offshore head-office service charges above the authorised-dealer limit. Dividend remittances remain subject to the domestic normal corporate income tax net and dividends withholding tax under South African tax law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the South African Exchange Control Regulations, all export proceeds must accrue in South Africa within 180 days from the date of shipment and must be sold to an authorised dealer in ZAR unless a prior FinSurv approval for a foreign-currency retention account or a specific exemption has been obtained. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% of the net export receipt, with only bank charges, documented foreign distribution expenses, and transit or shipping costs deductible before conversion. For a seawater desalination EPC company, construction-phase progress payments invoiced in USD to a foreign project owner must be routed through the domestic banking channel and liquidated into ZAR on receipt, with the underlying export invoice, construction certification, bill of lading or progress report, and SARB reporting or electronic Form A records retained for audit.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), in conjunction with South African authorised dealers under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act No. 9 of 1933) and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium viability for purely ancillary treasury functions, but not a permissible primary clearing rail for ZAR-denominated corporate liquidity. South Africa does not currently operate a full electronic-money-institution licensing regime comparable to the EU or UK; non-bank fintech platforms may obtain a payment service provider authorisation under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 but they cannot accept deposits or hold a ZAR float for the account of the company without a banking partner. Offshore digital EMIs not registered as subsidiaries of a South African authorised dealer may provide multi-currency accounts for minor incidental invoices, but any inflow into the ZAR banking system will still be routed through a South African clearing bank, triggering the full FICA/KYC/CDD onboarding trail. The entity should therefore treat offshore EMIs as supplementary FX concentration utilities only and keep statutory capital, escrow, customs, and supplier settlement ledgers inside tier-1 domestic banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inward remittances must be routed through an authorised dealer in South Africa. The parent or non-resident shareholder should remit the equity contribution by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the South African reflecting bank of the project entity, including the SARB inward payment purpose code for direct foreign investment in equity capital and a clear account description stating 'Foreign direct investment – share capital for ZA_PTY_LTD'. The company must furnish the authorised dealer with a copy of the subscribing shareholders' resolution and the new share issue register within 30 days. The bank will prepare the inward transfer reporting on behalf of the company. Where the contribution is structured as a shareholder loan rather than equity, the loan agreement must contain South African governing-law criteria, the amount must be reported in the foreign-liabilities registration data, and any related interest payments will be subject to withholding tax. Funds may be credited to a capital escrow account and drawn against milestone certificates, preserving traceability and supporting tax substance documentation.For construction and project procurement in the desalination EPC, commercial settlements should be effected by: (i) corporate-to-corporate SWIFT MT103 for down payments and balance-of-plant invoices, referencing the type of goods, service description, and underlying contract; (ii) documentary collection at sight or a commercial letter of credit opened by the project owner's bank, conforming with UCP600, where supply-chain security is required; and (iii) bank payment undertakings or domestic electronic clearing for South African subcontractor payments. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment ending on or after 31 March 2023 (reduced from 28% under s5 Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, as amended)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax withheld by the resident (Pty) Ltd as withholding agent under s64E ITA on any dividend declared on or after 1 April 2012. The liability falls on the shareholder, but the company must withhold and remit to SARS by the end of the month following the month of declaration, failing which the company and its directors bear collection liability under s64G and s191 TAA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: ZAR 100 million aggregate cross-border connected-person transactions for the year of assessment triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File preparation and the SARS TP disclosure in the ITR14. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds ZAR 1 billion and the local entity's cross-border connected transactions exceed ZAR 100 million. Country-by-Country reporting obligations follow SARS thresholds for large multinational groups. Documentation must be retained for five years and delivered to SARS within 90 days of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code comparable to offshore financial centres. Substance is nevertheless enforced through: the Place of Effective Management (POEM) test for corporate residence (s1 ITA); Controlled Foreign Company attribution rules (s9D ITA); the general anti-avoidance rule (s103(2) ITA); and transfer pricing substance documentation under s31 ITA. The desalination EPC (Pty) Ltd must demonstrate substantive local commercial presence: locally based project managers, engineering supervision, tendering and contract execution, real office and plant presence, payroll and accounting records within South Africa, and board decisions taken in South Africa. Merely holding contracts and invoicing via a mail-drop entity will expose the structure to re-characterisation as a sham conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty under s223 TAA of 125% of the understated tax for intentional evasion; interest at the SARS prescribed rate; criminal conviction under s235 TAA carrying a fine of up to ZAR 100,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. A further administrative penalty of up to ZAR 16,500 per month of non-submission applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or procure a false return are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, civil recovery proceedings by SARS under s159 TAA, and piercing of the corporate veil where the company is used as a mere facade. SARS may issue a notice rendering directors jointly and severally liable for unremitted company tax debts in insolvency context.
Shell Company / Sham Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: SARS may apply s103(2) GAAR to disregard the arrangement, deny deductions, and reattribute profits to the local entity; POEM analysis may render the company non-resident, triggering an exit charge under s9H on deemed disposal of assets (21.6% effective CGT), while s64O may re-characterise a collateral loan as a deemed dividend attracting 20% dividends tax. Understatement penalties of up to 125% apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be pursued for the company's unpaid tax where assets have been stripped for personal benefit; travel bans and senior executive disqualification may be imposed; fraudulent alienation of funds is actionable through SARS civil collection and criminal proceeds-of-crime litigation.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Secondary Adjustment

Statutory sanction: SARS makes a primary adjustment increasing taxable income and a secondary adjustment under s31(2) imputing a deemed interest-bearing loan at the SARS prescribed rate where funds remain unpaid; understatement penalties range from 10% (standard) to 25% (substantial understatement), 50% (unreasonable position), 75% (gross negligence), and 100%-125% for intentional or reckless behaviour; late payment interest accrues monthly.

Director personal liability: A director who personally directed or benefited from the non-arm's-length over-invoicing may be joined as a co-defendant and held personally liable for the understated tax and penalties; criminal fraud or theft charges may apply where project funds are diverted offshore.
Dividend, Interest and Royalty Withholding Tax Defaults

Statutory sanction: The company remains liable for the unremitted withholding tax, plus interest and the s223 understatement penalty: deliberate failure attracts up to 125%; failure to submit the required withholding schedules adds administrative penalties up to ZAR 16,500 per month. SARS treats the tax as collectable from the company regardless of shareholder default.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to withhold and pay dividends tax or who approve an unlawful reduced-rate distribution risk personal collection proceedings where the company cannot settle; SARS may pierce the veil and obtain civil judgments against directors under s159 TAA, with criminal exposure for fraudulent preferences in liquidation.
AML and Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanctions under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001 may reach up to ZAR 10 million per contravention; senior management/board members face criminal fines of up to ZAR 100 million and imprisonment for up to 15 years for typology offences; the National Director of Public Prosecutions may seize proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors and prescribed officers bear personal criminal liability where the company committed contraventions with their consent or through their negligence; disqualification from holding office and asset restraint orders may be imposed; individual travel bans are available pending investigation. For a Low-Risk_Standard project financing profile, the compliance burden remains material because the FATF-mandated beneficial ownership register applies to all South African companies.
Late Filing, Underestimated Provisional Tax and Persistent Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fixed administrative non-compliance penalties up to ZAR 16,500 per month of non-submission; SARS may issue a default assessment; interest accrues at the prescribed rate on all outstanding amounts; understatement penalty of 20% applies to the deferred tax on underestimated provisional payments; criminal conviction is possible where non-submission is accompanied by intent to delay or evade assessment.

Director personal liability: Where non-compliance continues and the company is liquidated without adequate provision for tax, SARS may recover the tax debt from directors jointly and severally under s159 TAA; directors may be summonsed and cross-examined in debt-collection proceedings, and deliberate disregard of tax obligations supports peremptory disqualification.
