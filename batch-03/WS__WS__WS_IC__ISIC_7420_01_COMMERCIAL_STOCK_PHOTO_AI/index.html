Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA) — Ministry for Revenue; Official web portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws/ (Tax Administration System online services)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must submit the prescribed Application for Tax Identification Number (TIN) to the Samoa Revenue Authority via the Tax Administration System, including the Certificate of Incorporation under the International Companies Act 1987, a certified copy of the Memorandum and Articles of Association, a completed Foreign Ownership Declaration, an original Certified Public Accountant (CPA) or legal practitioner certification of the entity's valid existence, a local registered address, and proof of the authorized agent's identity; the application is reviewed by the TIN Unit and a 9-digit TIN is issued upon approval, with a Taxpayer Identification Card; there is no requirement for local economic substance for the mere issuance of a TIN, but the TIN must be filed for VAGST and customs purposes if the entity conducts any taxable activity in Samoa.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign entity must submit the prescribed Application for Tax Identification Number (TIN) to the Samoa Revenue Authority via the Tax Administration System, including the Certificate of Incorporation under the International Companies Act 1987, a certified copy of the Memorandum and Articles of Association, a completed Foreign Ownership Declaration, an original Certified Public Accountant (CPA) or legal practitioner certification of the entity's valid existence, a local registered address, and proof of the authorized agent's identity; the application is reviewed by the TIN Unit and a 9-digit TIN is issued upon approval, with a Taxpayer Identification Card; there is no requirement for local economic substance for the mere issuance of a TIN, but the TIN must be filed for VAGST and customs purposes if the entity conducts any taxable activity in Samoa.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Samoa domestic law; an International Company is statutorily classified as a non-resident exempt entity under the International Companies Act 1987, and may elect to be treated as an ordinary resident company for Samoa income tax purposes only if it obtains a formal tax residence certificate from the SRA and files a voluntary election on Form IT-ELEC-01, subject to the Minister for Revenue's discretion; hybrid mismatch elections are not permitted in Samoa, and any entity attempting to exploit inconsistent treatment between jurisdictions may be subject to general anti-avoidance rules under the Income Tax Act 2012, Schedule 2 (GAAR provisions).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 (Samoa Tālā) annual taxable turnover threshold; businesses supplying goods or services in Samoa must register for VAGST within 14 days after the end of the month in which the threshold is reached or exceeded; overseas suppliers of digital services or electronic goods to Samoan consumers are subject to a separate offshore supplier registration regime effective from 1 January 2022, with an optional simplified registration for non-resident electronic marketplace operators.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

In accordance with the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2003 (Section 22 and VAGST Regulations), a registered person may zero-rate supplies of goods that are physically exported from Samoa within 28 days of the time of supply, provided that the supplier retains evidence of export; zero-rating for services applies only to services supplied to a non-resident recipient for use entirely outside Samoa, excluding services relating to land or goods situated in Samoa; digital stock photography and generative visual asset licensing services qualify for zero-rating where the non-resident customer acquires digital download rights and the payment is received into a Samoa-licensed bank account; the zero-rating must be claimed in the VAGST return by reference to the export vend code.

  • Samoa Customs Export Declaration (Customs Form ED-1) stamped by the Samoa Customs Division
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing the physical departure of goods (for goods); for digital services, a contractual service schedule and server-upload timestamp log
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt evidencing foreign currency payment from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice or tax invoice marked 'Zero-rated export' with reference to VAGST Act provisions
  • Proof of non-resident status of the purchaser (e.g., utility bill, registry extract, or passport/ID)
  • Certificate of Digital Delivery / download log from the stock photo platform
  • A signed declaration from the exporter attesting to the absence of local consumption in Samoa
  • Insurance certificate and packing list when applicable to physical goods

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-connected e-invoicing clearance regime is currently operative in Samoa; however, SRA requires electronic filing of VAGST returns and payment via the Tax Administration System, and businesses must issue sequential, serially numbered tax invoices (VAGST invoice) containing the supplier's name, TIN, invoice date, description, quantity, price, VAGST amount, and serial number; invoice records must be retained for a minimum of 7 years and made available on SRA request; the SRA has announced plans to pilot an electronic tax invoice registry for large taxpayers, but no binding secondary legislation has been enacted as of the knowledge cutoff.

The SRA may conduct post-export verification audits within 5 years of the zero-rating claim; if the export documentation is found to be false, missing, or fabricated, the entire zero-rated output tax must be clawed back and assessed as 15% VAGST, plus overdue late payment penalties of 2% per month on the unpaid tax and a late filing penalty of WST 1,000 for each defective return; in addition, the VAGST Act 2003 Section 49 imposes a criminal penalty of a fine not exceeding WST 100,000 or up to 5 years imprisonment for willful evasion, and Customs Act 2014 applies to customs documentation fraud with forfeiture of goods and a fine of up to WST 200,000; further, a fraudulent zero-rating claim under common law may constitute deception, and the entity can be deregistered from VAGST indefinitely.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is not freely convertible on an open-market basis; the CBS operates a managed float against a trade-weighted basket and requires all WST conversion to flow through authorized dealers. Current-account transactions (cloud hosting, SaaS subscriptions, contributor royalties, professional fees) are remittable within authorized-dealer delegated limits; amounts above the applicable threshold require a CBS exchange-control application. The capital account is restricted: a non-resident carrying on business inside Samoa requires certification under the Foreign Investment Act 2020, but a purely offshore International Company that markets digital stock-photo and generative visual licenses exclusively to non-residents maintains no domestic business footprint and therefore sits outside the domestic investment-licensing and capital-transfer perimeter. Profit distributions and shareholder-loan repayments to foreign owners require a foreign-exchange application supported by management/audited accounts and confirmation that the funds were earned offshore; approval is routinely granted where monies are held in an external USD account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters, CBS practice requires repatriation of export proceeds within three months of export/settlement and surrender of eligible proceeds into WST through an authorized dealer unless a foreign-currency retention facility is approved. The Samoa International Company is non-resident and its stock-photo and AI-visual licensing income is an offshore service export; the statutory surrender and conversion obligation therefore does not attach, and USD may remain offshore or in an external USD account at an authorized dealer. Treasury policy must nevertheless prohibit crediting revenue into a domestic WST-denominated account; any accidental WST credit must be reversed via an exchange-control restoration application before offshore disbursement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Apia - the monetary authority established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; the CBS administers the Exchange Control Act 1976, the Exchange Control Regulations 1977 and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2018 (as amended), licenses all commercial banks, and delegates defined exchange-control functions to authorized dealers under CBS-published delegated limits. The Minister of Finance retains residual exchange-control power; the Registrar of International Companies (within the Samoa International Finance Authority / Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour) administers the International Company registry.

Feasibility rating: Conditional Medium-Low. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Local File or Master File threshold is triggered for a Samoa International Company because it is outside the Samoan income tax net. For a hypothetical Samoa-resident taxpayer under the Income Tax Act 2015, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required for controlled transactions exceeding SAT 1,000,000 per annum. For the IC structure, properly documented intercompany contracts for stock photo licensing fees are prudent but not a tax-filing obligation.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 applies to International Companies carrying on relevant activities. For a stock photography and generative AI visual assets business, the material activity will be classified as an IP business if the IC holds copyrights, licensing income, or royalty-generating digital assets. That classification requires demonstrable economic substance in Samoa: local board meetings with real decision-making, a physical registered office or premises, qualified in-country employees commensurate with the activity, and adequate annual operating expenditure. An IC that operates as a passive asset-holding vehicle without engaging in active trade is outside the relevant activity net but must still file the annual Economic Substance notification with the Registrar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Foreign Anti-Abuse Recharacterisation

Statutory sanction: Foreign revenue authorities may recharacterise the IC's profits as taxable in the place of effective management, apply controlled foreign corporation rules, and impose penalties of up to 100% of the understated tax with interest. In Samoa, knowingly making a false declaration to the Registrar is an offence under the International Companies Act 1987 attracting fines up to SAT 100,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign substance declarations without verifying real activity are personally liable for civil restitution of diverted profits, may be subject to foreign tax collection orders, travel bans, and imprisonment for false declaration.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019, failure to satisfy the economic substance test attracts a fixed penalty of SAT 50,000 plus a daily surcharge of SAT 1,000. Persistent non-compliance leads to mandatory strike-off of the IC and disclosure to foreign competent authorities under the exchange of information framework.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for civil penalties and restoration costs; court-imposed travel bans can be issued during the regulatory investigation, and wilful non-compliance is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years.
AML Non-Compliance and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 exposes the IC to financial penalties up to SAT 500,000 and account freezes by the Samoan Financial Intelligence Unit; the registered agent and licensed bank also face administrative sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for failing to file suspicious transaction reports; imprisonment up to 10 years, mandatory restitution of laundered proceeds, and travel restrictions are available sanctions.
Director Misrepresentation and False Registry Filing

Statutory sanction: False filing under the International Companies Act 1987 is a criminal offence carrying fines of up to SAT 20,000 per false instrument, disqualification from acting as a director for up to 10 years, and mandatory compensation to parties harmed by the misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors cannot hide behind limited liability; courts routinely pierce the corporate veil, impose personal civil restitution, issue travel bans, and order imprisonment for wilful false certification enabling offshore tax abuse.
