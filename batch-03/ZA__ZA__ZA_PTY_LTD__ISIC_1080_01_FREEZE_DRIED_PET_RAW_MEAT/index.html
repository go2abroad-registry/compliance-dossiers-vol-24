Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling portal: https://efiling.sars.gov.za; corporate registration pre-clearance via Companies and Intellectual Properties Commission (CIPC) with SARS-permitted entity registration and bank verification via SARS eFiling or branch appointment

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Proprietary Limited company with CIPC to obtain a company registration number and Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI). 2) Apply to SARS for a corporate Income Tax reference number via eFiling or branch, submitting the CIPC registration certificate, MOI, and proof of appointed SARS tax representative if non-resident. 3) Register for VAT on SARS eFiling (VAT101) after determining taxable-supply status; mandatory once taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months exceed ZAR 1,000,000 or are expected to exceed that threshold in the next 12 months; voluntary registration is available from ZAR 50,000. 4) Register as an employer for PAYE, UIF, and SDL (EMP101) if persons are employed. 5) Register with SARS Customs (Customs & Excise Act) under the customs code for export declaration (SAD500) purposes and obtain a customs client code. 6) Foreign entities may obtain a non-resident tax reference number directly from SARS, but operational import/export requires a local fiscal representative and compliance with the SARS Customs registration for the bonded/manufacturing premises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a South African tax-resident (Pty) Ltd is subject to corporate income tax at 27% on its worldwide income (Taxation Laws Amendment Act 2022), with no pass-through to shareholders; dividends distributed are subject to 20% Dividends Withholding Tax unless treaty-reduced; foreign branch income is taxed with unilateral foreign tax credit relief, and the entity is subject to the continental territorial scope rules for controlled foreign company (CFC) attributions.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Statutory elections include: (i) Headquarter Company election under s9I of the Income Tax Act for centralised holding/information-technology services with effective 0% tax on foreign dividends, interest, and royalties; (ii) Hybrid equity instrument election and hybrid debt instrument election under s8E/s8F, requiring contemporaneous classification with anti-arbitrage (hybrid mismatch) adjustments under the 2020-2022 TAAR rules; (iii) VAT group registration election under s50A of the VAT Act; (iv) Micro Business Turnover Tax election (Turnover Tax Act) for entities with annual turnover no more than R1,000,000; (v) Functional currency election under s24I(10) for audits in a foreign functional currency; (vi) Section 11D R&D tax allowance election; (vii) 12BA / 12C accelerated capital depreciation election (including energy-efficiency and industrial expansion). Elections are filed as part of the annual corporate income tax return (ITR14) or via prescribed SARS notification forms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration when taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months exceed R1,000,000 or are reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in the next 12 months; voluntary registration from R50,000; R20,000 threshold for public authorities/associations; group companies may apply for a s50A VAT group with aggregated turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act, a supply of movable goods qualifies for the 0% VAT rate when the supplier delivers the goods to a recipient outside South Africa or delivers them to a carriage contractor for export, provided the goods are exported within 90 days of the date of supply (or a longer period permitted by SARS) and the supplier retains documentary proof of physical export. Vacuum freeze-dried pet raw meat (ISIC 1080) qualifies as movable goods; likewise, ancillary offshore services (section 11(2)(k)) that are physically rendered outside South Africa or in relation to goods being transported to non-residents may be zero-rated. Each zero-rated export supply must be separately proven; the burden of proof rests on the vendor.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) submitted electronically to SARS Customs and proof of exit (customs turnout or delivery certificate)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing loading of the freeze-dried pet meat consignment and its consignment to the foreign purchaser
  • Signed commercial invoice and packing list; for raw/animal-product goods, the veterinary health certificate and export permit issued by the Department of Agriculture, Land Reform and Rural Development (DALRRD)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank payment-advice) from the authorised dealer confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Certificate of origin, cold-chain temperature records and the exporter-of-record declaration under the Customs and Excise Act, if required
  • Contract with overseas buyer and insurance documents demonstrating the risk/beneficial ownership passed outside the Republic

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime is in force. SARS mandates electronic filing (eFiling or a SARS API-integrated software) for VAT201 returns and requires full tax invoices within 21 days of the date of supply; SARS is migrating to a PEPPOL-enabled e-invoicing architecture and, from 2025, requires large vendors to transmit transaction-level data through an authorised e-Filing / API service, but a hard, per-invoice real-time clearance mandate remains prospective. A valid ‘tax invoice’ must include supplier name, VAT number, invoice number, invoice date, description of goods/services, quantity, consideration, VAT amount, and, for exports, the customs export reference.

SARS may deny zero-rating if export proof is not retained, and will raise an additional VAT assessment at 15% on the original consideration plus prescribed interest (currently 10.5% per annum, assessed daily from the due date) and impose understatement penalties under the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, escalating from 10% (substantial understatement), 25% (failure to take reasonable care), 50% (no reasonable grounds), 75% (intentional) to 150% (intentional with intent to evade tax); criminal prosecution under s233-235 of the Tax Administration Act and VAT Act s58/59 may result in fines up to R2,000,000 and up to 5 years' imprisonment for fraudulent export documentation. SARS, in conjunction with the South African Reserve Bank (SARB) and the Financial Intelligence Centre, prioritises the audit of export-based VAT refund fraud; any unrepatriated foreign currency after 180 days is a contravention of the Exchange Control Regulations, rendering the exporter subject to administrative penalties and, in rem, forfeiture of the proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is classified as partially convertible. Current account transactions (trade, services, dividends, royalties) are free of exchange control approval, subject to documentary compliance through an Authorised Dealer. Profit remittance requires a SARS tax clearance pin, an accountants' certificate, and Form A1 payment declaration. Capital account transactions require FinSurv approval or use of the prescribed limits. Non-resident equity investments can be repatriated if the capital was introduced through formal channels and recorded in the company's share register and SARB records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export payment must be received within 180 days of shipment date unless an extension is granted by FinSurv. All export proceeds must be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer within 30 days of receipt, and converted into rand at the prevailing market rate unless an exemption is granted. Form EXP1 must be filed for each export consignment, and proof of receipt must be submitted to the Authorised Dealer on the foreign exchange liability expiry date.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for full banking or ZAR settlement capabilities. Offshore digital banks and electronic money institutions (EMIs) do not hold South African banking licences and cannot provide ZAR-denominated deposit accounts, access the South African clearing system, or accept export proceeds under exchange control. They may operate only as payment facilitators registered under the National Payment System Act, but a fully compliant treasury requires a domestic tier-1 bank account as the primary settlement rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds ZAR 100 million in a year of assessment; Master File: mandatory at the ZAR 100 million aggregate threshold; Country-by-Country Report: ZAR 10 billion consolidated group revenue (effective for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 January 2023; prior thresholds were ZAR 5 million for the Local File and ZAR 50 million for the Master File)

Economic substance scope: South Africa has no Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations. Substance is enforced through: (i) the 'place of effective management' corporate residency test; (ii) CFC attribution rules under s.9D requiring a genuine Foreign Business Establishment; (iii) the Principal Purpose Test in MLI-modified DTAs (treaty anti-abuse); (iv) the general anti-avoidance rule (s.103(2) ITA), which voids transactions lacking a bona fide commercial purpose; and (v) the impermissible tax avoidance understatement penalty (75% of the shortfall). A ZA Pty Ltd is expected to have physical premises, employees, board decisions, an operational bank account and production capacity in South Africa; a bare invoice-holding shell will fail the residency and substance tests and lose treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 100% of the tax shortfall for intentional evasion plus 10% per month late-payment penalty (capped at 12 months) and interest; criminal conviction under TAA s.234: fine and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; SARS may also impose a s.210 fixed administrative penalty and seek a preservation order over assets

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt (TAA s.159); criminal courts may impose imprisonment on individual directors; SARS can obtain travel bans and exit-control orders (TAA s.177) and asset restraint orders; civil restitution and disqualification from directing future companies
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: SARS applies s.103(2) GAAR to disregard the transaction and disallow deductions; s.31 transfer-pricing adjustments recharacterize the proceeds; the company may be deemed non-resident if its place of effective management is abroad, losing access to South African treaty benefits; CIPC may strike off or deregister the company for non-operation; directors may face civil claims for reckless trading (Companies Act 71 of 2008, s.22 and s.77)

Director personal liability: Corporate veil pierced where the company is a façade: directors are personally liable for the company's debts, including SARS claims; a court may order liquidation or judicial management; directors face confiscation and disqualification; personal travel restrictions imposed where tax debts exceed thresholds and departure risk exists
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment of taxable income to arm's length under s.31(2); secondary adjustment deeming the excess funds as a dividend subject to 20% dividends tax; understatement penalty of 50% (no reasonable grounds) to 75% (impermissible avoidance); interest runs from the effective date; failure to file mandatory TP documentation attracts an additional fixed penalty of up to ZAR 1,000 per month and potential criminal sanctions

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's-length pricing knowingly may be held liable for the adjusted tax; SARS may pierce the veil on a fraud basis; bankruptcy-type proceedings may follow; travel bans and asset freeze orders can be issued during the audit
Dividends Tax / Cross-Border WHT Default

Statutory sanction: The company remains liable for the unwithheld dividends tax and interest (s.64E ITA); SARS may recover the 15% royalty/interest WHT from the payer as well; understatement penalties of 25%–100%; late-payment penalty of 10% per month; anti-avoidance recharacterization as a dividend in the case of interest-free loans to connected persons (s.31 secondary adjustment)

Director personal liability: Directors as 'responsible persons' are personally liable for unremitted withholding taxes under TAA s.159; the 'piercing of the veil' permits creditors (SARS) to recover from shareholders who stripped the company of assets; criminal prosecution for failure to withhold with intent to defraud; travel bans under s.177 TAA
AML / FICA and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FICA penalties: fines up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment of up to 15 years for non-compliance and money-laundering conduct; CIPC administrative penalties and potential deregistration; the tax assessment is unaffected, but undeclared cash sales trigger VAT and corporate income tax under-declarations with penalties of up to 100% and interest

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly launder proceeds or conceal beneficial ownership face criminal imprisonment (up to 15 years per FICA/POCA provisions), asset confiscation, and travel restrictions; SARS may rely on the AML records to pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for the full tax debt
