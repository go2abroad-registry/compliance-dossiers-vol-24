Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), through its electronic filing portal (SARS eFiling at www.sarsefiling.co.za)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must file an application for income tax registration via a SARS-registered tax practitioner or directly on SARS eFiling, submitting the company registration documents (Form CoR 14.3), certified articles of incorporation and memorandum of association, identity documents of directors, and proof of a South African physical business address and representative office. Once the company is registered as a South African resident taxpayer, a SARS tax reference number is issued; separate VAT registration can be applied for via eFiling (Form VAT101) once the entity has a valid tax reference number. Non-resident directors may register through the official foreign applicant channel with additional address and nationality attestation; a SARS branch code and tax group are assigned upon finalisation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must file an application for income tax registration via a SARS-registered tax practitioner or directly on SARS eFiling, submitting the company registration documents (Form CoR 14.3), certified articles of incorporation and memorandum of association, identity documents of directors, and proof of a South African physical business address and representative office. Once the company is registered as a South African resident taxpayer, a SARS tax reference number is issued; separate VAT registration can be applied for via eFiling (Form VAT101) once the entity has a valid tax reference number. Non-resident directors may register through the official foreign applicant channel with additional address and nationality attestation; a SARS branch code and tax group are assigned upon finalisation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or general look-through election exists. A private company (Pty) Ltd is automatically classified as a South African tax resident if incorporated in South Africa or effectively managed in South Africa, hence taxable on worldwide income. Certain anti-hybrid rules under the Income Tax Act (Section 8F/8FA and Section 12D) apply to discriminate debt and equity; an election for thin capitalisation and transfer pricing adjustments must be disclosed. A controlled foreign company (CFC) election is only relevant for offshore subsidiaries, not for the domestic Pty Ltd itself.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of movable goods if the supplier has properly declared the export under the Customs and Excise Act, the goods are exported within 90 days of the time of supply and are accompanied by a valid export declaration and proof of their removal from South Africa. Offshore services supplied to non-residents qualify for zero-rating if the services are used entirely outside South Africa and the recipient is a non-resident, except for services directly in connection with movables situated in South Africa or services consumed in South Africa. For all zero-rated exports, the supplier must retain documentary proof that the goods have been exported or services delivered overseas.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

South Africa does not have a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. SARS eFiling requires VAT returns to be submitted electronically, and all tax invoices, credit notes, and supplier documents must be issued in a SARS-prescribed electronic or paper format containing the full invoice particulars. A taxpayer with annual taxable supplies over ZAR 10 million or submitting via eFiling process and electronic interchange is required to use SARS-approved electronic communication channels; however, no continuous API-based clearance is applicable. VAT e-invoices must be retained for five years and be readily accessible to SARS, with specific requirements for a unique invoice number, VAT number of the supplier, and value of tax charged.

SARS may disallow the zero-rating and raise an additional VAT assessment, plus charge understatement penalties of up to 200% of the tax shortfall for intentional disregard or fraud. Taxpayers are subject to a 10% additional penalty for failure to substantiate export documentation, and a 15% annual compound interest applied to the assessed VAT debt. Where a VAT refund has been paid and later found fraudulent, SARS will issue a deemed assessment to recover the refund and may simultaneously launch a criminal investigation; custodial sentences of up to five years can apply for fraudulent zero-rating claims. Deliberate documentation falsification is treated as a criminal offence with the same penalty framework as smuggling under the Customs and Excise Act, including asset forfeiture.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current international transactions, South Africa having accepted IMF Article VIII obligations in 1973. Dividends, interest, royalties and trade settlements may be remitted to non-residents after compliance with the Companies Act 71 of 2008 solvency-and-liquidity test, deduction of dividends tax at 20% where applicable (treaty relief may reduce this), and the enterprise's exchange-control record with its Authorized Dealer. The capital account remains controlled: inward non-resident equity must be routed through an Authorized Dealer and recorded on the SARB balance-of-payments (BOP) system to preserve future disinvestment and dividend repatriation rights. There are no ZAR convertibility restrictions on bona fide defense trade, but all defense-sector payments are subject to NCACC permitting and enhanced bank due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Regulation 7(1) of the Exchange Control Regulations, exporters must ensure payment for exported goods is received within 180 days of shipment (or such longer period as FinSurv approves for government-to-government defense contracts). All foreign currency export proceeds must be surrendered in full to an Authorized Dealer within 30 days of receipt and converted to ZAR at the ruling market rate; there is no permissible percentage retention unless a specific foreign-currency account is approved. For ISIC 3040 military-tactical exports, the NCACC permit and End-User Certificate must be presented before proceeds are banked, and any deferred payment structure requires FinSurv extension approval.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), administered through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 (as amended); the National Treasury retains policy oversight, and the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) supervises AML/CTF compliance under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low as a sole banking solution; Medium only as a complementary cross-border FX collection conduit. Legal Constraints: South Africa does not operate a standalone EMI licence comparable to the UK FCA framework. The National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 and SARB's National Payment System Department require authorization for payment services; an offshore digital EMI cannot open a domestic ZAR operational account, act as an Authorized Dealer, or issue a SARB-recognized inward-investment record, so all ZAR clearing and defense-trade settlement must settle through a licensed South African bank. FICA registration, sanctions screening and enhanced due diligence obligations cannot be satisfied by a fully-remote EMI for a high-risk ISIC 3040 entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder transmits funds by SWIFT MT103 from its bank to the South African Authorized Dealer, with remittance instructions identifying the South African (Pty) Ltd, the purpose as equity capital contribution or inward direct investment, and the applicable SARB BOP purpose code for inward FDI into an unlisted resident company. The receiving bank records the inflow on the eBOP system, designates the funds as Non-Resident, credits the Capital Escrow Account, and issues an inward-investment acknowledgment; the share allotment is then recorded in the company's securities register and reported to the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Release of escrowed capital into the operational account is conditional on completion of FICA source-of-funds verification and the NCACC compliance screening.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct government and diplomatic wires, each supported by the NCACC permit, commercial invoice, End-User Certificate and customs SAD 500 export declaration; (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700 series) opened by the buyer's central bank or ministry and confirmed by the South African bank, the preferred instrument for high-value armored-vehicle contracts; (iii) Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) for long-standing buyer relationships; (iv) domestic ZAR settlement through the Automated Clearing Bureau and the SAMOS RTGS system. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is required if the international transactions with connected persons exceed ZAR 5 million in gross income, deductions, or expenditure; a Master File is required for multinational groups with total consolidated group revenue exceeding ZAR 10 billion and South African covered transactions exceeding ZAR 5 million.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); instead, the doctrine of economic substance is enforced through controlled foreign company rules in the Income Tax Act section 9D, transfer pricing provisions in section 31, and general anti-avoidance rules in section 103; a shell company lacking real economic substance and management functions would be attacked as an impermissible tax avoidance arrangement, with re-characterisation of transactions and denial of treaty benefits.
