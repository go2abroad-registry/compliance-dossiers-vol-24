Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Samoa International Company must at all times maintain a registered office in Samoa at the premises of its licensed registered agent (International Companies Act 1987 s. 9). The Company itself is not required to maintain physical business premises in Samoa; a purely virtual and remote operational structure for a global IP HoldCo is lawful, provided the registered agent's physical address serves as the statutory service-of-process and venue address. Virtual-office arrangements supplied by third parties outside the registered agent's premises do not satisfy the statutory requirement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity incorporated as an International Company under the International Companies Act 2016 must submit Form R1 (Taxpayer Registration) to the Samoa Ministry for Revenue, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, director/shareholder passport copies, and registered office details. Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when exported from Samoa within 28 days of supply and supporting documentary evidence is retained. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident, the direct benefit occurs outside Samoa, and the recipient is not a Samoa resident. Royalties or license fees from foreign patents and trademarks payable to an International Company are, by reason of the company's offshore tax-exempt status, outside the VAGST net; where VAGST-registered, such supplies will be zero-rated on proof of foreign receipt.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory electronic invoicing clearance or digital API stamping regime exists in Samoa. Registered suppliers must issue a standard tax invoice containing prescribed particulars (name, TIN, description, amount, VAGST charged) within 28 days of supply.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims attract a shortfall penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded, monthly interest of 1.5%, and restoration of any refund received. Criminal sanctions include fines up to WST 20,000 per offence and imprisonment up to 3 years under the Tax Administration Act 2012 and the Customs Act 2014.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is fully convertible for current-account transactions (royalties, trade, services, dividends) and partially convertible for capital-account transactions, which require case-by-case CBS approval for residents. The WS_IC, as a non-resident International Company, is statutorily outside the Exchange Control Act 1976 and may therefore receive, hold and disburse USD, EUR and AUD without prior CBS approval; no mandatory conversion into WST applies to its offshore royalty receipts. Practical hurdles: (i) the WST has no deliverable offshore market and is managed against a trade-weighted basket, so all material treasury flows should be denominated in USD; (ii) any conversion of foreign currency into WST for local agent fees must be executed through a licensed authorised dealer and is subject to CBS large-transaction reporting; (iii) foreign-parent profit repatriation through the IC is lawful without CBS pre-approval, but upstream distributions must be documented as dividends under the IC constitution to prevent recharacterisation as a taxable royalty by the destination jurisdiction; and (iv) the IC's tax-exempt status requires a contemporaneous economic-substance file to avoid regulatory classification as an abusive shell or brass-plate vehicle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and CBS implementing notices, resident exporters of goods and services (including royalty income treated as invisible exports) must: (1) collect export proceeds within 180 days of the date of exportation or invoicing; (2) route all receipts through an authorised dealer in Samoa; and (3) offer the foreign-currency proceeds for conversion into WST within 30 days of crediting, unless an approved foreign-currency account or a CBS deferral is in force. The WS_IC, being a non-resident International Company, is exempt from these surrender obligations under the International Companies Act 1987, provided that all royalty income remains in foreign-currency accounts and no WST conversion occurs for the benefit of a Samoan resident without passing through an authorised dealer. Resident sub-licensees owing royalties to the IC must nevertheless satisfy the 180-day collection and 30-day offer-for-sale rules in their own export documentation, and the IC should require contractual confirmation of this compliance from each resident counterparty.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) (Samoan: Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), exercising statutory powers under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 (as amended), the Financial Institutions Act 1996, the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 (as amended). The CBS is the sole foreign-exchange control authority, licensed-bank supervisor and operator of the national payment and clearing infrastructure; offshore vehicle supervision is delegated in part to the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), and AML reporting is received by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-Low. Samoa has no licensing framework for independent EMI or e-money providers; the Financial Institutions Act 1996 confines deposit-taking and payment services to licensed commercial banks, and the CBS does not recognise offshore EMI licences as authorised-dealer status. A non-resident International Company may lawfully operate an offshore EMI account (for example Airwallex, Wise Business or Currenxie) as a collections aggregator for USD/EUR royalty receipts, provided the EMI is appropriately licensed in its home jurisdiction and the IC's Samoan registered agent is notified. Legal constraints: (i) the EMI must not become the place of effective management of the IC, or SIFA may challenge the Samoan tax-exempt and substance status; (ii) an offshore EMI cannot clear WST, obtain CBS approvals or settle domestic Samoan payments; (iii) correspondent banks frequently restrict IP-heavy and royalty-heavy flows to EMIs; and (iv) the Samoan AML framework treats the offshore EMI as an additional relationship requiring enhanced due diligence and annual declaration. Recommended structure: maintain the ANZ or BSP account as the principal clearing and treasury gateway, and use the EMI solely for low-value collection aggregation with a daily sweep back to the Samoan bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: the parent (for example a Hong Kong or Singapore holding company) instructs its bank to issue a SWIFT MT103 credit to the WS_IC account at ANZ Bank (Samoa) Limited or BSP Samoa. The wire must carry: Field 50K (ordering customer: parent entity), Field 57A (beneficiary bank and SWIFT address), Field 59 (beneficiary: WS_IC, exact account number), Field 70 (remittance information stating the precise purpose, e.g. 'Equity contribution for IP acquisition pursuant to Board Resolution dated [date]' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement under Loan Agreement dated [date]'), and Field 71A (charges: SHA). No CBS prior approval is required because the International Company exemption under the International Companies Act 1987 removes the Exchange Control Act 1976 reporting and surrender obligations for foreign-currency credits to a non-resident entity. The receiving bank nonetheless verifies the certificate of incorporation, SIFA economic-substance certificate, board resolution, contributor identity and source of funds; credits in excess of WST 100,000 equivalent are reported to the Samoa FIU as large-transaction reports. If the IC later converts part of the injection into WST for local cost payments, the conversion must be executed through an authorised dealer and may require a CBS application supported by the Samoan agent's invoices.Clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 international wire for outbound royalty disbursements to non-resident licensors, patent owners, sub-agents and intra-group service providers; each instruction must reference the underlying licence agreement, the commercial invoice and, for related-party payees, a board resolution approving the payment. The IC, as a tax-exempt non-resident entity, is not obliged to deduct Samoan withholding tax on royalties paid to non-residents, but must obtain a Samoan tax-clearance certificate if the payee is a Samoan tax resident; (2) Commercial documentary letters of credit issued or confirmed through ANZ or BSP under UCP600 for security-backed IP acquisitions or purchases of tangible collateral, with the traditional transport documents replaced by IP assignment certificates, notarised licence agreements and insurance certificates where no physical goods are shipped; (3) Same-day real-time gross settlement is not available for cross-border WST, so all settlements clear through the USD correspondent banking network. For related-party payouts exceeding USD 500,000 equivalent, the IC should pre-position with the bank a SIFA or registered-agent countersigned direction letter, an updated economic-substance calculation and the executed disbursement schedule.Forwards: outright deliverable forward contracts with a Samoan authorised dealer (ANZ or BSP Treasury) on USD, EUR, AUD or NZD against WST for documented commercial receipts or payments; CBS administrative practice permits corporate hedges up to a maximum tenor of 12 months, subject to production of the underlying royalty or licence agreement evidencing genuine exposure.NDFs: non-deliverable forwards on WST executed with international bank desks in Singapore or Hong Kong, used because no deliverable offshore WST market exists; permissible without CBS approval for a non-resident International Company as the Exchange Control Act 1976 does not apply to the IC's foreign-currency transactions, but the hedge must be documented under an ISDA confirmation and supported by a commercial purpose statement.Currency swaps: cross-currency swaps (USD/EUR against WST or USD against EUR) executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with a top-tier offshore counterparty or via ANZ/BSP Treasury; no regulatory approval is required for a non-resident IC, but the bank and counterparty must be satisfied that the swap serves a genuine commercial purpose, such as servicing IP-acquisition debt, and is not a speculative or unhedged derivative position.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Annual Return is due within three months after the anniversary of incorporation, as prescribed in the International Companies Act. A non-resident IC may file earlier if the registered agent obtains the relevant consent; no CIT filing applies because the entity is outside the income tax net.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Samoan entity must prepare a Local File and Master File if its aggregate related-party transactions exceed WST 15 million per annum, or if the group's consolidated revenue exceeds WST 100 million. For an exempt IC, the Revenue may still demand contemporaneous documentation where the licensing margin appears inconsistent with arm's length returns.

Economic substance scope: The Samoa International Companies (Economic Substance) Act applies to IP holding companies. Such an IC must demonstrate that core income-generating activities are managed and directed in Samoa, including regular board meetings, availability of qualified employees, physical premises, and operational expenditure proportionate to IP income. Outsourcing to a Samoan licensed service provider is acceptable, but the annual substance return must be filed with the Registry. Non-compliance results in penalties, exchange of information with foreign tax authorities, and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent IP Licensing

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction attracts a fine not exceeding WST 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years. Civil assessment may impose 200% of the tax underpaid (if any), with interest compounded at 20% per annum.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director signs or directs a false filing; directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of hidden licensing fees, personal travel bans can be issued pending investigation, and imprisonment applies upon conviction.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: A penalty equal to 10% of the IP-derived income for each non-compliant year, capped at WST 2,000,000, plus potential de-registration and adverse exchange of information to the beneficial owner's tax authority.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the annual substance penalty if they were aware of the sham structure; the Court may impose director disqualification for up to 10 years, freeze personal assets, and order restitution of fees earned from the arrangement.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fine up to WST 50,000 per offence per director; the registered agent's license may be suspended; the IC may be subject to regulatory audit and compulsory liquidation.

Director personal liability: Directors face up to 3 years imprisonment for money laundering facilitation, personal fines up to WST 100,000, and civil asset forfeiture of funds passing through the company.
Abusive Profit Repatriation and Round-Tripping

Statutory sanction: The Revenue recharacterizes the payment as a dividend, imposes a 15% dividend withholding tax (reduced to 0% for an exempt IC but exposed to treaty abuse), and applies a 20% negligence penalty plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the sham can be ordered to repay the distribution personally if the company is insolvent; travel bans, contempt proceedings, and criminal sanctions apply for failure to comply with the Revenue's demand.
