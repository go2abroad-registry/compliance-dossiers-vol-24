Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS), National Office Pretoria; operational portals: SARS eFiling (https://efiling.sars.gov.za) for income tax and VAT filings, registrations and VAT101 applications; SARS Customs division (SAD500 export declarations) at https://www.sars.gov.za; single national authority with no provincial tax distinctions (Unitary_National).

E-government filing portal

A resident (Pty) Ltd incorporated under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 via the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) is automatically matched with SARS to obtain its ten-digit Income Tax Reference Number; the entity must thereafter activate an eFiling profile and verify its banking details. For foreign-incorporated or non-resident shareholders, SARS registration requires certified incorporation documents, the Memorandum of Incorporation, directors/beneficial ownership declarations, local fixed establishment particulars and applicable ISIC/NTIC business codes. The VAT vendor application is a separate filing (VAT101) in eFiling; once approved SARS allocates a ten-digit VAT vendor number which constitutes the VAT ID, and refunds are locked until bank verification and processing of the vendor declaration are complete.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the (Pty) Ltd is a South African tax resident (incorporated in the Republic, thus residence-based worldwide taxation) and is chargeable to 27% Corporate Income Tax on taxable income; it cannot elect pass-through or disregarded entity treatment for direct tax purposes; dividends distributed attract 20% Dividends Tax at shareholder level; for indirect tax the entity is registered as the VAT vendor and is the principal exporting taxpayer under the VAT Act.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (1) Micro Business Turnover Tax election under the Sixth Schedule (annual gross turnover cap ZAR 1.5 million; the company must not be a VAT vendor while electing turnover tax); (2) voluntary VAT registration from ZAR 50,000 and compulsory registration at ZAR 1,000,000 taxable turnover; (3) VAT grouping election under s50 Value-Added Tax Act 89 of 1991 for 100% related vendor entities; (4) hybrid debt and hybrid equity recharacterisation elections under s25DA and s25DB of the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962; (5) participation exemption and foreign business establishment elections in the CIT return; (6) deemed vendor status election for non-resident suppliers of electronic/digital services; (7) binding private ruling applications to SARS to secure tax treatment on cross-border dairy commodity structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration when actual or expected annual taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 measured on a 12-month forward/backward test; voluntary registration starts at ZAR 50,000; non-resident suppliers of imported services or of electronic services may incur VAT registration obligations to SARS without reference to the local turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s11(1)(a) VAT Act to goods physically exported from South Africa to an export destination within the prescribed period (90 days) after the time of supply, provided the vendor holds prescribed documentary proof of export; s11(2)(l) zero-rates qualifying export services (services physically rendered outside South Africa, or supplied to non-residents not used in South Africa). Whole and skimmed milk powder commodity sales qualify at 0% upon physical dispatch to foreign buyers under Swift LC or TT Wire payment structures, with the input VAT on milk powder purchases retained as a refundable credit on the VAT 201 return; any sale diverted to local consumption is taxable at the headline rate.

  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from a South African port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC), being an authorised dealer's foreign-currency receipt confirmation (SWIFT MT103 / SARB Form B credit evidence)

South Africa does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control system and imposes no digital stamping of e-invoices. Mandatory VAT invoicing follows s20 VAT Act: full tax invoices for taxable supplies above ZAR 5,000 (abridged invoices permissible below), requiring supplier name and VAT number, customer details, quantity, price, and VAT amount; export sales must show a zero-rated indicator or 'tax invoice for export'. Returns are submitted electronically via SARS eFiling or the SARS API; customs export declarations (SAD500) are lodged electronically through SARS Customs. The SARS digital modernisation strategy contemplates expanded structured e-invoicing, but as of current law there is no pre-issuance tax authority clearance of invoices.

SARS subjects export zero-rating claims to targeted risk screening; where the export-proof bundle is incomplete or goods are diverted into the domestic market, output VAT at 15% is reimposed with interest from the original filing date (s45 VAT Act), plus understatement penalties of 10%-150% of the shortfall under s222/s223 Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011. Fraudulent zero-rating or false export-remittance schemes expose directors and vendors to criminal prosecution (TAA s234/s235 and Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964), retrospective clawback of refunded VAT (statutory five-year assessment period is extended without limit in fraud cases), vendor deregistration, and suspension of future export clearances via SARS/SARB cross-referencing of export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa operates a managed-float exchange rate regime with current-account convertibility for genuine trade transactions routed through an Authorized Dealer, supported by full commercial documentation (invoices, bills of lading, customs entries). Capital-account convertibility is conditional: inward foreign direct investment (equity subscription) does not require prior SARB approval but must be declared on Form F178 (Foreign Investment Declaration) within 30 days of receipt through the Authorized Dealer. Dividend profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted subject to: (i) proof of the original inward investment (F178 reference number), (ii) a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN, (iii) a board-approved solvency and liquidity declaration, (iv) audited or reviewed financial statements evidencing distributable profits, and (v) settlement of Dividends Withholding Tax. Remittances above ZAR 100 million require specific FinSurv approval; amounts below may be approved by the Authorized Dealer. Additional hurdles include the restriction on non-resident shareholders utilising domestic ZAR borrowings (loan-for-share financing) and limitations on capital repatriation on deregistration, which require tax clearance and cancellation of the F178 registration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in full in South Africa and credited to the exporter's account with an Authorized Dealer within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless a specific extended arrangement has been approved by FinSurv. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into ZAR; exporters may elect to retain applicable foreign currency receipts in an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or convert to South African Rand. The exporter must submit proof of receipt within the 180-day period, and any failure to repatriate must be reported to the Authorized Dealer with a request for extension or replacement foreign exchange cover, supported by aged receivables analysis and trade collection documentation.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv, also referred to as the Financial Surveillance Division) under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 (Act 9 of 1933), with delegated functions exercised by Authorized Dealers comprising South African commercial banks and their authorised foreign-currency offices.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for any offshore digital EMI acting as the primary treasury or settlement rail. Under the Currency and Exchanges Act and FinSurv exchange control rules, a South African resident corporate entity must conduct foreign-currency transactions through a locally authorised Authorized Dealer; maintaining an offshore bank or EMI account without SARB approval is prohibited. Offshore EMIs cannot issue ZAR settlements, cannot perform F178 inward investment reporting, and are outside the South African FICA AML/CTF supervisory net. A MEDIUM supplementary viability exists only for non-core parallel work: using a SWIFT-connected offshore EMI for advisory currency visibility or third-country trade disbursement where the underlying treasury exposure remains booked at the South African Authorized Dealer; even then, the South African entity's funds cannot be split outside approved FCA structures without FinSurv consent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder must remit equity capital by SWIFT MT103 with a compliant purpose code referencing 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' and the subscribing entity's name. Funds are directed to the (Pty) Ltd's Operational ZAR Current Account or USD Foreign Currency Account held with the SA Authorized Dealer. The bank will credit the account, issue an inward telegraphic transfer advice, and record the transaction on Form F178 (Foreign Investment Declaration - Inward) within 30 days; the company is issued an Exchange Control reference number that becomes mandatory evidence for future dividend declarations and capital repatriation. Required verification bundle: board resolution approving the share issue, signed subscription agreement, updated Memorandum of Incorporation, share certificates, F178 declaration, SWIFT advice copies, and banking-source declarations evidencing that the funds originate from the non-resident's genuine external account (no prohibited local ZAR loan-for-share conversion).Trade settlements for importing raw whole and skimmed milk powder are executed predominantly by SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers and irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit (Commercial LC) issued by the SA Authorized Dealer. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2023 (reduced from 28%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% dividends tax withheld at source under ss64E-64G of the Income Tax Act; the South African (Pty) Ltd acts as the withholding agent and must remit the tax to SARS within 30 days of declaration or payment.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

ITR14 due within 12 months of the financial year-end (SARS-issued notice of assessment deadline). Provisional tax: first payment by the last business day of the sixth month of the year of assessment; second payment by the last business day of the final month of the year of assessment; optional third top-up within six months after year-end if the second provisional estimate was understated.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary de minimis threshold exists under South African transfer-pricing rules (s31 ITA read with TAA s29). All cross-border transactions between connected persons — including raw milk powder purchases from affiliated producers and sales to affiliated distributors or traders — must be at arm's length and supported by contemporaneous TP documentation. OECD-aligned Local File and Master File are expected for any material controlled transaction; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least ZAR 10 billion. In practice SARS may request TP documentation for any controlled transaction, irrespective of amount.

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to Gulf Cooperation Council jurisdictions, but substance is legally embedded through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (ss80A-80L) which invalidates tax avoidance arrangements lacking commercial substance; (ii) the controlled foreign company (CFC) regime in s9D which attributes passive offshore income to South African shareholders unless the CFC demonstrates payroll and operational substance; (iii) the 'effective management' test for residence determinations; and (iv) transfer-pricing substance audits that disregard paper-only conduit structures. A dairy commodity trader is expected to demonstrate local board direction, physical procurement/logistics control, vesting of contractual inventories in South Africa, substantive export credit and forex risk management, and commensurate staff and office footprint; failure to satisfy these factors invites GAAR, TP secondary adjustments, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Deliberate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of up to 150% of the shortfall under s223 TAA for intentional tax evasion (75% for deliberate conduct not amounting to evasion; 50% for gross negligence; 25% for substantial understatement), plus full interest and criminal prosecution. Criminal conviction exposes the company to an unlimited fine and principal officers to imprisonment of up to five years (ss234-235 TAA).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise falsified ITR14 declarations face joint and several payment liability, asset forfeiture orders, civil restitution of evaded tax and penalties, statutory travel bans implemented through SARS s195 measures preventing departure from South Africa until the tax debt is settled, and up to five years' imprisonment for fraud or corruption offenses.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: SARS primary adjustment by reference to the arm's-length price under s31(2); secondary adjustment deeming an interest-bearing loan at the prescribed rate under s31(3); understatement penalty up to 150%; compounded daily interest; disallowance of claimed deductions; and potential CFC attribution of resulting foreign profits.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed the non-arm's-length contracts with knowledge are considered 'controlling persons' in SARS investigations; under s236 TAA directors are liable for corporate tax debts where wilful non-compliance is evidenced; in egregious schemes, personal liability extends to penalties, interest and prosecution for perjury in TP documentation certification.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Application of the GAAR (ss80A-80L) to void the arrangement; full recomputation as if the arrangement had not been concluded; understatement penalty up to 150%; interest; denial of treaty relief; withdrawal of SARS tax clearance certificates; VAT and customs penalty exposure under the Customs and Excise Act where dairy import/export documentation is false.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally stripped of the corporate veil where the company is a mere facade, with civil restitution of all tax avoided; travel bans and sequestration orders are issued; executive managers who certified substance declarations fraudulently are imprisoned for up to five years; repeat non-resident directors face exclusion from South African corporate registers.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment up to 15 years under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act; forfeiture of the underlying trade proceeds; SARS-linked penalties of 20% for failure to declare foreign bank accounts or transactions under exchange-control regulations; debarment of the company from public procurement and customs clearing.

Director personal liability: Directors are 'accountable institution' principals for FIC AML obligations; individual criminal liability attaches to directors who knowingly process tainted funds; travel bans, freezing orders over personal assets, and restrictions on leaving South Africa apply; director disqualification under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 follows any money-laundering conviction.
Dividends Tax Non-Compliance by Directors

Statutory sanction: The company remains liable for the full 20% dividends tax plus 10% penalty and daily interest; SARS may assess the unpaid dividends tax jointly against the company and each director who authorised the distribution (s64N ITA); criminal fines apply for failure to provide accurate dividend declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid dividends tax, interest and penalties; SARS may obtain a court order prohibiting the director from leaving South Africa until the debt is paid; in fraudulent TRC cases, directors face criminal fraud charges with imprisonment of up to five years and permanent debarment from serving as a director in South Africa.
