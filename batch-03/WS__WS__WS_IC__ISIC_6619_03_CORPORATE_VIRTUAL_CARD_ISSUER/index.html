Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Revenue Authority (SRA), under the Ministry for Revenue; official filing portal: https://www.sra.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended), the IC must first be registered with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA). Upon incorporation, apply to the SRA for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using Form TIN-01, attaching the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, and identification of directors/beneficial owners. The SRA issues the TIN within 5 business days. To activate the statutory tax exemption, the IC should obtain a Tax Exemption Certificate for offshore business activities from SIFA, confirming that income derived from sources outside Samoa is exempt from income tax and withholding tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists in Samoa. The IC is automatically treated as a tax-exempt offshore entity upon fulfilling SIFA compliance requirements. It may elect to be treated as a resident Samoa taxpayer only if it surrenders its IC status and re-domiciles as an ordinary domestic company. Hybrid mismatch elections under OECD BEPS rules are not applicable to exempt ICs, as Samoa maintains no corresponding domestic anti-hybrid provisions for this regime.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WS$100,000 (annual taxable turnover) for mandatory VAGST registration on any taxable activity, with voluntary registration permitted below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Samoa and services performed for a non-resident or non-resident-owned IC qualify for zero-rating under the Excise Tax Act 2005 (as amended), provided the supplier retains evidence of export and foreign payment. For an IC supplying virtual card issuing and spend management services exclusively to non-resident clients, the services constitute an exported service and may be zero-rated for VAGST if the IC elects to register; otherwise, the offshore revenue remains outside Samoa's tax net under the IC exemption. Financial and payment processing services supplied to non-residents are eligible for zero-rating where the direct beneficiary is located outside Samoa.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate currently applies in Samoa. VAGST-registered businesses must issue compliant tax invoices (manual or electronic) containing prescribed details. The SRA operates an online return filing and payment portal, but no continuous transmission to tax authorities is required.

False or fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract criminal prosecution, administrative penalties up to 200% of the evaded tax, and statutory interest on underpaid VAGST. The SRA may retrospectively assess and recover tax within 5 years from the relevant date, which is extended to 10 years for fraudulent activity, contractual, or wilful default. The taxpayer must retain export documentation for 7 years. The IC's tax exemption certificate can be revoked for material non-compliance, exposing the entity to full income tax and VAGST liability.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tālā (WST) is fully convertible for current account remittances with CBS General Permission, subject to anti-money laundering documentation. Capital account transfers, including shareholder equity injections and profit repatriation, require a CBS exchange control form (EC2/EC4) and may be subject to a 30-day approval cycle. International Companies (ICs) that adopt the non-resident status of the International Companies Act 1987 are generally ring-fenced from onshore capital controls when transacting exclusively in foreign currency through offshore-designated accounts; however, any conversion into WST or any transaction that affects Samoan residents breaks the ring-fence and triggers full CBS oversight. Profit remittance hurdles include the need for an annual audited financial statement that verifies the company's tax-exempt status and a certificate of clearance from the Samoa Inland Revenue Department.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa's Exchange Control Regulations require all service exporters, including offshore ICs providing payment processing and virtual card services, to repatriate export proceeds to a licensed authorized dealer in Samoa within 90 days of the invoice date. CBS General Permission No. 9 waives the mandatory surrender obligation for ICs that maintain a CBS-approved Foreign Currency Account, provided the exporter makes a quarterly cash-flow disclosure to the authorized dealer. Where insurance, freight, or card network settlement fee offsets are claimed, the net export proceeds may be repatriated; no fixed statutory conversion ratio (e.g., 25%) applies to ICs, but a domestic licensed service company must convert any WST-denominated revenue within two banking days using the interbank mid-rate.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS) operating under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984 and the Exchange Control Act 1972, with delegated administrative authority from the Samoa Ministry of Finance for currency, reserve, and cross-border payment oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed by the Central Bank of Samoa and cannot act as a primary deposit-taking institution for the local card-issuing treasury. Their permissible use is strictly limited to (i) acquiring merchant card acquirers in the SEPA/UK jurisdiction, (ii) providing an optional expense-control front-end, and (iii) pre-funding the card network sponsor account in non-WST currencies. Any Samoan domestic currency settlement or withdrawal must clear through a licensed Samoan commercial bank; otherwise the IC risks being deemed an unauthorized money service business under the Financial Institutions Act 1996.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection wiring from an offshore IC to the Samoa bank must be via SWIFT MT103 with the following compliance fields: `PURPOSE` code `GIFOR` or `CRED` for equity, and `LOCAL_INSTRUMENT` containing 'WS_IC VC Issuer - Capital Account Top Up'. The IC must file CBS Form EC4 within 14 business days. The beneficiary account must be labelled 'WS_IC Card Fund Escrow' to visibly segregate paid-up capital from operational float. The remitting bank's KYC officer must forward the original instruction via authenticated telex to avoid a duplicate CEMT (Cross-Border Exchange of Information) referral. Funds can be pre-cleared only after submission of the FSA (Samoa Financial Services Authority) registration certificate and the Central Bank's 'Eligible Non-Resident Entity' letter.The dominant clearing rail for virtual card trade settlement is the SWIFT MT103 remittance from the Samoan settlement account to the card network principal clearing bank (JPMorgan Chase, New York or Citibank, London) under the BIN sponsorship agreement, using purpose code `TRIC` (Trade in Services) or `NETT` (Network fee). Payment to non-network trade suppliers may additionally use a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) if the supplier requires documentary credit; however, the default operation is a straight MT103 without LC documentation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per income year triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation; Master File documentation is required where the foreign parent group reports consolidated annual revenue exceeding WST 100,000,000 and the Samoan entity exceeds the WST 1,000,000 controlled transaction threshold. These thresholds follow the Samoa Revenue Corporation's practical application of the arm's length standard under the Income Tax Act 1974 (Transfer Pricing Amendment Act 2015).

Economic substance scope: Yes. The International Companies (Amendment) Act 2015 and Samoa's Economic Substance Regulations require every International Company to demonstrate substantive economic presence in Samoa. For the virtual card issuer, compliance requires: (i) a licensed registered agent with a physical Samoa office; (ii) a registered office retaining statutory records; (iii) board meetings and material decisions documented in Samoa; (iv) core revenue-generating activities such as programme management, transaction monitoring, and settlement oversight directed from Samoa; and (v) at least two natural-person directors or one licensed corporate director. A card-issuing IC without real substance in Samoa is exposed to deregistration and the loss of the tax-exemption certificate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Exemption Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 1974 ss.120-124 and International Companies Act 1987 s.199(2)-(3): forfeiture of the tax-exemption certificate, full assessment at 27% corporate tax, civil penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded, and a criminal fine not exceeding WST 100,000, with continuing daily fines of WST 20,000 for persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent trading and false declarations; each director is jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties, may be disqualified from acting as a director for up to five years, and faces imprisonment of up to seven years where evasion is proven.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Application of the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations and the Companies Act 2001: deregistration and strike-off by the Registrar, revocation of the International Company licence, and re-assessment of the global card-issuing income as Samoan-resident income at 27% plus 1.5% monthly late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham IC are personally exposed to civil recovery actions by regulators and creditors; departure prohibition orders (travel bans) may be issued during tax recovery proceedings, and custodial sentences of up to five years apply where economic-substance statements are found to be false.
AML and Unlicensed E-Money / Card Issuing

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed financial business is an indictable offence carrying fines up to WST 2,000,000, revocation of any special card-issuing licence, confiscation of the entire processing float, and FIU civil penalties of WST 500,000 for AML compliance failures.

Director personal liability: Directors are held personally accountable for governance failures; courts may disqualify them from corporate management for up to ten years, order personal restitution of customer funds diverted in card settlements, and impose prison sentences of up to seven years for money-laundering facilitation.
Sanctions, Fraud and Cross-Border Fines

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act and Samoa's foreign-account-tax-compliance framework: immediate licence suspension, fines of WST 1,000,000 per programme, regulatory repossession of cardholder files, and referral for mutual legal assistance and criminal asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve such acts face extradition-capable money-laundering charges, personal asset forfeiture, and imprisonment for up to ten years; in civil claims, directors are personally liable to cardholders and network operators under wrongful-trading principles without limitation of liability.
