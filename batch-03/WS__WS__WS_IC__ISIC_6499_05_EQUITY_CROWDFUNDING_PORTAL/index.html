Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (a) export of goods evidenced by customs departure records; (b) supply of services to a non-resident recipient who is outside Samoa at the time of supply, including crowd-funding platform and commission services invoiced by the portal to non-resident Issuers; (c) services directly connected with international transport. An equity crowdfunding portal billing overseas startup Issuers outside Samoa satisfies the place-of-supply test and may claim 0% VAT, provided the issuer is not registered for VAT in Samoa and no local beneficiary exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs online CED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or commercial bank credit proof of funds received in Samoa
  • VAT zero-rated invoice signed by the supplier

No statutory e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate in force. Paper or PDF invoices are legally acceptable; there is no API clearance system, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control requirement in Samoa as of the current tax year.

MfR audit under the Tax Administration Act 2012 permits retrospective assessment up to 4 years (10 years in cases of fraud or wilful default). Wrongful zero-rating claims trigger shortfall penalties of 50% of the VAT undercharged for unintentional error and 150% for intentional or reckless conduct, plus late payment interest of 10% per annum. Wilful evasion is a criminal offense punishable by fine up to WST 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; for International Companies, SIFA may additionally revoke the international company license and cancel registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan tala (WST) operates under a partial-convertibility regime. Current-account payments for genuine trade and service transactions are generally permitted without prior approval. Capital-account transactions, including cross-border equity crowdfunding inflows and outbound profit or dividend remittances, require prior exchange-control consent from the Central Bank of Samoa. Profit and capital repatriation by a Samoa International Company is permissible after the company is in good standing with the Registry of International Companies, has settled any Samoa tax liabilities (which are normally nil under a tax-exempt IC structure), and has obtained an exchange-control release where the remittance exceeds a bank's threshold. In practice, banks require documentary evidence of the underlying investment deal, investor KYC files, and a certificate of compliance from the corporate administrator before wiring funds offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and Central Bank of Samoa regulations, residents must repatriate export proceeds to a licensed Samoa bank within 30 days of shipment, and any proceeds retained in a foreign currency must be sold to an authorised dealer for Samoan tala at the prevailing exchange rate within the same period unless the Central Bank of Samoa grants a foreign-currency retention allowance. Non-resident International Companies are normally outside the resident surrender obligation; however, a Samoa-resident operating branch, any Samoa employee, or an entity generating commercial revenue in-country would be required to comply fully with the repatriation and conversion timeline.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed banks in Samoa and cannot hold the regulatory escrow capital required for an equity crowdfunding portal. A foreign EMI or neobank offering virtual accounts and remote wire clearing would be treated as an unregulated service provider by the Samoa Financial Services Authority and would create immediate high-risk AML flags. Local bank connectivity via correspondent bank networks, not digital EMIs, is the only legally sound clearing and escrow route.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Investor funds are received in the capital escrow account at a Samoa-licensed commercial bank solely through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 inbound wires originating from the investor's own bank account or licensed payment account. Wires must be directed to the designated escrow bank account in Samoa with the relevant exchange-control purpose code agreed with Central Bank of Samoa, such as the code for direct investment in equity or securities. The bank will verify the investor's identity, the subscription documentation, and the matching project commitment before applying the necessary FX conversion from the foreign currency into WST or maintaining the asset at the confirmed foreign-currency equivalent. All inbound remittances are subject to the platform's AML screening, investor suitability assessment, and the bank's source-of-funds check; no digital-asset or crypto rails are permitted.Outbound disbursements from escrow to approved issuers after the funding campaign reaches its target are executed through SWIFT MT103 beneficiary-credit transfers to the issuer's professional bank account. In cases involving large procurement or raw-material purchases, a commercial letter of credit can be issued through the Samoa bank's correspondent network, typically by MT700, covering documents against payment. Disbursement of residual refunds to unsuccessful campaigns is also routed by SWIFT MT103 to the original investor's account or to a nominated refund processor trusted by the bank. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for International Companies (ICs) incorporated under Samoa's International Companies Act 1987; the resident domestic company headline rate is 27% if IC tax-exempt status is lost or domestic Samoan-source business is conducted.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — dividends paid by a Samoan International Company to non-resident shareholders are statutorily exempt from Samoan withholding tax under the International Companies Act 1987. No deduction, withholding, or reporting of Samoan dividend WHT is required.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 10,000,000 annual gross turnover is the threshold that triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation for companies within the Samoan income tax net. International Companies that remain tax-exempt have no direct TP documentation obligation, but if the crowdfunding portal operates through a related Samoan taxable subsidiary or the IC is reclassified as taxable, contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation must be prepared once connected-party transactions exceed WST 10,000,000 in annual turnover.

Economic substance scope: Samoa has not introduced a general Economic Substance Regulations regime for standard International Companies. However, the International Companies Act 1987, the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007, and Central Bank of Samoa licensing practice require a meaningful presence in Samoa: a registered office, a licensed registered agent, board minutes, adequate books and records, and a local AML compliance arrangement for high-risk crowdfunding activity. Without this substance, authorities may treat the IC as a shell and deny the zero-tax exemption or revoke the crowdfunding portal licence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Sham Exemption

Statutory sanction: Assessment of Samoan corporate income tax at 27% on all undeclared profits, recapture of 15% withholding tax on hidden distributions, and a fraudulent evasion penalty of up to 200% of the unpaid tax under the Income Tax Act 2012 and the Tax Administration Act 2012. Criminal prosecution may result in a fine of up to WST 100,000 and imprisonment for up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors, nominee shareholders, and the registered agent may be held jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalty, and interest where the IC acted as a mere instrumentality without separate commercial substance. The Samoan court can pierce the corporate veil, issue civil restitution orders, impose travel bans, and order passport surrender pending full payment.
Shell Company / False Substance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the International Company certificate, cancellation of the financial services licence, automatic strike-off after 90 days, and a monetary penalty of WST 50,000 under the International Companies Act 1987 and the Financial Institutions Act 1996.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who knowingly signed false declarations are personally liable for the penalty and costs; a court may impose a five-year disqualification from acting as a director in Samoa, and any director attempting to leave Samoa while liabilities remain is subject to a departure prohibition order.
AML / Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to WST 1,000,000 for a body corporate and WST 300,000 for an individual, together with forfeiture of transaction proceeds, under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007 and directions issued by the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit. Regulatory revocation of the crowdfunding licence follows automatically.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or direct money laundering activities are personally criminally liable and may be imprisoned; personal assets may be restrained by court order, and removal from the director register is mandatory upon conviction.
False Annual Return / Misleading Certification

Statutory sanction: Fine of WST 10,000 plus a daily penalty of WST 200 until corrected; the court may impose imprisonment for up to two years for knowingly furnishing a false return to the Samoan Companies Registry or the Ministry of Revenue.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent are jointly and severally liable for investor losses caused by misstatement; civil restitution orders may compel repayment of funds raised without disclosure, and personal travel bans remain in force until the court order is fully satisfied.
