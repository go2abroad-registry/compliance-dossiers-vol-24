Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), an agency of the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition, acting through the CIPC e-Services online portal and the compact registration process.

Governing statute

Companies Act 71 of 2008, as amended, particularly sections 11 (name reservation), 13 (incorporation), 14 (Notice of Incorporation and effectiveness), 19 (legal personality), 50 (shares) and 66-69 (board of directors), read with the Companies Regulations, 2011 (Forms CoR 9.1 and CoR 14.1).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notice of Incorporation (Form CoR 14.1) filed together with the standard Memorandum of Incorporation, culminating in the Certificate of Incorporation (Form CoR 14.3).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 64 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum issued or paid-up share capital applies to a Private Company ((Pty) Ltd) under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; a nominal authorised capital of 1,000 ordinary shares at R0.01 par value may be adopted in the MoI and subscribed for cash; no escrow, capital-deposit or bank verification rule is imposed at the incorporation stage.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the local fire authority under the National Building Regulations (SANS 10400 Part T) and municipal fire bylaws; structural/occupancy clearance by the municipal building control officer for the factory fit-out; electrical certificate of compliance (CoC) issued by a registered electrician per SANS 10142-1; Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993 compliance — machinery risk assessment, electrical and noise surveys (≤ 85 dB(A) action level), compulsory COIDA registration with the Compensation Fund, and workplace clearance issued by an approved inspection authority for manufacturing operations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum issued or paid-up share capital applies to a Private Company ((Pty) Ltd) under the Companies Act 71 of 2008; a nominal authorised capital of 1,000 ordinary shares at R0.01 par value may be adopted in the MoI and subscribed for cash; no escrow, capital-deposit or bank verification rule is imposed at the incorporation stage.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); primary portals: SARS eFiling (https://www.sarsefiling.co.za), SARS Customs electronic Declaration Processing (e-SAD500/ICES), and SARS branch network

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain South African tax identity as follows: (1) Incorporation route: register the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); the integrated CIPC-SARS interface automatically issues an income tax reference number for the company; (2) Non-resident route: entities not locally incorporated must apply directly to SARS for a non-resident income tax reference number using Form IT77, submitting certified foreign incorporation documents, constitutional documents, director passport copies, and a Power of Attorney designating a South African representative taxpayer; (3) VAT registration: file Form VAT 101 with SARS once taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (compulsory) or elect voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000; non-resident vendors must appoint a SARS-recognised local representative and furnish proof of foreign registration, banking details, and business activity; (4) Customs registration: apply to SARS Customs for an importer/exporter code via Form DA185 before clearing export declarations; all registrations are completed through eFiling or SARS branch lodgement with mandated identity verification for directors/authorised representatives.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - A South African (Pty) Ltd is a resident company (incorporated in South Africa, or managed and controlled from South Africa) taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the current 27% corporate income tax rate for fiscal years ending on or after 31 March 2023; distributions attract a 20% dividends withholding tax. No pass-through or check-the-box eligibility exists: the company is the taxable entity. Non-resident companies are taxed on a territorial basis (South African source income only), and resident companies benefit from the foreign business establishment exemption (s 10(1)(o)) and controlled foreign company rules (s 9D). Special Economic Zone (SEZ) companies may access territorial investment allowances under sections 12R and 12RA. — Foreign entities obtain South African tax identity as follows: (1) Incorporation route: register the (Pty) Ltd with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC); the integrated CIPC-SARS interface automatically issues an income tax reference number for the company; (2) Non-resident route: entities not locally incorporated must apply directly to SARS for a non-resident income tax reference number using Form IT77, submitting certified foreign incorporation documents, constitutional documents, director passport copies, and a Power of Attorney designating a South African representative taxpayer; (3) VAT registration: file Form VAT 101 with SARS once taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period (compulsory) or elect voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000; non-resident vendors must appoint a SARS-recognised local representative and furnish proof of foreign registration, banking details, and business activity; (4) Customs registration: apply to SARS Customs for an importer/exporter code via Form DA185 before clearing export declarations; all registrations are completed through eFiling or SARS branch lodgement with mandated identity verification for directors/authorised representatives.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A South African (Pty) Ltd is a resident company (incorporated in South Africa, or managed and controlled from South Africa) taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the current 27% corporate income tax rate for fiscal years ending on or after 31 March 2023; distributions attract a 20% dividends withholding tax. No pass-through or check-the-box eligibility exists: the company is the taxable entity. Non-resident companies are taxed on a territorial basis (South African source income only), and resident companies benefit from the foreign business establishment exemption (s 10(1)(o)) and controlled foreign company rules (s 9D). Special Economic Zone (SEZ) companies may access territorial investment allowances under sections 12R and 12RA.

Available election forms

South Africa does not operate a US-style check-the-box table for companies, but statutory elections available include: (1) Small Business Corporation election under section 12E of the Income Tax Act for qualifying (Pty) Ltd entities with annual turnover below ZAR 20 million, applying progressive reduced rates; (2) Research and Development allowance election under section 11D for pre-approved qualifying R&D expenditure; (3) Accelerated capital allowance election under section 12C for manufacturing plant and machinery (including computer equipment); (4) Industrial Policy Project allowance election under section 12I for greenfield or brownfield manufacturing investments; (5) SEZ tax allowance election under sections 12R/12RA for qualifying SEZ companies; (6) VAT group registration election under section 51 of the Value-Added Tax Act for related companies under common control to file as a single vendor; (7) Hybrid financial instrument elections under sections 8E, 8EA, 8F and 8FA, with anti-hybrid-mismatch interest disallowance rules aligned to BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory vendor registration: ZAR 1,000,000 (one million rand) taxable turnover in the preceding 12 consecutive months; voluntary registration permitted from ZAR 50,000 taxable turnover in a 12-month period; non-resident vendors undertaking taxable supplies in South Africa must register irrespective of local residence if the threshold is met or exceeded

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods (including rugged industrial computers and edge gateways) are supplied by a vendor and physically exported, provided the goods are consigned or delivered to the export destination within 90 days of the date of the tax invoice (extended to 120 days in prescribed circumstances) and documented proof of export is retained. Services supplied to non-residents qualify under section 11(2)(l) where the services are not physically rendered or applied in South Africa, are not connected with fixed property or movable property in South Africa, and the recipient is not a resident acting in a connected-person relationship. The zero rate is claimable only where the vendor holds the documentary export bundle at the time of the VAT return and for the statutory retention period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD500 / Form DA550) cleared by SARS Customs evidencing physical dispatch of the goods
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Airway Bill (air freight) evidencing consignment from a South African port of exit to the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list matching the export declaration and tariff classification (HS 8471/8473)
  • Inward foreign-currency receipt confirmation (bank-issued certificate of receipt, being the South African equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate, supported by SWIFT MT103 settlement records)
  • Zero-rated VAT tax invoice satisfying section 20 of the VAT Act
  • Certificate of Origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed at destination

As of the current regime, SARS does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based continuous transaction controls for VAT invoices. Vendors must issue compliant tax invoices under section 20 of the VAT Act and file VAT 201 returns electronically via eFiling or e@syFile. SARS has published draft e-invoicing frameworks (2023-2024) signalling phased adoption of a PEPPOL-based electronic invoicing exchange, but no compulsory clearance API is operational. Taxpayers must preserve full electronic accounting records and provide SARS remote access under sections 46 and 49 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; customs export declarations are processed electronically through the SARS Customs ICES/e-SAD500 system.

SARS audits export zero-rating claims by cross-matching SAD500 customs clearances, transport documents, and bank foreign-exchange receipts under the Reserve Bank exchange control reporting (Form EXA). Where zero-rating is disallowed, output VAT at 15% is clawed back by assessment together with statutory interest; understatement penalties under sections 223 and 234-235 of the Tax Administration Act may reach 200% of the tax shortfall for intentional evasion, with criminal liability of imprisonment up to 10 years or a fine up to ZAR 250,000 per offence. Customs fraud penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 91 of 1964 include fines up to ZAR 50,000 per declared item, forfeiture of goods, and revocation of vendor status; fraudulent VAT refund claims additionally trigger the refund recovery rules under sections 114-117 of the VAT Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including trade settlement, dividends, interest, royalties and management fees. Non-residents may invest freely in South African companies without prior exchange-control approval for ordinary inward foreign direct investment. Capital-account transactions, however, remain regulated: inward equity and shareholder loan proceeds must be reported through an authorised dealer and captured on the company's capital register. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted once the dividend is declared from after-tax profits, supported by a current SARS tax clearance or good-standing confirmation, satisfactory Beneficial Ownership due diligence, and a board resolution. Controlled outward capital flows, offshore guarantees, and excessive local borrowing by foreign-owned firms require additional FinSurv or authorised-dealer approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in South Africa within 30 days after shipment unless the authorised dealer grants extended terms, and all foreign currency received by a resident company must be surrendered to the authorised dealer within 30 days of receipt. The standard conversion requirement is 100% of export proceeds into rand, unless a retention account, advance-payment concession, or specific exemption has been approved by FinSurv. Each receipt must be traceable to the export bill of entry, commercial invoice, and proof of shipment; unreconciled shortfalls must be reported and justified to the authorised dealer.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) and registered authorised dealers, under the Exchange Control Regulations, 1961 and the Currency and Exchanges Manual (CEM).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low as a primary treasury rail. A foreign digital EMI or electronic-money institution cannot independently open a South African ZAR clearing account or provide local settlement/IBAN facilities unless it partners with a licensed authorised dealer or registered bank. Such institutions may serve only as supplemental foreign-currency collection wallets for export receivables, subject to FIC Act registration in South Africa, exchange-control restrictions on residents holding funds outside authorised channels, and the absence of SARB settlement membership. All high-value capital injections, export surrender obligations, and trade-credit settlements must therefore flow through a Tier 1 South African bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the company's designated capital account held with the appointed South African authorised dealer. The payment narrative must clearly state 'Inbound FDI equity subscription / share capital' and include the company's CIPC registration number; the reference should also identify the ISIC 2620 business scope. The bank will credit the capital account, convert the foreign currency to rand at the ruling market rate, and record the inflow on the company's capital register. Supporting documentation for the authorised dealer must include the share-allotment resolution, updated memorandum of incorporation, CIPC share register filing, source-of-funds confirmation, and proof of identity of the subscribing shareholder. No prior SARB approval is required for standard non-resident equity investment, but all such receipts must be declared to the Financial Surveillance Department through the authorised dealer for formal capital-importation recordation.Outbound trade disbursements should be settled through the appointed authorised dealer using SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier payments and advance-payment imports, and commercial documentary letters of credit governed by UCP600 for higher-value or risk-managed trade flows. A commercial LC is issued locally via MT700, confirmed or advised by the beneficiary's bank, and settled through MT400, MT103, or MT202 as appropriate. All payments must be supported by the relevant purchase order, commercial invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, customs bill of entry, and SARS/import duty evidence where applicable. Exchange-control release is handled by the authorised dealer on the basis of genuine current-account trade documentation; no separate SARB approval is required for bona fide import payments.
