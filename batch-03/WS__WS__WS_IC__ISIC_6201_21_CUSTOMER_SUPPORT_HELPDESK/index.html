Competent corporate registry

Registrar of International Companies, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour (MCIL), Government of Samoa, acting through licensed registered agents; administrative coordination with the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA).

Governing statute

International Companies Act 1987 (Samoa), as amended; ss. 4-8 (registration and incorporation), ss. 20-21 (registered office and registered agent), ss. 23-24 (directors and secretary), and s. 38 (exemption from Samoa domestic business restrictions). The Companies Act 2001 does not apply to an International Company.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of an International Company (prescribed Form 1) together with the executed Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered office consent, registered agent consent, and notices of first directors and secretary.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Samoa Inland Revenue Authority (SIRA), Ministry of Finance – official portal: https://www.sira.gov.ws

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) obtains an International Company Number from the Registrar of International Companies under the International Companies Act 1987. No statutory TIN is issued automatically because ICs are tax-exempt. If the IC voluntarily enters the local VAGST net by making taxable supplies in Samoa, it must apply to SIRA for a VAGST registration number using Form VAT-01, with the certificate of incorporation, foreign ownership declaration, and physical address details; normal processing is 7–10 business days.

No statutory TIN is issued automatically because ICs are tax-exempt. If the IC voluntarily enters the local VAGST net by making taxable supplies in Samoa, it must apply to SIRA for a VAGST registration number using Form VAT-01, with the certificate of incorporation, foreign ownership declaration, and physical address details; normal processing is 7–10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

An IC may elect to remain wholly offshore and tax-exempt under the International Companies Act, or it may elect to be treated as a resident ordinary Samoan company subject to income tax and VAGST by notifying the Registrar and SIRA. Samoa does not employ US-style check-the-box elections; hybrid mismatch elections under OECD recommendations have no statutory effect in Samoa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WS$100,000 annual taxable turnover or expected turnover within 12 months; registration is also mandatory for importers of taxable goods regardless of turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods where the supplier exports the goods within 28 days of invoice date, retains proof of physical export, and receives payment in convertible foreign currency. Offshore services supplied to non-resident customers are zero-rated when the recipient is outside Samoa, the services are performed wholly outside Samoa, and the benefit of the services is not enjoyed in Samoa. Customer support and helpdesk services delivered offshore qualify if they fall under this territorial exclusion.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUSDEC)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Commercially reasonable VAGST zero-rated invoice
  • Foreign exchange Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Currency Purchase/Repatriation record from the licensed bank
  • Evidence of effective payment from the non-resident recipient

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime in Samoa. VAGST invoices are paper or PDF-based. Electronic filing of returns is mandatory for registered businesses through SIRA's online portal (E-SIRA); API integration is not required.

SIRA audits may verify zero-rating evidence and claw back any erroneous input tax credits plus interest. Penalties include a 100% shortfall penalty on unpaid/overclaimed VAGST, late payment penalties of up to 25% of the tax, and administrative penalties of up to WS$50,000 per false document. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are criminal offences under the VAGST Act 2003 and Tax Administration Act, exposing the IC and its directors to fines up to WS$1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Samoa operates a managed-float foreign exchange regime in which the Samoan Tala (WST) is freely usable for domestic settlement but has very shallow offshore market depth. Current-account transactions, including SaaS/helpdesk subscription service fees and after-tax profit remittances, are generally permissible through authorized dealers with adequate underlying documentation, while capital-account transactions such as equity injections, offshore borrowings, and portfolio flows require prior written exchange control approval from CBS. The International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act 1987 is expressly exempt from exchange control legislation provided it carries on no Samoan domestic business, facilitating hard-currency retention and unrestricted offshore profit repatriation; nevertheless, any onshore WST account operation remains subject to CBS reporting and AML screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1984, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Samoa and offered for sale to an authorized dealer within six (6) months of the shipment date, with 100% of foreign currency receipts mandatorily surrendered into WST unless a specific retention or exemption facility is approved by CBS. For the exempt International Company in scope, mandatory surrender does not apply to subscription revenue collected offshore through Stripe Billing or PayPal, provided such receipts are settled outside Samoa and are not derived from Samoan domestic export activities; any foreign currency credited to an onshore bank account will still be recorded for balance-of-payments statistics and AML surveillance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; exercises statutory exchange control powers under the Exchange Control Act 1983 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1984, and supervises all licensed banks and financial institutions under the Financial Institutions Act 1996.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Samoa has no statutory electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing regime for offshore digital issuers; the Financial Institutions Act 1996 and CBS licensing requirements limit deposit-taking and regulated payment services to licensed physical banks and locally authorized financial institutions. Offshore digital EMIs cannot independently clear WST nor open a CBS-recognized settlement account. The viable structure is to retain Stripe Billing and PayPal as global acquiring and collection rails, with payouts routed via SWIFT/ACH into a licensed Samoan bank account that serves as the ultimate WST or USD settlement endpoint.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for dividends paid by an International Company to any shareholder, whether resident or non-resident, because IC distributions are wholly outside the Samoan income tax and withholding tax net. By contrast, dividends paid by a resident commercial company (non-IC) to a non-resident shareholder are subject to a domestic non-resident withholding tax of 15%, reducible under an applicable double tax agreement.

The annual fee is payable on or before each anniversary of incorporation; the annual return must be lodged within 6 months after each anniversary of incorporation. Tax-exempt status is maintained exclusively by timely payment of the annual fee and submission of the annual return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold is published in the Income Tax Act 2015; the Samoa Ministry of Revenue applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and in administrative practice requires contemporaneous documentation where cross-border related-party transactions exceed WST 2,000,000 per annum, aligned with the large taxpayer classification regime. A tax-exempt IC is not required to file a Local File or Master File with the MoR for its offshore SaaS operations, but must retain intercompany agreements, pricing matrices, board resolutions, and cost allocation records demonstrating arm's-length pricing in case the IC's exempt status or the group's Samoan subsidiary's deductions are challenged.

Economic substance scope: Samoa applies OECD-standard Economic Substance Regulations to International Companies. An IC must (i) be incorporated and maintain a registered office and licensed registered agent in Samoa; (ii) for geographically mobile activities, including digital services, demonstrate in its annual economic substance declaration adequate physical premises, qualified employees, core income-generating activity, and operational expenditure in Samoa proportionate to its declared business; and (iii) file an annual economic substance declaration. Failure to satisfy the substance test attracts penalties up to WST 500,000, forced deregistration, and heightened exposure to automatic exchange of information. A helpdesk SaaS IC operated with zero Samoan employees, fully cloud-hosted infrastructure, and passive subscription revenue is at elevated risk of failing the substance test and being reclassified as a non-tax-transparent shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Penalty of treble the tax evaded plus interest at the prescribed rate under the Tax Administration Act 2015; fines up to WST 1,000,000; criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 5 years; the IC's tax-exempt status is revoked with retrospective assessment of 25% CIT on all concealed income.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties where the corporate veil is pierced; the Registrar and the Ministry of Revenue may impose travel bans and asset-freezing orders; knowing signature of a false statutory declaration is independently punishable by imprisonment.
Prohibited Domestic Trading by International Company

Statutory sanction: Fine of WST 100,000 per month of continued breach, mandatory deregistration, forfeiture of profits derived from the unlawful Samoan activity, and refusal of reinstatement for a period of 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the prohibited trading face personal civil restitution to creditors and the Crown, disqualification from acting as a director of any Samoan company for up to 10 years, and personal liability for the company's income tax, VAT, and employment obligations arising from the unlawful activity.
Shell Company and Anti-Money Laundering Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Samoa's AML/CFT legislation and FIU enforcement powers: entity fines up to WST 2,000,000, forfeiture of all tainted property, deregistration, and referral to the Attorney-General for criminal prosecution; money laundering offences carry imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Registered agents and directors are jointly liable for AML compliance failures; individual fines reach WST 500,000; travel bans, passport seizure, and freezing of personal bank accounts may be ordered; directors of a shell IC used in fraud are personally liable to defrauded third parties on an agency and dishonest-assistance basis.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: The Ministry of Revenue may adjust taxable income to the arm's-length result, deny interest deductions on excessive debt under thin capitalisation rules, and impose a documentation-default penalty of 50% of the tax attributable to the adjustment, plus statutory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing documentation known to be false are criminally liable under the Tax Administration Act; where the entity is insolvent or struck off, directors and the controlling shareholder may be jointly assessed for the adjusted tax, interest, and penalties.
Failure to File Annual Return, Economic Substance Declaration, and CRS

Statutory sanction: Strike-off with loss of legal personality and vesting of assets in the Samoan Crown; late penalties of 10% plus 1% monthly interest; economic substance penalties up to WST 500,000; CRS non-disclosure fines up to WST 250,000 per undisclosed account.

Director personal liability: Directors of a struck-off IC remain personally liable for debts incurred after deregistration; travel bans may be imposed pending compliance; disqualification orders of up to 10 years apply where defaults arise from wilful neglect; directors may also be compelled to cooperate with foreign tax authority requests through Samoan court orders.
