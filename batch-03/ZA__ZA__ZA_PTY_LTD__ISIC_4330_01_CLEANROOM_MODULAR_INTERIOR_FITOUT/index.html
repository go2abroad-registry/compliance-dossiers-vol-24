Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); official self-service portals: SARS eFiling (https://efiling.sars.gov.za), SARS API platform for registered practitioners, and the ACMS (African Advanced Customs Management System) for customs and SAD500 declarations; principal office: Lehae la SARS, 299 Bronkhorst Street, Nieuw Muckleneuk, Pretoria. SARS is the single competent authority for income tax, VAT, customs duty, PAYE/UIF/SDL, dividends withholding tax and exchange-control reporting connected with trade settlement.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned incorporated (Pty) Ltd obtains its statutory identifiers in five electronic steps: (1) incorporation with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) to obtain the Companies Act registration number and Memorandum of Incorporation; (2) registration as a taxpayer on SARS eFiling (new company registration form), which issues the Income Tax Reference Number; (3) VAT vendor registration via VAT 101 once taxable supplies exceed or are reasonably expected to exceed R1,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period, or voluntarily once turnover exceeds R50,000 per 12-month period, after which SARS issues a separate VAT vendor number; (4) importer/exporter customs code registration through the SARS Customs module, enabling SAD500 export declarations and export zero-rating; and (5) PAYE/UIF/SDL employer registration via EMP 101 before hiring staff. The compliance pack requires the public officer appointment, foreign shareholder identification, CIPC certificate, local registered address and a board resolution; all registrations are completed electronically and SARS issues the respective tax references immediately on eFiling. A foreign company not incorporated in South Africa must also register as an external company with CIPC and then complete VAT/income tax registration for its South African permanent establishment, in which case only SA-source income attributable to the branch is taxed.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A South Africa-incorporated (Pty) Ltd is a resident company and a separate corporate taxpayer, taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate (for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022; previously 28%). Net capital gains are included in taxable income at an 80% inclusion rate, producing an effective company CGT rate of 21.6%. Dividends received are generally exempt under Section 10(1)(k) for domestic dividends, and foreign dividends may qualify for the participation exemption under Section 10B(3) where a 10% interest is held; dividends paid by the Pty Ltd attract dividends withholding tax at 20%, reduced under applicable tax treaties. No pass-through or transparent taxation is available to a Pty Ltd. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A South Africa-incorporated (Pty) Ltd is a resident company and a separate corporate taxpayer, taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate (for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2022; previously 28%). Net capital gains are included in taxable income at an 80% inclusion rate, producing an effective company CGT rate of 21.6%. Dividends received are generally exempt under Section 10(1)(k) for domestic dividends, and foreign dividends may qualify for the participation exemption under Section 10B(3) where a 10% interest is held; dividends paid by the Pty Ltd attract dividends withholding tax at 20%, reduced under applicable tax treaties. No pass-through or transparent taxation is available to a Pty Ltd. Territorial_Scope applies only to non-resident companies with a South African branch (taxation of SA-source income only), and Tax_Exempt_Zone status is not available; special economic zones provide preferential 15% corporate rates and VAT/customs incentives but not a full exemption.

Available election forms

South Africa has no US-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections available to the entity include: (1) VAT group registration under Section 50(1)(a) of the VAT Act, permitting a representative vendor to account for VAT on behalf of group companies; (2) hybrid instrument elections under Sections 8F and 23M-23N of the Income Tax Act, requiring symmetrical treatment of hybrid debt/equity mismatches with taxpayer election of the resulting tax position; (3) controlled foreign company elections under Section 9D, including the high-tax offset election and accounting-period elections; (4) assessed-loss set-off elections under Section 20, subject to the income-stripping limitations of Sections 20A and 20B; (5) capital allowance elections for Section 12C plant and machinery, Section 12B clean-energy assets and Section 11D approved research and development expenditure; and (6) designation as a 'designated group company' for intra-group dividends-tax relief and Section 24C allowance elections for construction contracts. Every election is made in the applicable return or by written application to SARS, and none alters the fundamental entity-level taxation of the Pty Ltd.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The ZAR is fully convertible for current-account trade and service payments, while capital-account flows such as intercompany loans, equity injections, and currency swaps are permitted but documented through a South African Authorized Dealer. Profit and dividend remittances by a non-resident shareholder of ZA_PTY_LTD require no prior SARB approval once the company holds a valid SARS Tax Compliance Status PIN (S19) and the remittance is executed on Form A; dividends remain subject to South African dividend withholding tax and any applicable double-tax treaty relief. Unusual related-party payments must be supported by SARS transfer-pricing documentation, and flows to or from sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions are prohibited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, 100% of the invoice proceeds must be repatriated into the South African banking system within 180 days from shipment; for cleanroom design and construction-services exports, proceeds are due within 180 days from invoice date unless the Authorized Dealer approves the contract milestone schedule. There is no mandatory conversion into ZAR (0% surrender ratio); exporters may lawfully credit the full export value to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) at an Authorized Dealer, but effectively 100% of the value must be returned onto onshore banking rails. The repatriation obligation is evidenced by Customs SAD500 export declarations and the bank's Balance-of-Payments filings; any extended payment terms, invoicing offsets, or foreign-currency expense netting must be disclosed to and approved by the Authorized Dealer before benefit is taken.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933, the Exchange Control Regulations 1961, and the SARB Exchange Control Manual (post-2021 liberalised dispensation), with the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) responsible for AML/CFT compliance oversight.
