Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) via the SARS eFiling portal

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must enrol on SARS eFiling, obtain an income tax reference number, and if taxable turnover is compulsory-registrable, submit a VAT 101 application to register for value-added tax. SARS will require the entity's incorporation certificate, proof of non-resident status, local registered address or representative office details, and appointment of a SARS-appointed tax representative or tax practitioner, after which SARS issues the income tax reference number and VAT vendor number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Africa does not provide check-the-box entity elections; classification follows the Companies Act and Income Tax Act. Election-like treatments exist in specific regimes such as section 45 tax rollover relief for intra-group transactions, section 8E/8EA preference-share rules, and group loss reallocation elections. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are limited by the taxation laws implementing OECD hybrid mismatch recommendations.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A vendor may zero-rate a supply of goods for export where the vendor has physically exported the goods under customs control within the prescribed period and retains documentary proof, including export declarations, bills of lading, contracts, and inward remittance evidence. Offshore services can be zero-rated when physically rendered outside South Africa or supplied to non-residents under the specified services provisions.

  • Customs Export Declaration and SARS SAD500 export entry
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical removal from South Africa
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank transfer proof matching the exported goods

South Africa has no real-time fiscal clearance or tax authority stamping mandate. Tax invoices must satisfy Value-Added Tax Act section 20 requirements and be issued within 21 days of supply. SARS eFiling and EDI channels are accepted, and taxpayers must retain electronic records and data for audit and submission purposes.

SARS may perform post-export audits and, where zero-rating is unsupported or fraudulent, raise additional assessments, claw back VAT with statutory interest, impose understatement penalties up to 100% of the tax shortfall under Tax Administration Act section 223, and pursue criminal sanctions including fines and imprisonment. Prescription periods are extended where returns were not submitted or fraud or misrepresentation is present.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African rand (ZAR) is freely convertible for current-account transactions (trade settlement, royalties, technical fees) through Authorised Dealers without FinSurv pre-approval. Capital-account convertibility is restricted: inward foreign equity investment is unrestricted and must be declared on Form A (AIS - Application for an Inward International Transfer); outward dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is lawful without FinSurv approval provided the foreign capital is 'vested' on the inward declaration and dividends are declared from post-1979 current profits, with each remittance executed on Form B together with an auditor's confirmation or a Financial Account Confirmation Facility (FACF) certificate. Capital repatriation (share capital reduction/liquidation distributions) requires prior FinSurv approval. Profit-remittance hurdles are therefore documentary rather than discretionary: maintaining a clean AIS declaration and a full audit trail of invoiced export proceeds received within the statutory window is the critical compliance condition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All proceeds from exported goods must be received into South Africa, or into an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held at an Authorised Dealer, within 180 days from the date of shipment. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may elect to retain export proceeds in foreign currency in the FCA or convert to ZAR at its discretion. The 180-day deadline is capable of extension only by the Authorised Dealer in limited, documented circumstances (non-payment by the foreign buyer, documentary dispute, or embargo), and failure to repatriate in time constitutes an exchange-control contravention attracting FinSurv penalties and banking-relationship risk.

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB), acting through the Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv) of the Exchange Control Division, with delegated authority exercised by Authorised Dealers pursuant to the Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1961.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. South Africa has no standalone EMI licence: acceptance of deposits from the public is reserved to banks under the Banks Act 90 of 1990, and non-bank payment service providers operate under the National Payment System Act 78 of 1998 and the FAIS Act 37 of 2002 through bank sponsorship. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud, etc.) can process cross-border payments only as payment initiation and settlement agents routed through a South African licensed clearing bank; they cannot lawfully hold the company's funds on deposit or replace a local transactional account. Exchange-control rules require export proceeds to be received in South Africa within 180 days, which forces the entity to maintain a qualifying local bank account or FCA. A fintech/EMI channel is therefore viable only as a supplementary disbursement rail for low-value supplier invoices, with all receipts ultimately washed through the local Authorised Dealer account to satisfy FinSurv reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct investment into the (Pty) Ltd is unrestricted and routed by SWIFT MT103 to the entity's ZAR current account or FCA at the Authorised Dealer, with the remittance instruction citing purpose code 1000 (inward equity investment) and the company registration number. On clearance of funds the bank issues the Form A / AIS declaration, which the company must sign and return within 30 days; the endorsed AIS ticket evidences 'vesting' of foreign capital and is the legal pre-condition for future dividend remittance and capital repatriation. If funding is provided as shareholder debt, the foreign loan agreement must be lodged with the Authorised Dealer to obtain a foreign-loan approval permit stating the registered loan amount and servicing conditions. Concurrent filings: CIPC beneficial-ownership return; FICA verification of the share-consideration line in the bank statement; and, where the equity exceeds a bank-determined threshold, a board resolution recording the issue of shares. Non-compliance with the inward-declaration deadline impairs both the dividend pipeline and the credibility of subsequent FX hedging applications.Outbound trade settlements for imported components and e-bike production inputs move across two primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers executed on Form B (Outward International Transfer) with attachment of the commercial invoice, bill of lading/air waybill, and supplier contract; and (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued via MT700 by the local Authorised Dealer to the overseas beneficiary bank, with negotiation against documents (commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, insurance certificate, bill of lading) on a sight or usance basis. Every outward payment requires the signed Form B declaration, proof of the underlying imported goods, and the SARS customs import entry (SAD 500) evidencing duty and VAT compliance. Prepayments to suppliers are capped at bank-approved limits; prepayments exceeding ZAR 50,000 require either customs clearance evidence or a supplier-bank guarantee/confirmation.
