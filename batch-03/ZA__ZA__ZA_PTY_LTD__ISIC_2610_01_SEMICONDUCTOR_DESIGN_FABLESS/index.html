Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS) – eFiling portal at https://www.sarsefiling.co.za

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first register as a taxpayer with SARS via eFiling, using the IT77 (company registration) form or outsourcing to a registered tax practitioner. Required supporting documents include the Memorandum of Incorporation, proof of registered office address in South Africa, directors' particulars, and evidence of appointment of a resident public officer. SARS issues an income tax reference number and, where relevant, a VAT vendor number after verifying the entity's constitutional documents and operational presence.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must first register as a taxpayer with SARS via eFiling, using the IT77 (company registration) form or outsourcing to a registered tax practitioner. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under the South African Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, no check-the-box pass-through election is available for a (Pty) Ltd; the entity is taxed at corporate level. Where hybrid or cross-border mismatch arrangements exist, elections are limited to those prescribed under the OECD BEPS Action 2 recommendations as domesticated in Section 8I and 8J for hybrid instruments; no elective fiscal consolidation or group relief is available beyond Section 14(1) companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration threshold: ZAR 1,000,000 on aggregate taxable supplies over any 12-month rolling period; voluntary registration permitted from ZAR 50,000 where a continuing business exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Section 11 of the VAT Act applies to (a) the supply of movable goods exported by the vendor, or by the purchaser under the vendor's authority, under a sale agreement, to a destination outside South Africa; and (b) the supply of services physically performed outside South Africa, or for the direct benefit of non-residents who are outside South Africa at the time of supply (subject to circular measurable use and enjoyment rules). A fabless semiconductor design entity can claim zero-rating on exports of IP licences and design IP core deliverables only where the recipient is a non-resident and the contract evidences a supply of services connected to foreign jurisdiction without any use in South Africa.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500 – Bill 1, Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods (or, for services, a signed contractual agreement explicitly transferring the IP/licence outside South Africa)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of foreign currency payment received in South African banking channel

SARS operates a structured e-invoicing framework under the VAT Act and the Customs and Excise Act. Vendor invoices must contain prescribed particulars (e.g., VAT vendor number, invoice number, supply date, consideration). Real-time fiscal clearance is not yet mandatory for domestic B2B invoices; however, all VAT returns are filed electronically via eFiling, and for exported goods, the Customs Clearing House (SAD500) processes declarations electronically through SARS' external customs module. Digital stamping is not generally required, but the SARS eFiling channel is the sole recognised submission portal for tax and customs declarations.

SARS may audit export zero-rating claims under Section 74 of the VAT Act and the Customs Control Act. Non-compliance, including failure to retain the prescribed documentation, furnishing false export declarations, or failure to repatriate foreign consideration within the exchange-control deadline, triggers a full VAT clawback plus interest from the date of the original zero-rated supply. Criminal penalties include a fine or imprisonment of up to five years for intentional evasion; civil understatement penalties under Chapter 16 of the Tax Administration Act (up to 200% of a repayment or excessive claim) apply. SARS also has four-year prescription extended to five years where fraud or misrepresentation is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South African Rand (ZAR) is administratively convertible: current account items (trade in services, IP royalties, invoiced export receipts, bona fide operational payments) are freely transferable through an Authorized Dealer with proper supporting documentation, while capital account flows (non-resident equity subscriptions, disinvestments, foreign borrowings, capital repatriations) require either prior FinSurv approval or completion of regulated declarations. Profit and dividend remittance is lawful once the Authorized Dealer verifies 'own funds' (paid-up share capital plus accumulated realized profits) on Form A1; the statutory 20% dividend withholding tax (or treaty-reduced rate) must be withheld for non-resident shareholders. Cross-border IP royalty outflows are subject to a 15% withholding tax unless a double tax treaty reduces the rate, together with SARS section 23D deductibility limits and section 31 transfer-pricing scrutiny. Practical hurdles include the 180-day export receipt and 30-day surrender requirements, the FIC 5% beneficial-ownership disclosure duties, the requirement that the company maintain a physical South African registered office and a local authorized signatory, and the prohibition on speculative open FX positions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: South African Reserve Bank (SARB) - Financial Surveillance Department (FinSurv), acting under the delegated authority of the Minister of Finance conferred by the Currency and Exchanges Act, No. 9 of 1933, and the Exchange Control Regulations promulgated thereunder. The National Treasury retains policy oversight; Authorized Dealers (registered commercial banks) execute day-to-day exchange-control decisions.
