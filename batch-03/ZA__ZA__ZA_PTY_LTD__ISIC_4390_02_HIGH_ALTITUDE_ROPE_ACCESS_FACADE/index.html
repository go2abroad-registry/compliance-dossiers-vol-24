Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

South African Revenue Service (SARS); portal: www.sars.gov.za and SARS eFiling (secure online filing system)

E-government filing portal

A domestic (Pty) Ltd is first incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Within 21 business days of incorporation or commencing business, the company must apply to SARS for income-tax registration via eFiling (form ITREG or electronic equivalent). For VAT, the company must submit a VAT 101 application (or eFiling equivalent) to the applicable SARS branch. Foreign non-resident entities must appoint an SARS-recognised representative taxpayer. Supporting documents: CIPC registration certificate and Memorandum of Incorporation, directors' identity documents or passports, shareholders' details, physical registered address in South Africa, and banking details. SARS issues an Income Tax Reference Number and, on approval, a VAT registration number (10 digits for VAT vendors) with an assigned tax period.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident South African company taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate; distributions to non-resident shareholders attract Dividends Tax withheld at 20%) — A domestic (Pty) Ltd is first incorporated with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). Within 21 business days of incorporation or commencing business, the company must apply to SARS for income-tax registration via eFiling (form ITREG or electronic equivalent). For VAT, the company must submit a VAT 101 application (or eFiling equivalent) to the applicable SARS branch. Foreign non-resident entities must appoint an SARS-recognised representative taxpayer. Supporting documents: CIPC registration certificate and Memorandum of Incorporation, directors' identity documents or passports, shareholders' details, physical registered address in South Africa, and banking details. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident South African company taxed on worldwide income at the 27% corporate income tax rate; distributions to non-resident shareholders attract Dividends Tax withheld at 20%)

Available election forms

South Africa has no 'check-the-box' regime; corporate classification follows the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962. Relevant elections: (1) Small Business Corporation (s12E) reduced-rate status for a qualifying Pty Ltd whose shareholders are all natural persons; (2) voluntary VAT registration under s23(2) of the VAT Act where taxable turnover exceeds ZAR 50,000 but is below the ZAR 1,000,000 compulsory threshold; (3) VAT cash accounting (payments basis) election under s9B for vendors with taxable turnover under ZAR 2,500,000; (4) the OECD-aligned anti-hybrid rules (s23M) apply automatically with no remedial election; (5) for indirect taxes, grouping of connected companies under s50(3) of the VAT Act allows a single vendor registration only on SARS approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration: ZAR 1,000,000 of taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months under s23(1)(a); voluntary registration from ZAR 50,000 under s23(2); separate rule: foreign suppliers of electronic services are required to register without a threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods: zero-rated under s11(1)(a) of the VAT Act where goods are supplied to a non-resident and exported within 90 days after the date of supply, with documentary proof of export obtained within 12 months. Export services: zero-rated under s11(2)(l) where supplied to a non-resident and the services are not used or consumed in South Africa. EXCEPTION ALERT: services physically performed on, or connected to, immovable property situated in South Africa (including rope-access structural glass works) do NOT qualify for s11(2)(l) zero-rating, even if invoiced to a foreign non-resident client; such work is standard-rated at 15% VAT. Any qualifying exports must also satisfy the Exchange Control requirement that proceeds be received in foreign currency and evidenced by an Inward Remittance Certificate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD500) through the SARS Customs clear-and-pay process
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Road Manifest evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from an authorised foreign-exchange dealer
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • SARS proof-of-export documentation file retained for five years

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate currently applies in South Africa. SARS supports voluntary OECD-standard 'SARS Authenticated E-Invoicing' (ASEIC) under s20(6) of the VAT Act, with phased modernisation expected. All vendors must issue SARS-compliant tax invoices under s20 of the VAT Act reflecting the words 'Tax Invoice'; VAT returns are lodged via eFiling; export zero-rating is substantiated by documentary proof of physical export retained for audit.

SARS conducts risk-scored audits of export refunds and may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and claw back claimed input VAT. Statute-backed consequences include: assessment of 15% VAT on the full invoiced value; additional tax under s60 of the VAT Act of up to 200% where non-disclosure or misrepresentation is not bona fide; 10%-20% understatement penalties under ss222-223 of the Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011; market-rate interest; and potential criminal prosecution for tax fraud. Fraudulently obtained refunds are recoverable in full and refunds may be frozen pending audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Africa maintains a partially convertible currency. Current account payments by authorised dealers are generally permitted with supporting documentation. Capital account flows require either authorised dealer approval or SARB FinSurv approval for amounts exceeding delegated limits. Non-resident holding company profit remittances (dividends, branch profits, management fees) are permitted under exchange control provided the underlying capital has been duly reported and applicable South African withholding taxes (dividend tax or interest withholding) have been settled. No automatic central bank approval is required for routine dividend remittances from audited profits, but all capital repatriation must be recorded through authorised dealers for balance-of-payments statistics and may be subject to documentary verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exports of goods must be paid in full within 90 days of shipment (extension is possible only with valid commercial justification and prior SARB FinSurv authorisation). All export proceeds received in foreign currency must be converted into rand via an authorised dealer within 30 days of receipt, unless the exporter holds an approved Foreign Currency Account (FCA). Advance payments for goods not yet shipped must be deposited with the exporter's authorised dealer within a prescribed deadline and normally require a guarantee or confirmed order.

Central bank supervision: Financial Surveillance Department of the South African Reserve Bank (SARB FinSurv), acting under the Currency and Exchanges Act, 1933 and Exchange Control Regulations, 1961

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for inbound capital or operational liquidity. Offshore digital EMI accounts (e.g. multi-currency wallets not regulated by SARB FinSurv) cannot serve as authorised dealers for conversion of export proceeds into ZAR and cannot hold funds in trust for a South African resident without violating the Exchange Control Regulations. Global licensed EMIs may be used only for outbound administrative payments to foreign suppliers where the underlying transaction is supported by invoices and cleared via the authorised dealer; even then the bank flagship relationship remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Late filing: Administrative Non-Compliance Penalty (ANP) of up to ZAR 16,000 per month for large businesses and ZAR 4,000 per month for small companies, plus a daily penalty of up to ZAR 250 per day. Late payment: 10% late-payment penalty plus interest at the prescribed rate (Repo + 7 percentage points). Underestimation of provisional tax: 20% of the shortfall. Criminal default may attract a fine of up to ZAR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years.
Box E — Assessed Loss Set-Off Prior-year assessed loss brought forward and applied, limited to 80% of current-year taxable income. If the company's ownership changes in contemplation of a foreign acquisition, the loss set-off may be denied under the s.20A anti-loss-trading rules. Late filing: Administrative Non-Compliance Penalty (ANP) of up to ZAR 16,000 per month for large businesses and ZAR 4,000 per month for small companies, plus a daily penalty of up to ZAR 250 per day. Late payment: 10% late-payment penalty plus interest at the prescribed rate (Repo + 7 percentage points). Underestimation of provisional tax: 20% of the shortfall. Criminal default may attract a fine of up to ZAR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years.
Box F — Taxable Income and Tax Payable Taxable income computed after set-offs and capital gain inclusion (80% of net capital gain); tax applied at 27% (or the SBC graduated scale); rebates and credits deducted. Apply the SBC rate only if all shareholders are natural persons; a corporate or non-resident-owned entity is taxed at a flat 27%. Late filing: Administrative Non-Compliance Penalty (ANP) of up to ZAR 16,000 per month for large businesses and ZAR 4,000 per month for small companies, plus a daily penalty of up to ZAR 250 per day. Late payment: 10% late-payment penalty plus interest at the prescribed rate (Repo + 7 percentage points). Underestimation of provisional tax: 20% of the shortfall. Criminal default may attract a fine of up to ZAR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years.
Box G — Provisional Tax Credits (IRP6) First and second provisional payments credited against the assessed tax; any third top-up payment credited. Non-resident shareholders cannot bear the company's provisional tax liability; underestimation of taxable income by more than ZAR 1 million or 20% triggers a 20% estimate penalty. Late filing: Administrative Non-Compliance Penalty (ANP) of up to ZAR 16,000 per month for large businesses and ZAR 4,000 per month for small companies, plus a daily penalty of up to ZAR 250 per day. Late payment: 10% late-payment penalty plus interest at the prescribed rate (Repo + 7 percentage points). Underestimation of provisional tax: 20% of the shortfall. Criminal default may attract a fine of up to ZAR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years.
Box H — Dividend Withholding Reconciliation Declared dividends to shareholders, identification of resident vs non-resident beneficial owners, and DWT withheld at 20%. Withhold 20% from dividends to non-residents unless a valid declaration and tax residency certificate are on file for treaty-exempt (e.g., 10% participation) relief; failure to withhold makes the company liable for the tax. Late filing: Administrative Non-Compliance Penalty (ANP) of up to ZAR 16,000 per month for large businesses and ZAR 4,000 per month for small companies, plus a daily penalty of up to ZAR 250 per day. Late payment: 10% late-payment penalty plus interest at the prescribed rate (Repo + 7 percentage points). Underestimation of provisional tax: 20% of the shortfall. Criminal default may attract a fine of up to ZAR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Section 31 ITA regulates all cross-border related-party transactions on an arm's-length basis. Mandatory contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (TP policy, Local File, Master File) is required where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds ZAR 100 million in a year of assessment; below the threshold, SARS may still demand documentation in an audit, and any related-party transaction must be supported by written pricing evidence (TNMM is widely used).

Economic substance scope: South Africa does not maintain a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but SARS applies OECD BEPS substance-over-form principles, the general anti-avoidance rule (s.103), and anti-treaty-shopping rules; a company claiming treaty benefits or foreign tax credits must demonstrate real hiring decisions, local employees, physical premises, bank accounts, and business purpose, failing which SARS can reconstruct transactions, deny deductions, or treat the entity as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Understatement penalty of 100%–200% of the shortfall for intentional and intentional-after-audit conduct (s.222–223 TAA); interest at the prescribed rate; criminal conviction — fine up to ZAR 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years per offence (s.235 TAA), plus fraud prosecution at common law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false ITR14 declarations can be convicted in their personal capacity; SARS may pursue civil restitution and, under s.162 TAA, obtain a warrant of arrest or departure-ban order where there is evidence the director will leave South Africa to evade payment.
Shell Company / Conduit / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: SARS reconstruction under s.103 (GAAR): disallowance of fee deductions, recharacterization of payments as deemed dividends, 75%–200% understatement penalties, denial of treaty benefits and foreign tax credits; controlled foreign company attribution under s.9D may apply in reverse flows.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the company is a facade; directors face civil restitution for misapplied funds and, in criminal cases, travel bans, disqualification, and imprisonment for fraud.
AML / FICA and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: FICA sanctions: fines up to ZAR 10 million and/or imprisonment up to 5 years per offence for accountable institutions; money-laundering convictions attract imprisonment up to 15 years; CIPC non-compliance with beneficial-ownership obligations attracts fines up to ZAR 500,000 plus daily default penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Public Officer can be prosecuted individually; SARS reports suspicious transactions to the Financial Intelligence Centre, and directors involved in laundering contract proceeds face criminal asset forfeiture and custodial sentences.
Non-Payment of Withheld Taxes / Reckless Trading

Statutory sanction: Directors' personal liability for un-remitted PAYE, SDL and UIF under s.234 TAA; 10% late-payment penalty and prescribed-rate interest on all un-remitted amounts; delinquency declaration under the Companies Act 2008 bars directors from holding office for 7 years or more.

Director personal liability: S.234 makes directors jointly and severally liable without a court order; SARS can sue the director's personal assets, obtain an attachment order, and apply for a departure-restriction order to prevent the director from leaving South Africa while tax debts persist.
