Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Samoa (successor to the Samoa Tax and Customs Administration); official government tax portal and e-services: www.samoagovt.ws

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned proprietary high-frequency trading vehicle is incorporated as an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1987 via the Samoa International Companies Registry (Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Labour). The Registrar issues a Certificate of Incorporation containing the IC registration number; because the IC automatically holds statutory tax-exempt status, no income tax TIN is issued. If the IC ever undertakes non-exempt domestic taxable supplies or elects to become taxable, it must file the MfR application for a Tax Identification Number and VAGST registration under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act 2003, supported by the certificate of incorporation, registered agent details and a written description of trading activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

An International Company may elect under the International Companies (Amendment) Act 1997 to become a taxable company by filing written notice with the Ministry for Revenue, thereby exiting the automatic exemption from income tax, VAGST and stamp duty. Samoa has no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elective regime; entity classification is determined statutorily by the International Companies Act and administratively by the Commissioner of the Ministry for Revenue, with no OECD hybrid branch elections available.

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAGST Act 2003, exported goods and services supplied to non-residents for consumption wholly outside Samoa are zero-rated. Algorithmic market-making and proprietary HFT execution fees charged to offshore counterparties qualify where the supplier is VAGST-registered, the services are physically performed from Samoa, no Samoan land or goods are involved, and written export evidence is retained. A tax-exempt IC cannot issue 0% invoices or reclaim input VAGST; zero-rating applies only after a valid election to be taxable and VAGST registration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA electronic declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or original Air Waybill evidencing physical export (for goods) or executed offshore service contract (for HFT services)
  • Certificate of Incorporation and proof of IC status from the Samoa International Companies Registry
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT payment instruction evidencing receipt of foreign currency from the offshore counterparty
  • VAGST invoice and reconciliation schedule showing the 0% rate applied
  • Annual return and tax-exempt status confirmation from the Ministry for Revenue where applicable

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance API, or digital stamping mandate exists in Samoa; registered persons must issue compliant VAGST invoices, maintain records for 7 years, and produce them to the Ministry for Revenue electronically on demand.

The Ministry for Revenue audits zero-rated claims under the VAGST Act 2003; fraudulent export zero-rating or false customs declarations attract penalty tax of 150% of the tax evaded, late-payment interest, clawback of any wrongfully refunded VAGST, criminal liability including imprisonment for fraudulent conduct under the Crimes Act 2013, and deregistration of the offending supplier. The Customs Act 2014 separately imposes forfeiture and administrative fines for false export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a fixed exchange-rate regime pegged to a trade-weighted basket of currencies comprising the USD, NZD, AUD, EUR, CNY, and JPY. Current-account transactions (goods, services, interest, and dividend payments) are generally convertible through authorized dealer banks upon submission of underlying documentation. Capital-account transactions (direct investment, portfolio flows, loans, and capital repatriation) require prior case-by-case approval from the Central Bank of Samoa. Registered International Companies (ICs) under the International Companies Act 1987 are statutorily exempt from the Exchange Control Act 1970; accordingly, the IC enjoys unrestricted current- and capital-account convertibility, free remittance of profits, dividends, and capital, and full freedom to hold and settle in USD or any foreign currency, subject only to authorized-dealer AML/KYC protocols and CRS/FATCA reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For ordinary resident exporters, the Exchange Control Act 1970 mandates that export proceeds be received within the contracted payment period and in any event no later than three (3) months from the date of shipment or service completion, and that the foreign currency be offered for conversion to an authorized dealer bank as directed by the Central Bank. For the International Company engaged in algorithmic high-frequency trading, proprietary trading revenues constitute exported financial services; however, the statutory exchange-control exemption under the International Companies Act 1987 disapplies all surrender and conversion obligations. The IC may retain and deploy 100% of its USD trading revenues both onshore and offshore without repatriation or conversion mandates, provided the bank records the economic purpose code for statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; the Bank administers the Exchange Control Act 1970 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1971, supervises all licensed commercial banks, and hosts the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) responsible for AML/CFT supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Samoa has no standalone electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing framework; digital banking and money transmission services require a license under the Financial Institutions Act 1996 (as amended) and full compliance with the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie) may serve only as supplementary clearing channels, not as the principal banking relationship, because of transaction-velocity thresholds, restricted prime-broker funding rails, and incompatibility with ultra-low-latency market-making settlement. A Samoa International Company is legally permitted to maintain offshore accounts, provided beneficial-ownership filings under the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) remain current and no prohibited business is conducted with Samoa residents.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent shareholder's or investor's bank to the IC's USD Capital Escrow Account at a tier-1 Samoa commercial bank (ANZ or BSP). The remitter must route funds through the receiving bank's USD correspondent (ANZ's or BSP's New York correspondent) and include ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI (equity injection) or CAPT (capital payment), with a remittance narrative matching the executed capital injection agreement. The receiving bank verifies the remitter's KYC file before crediting; cleared funds are then released to the Operational Account by internal book transfer upon board-approved treasury mandate. For funding sourced from the IC's own offshore brokerage accounts, purpose code CRED is used and a reconciled statement of the originating brokerage account must be provided.Outbound trade settlements and margin disbursements are executed via (i) SWIFT MT103 messages through the tier-1 bank's correspondent network to the prime broker's settlement bank, with final delivery on Fedwire for US-based counterparties; and (ii) direct exchange clearing rails (Direct_Exchange_Clearing), whereby the IC settles direct clearing-house obligations through a clearing membership or sponsored clearing arrangement. For any ancillary physical-commodity cover trades, a Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600 may be issued through the bank's trade finance desk. All outbound USD wires require pre-funded balances in the Operational Account with no intraday overdraft facilities, a pre-validated beneficiary standing settlement list, and FIX/API trade confirmations archived for a minimum of five years. Late filing incurs a fine of WST 50 per month, up to a maximum of WST 500. Non-payment of the annual government fee may lead to automatic strike-off of the IC, restoration fees, and late payment surcharges imposed by the Samoa International Finance Authority.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No transfer pricing documentation threshold is separately prescribed for International Companies. For Samoa resident companies under the Income Tax Act, mandatory Local File and Master File documentation is expected when aggregate related-party cross-border transactions exceed WST 1,000,000 per annum.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 and the Economic Substance Regulations require an International Company carrying on a 'relevant activity' to demonstrate economic substance in Samoa, including adequate physical presence, no outsourcing of core income-generating activity, and submission of an annual economic substance declaration. Proprietary algorithmic high-frequency trading is in scope if conducted through a Samoa operation; otherwise the IC should record 'no relevant activity' and maintain sufficient evidence to rebut any tax authority challenge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Sham Sheltering

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of the IC as a resident Samoa company and reassessment of 27% corporate income tax plus interest and penalties of up to 100% of the underpaid tax; criminal fine and imprisonment under the Samoa Income Tax Act 2012.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise or conceal the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; criminal conviction attracts a term of imprisonment not exceeding 5 years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off of the IC and freezing of bank accounts; civil penalty of up to WST 100,000 under the Economic Substance Regulations; failure to remedy within 30 days may lead to loss of tax-exempt status.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the penalty if they certified the annual declaration knowing it to be false; may face travel bans and disqualification from corporate management in Samoa.
Anti-Money Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to WST 500,000 and monetary penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2018; seizure of trading assets and accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are personally criminally liable for culpable AML lapses; cooperation may reduce penalties, but knowledge of suspicious transactions attracts up to 10 years imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of taxable income to an arm's length level, imposition of a 20% adjustment penalty, and interest on the resulting underpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate transfer pricing disclosures may be personally liable for penalties in their capacity as preparers of tax returns; immunity applies only upon demonstrating reasonable care.
Prohibited Trading and Capital Repatriation Evasion

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the IC's tax-exempt status, imposition of 27% corporate income tax on all income derived from Samoa, and penalty equal to 100% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil when the IC is used for fraud or evasion; directors are personally liable for restitution, may be subject to international travel bans, and can be extradited to Samoa for criminal prosecution.
