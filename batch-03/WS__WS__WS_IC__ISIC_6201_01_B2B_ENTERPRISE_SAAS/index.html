Tax ID Application Infrastructure

E-government filing portal

International Companies are not automatically required to obtain a Samoan TIN because they are tax-exempt offshore entities. Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover for domestic supplies; International Companies deriving only offshore B2B SaaS revenue fall outside this threshold and are not required to register for VAGST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Samoa and to services supplied to a non-resident customer if the services are not performed in Samoa and are not otherwise used or consumed in Samoa. For B2B Enterprise SaaS, the International Company may claim 0% VAGST when the customer is a foreign enterprise, payment is received from outside Samoa, the software/platform is accessed offshore, and no local Samoan establishment is the user or beneficiary.

  • Customs Export Declaration (where physical goods are involved; for cross-border SaaS this is substituted by a service export declaration and client contract)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for physical exports; for digital services: signed B2B service agreement and offshore usage/service delivery records)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation showing receipt of foreign sales proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping mandate is currently applicable in Samoa. Taxpayers must issue VAGST invoices in a form acceptable to Samoa Tax Administration, retain complete records, and ensure invoices clearly identify the supplier, recipient, description of supply, and VAGST treatment. Zero-rated export invoices must carry a supporting export declaration or service export substantiation.

Zero-rating claims are scrutinised by Samoa Tax Administration through document review and audit. Misstated or fraudulent export claims trigger retrospective VAGST assessments at 10% on the undeclared supply, late payment penalties, evasion penalties of up to 100% of the tax undercharged, and possible prosecution under the Tax Administration Act 2015, including fines and imprisonment for deliberate tax fraud. An International Company without substantiating contracts, offshore payment evidence, and customer non-residency proof cannot sustain a 0% rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a restricted offshore currency; current-account transactions are generally free through authorized dealers with documentary support, while capital-account remittances (equity injection, shareholder loans, distributions) require CBS prior approval or an applicable exemption. The WS_IC International Company is statutorily prohibited from offering services to, or transacting with, Samoan residents and from operating in WST; therefore all treasury flows are USD-denominated in foreign-currency accounts, eliminating convertibility risk. Profit-remittance analysis: the IC, being incorporated under the International Companies Act and managed and controlled outside Samoa, is a non-resident company under the Income Tax Act 2012; dividends paid from non-resident-source SaaS revenue carry no Samoan income tax or withholding tax, provided the non-resident management and economic substance position is documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Samoa's Exchange Control framework requires domestic exporters to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days of shipment and historically applies a mandatory partial conversion (commonly 15-25%) into WST. The WS_IC SaaS enterprise is not treated as an exporter of goods by CBS practice because services are rendered entirely outside Samoa and settled in USD; the IC is granted a standing exemption permitting 100% retention of USD in its designated foreign-currency operating account. No conversion or surrender order applies where funds never enter the WST clearing system; however, the authorized dealer must file quarterly inflow aggregates with CBS under the External Transactions Reporting regime.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (Faletupe Tutotonu o Samoa) is the sole exchange-control authority, operating under the Exchange Control Act 1973 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1974, with delegated execution via banks licensed as Authorized Dealers in Samoa. The Financial Services Authority of Samoa separately administers the International Companies Act 1987 (as amended) and supervises the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility for the WS_IC is Medium for USD/EUR receivable collection via Stripe Billing and Wise Business, and Low for any WST-denominated or Samoa-resident-facing service. Samoa has no dedicated EMI licensing regime, and the CBS Payment and Settlement Systems Regulations require a licence for activities touching the domestic WST system; offshore EMIs that settle only in foreign currency into a licensed Samoa bank account do not require a Samoan PSP licence. Wise does not offer WST accounts and will require an FSA letter of good standing plus certified director ID; Stripe accepts Samoa-registered International Companies on its Rest-of-World program and remits USD via ACH/wire to the BSP Samoa USD account.

Deadline: · Frequency:

No tax filing deadline applies. The annual registry return is lodged each year within the anniversary window (generally the month of incorporation anniversary) as advised by the registered agent; the exact date is governed by the International Companies Act and the terms of the agent's engagement.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A (no Samoa transfer-pricing filing threshold applies to a tax-exempt International Company; the TP documentation obligation arises only for entities within the Samoa income tax net and is measured in Samoan tala SAT).

Economic substance scope: Samoa does not apply domestic Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) to ordinary International Companies, and no Samoan substance filing is required solely by the ICA. Nonetheless, the IC must maintain a registered agent and registered office in Samoa for good standing. Foreign tax authorities will apply their own substance doctrines: a B2B SaaS IC that holds invoicing and intangibles without employees, business premises, economic risk or active management is likely to be recharacterised as a shell or conduit, causing foreign CFC/GILTI, permanent establishment or general anti-avoidance adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / abuse of the International Company exemption

Statutory sanction: Loss of the exemption; the Revenue assesses the IC as if it were a locally taxable company on the full profits derived through Samoa, adds a shortfall penalty (up to 150% of the underpaid tax for wilful conduct) plus statutory late payment interest, and may seek deregistration for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or submitted false certifications can be made personally liable for the unpaid tax through veil-piercing, barred from managing any Samoa entity, and prosecuted criminally for false declaration where fraud is proven.
Shell company / absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Samoa itself imposes no tax; however, the U.S., EU and other foreign tax authorities may recharacterise the arrangements, reattribute the SaaS/embedded-licence profit under CFC/GILTI or business profits provisions, and impose accuracy-related penalties of 20%-40% plus interest. The arrangement may also trigger secondary transfer-pricing adjustments on inventory/IP transfers.

Director personal liability: Executives of the foreign parent group are at risk of personal penalty certifications, foreign criminal tax exposure, personal civil liability for tax collection, and travel restrictions where charges of tax evasion are filed.
AML / KYC non-compliance and undisclosed beneficial ownership

Statutory sanction: The Office of the Financial Intelligence Unit can impose freezing orders and monetary penalties on the IC; the licensed agent can be fined and suspended; persistent violations result in deregistration and reporting to the Samoa Police.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who obstruct KYC or knowingly use the IC to launder subscription revenue are guilty of an offence under the AMLCFT Act 2007; on conviction they face fine and imprisonment, in addition to disqualification from acting as directors and personal confiscation orders.
False annual returns and fraudulent invoicing

Statutory sanction: False statements to the Registrar constitute an offence under the International Companies Act; penalties include fines, compulsory strike-off, and repayment of any fees or distributions made while the condition existed.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent trading: directors are personally liable for consequential losses and are subject to restitution orders, disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment where fraud is established.
