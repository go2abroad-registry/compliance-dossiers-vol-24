Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry for Revenue (MfR), Samoa, successor agency to the Samoa Revenue Authority; national tax portal: TAXIDENT e-services at https://www.mfrsamoa.ws, covering TIN registration, VAGST returns, income tax and electronic payments; Samoa Customs operates within the MfR for export clearance purposes.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company obtains its statutory TIN through the following SOP: (1) incorporate and register the IC with the Registrar of International and Foreign Companies under the International Companies Act 1987, maintaining a registered office and licensed resident agent in Samoa; (2) submit the prescribed TIN application through the TAXIDENT portal or via a licensed Samoan tax agent, attaching the certified certificate of incorporation, registered office and resident agent confirmations, board resolution authorising the tax representative, and the register of directors and shareholders; (3) file a declaration of offshore business scope to obtain the IC tax exemption certificate, which exempts the entity from income tax and VAGST on all offshore-derived business; (4) apply separately for VAGST registration only where the entity conducts a Samoan taxable activity or exceeds the WST 100,000 taxable-supply threshold, noting that an exempt offshore IC ordinarily remains outside the VAGST registration net; (5) receive the TIN certificate and activated TAXIDENT credentials within 5-10 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned International Company obtains its statutory TIN through the following SOP: (1) incorporate and register the IC with the Registrar of International and Foreign Companies under the International Companies Act 1987, maintaining a registered office and licensed resident agent in Samoa; (2) submit the prescribed TIN application through the TAXIDENT portal or via a licensed Samoan tax agent, attaching the certified certificate of incorporation, registered office and resident agent confirmations, board resolution authorising the tax representative, and the register of directors and shareholders; (3) file a declaration of offshore business scope to obtain the IC tax exemption certificate, which exempts the entity from income tax and VAGST on all offshore-derived business; (4) apply separately for VAGST registration only where the entity conducts a Samoan taxable activity or exceeds the WST 100,000 taxable-supply threshold, noting that an exempt offshore IC ordinarily remains outside the VAGST registration net; (5) receive the TIN certificate and activated TAXIDENT credentials within 5-10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Samoa has no US-style check-the-box classification; entity taxation follows the International Companies Act 1987 and the Income Tax Act 2015. Available statutory elections include: (a) an election by an IC to waive its exempt status and be assessed as an ordinary Samoan resident company, applying Samoa's territorial source rules which tax only Samoan-source income; (b) an election to form a VAGST group of related companies where a Samoan taxable activity exists; and (c) elections connected with controlled foreign company attribution where the IC owns reportable foreign interests. No hybrid mismatch election is available. Samoa applies general anti-avoidance and substance-based principles to entities claiming offshore exemption, and for US federal tax purposes the Samoa IC is a non-elective per se corporation that cannot choose pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 100,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory registration; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. A Tax_Exempt_Zone International Company with purely offshore SaaS revenue is not a taxable person, and no VAGST registration is triggered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAGST Act 1998, exports of goods and services may be zero-rated at 0%. A Samoan IC supplying micro-SaaS subscriptions to non-resident consumers qualifies where the software or IP is licensed and the digital service is delivered and consumed outside Samoa, the recipient is a non-resident at the time of supply, and no local Samoan supply nexus exists. Where a Merchant of Record such as Paddle or Lemon Squeezy acts as the supplier of record for destination-jurisdiction VAT, the IC's inward settlement payment from the Merchant of Record is treated as export-of-services consideration for Samoan VAGST purposes and may be zero-rated, supported by Merchant of Record settlement reports and subscriber-level sales evidence. The IC must retain proof that no revenue was derived from a Samoan domestic source and must not maintain a taxable presence in Samoa.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (CED) submitted through Samoa Customs/MfR for any physical goods movement
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or courier receipt evidencing exportation from Samoa
  • Commercial invoice and signed SaaS subscription/cloud services agreement identifying the non-resident recipient and offshore delivery of the digital services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement confirming receipt of export consideration in foreign currency into a Samoan licensed bank account
  • VAGST zero-rating support file including Merchant of Record settlement reports, KYC details of SaaS end-users, IP logs demonstrating offshore consumption, and the IC's tax exemption certificate

No real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate exists in Samoa. VAGST invoices must contain the statutory content prescribed under the VAGST Act 1998 and be retained for 7 years. Monthly VAGST returns and payments are filed electronically through the MfR TAXIDENT portal, which supports e-filing and e-payment but does not require API-integrated clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or SAF-T bulk reporting.

False export zero-rating or IC-exemption claims expose the entity to: (i) full retrospective clawback of the denied 15% VAGST with a reassessment window of up to 7 years (unlimited in cases of fraud or wilful evasion); (ii) penalty tax of up to 100% of the tax shortfall plus a late-payment surcharge of 5% per month capped at 25%, with interest; (iii) criminal liability for wilful evasion or fraudulent export documentation, carrying fines up to WST 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; and (iv) cancellation of the IC's tax-exemption certificate and compulsory deregistration. The MfR may also apply transfer-pricing and general anti-avoidance rules, and the Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act 2007 may attach financial-crime penalties where export documentation is fabricated to conceal undeclared offshore revenue.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) operates under a managed-float regime administered by the CBS. Current-account transactions for residents are generally convertible, but capital-account outflows and resident foreign-currency retention require CBS permits or licensed-dealer authorization. A Samoan International Company (IC) is statutorily treated as a non-resident, is prohibited from transacting in Tala with Samoan residents, and executes all treasury activity exclusively in a foreign functional currency (typically USD). Consequently, the IC enjoys full capital-account convertibility for inward shareholder equity or loan injections, and there are no CBS conversion permits or profit-remittance approvals applicable to dividends, director fees, or intercompany settlements originating from the IC's offshore USD accounts. The sole practical remittance hurdles are bank-level AML/beneficial-ownership scrutiny and correspondent-bank compliance, not CBS exchange-control authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBS Exchange Control Regulations, resident exporters must obtain declared export-license status, repatriate export proceeds to a licensed Samoan bank within the prescribed statutory window (normally ninety days from shipment), and convert a CBS-mandated percentage of the foreign proceeds into WST. A Samoan International Company is wholly exempt from export-proceeds surrender and mandatory conversion ratios because it is a non-resident foreign-currency entity generating SaaS subscription revenue exclusively through offshore Merchant-of-Record platforms (Paddle and Lemon Squeezy); therefore, no repatriation timeline, conversion obligation, or CBS surrender declaration applies to the IC treasury.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), Foreign Exchange Department, operating under the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1977; the offshore/international financial services sector is additionally supervised by the Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Samoa has no electronic-money-institution (EMI) or payment-service-provider licensing regime; the CBS does not recognize offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Business) as regulated deposit-taking or clearing institutions within Samoan jurisdiction. A Samoa IC may use a foreign EMI purely as a collection-side account only where the EMI's own jurisdiction and KYC policy accept a Samoa-licensed registered agent, apostilled corporate documents, and non-resident IC beneficial-ownership declarations; however, most global EMIs restrict onboarding for Samoa-domiciled entities or impose enhanced due diligence that materially delays payouts. The prudent blueprint is to use onshore commercial bank USD accounts as the primary treasury spine and permit Paddle/Lemon Squeezy payout settlements directly into those accounts, with no offshore EMI dependency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol requires the non-resident shareholder to originate a SWIFT MT103 (preferably GPST-tracked) from its home-bank account in the same legal entity name to the Samoa IC's USD operational account at BSP or Pacifika Bank. The wire instruction must carry a coherent purpose code aligned to CBS balance-of-payments classification, e.g., equity investment in an international company, direct investment for share subscription, or intercompany loan. The supporting verification bundle should include the IC board resolution authorizing share allotment or loan drawdown, signed subscription agreement or loan agreement, share register entry, allotment ledger, and a source-of-funds letter from the remitting bank. No CBS approval is required because the IC is non-resident, but the Samoan receiving bank will apply standard AML screening on the remitter, so the remitter's name and country of residence should match the disclosed beneficial-ownership schedule.All outbound settlements from the Samoa IC are executed in USD through SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 Cover only for nostro-refinancing) via the Samoan bank's global correspondent network in New York, Sydney, or Suva. For a micro-SaaS treasury, trade-related disbursements consist only of software-vendor payments (cloud hosting, licensing, developer subcontractors), platform fees, legal/accounting retainers, and profit distributions to the shareholder; commercial letter-of-credit rails are not applicable and would generate unnecessary KYC friction for ISIC 6201 digital services. Paddle and Lemon Squeezy settlement inflows arrive as net aggregated USD transfers supported by platform settlement reports; the IC treasury should reconcile each settlement against MoR statements and retain transaction-level export documentation despite the absence of Samoan physical-export formalities.Offshore USD/WST Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) executed with a global investment bank or licensed broker outside Samoa, permissible for a non-resident IC without CBS registration because the contract settles in USD offshore and does not involve a WST-denominated Samoan resident account.Cross-currency swap (USD vs WST) under an ISDA Master Agreement with a non-resident counterparty, permissible where no settlement occurs through a Samoan resident bank and the WST leg is deliverable only outside Samoa.Deliverable USD/WST forward contract with a CBS-licensed dealer is technically permissible for documented commercial exposure but is practically unavailable to International Companies and is not recommended given the IC's non-resident status and statutory prohibition on Tala transactions with Samoan residents; the pragmatic hedge is full USD natural hedging with immediate USD settlement via Paddle/Lemon Squeezy collection.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27% standard corporate income tax (CIT) for resident Samoa companies under the Income Tax Act 2015; 0% headline CIT for an International Company (IC) registered under the International Companies Act 1987, which operates in a statutory tax-exempt zone.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 2015 on dividends paid by taxable Samoa companies; 0% withholding tax on dividends, capital distributions, and share buybacks paid by an International Company under the International Companies Act 1987 exemption.

Due within 30 calendar days after each anniversary of incorporation; the annual government fee is payable on the same due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: WST 1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions (revenue plus expenses) above which Samoa TP documentation, including a Local File and Master File aligned with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, must be contemporaneously maintained and filed with the Samoa Ministry of Revenue upon request; below the threshold an exempt IC must still retain arm's-length pricing evidence for the IC's international credit and payment obligations.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (International Companies) Act 2019 applies to ICs conducting relevant activities, which includes IP holding and cross-border software licensing; a micro-SaaS deriving subscription revenue from overseas customers falls within scope and must demonstrate core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Samoa: locally managed decision-making, qualified staff resident in Samoa, operating premises, and expenditure proportionate to income; a purely passive IP-holder with zero presence is automatically presumed non-compliant and an annual economic substance declaration must be filed with the Registrar together with the annual return.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / abusive reliance on the IC exemption

Statutory sanction: Loss of the IC tax exemption, reassessment of derived global income at 27% CIT, monthly late-payment interest, and penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded under the Income Tax Act 2015; additionally a fine up to WST 1,000,000 for breach of the foreign-business licence condition of the International Companies Act 1987.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the IC is used as a sham; directors become jointly and severally liable for unpaid CIT and penalties, are exposed to departure prohibition orders (travel bans) issued by the Samoa Ministry of Revenue, and face imprisonment of up to 7 years for tax fraud.
Shell company / economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: A penalty of WST 250,000 per relevant activity plus WST 25,000 per month of continuing default under the Economic Substance (International Companies) Act 2019; the Registrar may cancel IC registration and revoke the tax exemption, resulting in full retrospective taxation at 27% CIT.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false economic substance declarations are personally liable to fines up to WST 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; veil-piercing applies where the IC is a sham, and a struck-off IC cannot be reinstated without court order.
AML / beneficial ownership and due diligence failure

Statutory sanction: Fines up to WST 300,000 for the company and WST 150,000 for each responsible officer; revocation of the registered agent's licence can void the IC's standing and cause automatic striking-off, and correspondent banks may blacklist the IC, terminating Paddle or Lemon Squeezy payout flow.

Director personal liability: Directors face disqualification orders, personal travel bans, and criminal prosecution for money laundering or concealment of the UBO; confiscation of personal assets proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2007.
Registry default / late annual renewal

Statutory sanction: WST 500 fixed late penalty plus WST 50 per day of continued default; automatic strike-off after 6 months with assets vesting in the Registrar, and any trading while struck off attracts fines up to WST 100,000.

Director personal liability: After strike-off, directors remain personally liable for debts contracted during the default, cannot reuse the company name for 5 years, and any asset distribution during the strike-off period is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment up to 3 years and personal restitution to creditors.
