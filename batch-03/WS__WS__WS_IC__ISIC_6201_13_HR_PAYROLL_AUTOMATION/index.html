Competent corporate registry

Samoa International Finance Authority (SIFA), International Companies Registry, Apia, Samoa; SIFA is the statutory regulator established under the International Finance Authority Act 2012.

Governing statute

International Companies Act 1987 (Samoa), as amended by the International Companies Amendment Acts 2015 and 2020; incorporation provisions at ss. 4-7 and 44-49; s. 8 (name restrictions); s. 33 (registered office); s. 38 (registered agent); ss. 50-62 (directors and secretary). Subsidiary delegated legislation: International Companies (Fees) Regulations and SIFA AML/CFT Rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form INC-1 – Application for Incorporation of an International Company, accompanied by executed Memorandum and Articles of Association; the Registry issues the Certificate of Incorporation of an International Company on approval.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 11 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. The Memorandum must declare authorized share capital (customarily USD 100,000) and at least one share at par value USD 1.00 must be issued to the subscriber. The USD 700 government fee covers authorized capital up to USD 1,000,000. No escrow deposit or paid-up capital funding requirement applies under the International Companies Act 1987.

Funding & unlock path: If a physical Samoa office is opened, obtain a Fire Clearance Certificate from the Samoa National Fire Services Authority under the Fire and Emergency Services Act; secure building compliance approval from the Ministry of Works, Transport and Infrastructure (MWTI) under the Building Code of Samoa; and register as an employer with the Accident Compensation Corporation (Samoa) under the Accident Compensation Act 1983 for occupational injury coverage.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital. The Memorandum must declare authorized share capital (customarily USD 100,000) and at least one share at par value USD 1.00 must be issued to the subscriber. The USD 700 government fee covers authorized capital up to USD 1,000,000. No escrow deposit or paid-up capital funding requirement applies under the International Companies Act 1987.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A Samoa International Company is statutorily exempt from income tax and is not required to obtain a standard tax identification number. For optional VAGST registration, the company must submit the VAGST registration application (Form VAGST-1) to the Samoa Revenue Authority together with its International Company certificate of incorporation, certificate of registered office, local registered agent details, and estimated annual turnover declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None - International Companies are automatically exempt under the International Companies Act 1987; no check-the-box, hybrid mismatch, or elective tax classification regime is available under Samoan law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

WST 50,000 annual taxable turnover threshold (mandatory registration); voluntary registration available at lower turnover for certain persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated at 0% VAGST applies to (i) tangible goods exported from Samoa under a contract of sale and removed from Samoa by the supplier; and (ii) services performed in Samoa that are supplied to a non-resident customer located outside Samoa, provided the services are not directly related to land in Samoa or goods situated in Samoa at the time of supply, and do not involve offshore gambling or gaming.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Samoa Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX credit record evidencing payment in the required currency
  • Tax invoice issued under VAGST Act showing 0% rate and export basis

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists. Samoa VAGST relies on conventional invoicing; standard VAT invoices may be issued manually or electronically, but no government API clearance or e-invoicing validation system is currently enforced.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims are subject to VAGST reassessment, interest accrual, and penalties under the Value Added Goods and Services Tax Act. Knowingly providing false export documentation can attract fines up to WST 50,000 and/or imprisonment. The Samoa Revenue Authority may retrospectively claw back input tax credits and zero-rating benefits for up to 8 years in cases of wilful non-disclosure or deliberate misstatement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Samoan Tala (WST) is a managed-float currency officially quoted against a trade-weighted basket of major trading-partner currencies, and Samoa has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, so current-account payments are freely convertible without CBS permit. Capital-account transactions by resident domestic companies require prior CBS approval through an authorized dealer, and profit remittance by resident non-IC companies to non-residents is subject to application and, where applicable, 15% non-resident withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 2012. The WS_IC (an International Company under the International Companies Act 1987, as amended, 'ICA') is statutorily exempt from Samoan exchange control and from all Samoan taxes, which removes the CBS approval step and the tax-clearance hurdle entirely; the IC may hold and freely transfer USD/EUR/AUD/NZD accounts with no convertibility restriction, provided it does not carry on business in Samoa with Samoan residents, does not hold interests in Samoan land, and keeps full beneficial-ownership records with its SIFSA-licensed registered agent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to the WS_IC: because the IC is exempt from the Exchange Control Act 1976, 100% of export receipts and SaaS subscription revenues may be retained in foreign currency, held offshore, or remitted to the IC's multi-currency account at a CBS-licensed authorized dealer without conversion to WST. For completeness, domestic Samoan goods exporters (resident companies, not ICs) operate under CBS exchange-control directions requiring repatriation of export proceeds through an authorized dealer within the prescribed period commonly fixed at 6 months from the date of export, and CBS may direct the authorized dealer to purchase a specified portion of those proceeds in WST; no fixed statutory conversion percentage is published, and the obligation does not extend to the WS_IC.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Samoa (CBS), established under the Central Bank of Samoa Act 1984; CBS administers Samoa's exchange control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1976 through CBS-licensed authorized dealers, supervises all licensed financial institutions, and houses the Samoa Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) established under the Money Laundering Prevention Act 2007.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM as a supplementary payments rail, not as the primary treasury or escrow bank. A properly licensed offshore EMI (e.g., FCA-, ASIC- or EU-supervised electronic money institution) may hold USD/EUR/AUD payment accounts for the WS_IC and execute high-volume payroll-disbursement rails to global client employees and subcontractors where correspondent-bank access of Samoan banks is limited. Legal constraints: (i) no offshore EMI may take deposits from or lend to Samoan residents or maintain WST-denominated customer accounts unless separately CBS-licensed; (ii) an EMI is not a CBS authorized dealer, so it cannot provide FX conversion or document the exchange-control exemption; (iii) all escrow, capital-injection, and hedge settlement flows must remain at a CBS-licensed authorized dealer; (iv) the EMI account must be disclosed to the Samoan bank during onboarding to maintain a coherent flow-of-funds audit trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all equity injections by SWIFT MT103 from each shareholder's own account to the WS_IC multi-currency account at a CBS-licensed authorized dealer (BSP Samoa or ANZ Samoa) in USD/EUR with remittance narrative 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION - WS_IC - ISIC 6201 PAYROLL PLATFORM'. No CBS exchange-control permit and no Foreign Investment Certificate is required because the ICA exempts the IC from the Exchange Control Act 1976 and the IC is prohibited from domestic business activity; the receiving bank will nevertheless require the allotment board resolution, share-register extract, UBO declaration, and source-of-funds letter prior to crediting. The use of an offshore EMI as an interim receiving vehicle is disfavored for capital injection because the Samoan bank must see a clean remitter-to-beneficiary equity chain to avoid reclassification of the credit as third-party liability.Cross-border outflows (software licensing, cloud-hosting fees, subcontractor coding fees, payroll disbursement to global client employees where the IC is the payer, and group management charges) clear by SWIFT MT103 corporate payment or MT202/MT202 COV bank-to-bank cover in USD/EUR/AUD. For material implementation contracts, an irrevocable documentary credit issued by SWIFT MT700 (and amended by MT707) with a confirming bank is permissible where the counterparty requires LC security; standby LCs (MT760) may back software-delivery obligations. Client-facing payroll disbursements in the United States and the Eurozone execute through the platform's Direct Debit/ACH rails at the offshore EMI or regional processor, with net treasury settlement swept to the Samoan account via SWIFT MT103; the Samoan bank is used for the IC's own corporate outflows and FX/hedge settlement. Domestic WST disbursements (registered-agent fees, SIFSA annual fees, CBS-related administrative charges) settle through the CBS-operated national payment system in WST from a dedicated WST sub-account, either by direct debit or EFT instruction; the IC must pre-fund the WST sub-account by minimal FX conversion and is not expected to maintain WST balances beyond administrative needs.Deliverable outright forwards (USD/WST, EUR/USD, USD/AUD and AUD/WST) executed with onshore CBS-licensed authorized dealers, documented under a standard ISDA or bank master FX agreement; no CBS pre-approval is required for the WS_IC by virtue of the ICA exchange-control exemption.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and USD/WST) for term-matching of USD-denominated infrastructure costs against EUR/USD-denominated subscription receivables, documented under ISDA 2002 with CSA collateral arrangements and a board-approved non-speculative treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) on WST via offshore bank desks for net WST exposure hedging only; permissible in principle under the IC exemption and subject to counterparty credit-line availability, given the thin onshore deliverable WST forward market.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory TP documentation threshold applies to a tax-exempt IC. For taxable entities, the general transfer pricing documentation threshold is WST 20 million in annual turnover or related-party transactions exceeding WST 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Samoa's Economic Substance (International Companies) Regulations require tax-exempt ICs carrying on relevant activities (including software/SaaS operations potentially classified as intangible property or service centre activities) to demonstrate economic substance in Samoa or a qualifying jurisdiction, including board management, adequate staff, and expenditure. Compliance is evidenced through the annual IC registry return.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Exemption Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disqualification of exempt status for the relevant year, assessment at the general 27% corporate rate, plus penalty tax of 20% and late payment interest at 1% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax if they knowingly enabled the misreport; civil restitution and personal liability orders apply.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Economic Substance Regulations, monetary penalty up to WST 500,000; possible compulsory strike-off; automatic exchange of information with partner tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally penalised for failing to appoint a qualified local director; travel bans and personal fines may be imposed.
AML / CTF and Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with the International Companies Act leads to penalties of up to WST 100,000 and forfeiture of limited liability; criminal prosecution under the Financial Intelligence Unit Act carries imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners involved in money laundering face asset confiscation, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (if exemption lost)

Statutory sanction: Assessment of additional tax at 27%, plus late-filing penalty of 20% of the adjusted profit; interest accrues at 1.5% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in the pricing decision may be jointly liable for the tax adjustment and separate criminal penalties for falsifying documents.
