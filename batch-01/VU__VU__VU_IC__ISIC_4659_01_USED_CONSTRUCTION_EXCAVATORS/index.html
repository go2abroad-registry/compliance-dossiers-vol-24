Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue (VCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official portal: https://customsinlandrevenue.gov.vu. Corporate registry and International Company (IC) administration: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), https://vfsc.vu. Customs declarations processed through the ASYCUDA World electronic single window.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Appoint a VFSC-licensed registered agent to incorporate an International Company under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]; the agent files the Memorandum and Articles of Association with the VFSC Registry. Step 2: Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and compliance certificate (typically within 24 hours). Step 3: A tax-exempt IC trading exclusively offshore receives automatic exemption under IC Act s.3 and is not required to obtain a VAT TIN or file tax returns. Step 4: If acting as importer/exporter of record in Vanuatu, obtain a customs code via the ASYCUDA World registration process. Step 5: Exceptionally, if taxable supplies were to be made in Vanuatu without VFSC approval (a regulatory breach), an 8-digit TIN and VAT registration would be applied for through the VCIR Taxpayer Registration Application within 21 days of exceeding the VT 10,000,000 annual turnover threshold. Step 6: File the annual VFSC return through the registered agent (administrative compliance only, not a tax filing).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (statutory zero-tax regime for International Companies; territorial restriction applies - no business with Vanuatu residents, no local real estate except approved administrative premises; foreign-source trading income, capital gains, and distributions remain outside the Vanuatu tax net entirely).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime exists in Vanuatu because no general income tax law is in force. The operative elections are: (a) incorporation election - shareholders elect the International Companies Act [Cap 222] regime to obtain the irrevocable statutory tax exemption; (b) voluntary VAT registration under s.29(2) VAT Act for persons below the mandatory threshold, useful for recovering input VAT on zero-rated export activities; (c) election to zero-rate certain services supplied to non-residents under the VAT Act First Schedule; and (d) customs transit/bond election under the Customs Act No. 14 of 2013 for re-export of used construction machinery through Vanuatu ports without VAT or duty. Vanuatu has no double tax treaties; treaty-shopping and hybrid mismatch concepts are therefore not applicable, but substance documentation remains prudent for correspondent banking and SWIFT LC facilitation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 (10 million vatu) annual taxable turnover; registration is mandatory within 21 days of exceeding the threshold; non-resident suppliers without a Vanuatu fixed establishment are generally outside the VAT net except for supplies physically enjoyed in Vanuatu.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% applies to goods physically removed from Vanuatu within 28 days of supply (or such longer period as the Commissioner allows) under the VAT Act First Schedule. Offshore services (trading facilitation, logistics, brokerage, inspection coordination for used hydraulic earthmovers and rollers) supplied to a non-resident outside Vanuatu at the time of supply, and not enjoyed in Vanuatu, are equally zero-rated. Eligibility requires an active VAT registration (mandatory or voluntary), a valid tax invoice, and documentary proof of export. Input VAT incurred on local costs supporting the export (storage, handling, freight, surveying) is creditable under s.17 and refundable under s.33 VAT Act. An unregistered IC trading goods directly from foreign suppliers to foreign buyers is outside the Vanuatu VAT system altogether, and re-export of used machinery through Vanuatu in customs transit/bond is not a taxable supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration in ASYCUDA World with customs officer exit endorsement
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill) evidencing foreign port of discharge and overseas consignee
  • Inward Remittance Advice/Certificate from the receiving bank evidencing settlement of export proceeds (banking documentary practice; no statutory exchange control exists in Vanuatu)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • VAT-compliant export tax invoice and, where applicable, container/haulage tracking reports evidencing physical removal

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API e-invoicing mandate applies. VAT-registered suppliers issue paper or PDF tax invoices containing the prescribed content under s.12 VAT Act (supplier identity, TIN, serial number, date, description, tax-exclusive consideration, VAT amount and rate). VAT returns (Form VAT 100) are filed electronically through the VCIR portal; customs export entries are lodged electronically in ASYCUDA World. Records must be retained for a minimum of 7 years under s.70 VAT Act.

Vanuatu law imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline and maintains no exchange controls; the 180-day parameter reflects standard export settlement practice and bank documentary policy rather than a statutory cap. Fraudulent export zero-rating or refund claims constitute an offence under s.82 VAT Act, punishable by imprisonment of up to 2 years and/or monetary fines at the court's discretion, together with recovery of the evaded tax, a penalty of up to 25% of the unpaid amount, and 2% per month default interest under s.75 VAT Act. Incorrect customs declarations attract penalties under the Customs Act No. 14 of 2013 of up to three times the value of the goods. The Commissioner may retrospectively claw back refunds, amend assessments, cancel registration, and refer the matter to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit under the AML/CFT framework; an IC breaching the no-resident-business covenant additionally risks VFSC deregistration and forfeiture of the s.3 statutory exemption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The VUV floats in a thin onshore FX market. Resident capital-account outflows require RBV consent; non-resident International Companies (ICs) that transact exclusively in foreign currencies and do not deal with Vanuatu residents are treated as external persons and exempt from capital-control restrictions under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222]. Dividend/profit remittances in USD require no RBV clearance, subject only to bank due diligence; any VUV-denominated transaction or resident payment would undermine the exemption.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory baseline for resident exporters: proceeds must be received within 6 months of export shipment and surrendered to an authorized dealer within 3 months of receipt, at a mandatory conversion ratio of 100% of net proceeds after directly attributed offshore expenses; deferred surrender requires specific RBV permission. The IC qualifies for automatic exemption as a non-resident foreign-currency trader, provided all used-machinery sale proceeds are settled outside Vanuatu in USD/AUD/EUR and no surrender obligation is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), acting under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 74] and the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act; routine administration is delegated to authorized dealers. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) maintains the IC register, UBO declarations, and AML/CFT supervision of registered agents.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low. Vanuatu lacks a dedicated e-money licensing regime; offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not RBV-authorized dealers and may support only ancillary USD collection and FX execution, never sole settlement. Third-country EMI balances must survive the correspondent 'chain of custody' review of the Vanuatu bank; any crypto-linked or unlicensed wallet facilitator is categorically incompatible with VFSC AML expectations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VUV 0 — Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing statute, no thin-capitalisation rule, and no statutory documentation threshold; the prudent non-resident trader is nonetheless recommended to prepare OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation whenever related-party transactions involving the purchase or onward sale of used excavators exceed VUV 50,000,000 (approximately USD 400,000) in any annual period, in order to pre-empt the tax administration of the counterparty jurisdiction.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) and does not require an International Company to prove payroll, premises, or core-income-generating activities within Vanuatu. The IC Act nevertheless prohibits onshore business without a ministerial licence, and the VFSC applies a beneficial-ownership due-diligence standard. OECD, EU listing, and FATF evaluations continue to scrutinise shell-structure risk, so practical substance — a local registered agent, a Vanuatu-registered office, properly executed board minutes, a Vanuatu bank account or a reputable offshore bank account in the IC's own name, and a complete commercial file for each excavator transaction (purchase invoice, bill of lading, inspection report, sale invoice, and SWIFT/LC payment route) — is required to avoid bank de-risking and cross-border re-characterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Offence under the International Companies Act (Cap 222) and the Penal Code: a fine not exceeding VUV 10,000,000 and a continuing daily default fine, together with an obligation to restore any tax advantage; in the foreign jurisdiction, the reconstructed corporate residence triggers full CIT, penalties up to 100–150% of the tax shortfall, and criminal tax-fraud charges.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced: directors and the controlling shareholder face personal civil liability for the full restitution sum, disqualification from serving as directors of any VFSC-regulated entity, asset-freeze orders, travel bans, and, upon extradition, imprisonment for tax offences in the shareholder's home country.
Shell Company and Abusive Use of the IC Exemption

Statutory sanction: Registry-level action by the VFSC: deregistration and strike-off of the IC, automatic loss of the legal personality that supports the limited-liability shield, and inclusion of the restructured owner on adverse international risk databases maintained by FATF, the OECD Global Forum, and correspondent banks.

Director personal liability: Deregistration retroactively exposes directors and shareholders to unlimited personal liability for the company's debts, including unpaid seller price of the excavators and unpaid LC reimbursements; directors also face a five-year ban from incorporation duties in Vanuatu and equivalent rejection in other offshore registries.
Anti-Money Laundering and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act (Vanuatu) and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act: money laundering is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment up to 20 years for individuals and a fine up to VUV 50,000,000 for a body corporate; the underlying transaction is voidable, and the funds are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner are jointly and severally liable for the confiscated value, face immediate travel bans and freezing orders issued by the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit, and, on conviction, custodial sentences with extradition risk to the buyer or seller jurisdiction.
Cross-Border Profit Stripping and Misalignment of Tax Residence

Statutory sanction: The foreign tax administration reallocates the profit under its controlled-foreign-company, transfer-pricing, or place-of-management rules; the IC is treated as tax-resident where central management and control actually lies, yielding back taxes, 100–200% accuracy-related penalties, and interest; Vanuatu levies no additional tax because the reallocated income is never subject to Vanuatu CIT.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign the intercompany sale and purchase agreements without arm's-length benchmarking are personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall as 'responsible persons'; anti-avoidance legislation in major trading-partner states permits criminal indictment of the directing mind and travel-banned cross-border enforcement against personal assets.
