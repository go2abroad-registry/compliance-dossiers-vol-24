Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) of the province where the head office is located, through its Business Registration Office and one-stop administrative division; online filing is conducted through the National Enterprise Registration Portal (dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn) under the Ministry of Planning and Investment (MPI).

Governing statute

Law on Enterprises of Viet Nam No. 59/2020/QH14 (Articles 25–27 on incorporation, Article 28 on legal representative, Article 49 on capital contribution deadline, Articles 74–76 on limited liability companies); Law on Investment of Viet Nam No. 61/2020/QH14 (Articles 36–39 on foreign investment registration and market access); Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP on Business Registration (Articles 12–33); Decree No. 31/2021/ND-CP on Implementation of the Law on Investment; Decree No. 23/2021/ND-CP on Employment Services (industry licensing condition).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Enterprise Registration (Giấy đề nghị đăng ký doanh nghiệp) as prescribed under Article 25 of Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP, accompanied by the company Charter, list of members, capital contribution schedule, and statutory attachments; for a foreign-invested LLC the dossier is filed after the issuance of the Investment Registration Certificate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tong Cuc Thue) under the Ministry of Finance of Viet Nam; online portal: https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn (National E-Tax Portal)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a foreign-invested limited liability company through the National Enterprise Registration Portal (https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn) or at the provincial Department of Planning and Investment to obtain an Enterprise Registration Certificate containing the preliminary tax identification number. Step 2: Activate the tax identity via the E-Tax Portal using the preliminary tax code, submit initial tax registration form (Form 05/DK-THGT), and obtain the definitive TIN. Step 3: Register the VAT calculation method (standard credit method), appoint a legal representative as the electronic transaction contact, and register an electronic tax invoice account with a digital signature certificate issued by an approved certificate authority. Step 4: Complete e-tax declaration registration and electronic invoice issuance authorization. Step 5: For foreign contractors without a domestic entity, the payer must register a Foreign Contractor Tax withholding account using Forms 02/GTGT or 05/GTGT on behalf of the non-resident before the first payment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income, with foreign tax credit relief; non-resident shareholders taxed at entity level; Export Processing Enterprises (EPEs) located in export processing zones enjoy territorial exemption on indirect taxes, but the LLC remains a fully taxable resident legal entity for corporate income tax

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Vietnamese tax law; the LLC is mandatorily classified as a taxable resident enterprise. Available statutory elections include: (i) election between the VAT credit method and the direct/gross-receipt method for eligible taxpayers, (ii) election by a foreign contractor to apply the Foreign Contractor Tax deduction method instead of the direct method (subject to conditions), (iii) election to file consolidated VAT returns with dependent branches, and (iv) election under domestic hybrid mismatch alignment rules (Decree 132/2020/ND-CP) to ensure deduction and timing consistency with the counterparty jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary VAT registration threshold for limited liability companies; all businesses with declared revenue must register for VAT and file monthly or quarterly returns. Only households and individuals not organized as enterprises with annual revenue below VND 100,000,000 may remain outside the VAT net; the effective threshold for LLCs is VND 0 of registration relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods and services supplied and consumed outside Viet Nam qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC and Decree 123/2020/ND-CP. For a Global EOR / staffing platform, the services must be supplied to a foreign organization or individual under a written contract, consumed entirely outside Viet Nam, and evidenced by payment received from abroad through a foreign-currency bank account. The taxpayer must maintain a complete service-performance file proving the offshore consumption and absence of domestic use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form HQ/2015/XK) bearing the electronic customs clearance barcode
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical export route (for physical exports)
  • Commercial Invoice issued in the name of the Vietnamese LLC
  • Export Sales Contract or Service Agreement signed with the foreign counterparty
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign-currency wire transfer proving payment from abroad
  • Zero-rated e-invoice with Tax Authority Invoice Code
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin (for physical goods) or Service Performance Certificate and detailed work log (for offshore services)

Mandatory e-invoicing since 1 July 2022 under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. Every e-invoice must contain a Tax Authority Invoice Code (ma so cua co quan thue) obtained in real time through the General Department of Taxation portal; XML invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority immediately at issuance with no physical stamping. For point-of-sale transactions, cash-register invoices with QR codes must be transmitted to the tax authority within 2 hours of the transaction. Real-time fiscal clearance uses an application-programming-interface (API) gateway and digital machine-readable stamping embedded in the invoice XML.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims are reassessed retrospectively with full VAT at 10%, plus late-payment interest at 0.03% per day on the unpaid amount, and administrative fines ranging from 1 to 3 times the evaded tax (theoretical cap of 3 times). Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline triggers foreign-exchange administrative penalties of up to 10% of the outstanding amount. Criminal liability may attach under Penal Code No. 100/2015/QH13 (Article 200) for tax evasion, with fines up to VND 4,500,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 20 years. Tax-exemption or zero-rating benefits may be clawed back for up to 10 years from the date of the fraudulent claim, and the taxpayer may be placed on the GDT high-risk audit watchlist.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

VND operates under a conditionally convertible regime. Current-account transactions, which include the receipt of Global Employer of Record (EOR) management fees and payroll-processing fees by the Vietnamese LLC from overseas clients, are convertible and freely transferable provided the underlying service agreements, invoices, bank receipts, and commercial documentation are maintained; no investment license approval is required for inward service revenue. Capital-account transactions, including shareholder capital contributions, intra-group loans, and equity increases, require a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at a licensed commercial bank under Circular No. 06/2019/TT-NHNN. Dividend and after-tax profit remittances are treated as capital-account transfers and are permitted only at year-end, after the annual audited financial statements are issued, the corporate income tax (CIT) finalization is completed, and the local tax authority confirms that no tax obligations remain outstanding; the remittance is executed through the DICA in tranches matching the finalized tax year. A pure offshore treasury model, in which service revenues are collected outside Vietnam via a non-licensed digital payment provider and local payroll is funded by informal remittance, is not permissible under SBV exchange-control law and would constitute unauthorized foreign-exchange activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vietnam does not impose general mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds for cross-border services. Under the amended foreign-exchange ordinance, specific mandatory-surrender obligations are limited to particular commodity groups such as crude oil and strategic minerals, with ratios set administratively by the SBV; these rules do not apply to an ISIC 7810 employment and staffing entity. The statutory obligation for the EOR company is to repatriate all service export receivables into a licensed foreign-currency account or the DICA held at an SBV-licensed credit institution, retaining the underlying contract, invoice, and non-cash settlement records for at least five years. Market practice and prudent bank risk policy require repatriation within 30 calendar days of the invoice due date; a materially longer lag without a documented bilateral payment plan will be treated by the bank as a suspicious disguised borrowing and will trigger enhanced AML review. There is no mandatory VND conversion ratio for service export receipts under the current framework.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV), the sole apex authority for foreign exchange administration, derives its mandate from the Law on the State Bank of Vietnam No. 46/2010/QH12 and the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange No. 28/2005/PL-UBTVQH11, as amended by Ordinance No. 05/2008/PL-UBTVQH12. The Ministry of Finance and the General Department of Taxation exercise supporting control over tax-clearance documentation required for cross-border profit repatriation; the licensing of banks, the operation of payment systems, and the authorization of all onshore foreign-exchange products rest exclusively with the SBV.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rated Low-Medium as a complementary rail only. Offshore digital EMIs and cross-border payment platforms, including Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Mercury, and similar providers, are not licensed to open or maintain local Vietnamese payment accounts for a resident corporate entity under Decree No. 101/2012/ND-CP on non-cash payments, as amended by Decree No. 70/2019/ND-CP, and Circular No. 39/2014/TT-NHNN on intermediary payment services. They cannot serve as the primary treasury or clearing vehicle for a Vietnamese LLC, cannot legally receive EOR revenue as a substitute for the DICA, and cannot disburse VND payroll to local employees. Lawful deployment is limited to the outbound payout function: the Vietnamese LLC may instruct its licensed onshore bank to convert USD/EUR surplus and fund a Wise or equivalent business account for disbursement of wages to overseas independent contractors residing outside Vietnam, provided the contractor agreements, tax declarations, and sanctions-screening records are retained. Use of a foreign EMI as the main inbound collection account would be classified as unauthorized intermediary payment service and a breach of SBV exchange-control regulations, exposing the entity to administrative sanctions and potential criminal liability under the Penal Code. The recommended architecture is a licensed local bank as the primary spine, with an EMI serving purely as an ancillary offshore contractor payout engine.

Offshore digital EMIs and cross-border payment platforms, including Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Mercury, and similar providers, are not licensed to open or maintain local Vietnamese payment accounts for a resident corporate entity under Decree No. 101/2012/ND-CP on non-cash payments, as amended by Decree No. 70/2019/ND-CP, and Circular No. 39/2014/TT-NHNN on intermediary payment services. They cannot serve as the primary treasury or clearing vehicle for a Vietnamese LLC, cannot legally receive EOR revenue as a substitute for the DICA, and cannot disburse VND payroll to local employees. Lawful deployment is limited to the outbound payout function: the Vietnamese LLC may instruct its licensed onshore bank to convert USD/EUR surplus and fund a Wise or equivalent business account for disbursement of wages to overseas independent contractors residing outside Vietnam, provided the contractor agreements, tax declarations, and sanctions-screening records are retained. Use of a foreign EMI as the main inbound collection account would be classified as unauthorized intermediary payment service and a breach of SBV exchange-control regulations, exposing the entity to administrative sanctions and potential criminal liability under the Penal Code. The recommended architecture is a licensed local bank as the primary spine, with an EMI serving purely as an ancillary offshore contractor payout engine.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% standard CIT rate for all resident companies under Vietnam's Law on Corporate Income Tax No. 14/2008/QH12, as amended by Law No. 32/2013/QH13.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at source under Vietnam's entity-level CIT system. A Vietnamese LLC may distribute after-tax profits to a foreign corporate shareholder without an additional dividend withholding tax, provided the statutory profit remission documents are completed through a licensed Vietnamese bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, compulsory Vietnamese transfer pricing documentation is triggered when the taxpayer's ordinary business revenue reaches VND 50 billion in a tax year and the aggregate value of related-party transactions reaches VND 30 billion in the same tax year. Even below these thresholds, documentation must be supplied immediately if the tax authority raises a TP audit query. The Master File is separately required when the ultimate parent group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds approximately VND 18,000 billion.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam has not enacted a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, Decree 132/2020/ND-CP applies the OECD-aligned substance-over-form and anti-avoidance principles requiring the Vietnamese EOR entity to receive profits consistent with the functions it performs, the assets it owns, and the risks it assumes. The LLC must maintain a physical office, locally employed HR and payroll tax specialists, actual employment contracts, Vietnamese payroll and social insurance records, and independent decision-making files. A letterbox entity that merely issues invoices and passes profits to a foreign platform affiliate will be re-characterised, disregarded, or denied deductions for lacking economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Revenue Under-Reporting and False Invoices

Statutory sanction: Administrative assessment, 20% supplementary penalty on under-declared tax, and 0.03% daily interest under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP. Criminal tax evasion under Article 200 of the Penal Code applies where evaded tax is from VND 100 million; fines may reach VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment may reach up to 7 years in serious or repeat cases.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, chief accountant, and any director who signs false CIT returns are jointly and severally liable for undercollected tax. Veil piercing is applied for instances of intentional misconduct, resulting in personal fines, asset confiscation, temporary exit bans, and imprisonment of the responsible directors.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the entity or re-characterise its transactions, attribute the entire platform profit to Vietnam, disallow related-party deductions, impose the 20% CIT adjustment plus penalties and interest, and refer the entity for revocation by the licensing authority and the Department of Planning and Investment.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who never exercise management functions cannot rely on the corporate veil. They are personally exposed to fines for false licensing declarations, administrative sanctioning, travel bans, disqualification from directorship, and criminal prosecution for intentional misrepresentation.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The General Department of Taxation will make a transfer-pricing adjustment, increase taxable income at the 20% CIT rate, apply a 20% penalty on the under-declared tax, charge 0.03% daily interest, and deny treaty relief under the Principal Purpose Test. The taxpayer is placed on the tax-authority risk blacklist and may be subjected to mandatory on-site TP inspections.

Director personal liability: Directors who choose, implement, and sign the intra-group pricing policy face personal liability for deficient TP documentation, withdrawal of treaty benefits, and collection from their personal assets where the pricing arrangement is found to be a sham.
Unlicensed EOR and Labour Supply Activity

Statutory sanction: The labour authority may impose administrative fines, confiscate unlawful service fees, and order immediate suspension of the unlicensed payroll platform. The tax authority independently re-characterises the illegal activity as ordinary Vietnamese-source business income, denies deductions for expenses connected to unlicensed operations, and restates all CIT and VAT liabilities.

Director personal liability: The legal representative who signs labour-supply contracts without the required licence is personally fined, disqualified from managing employment-service companies, subject to travel bans, and criminally exposed if the arrangement facilitated undeclared foreign workers or social insurance evasion.
AML and Cross-Border Payment Infrastructure Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative financial penalties under the Law on Anti-Money Laundering and Decree 116/2023/ND-CP, closure of bank and digital payment channels, revocation of licences, and confiscation of illicit proceeds. If the underlying funds derive from criminal activity, money laundering under Article 324 of the Penal Code may result in imprisonment of up to 15 years and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors identified as beneficial owners of unlawful payroll flows face criminal investigation, personal confiscation, exit bans, disqualification from managing regulated financial and payment institutions, and imprisonment for knowingly executing or authorising the unlawful cross-border transfers.
