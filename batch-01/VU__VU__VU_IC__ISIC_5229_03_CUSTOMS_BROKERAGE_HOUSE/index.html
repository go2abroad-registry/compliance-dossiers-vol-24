Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila; single-window agency for customs and inland revenue administration, official portal www.vrc.gov.vu. International Companies are incorporated and regulated by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC, www.vfsc.vu). No income tax registration is required because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, payroll tax, or dividend withholding tax.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company (IC) through a licensed VFSC registered agent and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and VFSC Certificate of Good Standing. Step 2: Apply to VRC for a Customs Broker Licence under the Customs Act and register as an ASYCUDA World user to obtain the electronic broker code required for all customs declarations. Step 3: Submit the standard Application for Taxpayer Registration to VRC to obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN), attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, certified identity documents of directors and shareholders, registered office evidence, and projected turnover statement. Step 4: If taxable turnover reaches or is expected to exceed VUV 4,000,000 in any 12-month period, mandatory VAT registration applies; voluntary VAT registration is permitted below this threshold. The VAT registration certificate states the VAT ID that must appear on tax invoices, customs entries, and brokerage documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Vanuatu International Company is statutorily exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and all entity-level duties under the International Companies Act [Cap 222], subject only to the fixed annual VFSC government fee. VAT at 15% is a consumption tax that applies only to taxable supplies made in or into Vanuatu; an IC acting as a customs broker for domestic clients must account for output VAT on local brokerage services. ICs transacting exclusively with non-resident clients, on goods and services consumed outside Vanuatu, remain outside the VAT net or qualify for zero-rating.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime because it imposes no entity-level or shareholder-level income tax. Available statutory mechanisms: (a) voluntary VAT registration below the VUV 4,000,000 threshold; (b) application to VFSC for authorization to carry on specified business in Vanuatu, which triggers local business licence and VAT compliance obligations; (c) election to apply 0% VAT to qualifying exported goods and export-connected services; and (d) continuation or redomiciliation of the IC into or out of Vanuatu under the International Companies Act on a tax-neutral basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 4,000,000 (four million vatu) of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period; mandatory registration within 30 days of exceeding the threshold; voluntary registration permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated where physically exported from Vanuatu within the period prescribed by the VAT Act and evidenced by an approved ASYCUDA export declaration. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient located outside Vanuatu with the benefit consumed outside Vanuatu, or when the services are directly connected to goods exported from Vanuatu, including customs brokerage, port handling, and international freight. A licensed IC customs brokerage house may therefore apply 0% VAT to clearance services rendered on export cargo, provided the brokerage contract, customs entry, and transport documents consistently evidence the export movement. Input VAT on overheads attributable to zero-rated exports is creditable and refundable by VRC. Caution: an IC that breaches the International Companies Act by carrying on domestic business with Vanuatu residents may be denied the offshore exemption and become fully exposed to local tax and licensing liabilities.

  • ASYCUDA World electronic Customs Export Declaration with customs release reference
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Vanuatu
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Brokerage services contract and zero-rated tax invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign currency receipt of export proceeds

No domestic electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping mandate is currently enforced in Vanuatu. Customs clearance is fully electronic: all import, export, and transit declarations must be filed by licensed brokers through the ASYCUDA World single-window system, which applies automated risk-scoring and issues electronic customs release references. The broker's TIN/VAT ID is cross-linked with the ASYCUDA broker code; tax invoices must be issued in the prescribed format and all supporting records retained for 7 years.

VRC conducts post-clearance audits and reconciles ASYCUDA export declarations against shipping manifests and foreign-exchange receipts reported by authorized banks. Fraudulent zero-rating or fictitious export claims trigger full retrospective clawback of input VAT credits and refunds, plus late-payment interest at 20% per annum. Criminal prosecution under the VAT Act may result in fines up to VUV 200,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; false customs declarations attract fines up to VUV 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years under the Customs Act, in addition to forfeiture of goods, suspension or cancellation of the customs broker licence, and disqualification of the VFSC registered agent.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations (1982); the Vatu (VUV) is convertible for all current international transactions and the RBV publishes a daily official mid-rate against a basket of AUD, USD, NZD, EUR, and JPY. Capital-account transfers by resident persons remain subject to Exchange Control Act declaration or approval, but a Vanuatu International Company (VU_IC) benefits from a statutory exchange-control exemption under the International Companies Act [CAP 222], s.11, permitting unrestricted multi-currency accounts and free remittance of dividends, royalties, and liquidation proceeds. The practical profit-repatriation hurdles are therefore documentary rather than statutory: trade-based money-laundering controls require the customs broker to evidence the underlying brokerage contract, the VCIR customs-broker license, and the VFSC Certificate of Good Standing before an authorized dealer releases large outward telegraphic transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Exchange Control Act [CAP 116] obliges resident exporters to ensure that foreign-currency export proceeds are paid to an authorized dealer in Vanuatu and, unless a specific exemption is granted, surrendered in exchange for Vatu within the timelines set by RBV Exchange Control Notices (in practice not later than 6 months after exportation). The mandatory conversion ratio for a nonexempt resident exporter is 100% of the export invoice value. However, an International Company is expressly exempt from exchange-control laws, so the VU_IC customs brokerage house is treated as a nonresident for this purpose: the compulsory surrender ratio is 0%, and export or brokerage-fee proceeds may be retained, converted, or remitted freely through its multi-currency accounts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) is the exclusive monetary and foreign-exchange authority, administering the Exchange Control Act [CAP 116] as the National Exchange Control Administrator pursuant to the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [CAP 125]. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) supervises the International Company under the International Companies Act [CAP 222] and licensed financial dealers under the Financial Dealers Act 2011; Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue (VCIR) licenses customs brokers under the Customs Act [CAP 145].

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for fully remote offshore electronic-money institutions; MEDIUM only where the EMI holds a VFSC Financial Dealer's Licence or an RBV banking licence with a physical Port Vila office and direct SWIFT/VATUS access. Legal constraints: the Banking Act [CAP 264] and the International Banking Act 2002 reserve deposit-taking to licensed banks; no domestic e-money issuance framework exists, so unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot lawfully maintain VUV payment accounts or act as the primary clearing bank for statutory customs duty payments, which must settle through an RBV-authorized commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital should be routed by the non-resident parent or beneficial owner via a single SWIFT MT103 to the designated operational account at ANZ, BSP, or BRED, quoting ISO 20022 purpose code 'ECAP' (Equity Capital) and, in the remittance narrative, 'Vanuatu International Company capital subscription - Customs Brokerage ISIC 5229'. The remitting bank must include the VFSC registration number and registered-agent name. The recipient RBV-authorized dealer will value-date the funds only after the KYC pack, board resolution, share certificate, and source-of-funds evidence (six months of bank statements plus a wealth declaration) are cleared. No prior RBV approval is required for an IC under the exchange-control exemption, but the receiving bank must submit a suspicious transaction report for any cash component exceeding the FIU threshold (VUV 1,000,000 equivalent).Customs duties and bonded-transit taxes are settled through the ASYCUDA World integrated payment module using bank-guaranteed debit authority with Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue; domestic VUV clearing executes in real time through the RBV-operated Vanuatu Automated Transfer System (VATUS), while cross-border freight, insurance, and foreign supplier payments use SWIFT MT103 (with MT202 for high-value AUD/USD/NZD/EUR/JPY transactions). Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) and demand guarantees may be issued by authorized dealer banks where the brokerage acts as importer-of-record or bond provider for bonded transit. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax at the national level; Vanuatu International Companies are exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax and stamp duty)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Vanuatu does not impose dividend withholding tax). Dividends declared and paid by a Vanuatu International Company to non-resident shareholders are not subject to Vanuatu tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

There is no CIT filing deadline in Vanuatu. VAT returns and payments are due within the period stated in the VAT Act, generally not later than one month after the close of the relevant VAT tax period. The VFSC Annual Return must be submitted within the period prescribed under the International Companies Act, generally tied to the annual anniversary of the company's registration date; late filing triggers VFSC late fees and possible strike-off action.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Not applicable - no CIT return Because Vanuatu does not levy corporate income tax, there are no income tax line items, chargeable income boxes, tax relief boxes or tax computation schedules to complete for an International Company. A non-resident owned Vanuatu IC does not file any income tax computation. The company should instead ensure its VFSC Annual Return identifies its registered agent, registered office, directors and ultimate beneficial owner as required by the International Companies Act and beneficial ownership rules. There is no penalty for failing to file a non-existent corporate income tax return. However, late VAT returns attract monetary penalties, default assessments, daily late-filing surcharges and interest on unpaid VAT. Failure to submit the VFSC Annual Return or pay the annual fee exposes the IC to late fees, deregistration, and liability of directors for improper dissolution.
VAT Return - Output Tax Box VAT at 12.5% is chargeable on taxable supplies of customs brokerage facilitation services supplied in Vanuatu to clients that are not otherwise exempt. The VAT return reflects gross taxable income, output VAT, VAT charged on expenses, VAT paid on imports relating to local services, and the net VAT payable to the Vanuatu VAT Office. A non-resident IC whose customs brokerage services are performed wholly offshore and which has no local establishment or VAT registration enters zero output VAT and files a formal explanation with the VAT Office if registered. If the IC is a licensed customs broker with a physical Vanuatu presence, local services are subject to VAT and must be declared. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold in Vanuatu vatu (VUV) exists. Vanuatu has no domestic transfer pricing legislation requiring Master File, Local File or Country-by-Country reporting. As a practical compliance matter, a customs brokerage IC should maintain intercompany contracts, pricing benchmarks, commission rates, cost allocations and income statements in VUV to establish arm's-length conduct if challenged by the VFSC under economic substance rules or by foreign tax authorities applying international transfer pricing standards.

Economic substance scope: The Vanuatu International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations apply to International Companies carrying on relevant activities: banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service center, intellectual property holding, and pure equity holding. Licensed customs brokerage as such is not automatically a listed 'relevant activity'; however, a customs brokerage IC that also offers logistics distribution, bonded transit management or agency services may cross into the distribution/service center category. A relevant IC must be directed and managed in Vanuatu, hold board meetings in Vanuatu, maintain adequate premises, employees and expenditure proportionate to core income-generating activities, and file the VFSC annual economic substance declaration. A customs broker that merely processes offshore paperwork without substance in Vanuatu will fail the economic substance test and can be struck off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and VAT / Customs Duty Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Vanuatu's Penal Code and Customs legal framework; court-imposed financial penalties; forfeiture and confiscation of the proceeds of crime; additional VAT assessments with interest and late-payment penalties; cancellation or suspension of the customs broker license.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct fraudulent customs declarations may be prosecuted as principals or accessories. The corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as a facade, leaving directors jointly and severally liable for evaded duties, damages and tax. Travel bans, surrender of passports, asset freezes and imprisonment up to 10 years may follow on conviction.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: VFSC strike-off, deregistration and potential dissolution of the IC; revocation of any tax exemption certificate; administrative penalties for non-filing; refusal, suspension or revocation of the customs broker license; inability to enforce contracts in Vanuatu courts.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally responsible for the company's liabilities if the company traded while insolvent or was used as an instrument of fraud. Directors may be disqualified, ordered to pay outstanding annual fees and liquidation costs, made subject to travel restrictions, and subjected to civil restitution claims by creditors and authorities.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Regulatory penalties and fines imposed by the Vanuatu FIU and VFSC; criminal penalties including imprisonment for wilful non-compliance; forfeiture of tainted property; revocation of the customs broker license; public disqualification from acting as a director.

Director personal liability: Individual directors, officers and compliance officers may be prosecuted for money laundering or failure to report suspicious transactions. Conviction may carry custodial sentences, personal monetary fines, travel bans and asset seizure; corporate veil-piercing is available where the company is a conduit for illicit proceeds.
Unlicensed Customs Brokerage and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties per customs infringement; seizure and forfeiture of goods; cancellation or non-renewal of the customs broker license; criminal prosecution for making or causing false declarations to Vanuatu Customs; contractual nullity of brokerage agreements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable where they exceed corporate authority, participate in forgery, or knowingly benefit from false declarations. The court may order civil restitution to the Government of Vanuatu, issue travel bans pending prosecution, and impose imprisonment in cases of deliberate fraudulent Customs evasion.
