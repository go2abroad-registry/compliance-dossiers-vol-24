Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila; official portal for customs declarations and tax e-services: https://www.customs.gov.vu

E-government filing portal

For a VU International Company (VU_IC): (1) incorporate through a VFSC-licensed registered agent and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and IC registration number; (2) as an offshore entity the IC is not normally required to hold a VRC TIN, because it is restricted from conducting onshore taxable business; (3) if the IC imports goods or otherwise needs a customs/VAT client code, file a VRC registration application accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered-agent certificate, director and shareholder due diligence, and a statutory declaration confirming that all activities are offshore; VRC will then assign a VAT/customs taxpayer number.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (Vanuatu International Company is statutorily exempt from domestic income tax, capital gains tax, and VAT on offshore supplies; the exemption is automatic under the International Companies Act 1993, not elective) — For a VU International Company (VU_IC): (1) incorporate through a VFSC-licensed registered agent and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and IC registration number; (2) as an offshore entity the IC is not normally required to hold a VRC TIN, because it is restricted from conducting onshore taxable business; (3) if the IC imports goods or otherwise needs a customs/VAT client code, file a VRC registration application accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered-agent certificate, director and shareholder due diligence, and a statutory declaration confirming that all activities are offshore; VRC will then assign a VAT/customs taxpayer number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Vanuatu International Company is statutorily exempt from domestic income tax, capital gains tax, and VAT on offshore supplies; the exemption is automatic under the International Companies Act 1993, not elective)

Available election forms

None. Vanuatu does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election or any hybrid mismatch election. The VU_IC tax-exempt status is fixed by statute and remains effective while the company does not carry on prohibited onshore activity or otherwise breach the International Companies Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; offshore royalty and licensing supplies received by the VU_IC from foreign franchisees are not charged to VAT, and a pure offshore IC is generally outside the compulsory domestic VAT net.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods and to services supplied to non-residents where the benefit is consumed outside Vanuatu, including offshore franchise royalty/licence fees paid by foreign franchisees to the VU_IC. The claim is available only where the IC can document that the service is supplied within an offshore arrangement and the supply is not consumed in Vanuatu; domestic onshore consumption is ineligible.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA-generated) covering the exported goods or the service underlying the royalty supply
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill/other transport document) evidencing physical movement of goods out of Vanuatu where goods are involved
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency export/royalty proceeds from the overseas franchisee

No real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate exists in Vanuatu. VAT-registered suppliers must issue prescribed tax invoices in paper or electronic form showing the VRC VAT number, description of supply, VAT-exclusive amount, and VAT charged; customs declarations are processed through ASYCUDA World, and VAT returns are filed on paper or via the VRC electronic channel without government clearance of individual invoices.

VRC may audit export documentation, inward remittances, and the offshore status of the IC. If a zero-rating claim is found to be false, unsubstantiated, or fraudulent, VRC may disallow the claim, claw back the VAT with interest, and impose statutory penalties under the VAT and Customs Acts, including monetary fines and custodial penalties for individuals who knowingly make false declarations; an IC engaging in prohibited onshore business may also lose its tax-exempt status retroactively.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. No surrender requirements, no repatriation restrictions, and no prior central bank approval applies to inbound capital contributions or outbound dividend, royalty, and trade payments. The only documentary obligations are bank-level AML/CFT source-of-funds checks. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject solely to the entity maintaining a valid tax exemption certificate and compliant contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Vanuatu. All export or licensing proceeds may be retained in VUV or in any foreign currency at the company's unrestricted discretion. The RBV does not require periodic surrender or conversion reporting; only standard transaction-level record keeping applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) — the sole monetary authority and banking regulator; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) supervises International Companies under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]. The RBV administers no separate foreign exchange administration; exchange controls were abolished in 1988.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium for royalty collection through offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury) as a supplementary clearing layer, but Low for capital escrow and primary treasury functions. Legal constraints: Vanuatu does not issue e-money institution licenses; offshore EMIs are not authorized by the RBV and cannot open VUV-denominated or Vanuatu domestic clearing accounts. Funds held with EMIs remain in foreign bank pools and may complicate VFSC substance reporting. Use an EMI only for low-value royalty aggregation, with automatic sweeping to an ANZ or BRED corporate account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold is codified for Vanuatu International Companies. Vanuatu has no general income or corporate tax regime, and ICs are exempt from income tax. Therefore, no local-file or master-file monetary threshold exists. However, if the IC is a franchise master-license holding entity deriving royalties from Chinese Malatang self-serve locations outside Vanuatu, the ultimate parent/beneficial owners should maintain OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation in the operating jurisdictions (e.g., China, Australia, New Zealand) to support arm's length royalty rates and avoid foreign-country transfer pricing adjustments.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu’s International Companies Act prohibits ICs from carrying on business with Vanuatu residents or owning land in Vanuatu. Vanuatu applies substance-related criteria for exchange of information and beneficial ownership transparency: the IC must maintain a registered agent in Vanuatu and adequate records, and the beneficial ownership of the franchise royalty stream must be identified. For foreign anti-avoidance purposes (e.g., China, Australia, EU), the IC must demonstrate real economic substance—substantive management, employees, office premises, and business decisions outside Vanuatu where the franchise operations occur. The Vanuatu ESR regime is not a separate annual filing, but Global Forum and beneficial ownership transparency standards apply; a pure mailbox/booking-company structure may be challenged as a conduit or sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion in Foreign Operating Jurisdictions

Statutory sanction: Under foreign law (e.g., China’s Corporate Income Tax Law, Australia’s ITAA 1936/1997, or OECD transfer pricing rules), the taxpayer faces back-taxes, fraud penalties of up to 100-300% of the tax evaded in certain jurisdictions, interest on underpaid tax, and the denial of treaty/deduction benefits. In Vanuatu, neither the IC Act nor the VFSC will protect the owner from foreign tax authority exchange-of-information requests.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be personally assessed for fraudulent tax evasion, have corporate veil pierced if the IC is found to be a mere sham/conduit, be subject to travel bans and asset freezing, and face criminal imprisonment in source jurisdictions for tax evasion or money laundering.
Prohibited Domestic Business Activity (Vanuatu IC Act)

Statutory sanction: Fine under the International Companies Act, revocation of the IC’s registration, strike-off, and liabilities for contracts entered into in Vanuatu. Government fees and prosecution costs may be imposed; the VFSC may disqualify the registered agent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow prohibited local business risk criminal penalties and civil restitution; in extreme cases, the court may impose director disqualification, personal fines, and imprisonment for carrying on an unlawful business under the IC Act.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu’s international transparency obligations permit response to foreign tax information exchange requests, and the IC may be struck off for failure to file beneficial ownership. In source jurisdictions, the royalty income may be re-attributed to the beneficial owner under controlled foreign company (CFC) rules, with penalties of 50-200% of the underpaid tax. On a global scale, financial institutions may file Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR) under AML laws for shell-company royalty flows.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for misrepresentation to VFSC or banks; the corporate veil may be pierced if the shell company is used as an instrument of fraud, leading to civil recovery from personal assets, travel restrictions, extradition risk, and imprisonment for money laundering or tax evasion.
AML / Sanctions Due Diligence

Statutory sanction: Under Vanuatu’s AML/CFT framework (Financial Service Providers (Amendment) Act, Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act) the registered agent may terminate the IC; VFSC may impose fines, revocation, and the IC’s bank accounts may be frozen. Foreign anti-money laundering fines for unexplained wealth may exceed US$1 million.

Director personal liability: Directors or UBOs found to control proceeds of crime face criminal prosecution, significant custodial sentences (up to 10-25 years in many jurisdictions), asset confiscation, and personal liability for the IC’s debts through fraudulent transfer piercing.
International Tax Transparency Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud and evasion penalties, 75-150% of the understated tax, plus interest; criminal tax perjury charges in global jurisdictions; mandatory disclosure penalties for unfiled foreign asset forms can reach hundreds of thousands of USD; possible denial of passport renewal and immediate departure prohibition.

Director personal liability: Individual beneficial owners (if directors) face imprisonment, personal fines, and punitive tax asset levies. The corporate structure provides no defense for tax fraud, and the Vanuatu IC’s limited liability is disregarded when the owner uses the IC to conceal income or evade tax.
