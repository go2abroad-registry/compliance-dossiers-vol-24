Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Funding & unlock path: If any physical production premise is established in Vanuatu (rare for an IC): (1) Fire Safety Certificate from the Vanuatu Fire and Emergency Services (VFES) covering sprinkler adequacy, egress, and emergency lighting; (2) structural load inspection and building permit from the relevant municipal authority; (3) workplace safety clearance from the Vanuatu Department of Labour under the Occupational Health and Safety Act No. 3 of 2017, including the submission of a risk assessment for thermoplastic/elastomer molding equipment; and (4) contractor waste-handling verification by DEPC. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Department of Customs and Inland Revenue, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official portal https://customs.gov.vu (ASYCUDA World single-window for customs). International Company incorporation and annual government fee compliance are administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) via its online e-filing portal.

E-government filing portal

Vanuatu levies no income tax, so no statutory income TIN is issued to International Companies. An International Company engaged in manufacturing and export obtains VAT identification exclusively by filing VAT Registration Form VAT-01 with the VRC once annual taxable turnover reaches or exceeds VUV 10,000,000 (or voluntarily when below the threshold). The supporting bundle comprises the printed or notarised Certificate of Incorporation, VFSC register extract confirming the registered agent and registered office, local registered agent consent, description of taxable activities and projected turnover, bank account or payment-rail details, and governing company documents. Goods export clearance additionally requires enrolment in ASYCUDA World as an exporter to obtain the Customs Exporter Code. All VFSC annual fees are settled online; no income-tax registration, tax clearance, or capital-import certificate is required for a standard offshore IC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid entity-classification election exists in Vanuatu law. An International Company may apply under section 67 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222] for deregistration or conversion to a domestic local company, which then falls within the ordinary Vanuatu VAT net but remains outside any income tax. For VAT, a person whose taxable turnover is below the statutory threshold may elect voluntary registration under section 6 of the Value Added Tax Act No. 12 of 1998. Export zero-rating requires the supplier to obtain Commissioner approval where the mandatory 28-day export window cannot be met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration applies when annual taxable turnover from taxable supplies made in Vanuatu equals or exceeds VUV 10,000,000 (ten million Vatu); voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under section 11 of the VAT Act, a supply of goods is zero-rated where the supplier exports, or causes the goods to be exported, from Vanuatu within 28 days after the date of supply (or a longer period allowed by the Commissioner) and retains the required documentary proof; zero-rating also applies to certain services supplied to non-resident recipients received entirely outside Vanuatu. For silicone stress-relief fidgets, the exporter must sell and consign goods to a non-resident purchaser, ensure physical export within the statutory window, claim the zero rate on the VAT return, and support the claim with the customs, transport, and remittance evidence described below.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate is in force in Vanuatu. Registered persons must issue a tax invoice, paper or electronic, containing the particulars prescribed by section 21 of the VAT Act: supplier name, VAT registration number, invoice date, description of supply, amount exclusive of VAT, VAT amount, and total consideration. Customs export declarations are processed electronically through ASYCUDA World, which serves as the sole digital clearance channel for exports.

The VRC may reassess and claw back all zero-rated output and input VAT credits where the documentary bundle is incomplete, the 28-day export window or Commissioner-approved extension is breached, or exported goods are diverted into domestic consumption. Fraudulent export zero-rating attracts the section 60 VAT Act late-payment penalty of 25% of the tax, plus monthly interest at 2%, monetary fines of up to VUV 5,000,000, imprisonment of up to three years under the VAT Act, and parallel money-laundering liability for falsified export or remittance documentation under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 60 of 2019. Vanuatu imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier is the evidentiary pipeline standard for bank remittance and export-proceeds substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a managed-float VUV with unrestricted current-account convertibility and de facto unrestricted capital-account convertibility for International Companies in Good Standing under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]. Non-resident ownership is 100% permissible; non-residents may hold foreign-currency and VUV accounts at authorized dealers. Profit, dividend, interest and capital repatriation require only administrative due diligence (board resolution, financial statements or tax-exemption certificate, VFSC good-standing certificate, annual fee payment) and statistical reporting to RBV via the bank; no exchange-control approval, quota, or onshore withholding tax applies to an exempt IC. Practical hurdles: correspondent-bank de-risking of small-island offshore jurisdictions, shallow onshore VUV money and FX markets, and reliance on USD/AUD as de facto settlement currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender ratio, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no obligation to convert export receipts into VUV. Exporters may retain foreign currency in external accounts or in on-island foreign-currency accounts at authorized dealers. Only balance-of-payments statistical reporting (Monthly International Transactions Reporting) through the dealing bank is mandatory. Recommended treasury policy: hold USD proceeds in the USD operating account and convert to VUV only for local operating spend to minimize bid-ask costs.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) — statutory monetary authority and administrator of the Exchange Control Act [Cap 51]; authorizes and supervises all on-island authorized dealers (commercial banks). The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) regulates the incorporation and ongoing licensing of International Companies and financial dealers, but does not administer FX policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low. Vanuatu has no dedicated e-money institution (EMI) licensing track; non-bank payment services fall under the Banking Act [Cap 62] or the Financial Dealers Act [Cap 70], both requiring a VFSC/RBV authorization that most offshore digital EMIs do not hold. Global EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex) generally decline Vanuatu-domiciled International Companies due to jurisdiction risk and the absence of on-island partner coverage. A VU IC may lawfully use an offshore EMI as a collection-only gateway for platform payouts, but proceeds must be swept periodically through an authorized dealing bank (ANZ/BSP) to satisfy Exchange Control Act [Cap 51] reporting and to preserve the on-island transactional audit trail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Vanuatu has no domestic transfer pricing legislation and therefore prescribes no VUV threshold for Local File or Master File documentation. A Vanuatu International Company is not obligated under local law to prepare transfer pricing documentation. However, if the entity transacts with group affiliates in jurisdictions applying OECD transfer pricing rules, the foreign tax authority may require the Vanuatu entity to provide an OECD-compliant analysis and benchmark study, especially where intercompany manufacturing, distribution, or royalty flows exceed the foreign jurisdiction's reporting threshold.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu's International Companies (Economic Substance) Act applies to International Companies undertaking relevant activities. Relevant activities include banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, banking and financing, leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centres, and pure equity holding. Manufacturing compression molding of food-grade silicone tactile fidget toys is not itself a listed relevant activity, but if the vanuatu entity receives revenue as a distribution centre, service centre, or regional headquarters for the brand, it must maintain physical presence, locally resident directors and staff, adequate premises, and annual VFSC economic substance reporting. A shell International Company without those substance indicators is immediately vulnerable to regulatory sanction and foreign anti-abuse recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company and Tax Exemption Abuse

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: deregistration, compulsory strike-off, and fines under the International Companies Act. Foreign jurisdictions: recharacterization of the International Company as a sham, retroactive denial of tax exemption, imposition of corporate income tax on the full global profit, withholding tax assessments, and civil penalties up to 100% or more of the tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for fraudulent trading and forced civil restitution of diverted profits. Courts in recipient jurisdictions may issue travel bans, asset freezes, and international arrest warrants; prolonged criminal tax evasion charges may result in imprisonment.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu economic substance penalties include substantial monetary fines, daily default penalties for continuing non-compliance, VFSC license cancellation, and compulsory dissolution. The VFSC may also exchange information with foreign tax authorities under Vanuatu TIEAs, exposing the group to foreign reassessment.

Director personal liability: Every director who signs a false economic substance declaration commits an offence under Vanuatu law and may be prosecuted personally, obtain a criminal record, face a travel ban, and be required to bear the costs of the regulatory investigation and any repatriation of assets.
AML / CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Vanuatu AML/CFT framework, offences may attract fines up to several million VUV, imprisonment of up to 15 years for money laundering, freezing orders, forfeiture of bank balances, and permanent revocation of the company's registration and bank relationship.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and beneficial owners are personally exposed to asset confiscation, extradition proceedings, travel bans, and custodial sentences where the authorities establish knowledge or reckless disregard of suspicious payment flows.
Transfer Pricing and Customs Valuation Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Foreign customs authorities may assess additional import duties, VAT/GST, anti-dumping duties, transfer pricing adjustments, interest, and penalties of 50% to 200% of the underpaid amount. Vanuatu does not tax the offshore profit, but it will not shield the group from source-country assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false customs declarations may be prosecuted for fraudulent evasion of duties in the importing country, may be banned from entering the jurisdiction, and may be ordered to personally restore any treasury benefit obtained; criminal imprisonment is possible in cases of invoicing fraud.
Unlicensed Onshore Activity and False VFSC Declaration

Statutory sanction: Offence under the International Companies Act punishable by fines, potential imprisonment of directors, revocation or refusal to renew the International Company registration, and compulsory strike-off. The VFSC may also refuse to issue any certificate of good standing needed for bank opening or tax exemption proof.

Director personal liability: Each director who knowingly permits unlicensed onshore activity is jointly and severally liable for the contracts and debts of the International Company in Vanuatu, may be disqualified from acting as a director, and may face criminal prosecution, travel restrictions, and imprisonment until trial or sentence.
