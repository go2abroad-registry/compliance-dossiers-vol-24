Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department (CIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Private Mail Bag 9075, Port Vila; VAT and TIN administration via the CIR VAT Office; company registry and International Company incorporation administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC); official portals include www.gov.vu and the ASYCUDA World customs clearance platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] through a VFSC-licensed registered agent; obtain Certificate of Incorporation and registered office in Port Vila. Step 2: If a Vanuatu VAT nexus arises, file the VAT Application for Registration (Form VAT 100/101) with the CIR, attaching certified copies of the Certificate of Incorporation, constitutional documents, directors' passports, registered office certificate, registered agent consent, and a bank reference; non-resident applicants may be required to appoint a local fiscal representative under the VAT Act. Step 3: The CIR issues a 12-digit V-TIN/VAT registration certificate within approximately 10-15 working days. Step 4: An IC engaged exclusively in offshore services to non-residents may lawfully remain unregistered and TIN-free, as no income tax return obligation exists; voluntary VAT registration is available to recover input VAT on local production costs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None for income tax: Vanuatu has no check-the-box, look-through, fiscal unity, or hybrid mismatch elections; the International Company is statutorily exempt from income tax, corporate tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty under Section 39 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222] and cannot elect to become taxable. Voluntary VAT registration is available under Section 5 of the VAT Act [Cap 270] for persons making or intending to make taxable supplies below the mandatory threshold, enabling input-credit recovery on zero-rated export services; no BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch or exit tax election has been enacted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies to any person carrying on a taxable activity with aggregate annual turnover exceeding VUV 10,000,000 (approximately USD 84,000) in a 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold with CIR approval. An International Company supplying digital subtitle and dubbing services exclusively to non-resident recipients generally falls outside the mandatory threshold because the supplies are zero-rated exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Section 9 of the VAT Act [Cap 270], supplies of exported goods and services supplied to non-resident persons located outside Vanuatu qualify for zero-rating where the CIR is satisfied that the goods were exported or the services were consumed outside Vanuatu. For the AI subtitle transcription and voice-cloning studio, digital post-production and audio dubbing services delivered over the internet to overseas streaming platforms, media houses, and dubbing agencies qualify as zero-rated exports where (i) the recipient is a non-resident not carrying on a taxable activity in Vanuatu, (ii) payment is received in foreign currency from overseas, (iii) the contract, creation, delivery, and acceptance of deliverables occur offshore, and (iv) the supplier retains contemporaneous documentary evidence of the offshore transaction. Services physically consumed inside Vanuatu, supplied to Vanuatu residents, or enjoyed within the territory are taxable at 15%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form C-Ex) processed and cleared in ASYCUDA World, required where physical deliverables such as master files, hard drives, scripts, or marketing materials are shipped
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of any tangible goods; not applicable where deliverance is purely digital
  • Commercial invoice and countersigned service contract identifying the non-resident recipient and confirming the offshore place of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 credit advice, or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency payment from the overseas customer
  • Digital proof-of-delivery record, delivery acceptance certificate, or client acceptance log for the subtitle and dubbing files

No mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Vanuatu. VAT returns are filed quarterly on paper or via the CIR e-tax portal; customs export declarations are processed through ASYCUDA World with electronic clearance at the border. Invoices must contain minimum statutory content (supplier name, TIN, description, tax rate, tax amount) under the VAT Regulations; there is no government-connected API or continuous transaction control mandate.

Not applicable - Vanuatu maintains no exchange control legislation, no statutory repatriation obligation, and no prescribed deadline; export proceeds may be retained offshore in any convertible currency. The CIR may raise a deficiency assessment within 4 years from the end of the relevant return period, extended to 8 years in cases of fraud or wilful evasion. Zero-rated export claims are disallowed when the taxpayer fails to produce the shipment, payment, and offshore-recipient evidence bundle; clawback assessments carry a default penalty up to 25% of the unpaid VAT plus ongoing late-payment interest, and the taxpayer's registration may be cancelled. Fraudulent or misleading export zero-rating claims constitute an offence under the VAT Act, punishable by significant fines, imprisonment, director disqualification, and criminal referral to the Office of the State Prosecutor.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is a freely issued, floating currency; Vanuatu operates an open capital account with no prior-approval regime for inward capital, FX conversion, or repatriation by International Companies. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted for cross-border services (AI subtitling, dubbing, post-production), and there are no statutory profit-remittance hurdles: International Companies are non-resident exempt entities and distributions attract no withholding tax. Practical constraints are structural rather than regulatory: the VUV interbank market is thin, correspondent-banking access is concentrated in Australian nostro banks, and offshore VUV positions are effectively non-deliverable; therefore treasury flows should be executed in AUD/USD with VUV balances minimized.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio exists under the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 37) or RBV prudential rules. Exporters and service providers may retain 100% of export and services receipts in designated foreign-currency accounts with authorized dealers and may convert at will. The only compliance obligations are (i) accurate recording in the dealer's external-transactions statistical return and (ii) retention of underlying service contracts and invoices to support source-of-funds verification; for AI subtitle services, platform settlement statements from Stripe/Wise or downstream aggregators must be reconcilable to client agreements and purchase orders.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), Port Vila, established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act No. 21 of 1988; the RBV is the central bank, monetary authority, banking supervisor, and the delegated foreign-exchange administration under the Exchange Control Act (Cap. 37). Registration and prudential oversight of International Companies is administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) under the International Companies Act 2008, with the RBV and VFSC jointly maintaining the AML/CFT supervisory framework and external-transaction reporting returns.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate for direct onboarding and settlement. Stripe does not offer acquiring to Vanuatu-registered entities, and Wise Business does not provide a native VUV local account; both can be routed only through a licensed payment facilitator or third-country EMI. Recommended construct: (i) hold the RBV-licensed ANZ or BSP account as the legal settlement spine; (ii) layer a regulated fintech EMI (Airwallex, Currenxie, or a multi-currency global account at a top-tier Singapore or Hong Kong bank) to capture Stripe/Wise receivables in USD/AUD; and (iii) sweep balances to the Vanuatu operational account via SWIFT MT103 on settlement-date cycles. Interposing a foreign EMI without disclosure to the Vanuatu bank triggers correspondent-bank de-risking and frozen-credit risk, so the EMI relationship must be declared in the account mandate and KYC file.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0.00% (Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no branch profits tax; an International Company (IC) is subject to no income-tax legislation at all — Vanuatu has not enacted an Income Tax Act).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no dividend withholding tax, no undistributed-profits tax, and no branch-repatriation tax. Dividends, distributions, and capital distributions paid by an International Company to any non-resident shareholder are free of Vanuatu tax and do not require a clearance certificate or remittance filing.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than 30 days after each anniversary of incorporation; the corresponding annual fee is payable by the anniversary date. A late fee accrues until filing and payment are completed.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly taxable period; return and full VAT payment due on or before the 15th day of the calendar month following the end of the taxable period.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 — Company name, registration number, and registered office Legal name of the International Company, its IC registration number, and the address of the registered office in Port Vila (must be the office of the licensed agent). The non-resident beneficial owner does not appear in the public register unless named as a director; the registered agent files all details and must be licensed under the Vanuatu Companies Act. Late submission of the Annual Return attracts a penalty of VUV 50,000 (approximately USD 415) per month or part of a month in addition to the annual fee. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Vanuatu transfer-pricing statute, Local File, or Master File obligation exists. The administratively recommended threshold for voluntary arm's-length documentation is VUV 100,000,000 (approximately USD 830,000) in aggregate related-party cross-border services revenue per financial year; above that level, a foreign audit or an OECD/EU enquiry is materially more likely to demand contemporaneous documentation, a group economic-substance file, and allocation of profit to value-generating employees and servers in the AI post-production workflow.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu has not enacted OECD/EU-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Nevertheless, the jurisdiction is monitored by the FATF, the OECD Global Forum, and the EU Code of Conduct Group; the VFSC requires the IC to maintain (i) a licensed registered agent in Port Vila, (ii) a physical registered office in Vanuatu, and (iii) adequate accounting records. An AI dubbing studio cannot rely on formal compliance: Stripe, Wise, and correspondent banks apply substance-based KYC, and foreign tax authorities will apply their own ESR/CBCR rules. The recommended substance blueprint for ISIC 5912 includes at least one resident director participating in board decisions, a Port Vila office, local bookkeeping/log archiving, employee or contractor agreements governed by Vanuatu law, and demonstrable control over the subtitle/voice-cloning IP conducted from records held in Vanuatu, even if heavy compute is executed on foreign cloud infrastructure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Foreign-Source Withholding Abuse

Statutory sanction: In Vanuatu: false declaration in the Annual Return or Business Licence application under the Penal Code (Cap 135) is an offence carrying a fine of up to VUV 1,000,000 (approximately USD 8,300) and imprisonment up to five years; the VFSC may additionally impose an administrative penalty of VUV 300,000 (approximately USD 2,490) for deceptive filings. In the source country: reassessment of the full gross revenue, denial of deductions, 100% gross-up assessments, and civil fraud penalties between 50% and 75% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the false declaration or knowingly direct the scheme are personally liable for civil restitution of the foreign tax avoided, may be disqualified by the VFSC for up to 10 years, and face extradition exposure in the source jurisdiction. The corporate veil is pierced because the IC is a sham; the shareholder's personal assets, including the AI voice models and subtitling catalogs, are reachable by foreign tax authorities.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Registrar will strike the company off the register for failure to provide details of effective management; the IC's contracts, including cloud SaaS subscriptions and licensing agreements, become voidable; the Business Licence is revoked; the IC loses limited-liability protection. Multilateral consequence: the entity is classified as a shell under OECD/EU action, triggering refusal of tax clearance, automatic CFC taxation in the shareholder's residence state, and closure of Stripe, Wise, and banking facilities due to de-risking by processors.

Director personal liability: After veil-piercing, shareholders and directors are jointly and severally liable for all IC debts; each director may be fined up to VUV 500,000 (approximately USD 4,150) and disqualified for 10 years. Non-resident principals are also exposed to travel bans issued by foreign tax courts and to local criminal proceedings once the VFSC refers the matter to the Public Prosecutor.
AML/CFT Violations in Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Under the Vanuatu Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing legislation, the IC and its registered agent face a fine of up to VUV 10,000,000 (approximately USD 83,000) and the responsible natural persons face imprisonment up to 10 years for money-laundering offences; VFSC administrative penalties for deficient AML systems reach VUV 500,000 (approximately USD 4,150) per breach.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner face civil asset-confiscation, freezing orders over the IC's IP, travel bans, and imprisonment. Regulators treat the AI voice-cloning and subtitle catalogs as confiscatable proceeds; personal liability attaches because the AML obligations attach to the beneficial owner, not only the nominal director, and the corporation's veil will not shield a principal who personally directed the payment structuring.
Statutory Filing Default and Business Licence Lapse

Statutory sanction: Compulsory strike-off by the Registrar; the company is dissolved, its assets vest in the state subject to claims, and its contracts become voidable at the counterparty's option. The cumulative financial sanction includes annual-return penalties of VUV 50,000 per month (approximately USD 415), a VUV 100,000 reinstatement fee (approximately USD 830), VRCS penalties of VUV 100,000 (approximately USD 830), and all accrued annual fees.

Director personal liability: The directors remain jointly and severally liable for debts incurred during the default period, including the unpaid fees of the registered agent and any VRCS assessments; a travel ban may be imposed by the VFSC pending clearance of arrears, and further imprisonment of up to two years is possible for trading after the company has been dissolved.
