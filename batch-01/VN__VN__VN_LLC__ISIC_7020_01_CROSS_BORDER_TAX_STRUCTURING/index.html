Business Registration Office under the provincial or municipal Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) of the jurisdiction where the registered office is located; Investment Registration Division of the same DPI for foreign-invested projects; National Business Registration Portal at https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn; National Public Service Portal for online submissions.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế) of the Ministry of Finance of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam; official e-tax and e-invoice portals: https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn and https://hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn; national business registration portal: https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn; local tax administration is carried out by provincial/sub-provincial Tax Departments (Cục Thuế) where the LLC has its head office.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishing a Vietnamese limited liability company must: (1) obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the competent Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) where foreign ownership triggers the investment registration procedure under the Law on Investment 2020; (2) file the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) application through DangKyKinhDoanh.Gov.Vn; (3) upon issuance of the ERC, the General Department of Taxation automatically issues a 13-digit enterprise tax identification number (MST) that is simultaneously the VAT identification number, with no separate TIN application required; (4) activate the enterprise's e-tax account at ThueDienTu.Gdt.Gov.Vn, appoint a legal representative and registered digital signature, and register for electronic tax declaration and payment; (5) register for e-invoicing on HoaDonDienTu.Gdt.Gov.Vn; and (6) file the CIT registration and, where the entity is a constituent entity of an MNE group, submit the OECD Pillar Two top-up tax registration particulars under Resolution 107/2023/QH15. A foreign contractor without a permanent establishment in Vietnam does not procure a Vietnamese TIN; instead the Vietnamese counterparty withholds Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) under Circular 103/2014/TT-BTC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Vietnamese LLC is a separate resident enterprise subject to corporate income tax at 20% on worldwide income. Vietnam does not recognize pass-through or transparent taxation for LLCs; the LLC itself is the taxpayer, with distributions to foreign shareholders subject to a separate 0% domestic CIT exemption for qualifying dividends and potential Foreign Contractor Tax on other remittances.

Available election forms

Vietnam has no U.S.-style check-the-box or general hybrid-mismatch election. Available statutory choices include: (i) an election for qualifying micro and small enterprises to apply a simplified presumptive CIT regime on a percentage of revenue under Decree 97/2023/ND-CP and related guidance; (ii) for non-resident foreign contractors only, an election between the Direct Method and the Deduction Method under Circular 103/2014/TT-BTC; (iii) an election to apply for an Advance Pricing Arrangement (APA) under the transfer pricing rules of Decree 132/2020/ND-CP; and (iv) for in-scope MNE groups, the election and application of the Vietnamese QDMTT (Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax) and IIR top-up tax regime under Resolution 107/2023/QH15 and Decree 139/2024/ND-CP. Vietnam also applies hybrid-mismatch-type defenses through the 30% EBITDA interest deduction cap, controlled-transaction substance requirements, and specific non-deductibility rules for intra-group payments lacking commercial substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold applies to a resident LLC: VAT registration is mandatory for every enterprise upon issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate, irrespective of revenue levels. For an individual or household business, the VAT Law sets a de minimis annual revenue threshold of VND 100,000,000, below which VAT is not imposed. A foreign contractor without a permanent establishment in Vietnam does not register for VAT; instead, VAT and CIT are withheld under the Foreign Contractor Tax mechanism, generally with no input VAT refund available under the Direct Method.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods and services qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 9 of Decree 219/2013/ND-CP and Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC when the following criteria are satisfied: (i) goods are physically exported through customs and covered by an electronic customs export declaration, or services are supplied to a foreign customer and consumed entirely outside Vietnam; (ii) the customer is a non-resident organization or individual with no Vietnamese business establishment; (iii) for consultancy services (including international tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory), the services must be produced, delivered, and used abroad, and the advisory deliverable must not be exploited in Vietnam by the Vietnamese LLC or any Vietnamese party; (iv) there is a valid international service agreement between the Vietnamese LLC and the offshore client; (v) payment is received in foreign currency through international banking channels and duly repatriated to Vietnam; and (vi) the exported service does not simultaneously benefit a Vietnamese resident establishment. A Vietnamese LLC providing cross-border advisory services to its offshore parent or a third-party foreign client may apply the 0% rate only where the substance of the service is demonstrably exported, not consumed in Vietnam.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for goods) cleared electronically through the VNACCS/VCIS customs system
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for goods; not applicable to pure service exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency deposit receipt issued by a licensed Vietnamese bank confirming receipt of export proceeds in Vietnam
  • International service or advisory agreement with the foreign customer, governing the cross-border supply of tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services
  • 0% VAT e-invoice carrying the GDT-compliant XML/QR code
  • Service completion certificate, final advisory report, and deliverables proving offshore consumption of the consultancy services
  • Certificate of corporate residency or other evidence establishing the foreign customer's non-Vietnamese status
  • Extract from the Vietnamese LLC's foreign-currency bank account showing receipt and date of export proceeds for the 180-day repatriation verification

Mandatory electronic invoicing applies under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC: every output VAT invoice must be created as an XML-format e-invoice through the GDT portal or an authorized e-invoice service provider, bear a compliant digital signature and QR code, and be transmitted to HoaDonDienTu.Gdt.Gov.Vn. Where the tax authority invoice code applies, the GDT performs real-time clearance by issuing a 10-digit or 12-digit verification code before the invoice becomes legally effective for tax purposes; otherwise, e-invoice data must be transmitted no later than the end of the next working day. Export goods are additionally cleared electronically through the VNACCS/VCIS customs system; service exports are substantiated through the 0% e-invoice and the statutory offshore-service documentation package.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict audit scrutiny. Absent compliant documentation or when payment is not received and repatriated within the statutory 180-day deadline, the tax authority may re-assess output VAT at 10%, disallow any input VAT refund claimed, clawback refunded amounts, and apply late-payment interest at 0.03% per day. Administrative penalties under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP range from fines equal to 20% of under-declared tax to up to 3 times the under-declared or evaded tax amount for deliberate fraud. Forgery of export invoices, customs declarations, international contract signatures, or bank remittance proofs triggers criminal liability under the Penal Code 2015 (Articles 200 and 341), with imprisonment up to 7 years for tax evasion involving, or causing tax loss of, VND 100,000,000 or more; all false refunds must be restored and the 10-year statutory record-keeping period remains subject to retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vietnamese Dong (VND) is a managed-floating, not fully convertible currency. Current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, royalties, consultancy fees) are freely transferable subject to underlying documentation, tax clearance, and anti-money-laundering verification. Capital-account transactions (FDI equity injections, intercompany loans, portfolio investments) are restricted and require prior registration through the Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) at an authorized commercial bank, the Investment Registration Certificate (IRC), and SBV approval for cross-border borrowings. Profit remittance is permitted annually only after publication of audited financial statements, full tax finalization, and satisfaction of the foreign-invested enterprise's ongoing disclosure obligations. VND has no meaningful offshore NDF or spot market, so cross-border settlement is effectively USD/EUR-denominated through licensed onshore banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated into Vietnam within 180 days from the date of customs clearance. The mandatory foreign-currency surrender ratio (historically 80% under Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN) was eliminated effective 2019 by Circular 06/2019/TT-NHNN; exporters may retain foreign currency in accounts at authorized commercial banks and freely convert subject to SBV quotations. The SBV retains authority to reintroduce mandatory surrender in balance-of-payments emergency conditions under the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange. For a cross-border tax-consulting entity, service receivables are classified as current-account earnings and must be credited to a foreign-currency or VND transaction account within the same 180-day timeline, with supporting export/export-of-services contracts.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam) — Foreign Exchange Control Department, administering the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange 2005, Decree 70/2014/ND-CP (as amended by Decree 100/2023/ND-CP), and Directive 04/2021/CT-NHNN on foreign exchange management.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for onshore VND/domestic clearing; Medium for offshore receivable collection only. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) do not hold a Vietnamese payment intermediary license under Decree 101/2012/ND-CP, cannot open VND settlement accounts, cannot host DICA structures, and cannot intermediate FDI capital contributions or statutory profit remittances. Their permissible use is limited to collection of consultancy fees in USD/EUR abroad; funds must be repatriated through an authorized onshore commercial bank with a compliant FX purpose code. They are therefore not viable as a primary treasury or clearing rail but may serve as a secondary receivable-assembly layer.

Politically Exposed Person (PEP) status of a shareholder or director.

Root cause: Politically Exposed Person (PEP) status of a shareholder or director.

Defense evidence: Signed PEP and source-of-wealth declarations; independent PEP screening report from a reputable due-diligence database; audited financial statements and tax returns evidencing legitimate income; engagement letters and pipeline contracts demonstrating genuine tax-structuring advisory revenue.

Root cause: Charter capital funding routed from related offshore entities lacking commercial substance.

Defense evidence: Signed share subscription agreement with a clear disbursement schedule; audited financial statements of the capital contributor; bank confirmation and statements of the remitting account; corporate-registry extract of the contributor; written board resolution explaining the economic purpose of the Vietnam investment.

Root cause: Statutory licensing gap for cross-border tax advisory services (ISIC 7020).

Defense evidence: Valid Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) and Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) reflecting ISIC 7020; tax agent certificate under Law on Tax Administration 38/2019/QH14 Article 102 where the service constitutes tax agency work; professional indemnity insurance; sample engagement letters showing non-regulated consultancy scope.

Defense evidence: Commercial rationale memorandum explaining cross-border fee economics; substance documentation (physical office lease, payroll records, board minutes); OFAC/EU/UN sanctions-list screening results; independent third-party AML review report; transaction-level audit trail of source and intended use of funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires (1) issuance of the IRC/ERC with an approved charter-capital schedule; (2) opening of the Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) at an authorized commercial bank within 30 days of IRC issuance; (3) remittance of charter capital via SWIFT MT103 with the standardized BPM6/ITRS FDI-equity purpose code (equity investment in direct-investment enterprises) and the IRC number quoted in the remittance field; (4) bank-side cross-border KYC and source-of-funds verification; (5) parking of funds in the DICA, with conversion to VND or retention in USD based on lawful operational needs; (6) matching of each withdrawal or conversion against the capital-contribution schedule registered with the DICA and the provincial Department of Planning and Investment; and (7) annual reconciliation with the State Bank of Vietnam's Foreign Exchange Control Department through the DICA reporting form.Clearing rails for outbound settlements: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct service payments, subcontractor fees, and disbursements above the USD 5,000 declaration threshold; (ii) irrevocable Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (at sight or usance) for procurement requiring transport or delivery evidence; (iii) SWIFT MT202 for interbank positioning of funds between the Vietnam treasury account and upstream correspondent banks; and (iv) domestic non-cash clearing (ACH-style interbank electronic payments) for VND-denominated operational expenses. All outbound FX payments require the underlying commercial contract, invoice, tax invoice, customs declaration (for goods), and, for dividend remittances, audited financial statements plus tax finalization (Form 02/ĐNTXU). Decree 23/2024/ND-CP prohibits single VND cash payments above VND 100 million, so all clearing must be non-cash.OTC FX forward contracts (VND vs USD/EUR) with maturity up to 365 days under Decree 92/2018/ND-CPFX swaps (spot component plus forward re-exchange) transacted with SBV-licensed authorized commercial banksForeign currency options (physically settled, underlying must be current-account trade-flow exposure)Cross-currency interest-rate swaps of floating/exchange VND and USD cash flows, available only from SBV-authorized derivative dealersNote: Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on VND are not permitted onshore; any offshore NDF hedging is outside the legal perimeter and cannot be booked through a Vietnam-licensed entity.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for onshore VND/domestic clearing; Medium for offshore receivable collection only. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) do not hold a Vietnamese payment intermediary license under Decree 101/2012/ND-CP, cannot open VND settlement accounts, cannot host DICA structures, and cannot intermediate FDI capital contributions or statutory profit remittances. Their permissible use is limited to collection of consultancy fees in USD/EUR abroad; funds must be repatriated through an authorized onshore commercial bank with a compliant FX purpose code. They are therefore not viable as a primary treasury or clearing rail but may serve as a secondary receivable-assembly layer.
