Competent corporate registry

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (GDT) of the Ministry of Finance of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam; national online portal: etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn (National Tax Portal / Cổng thông tin điện tử của Tổng cục Thuế)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested limited liability company must obtain a Tax Identification Number (MST) via the enterprise registration process administered by the Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) and the GDT. The procedure is integrated with the issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) and Investment Registration Certificate (IRC). Steps: (1) file an online application with the DPI through the National Business Registration Portal (vanban.gov.vn / dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn) including the company charter, list of members, legal representative identification, and capital contribution schedules; (2) upon issuance of the ERC, the GDT automatically allocates a 13-digit TIN/MST to the enterprise; (3) the LLC must then complete a tax registration dossier (Form 03-DK-TCT) updating supplementary tax information (bank accounts, authorized accounting officer, and tax agent appointments) within 15 business days; (4) foreign contractors or offshore principals with Vietnamese-sourced income are separately registered under withholding registration or may rely on local withholding by Vietnamese counterparts; (5) the LLC must obtain an electronic signature (USB token) for e-invoicing and declare tax via eTax or the GDT's HTKK offline software, after being activated by the local tax sub-department.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Vietnam does not operate a check-the-box classification system analogous to the U.S. regime. There are no elective pass-through classifications for corporate LLCs. A foreign-invested LLC is mandatorily subject to enterprise income tax (CIT) at entity level. Available elections and special regimes include: (i) election to apply for high-tech enterprise income tax incentives (10% CIT rate for 15 years, plus 4-year CIT exemption and 9-year 50% reduction) subject to meeting statutory conditions; (ii) election to apply investment project incentive certificates under the Law on Investment and Decree 218/2013/ND-CP, as amended; (iii) group consolidation election for related-party tax determination is not permitted; (iv) for hybrid mismatch purposes, Vietnam has no elective rules and follows substance-over-form, but taxpayers may elect to carry forward tax losses (maximum 5 years) with no carryback; (v) VAT election: an enterprise may elect the deduction method (the compulsory method for LLCs above the threshold) versus the direct method (applied to smaller enterprises, foreign contractors, and certain trading activities) — a foreign-invested LLC with standard accounting books must use the deduction method. Transfer pricing and permanent establishment rules apply mandatorily; no check-the-box hybrid branch/entity elections exist under Vietnamese domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 100,000,000 annual revenue from taxable goods and services (prior to 1 July 2024; under the amended VAT Law effective 1 July 2025, the increased registration threshold is VND 200,000,000 annual revenue except for business households and individuals with lower thresholds under Government guidance). Foreign suppliers that have revenue derived from Vietnam, including cross-border digital services, must register, declare, and pay VAT through the GDT's Foreign Supplier Portal (etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn) if annual revenue from Vietnam exceeds VND 100,000,000 or under the revised threshold of VND 200,000,000 effective July 2025, regardless of whether a local permanent establishment exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating under the Vietnamese VAT Law applies only to goods exported from Vietnam (including exports processed, manufactured, or sold to foreign entities and physically exported out of Vietnam) and services supplied to foreign organizations/individuals and consumed outside Vietnam (offshore services). Proprietary algorithmic trading services rendered by a Vietnamese LLC to overseas counterparties with no Vietnamese consumption, substantiated by contracts and evidence that the services are provided and used outside Vietnam, may qualify for 0% VAT provided the taxpayer holds valid export documentation. Specifically, VAT zero-rating is available for exported goods and export services (including technical services, information technology services, and offshore financial trading services) under Article 9 of Decree 209/2013/ND-CP and Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC, provided that: (i) the taxpayer has an export contract; (ii) the exported goods/services are actually delivered out of Vietnam; (iii) the taxpayer possesses a customs export declaration for goods; for services, a written contract with a foreign party, proof of payment from foreign currency, and documentation evidencing consumption outside Vietnam are required; and (iv) the taxpayer has not claimed input VAT credit attributable to domestic supplies. Financial derivative and HFT transaction services are treated as export services only if the recipient is non-resident, the services are not connected to Vietnamese immovable property or goods, and the service is physically or legally performed/consumed outside Vietnam; Vietnam's Law on Tax Administration requires the service provider to obtain a letter of non-tax residence or a certificate of tax residency from the foreign counterpart if applying the 0% rate.

  • Commercial Invoice issued by the Vietnamese LLC

Vietnam mandates a mandatory e-invoicing system effective 1 July 2022 (Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC). Invoices must be generated in XML format compliant with the GDT's standard data schema, signed with a digital signature (or a digital stamp affixed via an authorized intermediary), and transmitted to the GDT's e-invoice portal for validation and assignment of a unique invoice number. The GDT operates two modes: (i) offline e-invoice (without a real-time two-way API clearance) used by businesses selling to consumers; and (ii) online e-invoice with real-time data transmission to the GDT portal providing a tax authority code/approval number before issuance to the buyer, required for sales to state organizations and for regulated sectors. A foreign-invested LLC engaged in proprietary HFT activities (not making direct B2C supplies) must nonetheless connect through the GDT's API/eTax infrastructure for monthly VAT declarations; there is no physical fiscal stamping, but each invoice must show a QR code containing authentication data. Special fiscal receipts for securities transactions are not classified as VAT invoices; brokerage/clearing-related fees are subject to VAT under the standard service rules. Electronic invoices must be stored for 6 years and archived on servers located in Vietnam or accessible via authenticated channels to the GDT upon request.

Under Vietnam's tax administration regime, fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims trigger severe exposure under Law No. 38/2019/QH14 (Law on Tax Administration), Decree 125/2020/ND-CP (sanctions for tax and invoice violations), and the Penal Code (No. 100/2015/QH13 as amended). Consequences include: (i) retrospective clawback of the entire zero-rated benefit and any VAT refund already received, plus interest accrued at the rate of 0.03% per day (0.5% per month) from the date the refund was wrongfully obtained; (ii) administrative penalties ranging from 1 to 3 times the evaded amount (up to 20 million VND up to 1.5 times for procedural violations, and from 1 to 3 times for under-declaration/evasion); (iii) a late-payment interest charge of 0.03% per day on outstanding amounts; (iv) suspension of e-invoicing status, tax registration revocation, and public disclosure of non-compliant taxpayers; (v) criminal prosecution for fraudulent VAT refunds/evasion (offences involving VND 100 million or more in evaded tax, or VND 200 million or more in fraudulent refunds) carrying fines up to 4.5 billion VND and/or imprisonment from 6 months up to 20 years for organized or especially large fraud; (vi) statutory limitation periods for tax inspection and retroactive assessments are extended to 10 years for acts concerning fraud or evasion of tax, and unlimited time for refund clawback in cases of forged documentation or false declarations; (vii) managers and legal representatives may be personally jointly liable, and a finding of intentional misdeclaration blocks mitigating penalties and prevents the taxpayer from accessing future tax incentive statuses or investment license renewals.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vietnamese dong (VND) operates under a managed-floating regime with partial convertibility. Current-account operations, including trade settlement, service payments, and permitted transfers, are generally freely convertible through licensed credit institutions. Capital-account operations—such as foreign equity contributions, shareholder loans, and cross-border financing—remain subject to SBV licensing, registration, and strict routing through a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) at a licensed Vietnamese bank. Profit remittance abroad is permitted annually after corporate income tax finalization and audited financial statements are filed; no discrete SBV approval is required once tax obligations are satisfied and remittance formalities are completed. All foreign investment capital inflows and outflows must match the investment registration certificate and be tracked through DICA, with foreign-currency equity contributions subject to exchange-rate conversion risk. Full capital-account liberalisation is not implemented, and the SBV retains reserve powers to impose emergency surrender or repatriation restrictions during balance-of-payments stress.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Decree 70/2014/ND-CP and related SBV implementing circulars (notably Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN), Vietnamese exporters must repatriate export proceeds into a licensed bank account in Vietnam within 180 days of the export transaction/collection date. The current mandatory surrender ratio for ordinary export receipts is 0% following the gradual abolition of the historical 80–100% conversion mechanism; exporters may retain foreign currency in an authorised foreign-currency account for permitted uses, provided they comply with SBV reporting and bank-account discipline requirements. The SBV retains the legal authority to reintroduce partial or full surrender obligations temporarily under severe external-imbalance or foreign-reserve-depletion scenarios, and such emergency conversion ratios would apply across export receipts unless a statutory exemption is granted.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) — Ngan Hang Nha Nuoc Viet Nam

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as digital cross-border platforms do not hold an SBV authorised-entity licence and cannot open a DICA or perform VND clearing through Vietnam's national payment infrastructure. They may be used only for ancillary non-VND treasury flows and are wholly unsuitable for statutory foreign-invested-entity banking (equity injections, shareholder-loan servicing, profit remittance, export receipt collection). Vietnamese forex and tax authorities will not recognise unlicensed EMI settlement for regulatory compliance; all regulated capital and current-account movements must remain inside the licensed banking system. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder loans must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103/MT202 with full sender/receiver identification to the DICA maintained with an SBV-licensed commercial bank. The remittance instruction must include the Investment Registration Certificate identification number, and the bank must record the transfer against the statutory capital-disbursement schedule. Purpose codes should identify 'direct investment capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan', and all boarding will verify the funding via the investor's audited financial statements and the underlying investment licence. The SBV requires that the DICA be used for all inward direct-investment capital flows; conversion to VND may occur onshore at the bank's daily dealing rate. Any excess capital injections beyond the registered charter-capital schedule require an amended Investment Registration Certificate before completion.Outbound treasury disbursements for proprietary HFT market making will predominantly clear via the DICA or the operational foreign-currency account through SWIFT MT103 (single payments), MT202 (bank-to-bank transfers), or MT700 / documentary credit mechanisms under UCP600 where collateralised trade financing is employed. International payments will be settled through correspondent bank relationships, including Fedwire, CHIPS, and TARGET2, using SWIFT AA/CO messages and conforming to each recipient's AML/OFAC requirements. Commercial letters of credit are permissible only for goods-based settlements; for algorithmic trading collateral and margin returns, the standard clearing rail is MT103/202 with full 16-character settlement references to the ultimate exposure site, accompanied by daily margin statements from the clearing broker to the treasury desk.Deliverable OTC VND/USD forward contracts executed with an SBV-licensed bank, based on a documented underlying current or capital transaction.Onshore FX swaps and cross-currency swaps with an authorised Vietnamese credit institution, used to match short-term VND/USD funding and DICA conversion effects.Plain-vanilla VND/USD foreign-exchange options provided they are underwritten by a bank holding an SBV derivative licence and the hedge is traceable to a commercial exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore for Vietnamese residents; any synthetic offshore NDF must be avoided due to SBV capital-control and VAT/tax-opacity risks.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as digital cross-border platforms do not hold an SBV authorised-entity licence and cannot open a DICA or perform VND clearing through Vietnam's national payment infrastructure. They may be used only for ancillary non-VND treasury flows and are wholly unsuitable for statutory foreign-invested-entity banking (equity injections, shareholder-loan servicing, profit remittance, export receipt collection). Vietnamese forex and tax authorities will not recognise unlicensed EMI settlement for regulatory compliance; all regulated capital and current-account movements must remain inside the licensed banking system. Any attempted routing of inbound capital or outbound repatriation through an offshore EMI would expose the group to AML/KYC rejection, SBV administrative penalties, and corporate income tax/foreign-exchange reclassification risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, a Vietnamese entity is required to prepare a Local File if the total value of related-party transactions exceeds VND 30 billion in a tax year, or if the entity earns total consolidated revenue of more than VND 50 billion and its related-party lending value exceeds VND 20 billion. The Master File threshold applies when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds VND 1 trillion. Exceptions apply for entities with nil related-party transactions or those satisfying the de minimis exemptions.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to UAE ESR. Nevertheless, the tax administration applies substance-based anti-avoidance rules: the place of effective management doctrine, the beneficial ownership test, and general anti-avoidance provisions under the Tax Administration Law. A limited liability company with no physical office, employees, or substantive decision-making for its trading algorithms may be treated as a shell and disregarded for tax purposes, subject to re-characterisation and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and tax fraud

Statutory sanction: Under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP, the penalty for tax evasion is between 1 and 3 times the underpaid tax amount. Criminal liability is triggered under Article 200 of the Penal Code 2015 (amended 2017) where the evaded amount exceeds VND 100 million; sanctions include fines of VND 300 million to VND 1 billion and imprisonment for up to 7 years. Corporate sanctions are accompanied by judicial dissolution in serious cases.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where directors or legal representatives participated in, authorised, or had knowledge of the fraudulent acts. Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and civil restitution. A court may issue travel bans, passport seizures, and asset freezes, and directors face individual criminal prosecution and disqualification from management positions.
Shell company without economic substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny the entity's existence, recharacterise all transactions at the level of the parent company, disallow loss carryforwards, and impose penalties for underpaid tax. Under the Law on Enterprises 2020, the business registration certificate may be revoked for enterprises operating beyond their registered purpose or engaging in fraudulent registration.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable under civil law for the company's unpaid taxes when the shell structure is deemed an abuse of limited liability. Criminal prosecution for fraud, tax evasion, or money laundering may be pursued against individual directors, including imprisonment, travel restrictions, and confiscation of personal assets used in the scheme.
Transfer pricing non-compliance and false reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP, failure to provide transfer pricing documentation attracts an administrative fine of up to VND 40 million. The tax authority may independently re-determine the taxpayer's profit by reference to comparable third-party data and impose an additional assessment. The shortfall may be subject to penalties of 20% to 50% of the additional tax assessed, plus interest at 0.03% per day.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve incorrect transfer pricing documentation or intentionally falsify related-party transaction disclosures may be treated as joint participants in tax evasion, attracting criminal liability under Article 200 of the Penal Code. They may additionally be subject to civil claims for the shortfall suffered by the company and be barred from acting as directors in Vietnam.
Anti-money laundering (AML) and predicate-offence exposure

Statutory sanction: Under the Law on Anti-Money Laundering (Law 20/2022/QH15), failure to implement AML procedures can lead to administrative fines in the range of VND 50 million to VND 500 million for the company. Regulatory and criminal authorities may suspend the trading licence or withdraw securities and derivatives licences. If the entity facilitates money laundering, separate criminal sanctions apply under the Penal Code, including fines up to VND 2 billion and imprisonment of responsible individuals for up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers of the LLC have a statutory duty to establish adequate AML controls. Personal liability arises for civil damages caused by the company's laundering activity where the director failed to act in good faith or knowingly facilitated illicit flows. Travel bans and issuance restrictions can be imposed pending criminal proceedings, and directors may be prosecuted for money laundering in addition to the company's sanctions.
