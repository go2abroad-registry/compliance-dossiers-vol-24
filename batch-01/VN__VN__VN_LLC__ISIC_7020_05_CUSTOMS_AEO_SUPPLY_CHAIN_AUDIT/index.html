Competent corporate registry

Department of Planning and Investment (DPI, Sở Kế hoạch và Đầu tư) of the province or centrally-affiliated city where the registered head office is located; central electronic portal: National Business Registration Portal (dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn), operated by the Agency for Business Registration (Cục Đăng ký kinh doanh) under the Ministry of Planning and Investment.

Governing statute

Law on Enterprises No. 59/2020/QH14 (Enterprise Law 2020), specifically Articles 22-27 (enterprise registration), 31 (name rules), 47-50 (legal representatives), 52 (registered office), 74-81 (limited liability companies, charter capital, contribution), and 188-195 (FDI enterprise registration); Law on Investment No. 61/2020/QH14, Articles 36-38 and 44-45 (investment registration and project implementation); Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP on enterprise registration; Decree No. 31/2021/ND-CP guiding the Law on Investment; Circular No. 01/2021/TT-BKHDT (registration forms and dossiers); Civil Code No. 91/2015/QH13 (notarization and powers of attorney).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum charter capital applies to an ISIC 7020 management-consultancy LLC, which is not a conditional business line under the Law on Investment 2020 or the Law on Enterprises 2020. Charter capital is freely determined by the members, but the full subscribed capital must be contributed within ninety (90) days from issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate (Law on Enterprises 2020, Art. 75.2). Contributions by a foreign investor must be remitted from abroad into a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at a licensed bank in Vietnam, and every capital transfer must be evidenced by inward remittance records filed with the DPI and the customs/tax administration as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The Vietnamese LLC is a resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at the standard Corporate Income Tax rate of 20%. Pass-through treatment is not available under Vietnamese law. Foreign tax credits apply for foreign-source income; territorial sourcing rules apply to cross-border service income.

Available election forms

Limited statutory elections: (i) election between the VAT credit method and the direct method based on bookkeeping standards and business type; (ii) election of Small and Medium Enterprise incentives where statutory thresholds are met; (iii) election of preferential CIT rates/tax holidays in approved investment sectors or locations; (iv) no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are recognized; consolidated group tax filing is not permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Companies must register for VAT upon issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate with no minimum turnover threshold. Business households and individuals are required to register and declare VAT only when annual revenue exceeds VND 100,000,000 (approximately USD 4,000). Foreign contractors may additionally be subject to Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) withholding at source.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) goods actually exported from Vietnam and evidenced by customs declarations, including EPE/export-processing transactions; (ii) services supplied to foreign organizations or individuals and consumed entirely outside Vietnam; (iii) international transport and related services. Eligibility requires valid export/service contracts with foreign counterparties, compliant VAT e-invoices, full customs clearance documentation, proof of payment from non-resident bank accounts, evidence that services were delivered/consumed offshore, and a clean tax-compliance status. Input VAT refunds for zero-rated exporters are claimable when the accumulated refundable amount exceeds VND 300,000,000, subject to audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-declaration cleared in VNACCS/VCIS)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export/Service Sales Contract with foreign counterparty
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit advice proving payment
  • Certificate of Origin (where applicable)
  • Shipping order, container release evidence, and inspection certificates as required

Mandatory electronic invoicing under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. All VAT invoices must be generated via certified e-invoice software and transmitted to the e-Tax portal in real time or within 24 hours of issuance. Invoice data must be integrated through API access to etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn; each invoice carries a digital signature and QR code. Tax-authority-coded invoices are required for lower-compliance taxpayers, while self-issued coded invoices apply to high-compliance taxpayers. No physical stamping is required; customs export declarations are cleared exclusively through VNACCS/VCIS electronic data interchange.

Export zero-rating positions are subject to intensive post-clearance audits and desk reviews by the General Department of Taxation and Vietnam Customs. Fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims trigger retrospective clawback of all refunded input VAT, plus statutory late-payment interest from the refund date. Administrative penalties under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP (as amended) range from fines equal to 1–3 times the evaded amount to suspension of e-invoice authority and loss of refund eligibility under Circular 80/2021/TT-BTC. Repeated or intentional violations may constitute tax evasion or smuggling under the Penal Code, carrying criminal liability, corporate sanctions, and revocation of AEO customs authorization. All supporting documentation must be retained for a minimum of five years from the tax assessment deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

VND is a conditionally convertible currency. Current-account transactions, including cross-border payments for professional services such as AEO certification and supply-chain security audits, are convertible through licensed banks without prior SBV approval, subject to documentary verification. Profit remittance by a foreign corporate investor of a Vietnamese LLC is classified as a current-account item: the remitting bank requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax finalization certificates, a board/shareholder dividend resolution, and DICA debit documentation; there is no additional corporate-level dividend withholding because taxes are settled as CIT on net profits. The capital account, however, is restricted: FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, and external borrowings must be registered and routed through the LLC's dedicated Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA); equity withdrawal and loan repayments require DICA-authorized debits and underlying approval/registration documents. VND is not freely usable outside Vietnam; offshore NDF trading involving VND by Vietnamese residents is prohibited and no formal onshore VND NDF market exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the LLC's service exports (AEO certification and supply-chain audit fees), Circular 03/2025/TT-NHNN mandates repatriation of export proceeds into Vietnam within 180 days from completion of the service (date of invoice or service delivery), or such extended tenor not exceeding 365 days as the authorized bank approves on documented commercial grounds. Proceeds must be credited to the LLC's foreign-currency account at a licensed resident bank or directly to the LLC's DICA. There is no statutory percentage-based forced conversion/surrender requirement for FDI exporters: the LLC may retain foreign currency in licensed accounts but must use or sell it only through licensed banks for permitted purposes; conversion into VND occurs at the bank's regulated interbank rate with a disclosed spread. Documentary evidence at receipt: executed service contract, invoice, service completion certificate or audit deliverable, and SWIFT MT103 credit advice.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV; Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam) under the Foreign Exchange Ordinance No. 28/2005/PL-UBTVQH11 (as amended by Ordinance No. 06/2013/UBTVQH13), Circular 02/2021/TT-NHNN on Direct Investment Capital Accounts (as amended by Circular 22/2023/TT-NHNN), Circular 03/2025/TT-NHNN (effective 1 June 2025, replacing Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN) governing export, import and other trade-related foreign-exchange transactions, Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN (as amended) governing foreign-exchange derivatives, and the Law on Anti-Money Laundering No. 14/2022/QH15 with implementing Circular 17/2024/TT-NHNN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

WISE: Low-to-Medium feasibility as a regulated clearing rail. Legal constraints: Wise holds no intermediary payment service license under Decree 52/2024/ND-CP and is not supervised by the SBV as a payment institution; Vietnamese residents may not lawfully use unlicensed electronic payment platforms as the primary settlement channel for cross-border trade invoices, capital account movements, or profit remittances. Wise Business is acceptable only for incidental, low-value expense reimbursements via its licensed in-country correspondent banking partners, and never for DICA injections, dividend remittances, or statutory export-proceeds repatriation. Any Wise-generated VND payout to a Vietnamese beneficiary still requires a licensed local partner bank, and the accounting/audit trail must be reconciled through the principal bank relationship. Verdict: unsuitable as the sole or primary rail; acceptable as a contingency for staff travel and small consulting disbursements in USD/EUR, with full documentation in the treasury ledger.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All FDI equity contributions must be routed through the LLC's Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at a licensed Vietnamese bank under SBV Circular 02/2021/TT-NHNN (as amended). Protocol: (1) obtain the IRC/ERC and open the DICA; (2) the registered foreign investor remits USD/EUR/VND via SWIFT MT103 quoting in fields 70/72: the IRC number, the registered charter-capital tranche being subscribed, and the BOP transaction category 'Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Injection (góp vốn đầu tư trực tiếp)'; (3) the bank verifies that the remitter is exactly the investor named in the IRC, that the amount does not exceed the unsubscribed portion of registered charter capital, and that source-of-funds documents (shareholder approval, board resolution, funding source evidence) are attached; (4) any FX conversion to VND is executed at the bank's regulated interbank rate with disclosed fees; (5) funds are released only for the LLC's authorized use-of-capital purposes as declared in the investment file; (6) the bank and the LLC jointly file the SBV-prescribed monthly/quarterly foreign-exchange/BOP reports; (7) shareholders' loans, if deployed, require separate registration with SBV and must flow through the DICA or a separate loan account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: WISE: Low-to-Medium feasibility as a regulated clearing rail. Legal constraints: Wise holds no intermediary payment service license under Decree 52/2024/ND-CP and is not supervised by the SBV as a payment institution; Vietnamese residents may not lawfully use unlicensed electronic payment platforms as the primary settlement channel for cross-border trade invoices, capital account movements, or profit remittances. Wise Business is acceptable only for incidental, low-value expense reimbursements via its licensed in-country correspondent banking partners, and never for DICA injections, dividend remittances, or statutory export-proceeds repatriation. Any Wise-generated VND payout to a Vietnamese beneficiary still requires a licensed local partner bank, and the accounting/audit trail must be reconciled through the principal bank relationship. Verdict: unsuitable as the sole or primary rail; acceptable as a contingency for staff travel and small consulting disbursements in USD/EUR, with full documentation in the treasury ledger.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VND 30 billion (approximately USD 1.3 million) of aggregate related-party transaction value in a tax year triggers mandatory preparation of a Local File. Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue reaches VND 18,000 billion (approximately EUR 750 million). Taxpayers with no related-party transactions are exempt from documentation requirements.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE. However, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under the Law on Tax Administration 2019 (Articles 75-77), together with Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, requires that controlled transactions be supported by real economic substance. A foreign-invested LLC must maintain an actual office, local employees, contracts, invoices, and decision-making functions in Vietnam. A mere shell or conduit company will be disregarded for tax purposes; the tax authority will re-characterise transactions, deny treaty benefits, and may recommend revocation of the Investment Registration Certificate under the Enterprise Law 2020.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Tax Evasion)

Statutory sanction: If the evaded tax amount exceeds VND 100 million, criminal liability under Penal Code 2015 (as amended) Article 200 applies. Administrative penalties include fines up to three times the evaded tax amount plus late-payment interest at 0.03% per day. In serious cases, fines up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment up to 20 years may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who knowingly sign false returns or direct fraudulent receipt schemes are personally liable to restore misappropriated funds, may be subject to travel bans, and can be criminally prosecuted for tax evasion. The corporate veil is pierced where the LLC is used as a sham to perpetrate tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will re-determine taxable income and assess additional CIT at 20%, plus late-payment interest. Failure to provide TP documentation allows the authority to apply its best estimate and can lead to penalties up to three times the undercharged tax. Incomplete documentation can incur penalties of 20-50% of the additional tax assessed.

Director personal liability: The CFO and legal representative may be personally liable for the additional tax where deliberate distortion of intercompany documentation is proven. Directors may face administrative fines, travel bans, and in fraudulent cases criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Civil restitution may be claimed by shareholders or creditors for breaches of fiduciary duty.
No Economic Substance - Shell / Conduit Company

Statutory sanction: Expenses and offsets are disallowed, transactions are re-characterised, treaty withholding reductions are denied, and additional CIT plus interest are collected. The Investment Registration Certificate may be revoked under the Enterprise Law 2020 / Investment Law 2020 for sham business operations. Administrative fines can reach VND 200 million for continuing violations, and criminal penalties for fraudulent filing apply where amounts are material.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly responsible for company debts, their passports/exit visas may be blocked, and they may be prohibited from incorporating new companies if the entity is declared insolvent or fraudulent. Where profits are siphoned without proper corporate formalities, civil restitution for breach of fiduciary duty is recoverable personally.
Customs / AEO Compliance Failure (relevant to supply-chain audit scope)

Statutory sanction: Customs penalties under Customs Law 2014 and Decree 128/2021/ND-CP include fines up to VND 200 million per violation; duty evasion can attract criminal liability. AEO certification may be suspended or withdrawn, resulting in loss of customs facilitation benefits and heightened inspection rates.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign customs declarations or AEO attestations may be held personally liable for customs duty arrears, subjected to travel bans, and in egregious cases prosecuted under Penal Code Article 188 (smuggling) or Article 200 (tax evasion). The corporate veil is pierced when declarations are knowingly false.
