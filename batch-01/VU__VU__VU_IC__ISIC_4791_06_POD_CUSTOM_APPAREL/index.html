Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC) under the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management — official portal: https://www.customs.gov.vu. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) governs International Company incorporation and annual maintenance; VRC administers the Value Added Tax Act No. 23 of 1998 and Customs Act.

E-government filing portal

No TIN or VAT registration number is required for an International Company (IC), as it is statutorily exempt from all national taxes under Section 84 of the International Companies Act Cap 222. Procedure: (1) appoint a VFSC-licensed registered agent; (2) file the IC charter and constitution with VFSC; (3) pay annual VFSC licence maintenance fee; (4) no tax registration event is triggered at formation. Voluntary VAT registration is permitted via Form VAT 101 filed with VRC, but it is unnecessary for a pure offshore POD operation and confers no tax benefit, given that offshore supplies are exempt in any case.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The exemption is automatic and unconditional under the International Companies Act; Vanuatu operates no check-the-box, fiscal-transparency election, or hybrid-mismatch election regime. An IC cannot elect to become taxable, cannot elect pass-through treatment, and cannot opt into any direct tax framework because no income tax, corporation tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, wealth tax, or inheritance tax exists at the national level. Administrative elections are limited to VFSC corporate governance choices (e.g., share classes, capital currency) which carry no tax effect.

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 (approximately USD 84,000) annual value of taxable supplies made in the course of business in Vanuatu. Registration is compulsory once the threshold is met or exceeded; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. An IC whose supplies are wholly rendered to non-residents falls outside the chargeable scope and is not required to register.

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, government API reporting, or digital stamping regime currently exists in Vanuatu. VAT-registered businesses must issue tax invoices containing prescribed statutory content. For the POD model, commercial records maintained by Stripe and PayPal payment gateways, together with fulfilment-carrier shipment tracking, constitute sufficient evidentiary documentation. No electronic invoice clearance obligation applies to exempt International Companies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains a liberalized currency environment. The Vatu is fully convertible through licensed commercial banks for both current account and capital account purposes. There are no practical exchange controls preventing profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, or reinvestment by a registered International Company. FX transactions are executed at commercial bank dealing rates; the absence of para-allel market premiums and the absence of restrictions on non-resident ownership make the VUV a straightforward convertibility jurisdiction, subject only to standard correspondent banking due diligence on source of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation exists under Vanuatu law. Export receipts from print-on-demand apparel sales are not required to be converted into VUV, nor must they be remitted within a statutory deadline. Commercial banks may apply internal policy requirements such as lodgement into the entity's own settlement account, screening of the trade counterparty, and retention of platform payout statements for AML/CFT verification, but the proceeds may remain in USD, EUR, AUD, or other eligible foreign currencies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV); foreign exchange administration is integrated into the RBV under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act, with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) regulating International Companies and applying AML/CFT rules under the VFSC Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate. Vanuatu has no dedicated electronic money institution licensing regime, and offshore digital EMI wallets are not authorized to provide banking or depository services within Vanuatu. An offshore EMI may be used as a supplementary merchant settlement wallet for collecting Stripe/PayPal POD proceeds, but it cannot function as the primary treasury bank or issue VUV-denominated payment rails. Core cross-border disbursements and FX services must be routed through a licensed Vanuatu commercial bank to avoid unlicensed banking business risk under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 for inbound equity injections and shareholder working-capital advances. The beneficiary account name must match the Vanuatu International Company's registered exact name, and the payment narrative in Field 70 should clearly state 'IC Capital Injection/Working Capital' or 'Non-resident shareholder loan — operational funding'. No Vanuatu exchange-control approval is required. Receiving banks will verify the remitting entity and VFSC beneficial ownership record; therefore pre-clear the wire with the relationship manager and provide a board resolution, a certified share register, and source-of-funds evidence in advance. Platform refunds and chargebacks are settled back through Stripe/PayPal wallet balances before net sweep to the Vanuatu clearing account. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act [Cap 197]; International Companies (ICs) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] are expressly exempt from taxation on all income derived outside Vanuatu.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividends distributed by an IC to any shareholder, resident or non-resident. No dividend imputation, distribution tax, or advance assessment applies to profit repatriation.

POD sales to overseas customers settled via Stripe/PayPal are export supplies and zero-rated; no output tax is declared unless physical delivery occurs within Vanuatu. Non-filing attracts a penalty of up to VT 250,000 plus default interest at 12% per annum; deliberate VAT evasion triggers an additional 25% fraud penalty and criminal prosecution.
Line 2 Input tax: recoverable VAT on Vanuatu-sourced business expenses, offset against output tax. Invoices must bear a valid VRC tax identification number; foreign host-invoice expenses are not recoverable. Non-filing attracts a penalty of up to VT 250,000 plus default interest at 12% per annum; deliberate VAT evasion triggers an additional 25% fraud penalty and criminal prosecution.
Line 3 Net VAT payable or refundable for the period. An IC deriving all revenue from offshore sales should either confirm non-registration or file a nil return to avoid late-filing exposure. Non-filing attracts a penalty of up to VT 250,000 plus default interest at 12% per annum; deliberate VAT evasion triggers an additional 25% fraud penalty and criminal prosecution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None domestically (0 VUV threshold): Vanuatu has no transfer pricing statute, no Master File or Local File regime, and no Country-by-Country reporting obligation in its domestic law. However, when the beneficial owner's group records controlled transactions exceeding the OECD-aligned threshold applicable in the owner's residence country (typically USD 500,000 or AUD 750,000 in a fiscal year), TP documentation becomes a latent compliance burden and arm's-length benchmarking should be maintained defensively for the parent jurisdiction's audit.

Economic substance scope: No codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to Vanuatu ICs, unlike BVI or UAE law. The alternative 'negative substance' rule under Section 42 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222] prohibits the IC from carrying on local business and obliges it to maintain only a registered agent and registered office. Nevertheless, Vanuatu's membership in the OECD Inclusive Framework on BEPS and periodic EU listing assessments create rising pressure toward substance enforcement; a Vanuatu IC lacking board minutes, ledger books, and demonstrable commercial rationale is exposed to recharacterization as a sham in the beneficial owner's tax jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Blindness (Home-Jurisdiction Fiscal Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud penalty of 75% of the understated U.S. tax (IRC Section 6663) plus accuracy-related penalties of 20–40%; felony tax evasion under IRC Section 7201 carries fines up to USD 250,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, and restitution in full; EU member states apply equivalent fiscal fraud statutes with administrative penalties of 150–300%.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed filings at the beneficial owner's direction are jointly liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and international arrest warrants; nominee directors who knowingly enable evasion face personal criminal exposure and extradition risk despite the Vanuatu corporate veil.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Home tax authorities disregard the entity under general anti-abuse rules (e.g., IRC Section 7701(o) economic substance doctrine; EU ATAD General Anti-Abuse Rule), re-assert all net income to the individual or group, and impose accumulated interest, a 40% gross-misstatement penalty, and a 75% fraud penalty when intent is shown; the VFSC may separately revoke the IC's registration for misleading filings.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing applies: controlling shareholders and nominee directors are jointly and severally liable for the re-assessed corporate liability; personal bank and PayPal accounts are subject to freezing orders; directors face VFSC disqualification and market bans of up to 10 years.
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines up to VT 25,000,000 (approximately USD 210,000) per violation under the FTR Act 2013; conviction under the Proceeds of Crime Act [Cap 250] for money laundering carries imprisonment up to 20 years and mandatory confiscation of laundered assets and associated property.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the registered agent's principal are individually prosecutable; the VFSC may issue travel bans, freeze personal assets pending criminal trial, and bar directors from holding office in Vanuatu for up to 10 years.
VFSC Filing Default and Deregistration

Statutory sanction: Fixed late penalty of VT 50,000 plus cumulative reinstatement fees; the VFSC strikes the IC off the register, dissolving its separate legal personality, and assets vest in the Vanuatu Government or a liquidator; the company becomes unable to enforce third-party contracts.

Director personal liability: Dissolution removes the statutory limited-liability shield for post-dissolution debts; creditors may pursue directors personally; directors incur a five-year VFSC directorship disqualification and bear reinstatement costs; the beneficial owner loses banking continuity and foreign tax treaty protection.
Prohibited Local Business Activity by an IC

Statutory sanction: Monthly contravention fine up to VT 50,000 under Section 87 of the IC Act; mandatory VAT registration at 12.5% on local supplies with default penalties of 25% plus interest; contracts made in breach are voidable and unenforceable; the VFSC may revoke the IC's registration.

Director personal liability: Directors are strictly personally liable for contravention fines and indemnity obligations to counterparties; directorial disqualification follows; the IC is forced into conversion to a local company or liquidation, with directors bearing the liquidation costs.
OECD Pillar Two Global Minimum Tax Exposure

Statutory sanction: The parent jurisdiction imposes a top-up tax bringing the effective rate on the IC's allocable excess profits to 15%, including the POD e-commerce margin, enforceable through domestically enacted OECD Inclusive Framework rules; Vanuatu's 0% regime merely shifts the tax burden upward rather than eliminating it.

Director personal liability: Directors of the parent group and, exceptionally, of the Vanuatu entity face sanctions for failure to provide Pillar Two data and filing obligations; statutory penalties apply in the enforcing jurisdiction; the beneficial owner bears the economic cost and may face personal assessments if the group veil is pierced.
