Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum charter capital is imposed by the Law on Enterprises No. 59/2020/QH14 for an LLC engaged in ISIC 7120 (commercial inspection, testing and QC services). The members determine the charter capital freely; each member must contribute its subscribed capital in full within 90 days of the Enterprise Registration Certificate date. No escrow account is mandated by law for this sector; capital may be contributed by remitting lawful Vietnamese dong or permitted foreign exchange to the company's capital contribution account at a licensed bank in Vietnam, with bank credit advices retained as contribution evidence. Foreign investors must ensure the charter capital is consistent with the total investment capital stated in the Investment Registration Certificate.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 2: File the enterprise registration dossier with the provincial Business Registration Office (Phòng Đăng ký kinh doanh) in the form of a Limited Liability Company (single-member or multi-member); the dossier is filed online at dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. The LLC is mandatorily classified as a corporate income tax (CIT) taxpayer on its worldwide income (standard CIT 20%); 100% pass-through is not available. Statutory election/relief mechanisms: (i) foreign tax credit election for foreign-source income of resident enterprises under Circular 78/2013/TT-BTC, capped at the Vietnamese CIT payable on the same net foreign-source income; (ii) election to carry forward tax losses for up to 5 consecutive years (no carryback); (iii) request for CIT incentives only if the scope meets investment/sectoral incentive conditions (e.g., high-tech, supporting industry, new investment in eligible industrial zones); (iv) transfer pricing contemporaneous documentation election (Full Local File / Master File) under Decree 132/2020/NĐ-CP; (v) no domestic hybrid mismatch or partnership-classification election is recognized; MLI-consistent GAAR (Decree 132/2020) overrides artificial arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold triggers registration for a juridical entity: a foreign-invested LLC must register for VAT and issue e-invoices immediately upon incorporation and before any revenue activity. A household/individual business becomes VAT-registrable when annual revenue meets or exceeds VND 100,000,000; below that threshold it may register voluntarily. Any foreign contractor with a PE or carrying on activities in Vietnam is likewise required to register (or apply the Foreign Contractor Withholding mechanism).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies only where goods are physically exported from Vietnam and supported by an export sales contract, customs export declaration (VNACCS/VCIS), bill of lading/airway bill and non-cash payment in convertible currency received at a licensed bank. Services: 0% applies only to services rendered to foreign organizations/individuals and consumed entirely outside Vietnam, and to certain services directly related to export goods (e.g., international transport of goods and related loading/transit/dispatch services). A factory inspection, QC testing, or pre-shipment audit service performed by a Vietnamese LLC is treated as consumed in Vietnam unless the taxpayer can show that the service is performed for the account of a foreign principal, the inspection is a contractual condition of the export sale of the inspected goods, and the goods (or the service output) cross the Vietnamese border; the tax authority will deny 0% where the inspection report is delivered to the foreign buyer but the actual testing/audit activity occurs entirely in Vietnam - in that case VAT at 10% (or 8% for qualifying temporary-reduction supplies) must be charged. Zero-rating evidence includes the contract, foreign client certification, export customs declaration, inspection report, and bank receipt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Tờ khai hải quan xuất khẩu) in VNACCS/VCIS
  • Export Contract and Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • VAT electronic invoice for the export sale
  • Proof of non-cash foreign exchange settlement (SWIFT/bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds) - e.g., Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank statement
  • Certificate of Origin (where requested by importer/for preferential treatment)
  • Service completion certificate / inspection report and certificate of foreign consumption for exported services

Vietnam has a mandatory national e-invoicing regime (Law on Tax Administration 38/2019, Decree 123/2020/NĐ-CP, Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC). All VAT invoices must be electronic XML invoices with a machine-readable QR code, issued through the eTax e-invoice service. Two modes: (1) e-invoices with tax-authority codes are cleared in real time: the tax authority's system digitally stamps each invoice before issuance; this mode is compulsory for taxpayers that fail technical/IT compliance conditions; and (2) e-invoices without pre-codes (for compliant taxpayers) must have their full XML data uploaded/transmitted to the General Department of Taxation's e-invoice data portal on a per-issuance basis within the statutory deadline (generally no later than the end of the next working day; the eTax portal supports M2M/API transmission). There is no per-invoice approval for mode (2). Digital stamping is evidenced by the tax authority's 'mã của cơ quan thuế' and the e-invoice QR code; non-compliant paper/CSV invoices are legally invalid for input VAT input credit and customs clearance.

Penalties for improper zero-rating/export fraud: (i) reversal of 0% and imposition of 10% VAT on the under-collected sales, plus late-payment interest at 0.03%/day on under-remitted VAT; (ii) administrative penalties under Decree 125/2020/NĐ-CP - fines up to 3 times the evaded amount for tax evasion and additional fines for invoice violations (paper fake invoices, undeclared invoices), typically VND 10-50 million per e-invoice violation; (iii) customs/foreign-exchange scrutiny: failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days (or 360-720 days with State Bank approval) is punishable under Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN, with possible administrative sanctions for foreign-exchange violations and denial of future export zero-rating; (iv) criminal liability under Penal Code Article 200 where evaded tax exceeds VND 100 million - with evasion from VND 300 million or distinctive aggravating features leading to fines up to VND 15 billion, suspension of operation and imprisonment of up to 20 years for individuals; (v) retrospective re-assessment period is 5 years (or 10 years where fraud/omission is established) with interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vietnamese dong (VND) is not fully convertible. Current-account convertibility is available for the commercial inspection/testing/QC company's service-export fees, commissions, and ordinary operating disbursements, which may be converted through authorized credit institutions against underlying contract documentation. The capital account remains conditional: equity injections, shareholder loans, divestments, and profit remittances must follow the licensed Investment Registration Certificate (IRC), be routed through a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) at a licensed bank, and must clear tax, audit, and SBV foreign-exchange reporting gates. Profit remittance is permitted annually after audited financial statements show distributable after-tax retained earnings and after full corporate income tax settlement; no additional dividend-remittance withholding is imposed on the foreign shareholder of a Vietnamese LLC, but the remitting LLC must submit to the bank the profit-allocation resolution, audited statements, tax finalization proof, and the SBV-required documentation package.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service-export proceeds (inspection and QC fees invoiced by the LLC to offshore principals) are current-account receipts and must be repatriated directly to an authorized bank account. Under SBV Circular No. 05/2014/TT-NHNN, export earnings may be credited to and retained in the LLC's foreign-currency account, with the bank applying a 90-day repatriation monitoring window measured from the contractual payment due date unless the underlying contract expressly provides a longer payment term. There is no general mandatory conversion ratio for ordinary service exporters: up to 100% of foreign-currency export proceeds may be retained on account or sold to the servicing bank at the SBV-listed rate. Mandatory conversion/surrender obligations apply only to specified state-regulated goods (e.g., crude oil) under annual SBV directives and therefore do not affect the commercial inspection LLC. Proceeds may not be parked in non-bank digital wallets, crypto corridors, or offshore EMI accounts.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV / Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam). Foreign-exchange administration is exercised through the SBV Foreign Exchange Department under the Foreign Exchange Ordinance No. 06/2005/PL-UBTVQH11, Decree No. 70/2014/ND-CP, and SBV Circular No. 05/2014/TT-NHNN as amended by Circular No. 01/2019/TT-NHNN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low legal viability. Foreign digital EMI/neo-bank rails (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business-class wallets, and comparable offshore accounts) are not recognized in Viet Nam as licensed banking accounts and cannot be used as the primary cross-border clearing rail for an FDI LLC. They fail the mandatory SBV reporting rails, cannot evidence export-proceeds repatriation, cannot serve as a Direct Investment Capital Account, and create AML misclassification risk. They may operate only as an ancillary convenience for de minimis client refunds in foreign jurisdictions where a principal insists on the rail, but the statutory books and SBV surveillance must treat the licensed Vietnamese bank account as the exclusive repository of export proceeds and investment capital.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Charter capital must be remitted by the foreign investor only to the LLC's Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) at a licensed commercial bank, quoting the purpose 'contribution to charter capital of the foreign-invested enterprise' and the IRC registration number; the bank verifies the inflow against the IRC schedule, the charter contribution timetable, and SBV reporting codes, after which funds may be converted to VND for operating use. All export-of-services fees from offshore principals are settled by SWIFT MT103 credits to the LLC's foreign-currency or VND current account; each credit must carry the payer's name, a reference to the underlying inspection contract, an invoice, and the standard SBV balance-of-payments purpose classification for service-export receipts. No third-party, crypto, or digital-EMI corridor is acceptable for inbound funding.Supplies, subcontractor fees, laboratory calibration, rent, and payroll are settled via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border counterparties, supported by underlying contracts, tax invoices, and withholding-tax receipts where applicable; for goods-related service components such as calibrated testing equipment or reference materials, a commercial letter of credit or documentary collection may be used if the counterparty requires bank confirmation. Domestic VND payments clear through the interbank EFA system and NAPAS. Because inspection/QC service exports do not move goods, transport documents are not required; the LC route is limited to tangible-equipment acquisitions and back-to-back performance guarantees demanded by clients for factory-audit mandates.Onshore VND/USD spot FX with an authorized credit institution against current-account underlying transactionsOnshore VND/USD FX forward with maximum tenor of 365 days supported by a documented current-account or debt-service exposureOnshore VND/USD FX swap with an authorized bank for short-term liquidity bridgingCross-currency swap linked to a registered shareholder loan or syndicated facility on a case-by-case SBV-licensed basisOffshore VND non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible as a hedging or clearing rail; all hedging must be executed through the licensed onshore banking system

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low legal viability. Foreign digital EMI/neo-bank rails (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business-class wallets, and comparable offshore accounts) are not recognized in Viet Nam as licensed banking accounts and cannot be used as the primary cross-border clearing rail for an FDI LLC. They fail the mandatory SBV reporting rails, cannot evidence export-proceeds repatriation, cannot serve as a Direct Investment Capital Account, and create AML misclassification risk. They may operate only as an ancillary convenience for de minimis client refunds in foreign jurisdictions where a principal insists on the rail, but the statutory books and SBV surveillance must treat the licensed Vietnamese bank account as the exclusive repository of export proceeds and investment capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VND 50 billion (approximately US$2 million) of related-party revenue in a fiscal year; also lower thresholds for related-party transactions with exemptions: small taxpayers with below VND 50 billion revenue and related-party transactions below VND 30 billion may be exempt from certain documentation, but the related-party transaction disclosure form must still be filed if the enterprise has related-party transactions exceeding VND 30 billion, subject to detailed thresholds under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam applies the substance-over-form doctrine and requires related-party transactions to reflect economic substance; although Vietnam does not have 'Economic Substance Regulations' identical to the UAE-style ESR, the domestic TP regime (Decree 132/2020/ND-CP) and CIT law deny deduction for transactions lacking commercial substance or conducted with shell entities; the General Department of Taxation can recharacterize, adjust pricing, impose deemed income, and apply penalties. The foreign contractor tax and permanent establishment rules further require substantive Vietnam presence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent CIT Underdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Vietnam's Tax Administration Law and Penal Code: criminal liability for tax evasion (imprisonment up to 20 years in aggravated cases); administrative fines of 1-3 times the evaded tax amount; late-payment interest at 0.03%/day; forced collection and suspension of business operations

Director personal liability: Directors/legal representatives are personally liable for filing and payment failures; the corporate veil can be pierced in fraud or intentional evasion cases, exposing personal assets for restitution; travel bans, exit visa denials, and imprisonment of directors and signatories are available enforcement measures
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment, disallowance of intercompany payments, deemed CIT and foreign contractor tax assessments, fines of up to 3 times tax arrears, and cancellation/disqualification of enterprise registration under the Enterprise Law and Investment Law

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution for tax debts if they knowingly operated a sham entity; a court may pierce the veil where the LLC is a mere instrumentality, and legal representatives can be banned from establishing new enterprises and may face criminal prosecution for fraudulent bankruptcy or tax evasion
AML / Money Laundering Through Trade or Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering Law (effective 2022-2023): administrative fines up to VND 2 billion for non-compliant organizations; criminal money laundering penalties including imprisonment up to 15 years; freezing of accounts and confiscation of proceeds

Director personal liability: The State Bank of Vietnam and investigating police may impose personal monetary penalties on compliance officers and directors for AML failures; willful involvement attracts criminal liability, travel restrictions, and extradition exposure
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: TP audit adjustments with additional CIT, late-payment interest, and fines up to 3 times the under-declared tax; penalties for failure to provide documentation equal to 50% of the determined tax adjustment (or 100% in bad faith); administrative fines on enterprises up to VND 20-30 million for documentation failures

Director personal liability: Directors and accountants who sign false TP documentation can be held personally liable for administrative fines; serious willful falsification triggers criminal tax evasion liability; tax authorities may recover debts from personal bank accounts and initiate criminal complaints against legal representatives
Unlicensed Business Operation / Unlawful Provision of Inspection Services

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties for illegal business activities: fines up to VND 100 million or more depending on sector; forced suspension of operations; cancellation of tax code; the tax authorities may compute CIT on a deemed basis, and contracts may be void

Director personal liability: The enterprise's legal representative is subject to fines and may be prohibited from acting as legal representative; if the unlicensed operation caused significant tax or investment law violations, criminal prosecution for illegal business activities is possible
