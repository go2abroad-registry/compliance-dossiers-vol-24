Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Service (VCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila, Republic of Vanuatu; official portal: https://customs.gov.vu. Company and tax-exemption registration is administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) at https://www.vfsc.vu. Investment facilitation is handled by the Vanuatu Investment Promotion Authority (VIPA).

E-government filing portal

Vanuatu does not levy national income tax, capital gains tax, or withholding tax and does not operate a general domestic Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) system for income tax purposes. An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] receives its statutory identifier (VFSC Registration Number / I.C. No.) directly from the VFSC upon incorporation. Because the IC is exempt from all Vanuatu taxes on international business activities, it is not required to register with VCIR for income tax or VAT purposes and files no annual tax return. If the IC physically moves goods through Vanuatu customs, it (or its licensed customs broker) must obtain a customs import/export processing account with the VCIR Customs Division; no fiscal TIN is assigned. Foreign investment participation in the IC is permitted without local ownership; a Foreign Investment Certificate (FIC) is conditionally required for foreign enterprises carrying on restricted onshore activities, but an IC's offshore subscription-box operations are generally outside the FIC regime. For AML/CTF purposes, the IC must maintain beneficial-ownership records with licensed registered agents under the AML/CTF Act [Cap 293].

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under section 51 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222], the IC elects to claim statutory tax exemption by applying to the VFSC each year for a Certificate of Tax Exemption and paying the prescribed annual government fee; the exemption covers income, capital gains, corporate taxes, stamp duties, and all present forms of national taxation arising from international business activities. No US-style check-the-box election exists, and Vanuatu has no local hybrid-mismatch or tax-transparent classification rules because it imposes no CIT/VAT. The IC is statutorily restricted from carrying on business with Vanuatu residents or owning Vanuatu real property (except under prescribed exemptions), and breach of these restrictions may void the exemption and expose the entity to municipal business licence taxes and penalties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT or GST regime in force in Vanuatu; indirect taxation is limited to customs import duty under the Customs Act [Cap 145] and the Customs Tariff Act, excise duty on selected goods, municipal council business licence taxes, and stamp duty on limited instruments. Exports of subscription-box merchandise are not subject to any Vanuatu indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no VAT/GST statute exists. A customs processing account is required for physical import/export clearances, and a municipal business licence (nominal annual fee in VUV) is required only if the IC conducts domestic onshore business in Vanuatu, which an IC is generally prohibited from doing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - because Vanuatu has no VAT/GST, exports are not 'zero-rated'; there is no VAT output tax to zero-rate and no VAT refund mechanism. Goods exported from Vanuatu are outside the scope of any Vanuatu indirect tax, and subscription-box merchandise exported by an IC is not subject to export duty unless specifically scheduled under Vanuatu customs tariff law (consumer packaged goods are generally not scheduled). Import duty paid on goods brought temporarily into Vanuatu and later re-exported is not refundable under a VAT/drawback mechanism because that mechanism does not exist; the IC is therefore advised to arrange drop-shipment so that goods do not enter Vanuatu customs territory.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Negotiable transport document (bill of lading or air waybill) evidencing physical export
  • IC registration certificate and customs processing account reference (if physical export through Vanuatu)

Vanuatu imposes no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based tax clearance mandate for B2B or B2C transactions. Customs transactions are declared through the electronic single-window system ASYCUDA World operated by VCIR, which requires electronic lodging of export/import declarations and supporting documents, but there is no tax-authority pre-clearance of commercial invoices. Subscription payments received through Stripe Billing or Authorize.Net are subject to standard AML/CFT record-keeping rather than to any e-invoicing tax-control regime.

No VAT zero-rating fraud or tax-clawback regime exists because Vanuatu levies no VAT/GST. Scrutiny is limited to: (i) accuracy of customs export declarations under the Customs Act [Cap 145], where false declarations attract fines, forfeiture of goods, and prosecution; (ii) compliance with the International Companies Act [Cap 222] and the annual Certificate of Tax Exemption maintenance, failure of which can result in loss of tax-exempt status and retrospective municipal liability; (iii) AML/CFT record-keeping obligations under the AML/CTF Act [Cap 293] for beneficial ownership and transaction monitoring; and (iv) sanctions/export-control screening where subscription-box goods are shipped to restricted destinations. There is no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline under Vanuatu law: the Vatu (VUV) is freely convertible, and the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu imposes no exchange-control obligation on export proceeds. This field is recorded as '0' to reflect the absence of any legally mandated repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a liberalised exchange-control regime rather than full statutory deregulation. Current-account payments for imports, logistics, gateway commissions, and professional fees are freely executable through authorised dealers without prior RBV approval. Capital-account inflows and outflows attributable to a non-resident Vanuatu International Company do not require per-transaction approval but are subject to evidence-based filing by the authorised dealer. The Vatu (VUV) is an independently floating currency in a thin domestic market; settlement is available through four authorised-dealer banks. Profit remittance is legally unrestricted and the tax-exempt IC faces no dividend withholding tax or income tax in Vanuatu. The practical convertibility friction is institutional de-risking: FATF grey-listing history and correspondent-bank policies compel banks to demand proof of beneficial ownership, trade substance, and source of funds before executing distributions. An IC may elect a foreign functional currency and retains only operational VUV exposure for local outlays.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exchange-control surrender obligations apply to Vanuatu-resident exporters: export proceeds must be repatriated to an authorised dealer within the contractual settlement window (standard dealer guidance requires receipt within 30 days of the relevant shipment date) and converted into Vatu where the underlying contract is denominated in foreign currency. This surrender mandate does not apply to a Vanuatu International Company formed under the International Companies Act [Cap. 259], because the IC is statutorily treated as a non-resident offshore entity conducting business outside Vanuatu. The exemption is maintained by annual filing of: (i) the VFSC Certificate of Compliance, (ii) board minutes confirming that all gross revenue is derived from non-resident customers, and (iii) the IC's designated non-resident identifier maintained by the RBV-authorised dealer. Failure to refresh these records can retroactively subject foreign-currency receipts to repatriation and forced VUV conversion on an invoice-level basis.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) and the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC). The RBV is the exchange-control authority under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 97] and issues administrative Exchange Control Notices enforced through RBV-designated authorised dealers, while the VFSC is the registrar and AML/CFT supervisor for Vanuatu International Companies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as an ancillary settlement layer, but not as a replacement for the Vanuatu bank-account requirement. Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net are the targeted gateway rails and are characterised in the treasury architecture as payment-institution collection lockboxes; they are typically registered to an operating entity in the US/EU and swept daily to the VU IC's USD account at an authorised dealer. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Ebury, or OFX-platform equivalents are legally viable for multi-currency collection, FX-cost compression, and batch disbursement to subscription-box suppliers, provided no Vanuatu deposit-taking business is conducted. Under the Vanuatu Banking Act [Cap. 46], an entity may not hold itself out as accepting deposits in Vanuatu without an RBV bank licence, so an offshore EMI must remain a non-deposit-taking payment trigger. All funds must ultimately clear to the VU IC's on-island or offshore authorised-dealer account; the bank's EDD file will re-trace every EMI disbursement and treat off-platform cash flows as a compliance red flag.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: the beneficial owner's home bank initiates a SWIFT MT103 with field 70 remittance information coded 'EQUI - SHARE CAPITAL - VU__VU__VU_IC__ISIC_4791_08_SUBSCRIPTION_BOX_SERVICE'; the beneficiary is the IC's USD-denominated account at ANZ or Bred. The authorised dealer must hold in advance a certified KYC pack comprising the VFSC Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, board resolution approving the share issue, executed share subscription schedule, and an ISIC 4791 business plan. The dealer records the credit as equity capital on the monthly RBV electronic exchange-control report, which is the only statutory verification required for a non-resident IC. If the injection takes the form of a shareholder loan, the wire must use ISO purpose code LOAN with a signed shareholder-loan agreement and an arm's-length interest rate documented in the group's transfer-pricing file. No VUV conversion is required at the entry point; the IC maintains USD as its functional currency and may hold VUV balances only for domestic operating costs.Routine supplier invoices for box components, packaging, and freight below approximately USD 25,000 clear by SWIFT MT103 through the authorised dealer or through an EMI batch-settlement rail using ISO purpose codes SUPP (supplier payment) or INTC (intra-group payment where procurement is routed through an affiliate); SWIFT GPI is recommended for end-to-end tracking and rapid reconciliation. For container-scale inventory builds, the bank will structure a Commercial Letter of Credit, sight or 30-day usance, issued in favour of the supplier and payable against original bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and CMR where applicable; the LC transaction gives the bank documentary assurance and reduces the frequency of ad hoc AML reviews. Gateway and EMI balances are swept daily from Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net to the IC's authorised-dealer USD account, with residual VUV amounts converted at spot only for bonded-warehouse rent, customs handling, port fees, and local professional services.Outright VUV/USD forward contract with a maturity of 31 days to 12 months through an RBV-authorised dealer, documented as trade-hedging for the subscription-box import cycle and local logistics costs.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) on VUV/USD concluded with an offshore banking counterparty and settled in USD, permissible while the IC remains a non-resident entity without a VUV-collateralised loan requiring a deliverable hedge.Cross-currency swap and FX-swap ladder between USD and VUV executed under an ISDA 2002 master agreement with an authorised dealer for seasonal working-capital management.European-style USD/VUV currency options purchased by the IC on a buy-side premium basis only, used to cap conversion cost on optional dividend distributions and prohibited for speculative option-selling.Natural-supply-chain hedge through USD-nominated supplier contracts matched to gateway USD receipts, with weekly spot conversion of only the residual VUV operating outlay in accordance with the dealer's FX policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as an ancillary settlement layer, but not as a replacement for the Vanuatu bank-account requirement. Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net are the targeted gateway rails and are characterised in the treasury architecture as payment-institution collection lockboxes; they are typically registered to an operating entity in the US/EU and swept daily to the VU IC's USD account at an authorised dealer. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Ebury, or OFX-platform equivalents are legally viable for multi-currency collection, FX-cost compression, and batch disbursement to subscription-box suppliers, provided no Vanuatu deposit-taking business is conducted. Under the Vanuatu Banking Act [Cap. 46], an entity may not hold itself out as accepting deposits in Vanuatu without an RBV bank licence, so an offshore EMI must remain a non-deposit-taking payment trigger. All funds must ultimately clear to the VU IC's on-island or offshore authorised-dealer account; the bank's EDD file will re-trace every EMI disbursement and treat off-platform cash flows as a compliance red flag.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% - Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax at the national level; an International Company (IC) is additionally protected by the absolute statutory tax exemption in section 139 of the International Companies Act, Cap. 222 (ICA).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividends, deemed dividends, or any distribution made by an IC to resident or non-resident shareholders, and no exchange-control restriction applies to the remittance of profits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold - Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing legislation and prescribes no Local File or Master File monetary thresholds in VUV. Instead, the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 imposes a Vanuatu substance test for the distribution and service centre relevant activity: (i) the IC is directed and managed in Vanuatu; (ii) it has an adequate number of employees in Vanuatu; (iii) it incurs adequate operating expenditure in Vanuatu; (iv) it maintains physical premises in Vanuatu; and (v) the core income-generating activities are performed in Vanuatu. Intercompany pricing should still be benchmarked on arm's length terms as a matter of international best practice to avoid red flags in the home jurisdiction of the ultimate beneficial owner.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 (as amended) applies to all ICs carrying on a relevant activity. A subscription-box IC that purchases subscription-box inventory from a connected person and resells it to customers, or distributes goods or services for a connected person, conducts the relevant activity of distribution and service centre. The IC must maintain economic substance proportionate to the activity: local board meetings and records, local management, local employees, local premises, and locally performed core income-generating activities (order management, inventory handling, logistics, and customer-support functions). An annual ESR notification must be filed with the VFSC; the VFSC and the Domestic Revenue Department cooperate with foreign tax authorities through information-exchange mechanisms. Non-compliance exposes the IC to court-ordered penalties of up to VUV 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 2 years, continuing daily default fines, and deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company - Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Court-imposed penalty of up to VUV 10,000,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 2 years for the company and responsible officers, plus continuing daily penalties; VFSC deregistration of the IC; automatic exchange of information with the jurisdiction of the ultimate parent and beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who consented or connived in the breach are personally liable to fines and imprisonment; the court may order surrender of passports and travel restrictions, freeze personal assets, and require personal restitution of diverted profits; the corporate veil does not shield directors in ESR prosecutions.
Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filings

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction with fines and imprisonment under the ICA and the VAT Act; VAT default assessments, penalty tax and interest; cancellation of the certificate of incorporation; court orders for restitution of diverted profits; and potential confiscation of the subscription-box business assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and the signatory officers are prosecuted personally as principal offenders, not merely as agents of the company; civil restitution orders attach to personal assets; travel bans may be imposed pending criminal proceedings; and imprisonment of natural persons applies for conviction.
AML / Financial Transactions Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Substantial fines under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act, confiscation of the proceeds of crime, imprisonment for money-laundering offences, revocation of any VFSC authorization, and freezing of the IC's bank accounts by court order.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal money-laundering convictions, confiscation orders against personal and corporate assets, passport surrender and travel restrictions, cross-border asset-tracing exposure, and lifting of the corporate veil so that the ultimate beneficial owner can be pursued directly.
Prohibited Domestic Trading Without Business Licence

Statutory sanction: The offending contracts are unenforceable in Vanuatu courts; monetary fines are imposed per contravention under the Business Licence Act; the IC's tax-exempt status may be suspended; and the VFSC may strike the company off the register.

Director personal liability: Directors risk personal fines, disqualification from managing any Vanuatu-licensed entity, and criminal liability for continued abuse; the court may issue management disqualification orders and travel restrictions during proceedings.
False Beneficial Ownership Declaration

Statutory sanction: Fines and imprisonment for false declarations under the ICA; confiscation of shares and dividends; deregistration of the IC; and exchange of information with the home tax authority of the ultimate beneficial owner through CRS and intergovernmental cooperation channels.

Director personal liability: The ultimate beneficial owner and each signatory director face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment, international travel bans, asset-freeze orders, and disqualification from holding office in any VFSC-regulated entity; the corporate veil is pierced to attribute the subscription-box profits to the true owner.
