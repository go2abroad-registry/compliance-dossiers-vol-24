Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR) – official portal: https://customsinlandrevenue.gov.vu; taxpayer services via the Vanuatu Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) online registration system administered by the VAT and TIN Unit of DCIR.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned International Companies (ICs) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] must apply for a Vanuatu TIN by submitting (1) the Application for Taxpayer Identification Number form (Form TIN-01), (2) certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation, (3) certified copy of the Registered Agent license and registered office confirmation, (4) particulars of directors, shareholders, and ultimate beneficial owners, and (5) proof of physical/virtual registered office address in Port Vila. The application may be filed by the licensed registered agent. Upon approval, DCIR issues a TIN certificate within 5–10 business days. The same TIN is used for VAT registration where applicable, though most ICs remain outside the VAT net because they conduct zero-rated or offshore activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Foreign-owned International Companies (ICs) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] must apply for a Vanuatu TIN by submitting (1) the Application for Taxpayer Identification Number form (Form TIN-01), (2) certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation, (3) certified copy of the Registered Agent license and registered office confirmation, (4) particulars of directors, shareholders, and ultimate beneficial owners, and (5) proof of physical/virtual registered office address in Port Vila. The same TIN is used for VAT registration where applicable, though most ICs remain outside the VAT net because they conduct zero-rated or offshore activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Vanuatu law. Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no withholding tax, and no estate or gift tax. An International Company is unconditionally exempt from all present and future taxes on income, assets, and transactions, subject only to the payment of annual government registration and license fees. The only relevant election is the VAT registration election under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap. 273], available to any person making taxable supplies in Vanuatu, including a non-resident IC that voluntarily registers. No hybrid mismatch election is available because Vanuatu has not enacted hybrid mismatch rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 in taxable turnover in any 12-month period; voluntary registration permitted below threshold; mandatory registration applies to non-resident suppliers only where taxable supplies are made in Vanuatu in the course of a taxable activity carried out in Vanuatu — offshore restaurant revenue is generally outside the VAT net.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap. 273] applies strictly to (a) the supply of goods exported from Vanuatu by the supplier, (b) the supply of services directly connected with exported goods, and (c) international transport services and services physically performed entirely outside Vanuatu. An overseas hotpot restaurant and condiment supply operation physically performed at foreign locations does not create Vanuatu VAT supply; therefore, zero-rating under Vanuatu law is not the operative relief. Instead, the overseas revenue is simply outside the scope of Vanuatu VAT because the place of supply is the foreign jurisdiction. Input VAT incurred in Vanuatu (e.g., on minor local procurement) is not attributable to taxable supplies and is generally irrecoverable unless the IC voluntarily registers for VAT and can establish that Vanuatu-sourced supplies have been made; in practice, the IC claims no refunds. Any intra-group condiment goods shipped from Vanuatu to overseas restaurants must be physically exported and supported by customs evidence to obtain zero-rating of the local supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form CED) processed through the Vanuatu Customs Asycuda World system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Vanuatu ports (Port Vila or Santo)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a licensed Vanuatu bank evidencing settlement of the export sale in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice matching the export declaration
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (where preferential treatment claimed, though none available for hotpot condiments)
  • Export permit under the Vanuatu Export Control Act where food-grade condiments require phytosanitary or health certification

Vanuatu has not implemented real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance infrastructure. There is no mandatory e-invoicing portal, no API-based continuous transaction controls (CTC), and no digital tax stamp requirement. VAT-registered businesses must issue sequential, serially numbered tax invoices in physical or PDF form containing prescribed particulars (supplier name and TIN, purchaser name and TIN for B2B supplies, date, description, quantity, price, VAT amount, and total). Records must be retained for 7 years. A phased e-invoicing reform is under study, but no statutory mandate is in force as of the applicable tax year.

DCIR and the Vanuatu Customs Service may conduct joint audits of zero-rated export claims. Penalties for fraudulent zero-rating or false export substantiation include: (1) repayment or clawback of the entire VAT refund attributable to the false claim, (2) a civil penalty of 100% of the tax sought to be evaded or a fixed penalty of up to VUV 200,000 for failure to comply with statutory record-keeping obligations, (3) interest at the statutory rate of 10% per annum on unpaid VAT, (4) criminal prosecution for tax fraud or knowingly furnishing false documentation with fines up to VUV 5,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years under the Value Added Tax Act and the Customs Act [Cap. 92], and (5) debarment of the licensed customs broker or registered agent involved in the fraudulent filing. Directors of the IC may be held personally liable where refunds are obtained without genuine physical export or where the 180-day foreign currency repatriation deadline is not met without a DCIR-approved extension.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) is convertible for documented current-account transactions (goods, services, invisibles) through authorized dealers, subject to prescribed exchange-control forms. Resident capital-account transfers require RBV approval, which is routinely granted for bona fide documented transactions. A Vanuatu International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from the Exchange Control Act and is treated as a non-resident, so inward capital injections, profit repatriation, and shareholder distributions flow through unrestricted foreign-currency accounts with zero dividend withholding and zero capital-gains tax in Vanuatu. Residual practical hurdles are (i) correspondent-bank de-risking after Vanuatu's historical FATF/EU listings, (ii) heightened enhanced-due-diligence (EDD) documentation, and (iii) the shallow onshore interbank VUV market for large-liquidity conversions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters, full export proceeds must be collected through a Vanuatu authorized dealer within 6 months from shipment date under the export declaration regime (Form E); there is no mandatory conversion ratio (0%), and proceeds may be retained in foreign currency with dealer approval. The Vanuatu IC, being exchange-control-exempt and non-resident, is subject to no surrender or conversion obligation on overseas hotpot dining receipts or condiment export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), exercising delegated exchange-control authority of the Minister of Finance under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 138]

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility. Leading digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Mercury) generally exclude Vanuatu corporate clients or impose jurisdiction-wide restrictions stemming from residual AML/CFT risk and Vanuatu's limited correspondent-banking network. Offshore EMIs that accept Pacific corporate entities require EDD packages, a substantive link to the EMI's licensed jurisdictions, and transactional caps; a Vanuatu IC holding overseas restaurant operations without domestic substance will typically fail EMI underwriting. Recommended posture: use a tier-1 Vanuatu authorized dealer as the primary clearing core and employ EMIs only as ancillary collection tools for small-ticket condiment e-commerce transactions, never for capital injection or trade settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the ultimate shareholder's corporate or private account to the IC's designated USD operating account at a Vanuatu authorized dealer, with remittance narrative 'INVESTMENT / SHARE CAPITAL' and the IC registration number on the beneficiary line. The supporting envelope must show a board resolution allotting shares or approving the shareholder loan, an executed loan agreement where applicable, source-of-funds evidence (bank statements, sale agreement, dividend mandate), and a UBO declaration. Since the IC is exchange-control exempt, no RBV prior approval is required for the inward credit; the bank's EDD file, however, must be complete before value dating, and the SWIFT Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR) must be archived for audit.Routine condiment and equipment settlements use SWIFT MT103 at the authorized dealer against invoices, packing lists, and bills of lading; large recurring bulk-import programs are secured through commercial letters of credit advised via MT700/MT707, with the Vanuatu bank as issuing bank and the supplier's bank as negotiating bank, payable against compliant shipping documents. All disbursements carry explicit trade-purpose narratives (e.g., 'GOODS IMPORT' or 'ROYALTY PAYMENT'), and transactions above the dealer reporting threshold are backed by the master contract, pro-forma invoice, and transport evidence to satisfy Exchange Control Act documentation rules and correspondent-bank audit expectations.Onshore deliverable FX forward (USD/VUV) entered with a Vanuatu authorized dealer for tenors up to 12 months, covering trade payables and receivable conversion.Offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) referencing VUV with a London or Singapore prime counterparty, permissible for the exchange-control-exempt non-resident IC without RBV approval.Cross-currency swap (USD/VUV or USD/CNY) executed with a licensed non-resident bank, permitted for the IC as a non-resident exempt entity subject to bank-internal product eligibility and EDD sign-off.

A certified audit is mandatory only where the company's constitution or articles require it, shareholders so resolve, or a third-party financier (such as a bank financing the overseas hotpot restaurant operations) imposes an audit covenant.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable as Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing legislation. Best-practice threshold for foreign counterparties requesting OECD-aligned documentation: VUV 50,000,000 in connected-party annual turnover, corresponding to approximately USD 420,000.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu's Economic Substance Regulations are applicable to entities performing relevant activities for foreign multinational groups. Full-service hotpot dining, restaurant operations, and condiment supply to customers in Vanuatu are not designated relevant activities. However, an International Company should maintain demonstrated substance in Vanuatu through local premises, local employees, local board oversight, and locally incurred operating expenses wherever the entity receives overseas franchising, trademark, or related-party income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Improper Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Revocation of International Company registration, disgorgement of undeclared revenue equivalents, a fine of up to VUV 5,000,000, and prosecution for false declaration under the International Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents are jointly and severally liable for all unpaid government fees and penalties. Travel bans may be imposed and prison terms of up to five years may apply for fraudulent use of tax-exempt status.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Striking-off from the companies register, prohibition on holding a VFSC license for ten years, and automatic referral to the FATF-style Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering for treaty-shopping risks.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors, even if nominee, face civil restoration of misdirected revenues, personal liability for creditor claims, travel restrictions, and disqualification as directors in any VFSC-regulated industry.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties up to VUV 15,000,000 for severe breaches, suspension of the foreign-exchange and merchant-acquiring licenses, compulsory reporting to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit, and public revocation of the International Company certificate.

Director personal liability: Senior executives and directors are personally liable for AML and counter-financing of terrorism violations. Individual fines may reach VUV 2,000,000; imprisonment of up to seven years and blocking of personal bank accounts can be ordered.
Transfer Pricing and Undocumented Related-Party Flows

Statutory sanction: While Vanuatu itself imposes no CIT, the VFSC may suspend the International Company for misuse of the exempt regime, and exchange-of-information requests from foreign tax authorities will be honored. Foreign jurisdictions may impose transfer-pricing adjustments, gross-up taxes, and penalties equal to 100–200% of the adjusted profit.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in signing the pricing agreements or approving the royalty invoices may be personally assessed by foreign revenue authorities under anti-avoidance and CFC rules, and may face civil recovery actions in Vanuatu courts for breach of fiduciary duty.
Unlicensed Restaurant Operations

Statutory sanction: Immediate closure of the restaurant premises, confiscation of equipment and condiment stock, official warnings published by municipal authorities, and cumulative fines of VUV 500,000 per day of unlicensed operation.

Director personal liability: The owning company's directors are personally liable for unpaid fines, employee unpaid wages, and supplier claims. In case of food safety negligence, criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment of up to three years and a lifetime ban from obtaining a Vanuatu business license.
