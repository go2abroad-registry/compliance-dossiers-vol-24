Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Authority (VRCA), Port Vila — central administration of VAT, customs, and business licensing; e-services and tax portal at https://www.vrca.gov.vu; corporate registry and International Company (IC) licensing administered separately by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) at https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] with the VFSC; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and memorandum/articles; VFSC issues a company registration number but the IC is not automatically granted a Vanuatu tax identification number. Step 2: Because an IC is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes (including VAT on offshore activities), no TIN/VAT ID is required for purely non-resident business; the Certificate of Incorporation plus a notarised director resolution asserting offshore status suffices as the tax identity instrument. Step 3: If the IC elects to register for VAT (e.g., to claim input credits or to serve in-scope local customers), file Form VAT-01 (Application for Registration) with VRCA along with certified copies of the Certificate of Incorporation, registered agent consent, local physical address, proposed commencement date, and projected annual turnover; VRCA issues a 9-digit VAT registration number within 15 business days. Step 4: For staff or premises, obtain a Business Licence from the relevant Provincial Council or Port Vila Municipal Council, which references the VRCA tax file, but no federal income tax registration is triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: An International Company is subject to a total statutory tax exemption under section 3 of the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] — no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no withholding tax, no stamp duty, no estate or gift tax, and no VAT liability on offshore business; the exemption applies automatically on incorporation, conditional only on (i) no local-source income from Vanuatu residents, (ii) no land ownership, and (iii) no local banking business.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Vanuatu law. The only relevant elections are (a) voluntary VAT registration for an otherwise-exempt IC under the Value Added Tax Act No. 4 of 1998 (section 7, voluntary registration conditions); (b) conversion from IC status to a domestic company under Part 12 of the International Companies Act, thereby abandoning the tax-exempt regime and becoming subject to VAT (12.5%) and Business Licence fees; and (c) re-domiciliation into Vanuatu under section 177 of the IC Act, preserving the exemption. Vanuatu has not enacted hybrid mismatch rules or CFC anti-deferral rules; ICs cannot elect treat-through or entity-level taxation because the jurisdiction imposes no income tax at any level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Vatu (VT) 5,000,000 (approximately USD 41,000) of annual taxable supplies; mandatory registration for any person carrying on a taxable activity whose total value of taxable supplies in any 12-month period exceeds or is expected to exceed VT 5,000,000; voluntary registration permitted below the threshold; a foreign entity with no physical presence but making supplies to Vanuatu customers is not subject to compulsory VAT registration unless it carries on a 'taxable activity' in Vanuatu (i.e., through a fixed establishment, agent, or local staff).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under section 13(1)(f) and Schedule 2 of the VAT Act applies to (i) exported goods and (ii) services supplied to a person who is not resident in Vanuatu and who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply, where the services are for use, consumption, or exploitation wholly outside Vanuatu. Digitally delivered services — including AI avatar video generation, SaaS subscriptions, cloud processing, and software licensing — qualify as exported services if the customer is a non-resident, the contract is concluded offshore, payment originates from a non-resident bank or payment processor, and the service is delivered via the internet with no end-user located in Vanuatu; no local VAT is charged and the supply is reported as zero-rated on the VAT return. Input VAT attributable to making zero-rated exports may be recovered in full by a VAT-registered exporter; a wholly offshore IC with zero local purchases typically has no input credits to claim. For an IC exercising its statutory exemption (not VAT-registered), no VAT return is filed and no refund mechanism applies.

  • Commercial Invoice (itemised, showing 0% VAT, contract reference, and supplier VAT number if registered)
  • Signed Offshore Service Contract / Master Subscription Agreement evidencing non-resident customer and exclusive offshore use
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Payment Processor Settlement Evidence (Stripe/Paddle payout records, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement showing receipt in Vatu or foreign currency from the non-resident counterparty)
  • Customer Non-Residence Evidence (passport/CRB extract of overseas incorporation, IP address and geolocation logs, delivery domain or cloud region logs outside VU)
  • Service Delivery and Usage Evidence (access logs, SaaS usage reports, or email confirmation of download/streaming) demonstrating no Vanuatu end-user
  • Where physical goods or media are exported alongside services: Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World), Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, Packing List, and Certificate of Origin — not required for pure intangible digital services

No real-time fiscal clearance API, e-invoicing clearance authority, or digital stamping regime is currently mandated for general businesses or SaaS exporters. VRCA has progressively deployed Electronic Fiscal Devices (EFD) and fiscal invoice requirements for fixed-establishment retailers and hospitality operators under the VAT (Electronic Fiscal Devices) Regulations, which require submission of daily Z-reports to VRCA; however, pure offshore digital-service exporters (AI video generation SaaS) operating from Vanuatu without local onward supplies are outside the EFD mandate. All registered businesses are nonetheless obliged to issue sequential VAT invoices bearing the VAT registration number, retain electronic records for 7 years, and file monthly or quarterly VAT returns via the VRCA online portal as notified in the VAT return filing schedule.

VRCA may conduct an audit within 7 years of the relevant tax period (VAT Act section 61). Where zero-rating is claimed on a service subsequently shown to be consumed in Vanuatu, or where the non-resident evidence is forged or materially deficient, the VAT becomes payable at 12.5% on the full consideration and the input credits are clawed back. Civil penalties: late tax up to 25% of the amount plus interest at 12% per annum (sections 44-45); incorrect returns with no reasonable excuse attract a penalty of 25% of the tax shortfall. Criminal fraud consequences: fines up to VT 1,000,000 (approx. USD 8,300) and imprisonment up to 7 years for knowingly providing false information in a VAT return or refund claim (section 58), plus mandatory disqualification of the IC's registered agent. In practice, export zero-rating for pure digital services is sustained by maintaining a contemporaneous digital evidence pack (contract, IP geolocation logs, payment provenance, and non-resident certification) for at least 7 years; absence of any one element triggers recharacterisation as taxable domestic supply. Vanuatu has no exchange-control legislation, so the 180-day reputational/deadline convention is contractual rather than statutory; failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days does not attract fiscal penalties but can undermine the documentary proof of offshore supply and thereby expose zero-rating to clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu is an IMF member operating under a conventional pegged arrangement: the Vatu (VUV) is pegged to an unpublished trade-weighted basket of Vanuatu's principal trading-partner currencies (the basket replaced the former SDR peg in 2011). Vanuatu maintains current-account convertibility consistent with IMF Article VIII: bona fide trade and service payments are permitted without licensing, subject only to authorized-dealer documentation. The primary legal construct for this treasury is that a Vanuatu International Company (VU_IC) is exchange-control-exempt for all foreign-currency dealings: the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] provides that an IC may hold and deal in any foreign currency without exchange-control approval and, as a licensing condition, must not transact business with Vanuatu residents or hold, use, or deal in Vatu. Consequently the VU_IC's USD capital, SaaS subscription revenue, and distributions are fully convertible and freely remittable; no RBV approval or Vatu conversion arises. Practical hurdles are convertibility frictions, not legal prohibitions: the VUV is a thin, non-deliverable offshore currency with wide bid/ask spreads, so all invoicing, liquidity, and clearing must be pre-funded and executed in USD. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted for ICs (no withholding tax, no RBV permit) provided the company maintains statutory records, audited financial statements, and evidence that no Vanuatu-resident source revenue was generated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the RBV exchange-control framework, export proceeds from goods or services exported from Vanuatu must be repatriated into the Vanuatu banking system through an authorized dealer; the standard dealer condition applied by ANZ and Bred requires receipt within 90 days from the date of the export invoice and no later than six months from shipment, with the export contract, invoice, customs/service documentation, and SWIFT credit advice retained for five years. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio to VUV: authorized dealers permit retention of proceeds in the invoice currency, and the mandatory surrender ratio for an International Company is effectively nil (0%) because the VU_IC is prohibited from holding Vatu and is exempt from exchange-control reporting for foreign-currency receipts. For the AIGC SaaS revenue profile, no surrender obligation arises at all: the VU_IC neither operates a Vanuatu export license nor touches the VUV clearing system; subscription software revenue is collected offshore via a merchant-of-record (Paddle) and remitted directly into the IC's USD account. Should any Vatu balances ever appear on the account (e.g., erroneous local refund), the bank will require immediate conversion back to USD via an authorized dealer or onward repatriation, as Vatu holdings by an IC constitute a licensing breach.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), operating under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 62], the Exchange Control Regulations, and the Financial Institutions Act; the RBV licenses all commercial banks and authorized foreign-exchange dealers and delegates routine approvals to authorized dealers. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) regulates the International Company and its registered agent, while the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) supervises AML/CFT obligations of banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

RATING: LOW. Direct onboarding of the VU_IC with mainstream fintech EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, Revolut Business, Ebury) is not feasible: none of these platforms supports Vanuatu as a licensed customer jurisdiction; and any EMI collecting or holding funds for a Vanuatu entity would require a Vanuatu banking or international-banking license under the Financial Institutions Act, which no foreign EMI holds. Correspondent de-risking has also reduced the appetite of global EMI banks to clear for Vanuatu-flagged payees. The compliant workaround is a two-tier structure: (i) Paddle acts as Merchant of Record, contracting directly with end customers and remitting net SaaS revenue by SWIFT MT103 to the VU_IC's Bred or ANZ USD account (Paddle does not require a Vanuatu merchant account because the merchant-of-record is the seller); (ii) any EMI pass-through must be limited to an affiliate in a supported jurisdiction (e.g., Singapore or United States) that holds the Stripe account only as a collection conduit, immediately sweeping funds by intercompany wire to the VU_IC under a written payment-flow and IP-licensing deed. Stripe direct merchant registration is NOT available for Vanuatu entities and must never be attempted on the VU_IC.

Master / Local File threshold: No Local File or Master File is mandated because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax. Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations apply to International Companies and Foundations. AI avatar video generation and licensing is treated as an IP business or digital service relevant activity. The entity must demonstrate board direction and management in Vanuatu, adequate physical premises, full-time employees in Vanuatu, and significant local operating expenditure proportionate to revenue.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False ESR Declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to VUV 100,000 per infraction, daily surcharges for continued default, deregistration or strike-off of the IC, and criminal fines upon conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be held personally liable for restitution to the VFSC, disqualified from managing companies, restricted from leaving Vanuatu, and subject to imprisonment for fraudulent declarations.
Shell Company Abuse / Absent Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of incorporation certificate, compulsory strike-off, and corporate fines up to VUV 50 million under the International Companies Act and Economic Substance Regulations.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines, disqualification orders, and potential criminal prosecution; beneficial owners risk being identified in international exchange-of-information requests and denied treaty benefits.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: AML penalties up to VUV 10 million per violation, asset confiscation, mandatory suspicious transaction reporting, and freezing orders sustained by the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may incur criminal liability for money laundering facilitation, be subjected to travel bans, and face imprisonment of up to ten years for knowingly enabling untraceable cross-border transactions.
