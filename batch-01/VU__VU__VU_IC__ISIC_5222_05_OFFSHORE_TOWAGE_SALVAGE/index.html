Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), VAT and Domestic Taxes Unit, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management — official portal: https://www.vrevenue.gov.vu; e-filing portal: VRC e-Services; customs clearance platform: ASYCUDA World.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the International Company (IC) with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 240] and obtain the VFSC certificate of incorporation carrying the statutory company registration number. Step 2: If the IC imports goods into Vanuatu or elects voluntary VAT registration, file the prescribed VRC application for a Tax Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID), supplying a certified copy of the VFSC incorporation certificate, evidence of the registered office address in Vanuatu, the director and shareholder register, a bank reference from a Vanuatu-licensed bank, and evidence of the foreign/offshore ownership and control. Step 3: For a purely offshore towage and salvage operation, no domestic income tax registration or VAT registration is required; the IC must annually file the VFSC renewal declaration confirming that no Vanuatu-source domestic business is carried on and pay the fixed VFSC annual registration fee (a regulatory fee, not a tax). Step 4: Where the IC operates Vanuatu-flagged tug or salvage vessels, obtain the vessel registration certificate from the Vanuatu Maritime Authority under the Maritime Act [Cap 131]; this certificate, together with the VFSC certificate, is retained in the export substantiation bundle.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Vanuatu has no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or withholding tax regime; the International Company is an extraterritorial exempt entity under section 92 of the International Companies Act [Cap 240], exempt from all Vanuatu taxes, duties, and fees (including VAT) in respect of offshore business. The exemption is territorial: towage and salvage services physically performed on the high seas or outside Vanuatu's 12-nautical-mile territorial sea remain fully outside the scope of Vanuatu taxation; any supply consumed within Vanuatu customs territory would be subject to the standard VAT and customs rules.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Vanuatu because the jurisdiction does not impose income tax and maintains no broad double-tax treaty network. The operative statutory elections are: (1) election to constitute the company as an International Company under Part II of the International Companies Act, recorded in the constitution and effective while IC status is renewed; (2) voluntary VAT registration under section 5 of the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 283] even where turnover is below the compulsory threshold, permitting a VAT-registered IC to claim 0% export zero-rating on qualifying offshore services; (3) contractual election, at each salvage or towage engagement, to perform the service under a standard international form (Lloyd's Open Form, BIMCO TOWHIRE, or a lump-sum salvage contract) with consideration payable in a foreign currency outside Vanuatu, thereby preserving extraterritorial exemption or export zero-rating.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 15,000,000 (fifteen million vatu) taxable turnover in any consecutive twelve-month period triggers compulsory VAT registration; the threshold was raised from VUV 1,000,000 under VAT (Exemption and Registration Threshold) legal instruments adopted in the 2019–2021 economic recovery programme; businesses below the threshold may elect voluntary VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT export zero-rating is available where the service is supplied to a non-resident customer and the towage or salvage services are physically performed, or directly consumed, outside Vanuatu, including deep-sea ocean towage, wreck removal, maritime emergency salvage, and ship towage on the high seas or within foreign territorial waters. Eligibility also requires that the Vanuatu supplier retains evidence that the tug or salvage unit departed Vanuatu customs territory (where departure occurs), that the service was rendered to a foreign shipowner, underwriter, or salved-interest, and that consideration is expressed in and settled from a foreign currency. Where an International Company is not VAT-registered, the supply is not 'zero-rated' but is outside the scope of VAT, with tax exemption evidenced by the VFSC certificate and the International Companies Act exemption certificate; voluntary VAT registration permits the IC to substantively claim 0% on the same qualifying offshore services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) filed through ASYCUDA World and electronically released by Vanuatu Customs, evidencing the departure of the tug or salvage units from Vanuatu customs territory; where no goods movement occurs because the service was rendered wholly at sea, a certificate of non-lodgement or written VRC Customs confirmation that no export declaration is applicable.
  • Original or notarised copy of the signed salvage or towage contract (Lloyd's Open Form 2011, BIMCO TOWHIRE, or lump-sum salvage agreement) clearly identifying the foreign shipowner, the salved or towed vessel, the nature of the services, and the international consideration expressed in a foreign currency.
  • Ocean towage bill of lading, tug voyage order, mate's receipt, sea protest, or electronic voyage record, together with departure and arrival notices and GPS/AIS track logs proving that the tug or salvage assets were physically outside Vanuatu territorial waters during the entire performance of the engagement.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Vanuatu-licensed bank, or the corresponding SWIFT MT103 credit advice from the paying foreign bank, confirming receipt of the foreign-currency fee from the non-resident principal; where payment is received outside Vanuatu, the bank-certified receipt of credit into the IC's offshore account must be supplied.
  • VFSC certificate of incorporation of the International Company and, where applicable, the Vanuatu Maritime Authority ship registration certificate for the tug or salvage vessel, substantiating the extraterritorial tax-exempt character of the operating entity.

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime is currently enacted for VAT. Registered suppliers must issue sequential VAT invoices (paper or PDF) bearing the supplier's VAT/TIN, retain full tax invoices and supporting charter-party and voyage records, and file VAT returns electronically via the VRC e-Services portal at monthly intervals as prescribed. Export declarations are lodged electronically through ASYCUDA World and receive electronic customs release; the absence of digital fiscal stamping does not relieve the registrant of the six-year statutory record-keeping obligation. The export substantiation file must be reproducible in electronic, auditable form for VRC inspection.

VRC VAT auditors examine export zero-rating claims against the contractual chain, vessel AIS/GPS records, bank payment confirmations, and customs release data. Penalties under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 283] include: a default assessment plus an administrative penalty of up to 100% of the tax shortfall; additional fixed penalties for failure to maintain proper VAT records; and statutory interest accruing on unpaid or clawed-back VAT. Fraudulent export claims and false or misleading statements made to obtain a refund are criminal offences under the Revenue Administration Act and the Customs and Excise Act, carrying on conviction a fine of up to VUV 2,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years. Where the mandatory 180-day foreign-exchange remittance condition is not satisfied, or the transaction is substantiated on paper only, the zero-rating is reversed, VAT at 12.5% is assessed on the gross consideration with interest and penalties, and no export drawback or refund is payable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV, ISO 4217) is managed under an RBV-administered float referenced to a USD-centric trade basket. Vanuatu repealed the Exchange Control Act [Cap 106] in 1997, abolishing administrative exchange-control approvals for non-residents; both current-account and capital-account transactions are thus freely convertible. No capital transfer licence, prior RBV consent, or profit-remittance permit is required for an International Company. Practical frictions are limited to: (i) the thin offshore VUV market (typical two-way spread 50-150 pips at Port Vila); (ii) correspondent-bank de-risking sensitivity in USD clearing; and (iii) FIU reporting of every credit or debit equal to or exceeding VUV 1,000,000 (approximately USD 9,000 under the AML/CFT Act No. 14 of 2022 and VFSC AML/CFT Regulations). An International Company under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] is tax-exempt in Vanuatu and may remit dividends, salvage awards, and intercompany settlements offshore without withholding or repatriation quotas.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Vanuatu imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or retention ratio for export, towage, or salvage proceeds. A towage and salvage International Company may retain 100% of foreign-currency earnings in an offshore USD/EUR/AUD nostro account. Continuing obligations are informational: inward remittances referencing salvage awards or time-charter hire must (i) be recorded in the IC's annual VFSC registry filing, (ii) reconcile with the VMA vessel registration and bareboat charter endorsement in force, and (iii) carry in SWIFT remittance information the governing contract reference (e.g., TOWCON, TOWHIRE, SALVAGE/LOF 2000). Coastal towage or salvage performed within Vanuatu territorial waters, if any, is outside the permitted offshore scope of an International Company and would require a domestic shipping operating licence; such income, if inadvertently booked, may be subject to reporting to Customs and the VMA under shipping revenue circulars and could expose the IC's tax-exempt status.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) is the central bank, currency issuer, and prudential regulator of licensed commercial banks and international banks. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) acts as the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) for AML/CFT supervision and is the registrar of International Companies and international bank licensees under the International Banking Act [Cap 191]. The Vanuatu Maritime Authority (VMA) is the statutory registrar of the international ship registry relevant to tug and salvage vessels.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low. Vanuatu has no statutory licence category for foreign electronic-money institutions or payment fintechs. Non-bank issuance of e-money to Vanuatu residents requires RBV authorisation under the national payment-system oversight framework; no foreign EMI currently holds such authority to maintain VUV client-money balances. International EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut, Currencycloud) therefore cannot serve as the principal treasury bank for a Vanuatu International Company because the IC must evidence substantive corporate presence, an audit trail of accounting records, and a qualifying banking relationship with a licensed VU institution. Recommended architecture: (i) ring-fence the ANZ or BSP Vanuatu account as the compliance-anchor and escrow account; (ii) permit a Tier-1 foreign EMI or e-money wallet to act solely as a payout hop for crew wages, port disbursements, and subcontracted salvage teams; and (iii) ensure every wallet topping is initiated by a SWIFT MT103 credit from the Vanuatu bank so the cross-border chain remains visible to the VFSC and FIU.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection and paid-in surplus must arrive by a single-dealing SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer from the parent, ocean fund, or ship financier into the IC's dedicated capital escrow account at ANZ Port Vila (offshore USD book). Field 70 (Remittance Information) must read 'CAPINJ-IC-ISIC5222' and the narrative must state 'Deemed Capital Contribution to Vanuatu International Company for Deep-Sea Tug Acquisition and Salvage Working Capital'. For correspondent-bank routing, the sending bank should use the IMF BPM6/ECB BOP purpose code applicable to the purchase of vessels and acquisition of transport equipment; generic SWIFT purpose codes 'GIFT' or 'REM' must not be used. The receiving bank will require: (i) a notarised board resolution authorising the account, the exact capital amount, and the authorised signatories; (ii) the VFSC International Company Certificate and current registry extract; (iii) a compliance letter from the sending bank confirming that the remitter is not sanctioned and that the funds are sourced from lawful operations; (iv) alignment of the remitter name with the UBO register on file; and (v) an escrow release instruction stating that funds will be released only against the tug's bill of sale, the VMA registration certificate, or a board-approved operational drawdown. Every outbound wire exceeding USD 50,000 must reference the underlying towage, salvage, or purchase contract to ensure traceability before the bank will execute.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (USD/VUV) with a maximum tenor of 12 months executed with an RBV-authorised dealer (BSP or ANZ Port Vila) limited to firm trade receivables and payables, with no speculative or naked positions permitted.Cross-currency swaps (USD/VUV or EUR/VUV) with a Vanuatu-licensed commercial bank or its Australian/French parent, documented under a 2014 ISDA master agreement subject to Vanuatu law, to manage timing mismatches between salvage-award receipts and vessel-instalment payments, in each case on a back-to-back underlying cash-flow basis.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on VUV are not permissible; no active offshore NDF market exists for the Vatu, and the RBV does not recognise NDFs as eligible treasury instruments for authorised dealers.Short-term USD money-market sweeps and overnight Eurocurrency deposits denominated in USD, EUR, or AUD held at the Tier-1 Vanuatu banks, noting that RBV depositor compensation does not extend to non-resident International Company offshore accounts; margin is therefore collateralised against the VMA entry bonding for the tug registry.Marine hull, war-risk, and salvage-liability hedging is a non-FX programme placed offshore through a Lloyd's of London broker, kept separate from the FX and cash-management platform.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-Low. Vanuatu has no statutory licence category for foreign electronic-money institutions or payment fintechs. Non-bank issuance of e-money to Vanuatu residents requires RBV authorisation under the national payment-system oversight framework; no foreign EMI currently holds such authority to maintain VUV client-money balances. International EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut, Currencycloud) therefore cannot serve as the principal treasury bank for a Vanuatu International Company because the IC must evidence substantive corporate presence, an audit trail of accounting records, and a qualifying banking relationship with a licensed VU institution. Recommended architecture: (i) ring-fence the ANZ or BSP Vanuatu account as the compliance-anchor and escrow account; (ii) permit a Tier-1 foreign EMI or e-money wallet to act solely as a payout hop for crew wages, port disbursements, and subcontracted salvage teams; and (iii) ensure every wallet topping is initiated by a SWIFT MT103 credit from the Vanuatu bank so the cross-border chain remains visible to the VFSC and FIU.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% - Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax; no operative income tax statute exists, and an International Company (IC) is expressly exempt from all income taxation under Section 57 of the International Companies Act 1992 (as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Vanuatu law imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an IC to non-resident shareholders. Section 57 of the International Companies Act 1992 exempts distributions, and no withholding statute exists. Dividends may be paid in any currency without government consent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - Vanuatu has no transfer pricing statute and no prescribed Local File or Master File thresholds in Vatu. The functional substitute is the record-keeping obligation under the International Companies (Amendment) Act 2019: the IC must maintain accounting records and underlying documents for at least 7 years, sufficient to show commercial rationale if a foreign tax administration makes a transfer pricing or CFC adjustment. The practical threshold is therefore any related-party transaction: intercompany towage master agreements, salvage contracts with affiliated underwriters, vessel charter-hire to connected entities, or management fee arrangements should in all cases be evidenced by arm's length contracts, benchmarked pricing, invoices and bank statements.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not enact an Economic Substance Regulations statute in the BVI/Cayman style. Substance is instead enforced indirectly: (i) Section 5 of the International Companies Act 1992 prohibits the IC from trading with Vanuatu residents, owning Vanuatu land or soliciting local public investment, thereby confining the IC to genuinely offshore operations; (ii) the AML/CFT Act 2014 obliges the licensed registered agent to verify and hold beneficial ownership and CDD files; (iii) OECD FHTP and FATF reviews expect demonstrable economic activity within the IC regime, and foreign parent jurisdictions apply their own economic substance, GAAR and CFC attribution rules. A towing and salvage IC owning or chartering tugboats must therefore demonstrate real operational substance - contractual negotiation, voyage management, billing, insurance and risk control - beyond the registered agent's address.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Facilitation and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu AML/CFT Act 2014 (as amended): money laundering offences carry custodial penalties of up to 20 years and corporate fines of up to VT 100 million; the VFSC may strike off the IC and revoke the registered agent's licence; foreign tax authorities may recover the full evaded tax plus penalties and pursue assets through mutual legal assistance.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners face criminal prosecution, asset seizure, extradition under Vanuatu's Mutual Legal Assistance and Extradition arrangements with Australia, New Zealand and the UK, travel bans, senior personal bankruptcy in civil restitution, and joint and several liability for the company's unpaid tax and penalties.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act 1992 (as amended) and VFSC enforcement practice: monetary penalties, director disqualification orders, strike-off, refusal of good-standing certificates and adverse AML classification. Internationally, the regime has on multiple occasions drawn OECD FHTP scrutiny and EU non-cooperative listing criteria, causing correspondent banks to apply enhanced due diligence or de-risk Vanuatu ICs; a shell finding by a foreign court triggers CFC reattribution of all income and penalties of 100% to 200% of tax.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are disqualified from office in Vanuatu; controlling shareholders are deemed to have received the entity's income directly and are assessed personally; foreign courts may issue worldwide asset freezing orders and travel restrictions pending restitution.
Beneficial Ownership Concealment and CRS Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the AML/CFT Act 2014 and the International Companies (Amendment) Act 2019: fines of up to VT 10 million against the entity and VT 1 million against individual officers; criminal sanctions for false declarations; strike-off of the company. Cross-border, the IC and its agent are exposed to automatic exchange of information (CRS) and exchange of information on request under the OECD Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters, to which Vanuatu is a party.

Director personal liability: Personal fines, imprisonment of up to 10 years for false beneficial ownership declarations, prohibition from acting as a director of any Vanuatu entity, recovery of the costs of the foreign regulatory investigation, and extradition exposure for perjury-type offences in the requesting state.
Unlawful Onshore Activity and Unlicensed Marine Operations

Statutory sanction: Breach of Section 5 of the International Companies Act 1992 renders the offending contract unenforceable in Vanuatu and subjects the IC to fines and strike-off; the Ports and Harbours Act and Maritime legislation impose licence suspension, daily fines and vessel detention; unpaid VAT and business licence liabilities become immediately due with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised local trading are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid fines and third-party losses; in serious cases they face criminal negligence charges, exclusion orders from Vanuatu, and personal suit by injured counterparties, including crew injury claims under maritime lien law.
