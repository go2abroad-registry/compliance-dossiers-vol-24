Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management (no standard income-tax administration exists); company and entity registry administration is vested in the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), official portal https://www.vfsc.vu, with customs operational clearance processed through ASYCUDA World.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Appoint a VFSC-licensed Registered Agent, as required under the International Companies Act (Cap 222); non-resident ownership is permitted and no minimum capital is required. Step 2: The agent files the Memorandum and Articles of Association with VFSC; the resulting VFSC Registration Number serves as the entity's statutory identifier in lieu of any TIN. Step 3: Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing; a Vanuatu resident agent is mandatory. Step 4: No inland-revenue TIN, VAT ID, or tax registration is issued or required because Vanuatu imposes no income tax and has no VAT/GST regime; the International Company is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxation, stamp duty, and exchange-control filing obligations. Step 5: Registration with Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue for an importer code is triggered only if the IC physically imports goods into Vanuatu; pure offshore e-commerce warehouse fulfillment under ISIC 5210 does not trigger any domestic tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu domestic law provides no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election because the International Company is absolutely tax-exempt under the International Companies Act (Cap 222). For foreign tax purposes, a Vanuatu IC is an eligible entity (it is not on the IRS per-se corporation list), so a U.S. Federal Form 8832 check-the-box election may be filed to classify it as a disregarded entity, partnership, or corporation; single-member default is disregarded status and multi-member default is partnership. Vanuatu has not implemented BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch rules, so the IC may operate as a hybrid financing vehicle in cross-border structures without adverse domestic consequences.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Vanuatu has no VAT, GST, or general sales tax; residual indirect levies are customs import duties under the Customs Act (Cap 61), a turnover tax on specified domestic services, and stamp duty, none of which apply to an exempt International Company engaged in offshore e-commerce fulfillment.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST statute exists in Vanuatu and therefore no economic-turnover threshold for indirect-tax registration is fixed in Vanuatu vatu (VUV). A local Business Licence fee applies under the Business Licences Act only to activities carried on within Vanuatu and is inapplicable to an IC with no local fixed place of business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — because Vanuatu levies no VAT/GST, there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim and no refund mechanism to engage. All exports of goods and offshore services, including third-party logistics order fulfillment for overseas e-commerce (ISIC 5210), fall entirely outside the scope of Vanuatu indirect taxation, and no application, ruling, or prior approval is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World submission) where goods physically transit Vanuatu
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export shipment and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Good Standing issued by VFSC evidencing tax-exempt status and corporate valid existence

None — Vanuatu imposes no mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API integration, or digital stamping. Import and export movements are declared solely through the ASYCUDA World customs clearance platform; commercial documentation remains paper or PDF based with no fiscal clearance stamp for offshore services.

No domestic export zero-rating claim exists, so no Vanuatu VAT refund can be clawed back; however, materially false customs declarations attract penalties under the Customs Act (Cap 61), including monetary fines, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment, and misuse of the International Company's exempt status may lead to VFSC deregistration and disqualification of directors. In foreign jurisdictions that do impose VAT, any fraudulent zero-rating, export-fraud, or input-credit scheme executed through the Vanuatu IC may trigger retrospective assessments, interest, penalties, and joint liability against the IC and its beneficial owners; the 180-day proceeds-repatriation benchmark is applied by third-party banking and tax due diligence as a traceability standard despite the absence of domestic Vanuatu exchange controls.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is freely convertible for all current account transactions when effected through authorized dealers. Capital account transactions remain formally subject to exchange control supervision; however, non-resident International Companies face minimal impediments, as inbound capital injections and outward profit remittances are handled by banks as routine before-approval-exempt transfers when supported by corporate documentation. Profit remittance hurdles relate primarily to anti-money laundering source-of-funds verification rather than FX convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio is imposed on export proceeds under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 33]. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the exporter's discretion; periodic reporting to the RBV through authorized dealers is required for balance-of-payments statistics only.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex) can serve as supplementary treasury rails for multi-currency cross-border payments after a seamless API integration; however, they are not locally authorized to hold VUV balances or clear domestic transactions. Using such EMIs as the primary liquidity vehicle would trigger unlicensed payment-service risk and may be rejected by Vanuatu auditors because of the absence of local AML/CFT supervisory cooperation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Wire transfers must be routed via SWIFT MT103 through an authorized dealer in Vanuatu, using the purpose code 'Capital Injection' or 'Equity Contribution' for Regulatory Reporting (Economic and Financial Statistics). The beneficiary account must carry the IC legal name and account number, and the bank must hold on file: a board resolution, share subscription documents, and a statement of source of funds. For VUV amounts, an exchange-control declaration (Form EC1) is completed by the bank; no RBV pre-approval is required for non-resident equity injections into an International Company.Cross-border payments for overseas warehouse subcontracting, freight forwarding, and order fulfillment are cleared through SWIFT MT103 direct remittance. Larger contracts may be backed by commercial letters of credit issued in favour of foreign 3PL vendors, with settlement against presentation of warehouse receipt, packing list, and proof of delivery. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A in VUV — Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing legislation, no Local File or Master File obligation, no country-by-country reporting threshold, and no thin-capitalisation rules. Documentation duties arise only indirectly under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019, which requires an IC to evidence locally performed core income-generating activities where the cross-dock/overseas fulfilment activity is within the 'distribution and service centre' category.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019 obliges every IC to file a substance declaration with VFSC. For ISIC 5210 cross-dock/overseas 3PL fulfilment, the relevant activity is 'distribution and service centre'. The IC must: (i) be managed and controlled in Vanuatu (board meetings and minutes held in-country), (ii) maintain adequate physical premises and full-time employees in Vanuatu, and (iii) perform the core income-generating activity in Vanuatu. Vanuatu suspended enforcement during 2021–2022 but has recommitted to phased enforcement to address FATF grey-listing and OECD/Global Forum scrutiny. A 3PL IC that is a mere contract name-plate without local workforce, office, or bank account will fall within the 'no economic substance' category and must be reported as such; banks increasingly require an economic substance return receipt before opening or maintaining USD settlement accounts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company / sham economic substance under the Economic Substance Act

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019, VFSC may impose a financial penalty of up to VUV 1,000,000 per breach plus VUV 100,000 for each continued month of non-compliance, order deregistration, and exchange intelligence with foreign tax authorities under TIEAs.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false substance declaration are personally liable for the penalty; courts may issue asset freezes and travel bans. Shadow directors at the Hong Kong/China operations hub identified through board minutes, email control, or bank mandates are equally exposed to civil restitution claims.
Fraudulent trading and false statutory declaration (International Companies Act)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to VUV 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months per offence. On winding-up, the court may order persons knowingly party to fraudulent trading to personally discharge all company debts — enforceable directly against the parent entity that dictated the sham.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution of any distributions paid when solvency tests were breached; personal liability for unpaid third-party logistics invoices, customs duties, and merchant refunds; disqualification from managing any VFSC-regulated entity and criminal custodial exposure.
Anti-money laundering / unexplained wealth (Financial Transactions Reporting Act [Cap 261])

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to VUV 1,000,000 per count, proceeds-of-crime confiscation orders, mandatory Suspicious Transaction Report filing by the licensed agent, and freezing of accounts held with clearing banks; referral to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Imprisonment for directing the money-flow scheme; disqualification orders; loss of access to the targeted payment rails (ACH, Airwallex, wire) resulting in stranded customer funds and personal exposure to merchant and consumer claims.
Failure to maintain registered agent / registered office and non-payment of annual fees

Statutory sanction: 10% surcharge on unpaid government fees; automatic strike-off after 60 days without notice; on strike-off the company ceases to exist and all Vanuatu-situs assets, including bank balances held through VFSC-licensed banks, vest in the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Loss of limited-liability protection for debts incurred after the strike-off date; directors become personally liable for all e-commerce fulfilment contracts and payment obligations transacted in the company's name thereafter.
