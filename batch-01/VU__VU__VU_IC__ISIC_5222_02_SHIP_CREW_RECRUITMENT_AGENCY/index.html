Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), an integrated revenue authority under the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official services portal and lodgement channels accessible via the VRC administrative website and the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) company registry portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company (IC) is incorporated through a licensed registered agent at the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC); the agent files the notice of incorporation, memorandum and articles, and beneficial ownership declarations. No automatic VAT or corporate income tax registration arises, as the IC is subject to the statutory tax exemption under the International Companies Act (Cap. 222). If a TIN is sought for customs duty accounts, employer obligations, or other administrative purposes, the agent submits a TIN application to VRC using the certificate of incorporation, a certified copy of the registered agent's licence, the registered office confirmation, and the passport/certified identification of the ultimate beneficial owner; VRC issues the TIN upon verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box, partnership classification, or hybrid-mismatch election is available under Vanuatu law. The International Companies Act (Cap. 222) automatically grants the IC an exemption from all Vanuatu taxes (income tax, stamp duty, capital gains, and any future turnover tax) for a renewable 20-year period; the entity's tax classification is fixed by statute and cannot be varied by shareholder or board election. No fiscal transparency regime exists, and an IC cannot elect to be treated as a corporation, partnership, or pass-through for Vanuatu tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT/GST or sales and use tax in Vanuatu; the indirect taxation framework comprises customs and excise duties, a prescribed Turnover Tax (e.g., 3.5% on hospitality and tour services), and municipal Business Licence Tax, none of which attach to an exempt International Company when properly constituted.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Vanuatu has no VAT/GST statute and therefore no economic turnover threshold for indirect tax registration. The only potentially applicable triggers are: (i) customs registration as importer/exporter for physical goods movements, and (ii) municipal business licence registration for any actual economic operation within Port Vila or Luganville; International Companies remain exempt provided no business is conducted with Vanuatu residents and no land is held.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Vanuatu does not levy VAT/GST, so there is no export zero-rating mechanism to claim. The export of ship crew recruitment and maritime crew management services by an IC falls entirely outside Vanuatu's indirect tax net: no turnover tax, export duty, or other consumption tax is imposed on offshore service fees settled by non-resident shipping principals. Physical export of goods is generally zero-rated by absence of duty, except for scheduled commodities subject to specific export duties under the Customs Act; a crew agency does not ordinarily generate goods exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (only where crew stores, equipment, or mobilisation effects are shipped out of Vanuatu)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (evidencing physical movement of any accompanying goods, stores, or equipment)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT banking credit evidence from the vessel owner/operator (to substantiate the offshore consideration received for crew supply and payroll services)

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, API reporting, or digital stamping mandate exists in Vanuatu; VRC operates a risk-based customs declaration processing system, and invoicing remains a private commercial matter subject to normal record-keeping rules under the corporate registry and AML frameworks.

There is no VAT zero-rating to claw back; the material legal risks arise from (i) misrepresenting goods to VRC customs to obtain invalid duty relief on crew stores or vessels, which yields assessments, interest at the statutory rate, civil penalties under the Customs Act, and seizure/forfeiture of goods; (ii) an IC engaging in prohibited domestic business with Vanuatu residents, which voids the statutory tax exemption and retrospectively exposes the entity to income tax, municipal business licence tax, turnover tax, and penalties; and (iii) false declarations or AML breaches under VFSC supervision, which may result in registration cancellation, deregistration of the IC, financial penalties, and adverse regulatory sanction against the licensed registered agent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a liberal exchange-control framework with no material capital-account restrictions. The Vatu (VUV) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. For an International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222], share subscription, capital injections, loan servicing, dividend distribution, and profit repatriation to non-resident owners are permitted without prior RBV approval, quantitative quotas, or withholding levies. The IC is a tax-exempt non-resident vehicle, so no taxation or exchange-control surcharge attaches to outward remittances. The practical constraints are liquidity depth (VUV/USD/AUD/EUR), correspondent-bank de-risking appetite, and documentation formality rather than statutory convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio on export or service receipts. Crew recruitment management fees, manning agency service revenue, and payroll-funding remittances may be retained in any convertible currency (USD, AUD, EUR, NZD, GBP) in onshore or offshore accounts. No time-bound repatriation or conversion window applies. The only obligations are AML/CFT reporting by RBV-licensed banks of foreign-currency and wire transactions above thresholds prescribed by the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit (VFIU), plus accurate booking of the economic substance of the transaction to satisfy beneficial-ownership transparency requirements.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 282], acting as the central bank, monetary authority, and licensing body for all commercial banks and licensed FX dealers in the Republic of Vanuatu.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for universal offshore digital EMIs. Tier-one overseas platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business) generally apply blanket territorial restrictions to Vanuatu tax-exempt entities, demand local substance, an RBV-licensed domestic bank relationship, or reject VU offshore entities outright under internal jurisdiction policy; a foreign EMI is not RBV-licensed to provide payment services into Vanuatu, which limits deposit-taking and dispute-recourse protections. A niche maritime payroll EMI specialising in seafarer disbursements is viable as a supplementary clearing rail but cannot serve as the sole treasury backbone. Recommended architecture: BSP/NBV domestic account as primary liquidity anchor with a specialised shipping-payroll EMI as a settlement accelerator.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the IC is routed via SWIFT MT103 for standard equity injections and MT202/COVER for large bank-to-bank transfers, denominated in USD or AUD and credited to the Capital Escrow Sub-Account under the bank relationship. The wire payment reference must clearly state 'Capital Contribution / Paid-in Surplus — [Company Name]' and be supported by a sole-member or board resolution authorising the capital injection and specifying the account of credit. Vanuatu does not operate a mandatory SAFE-style purpose-code regime, but the narrative field and underlying documentation must align with the crewmanning business model and identify the remitter's source of funds to avoid an AML compliance hold. The receiving bank will require: (i) registry extract or share-certificate evidence; (ii) corporate resolution; (iii) remitter bank source-of-funds confirmation; and (iv) UBO declaration of the contributing member.Outbound treasury flows clear through: (1) SWIFT MT103 priority payments for seafarer wage settlements in the crew member's designated currency (USD, EUR, PHP, IDR, INR, and other major currencies) via the bank's FX dealing desk; (2) multi-currency host-to-host payroll files initiated through BSP or NBV direct channels for vessels under pooled crew management; (3) commercial Letters of Credit, both sight and usance, issued or confirmed for shipowner/charterer reimbursement and master-hire obligations; and (4) interbank domestic VUV/USD instant transfers for Port Vila operational expenditures and local statutory licence fee payments.Onshore deliverable forward FX contracts (USD/AUD/EUR versus VUV) with tenors up to 12 months executed through RBV-licensed commercial banks, subject to the bank's credit line and underlying crew-payroll exposureCross-currency swaps (fixed-to-fixed and floating-to-fixed) transacted bilaterally with the relationship bank for rebalancing multi-currency payroll funding and matching USD receivables against EUR/PHP disbursement liabilitiesFX swaps (spot versus forward) used for settlement-date liquidity bridging between VUV, USD, and AUDOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in VUV are not permissible due to the absence of a liquid NDF market for the Vatu; any non-VUV NDF (e.g., AUD/USD) may be used solely to hedge third-currency payroll exposure and must be documented under the corporate treasury policy

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing regime exists in Vanuatu. There is no Local File or Master File requirement and no comparable threshold in local currency (Vatu). The country does not impose income tax, so the arm’s length principle is not codified in domestic law, but a prudent IC should document intra-group charges for crew payroll, management fees, and procurement as a safeguard against any substance or AML challenges.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu’s International Companies (Economic Substance) Act, No. 28 of 2019 requires an IC engaged in a 'relevant activity' to demonstrate adequate economic substance in Vanuatu. A ship crew recruitment agency is categorically related to 'shipping' or a 'distribution and service centre' depending on the precise revenue model. The IC must maintain a physical office or arrangement for board meetings in Vanuatu, have locally based staff where applicable, and incur proportionate local operating expenditure. A strict 'one-man shell' with no supporting substance is considered non-compliant and may trigger a penalty and forced strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to File Annual Return / Striking Off

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty up to VT 20,000; subsequent default leads to administrative strike-off under Section 123 of the International Companies Act. The company’s assets are dissolved and may be claimed by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for all debts of the company if the company trades after being struck off. The Court may grant restoration only upon payment of penalties and a bond, potentially exposing directors to personal costs.
Carrying on Domestic Business without a Local Trade License

Statutory sanction: The IC is prohibited from carrying on business in Vanuatu. Violations may result in a fine up to VT 500,000 and confiscation of profits earned from the unauthorized activity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the company to conduct unlicensed domestic business may be convicted of an offence, leading to a personal fine of up to VT 200,000 and imprisonment for up to two years.
Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2002 (as amended), an IC can be fined up to VT 100 million. The VFSC can revoke the certificate of incorporation and refer to the Financial Intelligence Unit for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face imprisonment up to 15 years and/or fine up to VT 50 million. Travel bans may be imposed pending investigation. The court can freeze personal assets and order disgorgement of any illicit gains.
Providing Crewing Services Without a VFSC or Maritime License

Statutory sanction: An unlicensed recruitment operation is a criminal offence under the Maritime Workers Act and the VMS circulars; fines reach VT 1 million per vessel crew placed, and the company is barred from all maritime business.

Director personal liability: Each director is personally criminally liable for every crew placement made without a licence, with penalties of imprisonment for up to 5 years and a personal fine not exceeding VT 2 million. In addition, all fees collected may be recoverable as a civil debt.
False Declaration of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to VT 5 million and mandatory strike-off. Any jurisdictional benefits, including the IC’s zero-tax status, are retroactively revoked, and the IC is liable for any unpaid domestic taxes on income attributable to Vanuatu.

Director personal liability: Directors signing a false declaration may be convicted of perjury and fraud, incurring personal fines up to VT 3 million and imprisonment up to 7 years. They are also jointly and severally liable for the company’s tax liabilities if the veil is pierced.
Tax Evasion in the Shareholder’s Home Jurisdiction

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu does not impose a sanction itself but will exchange data under TIEAs and the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters. The shareholder’s home country has full jurisdiction to assess penalties.

Director personal liability: The director/shareholder is exposed to home-country criminal tax evasion charges. Vanuatu courts have recognized foreign judgments for tax collection under the Reciprocal Enforcement of Judgments Act; personal assets in Vanuatu can be attached, and a foreign arrest warrant can be executed in Vanuatu for extraditable tax offences.
