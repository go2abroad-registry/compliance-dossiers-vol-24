Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue (VCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management (MoFEM); electronic filing via ASYCUDA World and the Vanuatu National Single Window (VNSW). Corporate identity is administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC).

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act Cap 222 is statutorily tax-exempt and is not required to obtain a domestic TIN or VAT ID; the VFSC certificate of incorporation serves as the statutory identifier. For customs export operations, the IC must register as a trader/exporter with VCIR in ASYCUDA World to obtain a Business Identification Number (BIN); a municipal business license (e.g., Port Vila City Council) is required only if a physical office is maintained. No turnover-based tax registration is triggered.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act Cap 222 is statutorily tax-exempt and is not required to obtain a domestic TIN or VAT ID; the VFSC certificate of incorporation serves as the statutory identifier. For customs export operations, the IC must register as a trader/exporter with VCIR in ASYCUDA World to obtain a Business Identification Number (BIN); a municipal business license (e.g., Port Vila City Council) is required only if a physical office is maintained. No turnover-based tax registration is triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None available. Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or withholding tax; the IC is exempt from all Vanuatu taxes and files no returns. No check-the-box, CFC, or hybrid-mismatch elections exist in domestic law, and Vanuatu maintains no double-tax treaties. The IC is statutorily barred from trading with Vanuatu residents or owning real property; breach of this condition can result in loss of tax-exempt status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax in force. Vanuatu's indirect revenue derives solely from customs import duties (Customs Act Cap 123, principally on alcohol, tobacco, fuel, and vehicles), stamp duty, municipal license fees, and a 12.5% hotel accommodation tax; none apply to an IC exporting returned goods or rendering offshore reverse-logistics services. Legislative proposals to introduce VAT have not been enacted.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; no consumption tax exists and therefore no turnover threshold triggers indirect-tax registration. Threshold: VUV 0 / N/A.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable by operation of law: with no VAT/GST in force, exports are outside the scope of indirect taxation rather than zero-rated. The IC charges no VAT on reverse-logistics fees and exports returned merchandise free of export duty. No refund mechanism exists and no export-VAT substantiation standard applies.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration filed in ASYCUDA World
  • Commercial invoice referencing the VFSC International Company registration number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Vanuatu
  • Packing list with condition/quality-grading report for returned merchandise
  • VFSC certificate of incorporation as proof of exporter identity
  • Certificate of Origin (only where required by the destination country)

None. Vanuatu imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no government-API e-invoicing mandate, and no fiscal digital stamping. The only electronic filing obligation is the customs export/import declaration submitted via ASYCUDA World through the Vanuatu National Single Window; no revenue-authority invoice clearance or stamp is required.

Vanuatu has no exchange-control legislation and imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the Vatu is freely convertible and foreign currency proceeds may be retained offshore indefinitely. No export-VAT clawback risk exists because no indirect tax is charged or refunded. Residual compliance exposure is customs-based: false export declarations, undervaluation, or unlawful duty-drawback claims attract fines and forfeiture under the Customs Act Cap 123, and any prohibited domestic trading by the IC may lead to revocation of its International Company license and forfeiture of tax-exempt status.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; current-account payments (trade invoices, logistics service fees, returns-grading charges, profit remittances) are freely convertible through authorised dealers without central-bank licensing. The VUV is managed against a weighted currency basket (primarily AUD and USD), is non-deliverable offshore, and settles only within Vanuatu. Capital-account transactions (equity injection, shareholder loans, acquisition funding) remain subject to RBV pre-approval thresholds or dealer sub-limits. Vanuatu levies no income tax, no capital gains tax, and no withholding tax on outbound dividends, interest, or royalties, so profit remittances face no tax-clearance hurdle but require an Exchange Control application and verified financials.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods and services (including ISIC 5229 reverse logistics and quality-grading fees), full export proceeds must be received through an authorised dealer in Vanuatu within six (6) months of shipment or invoice date and 100% of the proceeds must be surrendered/offered for sale into a VUV-denominated account, unless the RBV has granted a Foreign Currency Retention Account approval. The International Company is treated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes, enabling foreign-currency account facilities, but the surrender obligation still applies to export earnings. Failure to surrender constitutes an offence under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 62].

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act No. 2 of 1988, is the central bank and sole statutory exchange-control authority. Operational exchange-control administration is delegated to authorised dealers (licensed commercial banks) under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 62]. The Vatu (VUV) is the domestic legal tender, and all authorised FX movements must pass through the RBV-regulated banking system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium for VUV-denominated treasury design. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex) are not licensed as authorised dealers by the RBV under the Financial Dealers Act [Cap. 202], cannot access VUV local clearing, and cannot satisfy the export-proceeds surrender rule. They are viable only as ancillary USD/AUD/GBP settlement conduits for inbound fees from international returns-grading clients. The Vanuatu domestic banking account remains the mandatory liquidity spine.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in Vatu — Vanuatu has no codified transfer pricing law, no arm's-length statutory section, and no Local File / Master File threshold. Documentation is nonetheless mandated indirectly by the Economic Substance Regulations: any relevant entity carrying on a 'relevant activity' (which includes distribution and service centres and headquarters, covering ISIC 5229 reverse logistics) must maintain contemporaneous substance records — premises, employees, expenditure — regardless of turnover. For cross-border group transactions, bankers and foreign tax counterparts will apply OECD transfer pricing standards to the IC's intercompany charges; adequate documentation should cover the logistics service fee, returns-handling margin, and quality-grading revenue.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as amended 2021) apply fully to International Companies. A reverse-logistics and returned-goods grading IC that performs distribution and service-centre functions must demonstrate: (a) management and control exercised in Vanuatu through regular board meetings recorded in minutes; (b) adequate physical premises in Vanuatu; (c) a proportionate number of full-time employees in Vanuatu; and (d) adequate operating expenditure incurred in Vanuatu. Pure holding ICs enjoy reduced substance requirements, but trading ICs in the ISIC 5229 scope do not. Non-compliance results in VFSC assessment, a compliance notice, an administrative fine of up to VT 20,000,000, and potential strike-off; VFSC also shares non-compliance findings with treaty and TIEA partners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abusive Use of Tax-Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the Tax Exemption Certificate under Section 4 of the International Companies Act; loss of the zero-tax regime; VFSC-ordered cancellation of registration; financial penalty of up to VT 10,000,000 under the Companies (Amendment) Act 2024. Foreign tax authorities may additionally re-characterise the IC as a sham and impose penalties of 100%–200% of the underlying understated profit.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of all unpaid foreign taxes and penalties; Vanuatu courts may issue travel bans pending recovery proceedings under the Reciprocal Enforcement of Judgments Act; wilful evasion constitutes a criminal offence under the Penal Code [Cap 135] with imprisonment of up to 3 years for each count of false declaration.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: VFSC compliance notice followed by administrative penalty of up to VT 2,000,000 for the first ESR offence and up to VT 20,000,000 or 3 years' imprisonment for a continued wilful default; VFSC has power to strike the IC off the Register and to notify the foreign tax authority under the exchange-of-information article of the relevant TIEA or DTA.

Director personal liability: Directors face statutory personal fines, disqualification from managing Vanuatu companies for up to 5 years, and civil claims by the Registered Agent for unpaid fees; where the ESR violation is accompanied by false attestation, directors face criminal conviction and imprisonment; travel bans may be imposed during the VFSC investigation to prevent removal of records.
AML/CFT and Fraudulent Payment Routing

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 5 of 2022 and the 2023 Regulations, the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit (NFIU) may impose administrative penalties of up to VT 10,000,000 per breach; the VFSC may suspend or revoke the Registered Agent's license and order the closure of the IC's bank accounts; funds may be frozen and forfeited under restraint orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for criminal fines of up to VT 5,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 years for knowingly facilitating money laundering; the AML statute permits direct piercing of the corporate veil so that personal assets of the beneficial owner are subject to confiscation; a travel ban under the Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act may be applied to prevent flight.
False Statutory Declarations to VFSC and Tax Residency Abuses

Statutory sanction: An offence under the International Companies Act and the ESR Regulations punishable by a fine not exceeding VT 10,000,000 and cancellation of the IC's registration; the Tax Residency Certificate is revoked with retrospective effect, exposing all treaty-reduced payments to full domestic withholding tax and interest in the source jurisdiction; the VFSC will publish the strike-off in the Official Gazette.

Director personal liability: Directors making the false declaration are individually criminally liable and subject to imprisonment of up to 3 years; civil liability includes restitution of all treaty benefits improperly obtained and the foreign taxes evaded, with compound interest; Vanuatu courts may issue a freezing order over personal bank assets and a travel ban pending the criminal trial; disqualification from holding any office in a Vanuatu company for a period of 5 years is mandatory.
