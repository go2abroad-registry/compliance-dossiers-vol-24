Governing statute

International Companies Act [Cap 222] (Vanuatu), as amended by the International Companies (Amendment) Act No. 30 of 2019; key sections include s.1 (application and exemption from domestic tax), s.6 (powers of incorporation), s.7 (Certificate of Incorporation), s.9 (registered office), s.18 (memorandum of association), s.31 (directors), and s.45 (company secretary and officers).

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department (VCIRD), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; taxpayer registration and filing portal accessible via https://www.vancustoms.gov.vu and the VCIRD online taxpayer services portal, with customs export processing through the ASYCUDA World national single window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) obtains a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the VCIRD TIN Application Form accompanied by: certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation issued under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]; Memorandum and Articles of Association; registered office consent letter from the licensed Vanuatu registered agent; and KYC documents (passport, proof of address, and beneficial ownership declaration) for each director and ultimate beneficial owner. Applications are lodged online via the VCIRD portal or in person at Port Vila or Luganville offices; the TIN is ordinarily issued within 3-7 business days. Where the IC undertakes taxable supplies of goods in Vanuatu or opts to claim export zero-rating, it must additionally complete VAT registration (Form VAT-R) under the same TIN, either compulsorily when annual taxable turnover exceeds VT 10,000,000 or voluntarily when below threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned International Company (IC) obtains a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the VCIRD TIN Application Form accompanied by: certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation issued under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]; Memorandum and Articles of Association; registered office consent letter from the licensed Vanuatu registered agent; and KYC documents (passport, proof of address, and beneficial ownership declaration) for each director and ultimate beneficial owner. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no check-the-box, disregarded-entity, or other elective entity-classification regime. An International Company is statutorily exempt from all national income tax, corporate tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, withholding tax, and VAT on international business activities by operation of section 24 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222]. No hybrid-mismatch or look-through election is available or required; the IC files only the annual VFSC compliance returns and does not file a domestic income tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 of annual taxable turnover (mandatory registration); voluntary registration is expressly permitted below the threshold for exporters and persons supplying zero-rated goods or services such as ocean freight of export cargo.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the VAT Act for: (i) goods physically exported from Vanuatu by the registered supplier within the prescribed timeframe, including bulk Portland cement clinker shipped by ocean vessel to non-resident wholesale buyers; (ii) international freight, shipping, and transport services in respect of such export goods; and (iii) services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Vanuatu when the service is physically performed and the direct benefit of which is enjoyed wholly outside Vanuatu. The supplier must retain full documentary evidence of export and separately record zero-rated sales in the periodic VAT return; input VAT incurred on export-related purchases (port handling, inland haulage, vessel charter inputs, and agency fees) is creditable or refundable to the extent attributable to zero-rated turnover.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World electronic export declaration, assessed and cleared by VCIRD Customs)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (issued to non-resident consignee, evidencing the outbound movement of clinker from a Vanuatu port)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (HS Code of Portland clinker, quantity, Incoterms 2020, vessel name and voyage number)
  • Certificate of Origin (where required by the destination customs authority)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign exchange records evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Input VAT invoices for export-attributable purchases and the supporting VAT return schedules

Vanuatu does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, government API pre-clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate. A VAT-registered person must issue tax invoices (paper or secure PDF) containing the statutory particulars under the VAT Act: supplier name and TIN, purchaser name and TIN, invoice date, description and quantity of goods, value, applicable VAT amount, and, for zero-rated exports, the basis of zero-rating together with reference to the underlying customs export declaration. Customs export procedures are transacted electronically through ASYCUDA World.

Under the VAT Act and related VCIRD enforcement practice, any export zero-rating claim that is false, inflated, or unsubstantiated attracts: immediate retrospective clawback of the VAT refund together with additional tax of 25% per annum on the underpaid amount; monetary fines of up to VT 5,000,000 per offence for the company and its officers; imprisonment of up to 10 years for directors or managers involved in fraudulent evasion; and possible suspension or cancellation of the TIN and VAT registration. Upon audit, VCIRD may require production of the complete export documentation bundle within 14 days; failure to substantiate the physical export, the foreign consignee, or the receipt of proceeds reverses the zero-rating from the original filing date with penalty tax accruing accordingly.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a freely convertible currency system. There is no general exchange-control act, no central bank approval requirement for current-account or capital-account flows, and no blanket restriction on foreign-currency holdings or remittances. The Vatu is fully convertible through licensed commercial banks. Capital contributions, dividend distributions, liquidation proceeds, profit repatriation, and outward loan repayments are not subject to statutory convertibility hurdles. Actual operational constraints are limited to the relatively shallow offshore VUV market and the correspondent-banking compliance thresholds imposed by international partner banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio to Vatu. Export receipts may be retained in the original invoiced currency, converted at freely negotiated commercial rates, or credited to a foreign-currency operational account. Licensed banks apply only general anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist financing, sanctions screening, and know-your-customer due diligence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act [Cap. 261] and RBV directives.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), acting as the central bank and monetary authority under the Reserve Bank Act [Cap. 125], with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) responsible for International Company licensing and registry supervision. Vanuatu has no separate exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Vanuatu does not maintain a regulated electronic-money institution or payment-service provider licensing class distinct from commercial banks. Cross-border digital EMIs and non-bank payment platforms cannot obtain a local Vanuatu licence and are not acceptable as principal clearing or settlement entities for the IC treasury structure. Such platforms may be used only as supplementary invoicing/conversion conveniences, never as primary liquidity rails, because they create unlicensed payment-services exposure and weaken the local banking nexus. All regulated settlement must remain with a Vanuatu licensed commercial bank or its licensed foreign correspondents.

0% - Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax; International Companies are statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu income tax, VAT, stamp duty, capital gains tax, and withholding taxes under s.3 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222].

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividends declared or paid by an International Company to any shareholder, resident or non-resident, and no currency or capital controls restrict the repatriation of dividend proceeds except for AML verification of the receiving bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - Vanuatu has no domestic transfer-pricing legislation, no Local File or Master File obligations, and no transactional thresholds. For a wholesale cement and clinker distribution group, the relevant documentation thresholds are those of the parent and market jurisdictions (e.g., OECD-aligned rules generally require a Local File where related-party cross-border transactions exceed USD 1 million, and a Master File where group turnover exceeds USD 850 million). The Vanuatu IC must nevertheless maintain contemporaneous pricing evidence for its purchase and resale of Portland clinker to substantiate arm's length treatment before foreign auditors.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu has not enacted an Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act in the BVI/Cayman model. However, an International Company must maintain a registered agent and registered office in Port Vila, keep accounting records, and file beneficial ownership data under the Beneficial Ownership Act 2017. Vanuatu is presently included on the EU list of non-cooperative jurisdictions for tax purposes, and foreign tax authorities will apply their own economic substance, CFC, and GAAR rules to re-characterise the Vanuatu IC as a sham if management and control or core profit-generating functions for the clinker trade occur in a high-tax market country. A passively held VU IC with a licensed agent, proper board minutes, and genuine contractual documentation will withstand scrutiny; a paper-only shell will not.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Foreign Tax Evasion via Offshore Billing (CFC / CRS Mismatch)

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu itself has no income tax sanction, but Vanuatu has participated in the OECD Common Reporting Standard and Automatic Exchange of Information since 2018 and will exchange financial account data with treaty partners. In the shareholder's home jurisdiction the consequence is deliberate tax evasion: increased assessments of up to 200% of the tax evaded, criminal fines, compensatory interest, and forfeiture orders covering the full value of the hidden offshore account.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false foreign tax returns or directed the sham billing arrangement are personally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid tax and may face criminal prosecution with travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment of up to 10 years under typical foreign tax fraud statutes; courts will pierce the Vanuatu corporate veil because the IC is a mere instrumentality of the controlling shareholder.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act [Cap 222], continuing failure to maintain a registered office and agent, or filing false compliance declarations, attracts a fine of up to VT 1,000,000 (approx. USD 8,500) plus a daily default fine of VT 10,000 (approx. USD 85) per day. The Registrar may strike the company off, causing all assets to vest in the Government. Internationally, EU listing and OECD substance requirements compel banks and counter-parties to treat the entity as non-compliant, resulting in account closure and the denial of treaty or withholding benefits.

Director personal liability: The nominee director is personally liable for the local fines; foreign tax courts will disregard the entity and impose the full tax liability on the UBO, authorising seizure of personal assets and restraint orders over director bank accounts in the market jurisdiction.
Money Laundering / Trade-Based Money Laundering (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Contravention of the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 13 of 2018: a body corporate is liable to a fine of up to VT 20,000,000 (approx. USD 170,000); a natural person is liable to a fine of up to VT 10,000,000 (approx. USD 85,000) or imprisonment for up to 12 years, or both. The Vanuatu FIU and VFSC may freeze accounts, revoke the registered agent's licence, and confiscate the proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for the corporate fine; restraint orders, travel bans, and asset confiscation orders apply to personal property; directors are extraditable under mutual legal assistance arrangements to the foreign market country where the underlying customs or value-added tax fraud occurred.
Failure to File Annual Return and Pay Annual Licence Fee

Statutory sanction: A late penalty of VT 20,000 per month of delay plus a 20% surcharge on the unpaid licence fee; after 12 months of continued default the Registrar may strike the company off under ss.109-112 of the IC Act, at which point all company assets automatically vest in the Registrar for sale or disposal in favour of the Government of Vanuatu.

Director personal liability: After striking-off, the limitation of liability ceases and each director is jointly and severally liable for all company debts and obligations incurred after the strike-off; the Supreme Court may impose disqualification orders of up to 5 years preventing the directors from acting in any company in Vanuatu.
Prohibited Local Business and VAT Evasion

Statutory sanction: The IC Act prohibits International Companies from carrying on business with Vanuatu residents, owning land, or holding leases other than the registered office lease. Contravention attracts a fine of up to VT 1,000,000 (approx. USD 8,500) and a daily default fine of VT 50,000 (approx. USD 425), the loss of tax-exemption for the offending transaction, and retrospective assessment of 12.5% VAT plus late penalties of up to 25% of the tax due by the Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Authority.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorised the local transaction are personally liable for the unpaid VAT, the penalty, and any tax default interest; making a false declaration to the Commissioner of Revenue is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment of up to 2 years, and directors may be removed from office by court order.
