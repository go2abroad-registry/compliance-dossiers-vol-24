Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue & Customs Authority (VRCA), Port Vila, Republic of Vanuatu; the statutory offices are the Revenue Division and Customs Division of VRCA, which jointly administer the Value Added Tax Act No. 14 of 1998 (as amended by the Value Added Tax (Amendment) Act 2023), Customs Act [CAP 77], Excise Tax Act [CAP 78], Withholding Tax Act, and Stamp Duties Act. The official taxpayer portal is https://vrca.gov.vu; all customs declarations are processed electronically through the ASYCUDA World single-window system. Company registration and International Company (IC) administration are handled separately by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) at https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

An International Company registered under the International Companies Act [CAP 222] is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes and is not required to obtain a tax identification number unless it voluntarily carries on a taxable activity inside Vanuatu, which the IC Act otherwise prohibits. Standard procurement procedure for any foreign entity requiring a Vanuatu taxpayer identity: (1) determine whether the entity has a Vanuatu taxable activity, customs presence, or import/export nexus; (2) if mandatory registration applies, submit the VRCA VAT registration application (Form VRCA-VAT-01) within 21 days of commencement or of crossing the registration threshold, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered-office certificate from the Vanuatu registered agent, specimen tax invoice, and identity documentation for directors and beneficial owners; (3) receive the VAT registration certificate and unique VAT identification number; (4) use the same VAT number in ASYCUDA World for customs transactions; (5) obtain a municipal Business License only if a physical place of business exists in Vanuatu. In the standard offshore copper cathode arbitrage model, the IC executes LME warrant transfers and documentary letters of credit entirely outside Vanuatu, the goods never enter Vanuatu customs territory, and therefore no tax ID is procured; the IC's VFSC registration number remains its sole identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu does not operate a check-the-box partnership classification system or an OECD-style hybrid mismatch election regime. The operative statutory classifications and elections are: (i) the election to incorporate as an International Company rather than a domestic company under the International Companies Act [CAP 222], which places the entity in the statutory tax-exempt offshore class; (ii) voluntary VAT registration for persons below the compulsory threshold, subject to VRCA discretion and a minimum registration period; (iii) conversion of a domestic company to an International Company, and reconversion, upon VFSC approval; and (iv) the election to zero-rate qualifying exports of goods under the VAT Act. No corporate income tax election exists because Vanuatu imposes no income tax, capital gains tax, or inheritance tax; the 15% final withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties paid to non-residents under the Withholding Tax Act is inapplicable to exempt International Companies and their non-resident shareholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 10,000,000 (approximately USD 83,500 equivalent) of aggregate taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period, effective from 1 September 2023; the previous compulsory threshold was VUV 4,000,000. Voluntary registration is available below the threshold with VRCA approval, and the threshold is sector-neutral.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT Act, a supply of goods is zero-rated when the supplier exports the goods from Vanuatu and the goods are physically exported by the supplier within 28 days, or within such further period as the Comptroller approves, and the supplier retains the prescribed documentary evidence. Services directly connected with goods in transit through Vanuatu, international freight, and any supply of goods that are not in Vanuatu at the time of supply are also zero-rated or outside the scope of VAT. For a Vanuatu International Company trading Grade A refined copper cathodes on LME terms, where the cathode is procured from a non-Vanuatu refinery and delivered outside Vanuatu with title passing via LME warrant transfer and payment by documentary LC, no Vanuatu VAT charge arises and no refund mechanism is required. To the extent that input VAT is incurred on goods or services bought in Vanuatu for the trading operation, the input credit is available under the standard credit rules, and any excess attributable to zero-rated exports is refundable upon application subject to VRCA verification of the export evidence bundle.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed in ASYCUDA World (Export SAD/Entry) evidencing physical departure from Vanuatu, where the goods leave Vanuatu customs territory
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, endorsed to the offshore buyer, evidencing direct shipment without a Vanuatu port call or transshipment
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated tax invoice in the statutory VAT Act form, referencing the Grade A copper cathode specification and LME warrant number
  • Packing list, weight certificate, and refinery certificate of analysis issued by an LME-approved refinery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 payment evidence demonstrating receipt of the export sale proceeds
  • LME warrant transfer statement and delivery order evidencing the chain of title through non-Vanuatu LME-registered warehouses

Vanuatu does not currently mandate real-time e-invoicing, continuous transaction controls, digital fiscal stamping, or government clearance of invoices. Registered taxpayers must issue paper or PDF tax invoices satisfying the content requirements of the VAT Act (supplier and recipient name and VAT number, description, quantity, consideration, VAT amount, and zero-rating statement where applicable) and must file VAT returns electronically through the VRCA portal. All import and export declarations must be lodged electronically through ASYCUDA World, which assigns a clearance reference and applies automatic selectivity and risk profiling; this constitutes a customs clearance obligation rather than an invoice-stamping mandate. Payment rails relevant to Grade A copper cathode arbitrage (LME warrant transfers and documentary letters of credit) fall entirely outside this clearance mandate.

VRCA operates a risk-based audit program concentrated on export zero-rating and refund abuse, including fictitious paper exports without ASYCUDA World clearance, staged LME warrant chains, and refund claims unsupported by inward remittance evidence. Consequences of non-compliance include: (i) retrospective clawback of VAT credits or refunds with statutory interest; (ii) penalties of up to VUV 500,000 per false statement, with cumulative per-offence fines; (iii) imprisonment of up to 2 years for knowingly false VAT returns or customs declarations; (iv) seizure and forfeiture of goods under the Customs Act; and (v) suspension or deregistration of the taxpayer's refund access. In copper cathode transactions, VRCA cross-checks physical movement evidence, bill-of-lading issuance, LME warehouse delivery records, and SWIFT payment flows; any misrepresentation of a non-Vanuatu transit as an export from Vanuatu, or the conversion of a domestic sale into a zero-rated export through forged documentation, triggers criminal referral and a minimum clawback equal to the full VAT amount plus penalties. Although Vanuatu imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation window for International Companies and the 180-day benchmark reflects documentary LC and banking compliance practice, export records must be retained for at least 7 years to satisfy audit scrutiny. The IC statutory tax exemption does not immunize the entity from VAT Act or Customs Act liability arising from fraudulent misrepresentations in tax and customs administration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is not a globally traded or freely convertible currency outside the South Pacific; however, Vanuatu maintains practical current-account convertibility for bona fide merchandise trade, with non-resident-controlled International Companies permitted to maintain foreign-currency accounts (USD, AUD, EUR) at RBV-licensed commercial banks. Capital-account movements are permitted under a notification-based exchange-control regime rather than a prior-approval regime. Profit and dividend remittances require an audited balance sheet, board resolution, and a signed exchange-control declaration filed by the remitting bank for balance-of-payments statistics; no withholding tax or capital gains tax is levied on an exempt International Company, subject to no VU-source trading activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no mandatory surrender or minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds received by an International Company. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in USD/AUD accounts at licensed banks. For trade settlement and regulatory traceability, the exporter must retain the customs export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and proof of payment so that the Vanuatu receiving bank can complete RBV balance-of-payments reporting. If a domestic pre-export finance facility is used, the financing bank may contractually impose a 180-day collection/realisation covenant.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) — the sole monetary authority and exchange control administrator under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 112].

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low direct viability. Offshore digital EMI and virtual-account platforms are not licensed by the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu and cannot access local clearing, LME settlement, or VUV-based rails. They may be used only as ancillary collection/float vehicles outside the regulated banking perimeter; all physical settlement, LC issuance, and delivery-vs-payment flows for copper cathode wholesale must be executed through RBV-licensed commercial banks or their international correspondent banking network. Using offshore EMIs as the primary treasury route would raise severe AML, sanctions, and audit-ability concerns.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity injections must be executed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the IC's licensed VU bank account, with a clear beneficiary line naming the International Company and the remittance narrative as 'Equity Contribution to VU_IC per Shareholder Resolution'. The receiving bank may request the IC's board resolution, shareholder register extract, and a certified copy of the share subscription/resolution for verification. The purpose should be classified as direct investment/equity participation in the RBV balance-of-payments record; no prior RBV approval is required for a standard non-resident equity injection into an exempt IC. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax at the national or sub-national level; an International Company (VU_IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act (Cap. 222) is wholly outside the income tax net.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu law imposes no withholding tax on dividends, no undistributed-profits tax, and no capital gains tax on shareholder distributions, regardless of the recipient jurisdiction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer-pricing documentation threshold exists in Vanuatu for International Companies (zero CIT; no domestic TP rules enacted). As a prudential OECD-aligned benchmark, the IC voluntarily maintains a Master File/Local File when intercompany commodity transactions exceed VUV 100 million equivalent per annum to defend against CFC, GILTI, and ATAD adjustments in the ultimate-parent jurisdiction.

Economic substance scope: No domestic Economic Substance Regulations apply: Vanuatu does not administer ESR filings, gross-tax tests, or substance questionnaires for International Companies. The operative restrictions are statutory: under the International Companies Act (Cap. 222) an IC may not carry on business in Vanuatu, may not offer shares/securities to the Vanuatu public, and may not hold an interest in Vanuatu land except in narrow prescribed circumstances. The controlling economic-substance screening layer is therefore external (US CFC/GILTI, EU ATAD, OECD Pillar Two QDMTT and secondary top-up rules). Prudent ICs evidence substance through offshore board minutes, a VFSC-licensed registered agent, segregated bank accounts, independent LME warrant custody statements, signed trade confirmations, and complete documentary LC files maintained as digital archives for AML/CFT review.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Offshore Attestation

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under the International Companies Act (Cap. 222) and general Vanuatu criminal law: statutory fines plus imprisonment of up to 2 years per offence, VFSC deregistration, and recapture of diverted tax by the foreign revenue authority through exchange-of-information or judgment enforcement; civil penalties in the sourcing jurisdiction can exceed 100% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or procure false declarations are personally liable: civil restitution of evaded taxes, disqualification orders, travel bans, asset-freeze orders, and imprisonment of up to 2 years under Vanuatu law; foreign penal or civil judgments may be enforced under mutual legal assistance arrangements.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: No direct Vanuatu ESR sanction (no ESR regime exists); consequences arise externally — OECD Pillar Two top-up tax at up to 15%, CFC/GILTI inclusions, denial of treaty/administrative benefits, and correspondent-bank de-risking — and domestically through FATF-greylist-related AML enforcement and VFSC scrutiny of the registered agent CDD file.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally for unpaid foreign taxes on a joint-and-several basis where the corporate veil is pierced; travel bans and director-disqualification orders may issue in the foreign jurisdiction; in Vanuatu, the registered agent may resign causing strike-off, leaving directors exposed to indemnity claims by financiers and counterparties for consequential loss.
AML/CFT and Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Enforcement under the Vanuatu AML/CFT Act (No. 4 of 2014), Financial Institutions (AML) directives, and Proceeds of Crime Act (Cap. 293): substantial administrative fines against the company, forfeiture of the proceeds, FIU asset-freeze orders, revocation of the registered agent licence, and FATF compliance consequences; money-laundering convictions carry custodial penalties of up to 20 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who aid money laundering face individual prosecution: multi-year custodial sentences, personal pecuniary penalties, travel bans pending investigation, confiscation of personal assets, and prohibition orders barring future company management in Vanuatu and mutually-recognizing jurisdictions.
Late Filing and Strike-Off

Statutory sanction: Administrative strike-off from the VFSC register with loss of separate legal personality; assets vest in the Government of Vanuatu as bona vacantia; restoration requires a court order plus payment of arrears, an administrative late penalty of approximately VUV 25,000, and agent re-engagement costs.

Director personal liability: Directors trade at personal risk after strike-off: joint-and-several liability for debts and contracts entered after dissolution, inability to execute LME warrant transfers in the company name, and criminal exposure if de facto continuation of business is proven.
