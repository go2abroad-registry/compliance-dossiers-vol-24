Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Business Registration Agency of the Ministry of Planning and Investment (MPI) as the overarching body; operational authority exercised through the Business Registration Office of the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) where the Company's registered office is located; online filings are accepted via the National Enterprise Registration Portal (thongtindoanhnghiep.gov.vn).

Governing statute

Law on Enterprises No. 59/2020/QH14 (Enterprise Law 2020), effective 1 January 2021, in particular Articles 22 (conditions for enterprise registration), 25 (application documentation), 44 (mandatory charter contents), 45 (charter content requirements for LLCs), 47 (three-working-day issuance deadline for Enterprise Registration Certificate), 50 (right to reject and procedural recourse), 72-89 (limited liability company formation, members, charter capital), 75 (obligation to contribute charter capital within 90 days), and 196 (registered office and corporate seal). Elaborated by Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP on enterprise registration; for foreign investors, Law on Investment No. 61/2020/QH14, Articles 36-37 and Decree No. 31/2021/ND-CP on market access conditions.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Enterprise Registration (Giay de nghi dang ky doanh nghiep) under Form I-1 of Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP, submitted together with Form I-2 (Company Charter) and member identity instruments; the DPI issues the Enterprise Registration Certificate (Giay chung nhan dang ky doanh nghiep) as the conclusive incorporation title.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum charter capital is fixed for multilingual localization activities under ISIC 7490, which constitute a non-conditional business line; accordingly, members may declare a charter capital appropriate to the venture. The declared charter capital must, however, be paid in full within ninety (90) days from the date of issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate (Article 75, Law No. 59/2020/QH14); no escrow account or bank-locked deposit is independently required. Foreign investors are permitted 100% ownership and are not subject to a capital floor under Decree No. 31/2021/ND-CP for this scope.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế) of the Ministry of Finance, Socialist Republic of Viet Nam; national e-Tax portal: https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn (legacy portal: thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn); customs export declaration authority: General Department of Vietnam Customs (Tổng cục Hải quan) operating the VNACCS/VCIS national single window and the Vietnam National Single Window (NSW) for trade facilitation.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested Limited Liability Company obtains its Tax Identification Number (MST - Mã số thuế) without a standalone tax registration application because Vietnam operates an integrated enterprise-and-tax registration mechanism. Procedure: (1) obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment or the relevant Industrial Zone / Export Processing Zone authority for new foreign-invested projects; (2) file the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) application containing the charter, list of members, legal representative and headquarters address; (3) the General Department of Taxation automatically issues the MST upon ERC issuance, in accordance with Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP on business registration and the Law on Tax Administration No. 38/2019/QH14; (4) activate the e-Tax account on the national e-Tax portal and register for electronic invoice issuing status in the General Department of Taxation's invoice registration system; (5) open a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) and a general current account at a licensed commercial bank; (6) if exporting or importing physical goods or media, register as a customs declarant through the VNACCS/VCIS system and obtain a customs code; the MST is the uniform identifier for all CIT, VAT, PIT withholding, Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT), customs and social insurance filings. For localization activities with no physical import-export, only e-Tax and e-invoice activation are required; a customs registration is not necessary unless media masters or hardware cross borders.

Available election forms

Viet Nam does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections: (a) VAT method election: an LLC may elect the credit/deduction method only when its annual revenue exceeds VND 1 billion, but a newly incorporated LLC meeting accounting standards may voluntarily register for the deduction method; firms under the threshold use the direct method on invoiced added value unless they elect the deduction method. (b) Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) election: a foreign service provider with a PE or an enterprise contracting cross-border can elect the 'normal VAT/CIT filing method' or the 'deemed assessment method' for withholding purposes; the deemed method is mandatory unless a valid Vietnamese tax registration and proper books are demonstrated. (c) Foreign Tax Credit election: resident LLCs may elect to credit or deduct qualifying foreign income taxes on worldwide income; no deemed-paid credit is available for dividends from foreign subsidiaries beyond treaty/legislative provisions. (d) CIT incentive election: an eligible EPE/High-Tech/Industrial-Zone project must file a tax registration addendum and apply for a CIT incentive certificate (exemption for up to 4 years, 50% reduction for the next 9 years, standard rates of 10%, 15% or 17% depending on zone and scale); the election is made by indicating the incentive within the self-assessment return. (e) Loss carryforward is mandatory and non-elective: losses offset against income over the next 5 consecutive years, with no carryback. No group consolidation election exists; no hybrid-election mechanism is available because Decree No. 132/2020/ND-CP imposes a mandatory disallowance of interest on hybrid instruments treated as equity under foreign law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 0 for corporate entities — every LLC must register for VAT at incorporation regardless of initial capital, turnover or duration; there is no de-minimis turnover exemption for companies. For household businesses (hộ kinh doanh) an annual turnover threshold of VND 100,000,000 applies for VAT and CIT exemption under the Law on Tax Administration; such household entities may voluntarily register for VAT. Export processing enterprises (EPE) and cross-border service providers must still obtain an MST and register for the 0% export trader status from the first invoice, and may file quarterly VAT returns on a cash-basis where their turnover is modest.

Mandatory electronic invoicing for all VAT-registered taxpayers under Decree No. 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular No. 78/2021/TT-BTC. E-invoices must be issued in standardized XML (GS1VN data model) and transmitted to the General Department of Taxation via the e-Tax portal or through GDT-certified software using the TCBS API. Most taxpayers operate under the 'invoice authorization code' (Mã của cơ quan thuế) mechanism: the software sends each invoice record to GDT, GDT validates and returns a unique fiscal code, and the invoice is legally valid only after that code is affixed and the data is archived on the GDT server. Paper invoices and pre-printed forms are invalid since 1 July 2022. Retail, catering, petrol and B2C digital platforms must use 'e-cashier invoices' (hóa đơn điện tử khởi tạo từ máy tính tiền) with real-time, line-item clearance through GDT APIs. There is no physical stamping requirement; digital signatures, XML envelopes and reversible QR codes serve as the authenticity seal. For B2B export invoices (including localization services), the taxpayer may use self-generated e-invoices without a CQT code only after completing registration of the invoice form and after validating export documentation; such invoices must be reported to GDT in batch within 24 hours of issuance or at the statutory reporting interval for non-coded invoices, whichever is earlier.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Viet Nam operates a managed-float regime in which the Vietnamese đồng (VND) is not freely convertible outside the licensed interbank market and cross-border capital transactions are heavily controlled. Current-account transactions, including service exports and profit remittances related to genuine operating flows, are generally convertible: an authorized bank may sell foreign currency for import/export, service, and investment-related current payments upon submission of a valid underlying contract, invoice, and tax certificate, with no SBV pre-approval. Capital-account transactions, by contrast, are not freely convertible: foreign equity contributions, shareholder loans, loan repayments, and capital repatriation must be routed through a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at an authorized bank, and certain transactions require prior registration with the SBV or the licensed bank. Profit remittance is permissible once annual audited financial statements, a completed corporate income tax finalization, and a tax-obligation clearance/confirmation are obtained; in practice, banks will block an outward remittance that lacks the audited statement, tax finalization, and board resolution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For an ISIC 7490 localization service exporter, all foreign-currency receipts from non-resident clients are service export proceeds and must be credited to a foreign-currency account at an SBV-licensed bank in Viet Nam. The statutory benchmark is that proceeds are repatriated no later than the contractual due date; where the contract is silent, the bank applies a 90-day window from the invoice/service-completion date. There is currently no mandatory percentage-surrender ratio in force for service export earnings; exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency and elect to sell them to the authorized bank when VND liquidity is needed. The SBV retains emergency reserve power to impose a temporary surrender ratio in periods of severe balance-of-payments pressure, in which case the ratio would be published in an SBV decision and applied from its effective date. The treasurer should maintain original signed localization agreements, per-invoice reconciliation, and MT103/MT202 payment evidence to demonstrate timely repatriation and to support the lawfulness of retained foreign-currency balances.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV; Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam), the central bank and foreign exchange administration authority responsible for exchange-rate policy, capital-account controls, licensing of authorized credit institutions for FX activities, and supervision of cross-border payments and treasury compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Conditional and ancillary only. Wise B2B and Payoneer are unlicensed foreign EMI/payment-service providers in Viet Nam and cannot be used as a primary treasury clearing rail, a VND current account, or a capital-account channel. They are acceptable as a collection-side convenience for low-risk localization fees from non-resident clients, provided all funds are ultimately settled through a local sponsor bank into the VN company's bank account, each inbound credit is reconciled to a specific invoice and service-export declaration, and the company maintains its complete cash/fiscal position in onshore licensed accounts. The SBV does not recognize these EMIs as authorized credit institutions; using them to circumvent DICA rules or to receive equity capital would be unlawful.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The investor initiates a SWIFT MT103 from its offshore bank to the VN LLC's DICA at the onshore licensed bank. Beneficiary account must be the DICA; Payment Details/Remittance Information must identify the IRC number, the entity name, and the purpose 'equity capital contribution'. The receiving bank reconciles the message against the DICA, IRC, ERC, charter, and the registered capital-contribution schedule before crediting. Once credited, the foreign-currency balance may be sold to VND at that bank's published exchange rate for operating liquidity; conversion is treated as a separate FX transaction and cannot be effected directly inside the DICA. Any subsequent movement from the DICA to the company's ordinary VND/FX operating account requires a board resolution or capital-utilization note describing the operating purpose. Equity remittances that are denominated in a non-USD currency should be converted into USD at an offshore bank before transmission, or accepted only if the VN bank explicitly agrees to multi-currency DICA crediting.The primary rail for service-provider payments is SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the LLC's licensed-account treasury. For counterparties in higher-risk jurisdictions or for large engagements, the bank will require the underlying localization/subcontract agreement, an invoice, proof of service acceptance, and the foreign contractor withholding tax certificate (VAT/CIT certificates) before executing the transfer. For physical deliverables such as dubbing or subtitling equipment integrated into localization work, an irrevocable sight letter of credit issued under UCP600 through Vietcombank/VietinBank/BIDV is the prudent commercial-clearing rail, with the shipping documents routed through the advising bank. VND-denominated disbursements to domestic suppliers can be cleared via NAPAS 24/7 or standard local ACH. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditional and ancillary only. Wise B2B and Payoneer are unlicensed foreign EMI/payment-service providers in Viet Nam and cannot be used as a primary treasury clearing rail, a VND current account, or a capital-account channel. They are acceptable as a collection-side convenience for low-risk localization fees from non-resident clients, provided all funds are ultimately settled through a local sponsor bank into the VN company's bank account, each inbound credit is reconciled to a specific invoice and service-export declaration, and the company maintains its complete cash/fiscal position in onshore licensed accounts. The SBV does not recognize these EMIs as authorized credit institutions; using them to circumvent DICA rules or to receive equity capital would be unlawful.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VND 50 billion total annual revenue or VND 30 billion total related-party transaction value (whichever applies) triggers mandatory preparation of a Local File. In addition, if the ultimate parent group (whether in Viet Nam or abroad) has consolidated revenue exceeding VND 18,000 billion or total consolidated assets exceeding VND 20,000 billion, the VN LLC must prepare a Master File. Country-by-Country (CbC) report is mandatory when the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds VND 18,000 billion. These thresholds are set out in Decree 132/2020/ND-CP and Circular 80/2021/TT-BTC.

Economic substance scope: Viet Nam's transfer pricing and anti-avoidance framework under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP requires that related-party transactions have genuine economic substance and be structured with a sound business purpose. Article 17 specifically allows tax authorities to re-characterize transactions that lack economic substance, including transactions whose primary objective is to obtain tax benefits without actual commercial reality. For a localization agency owning minimal staff, no operating premises, or contracts that merely channel payments to a foreign affiliate, the substance doctrine may invalidate deductions, add deemed income, and impose penalties. The General Department of Taxation also applies an Economic Substance guidance consistent with OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action 3 principles. The VN LLC must be able to demonstrate real functions, assets, risks, and decision-making in Viet Nam.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / False corporate income tax return

Statutory sanction: Under the Vietnamese Penal Code 2015 (Article 200 as amended 2017), legal entities that commit tax evasion with improper revenue exceeding VND 100 million are subject to fines ranging from VND 300 million to VND 4.5 billion; natural persons (directors, controllers) face fines up to VND 4.5 billion and/or prison terms of 1 to 20 years, depending on the amount illegally evaded. Administrative sanctions in lesser cases include a penalty of 20% of the underpaid tax, plus late-payment interest at 0.03% per day and potential suspension of business operations.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives, chief accountants, and controlling shareholders can personally be held criminally liable. Courts can pierce the corporate veil if the company is an alter ego of the director; assets of the director may be seized for civil restitution. Exit bans (travel bans) are automatically applicable for unpaid tax debts until repayment.
Shell company / Lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may re-characterize the entire business as a conduit arrangement, disallow all deductible expenses, assess CIT at 20% with a 20% penalty on underpaid tax, impose late-payment interest, and, for 3 consecutive years, escalate to criminal tax evasion. The tax authority may also refuse to issue tax finalization certificates or PTI (Payment of Tax – Income) documents necessary for profit repatriation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the whole tax deficiency where they knowingly orchestrated the shell arrangement. Civil restitution, dissolution, and travel bans apply; criminal imprisonment is possible if the arrangement defrauds the State of more than VND 1 billion.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Vietnamese tax authorities can apply TP adjustments, recalculate taxable income, and impose penalties of 20% of the underpaid tax plus late-payment interest of 0.03% per day. Failure to submit TP documentation or CbC report may result in administrative fines of VND 20 million to VND 50 million; an adjusted TP audit may also be criminalized if deemed intentional evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and chief financial officers who sign documentation or approve transactions may be subject to administrative fines and criminal liability for fraud. Courts may annul the separate legal personality of the subsidiary and attach personal assets of the director if the intercompany pricing was structured to circumvent tax obligations.
Anti-Money Laundering / Cross-border remittance irregularities

Statutory sanction: Under the Penal Code 2015 Article 324, money laundering by a legal entity is punishable by fines up to VND 20 billion; natural persons who are directors or beneficial owners face imprisonment of 2 to 15 years. Violations of foreign exchange control provisions can lead to administrative fines up to VND 200 million, forced repatriation, and suspension of payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally arrested and imprisoned; assets can be frozen; travel bans apply; the corporate veil is pierced if the company is operated as a vehicle for laundering criminal proceeds.
Failure to withhold Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT)

Statutory sanction: The VN LLC as withholding agent is liable for the unwithheld FCT amount, plus a penalty of 20% of the underwithheld tax and late-payment interest at 0.03% per day. The deductible expense paid abroad is disallowed for CIT purposes until the FCT is settled. Repeated violations can trigger criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or knowingly allow the non-withholding may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate debt; criminal proceedings may be instituted for intentional failure. Travel bans and property attachment are available enforcement measures.
