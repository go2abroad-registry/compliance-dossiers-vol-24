Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; Department of Finance and Treasury (tax policy and revenue administration); Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue (customs duties, excise, and stamp duties); Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) as the regulator of International Companies. Official portals: www.finance.gov.vu, www.vfsc.vu

E-government filing portal

Vanuatu does not operate an income tax system and does not issue tax identification numbers for income tax purposes. A foreign-owned International Company (IC) is constituted by a Registered Agent under the International Companies Act [Cap 222], and the VFSC issues a Certificate of Incorporation and a company registration number. No separate TIN or VAT ID is required. If the IC imports or exports physical goods, it must obtain a customs importer/exporter code from Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue by filing a customs registration application together with the certificate of incorporation, registered agent confirmation, and principal place of business information. A provincial Business Licence may be required if the IC carries on physical business within Vanuatu, but that is a licensing matter and not a tax registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu law does not provide a check-the-box system or any domestic hybrid mismatch election. An International Company is automatically exempt from Vanuatu income tax, corporate tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty, and any future direct tax, pursuant to the International Companies Act. The company may, in its constitutional documents, elect to operate as an exempt International Company and prohibit domestic business activities in Vanuatu. Foreign tax classification of the IC is purely determined by the investor‘s home jurisdiction rules for controlled foreign companies, CFC look-through, or entity transparency; Vanuatu makes no domestic election available for such treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Vanuatu has no VAT, no GST, and no general sales or use tax. The only indirect levies are customs import duties, selective excise duties, stamp duties, and tourism promotion levy. No consumption tax registration obligation exists.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. For customs purposes, any importer or exporter must obtain a Customs Importer/Exporter Code irrespective of turnover. For physical business activity within Vanuatu, a provincial Business Licence is required; there is no statutory turnover threshold for tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Vanuatu. Since Vanuatu does not impose VAT, there is no domestic mechanism for zero-rating exports or for claiming input VAT export refunds. All exports of collectible trading cards and similar goods are outside the Vanuatu indirect tax net. If the goods are exported from Vanuatu, customs export entry is required; no local tax zero-rating approval or refund application procedure applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA export entry) — mandatory for physical export
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill — mandatory to evidence international transport
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List — mandatory for customs valuation and description of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — not required under Vanuatu exchange control or customs law, but may be included in the export dossier for banks or foreign tax authority substantiation

No electronic fiscal invoice, real-time clearance, digital stamping, or API-based tax clearance mandate applies in Vanuatu because no VAT/GST system exists. Customs processing is performed electronically through the ASYCUDA World single-window system for export and import declarations, and customs duties are settled before clearance. There is no requirement to transmit invoices to any tax authority.

There is no Vanuatu VAT zero-rating clawback because no VAT benefit exists. However, false customs declarations, misdescription of goods, or under-valuation in an export entry may subject the exporter to penalties under the Customs and Excise Act, including monetary fines, forfeiture of goods, imposition of additional duties, and, in serious cases, criminal prosecution. Records supporting customs declarations should be retained for at least seven years and made available to Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue upon request. Since Vanuatu imposes no exchange controls, no statutory FX repatriation deadline applies; the 180-day period stated in this dossier is a placeholder reflecting international best practice and may be imposed by foreign tax or banking regulations rather than Vanuatu law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a floating exchange-rate regime with no material current-account exchange controls. The vatu is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including proceeds from the sale of collectible trading cards and direct trade-related payments. Capital-account transactions of an International Company are largely unrestricted under the International Companies Act, and profit or capital remittances to non-resident shareholders are lawful without prior regulatory approval. The principal practical hurdles relate to banking-level source-of-funds checks and counterparty confirmation rather than statutory exchange-control limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu does not impose an export-proceeds surrender obligation and there is no statutory timeline for repatriation or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. The RBV prescribes anti-money-laundering screening on all inward remittances; once cleared, proceeds are freely credited to the International Company's domestic account and may be retained in VUV or converted at the prevailing market rate.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI providers are not licensed or supervised by the RBV and cannot operate as direct settlement institutions in Vanuatu. Crediting proceeds from an unlicensed EMI into a local Vanuatu bank account is operationally possible only after complete transparency on the originator and underlying commercial transaction. Legal feasibility is restricted by the RBV's AML/CFT licensing framework, which requires any entity providing payment services or money transmission to hold a licensed financial institution status in Vanuatu. Therefore, a licensed physical Vanuatu commercial bank account is mandatory for the treasury and clearing backbone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident shareholders are routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the shareholder's bank to the Vanuatu commercial bank account. The settlement instruction must clearly state 'Equity Capital Contribution — International Company VU_IC' and cite the business purpose of ISIC 3240 printing and trading-card publishing. No prior RBV approval is required, but the receiving bank will perform AML/UFC screening and may require the shareholder to provide recent bank statements, source-of-wealth, and a certification of the capital injection from the board. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable (VUV 0) - Vanuatu domestic law prescribes no transfer pricing documentation regime, no Local File, no Master File, and no transactional threshold for International Companies. Related-party pricing should nevertheless be arm's length and documented to OECD BEPS standards because the ESR administrative process and foreign tax authorities may scrutinise the IC.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) framework, as amended in 2021 and 2022, applies to ICs performing relevant activities: banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, holding company, intellectual property business, and distribution and service centres. A collectible-trading-card publisher that holds brand art and exploits it through remote sales is typically in scope for both the IP business and the distribution and service centre categories. Compliance requires: (i) management and control in Vanuatu; (ii) adequate physical premises, employees, and local expenditure; (iii) core income generating activities performed in Vanuatu; and (iv) an annual substance declaration filed with VFSC. A purely passive IC may remain outside the relevant-activity scope but must file to evidence that non-activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Prohibited Domestic Business and Trading with Vanuatu Residents

Statutory sanction: Offence under the International Companies Act (Cap. 222): offending transactions are void against the IC, fines are imposed on the company and each officer, and VFSC may order strike-off. Unlicensed local business operations additionally trigger VAT registration obligations at 12.5%, customs duties, and separate criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit resident dealings face personal fines, disqualification, deregistration, and personal liability for corporate debts arising from the prohibited course of business.
Economic Substance Failure and Shell Company Doctrine

Statutory sanction: ESR administrative penalties per contravention, daily default fines, mandatory information exchange with foreign and OECD or EU authorities, deregistration, and loss of the Section 20 tax-exemption protection as the legal fiction is disregarded. The IC may be re-characterised as a tax resident elsewhere for all open periods.

Director personal liability: Officers signing false substance declarations face criminal fines and imprisonment on conviction; directors may be disqualified from acting on any VFSC-regulated entity, and foreign courts may appraise taxes, interest, and penalties personally against directors under anti-avoidance rules.
False Statements, Fraud, and Fraudulent Trading

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under the IC Act: monetary fine and term of imprisonment, strike-off, forfeiture of filings, and reinstatement only by Court order after payment of all penalties. Creditors may petition for winding up and demand restitution of misapplied assets.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors and officers are personally liable for restitution of dissipated corporate assets and court costs; travel restrictions may be imposed while enforcement is pending, with custodial sentences for fraudulent trading.
AML/CTF Breach and Unlicensed Financial Dealing

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed financial dealing is an offence under Cap. 70 with fines, imprisonment, asset forfeiture, and VFSC licence revocation. The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2018 imposes customer due diligence, record-keeping, and reporting duties on reporting entities; breach attracts regulatory penalties and civil recovery.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines, disgorgement of processing proceeds, travel bans during investigation, and imprisonment in aggravated cases; merchant accounts are terminated and chargeback losses are recovered personally where nominee arrangements are proven.
Beneficial Ownership and Annual Fee Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: VFSC imposes late-payment surcharges, suspends the IC from transacting, strikes the company off the International Register after notice, and reports non-compliance to foreign tax authorities under the exchange-of-information framework. Reinstatement requires a Court order plus payment of all unpaid fees and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors remain statutorily liable for the IC's debts contracted during the non-compliant period, face monetary penalties, may be barred from acting as directors of any Vanuatu entity, and travel bans may issue for outstanding VFSC obligations.
