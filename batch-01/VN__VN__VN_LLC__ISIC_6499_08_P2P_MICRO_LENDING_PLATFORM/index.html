Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
General Department of Taxation of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam (Tong cuc Thue) under the Ministry of Finance; national tax e-filing portal: https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn; electronic declaration portal: https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn; foreign supplier information portal: https://fsr.gdt.gov.vn

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign-invested LLC: (1) obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment or the relevant industrial/export processing zone authority as applicable; (2) obtain an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); the enterprise code stated on the ERC is automatically the statutory tax identification number (MST) and serves simultaneously as the CIT, VAT, and payroll tax identification number; (3) activate electronic tax filing registration through eTaxVN at the provincial tax office using the issued TIN and digital certificate; (4) for a foreign digital supplier without a local legal entity, register directly on the General Department of Taxation's Foreign Supplier Portal and obtain a foreign supplier TIN, after which VAT and foreign contractor tax are declared and paid online. A separate physical VAT registration certificate is not required because the MST is the unified tax identity.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-invested LLC: (1) obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment or the relevant industrial/export processing zone authority as applicable; (2) obtain an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); the enterprise code stated on the ERC is automatically the statutory tax identification number (MST) and serves simultaneously as the CIT, VAT, and payroll tax identification number; (3) activate electronic tax filing registration through eTaxVN at the provincial tax office using the issued TIN and digital certificate; (4) for a foreign digital supplier without a local legal entity, register directly on the General Department of Taxation's Foreign Supplier Portal and obtain a foreign supplier TIN, after which VAT and foreign contractor tax are declared and paid online. A separate physical VAT registration certificate is not required because the MST is the unified tax identity.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
Vietnam does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election; a VN LLC is treated as a non-pass-through, separately taxable corporate entity for CIT purposes. Domestic tax law does not recognize foreign entity classification elections or hybrid mismatch elections. The only available elective mechanisms are: (i) election between the VAT credit (deduction) method and the direct method on value-added/turnover for qualifying newly established or small enterprises, subject to General Department of Taxation approval; (ii) formal application for investment-promotion CIT incentive certificates rather than an elective entity classification; (iii) in the cross-border context, foreign suppliers may elect to either register directly under the Foreign Supplier Portal or have a Vietnamese counterparty withhold foreign contractor tax; and (iv) loss carry-forward utilization through annual CIT finalization, subject to the statutory five-year limitation. None of these mechanisms changes the default entity-level taxation of the LLC.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No VAT registration exemption or economic turnover threshold applies to a resident LLC; compulsory VAT registration begins from the first taxable supply. A statutory monetary exemption threshold of VND 100,000,000 in annual revenue applies only to small household businesses and individual taxpayers. Foreign digital suppliers providing e-commerce or digital services to Vietnamese customers are required to register and pay VAT through the Foreign Supplier Portal from the first supply with no turnover threshold, under Decree 85/2021/ND-CP and implementing tax circulars. For a P2P lending platform, credit extension and interest income are VAT-exempt financial services, while separately invoiced platform, underwriting, and collection facilitation fees are standard-rated VAT supplies at 10%.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods exported from Vietnam qualify for the 0% VAT rate where the seller maintains an export contract, a customs-validated export declaration, transport documentation, and evidence that the goods left Vietnam, including sales to export processing enterprises. Services supplied to foreign organizations/individuals may qualify for 0% VAT if the services are performed for use outside Vietnam, the customer is a non-resident, and payment is received from abroad in foreign currency. For the fintech/P2P lending platform, algorithm-based underwriting or platform services supplied to a foreign customer may potentially be zero-rated if true offshore consumption is demonstrable; lending and credit-related activities generally remain VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated. Exporters with residual input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports of at least VND 300,000,000 may apply for a refund after the relevant month/quarter, provided all documentary substantiation is complete and no tax arrears exist.

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for all VAT taxpayers under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. Invoices must be issued through a GDT-approved electronic invoicing system. Where pre-coded invoices are used, the invoice must carry a tax authority authentication code generated by the GDT national e-invoice system; where non-precoded e-invoices are used, invoice data must be transmitted electronically to the GDT within the legally prescribed period. Retail and point-of-sale service businesses, including fintech platforms, must use cash-register e-invoices with real-time data transmission to the tax authority. The LLC must integrate its lending and platform systems with Vietnam's e-invoice APIs or use a GDT-certified service provider to ensure timely clearance and digital stamping/authentication of all invoices. P2P loan interest is excluded from invoicing as a taxable supply, but platform fees and service charges require compliant e-invoicing with 10% VAT.

      
Export zero-rating claims are subject to documentary audit by the General Department of Taxation for five years from the statutory filing date, extending to ten years where omission, evasion, or fraud is indicated. If substantiation fails, the 0% rate is denied, input VAT refunds are clawed back with retrospective effect, and the taxpayer must pay the deficient output VAT, a 20% underpayment penalty, and late-payment interest at 0.03% per day. Administrative penalties for irregular invoices and false export declarations may reach three times the evaded tax amount. Criminal liability for VAT export fraud and tax evasion under Article 200 of the Penal Code includes fines up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment up to seven years for individuals, with higher penalties for organized schemes. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the mandatory 180-day period also exposes the enterprise to foreign-exchange control penalties imposed by the State Bank of Vietnam.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Vietnamese Dong (VND) is conditionally convertible: current-account transactions are freely convertible at SBV-licensed credit institutions upon presentation of underlying transaction documents and declaration of the applicable payment/remittance purpose code prescribed by Circular No. 05/2017/TT-NHNN, whereas capital-account transactions require prior SBV registration or approval and are executed exclusively through the legally registered Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) of the foreign-invested LLC. Profit remittance is permissible but subject to four cumulative hurdles: (i) audited annual financial statements prepared under Vietnamese Accounting Standards showing net distributable after-tax profit; (ii) full settlement of all prior-year losses, corporate income tax, and other payable obligations; (iii) completion of annual enterprise information reporting and confirmed foreign-investor capital contribution records; and (iv) presentation of the bank certificate of capital contribution, DICA registration confirmation, and the board resolution approving dividend distribution. For a P2P micro-lending platform classified as High-Risk Special License, the bank will additionally scrutinize the credit-loss reserve, borrower receivables concentration, and the valid statutory license before executing any dividend or profit-stream remittance.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
The mandatory foreign-currency surrender ratio for export proceeds of goods and services is currently 0% as set by the State Bank of Vietnam's annual surrender decisions, meaning the foreign-invested LLC may retain all export service receipts in foreign currency or voluntarily convert them into VND. Nevertheless, the statutory repatriation mandate remains fully in force: all foreign-currency proceeds arising from exports of services, fintech fees, algorithmic underwriting service fees, or cross-border platform revenues must be brought back to Vietnam and credited to a licensed credit institution through the DICA within 180 days from the date of completion of the export service procedure as evidenced by the underlying transaction documentation. The treasury must map each inbound SWIFT credit to the corresponding invoice and service contract, record the balance-of-payment transaction code on the bank's FX application, and retain the full 180-day repatriation dossier at the relationship branch; failure to repatriate constitutes an exchange-control violation punishable under the Foreign Exchange Ordinance and Decree No. 88/2019/ND-CP (as amended by Decree No. 143/2021/ND-CP), including administrative monetary penalties and possible suspension of the enterprise's FX accounts.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam; SBV), acting as the central bank and the exclusive authority over the foreign exchange control regime of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam. Regulation and enforcement are exercised by the Foreign Exchange Control Department (Vụ Quản lý ngoại hối) and the Banking Supervision Agency, which jointly administer foreign currency licensing, registration of Direct Investment Capital Accounts, approval of foreign loans, determination of the mandatory surrender ratio, and sanctioning powers under the Foreign Exchange Ordinance No. 06/2005/PL-UBTVQH11 (as amended by Ordinance No. 10/2016/UBTVQH13) and its implementing instruments, notably Circular No. 15/2015/TT-NHNN (as amended by Circular No. 06/2019/TT-NHNN), Circular No. 05/2017/TT-NHNN, and Circular No. 08/2023/TT-NHNN.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
20% (standard corporate income tax for all resident enterprises, including foreign-invested limited liability companies)

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
Domestic corporate dividend withholding tax: 0% (Vietnam imposes no withholding tax on dividends distributed by an LLC to a foreign corporate shareholder; distributions are made from the after-tax profit of the Vietnamese entity and are not re-taxed at the corporate level). Individual foreign shareholders are subject to a final personal income tax (PIT) withholding of 5% on dividend income. Under the Vietnamese double-taxation treaties, the common treaty allocation rates are 5-10% for dividends, but because the domestic law rate on corporate dividends is already 0%, no treaty claim is needed by a corporate shareholder; the 0% domestic outcome is always the maximum benefit.

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
No later than the 90th day after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the deadline is 31 March of the following year. The annual finalization is also the compulsory deadline for the full settlement of prior quarterly provisional CIT payments.

      

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
No later than the 30th day after the end of the respective quarter: 30 April for Q1, 31 July for Q2, and 31 October for Q3. For a fiscal year that does not align with the calendar year, the same 30-day rule applies to each quarter of that fiscal year.

              Box E-01 (Chuyển lỗ / Loss carry-forward)
              Tax losses previously assessed and approved, carried forward and offset against current-year taxable income.

              The applicable CIT rate, normally 20%; incentive rates of 10%, 15%, or 17% are entered only when supported by an unexpired tax incentive code.

              Box I-01 (Thuế tạm nộp / Quarterly provisional payments)
              Sum of the quarterly CIT provisional payments made during the year using Form 01/TNDN (including the fourth-quarter settlement paid with the finalization).
              Provisional payments must reach at least 80% of the final annual CIT payable; an underpayment of the 80% threshold attracts a fine equal to 20% of the shortfall plus late-payment interest.
              Late filing attracts an administrative fine tiered at VND 1.25 million, VND 2.5 million, VND 5 million, VND 8 million, and up to VND 15-50 million for delays exceeding 60 days (Decree 125/2020/ND-CP); in all cases, late-payment interest accrues at 0.03% per day (approximately 10.95% per annum) from the statutory due date.               Box J-01 (Còn phải nộp / Additional tax or refund)
              The difference between the final CIT payable in Box H-01 and the provisional payments in Box I-01; a positive balance is payable by the statutory deadline, a negative balance becomes a refund request.
              A tax refund to the collapsing company is paid within 6 months of the closing of the tax file; the foreign shareholder cannot offset such refunds against unrelated Vietnamese tax obligations except under strict offset rules.
              Late filing attracts an administrative fine tiered at VND 1.25 million, VND 2.5 million, VND 5 million, VND 8 million, and up to VND 15-50 million for delays exceeding 60 days (Decree 125/2020/ND-CP); in all cases, late-payment interest accrues at 0.03% per day (approximately 10.95% per annum) from the statutory due date.               Line 1 – Tax identification number
              Vietnamese enterprise tax code.
              Must match the code printed on the investment registration certificate.
              Late filing of the quarterly provisional form is subject to fines from VND 5 million to VND 50 million depending on the number of days delayed; failure to remit the provisional tax due results in late-payment interest at 0.03% per day. The most serious consequence is the so-called '80% rule': if the sum of provisional payments is less than 80% of the final annual CIT liability, the taxpayer must pay a penalty equal to 20% of the shortfall plus the 0.03%-per-day interest from the applicable quarterly due date.

              Prudent estimate of taxable income determined on a quarter-to-date basis, reflecting known permanent differences.
              The estimate must not be artificially minimized; estimated income must be sufficient to trigger provisional payments meeting the 80% rule.
              Late filing of the quarterly provisional form is subject to fines from VND 5 million to VND 50 million depending on the number of days delayed; failure to remit the provisional tax due results in late-payment interest at 0.03% per day. The most serious consequence is the so-called '80% rule': if the sum of provisional payments is less than 80% of the final annual CIT liability, the taxpayer must pay a penalty equal to 20% of the shortfall plus the 0.03%-per-day interest from the applicable quarterly due date.
            

            
            

              Line 3 – Estimated CIT payable for the year-to-date
              Estimated taxable income multiplied by the statutory or incentive CIT rate.
              Incentive rates may be used only where the incentive code is already valid.
              Late filing of the quarterly provisional form is subject to fines from VND 5 million to VND 50 million depending on the number of days delayed; failure to remit the provisional tax due results in late-payment interest at 0.03% per day. The most serious consequence is the so-called '80% rule': if the sum of provisional payments is less than 80% of the final annual CIT liability, the taxpayer must pay a penalty equal to 20% of the shortfall plus the 0.03%-per-day interest from the applicable quarterly due date.
            

            
            

              Line 4 – Less CIT paid in prior quarters
              Sum of provisional amounts already remitted in earlier quarters.
              No foreign currency conversion issues arise because the Vietnamese entity always declares and pays in VND.
              Late filing of the quarterly provisional form is subject to fines from VND 5 million to VND 50 million depending on the number of days delayed; failure to remit the provisional tax due results in late-payment interest at 0.03% per day. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: VND-denominated thresholds under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP. Local File (and related-party transaction forms) must be prepared if the taxpayer has any related-party transaction and meets at least one of the following: (1) total revenue from selling goods or providing services to related parties in the tax year exceeds VND 50 billion; (2) total value of related-party transactions (including loans, borrowings, purchase of assets, services, and interest paid) exceeds VND 30 billion; or (3) total interest expense and related loan costs paid during the tax year (including to unrelated parties) exceeds VND 20 billion. The Master File is mandatory when the ultimate parent (whether foreign or Vietnamese) of the group achieves consolidated revenue of VND 18,000 billion (approximately USD 750 million) or more. Country-by-Country Report is required when the Vietnamese ultimate parent entity has consolidated revenue of VND 18,000 billion or more and also for certain foreign-controlled groups meeting the same consolidated-revenue test. The related-party loan-interest deduction is further capped at 30% of the taxpayer's EBITDA (interest ratio cap); the disallowed excess interest may be carried forward to later years within the 5-year window.

      
Economic substance scope: Vietnam does not operate a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the transfer-pricing law (Decree 132/2020/ND-CP) incorporates rigorous substance-over-form and anti-abuse doctrines: (1) the tax authority may disregard, recharacterize, or allocate income from a related-party transaction if the arrangement lacks commercial rationality or economic substance, or if one party the enterprise is related to is neither functionally nor risk-bearing capable of performing the purported functions; (2) BEPS Action 6 minimum-standard is applied to deny treaty benefits where a foreign investor is a mere conduit interposed mainly to obtain a Vietnamese DTA advantage; (3) the Vietnamese entity is expected to perform the core value-creating functions in-country – for a P2P/lending platform this means actual underwriting staff, credit-risk managers, data scientists, decision-makers, board meetings, servers/IT systems, and audited financials in Vietnam. A 'shell' LLC holding only legal title, with no employees or decision-making, will have its related-party transactions recharacterized and its attributable income increased; the tax authority may further assert a permanent establishment of the foreign parent in Vietnam.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Criminal False Declaration
        
Statutory sanction: Administratively, the under-declared amount is subjected to a fine equal to 20% of the underpaid tax plus late-payment interest at 0.03% per day (approximately 10.95% per annum). Criminally, the Penal Code (No. 100/2015/QH13, as amended by Law 12/2017/QH14) Article 200 applies: tax evasion from VND 100 million to under VND 300 million carries a fine of VND 100-500 million or non-custodial reform up to 3 years; evasion of VND 300 million or more, or with aggravating factors such as abuse of position, carries imprisonment of up to 5 years. A legal entity convicted of tax evasion is subject to a fine of up to VND 5 billion, suspension of activity for up to 36 months, and prohibition from doing certain lines of business.

        
Director personal liability: The legal representative (chairman, general director, CEO) and the chief accountant who sign or directly order the false return are individually criminally liable and may be imprisoned for 1-5 years. The tax authority can additionally impose a temporary exit ban (suspension of exit from Vietnam) on the responsible individual under Article 217 of the Law on Tax Administration, freeze personal bank accounts, seize and auction personal assets, and, in cross-border cases, transmit the dossier through mutual legal assistance and INTERPOL.

      

      
      

        Unlicensed Consumer-Lending / AML Breach (P2P Platform)
        
Statutory sanction: Administratively, SBV may impose fines up to VND 300-500 million and order the immediate cessation of the unlicensed lending operation, revocation of any incidental payment-intermediary license, and confiscation of gains. Criminally, Penal Code Article 180 (illegal operation of banking or other financial activities) applies: a natural person faces a fine of VND 100 million to VND 1 billion, imprisonment for up to 5 years, and a prohibition from business activity for up to 5 years; a legal entity faces fines up to VND 2 billion, suspension of operations, and compulsory deregistration. A fake or undercapitalized lending scheme directed at retail borrowers may also be prosecuted as fraud under Penal Code Article 174, carrying up to 12 years' imprisonment for an individual and corresponding corporate fines.

        
Director personal liability: The directors and officers who designed or managed the illegal credit or lending scheme are criminally prosecuted personally; they face imprisonment, personal fines, asset seizures, and temporary exit bans. Under the Law on Prevention and Combating of Money Laundering (Law 14/2022/QH15), directors are also personally accountable for the implementation of AML policies; failure to report suspicious P2P transactions can lead to independent AML fines and criminal money-laundering charges under Penal Code Article 324, which carry imprisonment of up to 10 years for the natural-person manager.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Treaty-Abuse Substance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: The transfer-pricing authority will disregard the shell entity and will re-attribute income to the offshore principal; the Vietnamese LLC is then treated as a permanent establishment of the foreign company, subjecting the foreign principal to a 20% CIT on the Vietnam-attributable income plus a 20% understatement penalty and 0.03%-per-day interest. Payments labeled as interest or service fees from the shell to the offshore parent may be recharacterized as disguised dividends, losing any DTA benefit and incurring the same deficiencies. If the shell has obtained unjustified tax refunds, the refund can be clawed back with penalties, and the company risks being dissolved for operating outside the registered business purpose.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly channel profits through an economically hollow Vietnamese entity are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties if the company becomes insolvent; they may be personally barred from the territory of Vietnam (exit ban) and from acting as managers of any Vietnamese enterprise for up to 3 years. In aggravated cases involving fictitious contracts, the responsible director incurs criminal liability for tax evasion; involvement in laundering the diverted funds triggers money-laundering prosecution with imprisonment of up to 10 years.

      

      
      

        Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) Non-Withholding or Disguised Remittance
        
Statutory sanction: The Vietnamese LLC, as the withholding agent, is liable for the un-withheld FCT on the full gross payment, plus late-payment interest at 0.03% per day, and an administrative fine equal to 20% of the under-withheld amount (or up to 1-3 times the omitted amount in cases of intentional failure). The offshore vendor is considered to have a permanent establishment in Vietnam if the contractual activity is performed in Vietnam, in which case the vendor is assessed on net profit rather than gross, with the increased tax being assessed to the Vietnamese payor on a joint-and-several basis.

        
Director personal liability: The CFO, chief accountant, and legal representative who authorized the foreign payments are individually obliged to ensure the FCT was withheld; if the company cannot satisfy the liability, the responsible directors' personal assets may be pursued, and the authority will issue a temporary exit suspension against them. A repeated pattern of non-withholding with a cumulative unremitted FCT exceeding VND 100 million is treated as criminal tax evasion, and the directors face imprisonment as well as civil restitution.

      

      
      

        Transfer-Pricing Documentation Failure and Related-Party Interest Cap
        
Statutory sanction: Upon non-submission of TP documentation, the tax authority is entitled to determine the Vietnamese entity's income by reference to an adverse benchmark of comparable transactions and may apply a penalty equal to 20% of the additionally assessed CIT, plus 0.03%-per-day late interest. Administrative fines apply for failure to submit documents (categorized under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP at VND 40-70 million per occurrence). Interest expenses exceeding the 30% EBITDA cap are disallowed from deduction; the disallowed portion is not permanently lost but may be carried forward to the next tax year and across a maximum of the next 5 years, with a correspondingly heavier administrative burden to prove the carry-forward.

        
Director personal liability: The director and CFO are personally responsible for the completeness and truthfulness of the TP documentation attached to the annual CIT return. If the absence of documentation is coupled with an underpaid CIT in excess of VND 100 million, they are exposed to criminal tax evasion charges and imprisonment up to 5 years. Even without criminal referral, the tax authority will issue an exit ban and asset-freeze order against the responsible individual to secure the recovery of the assessed tax, penalties, and interest.
