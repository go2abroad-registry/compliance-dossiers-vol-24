Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Business Registration Office (Phòng Đăng ký kinh doanh), Department of Planning and Investment (Sở Kế hoạch và Đầu tư) of the province/city in Viet Nam where the LLC establishes its head office; national supervision by Agency for Business Registration, Ministry of Planning and Investment.

Governing statute

Law on Enterprise No. 59/2020/QH14 (the "Enterprise Law 2020"), particularly Articles 29, 30, 31, 33 and 64 through 75; Law on Investment No. 61/2020/QH14, articles on market access and investment registration; Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP on business registration; and, for the specific legal-services scope, Law on Lawyers No. 20/2012/QH13 and Decree No. 123/2013/ND-CP.

Formation / constitutional filings

Enterprise Registration Application (Giấy đề nghị đăng ký doanh nghiệp) in the form annexed to Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP, together with the Company Charter, the List of Charter Members, and notarized legal-status copies of each member/investor.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum registered capital is prescribed for legal-services LLCs under the Enterprise Law 2020. Charter capital is fixed in the Charter and must be fully contributed within 90 days after the Enterprise Registration Certificate is issued; no escrow bank account is required as a matter of enterprise law, but the LLC must open a capital-contribution receipt and report contributions, and the foreign equity contribution must comply with the law on foreign exchange control. For legal-services activities, the Ministry of Justice may impose additional conditions on law-firm establishment and practising lawyers, including nationality, residency and academic qualification conditions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation of Vietnam (Tong Cuc Thue, Ministry of Finance). Primary portals: National Business Registration Portal (Dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn), National e-Tax Portal (thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn), and National e-Invoice Portal (hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn). Provincial-level administration is carried out by the municipal/provincial Tax Department (Cuc Thue) where the LLC's head office is registered.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) or the relevant Export Processing/Industrial Zone Authority, supported by the foreign investor's incorporation documents and legalization of signature. Step 2: File the Enterprise Registration Dossier through the National Business Registration Portal to obtain the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); the business tax identification number (Ma so thue, MST) is automatically and simultaneously issued by the tax authority upon ERC approval, integrated with the national tax infrastructure. Step 3: Complete post-licensing tax identity setup at the provincial Tax Department, including electronic tax registration (Form 08-DK-TCT prefill), bank account disclosure, e-Tax account activation, and digital certificate (token) registration for e-filing. Step 4: Register the e-invoice account with the General Department of Taxation (hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn) and apply for the use of e-invoices with tax authority codes; this registration is mandatory before issuing the first 0% VAT export invoice. Step 5: For a foreign-invested LLC, no separate VAT ID is needed; the MST serves as the unified TIN for CIT, VAT, customs (in certain schemes), social insurance, and e-invoice purposes. Step 6: If the LLC simultaneously operates as a customs declarant, obtain a customs code and register bank guarantee accounts with the General Department of Customs; this is auxiliary to the tax identity. No passport of TIN is required; the procedure is fully digital and typically completed within 15 working days of IRC issuance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A foreign-invested LLC incorporated under the Law on Enterprises is per se a Vietnamese-resident enterprise under the Law on Enterprise Income Tax (CIT Law No. 14/2008/QH12, as amended by Law No. 32/2013/QH13). It is taxed on worldwide income and has no pass-through eligibility; profits are subject to CIT at the 20% standard rate plus a 0% dividend withholding tax for non-resident shareholders remitting dividends out of Vietnam. The LLC is not a transparent entity, and no partnership-style pass-through is recognized for tax purposes. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A foreign-invested LLC incorporated under the Law on Enterprises is per se a Vietnamese-resident enterprise under the Law on Enterprise Income Tax (CIT Law No. 14/2008/QH12, as amended by Law No. 32/2013/QH13). It is taxed on worldwide income and has no pass-through eligibility; profits are subject to CIT at the 20% standard rate plus a 0% dividend withholding tax for non-resident shareholders remitting dividends out of Vietnam. The LLC is not a transparent entity, and no partnership-style pass-through is recognized for tax purposes. Territorial scope: for a resident enterprise, export services rendered to foreign clients are taxable at 0% CIT? No, they are taxable to CIT as ordinary income; the territorial classification is irrelevant inside the CIT net. However, the 0% VAT export regime (detailed in indirect taxation) applies. For inbound non-resident service providers, a separate Foreign Contractor Tax regime applies, which is not applicable to this locally incorporated LLC.

Available election forms

Vietnam does not administer a US-style 'check-the-box' classification election; all LLCs are per se CIT-resident entities. Statutory elections and formal mechanisms that are available and relevant: (1) Fiscal year election: the company may elect a calendar-year accounting period (default) or an approved fiscal year ending 31 March, 30 June, or 30 September (must file notice with tax authority). (2) VAT credit method election: where annual revenue from sale of goods/services is below 1 billion VND (approximately USD 40,000), the taxpayer may elect either the credit method or the direct method; once the credit method is elected or revenue exceeds the threshold, the credit method is mandatory. (3) Loss carry-forward election: the taxpayer may elect to carry forward CIT losses (no carryback) for an uninterrupted period of up to 5 years. (4) Deduction of actual R&D expenditure: an election exists to apply a special cap (10% of annual taxable income) for certain R&D costs (Law on Technology Transfer), although not relevant to a litigation practice. (5) Fixed-asset depreciation method election: taxpayers may elect straight-line, declining-balance, or unit-of-production depreciation per Circular 45/2013/TT-BTC, subject to consistency requirements. (6) Foreign exchange difference election: eligible dates for FX revaluation may be selected within the CIT declaration cycle. (7) Hybrid mismatch arrangements: no elective opt-out exists; Decree 132/2020/ND-CP (transfer pricing and BEPS Action 2) mandatorily disallows deductions for hybrid mismatch transactions that result in double non-taxation or double deduction. There is no separate 'hybrid mismatch election' prescribed in Vietnamese law; the rule is mandatory and enforced through transfer-pricing documentation. Group consolidation of tax losses is not permitted; each LLC files standalone CIT returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For an LLC: no registration threshold exists — VAT registration, invoicing, and periodic filing obligations commence on the date of Enterprise Registration Certificate issuance irrespective of turnover. For sole proprietorships/household businesses (not applicable to LLC), a 100,000,000 VND per annum threshold applies. Because this entity is an LLC performing export-exempt? No, the entity performs legal services at 10% standard rate domestically and 0% for exported services, therefore it falls under the credit method and cannot elect the direct method if annual turnover is or is expected to be above 1 billion VND (the statutory credit-method threshold per Article 11, Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC). The company must also obtain a tax-authority-code e-invoice sequence even when producing 0% export invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Legal services provided by a Vietnamese LLC to a foreign client qualify for the 0% VAT rate if, and only if, the following cumulative statutory conditions under Article 9, Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC are satisfied: (1) the service recipient is a foreign organization registered in a foreign country or a foreign individual residing overseas; (2) the service is consumed entirely outside Vietnam — for cross-border commercial litigation and multi-jurisdictional arbitration, this is evidenced by contractual governing law (non-Vietnamese), the venue/seat of arbitration or litigation (outside Vietnam), and delivery of legal advice/representation outputs to a foreign addressee; (3) the service is not in the exclusion list (legal services are not excluded; exclusions concern natural resources, telecommunications, transport, insurance, banking, and services in taxable zones); (4) the foreign client pays in a convertible foreign currency via a Vietnamese licensed bank; (5) the Vietnam LLC holds a full export service contract and a payment receipt/remittance record; (6) the e-invoice shows 0% VAT and contains the contract number and foreign-currency amount; and (7) the LLC has submitted an export declared/using list (Bang ke hoa don dich vu xuat khau) along with the supporting contract and the foreign client's registration/license certificate to the tax authority. Export zero-rating is not optional; if the economic substance test fails, the service is subject to 10% VAT on the invoiced amount plus penalties.

  • Customs Export Declaration (only for physical goods; for pure cross-border litigation/arbitration services, no customs declaration is filed — the controlling substitute is the export service contract and the List of Exported Service Invoices (Phu luc Bang ke 01/GTGT)).
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (applicable only to incidental physical exhibits, evidence files, or hearing documents; for services, the equivalent evidence is the documented date-stamped delivery of legal memoranda, court/arbitral filings, and hearing attendance records performed outside Vietnam).
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC): official certificate issued by an authorized Vietnamese commercial bank evidencing receipt and conversion? or receipt of foreign currency from the foreign client, showing the matching invoice number and contract reference; at minimum, the bank statement showing the cross-border receipt without domestic intermediation.
  • Export service contract in a legally valid form, containing: place of performance, governing law clause (foreign law), fee schedule in a freely convertible foreign currency, and dispute resolution venue outside Vietnam.
  • Zero-rated e-invoice with tax-authority code transmitted to the GDT portal, stating 0% VAT and referencing the export contract number.
  • Foreign client's certificate of registration/license issued by its home jurisdiction (translated and legalized if requested), used to verify the non-resident status of the recipient.
  • Detailed work logs / deliverables register (advices, court filings, briefs, memoranda, invoices) demonstrating that the service was actually rendered and completed outside Vietnam and not used within Vietnam.

All VAT invoices (including 0% export invoices) must be electronic invoices per Decree 123/2020/ND-CP, generated by a certified software application and transmitted to the General Department of Taxation's e-invoice portal (hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn). Two regimes exist: (A) E-invoices with tax-authority codes (most common for new enterprises): each invoice is transmitted in real-time to the GDT portal, which affixes a digital authentication code (ma cua co quan thue) and returns the encrypted code instantly; the invoice is deemed cleared for fiscal purposes only upon receipt of this code. This is, in effect, an electronic clearance/stamping mandate. The API integration is supported through licensed e-invoice service providers (CENs) that interface with the GDT's centralized system. (B) E-invoices without tax-authority codes: permitted for certain internal-use cases or for enterprises meeting technical conditions (digital signature, periodic transmission, and stricter pre-issuance validation), but not recommended for an active exporter due to higher audit risk. Mandatory fields include the 0% VAT rate indicator and the reason code for VAT exemption, if applicable (Article 91). Invoices must be transmitted to the tax authority's archive within 1 working day of issuance? For coded e-invoices the data is transmitted in real-time; other invoice data must be transferred through the vendor portal not later than the 15th of the month following the invoicing period. XML/JSON schema, digital signature of seller (using registered token), and the GDT's assigned invoice series are mandatory. There is no separate physical stamp; the tax authority code operates as the digital stamp. Since July 2022, all transactions (including services of a litigation chamber) must be e-invoiced; paper invoices are rejected for input VAT credit and are treated as unissued from a fiscal standpoint.

The General Department of Taxation and Customs combined enforcement framework authorizes retrospective VAT clawback for export zero-rating fraud or misdeclaration. If the 0% rate is denied, the tax authority will reassess 10% VAT on the full invoice amount, impose a late-payment interest charge at 0.05% per day (approximately 18.25% per annum) computed from the original due date to the payment date, and apply administrative penalties per Decree 125/2020/ND-CP: (a) underdeclaration: penalty equal to 20% of the additional tax assessed; (b) taxable-amount understatement with no fraud: 20% plus full interest; (c) fraudulent evasion: penalty of 1.0 to 3.0 times the evaded VAT amount, with possible criminal referral under Article 200 of the Penal Code 2015 (amended 2017) if evaded tax exceeds 100,000,000 VND (approximately USD 4,000) and other statutory elements are met, carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment, a fine up to 4,500,000,000 VND, and prohibition from holding office/business activity for up to 5 years; (d) issuance of false invoices: Article 203 penalties up to 20 years' imprisonment for invoice crimes; (e) corporate offenders may face suspension of e-invoice issuance authority, temporary closure of the head-office address, revocation of the tax code in extreme cases, and removal from the 'good taxpayer' green channel and customs priority regime. Retrospective clawback periods: 5 years for unintentional underdeclaration (General Tax Administration Law 38/2019/QH14, Article 8?) and 10 years for fraud/evasion, extending to a full supply-chain audit of supporting documentation, foreign remittance records, and correspondence proving the offshore consumption of legal services. Legal-entity representatives (General Director/CEO) are jointly and severally liable, and will be named in criminal proceedings for fraudulent zero-rating schemes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vietnamese đồng (VND) is not fully convertible. Current-account transactions are de facto convertible upon documentary verification by an authorized bank: a foreign-invested law firm may freely receive and pay overseas legal fees, arbitrator invoices, expert-witness costs, court charges, and arbitration-institution fees, provided the underlying engagement contract, invoice, and tax compliance documents are filed with the servicing bank. Capital-account transactions, by contrast, are restricted: equity injection must pass through a registered Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA); shareholder loans with a term above one year must be registered with the SBV; and profit remittance is permitted only after the annual audited financial statements are completed, the corporate income tax finalization obligation is settled, and a board resolution on dividend distribution is executed. For a litigation-services LLC, remittance of fees received from foreign clients is classified as an export of services (current account) and enjoys full convertibility, but a bank will require an export-services statement, the signed retainer, and an invoice to evidence the economic substance of the cross-border service provision. Profit distribution to a foreign parent is subject to a further condition that the LLC has no accumulated losses and has set off all prior-year losses against taxable income, as certified by the tax authority; the remittance itself attracts no Vietnamese withholding tax when the CIT finalization obligation is satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory percentage surrender of export-proceeds foreign currency to VND; the statutory surrender regime was abolished in 2012. However, all export proceeds — including cross-border legal, arbitration, asset-tracing, and expert advisory services — must be repatriated to Vietnam within 90 days from the date of service completion (for goods, 180 days, extendable to 360 days with prior bank approval). The proceeds must be credited to a foreign-currency demand account or sold immediately to an authorized bank; retention of unrepatriated export proceeds abroad is an administrative violation. For a litigation chamber, proceeds earned by the foreign-litigation team (e.g., hourly fees billed from a foreign database or paid into an offshore collections account) must nevertheless flow back to the VN_LLC operating account within the 90-day window. Non-compliance triggers penalties under Decree 88/2019/ND-CP (as amended by Decree 143/2021/ND-CP), including fines up to VND 200 million, suspension of foreign-exchange activities, and an SBV order compelling the sale of foreign currency; criminal prosecution under the Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 14/2022/QH15 may also apply where the non-repatriation is used to conceal ownership of recovered arbitration awards.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV; Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam), the sole monetary authority and foreign-exchange regulator, acting under the Law on the State Bank of Vietnam No. 46/2010/QH12 (as amended in 2017) and the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange No. 28/2005/PL-UBTVQH11 (as amended by Ordinance No. 04/2013/UBTVQH13). The binding implementing instruments for treasury operations are: (i) Circular No. 06/2019/TT-NHNN governing foreign-exchange management for foreign direct investment (FDI), current transactions, Direct Investment Capital Accounts, and profit remittance; (ii) Circular No. 02/2021/TT-NHNN replacing Circular No. 15/2015/TT-NHNN, governing spot, forward, swap, and option foreign-exchange transactions between authorized banks and corporate customers; and (iii) Circular No. 23/2014/TT-NHNN, as amended by Circular No. 02/2019/TT-NHNN, governing payment-account opening and customer identification (KYC/eKYC). Administrative FX sanctions are imposed under Decree No. 88/2019/ND-CP, as amended by Decree No. 143/2021/ND-CP, with fines up to VND 200 million and forced-conversion orders for repatriation breaches.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for the primary trust/IOLTA clearing rail; Medium for ancillary collection sub-ledgers. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud, MultiPass) are not licensed as banks by the SBV and may not lawfully hold VND, open client-funds escrow accounts, or settle arbitration awards on behalf of a Vietnamese-licensed law firm. Under Decree No. 101/2012/ND-CP (as amended by Decree No. 80/2016/ND-CP) and Circular No. 39/2014/TT-NHNN, only licensed payment intermediaries may process non-cash payments in Vietnam; EMIs must contractually route through a licensed Vietnamese commercial bank, and the bank remains the account-holding institution of record. Consequently, an EMI may be used as an overseas receivables sub-ledger for invoice-to-invoice matching of foreign client fees, but the operative clearing rail and all client-funds custody must be held at an authorized bank within Vietnam. Legal consequence of using an unlicensed EMI as principal rail: the account relationship is voidable, export-proceeds repatriation obligations are breached, and the firm risks tax authority challenge on invoicing substance.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

IOLTA/client-funds account classification gap: Vietnamese payment-account regulations do not recognize an 'IOLTA' or 'lawyers trust account' as a distinct statutory account species; banks applying strict SBV rules may classify an escrow fund as a hybrid 'custody deposit' or 'deposit for expenses' account, raising refusal or recharacterization risk.

Root cause: IOLTA/client-funds account classification gap: Vietnamese payment-account regulations do not recognize an 'IOLTA' or 'lawyers trust account' as a distinct statutory account species; banks applying strict SBV rules may classify an escrow fund as a hybrid 'custody deposit' or 'deposit for expenses' account, raising refusal or recharacterization risk.

Defense evidence: (1) A notarized copy of the Law Practice License issued by the Ministry of Justice under the Law on Lawyers No. 65/2006/QH11 (amended 2012); (2) a Board resolution defining the client-funds account as a segregated escrow capable of holding litigation proceeds; (3) a client-funds holding policy compliant with Article 25 of the Law on Lawyers and the Vietnam Bar Federation's Code of Professional Conduct and Ethics (2019), demonstrating no commingling with operating funds; (4) a sample client engagement letter with an express waiver of interest and an instruction that awarded sums pass through the segregated sub-account; (5) a weekly reconciliation template, with an undertaking to produce the register on SBV demand; and (6) confirmation that the bank's own 'custody deposit' internal product code (e.g., product code 08 or equivalent) is accepted as the booking vehicle for the underlying arbitration award.
Beneficial-ownership opacity: the 25% look-through requirement under the Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 14/2022/QH15 and Decree No. 48/2024/ND-CP obliges the bank to identify every natural person UBO; foreign silent partners, discretionary family trusts, and litigation-funding backers behind the LLC are classic red flags.

Root cause: Beneficial-ownership opacity: the 25% look-through requirement under the Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 14/2022/QH15 and Decree No. 48/2024/ND-CP obliges the bank to identify every natural person UBO; foreign silent partners, discretionary family trusts, and litigation-funding backers behind the LLC are classic red flags.

Defense evidence: (1) Full corporate registry extract of the LLC with the shareholder register; (2) a manually executed UBO declaration table showing each natural person holding above 25%, with passport copies and residential-address proof; (3) source-of-wealth declaration for each UBO covering the initial capital contribution, corroborated by bank statements or audited financial statements of the source entity; (4) where litigation-funding or third-party funding exists, the funding agreement, the funder's KYC pack, and a legal-memorandum certifying that the funding is legitimate and does not constitute an unlicensed lending activity under Vietnam's civil code; and (5) authorization for the bank to inspect the national enterprise database (Đăng ký quốc gia) to confirm no mismatch with the submitted UBO list.
Sanctions and politically-exposed-persons (PEP) exposure: cross-border commercial litigation frequently involves parties residing in or linked to sanctioned jurisdictions, or state-owned enterprises from high-risk jurisdictions, triggering UN Security Council, OFAC, EU, and SBV-issued watch-list screening; an automatic hit will freeze the account and generate a report to the SBV AML Division.

Root cause: Sanctions and politically-exposed-persons (PEP) exposure: cross-border commercial litigation frequently involves parties residing in or linked to sanctioned jurisdictions, or state-owned enterprises from high-risk jurisdictions, triggering UN Security Council, OFAC, EU, and SBV-issued watch-list screening; an automatic hit will freeze the account and generate a report to the SBV AML Division.

Defense evidence: (1) A documented sanctions/PEP screening policy with a clear client-onboarding matrix; (2) negative sanctions clear-sheet certificates produced by an independent screening vendor for each matter, including the adverse-party and the counterparty's counsel; (3) the managing partner's 'lawyer's non-engagement certificate' confirming that no client receives settlement proceeds for the benefit of a designated person; (4) a transaction-monitoring protocol committing the firm to a 24-hour SBV notification under Article 42 of the AML Law should a client become sanctioned mid-matter; and (5) the escrow agreement clause prohibiting settlement disbursements to any entity on the EU/OFC/UN consolidated lists.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Viet Nam does not impose a separate dividend withholding tax on after-tax profit distributions from an LLC to its non-resident shareholder. The distributed profit is subject only to the 20% CIT already paid at the entity level. Remittance of profits abroad is otherwise unrestricted provided tax obligations are fully settled.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VND 30,000,000,000 (VND 30 billion) aggregate annual related-party transaction value; taxpayers with total annual revenue below VND 50,000,000,000 and related-party transactions below VND 30,000,000,000 may qualify for exemption from preparing the Local File and Master File. Related-party transaction declarations (Forms 01, 02 and 03) are made on the annual CIT finalization Form 03/TNDN package under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP.

Economic substance scope: Viet Nam has not enacted a standalone Economic Substance Regulation statute of the style used in Mauritius or the BVI, but the Law on Tax Administration (No. 38/2019/QH14) and Decree 132/2020/ND-CP introduce a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and substance over form. Arrangements lacking commercial rationale, personnel, premises, and risk-bearing functions will be recharacterized and disregarded. Thin capitalization rules apply: net interest expenses, including related and third-party debt, are deductible only up to 30% of EBITDA for CIT purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax amount under the Law on Tax Administration; late payment interest 0.03% per day; criminal liability under Article 200 of the Penal Code with fines up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment of up to 7 years when the evaded amount reaches VND 100,000,000 or more.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and managing directors who ordered or knowingly facilitated the evasion are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax; criminal penalties apply personally. The tax authority may impose an exit ban (travel hold) preventing directors from leaving Viet Nam during investigation and collection.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the structure, disallowance of the entity's invoices and deductions, additional CIT at 20% plus late interest, fines for false registration up to VND 1,000,000,000, and potential revocation of the enterprise registration certificate under Article 216 of the Law on Enterprise if the entity is found to be a facade.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the unpaid tax following piercing of the corporate veil; courts can order restitution of assets transferred out; the State Bank and tax authorities coordinate to impose travel bans; imprisonment is possible under fraud and tax evasion provisions when criminal intent is established.
Anti-Money Laundering and IOLTA Trust Account Abuse

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to VND 200,000,000 per AML violation, mandatory reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit (State Bank), temporary suspension of the legal-services license, seizure of suspect account balances, and criminal money-laundering penalties under Article 324 of the Penal Code including fines of up to VND 3,000,000,000 and imprisonment up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and compliance officers are subject to personal criminal accountability for structured transactions; travel bans are commonly used; directors can be held civilly liable to harmed trust-account beneficiaries; professional liability insurance is void; directors may be disqualified from serving as bank signatories or anti-money-laundering compliance officers.
Transfer Pricing Violations and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax authority restating taxable income by applying an arm's length method, additional CIT at 20%, late payment interest, administrative penalties of 1 to 3 times the evaded amount for material misstatement, and fines of up to VND 100,000,000 for failure to submit required transfer pricing forms and documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing declarations are personally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties; criminal tax evasion liability under Article 200 of the Penal Code may follow if the corrected tax exceeds statutory thresholds; an exit ban may remain in force until the assessment is paid.
