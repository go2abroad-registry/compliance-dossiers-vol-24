Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A physical registered office in Vanuatu is mandatory, but the office of a licensed registered agent may serve as the statutory address. For the aircraft-engine dry-leasing International Company, a pure virtual or mail-forwarding address is commercially permissible for incorporation and low-risk compliance, but it is not advisable: the company must demonstrate real commercial substance by maintaining board meeting minutes, lease agreements, insurance certificates, maintenance and engine records, escrow and bank accounts, and economic substance evidence. The VFSC and Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit generally treat a bare mail-forwarding office as a red flag for a medium-risk enhanced aviation leasing entity, especially where cross-border wires, escrow arrangements, and foreign lessees are present.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital. The Company may register with a nominal capital as low as USD 1 in shares of no par value; no escrow deposit, capital verification, or payment-up requirement applies under the International Companies Act.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable - the International Company maintains no physical premises, employees, or workplace in Vanuatu beyond the registered agent's address; no fire safety certificate, structural load inspection, or occupation health and safety clearance is required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (CIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Republic of Vanuatu. Operational portal: https://customs.gov.vu/; ASYCUDA World single-window for customs declarations; VFSC (Vanuatu Financial Services Commission) for International Company incorporation and corporate registry privileges.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] engaging in dry aircraft engine leasing: (1) Incorporate the VU_IC with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) via a licensed Registered Agent, submitting Form 01 and obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation and constitution; (2) Appoint and maintain a Vanuatu-registered office (standard fiduciary agent) to receive service of process; (3) File the mandatory Annual Economic Statement and tax status declaration with VFSC, confirming the effective place of management is outside Vanuatu and no local-sourced income is derived; (4) Apply for a TIN (Common Identification Number) with the CIR/VFSC joint domain, only where a limited presence is established, or elect VAT Group registration for local purchases requiring a VAT ID (Form-01/TIN-101); (5) For pure offshore aircraft-engine dry leasing transactions, obtain a Certificate of Non-Origin (Letter of Exempt Status) through the registered agent, confirming the lease receivables are strictly foreign-sourced and the IC is not subject to passport taxation in the Republic of Vanuatu.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident International Company; territorial exemption of all foreign-sourced aircraft lease income; no capital gains, income, or withholding tax on non-residents; exempt from exchange control; the offshore lease contracts and associated turbofan engine assets are statutorily immune from national taxation).

Available election forms

Vanuatu does not administer a US-style 'check-the-box' election. A VU_IC may, through its Registered Agent, file a voluntary election to become subject to the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 75] for intra-Vanuatu supplies if it establishes physical presence/domestic turnover, or may irrevocably elect to remain an exempt crude fiscal resident; election Form A-02 to the CIR is available to treat mixed local/offshore operations; hybrid mismatch adjustments under the Arbitrage Act do not apply to international companies as they are outside the Vanuatu tax base, but an anti-avoidance declaration may be lodged to suppress treaty-shopping or double-dip deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 (ten million Vatu) annual taxable turnover for a resident business, or VT 2,500,000 per quarter, whichever is reached first; a pure dry aircraft-engine lessor with offshore operations falls below the nexus threshold and is not compulsorily registrable in Vanuatu for VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) the supply of goods physically exported from the Republic of Vanuatu to a non-resident; (ii) services supplied to a non-resident, in respect of which the benefit is wholly enjoyed outside Vanuatu; and (iii) international transport-related services. For the turbofan aircraft-engine dry leasing scope, the lease supply is treated as an export of services if the engines are stationed outside Vanuatu, the lessee is non-resident, the law-of-the-lease contracts designate a foreign situs, and all consideration settles via SWIFT wire/escrow into a non-resident account; the VU_IC must document the offshore deployment certificate to substantiate the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (standard Single Administrative Declaration — SAD) transmitted electronically via ASYCUDA World, stamped and cleared by the Port of Vanuatu customs officer (or approved external-franchise clearance)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Turbofan Engine Movement Log demonstrating the engine's delivery and situs outside Vanuatu (e.g., foreign MRO base, FBO, or intermediate OECD jurisdiction)
  • International Lease Agreement and Full Legal Instrument evidencing title, dry lease terms, and non-resident lessee coverage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or authentic SWIFT MT103 wire transfer receipt proving receipt of lease rental from the non-resident operator into a VU IC corporate bank or escrow account
  • VU zero-rating certificate (Tax Office Export Support Form VU-EXP/01) endorsed by the Registered Agent and a licensed Vanuatu accountant

No real-time national e-invoicing clearance or XML API digital stamping mandate is applicable for pure cross-border transnational billing; however, mandatory digital cargo screening and customs clearance are processed via the ASYCUDA World single-window integrated system. Exporting entities must file the electronic Single Administrative Declaration (SAD), SASP report, and associated engine movement records; the CIR will verify the destination of goods or services via the Customs Electronic Registry. Tax invoices issued by an exempt IC offshore are not required to bear fiscal imprints, but billers must retain all title documentation in the VU registered office for audit verification.

Failure to substantiate zero-rating on or before the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline triggers a retrospective VAT clawback equal to the full 15% headline rate on gross lease rentals, plus a 200% penalty of the deficient tax, a 20% per annum late payment interest charge, and judicial fines of up to VT 5,000,000 imposed under the Customs and Inland Revenue (Penalties) Act 2014. Where an offshore IC is found to have manufactured false affidavits of export, the VFSC has the authority to wind up the entity, revoke the International Company license, and the CIR attorneys-general may apply criminal sanctions for tax evasion and false statement of residence, including mandatory personal liability of directors and removal of the tax exemption identity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is accepted as fully convertible for current-account transactions and for most permitted capital-account flows. Vanuatu has no formal exchange-control statute; the RBV imposes prudential reporting requirements on licensed banks rather than prior transactional approvals. An International Company may maintain foreign-currency and domestic-currency accounts, pay aircraft engine lease rentals offshore, and remit dividends, loan principal, interest, and capital repatriation proceeds without exchange-control authorization. In practice, banks will require lease agreements, invoices, board/director resolutions, UBEs, and source-of-funds evidence to process profit remittance and capital transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement in Vanuatu. Export receipts derived from aircraft engine dry leasing may be retained in any eligible foreign currency, held in a VUV or USD account, or converted as needed at prevailing commercial bank rates. No mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation time limit applies. Lease rental receivables should be supported by signed lease agreements, engine delivery/acceptance certificates, rental invoices, and maintenance reserve schedules to satisfy bank due diligence and RBV statistical reporting expectations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail. Vanuatu has no dedicated e-money institution or EMI licensing regime, and the RBV supervises only licensed commercial banks and licensed financial dealers. A non-resident offshore digital EMI cannot obtain direct clearing access to Vanuatu payment systems and would normally be treated as a third-party payment intermediary rather than a bank counterparty. Cross-border EMIs may be used on a limited ancillary basis for collection of lease rentals only if the IC maintains a licensed local bank account as the principal operational account and the EMI clears through a RBV-supervised correspondent bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital injections should be routed as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the ultimate parent or investor bank to the Vanuatu IC's VUV or USD settlement account at a licensed commercial bank. The payment reference should include the IC legal name, company number, and a clear callback in the free-text field indicating 'Equity injection for aircraft engine dry-leasing operating/finance lease portfolio'. The remitting bank should use a commercial purpose code such as 'Corporate equity investment' or 'Investment in non-resident enterprise' where the correspondent bank requires such code. The receiving Vanuatu bank will verify the transfer against corporate resolutions, share subscription documents, KYC/UBO filings, and the AML/CFT source-of-funds breakdown. No RBV prior approval is required; however, the receiving bank is obliged to maintain records and report threshold-triggered transactions to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit.Outbound trade disbursements for aircraft engine purchases, security deposits, lease termination compensation, and maintenance reserves should be executed via SWIFT MT103 direct credits from the Vanuatu IC's bank account to the payee's correspondent banking account. For high-value engine acquisitions, a commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by a Vanuatu bank or a prime international confirming bank provides title and payment protection against presentation of the engine purchase agreement, bill of sale, airworthiness export certificate, and release/dispatch documents. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0.0% – Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax. An International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] is statutorily exempt from all direct taxation: no CIT, no branch profits tax, no capital gains tax, and no dividend or royalty withholding at source.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - no dividend withholding tax is imposed by Vanuatu on distributions made by an International Company to any non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - Vanuatu has no transfer pricing legislation and no Local File or Master File thresholds. The relevant obligation is the economic substance filing duty, which applies to any International Company deriving income from a relevant activity such as financing and leasing irrespective of turnover amount.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The International Companies (Economic Substance) Amendment Act No. 38 of 2019 requires every Vanuatu IC carrying on a relevant activity - expressly including financing and leasing, which covers aircraft engine dry-leasing - to satisfy an economic substance test. The IC must be managed and controlled in Vanuatu, maintain adequate premises, employ a suitable number of qualified staff, incur adequate operating expenditure, and perform all core income-generating activities locally. An annual substance return is mandatory, and failure to comply exposes the IC to financial penalties, deregistration, and compulsory exchange of information with treaty partners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax and VAT Evasion

Statutory sanction: Default assessment, interest at 20% per annum on unpaid VAT, a civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax shortfall under the VAT Act [Cap 273], and criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment for individuals involved.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorize false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalties; the corporate veil may be pierced where the IC is operated as a mere facade, exposing directors to civil restitution, asset seizure, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalties of up to VT 5,000,000 plus daily default fines, deregistration and striking-off by VFSC, loss of the IC's tax-exempt status, and automatic exchange of information with foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly make false substance declarations face personal fines and disqualification orders; where cross-border enforcement arises, foreign courts may issue travel bans, sequestration orders, and restitution judgments piercing the corporate veil.
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Supervisory penalties by VFSC and the Financial Intelligence Unit, forfeiture of assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act, revocation of licensed agent relationships, and criminal prosecution for money laundering with substantial fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal liability for money laundering, civil confiscation of personal assets, and travel restrictions; banks and escrow providers may freeze accounts controlled by the IC, effectively collapsing the leasing operation.
Fraudulent Filing and Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Fines under the International Companies Act [Cap 222], deregistration of the IC, cancellation of the registered agent's licence for complicity, and court-ordered restitution to affected creditors.

Director personal liability: Directors who procure or consent to false filings are personally liable for civil restitution, may be disqualified from acting as directors, and may be imprisoned; the corporate veil will be pierced to recover misapplied lease proceeds.
