Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The International Companies Act [Cap. 222] imposes no minimum authorized or paid-up capital and no escrow account is required; at least one (1) share of no par value must be allotted to the subscriber.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; national VAT and tax administration portal: https://vat.gov.vu and ASYCUDA World customs clearance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a fulfilment/kitting operation in Vanuatu must apply for a Taxpayer Identification Number and VAT Registration with the Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department via the prescribed form before commencing taxable activity or immediately upon exceeding the VAT registration threshold. The application must include the certificate of incorporation, the International Company registered agent details, constitutional and corporate ownership documents, local place of business evidence, projected taxable turnover and export volume descriptions. The Department verifies the application and issues a Taxpayer Identification Number and VAT Registration Certificate. Non-resident international companies with no physical presence or taxable activity in Vanuatu are not required to obtain a domestic TIN for offshore operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone; the Vanuatu International Company is an offshore non-resident entity under the International Companies Act and is outside the normal Vanuatu income tax net. No income tax, corporation tax, capital gains tax, dividend tax or withholding tax is imposed on non-resident offshore income; however, a physical kitting and fulfilment centre in Vanuatu that makes taxable supplies is subject to VAT compliance and must register once the statutory turnover threshold is exceeded.

Available election forms

Vanuatu does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election or hybrid-mismatch elections. The International Company is statutorily classified as a non-resident offshore entity. For VAT, a person whose taxable turnover is below the statutory threshold may elect voluntary registration, and a registered person may apply for deregistration when no longer carrying on taxable activity. No group VAT registration, cash accounting election or simplified annual VAT election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Vatu (VT) 5,000,000 of taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; persons making exclusively zero-rated export supplies are taxable persons and are required to register when the threshold is met. A foreign International Company with a Vanuatu fulfilment centre is considered to have a taxable activity and must monitor taxable turnover, including zero-rated export supplies, for registration purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VaRit registration supplies of goods and ancillary kitting, packaging and assembly services qualify for the zero per cent VAT rate when the goods are physically exported from Vanuatu by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf, are exported within the statutory removal window commonly applied in Vanuatu of 28 days from the time of supply, and are supported by evidence of export to a destination outside Vanuatu. The zero-rating treatment also applies to international transport-related supplies and to services supplied to non-resident persons in respect of goods located outside Vanuatu. A registered fulfilment operator must show that the goods left Vanuatu and that the services performed were integral to the export supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration approved by Vanuatu Customs through ASYCUDA World
  • Bill of Lading or signed Air Waybill showing the port of discharge or destination outside Vanuatu
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sale or fulfilment services contract identifying the foreign buyer and the ownership of the exported goods
  • Packing list and itemised bill of materials for kitting, packaging and assembly services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/payment advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign purchaser
  • Shipping vessel or airline departure register, container seal records and other proof of physical movement out of Vanuatu
  • Zero-rated supply register and VAT invoice records linking each export declaration to the corresponding VAT return

Vanuatu does not currently mandate a real-time e-invoicing fiscal clearance system, SAF-T reporting or digital stamping. Registered persons must issue VAT invoices containing prescribed particulars, including the supplier's name, Taxpayer Identification Number, VAT Registration Number, recipient details, description of goods or services, VAT rate and VAT amount. Customs export declarations are lodged electronically through ASYCUDA World and must be aligned with the supporting export evidence retained for VAT zero-rating and refund purposes.

The Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department may audit zero-rated export claims and input VAT refunds by reference to shipping and customs evidence, foreign-exchange receipts and movement records. If goods were not actually exported, were falsely documented, or export proceeds were not received and repatriated within the prescribed window, the zero rating is clawed back and VAT becomes payable at 12.5 per cent on the supply. Penalties include additional tax on the evaded or incorrectly claimed VAT, late payment interest, cancellation of input VAT credits, and criminal prosecution including fines and imprisonment for knowingly providing false export documents. Retrospective assessments may be issued for extended periods in cases of fraud or deliberate non-disclosure, and directors and registered agents may be held jointly liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account purposes. There are no exchange-control laws, no capital-account approval, and no restrictions on the establishment or operation of foreign-currency accounts. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, repayment of shareholder loans, and full repatriation of capital can be effected without central-bank authorization, subject only to standard banking due-diligence and financial-transaction reporting obligations under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act. Because Vanuatu operates a zero-tax, substance-sensitive offshore regime, the practical hurdle for a tax-exempt International Company is not convertibility but the ability to produce commercial and operational evidence that satisfies Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Know-Your-Customer (KYC) standards imposed by the international correspondent banks clearing vatu or foreign-currency wires.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Vanuatu imposes no statutory surrender obligation on export receipts, no mandatory conversion ratio into VUV, and no entry timeline for foreign-currency proceeds. Export proceeds may be retained in an offshore foreign-currency account, in a domestic VUV account, or in a multi-currency account at a licensed Vanuatu bank without penalty. The only compliance requirement is that the underlying export documentation — commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading/airway bills, customs export entries, and bank collection records — must be retained for a minimum of five years and produced to the bank or the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit upon request as audit evidence of the legitimacy of the trade flows. No surrender certificate or central-bank form is required.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) — Port Vila; the sole monetary authority and prudential regulator of licensed commercial banks in Vanuatu, with supervisory responsibility over the national payment and settlement systems and the custody of official foreign-reserve assets, but without exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium with structural limitations. Cross-border digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Wise, Payoneer, and similar platforms can be onboarded as service providers to a Vanuatu International Company, offering multi-currency corporate accounts in AUD, USD, EUR, and GBP, as well as virtual-account and batch-payout capabilities. Their legal and operational viability is constrained by three factors: (1) they hold no Vanuatu banking or EMI licence and are accordingly not supervised by the RBV or the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC); (2) they cannot clear or hold VUV-denominated balances and must route all Vanuatu-related transactions through foreign correspondent banks; and (3) balances held with them are not covered by any Vanuatu deposit-protection arrangement. Therefore, EMIs are acceptable as a secondary liquidity engine for cross-border trade and ACH-based disbursements, but they cannot replace the relationship with a licensed Vanuatu commercial bank for local regulatory substance, VUV clearing, or capital escrow obligations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

International Company (IC) with tax-exempt status, non-resident directors, and no demonstrable economic substance in Vanuatu.

Root cause: International Company (IC) with tax-exempt status, non-resident directors, and no demonstrable economic substance in Vanuatu.

Defense evidence: Certified copy of Certificate of Incorporation and current Memorandum & Articles; registered agent's certificate of due diligence; physical address evidence, including a lease agreement, a recent utility invoice and office photographs; a payroll and staffing record showing that at least one director or compliance officer is physically employed in Vanuatu; a board resolution adopting an economic-substance policy; and a current annual return filed with the VFSC.
Complex beneficial ownership using trust, nominee shareholder, or multi-layer offshore structures.

Root cause: Complex beneficial ownership using trust, nominee shareholder, or multi-layer offshore structures.

Defense evidence: Complete trust instrument or nominee declaration; certificate of registration of the trust (where required); full name, residential address, passport copy and source-of-wealth statement of the settlor, trustee, protector and each beneficiary; a chart showing the 25%+ ownership through to the natural person; an official beneficial-ownership register prepared in the VFSC-prescribed form; and a banker's reference from an accepted institution covering the ultimate beneficial owner.
High-frequency ACH or Airwallex payment volumes inconsistent with a low-risk warehouse, kitting, and packaging business.

Root cause: High-frequency ACH or Airwallex payment volumes inconsistent with a low-risk warehouse, kitting, and packaging business.

Defense evidence: Three-way inventory reconciliation between purchase orders, warehouse movement logs and sales orders; customs entry/exit records for goods moving through the fulfilment centre; executed warehousing and kitting service contracts with at least three distinct e-commerce principals; monthly commercial invoices and packing lists matching the transaction value; a projected 12-month cash-flow model; and a written compliance narrative explaining the absence of unlicensed financial-activity.
Inbound capital injection from a group shareholder or connected party where the economic basis is not clearly evidenced.

Root cause: Inbound capital injection from a group shareholder or connected party where the economic basis is not clearly evidenced.

Defense evidence: Executed capital subscription agreement or intra-group shareholder loan agreement; board minutes authorising the receipt of funds; six months of bank statements from the remitting entity; a group structure chart with the ownership interests; a commercial assessment demonstrating the capital is required for purchase-order financing or inventory build-up; and a preliminary financial model showing debt-service capacity and source of repayment. This packet should be accompanied by an independent lawyer's opinion that the transaction has no money-laundering or tax-evasion indicators.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital must be routed through a correspondent-capable commercial bank using a SWIFT MT103 message. The instruction should specify the beneficiary account at the Vanuatu bank and include in SWIFT Field 70 one of the following narrative tags: 'INCAP-IC' for equity injection, 'INLOAN-IC' for a shareholder loan, or 'SK-PURCHASE' for purchase-order funding. The remitting bank must transmit the sending institution's BIC, the borrower's Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) where available, and complete source-of-funds information. For settlement in USD, the preferred path is through an international intermediary in New York such as an ANZ New York correspondent or BNY Mellon; for AUD, through the Australian segment of ANZ; for EUR, through BRED Banque Populaire in France; and the receiving Vanuatu bank will convert to VUV at its spot rate if required. RBV authorisation is not required, but the Vanuatu receiving bank must tag the credit as a capital transaction and maintain a full audit trail, including the remittance instruction, confirmations, and a copy of the board resolution.For supplier invoices and operational costs, the standard rail is a SWIFT MT103 message processed through the Vanuatu bank's foreign correspondent, with payment purposes coded 'PROP' (goods) or 'ADV-REFUND' (returns). Direct USD disbursements should instruct the beneficiary to settle via CHIPS or FedWire, while AUD disbursements clear through the Australian high-value settlement system via ANZ Australia. For high-value procurement invoices in excess of USD 50,000, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC) is the recommended instrument, issued by the Vanuatu bank by SWIFT MT700 and payable by MT202 against a clean presentation of commercial documents; this mitigates counterparty risk and provides the necessary customs and compliance trail. VUV-denominated disbursements must be settled only through a licensed domestic clearing participant. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax; International Companies are statutorily exempt from all income, profits, and capital taxes under the International Companies Act (Cap 222), evidenced by a Tax Exemption Certificate issued by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for International Companies making distributions to non-resident shareholders under the statutory exemption in the International Companies Act (Cap 222); the general statutory withholding rate of 15% under the Income Tax Act applies only where exempt status has been lost or breached.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No vatu-denominated Local File or Master File thresholds are codified because exempt International Companies pay no corporate income tax; the operative thresholds are those of the ultimate parent's tax jurisdiction, typically EUR 7,500,000 aggregate related-party turnover or USD 10,000,000 intercompany transactions in line with OECD Chapter V practice. For the ISIC 5210 kitting entity, a transfer pricing policy memorandum should nevertheless be retained documenting the arm's length remuneration for value-added logistics services in order to prevent recharacterisation by the onshore parent regulator.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu applies economic substance requirements to International Companies in fulfilment of its OECD BEPS and EU Code of Conduct commitments. Fulfilment, kitting, and related-party logistics activity constitutes a 'distribution and service centre business' relevant activity; the International Company must evidence physical premises, qualified local employees, local operating expenditure, and strategic decision-making in Vanuatu, and must file the annual economic substance notification with the VFSC. The standard-risk tier applicable to ISIC 5210 (no statutory licence, ACH/Airwallex payment rails, low AML risk) requires a proportionate but genuine substance footprint; a material shortfall triggers exchange of information with the foreign parent's tax authority under Vanuatu's competent authority agreements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Sham Offshore Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: revocation of the tax exemption certificate, retroactive exposure to the 15% withholding tax regime, pecuniary penalties under the Income Tax Act, and referral to the VFSC for strike-off. Foreign jurisdiction: CFC and general anti-avoidance recharacterisation, denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, accuracy-related penalties of up to 100% to 200% of the avoided tax, and interest accrual.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false exemption declarations face fines and imprisonment under Vanuatu law; in the foreign parent jurisdiction, shadow-director and participation-note theories may expose directors to civil restitution orders, director disqualification, and cross-border travel bans.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: rejection of the annual economic substance attestation, administrative penalties under Vanuatu's economic substance framework, removal from the register, and automatic exchange of information with the parent's jurisdiction under the multilateral competent authority agreement. Foreign jurisdiction: the foreign tax authority disregards the entity, applies PFIC or CFC taxation, and imposes late-payment and accuracy penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for false substance certifications and for restoration costs following strike-off; foreign courts may impose personal liability under economic substance and GAAR rules where the director controlled the substance-free structure, including freezing orders, travel restrictions, and contempt proceedings.
AML/CFT Regulatory Breach and Illicit Fund Movements

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act, forfeiture of transaction proceeds, suspension of the registered agent's licence, and referral to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit for criminal prosecution; systemic breaches also trigger correspondent bank de-risking and reputational blacklisting.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability, fines, and imprisonment for money laundering under Vanuatu law; regulators may impose personal travel bans pending investigation, and foreign counterparties can obtain asset freezes through mutual legal assistance.
False Filings, Misrepresentation and Fraudulent Dissolution

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: offences under the International Companies Act (Cap 222) and ancillary companies legislation; per-filing fines, compulsory deregistration, disqualification from conducting offshore activity in Vanuatu, and additional penalties under the fraud provisions of the Penal Code where documents were falsified.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines and imprisonment for false declarations and fraudulent trading; the court may pierce the corporate veil to hold directors personally liable for unpaid VAT, agent fees, and third-party contractual debts, and may bar them from operating any company registered in Vanuatu.
