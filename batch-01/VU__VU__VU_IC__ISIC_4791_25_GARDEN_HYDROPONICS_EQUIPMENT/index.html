Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRCS), an agency of the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila, Republic of Vanuatu; registration services are available at VRCS offices in Port Vila and Santo, with no fully integrated national digital taxpayer portal.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities making taxable supplies in Vanuatu must apply to VRCS for VAT registration using Form VAT-01, accompanied by certificate of incorporation, proof of business address, bank account details, expected taxable turnover, and a statement of intended compliance with the Value Added Tax Act. Upon approval, VRCS issues a unique VAT registration number. International Companies incorporated under the International Companies Act are exempt from national direct taxes and therefore do not require an income tax registration number; however, VAT registration remains compulsory if the statutory turnover threshold is met or if the entity imports goods into Vanuatu on a continuing basis.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Foreign entities making taxable supplies in Vanuatu must apply to VRCS for VAT registration using Form VAT-01, accompanied by certificate of incorporation, proof of business address, bank account details, expected taxable turnover, and a statement of intended compliance with the Value Added Tax Act. Upon approval, VRCS issues a unique VAT registration number. International Companies incorporated under the International Companies Act are exempt from national direct taxes and therefore do not require an income tax registration number; however, VAT registration remains compulsory if the statutory turnover threshold is met or if the entity imports goods into Vanuatu on a continuing basis.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None applicable. Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax and no capital gains tax; therefore, no check-the-box elections or hybrid-mismatch elections are available. The International Companies Act grants an unconditional statutory tax exemption for international companies, subject only to indirect tax (VAT and customs duties) registration and compliance obligations where triggered.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 of taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Vanuatu are eligible for zero-rating under the Value Added Tax Act, provided the supplier obtains and retains documentary evidence that the goods left Vanuatu within the statutory timeframe and that the recipient is outside Vanuatu. Services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Vanuatu may also qualify for zero-rating where they are performed overseas or directly in connection with exported goods. For online sales of garden hydroponics equipment, zero-rating is available only where the equipment is physically shipped from Vanuatu to a foreign destination and all export formalities are completed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusEx/Export Entry) approved by Vanuatu Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Vanuatu
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods, quantity, value, and HS tariff classification
  • Proof of export timing, including port/airport departure stamps and shipping carrier records
  • Foreign inward remittance certificate or bank settlement statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists in Vanuatu. There is no mandatory API-based e-invoicing system, no pre-clearance tax stamping, and no continuous transaction control regime. Registered persons must issue VAT invoices that satisfy the statutory content rules under the Value Added Tax Act; both paper and electronic VAT invoices are accepted provided they contain all required particulars and are supported by underlying accounting records.

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs may subject export zero-rating claims to enhanced document verification. Where a zero-rated export is unsupported by the mandatory documentation bundle, or where fraud or misrepresentation is established, VRCS may retroactively assess VAT at 12.5% on the gross supply, impose a penalty of up to 50% of the assessed tax, and charge statutory default interest from the date the VAT became due. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export VAT claims may result in fines up to VUV 2,000,000 and/or imprisonment. Retrospective VAT assessments generally extend to all supplies within a four-year limitation period, with no cap where fraud is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations: the VUV is fully convertible for current account payments and transfers with no government approval required. The capital account is liberalized for non-residents; a Vanuatu International Company (IC) is statutorily treated as non-resident and exempt from the Exchange Control Act, permitting unrestricted retention of export proceeds, unlimited holding of USD/AUD/NZD/EUR accounts, and unimpeded repatriation of dividends, interest, and loan principal. No profit-remittance hurdles or withholding taxes apply to an IC. Practical friction is limited to the thin offshore VUV market: daily liquidity is shallow, bank spreads on VUV crosses are wide, and material treasury flows should therefore be kept in USD to avoid conversion drag.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Resident exporters must repatriate export proceeds to an authorized dealer within six months of shipment and comply with RBV surrender directives. International Companies are exempt from surrender and repatriation obligations under the exchange-control exemption in the International Companies Act [Cap 222]; all online retail receipts may be retained offshore in hard currency with zero mandatory conversion to VUV, provided the entity maintains valid IC status and a non-resident treasury function.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) administers exchange control under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 75] and regulates authorized dealers; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) licenses and supervises International Companies. The Vatu (VUV) is issued by the RBV and managed against a weighted basket of the currencies of Vanuatu's major trading partners.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium. Major global EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex) do not support direct onboarding of a pure Vanuatu IC with VUV capability. EMIs that accept Pacific offshore structures typically require a regulated parent guarantee, a fully disclosed e-commerce business, and impose transaction-perimeter limits. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VUV 0 — no statutory transfer pricing regime; no Local File or Master File obligations are imposed on Vanuatu International Companies. Nevertheless, under Section 43 of the International Companies Act, the IC must maintain intercompany invoices, general ledgers, and bank records sufficient to disclose the true financial position and must keep such records for at least 7 years at the registered agent's address or at another disclosed location.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to the BVI or Seychelles models. Under the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices monitoring, Vanuatu applies light-touch substance requirements: appointment of a registered agent, maintenance of a registered office in Port Vila, compliance with the AML/CFT beneficial ownership register, and annual return filing. A pure online retail IC with no employees, office, or physical presence in Vanuatu is not carrying on a 'relevant activity' (banking, shipping, financing, distribution, IP holding, or service centre), and therefore faces no additional economic substance mandates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Under the Value Added Tax Act, evaded VAT is recoverable with penalties up to 200% of the tax; the Customs Act applies fines up to VUV 2,000,000; false declarations in company filings attract a fine up to VUV 5,000,000 under the International Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax or registration declarations are liable to imprisonment up to 3 years, asset confiscation, and civil restitution. The Supreme Court may impose a travel ban pending fraud proceedings, and directors may be personally barred from managing any Vanuatu-licensed company.
Shell Company Abuse and Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: The VFSC may strike the company off the register and impose a restoration penalty of VUV 50,000; in egregious cases the Supreme Court may order winding-up, forfeiture of the IC's domain name and payment-channel accounts, and deregistration of the local agent. Foreign revenue authorities may treat the IC as sham and deny the zero-tax status retroactively.

Director personal liability: Where the company is a mere façade, courts will pierce the veil: directors are personally liable for the obligations of the online retail business, including customer refunds, supplier claims, and foreign tax assessments. Personal fines, asset restraint orders, and extradition on tax-fraud charges also apply.
AML and Financial Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act, a body corporate is liable to fines up to VUV 100,000,000; the underlying transaction is voidable; the VFSC may suspend the registered agent's licence. Money laundering is punishable by imprisonment up to 20 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecuted for money laundering where wilful blindness is proven; personal fines up to VUV 50,000,000, forfeiture of personal assets, 10-year disqualification from acting as a director of any Vanuatu company, and detention or travel bans pending trial are authorised sanctions.
Local Market Infringement and Unlicensed Retail in Vanuatu

Statutory sanction: Section 53 of the International Companies Act provides that an IC carrying on business with Vanuatu residents commits an offence punishable by a fine up to VUV 1,000,000, compulsory deregistration, and unenforceability of all local contracts entered by the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors are civilly liable to local creditors and third parties on the basis of breach of statutory warranty; the Supreme Court may issue a travel ban preventing directors from departing Vanuatu pending resolution, and on conviction directors may face imprisonment up to 2 years plus restitution orders.
