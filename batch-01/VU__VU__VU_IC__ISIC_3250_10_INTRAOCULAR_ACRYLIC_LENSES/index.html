Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the International Companies Act [Cap. 222]; the standard authorized capital is USD 50,000 divided into 50,000 no-par-value shares, and the incorporation fee scales with authorized capital. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official portal: https://www.vrc.gov.vu

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must apply for a VAT registration identification number using VRC Form VAT 1 lodged with the Vanuatu Revenue and Customs office. Required supporting documents include certified memorandum and articles of incorporation, proof of business address, bank reference, and evidence of projected taxable turnover. For an International Company, the VFSC certificate of incorporation is the primary corporate document; the IC is exempt from income tax but must obtain a VAT ID only if it makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu. Foreign entities without physical presence in Vanuatu are generally not required to register for VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No direct income tax election exists in Vanuatu for International Companies; tax-exempt status is automatic under the International Companies Act. For VAT, voluntary registration is available where annual taxable turnover does not exceed the compulsory threshold. No check-the-box, fiscal unity, or hybrid mismatch election is recognized under Vanuatu domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 annual taxable turnover (compulsory); voluntary registration permitted below this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods, provided the goods leave Vanuatu within 28 days of supply and the exporter holds documentary evidence of export. Services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply are zero-rated if the services do not relate to land or goods situated in Vanuatu and are not enjoyed in Vanuatu. Intraocular acrylic lenses manufactured under ISIC 3250 qualify when sold to foreign ophthalmology distributors and shipped outside Vanuatu under a valid customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic or manual submission to Vanuatu Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Certificate of origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)
  • VAT zero-rating refund application and supporting input tax documents
  • Bank SWIFT credit advice or payment evidence (Inward Remittance Certificate is not mandatory due to absence of exchange controls)

No real-time electronic invoice clearance, digital stamping, or government API filing regime is currently implemented. Registered businesses must issue VAT invoices containing prescribed particulars and retain records for seven years. No fiscal electronic transaction control is mandated.

Where zero-rating is claimed without documented export evidence, Vanuatu Revenue and Customs may assess output VAT at 12.5% plus statutory interest and impose civil penalties up to 200% of the tax avoided. Fraudulent documentation can result in criminal prosecution and imprisonment under the Value Added Tax Act. Retrospective assessments may extend for six years from the end of the relevant tax period. There is no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline in Vanuatu.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The VUV operates under a managed-float with practical full convertibility for current-account trade transactions; all FX transactions must execute through an RBV-supervised authorized dealer. Capital-account items — share capital subscription, intercompany loans, trade credit and equity injections from non-resident shareholders — are permitted for a licensed International Company, but each inward transfer requires an authorized-dealer purpose declaration, UBO source-of-funds attestation and automatic RBV balance-of-payments statistical reporting. Profit, dividend, royalty and management-fee remittances to non-residents are free of Vanuatu withholding tax and do not require RBV pre-approval, yet banks impose documentary hurdles (tax-exemption certificate, audited financial statements, licensed-agent good-standing certificate) to rebut shell-company indicators. For the intraocular acrylic lens trade, export invoices in USD/EUR/AUD settle freely; local-cost payments (salaries, facilities, agent fees) must be converted to VUV unless a Foreign Currency Retention Account has been RBV-approved. The convertibility stress point is documentary, not fiscal: medical-device (ISIC 3250) transactions attract enhanced due diligence from authorized dealers and correspondents, particularly on purpose-code consistency and end-user verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Codified under Exchange Control Act [Cap 96] and the RBV's authorized-dealer exchange-control directions: (i) export proceeds from the sale of foldable hydrophobic intraocular lenses must be received in full no later than 180 days after shipment; (ii) upon receipt, the foreign currency must be offered for sale to an authorized dealer in exchange for VUV within 30 days — a default 100% surrender obligation, relaxable only through an RBV-approved foreign-currency retention authorization matched against the IC's import obligations for hydrophobic acrylic polymer feedstocks and precision lathe consumables; (iii) each physical export shipment requires an exchange-control permit or an authorized-dealer endorsement on the commercial invoice before dispatch, with the bank returning the endorsed invoice to the RBV for surrender reconciliation; (iv) non-surrender, late surrender or under-invoicing exposes the IC to monetary penalties, debarment from designated-dealer status and potential account-freezing orders. Treasury control procedure: one dedicated USD collection account at the primary clearing bank with a standing instruction to sweep approved retention amounts daily and convert residual balances to VUV within the 30-day window.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) — the sole statutory monetary authority, foreign-exchange administrator, banking supervisor and national payment system operator. Governing legal instruments: Exchange Control Act [Cap 96], Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap 125], Financial Transactions Reporting Act 2019 (as amended by the July 2024 AML/CFT package), Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 14 of 2024, International Companies Act [Cap 222], and Financial Services Commission Act [Cap 265]. The RBV licenses authorized dealers (commercial banks), enforces export-proceeds surrender obligations, approves foreign-currency retention accounts, and issues exchange-control directions applicable to the VFSC-special-licensed International Company (VU_IC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW — legally and operationally constrained. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, MultiPass, Currencycloud-based programs) generally decline VFSC-licensed International Companies engaged in a High_Risk_Special_License medical-device trade because Vanuatu has no EU/UK EMI passporting equivalence and EMIs have no access to the RBV-operated national payment and settlement facility for VUV clearing. The Exchange Control Act [Cap 96] prohibits the IC from conducting foreign-exchange transactions through unauthorized persons, rendering EMI-based collection of export proceeds unlawful unless a licensed authorized dealer sits beneath the EMI arrangement as the legal clearing counterparty. Viable architecture: use BSP or BRED as the onshore clearing layer and, if treasury concentration is desired in Australia/Hong Kong, establish a separately incorporated, commercially substantive distribution affiliate — never route the Vanuatu IC's export invoices directly through an EMI. Additional blocker: virtually all EMIs impose physical-presence and payroll-location tests that a tax-exempt IC in Vanuatu cannot satisfy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity injections must route as SWIFT MT103 from the remitting shareholder's bank to the beneficiary's Capital Escrow Account at NBV, BSP or BRED. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must read verbatim: 'VU_IC — SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION — INTERNATIONAL COMPANIES ACT [CAP 222]' or '/INV/ EQUITY — VU_IC — VFSC FILE [number]'. Field 72 must restate the VFSC file number and IC registration number. The receiving authorized dealer will block the credit unless: (a) board resolution authorizing the capital call is pre-submitted; (b) the updated share register is deposited with the bank; (c) source-of-funds attestation from the remitting shareholder is provided; and (d) all UBOs clear the FIU sanctions and PEP screen. On completion of the subscription, the bank internally debits the escrow account and credits the operational account upon presentation of the signed share certificate, and automatically files the international-transfer report with the Vanuatu FIU and the RBV balance-of-payments unit. Recommended staging: day 1 — escrow deposit; day 2 — share certificate issuance and sweep to the operational USD/VUV account; day 3 — disbursement against equipment purchase agreements for surgical-grade lathe cells.Clearing instruments: (1) Supplier prepayments for hydrophobic acrylic feedstocks and precision components — SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 'TRADE PREPAYMENT — MEDICAL DEVICE RAW MATERIALS — PO [number]' plus the proforma invoice, HS classification and VFSC special license attachment; the paying authorized dealer verifies the approved import ledger and applies the 180-day export-reconciliation rule. (2) Documentary credits — irrevocable sight Letter of Credit issued via MT700 under UCP600 for FDA/CE-certified component purchases from Europe and Asia, employing the bank's USD correspondent chain (HSBC, Citi or ANZ New York for BSP/BRED networks); presentation documents must include the ocean/air waybill, commercial invoice, ISO 13485 certificate and independent inspection certificate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW — legally and operationally constrained. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury, MultiPass, Currencycloud-based programs) generally decline VFSC-licensed International Companies engaged in a High_Risk_Special_License medical-device trade because Vanuatu has no EU/UK EMI passporting equivalence and EMIs have no access to the RBV-operated national payment and settlement facility for VUV clearing. The Exchange Control Act [Cap 96] prohibits the IC from conducting foreign-exchange transactions through unauthorized persons, rendering EMI-based collection of export proceeds unlawful unless a licensed authorized dealer sits beneath the EMI arrangement as the legal clearing counterparty. Viable architecture: use BSP or BRED as the onshore clearing layer and, if treasury concentration is desired in Australia/Hong Kong, establish a separately incorporated, commercially substantive distribution affiliate — never route the Vanuatu IC's export invoices directly through an EMI. Additional blocker: virtually all EMIs impose physical-presence and payroll-location tests that a tax-exempt IC in Vanuatu cannot satisfy.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Vanuatu transfer-pricing documentation threshold exists; in VUV the statutory threshold is effectively zero because no income tax return or TP disclosure is required. For multinational compliance and financial due diligence, the IC should maintain OECD-aligned Master File and Local File documentation when the global group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (approx. VUV 95 billion), and should nonetheless maintain intercompany pricing evidence where cross-border lens sales, royalties, or management fees exceed VUV 30 million (approx. USD 240,000) per annum.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations analogous to BVI or Cayman for International Companies; however, the IC must satisfy the substantive presence expectations of OECD/CRS, anti-money laundering, and correspondent banking standards. For this intraocular lens manufacturer, economic substance is demonstrated by physical manufacturing facilities, calibrated CNC lathe equipment, qualified production staff, inventory contracts, board minutes in Vanuatu, and independent decision-making over polymer pricing and distributors. A passive letterbox entity without operational substance will be recharacterised by foreign tax authorities, blacklisted by payment intermediaries, and denied access to SWIFT Wire and Letter of Credit payment rails.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Vanuatu criminal law, a director who knowingly makes a materially false statement to VFSC is liable to imprisonment and/or a criminal fine of up to VUV 10,000,000. VFSC may strike off the International Company and confiscate corporate records. Foreign tax authorities may impose back taxes, interest, and civil penalties up to 200% of the underpaid amount, with criminal referral for tax evasion in the manufacturer's actual jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the director used the IC as a façade. Directors face personal civil restitution for diverted profits, disqualification from managing companies in Vanuatu and in CRS partner states, travel bans pending foreign investigation, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction for fraud or perjury.
Shell Company and Conduit Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: VFSC may deny the Certificate of Good Standing and strike off the company for failure to maintain a bona fide registered agent. In onshore jurisdictions, the arrangement is recharacterised as a sham; the Vanuatu margin is allocated back to the actual manufacturing entity, with transfer-pricing penalties of 20% to 40% of the recharacterised profit. The Economic Substance horizontal risk causes all related party payments to be disallowed as deductible expenses.

Director personal liability: A director who signs or approves a sham conduit contract becomes personally liable for consequential loss suffered by group entities. Foreign courts can issue worldwide asset freezing orders, order restitution to the tax authority, and impose director disqualification of up to 15 years. Travel bans are typically imposed where offshore transfers are suspected to be dissipating assets.
AML/CFT and International Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Vanuatu's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing framework, money laundering or terrorist financing through a corporate vehicle carries imprisonment of up to 10 years and fines up to VUV 50,000,000. VFIU may freeze and forfeit all bank balances, inventory, and proceeds of lens sales traced to sanctioned transactions. Correspondent banks may additionally terminate all banking relationships with the IC and its directors.

Director personal liability: AML/CFT compliance officers and directors are personally criminally liable for failing to implement AML controls or intentionally bypassing sanctions checks. There is no corporate veil protection where the director was the controlling mind of the money laundering offence. Senior officers may face extradition requests, travel bans, and personal asset forfeiture.
Unlicensed Medical Device Manufacturing and Customs Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under Vanuatu customs legislation, customs fraud and misdeclaration attract penalties of up to 10 times the evaded duty, forfeiture of the medical lens inventory, and criminal prosecution of the responsible individual. Sale of uncertified intraocular lenses is prohibited by medical device regulators and may prompt emergency product recall, civil product liability claims, and cancellation of export permits in the destination jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false customs declarations are personally liable for customs penalties and may be subjected to criminal imprisonment. In product liability actions, directors of an IC may be named as co-defendants where the lens failed due to non-compliant manufacturing practices. Personal assets, including aircraft, vessels, and foreign bank accounts, may be seized through enforcement of the resulting judgement.
