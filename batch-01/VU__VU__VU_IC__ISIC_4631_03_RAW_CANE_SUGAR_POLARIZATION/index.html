Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Service (VRCS), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; company and international company registry administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC). Official portals: https://vrcs.gov.vu and https://vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company (IC) is incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] through a licensed registered agent in Port Vila. The agent lodges the memorandum and articles, registers the registered office and agent, and files the declaration of exempt status; the VFSC Registrar then issues the Certificate of Incorporation and, where applied for, a Tax Exemption Certificate confirming relief from all direct taxation. Because Vanuatu imposes no income tax, corporate tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, or VAT, a separate statutory VAT ID is not issued. The IC obtains a TIN (Tax Identification Number) from the VRCS principally for customs, import duty, and statistical records; for physical trading premises, a business license must be obtained from the Vanuatu Investment Authority or the relevant provincial/municipal council.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Not applicable. Vanuatu has no check-the-box, entity-classification election, or hybrid mismatch election because its domestic law imposes no entity-level or pass-through income tax. An International Company may elect statutory 'exempt company' status under the International Companies Act [Cap 222], evidenced by a certificate issued by the VFSC Registrar; a foreign company may continue or redomicile in Vanuatu and retain its foreign hybrid classification for the purposes of foreign tax law. No CFC, thin-capitalization, ATAD, or public CbCR filing elections apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no VAT, GST, or sales/use tax operationally in force); customs import duties under the Customs Act [Cap 38], zero-export duty, and tourism-specific levies apply. A Value Added Tax Act was certified but its commencement remains deferred by successive government orders; until operational commencement, the effective indirect tax burden on sugar commodity exports is nil.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST. Customs importer registration is required for recurring clearance regardless of transactional value; a business license is required from the relevant authority where any economic activity occurs. No minimum threshold is prescribed for registration as an International Company engaged in wholesale export trading.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. With no operative VAT/GST, export sales of VHP raw cane centrifugal sugar (HS 1701.14) are not zero-rated and no input VAT refund mechanism arises. Duty treatment is exclusively customs-based: physical ocean exports are free of export duty and export taxes, and duty-free or dutiable status depends on the import tariff regime in the destination country, not on Vanuatu zero-rating rules. Any preferential duty claim in the importing jurisdiction is supported by a Vanuatu Certificate of Origin.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Vanuatu SAD / ASYCUDA World entry)
  • Bill of Lading evidencing physical ocean shipment to the overseas buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/LC documentary evidence of payment proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (for any preferential tariff claim)
  • Shipping authority letter and, where requested, VFSC corporate standing certificate

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate is in force. Customs declarations and clearance are processed electronically through the ASYCUDA World system operated by the VRCS Customs Division; traders submit electronic entries with supporting documents retained per Customs Act requirements. No national e-invoice clearance authority is designated.

Vanuatu maintains no exchange-control regime, so no statutory FX repatriation deadline is imposed; the 180-day convention is adopted contractually and evidenced by LC/SWIFT records for customs and bank audit files. Customs penalties for false declaration, undervaluation, or smuggling include forfeiture of goods, fines up to VUV 200,000, and imprisonment under the Customs Act [Cap 38]; mis-declaration may be reassessed with a 10% surcharge. Misuse of the VFSC Tax Exemption Certificate risks revocation and recovery of any duties or taxes otherwise payable in full, together with disqualification of the IC and its directors by the Registrar. Fraudulent export documentation, including fabricated bills of lading or inward remittance certificates, may attract criminal liabilities under the Penal Code and the Financial Dealers Act and is treated by VFSC-licensed banks and agents as high-scrutiny conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is a convertible currency pegged to an undisclosed trade-weighted basket anchored primarily to USD and AUD. Current-account items (trade settlements, freight, insurance, agency fees) are freely convertible without individual exchange-control approval. The capital account is substantially open: a Vanuatu International Company (VU_IC) may issue shares to non-residents and freely remit dividends, liquidation proceeds, and management fees without any statutory tax or convertibility hurdle, subject only to bank-level AML/KYC verification and VFSC annual licence renewal. No profit-remittance tax, dividend withholding tax, or capital gains tax applies to the IC's foreign-source VHP raw sugar trading income under the tax-exempt IC regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory VUV conversion ratio exist in Vanuatu. A VU_IC may retain 100% of raw cane sugar export proceeds in USD or any freely usable currency in its Vanuatu AD account or offshore account. ADs must submit external transaction statistics to the RBV for balance-of-payments purposes, but there is no repatriation timeline, no conversion mandate, and no minimum holding period for export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) acting as the delegated Exchange Control authority under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 108]; foreign exchange administration is executed through RBV-licensed Authorised Dealers (ADs) operating in Port Vila and Luganville.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility. Vanuatu has no dedicated EMI or neo-bank licensing statute; the Banking Act [Cap 34] and the International Banking Act [Cap 139] reserve deposit-taking, payment clearing, and trade credit functions for licenced banks. VFSC Category licences cover securities and financial dealers, not clearing banks. Reputable offshore EMIs (Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business, and similar) generally decline Vanuatu ICs because of correspondent-bank de-risking and the offshore-IC perception profile; an EMI may serve only as a secondary expense/FX rail, never as the primary liquidity or LC clearing channel for raw sugar trades.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders remit via SWIFT MT103 from a recognised international bank to the IC's USD account held at a Vanuatu AD. Beneficiary field 59 must state the IC legal name and VFSC registration number; field 70 (Remittance Information) must read 'SHARE CAPITAL INJECTION - VU_IC' together with the allotment resolution date. The AD applies the Exchange Control (General) Exemption Order to effect the credit without prior RBV approval, but requires the originator's banker reference, the certified share allotment resolution, and a source-of-funds declaration. No minimum paid-up capital is required by the VFSC; nevertheless, a first deposit of USD 20,000-50,000 is recommended to activate the trade-finance credit profile and satisfy the AD's internal rating model.Primary rail: Revolving Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) issued by the Vanuatu AD or, for lines above USD 5,000,000, issued in Port Vila and confirmed by a Singapore or Hong Kong position bank settling in USD via Fedwire/CHIPS. Supplier payments for VHP sugar cargoes use sight or 30-90 day usance drawings, with documents presented at the beneficiary's counters and reimbursement via MT103/MT202 cover. Freight and insurance use MT103 with field 70 citing 'VOYAGE NUMBER - POL/POD - GRADE VHP' plus the vessel IMO reference. Sale-side proceeds enter via an incoming export LC advised through the Vanuatu AD and discounted at approximately 90-110 basis points above SOFR. Fallback rail: documentary collection (D/P or D/A) against pre-screened Asian counterparties only, to eliminate open-account transit exposure on cargoes in transit.USD/VUV outright forward contracts with RBV-licensed Authorised Dealers (ANZ, BSP, BRED) with tenors up to 12 months, collateralised by cash margin or trade receivables.Offshore USD/VUV non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) under ISDA 2002 with a Singapore or Sydney prime broker, settled in USD because VUV is non-deliverable beyond Vanuatu banking channels.USD/AUD and USD/CNH deliverable forwards or swaps executed with the Vanuatu AD's offshore treasury desk, used to hedge freight, port handling, and refinery-consumable costs.Cross-currency swaps (USD principal versus VUV) with an Authorised Dealer, subject to RBV notification where a Vanuatu-resident counterparty is involved; plain-vanilla structures only, with speculative VUV option positions restricted by AD internal policy.
