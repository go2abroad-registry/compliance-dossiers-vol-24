Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Authority (VRCA), established under the Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Authority Act No. 13 of 2014; official portal https://www.vrca.gov.vu, with customs declarations processed electronically through ASYCUDA World. Company incorporation and licensing are administered separately by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company (IC) with VFSC under the International Companies Act [CAP 222] to obtain the VFSC registration number and Certificate of Incorporation. Step 2: Obtain no income-tax TIN because Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax and none is issued. Step 3: Apply to VRCA for a VAT Registration Number only if the IC makes domestic taxable supplies above VUV 5,000,000 per annum; ICs conducting exclusively international business are automatically exempt under s.55 of the IC Act and typically require no VAT number. Step 4: For physical export or import of endoscope hardware, open a Customs Client Account with VRCA Customs Division through ASYCUDA World to obtain a customs client code and file electronic entries.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone; the Vanuatu International Company is a non-resident offshore vehicle automatically exempt, without election, from corporate income tax, personal income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, stamp duty, VAT, and customs duties on all international business activities under s.55 of the International Companies Act [CAP 222], provided it does not trade with Vanuatu residents or hold land in Vanuatu; liability is limited and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, hybrid-mismatch, or CFC election regimes exist because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax. The s.55 IC Act exemption is automatic and self-executing; administratively, the IC may request a Certificate of Exemption from VFSC for bank and third-party reliance, and may technically apply for voluntary VAT registration under s.6 of the VAT Act to recover input VAT on domestic procurement, though this would create filing obligations and is generally inadvisable for a pure offshore exporter.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 (approximately USD 41,700) of annual taxable turnover in any 12-month period; registration is compulsory above the threshold and voluntary below it, and zero-rated export sales of an offshore IC fall outside the domestic VAT net.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Schedule 1 of the VAT Act for: (1) goods physically exported from Vanuatu within 30 days of supply or such further period as the Commissioner approves; (2) international services supplied to recipients outside Vanuatu, including offshore servicing, warranty, training, and software updates for surgical laparoscopy towers; and (3) supplies for international ships or aircraft. The supplier must retain documentary proof of physical export and foreign receipt; any supply to a Vanuatu resident would be standard-rated at 12.5% and would also breach the IC Act's domestic-trading prohibition.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World entry outwards)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Vanuatu
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing foreign-currency settlement
  • Commercial Invoice and zero-rated VAT Tax Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (issued by the Vanuatu Chamber of Commerce and Industry)

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or continuous transaction-controls mandate exists in Vanuatu. Registered persons must issue VAT tax invoices meeting the content requirements of Schedule 3 of the VAT Act and file periodic VAT returns through VRCA's online services portal; customs entries are cleared electronically through ASYCUDA World without supplementary digital stamping.

Vanuatu imposes no exchange-control law, so the 180-day figure is a commercial counterparty and clawback-risk benchmark rather than a statutory repatriation deadline. VRCA may raise default assessments and claw back improperly zero-rated supplies, with late payments subject to a 25% penalty plus 15% per annum statutory interest; fraudulent zero-rating claims are prosecutable under ss.41–42 of the VAT Act with fines up to VUV 5,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years, and mirror-image penalties apply for false customs export declarations under the Customs Act [CAP 91]. An IC abusing the s.55 exemption through concealed domestic trading additionally risks VFSC strike-off and assessment of all standard-rated supplies with penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a liberalised floating exchange rate regime for the Vatu (VUV). The Exchange Control Act [Cap. 77] formally reserves approval powers over certain foreign exchange transactions; however, the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] expressly exempts International Companies (ICs) from exchange control restrictions, so the VU_IC entity may freely receive foreign currency, convert balances, and remit profits without prior RBV authorisation. Current-account convertibility is effectively unrestricted: payments for imported endoscope sub-components, royalties, commissions, logistics, and professional fees are processed freely through licensed commercial banks. Capital-account convertibility is equally unrestricted for ICs: shareholder equity injections, shareholder loans, intra-group repayments, and liquidation distributions carry no RBV permit requirement and crystallise no domestic withholding tax. The substantive convertibility constraint is offshore: VUV is a highly illiquid minor currency with no active deliverable market in Singapore, London, or New York; therefore practical convertibility is achieved by maintaining USD/EUR/AUD multicurrency accounts at ANZ Vanuatu or BRED Vanuatu and converting only the VUV tranche actually needed for local overheads (registered agent fees, legalisation, banking charges). Profit remittance is completely tax-neutral because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax, no dividend withholding tax, no interest withholding tax, and no capital gains tax on International Companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists for the VU_IC. Under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 77] as applied in practice, an IC exporting Ultra-HD 4K laparoscopy tower systems is not required to convert export proceeds into VUV, and no fixed surrender timeline (e.g., 30/90 days) applies. Export collections may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, AUD, or any other permitted currency in an onshore or offshore account of the IC, may be netted against payable procurement commitments, and may fund dividend repatriation without RBV approval. The only operational compliance duty is bank-level reporting: licensed banks must file large-transaction and suspicious-transaction reports with the VFIU under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 21 of 2006, and all international wires flowing through ANZ/BRED are captured in SWIFT message records retained for VFIU inspection. The IC must preserve underlying export contracts, commercial invoices, bills of lading, certificates of origin, and inspection certificates to evidence the commercial genuineness of each USD receipt in the event a correspondent bank raises a trade-based money laundering query.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 125]; jointly administers the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 77] with the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit (VFIU) and supervises licensed banks under the Banking Act [Cap. 67].

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low (Conditional). Vanuatu law has no statutory category for standalone electronic money institutions; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) licenses commercial banks, insurers, and securities dealers, and does not issue EMI or payment-institution licences. Every transaction possessing a Vanuatu nexus must be held at an RBV-licensed bank. Offshore regulated EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions or US state-chartered fintech banks) may open multicurrency accounts for the IC as a non-resident legal person, and such accounts are valid for low-value disbursement and small receivables collection; however: (i) the initial capital injection and all high-value trade disbursements must remain booked at an RBV-licensed bank to preserve the commercial substance narrative of the VFSC-registered IC; (ii) processing material export proceeds through an external EMI without a Vanuatu-regulated intermediary raises correspondent de-risking risk because FATF and EU monitoring frameworks associate such structures with shell-company opacity; (iii) the IC Act non-resident perimeter is preserved by using EMIs exclusively for non-Vanuatu trade flows, never for Vanuatu-resident counterparties. Recommended architecture: ANZ or BRED as the primary treasury spine; a single regulated EMI confined to sub-USD 10,000 supplier convenience payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (statutorily exempt as a Vanuatu International Company under the International Companies Act, Cap 222; resident domestic companies are subject to 15%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an International Company; non-resident shareholders receive dividends free of any Vanuatu tax).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold under Vanuatu law for International Companies. Local threshold is N/A; must comply with foreign-parent-country OECD-aligned transfer pricing rules if the parent jurisdiction imposes such requirements.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu applies economic substance requirements under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019, but only for International Companies carrying on relevant activities (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing/leasing, headquarters, holding company, intellectual property, distribution and service center). The manufacture of surgical laparoscopy towers is not a relevant activity; nevertheless the IC must maintain adequate substance for its actual foreign operations if it holds taxable income relevant to another jurisdiction, and must file the annual ESR declaration when required.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Compliance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of the International Company status, withdrawal of tax exemption, a fine up to VT 500,000, and forced deregistration.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid taxes and subject to criminal conviction, including travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment of up to 2 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Defect

Statutory sanction: Penalties of up to VT 250,000, stripping of the IC tax exemption, exchange of beneficial ownership information to foreign tax authorities, and compulsory winding up.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face director disqualification, fines of up to VT 1,000,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years for knowingly maintaining a shell structure.
AML / CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act [Cap 279], fines up to VT 10,000,000, suspension of the registered agent, and revocation of the IC's certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors' personal assets may be seized; a court may issue travel bans and impose imprisonment up to 10 years for wilful money laundering offences.
Prohibited Domestic Business

Statutory sanction: The IC incurs a financial penalty of VT 1,000,000 and may be ordered to be wound up by the Supreme Court under Section 36 of the IC Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permitted the domestic trading are jointly and severally liable for all contractual debts incurred, and may be imprisoned for up to 3 years.
