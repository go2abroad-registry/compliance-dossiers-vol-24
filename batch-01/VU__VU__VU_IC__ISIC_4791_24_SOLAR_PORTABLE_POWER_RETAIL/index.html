Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila, Vanuatu; official portal: https://www.vanuatucustoms.gov.vu (VAT registration, customs declarations, ASYCUDA World). Corporate registry and offshore licensing: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), https://www.vfsc.vu. Business licensing (local activity only): Vanuatu Investment Promotion Authority (VIPA), https://www.investinvanuatu.vu.

E-government filing portal

Vanuatu imposes no income tax and maintains no general national TIN. A foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company (IC) obtains its statutory identity exclusively from VFSC: (1) file Articles of Incorporation through a licensed registered agent; (2) obtain Certificate of Incorporation and VFSC company number; (3) appoint a resident registered agent and, if required, a local secretary; (4) register for VAT with VRC on the prescribed VAT Registration Form 001 only if the IC makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu or elects voluntary registration, thereby receiving a VRC VAT Identification Number; (5) no further tax registration is required for a non-resident IC whose management, sales, and operations are conducted entirely outside Vanuatu.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Vanuatu imposes no income tax and maintains no general national TIN. A foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company (IC) obtains its statutory identity exclusively from VFSC: (1) file Articles of Incorporation through a licensed registered agent; (2) obtain Certificate of Incorporation and VFSC company number; (3) appoint a resident registered agent and, if required, a local secretary; (4) register for VAT with VRC on the prescribed VAT Registration Form 001 only if the IC makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu or elects voluntary registration, thereby receiving a VRC VAT Identification Number; (5) no further tax registration is required for a non-resident IC whose management, sales, and operations are conducted entirely outside Vanuatu.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists because Vanuatu has no corporate income tax code. Statutory options relevant to the IC: (i) voluntary VAT registration under the Value Added Tax Act for persons below the VUV 5,000,000 threshold, enabling input credit recovery on domestic costs; (ii) election to zero-rate exported goods and offshore services where the IC is VAT-registered; (iii) continuation or redomiciliation into Vanuatu under the International Companies Act, permitting transfer of foreign incorporation with a Ministerial certificate; (iv) election to obtain a Ministerial Certificate of Exemption confirming the IC's statutory tax-immune status. Non-resident status for exchange-control and AML purposes is automatic for ICs that do not hold a Vanuatu domestic business licence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 annual value of taxable supplies made in Vanuatu (compulsory registration); voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold; a pure offshore IC with no Vanuatu domestic supply has no VAT registration nexus and remains outside the VAT net; imports entering Vanuatu are taxed at the border at 12.5% VAT plus applicable customs duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and offshore services qualify for 0% VAT where the goods are physically exported from Vanuatu within 28 days of supply (or a longer period approved by the Commissioner) and the supplier retains commercial evidence of export; services performed wholly outside Vanuatu or supplied to a non-resident, including the IC's cross-border online retail of portable solar generators and panels where title and risk pass to foreign consumers outside Vanuatu, are either zero-rated or entirely outside the scope of Vanuatu VAT. For the IC, the controlling conclusion is that no Vanuatu VAT charge arises on foreign sales, so no zero-rating claim is required in the ordinary course of the offshore e-commerce model.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World CUS/EXP form and exit certificate)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Vanuatu
  • Commercial Invoice or Sales Contract for the exported goods
  • Packing List and HS Classification Supporting Materials
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Proof of Payment or Purchase Order from the offshore buyer

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping, or B2B e-invoicing mandate is in force. Suppliers may issue paper or PDF VAT invoices containing prescribed particulars (supplier name and VAT ID, invoice date, description of supply, consideration, and VAT amount). Import and export declarations are processed electronically via the ASYCUDA World single-window customs clearance system, which requires electronic filing of the Customs Declaration (CUS/EXP and CUS/IMP forms) with supporting documentation.

VRC audits zero-rated claims strictly and may reopen assessments within a standard three-year period, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or deliberate misstatement. Where export documentation is deficient, the Commissioner claws back VAT at 12.5% on the invoiced value, denies refunds, applies penalty interest, and imposes civil penalties of up to twice the tax evaded for wilful evasion; criminal proceedings may also be instituted for false declarations. For International Companies, substantiated involvement in fraudulent zero-rating or customs misdeclaration additionally triggers VFSC deregistration and forfeiture of the statutory tax-immune status, with directors and officers jointly and severally exposed to personal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains an exchange-control-light environment for non-resident International Companies. The Vatu (VUV) is the domestic legal tender and is not freely deliverable outside Vanuatu; practical convertibility occurs only through RBV-authorised commercial banks at commercial exchange rates. Current-account proceeds from cross-border e-commerce sales may be received in USD or another freely usable currency and converted to VUV without prior regulatory approval. Capital-account inflows such as share capital and shareholder loans are permitted for International Companies without a foreign-investment licence, provided the funds are remitted through the formal banking system from the shareholder's own account. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are not subject to Vanuatu withholding tax or exchange-control approval in the standard case, but each wire is independently screened by the receiving bank for sanctions, PEP status, source of funds and beneficial ownership transparency. The material treasury hurdle is therefore not regulatory convertibility but bank onboarding risk classification for a tax-exempt offshore vehicle; the IC should carry full commercial substance documentation in the source market to support account opening and routine settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation is imposed on Vanuatu International Companies. There is no prescribed timeline for bringing export receipts into Vanuatu and no mandatory conversion ratio into VUV. The IC may lawfully maintain export proceeds in USD, EUR, CNY or other foreign currency in an authorised-dealer bank account. For AML and balance-of-payments data purposes, the bank will expect the IC to preserve the underlying sales invoices, payment-rail settlement reports from Stripe or Affirm, shipping or courier evidence, and a coherent record-to-report reconciliation trail. Failure to produce this trail may cause the bank to treat the IC as a shell and impose enhanced due diligence or account restriction.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a secondary clearing layer, Low as the sole banking relationship. Legal constraints: a foreign electronic-money institution or digital bank is not licensed by the RBV to provide Vanuatu VUV-denominated payment services to a local IC, and many international EMIs decline Vanuatu-incorporated entities or impose enhanced customer due diligence. The IC may use an offshore EMI such as Wise Business or Airwallex to aggregate USD settlement proceeds from Stripe and Affirm and to make supplier disbursements, but the EMI will require a complete VFSC company file, registered-agent letter, UBO declaration, proof of genuine online retail operations, and a source-of-funds explanation. The EMI should never be used as the primary treasury account; a licensed Vanuatu bank account must remain the formal settlement and compliance anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial capital injection must be routed over SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bank-to-bank transfer from the shareholder's personal or corporate bank account directly into a dedicated Capital Escrow Account opened in the name of the Vanuatu IC at the primary Vanuatu settlement bank. Crypto proceeds, cash deposits, third-party payments, and Stripe or Affirm revenue settlements must never be used as capital injections. The bank will require prior notification of at least 48 hours, a board resolution authorising the injection, share subscription documents, a source-of-funds declaration, and the recipient bank's standard purpose code for equity financing or capital contribution. The remitter name and ultimate beneficial owner must match the bank's approved KYC records exactly. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0.00% - Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, payroll tax, or dividend withholding tax; International Companies (ICs) are statutorily exempt under Section 128 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222].

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0.00% - Vanuatu imposes no domestic withholding tax on dividends, distributions, or liquidation surplus paid by an International Company.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual fee and return must be filed no later than the incorporation anniversary date; a 30-day grace window applies before penalty accrual; failure for 90 consecutive days triggers strike-off proceedings under Section 117 of the International Companies Act.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Renewal must be completed by 31 December of the preceding year for the following year; new licences must be obtained before commencement of business. A late-renewal window of 30 days exists with penalty; thereafter operation is unlawful.

Deadline: · Frequency:

VAT return and payment must be lodged no later than 30 calendar days after the end of each VAT period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer-pricing, Local File, Master File, or CbC reporting regime exists in Vanuatu, and no VUV revenue threshold is prescribed. The absence of transfer-pricing rules creates residual BEPS risk: foreign tax authorities (e.g., China on the supplier side or the U.S. on the reseller side) may apply their own TP/CbC legislation to the pricing of solar equipment purchases and the zero-tax IC margin.

Economic substance scope: Applicable - the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2020 (No. 33 of 2020), enacted in response to the EU listing and OECD/Global Forum reviews, imposes mandatory substance requirements on ICs carrying on 'relevant activities', including 'Distribution and Service Centre Business' (purchasing portable power units from manufacturers and reselling them to customers), 'Intellectual Property Business' (brand licensing), and 'Financing and Leasing Business' (BNPL instalment receivables). The IC must maintain a registered office and physical presence in Vanuatu, hold board meetings with local strategic management in Vanuatu, employ an adequate number of full-time staff, and incur sufficient local operating expenditure. An annual economic substance notification/report must be filed through the Registered Agent. A zero-presence online retail IC is indistinguishable from a shell company and will fail substance assessment; this is the single most important compliance vulnerability for the structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Misuse of Tax-Exempt IC Status

Statutory sanction: Immediate loss of the Section 128 tax exemption; compulsory conversion to a Vanuatu local company; fine up to VUV 1,000,000 under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] and the Business Licence Act; restitution of forgone VAT and licence fees; deregistration of the IC.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner are personally liable for unpaid VAT, business licence fees, and penalties once the corporate veil is pierced for fraud; criminal prosecution under the Penal Code [Cap 135] for fraud, forgery, and perjury; travel bans and asset-freezing orders are available and routinely used.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Act administrative penalties ranging from VUV 100,000 to VUV 1,000,000; compulsory strike-off; adverse exchange of information with foreign tax authorities (CRS/AEOI); reinclusion of Vanuatu on the EU blacklist, jeopardising the entire jurisdiction's offshore regime.

Director personal liability: Civil fines assessed jointly and severally against every director; personal liability for knowing misrepresentation; disqualification orders prohibiting management of any Vanuatu company for up to 10 years; removal from the VFSC register of approved persons.
AML/CTF and International Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Confiscation of proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002; money-laundering conviction under the Penal Code [Cap 135]: imprisonment up to 20 years and substantial fines; regulatory monetary penalties up to VUV 10,000,000 under the AML/CTF Act [Cap 155]; revocation of the Registered Agent's licence.

Director personal liability: Direct criminal liability for directing, financing, or concealing the offence; international travel bans and INTERPOL red notices; extradition to the requesting state; criminal-asset confiscation extending to personal property and foreign bank accounts.
Fraudulent Misrepresentation to VFSC and False Filings

Statutory sanction: Prosecution for perjury and forgery under the Penal Code [Cap 135]; VFSC cancellation of the IC's registration without a restoration right; monetary penalties up to VUV 1,000,000 against the company and each complicit agent.

Director personal liability: Imprisonment up to 7 years for each officer who signs a knowingly false document; restitution orders for all profits derived from the misrepresentation; prohibition orders barring re-incorporation in Vanuatu; civil liability to creditors and minority shareholders for misrepresentation.
