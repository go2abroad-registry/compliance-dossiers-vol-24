Provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) of the province or centrally administered city where the registered office is located; online submission through the National Business Registration Portal (https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn) for the Enterprise Registration Certificate and through the National Investment Registration Portal for the Investment Registration Certificate.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Viet Nam imposes no statutory minimum charter capital for a foreign-invested LLC engaged in virtual concert hosting and digital ticket tokenization, and no escrow account is required by general company law. The charter capital must, however, be stated in the charter and subscribed in full by each member; under Article 50(3) of the Law on Enterprises, contributed capital must be brought into the company in full within 90 days after issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate unless the charter stipulates a shorter or longer term not exceeding the statutory regime. A foreign investor contributing equity must evidence the contribution through a foreign direct investment capital account opened at a licensed bank in Viet Nam.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-prevention and fire-fighting compliance under Decree No. 136/2020/ND-CP is required for the leased head-office premises; the enterprise shall obtain the competent fire-police department's written approval or a written acknowledgement that the office is covered by the building owner's existing fire-safety certificate. Structural-load inspection applies only if the company constructs or installs heavy equipment, which is not expected for a virtual-event ticketing operation. Under the Labor Code No. 45/2019/QH14 and Decree No. 44/2016/ND-CP, the employer must perform a workplace risk assessment, implement occupational safety measures, and arrange annual safety training for employees. No separate civil-defense clearance is required for a metaverse-based virtual concert without physical spectators or premises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế) under the Ministry of Finance of Viet Nam; e-Tax Portal for tax administration and filing: thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn; National One-Stop Portal: dichvucong.gdt.gov.vn.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested limited liability company is established by registering through the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI), which issues the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC). Under the national integrated business-tax registration procedure (Decree 01/2021/ND-CP), the DPI automatically transmits registration data to the General Department of Taxation, and the Tax Identification Number (TIN) is issued ex officio. A foreign supplier without a permanent establishment in Viet Nam (e.g., a metaverse ticket platform selling to Vietnamese consumers) must register on the Foreign Supplier e-Tax Portal at etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn and obtain a dedicated tax code pursuant to the Law on Tax Administration (No. 38/2019/QH14) and Decree 85/2021/ND-CP.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the LLC is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to Corporate Income Tax (CIT) at the standard 20% rate; no pass-through treatment is available. Territorial scope: resident enterprises are taxed on worldwide income, with double-tax relief for foreign-sourced income; non-resident enterprises are taxed only on Vietnam-source income. Qualifying enterprises located in Export Processing Zones (EPZs), high-tech zones, and economic zones may benefit from Tax_Exempt_Zone incentives, including a preferential CIT rate of 10% for up to 15 years and additional exemption/reduction periods. — A foreign-invested limited liability company is established by registering through the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI), which issues the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the LLC is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to Corporate Income Tax (CIT) at the standard 20% rate; no pass-through treatment is available. Territorial scope: resident enterprises are taxed on worldwide income, with double-tax relief for foreign-sourced income; non-resident enterprises are taxed only on Vietnam-source income. Qualifying enterprises located in Export Processing Zones (EPZs), high-tech zones, and economic zones may benefit from Tax_Exempt_Zone incentives, including a preferential CIT rate of 10% for up to 15 years and additional exemption/reduction periods.

Available election forms

Viet Nam does not provide US-style check-the-box elections or OECD hybrid mismatch elections. Available statutory elections are limited to: (i) selection of straight-line depreciation life within prescribed bands for fixed assets; (ii) election by an eligible taxpayer to apply the most favorable CIT incentive when multiple incentive regimes overlap; and (iii) voluntary VAT registration under the credit-invoice method by enterprises whose revenue is below the compulsory VAT threshold. Hybrid mismatch consequences are not recognized, and no corresponding election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 1,000,000,000 of annual taxable revenue for domestic enterprises and foreign-invested LLCs (mandatory VAT registration); voluntary registration permitted below the threshold. Foreign non-resident suppliers of digital/electronic services (including virtual event ticketing) are mandated to register through the Foreign Supplier Portal when annual Vietnamese-source revenue exceeds VND 100,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to: (i) export goods physically leaving Viet Nam, supported by a customs export declaration; (ii) services provided to foreign organizations or individuals and consumed entirely outside Viet Nam, including virtual performance ticketing supplied to non-resident purchasers where the supplier can evidence the offshore status of the buyer, cross-border payment in foreign currency, and offshore consumption; (iii) goods and services supplied to non-residents or export processing enterprises within EPZs, bonded warehouses, and export-processing zones; and (iv) international transportation services. The taxpayer bears the burden of proof and must retain the full statutory documentation bundle.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic declaration in the VNACCS/VCIS customs system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods (not applicable to pure offshore services)
  • Export sales contract with the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice issued to the foreign counterparty
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or foreign-currency bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of proceeds from abroad
  • Zero-rated e-invoice in the statutory XML format with tax-authority-assigned code

Viet Nam mandates electronic invoicing (e-invoices) in a standardized XML format (Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC). E-invoices must bear a tax-authority-assigned invoice serial number and code, and the invoice data must be transmitted to the National e-Invoice System (hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn) within 24 hours of issuance. There is no physical fiscal stamp or real-time transactional clearance, but the system applies pre-issuance validation for invoice codes and export invoices must be cross-referenced with electronic customs declarations on VNACCS/VCIS.

The tax authorities, together with the Vietnam Customs, cross-validate export declarations, e-invoice data, and foreign-currency remittance records. A zero-rating claim lacking substantive documentation is treated as tax evasion and is subject to clawback of the refunded VAT, late-payment interest of 0.03% per day, and administrative fines of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax amount under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP. Issuance of illegal invoices attracts fines up to VND 20,000,000 per invoice. Tax evasion involving amounts exceeding VND 100,000,000 may trigger criminal prosecution under Article 200 of the Penal Code, with penalties including fines and imprisonment. The inspection/audit look-back period is 5 years, but no time limit applies in cases of fraud or intentional misdeclaration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam maintains a bifurcated convertibility regime: the current account is largely convertible, while the capital account is subject to administrative control. For an ISIC 7110 virtual concert and metaverse ticketing entity, receipts from non-resident customers for digital services, virtual performance licensing and tokenized ticketing constitute current-account service transactions and may be freely credited in foreign currency, provided the underlying commercial documentation (service contract, e-invoice or settlement report from the payment platform, and supporting narrative of service delivery) is presented to the licensed servicing bank. Mandatory capital-account controls apply to (i) equity capital contributions, which must enter through the Foreign Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA / tai khoan von dau tu truc tiep) registered with the SBV via the servicing credit institution; and (ii) profit remittances abroad, which are permitted on an annual or six-monthly basis only after the statutory audit is completed, the corporate income tax (CIT) finalization is filed and no CIT, VAT or social insurance liabilities remain outstanding; the remittance is made through the DICA using balance-of-payments (BOP) code 540030 (repatriation of profit from current-year or retained earnings). Full divestment and residual capital repatriation require SBV-approved liquidation and a Capital Account Settlement Certificate. Profit and service proceeds are freely convertible into foreign currency at the authorized bank's quoted rate, but cryptocurrency, including USDC, is not recognized as a lawful payment instrument or a foreign-exchange conversion channel; the SBV has confirmed under guidance (Official Letter 5747/NHNN-PC and Decree 101/2012/ND-CP as amended by Decree 80/2016/ND-CP) that digital assets are neither legal tender nor accepted settlement currency. Accordingly, USDC-denominated revenue must be liquidated into USD offshore and then remitted to Vietnam via SWIFT, or converted onshore only after an offshore conversion step, in order to preserve lawful convertibility and avoid an unlicensed 'de facto' FX business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular No. 02/2021/TT-NHNN as amended by Circular No. 10/2022/TT-NHNN, 100% of export proceeds earned by a foreign-invested LLC from the supply of cross-border services (including virtual performance hosting, metaverse ticketing and digital content licensing) must be repatriated to a licensed bank in Vietnam through a foreign currency account (tai khoan ngoai te / FCD) within 90 calendar days of the invoice date or the date of service completion, whichever is earlier. There is no mandatory conversion ratio in force today; the mandatory surrender requirement (historically up to 80%) was abolished, and the entity may freely retain the foreign currency, deposit it, convert it to VND at the SBV-referenced exchange rate band, or use it for lawful authorized payments. The servicing bank must be provided with a transaction schedule and underlying service settlement evidence, and the repatriation obligation extends to all proceeds received through foreign-payment intermediaries (e.g., Stripe or offshore EMI accounts), requiring the entity to instruct such platforms to sweep net settlement balances to the onshore FCD account within the statutory window. Non-compliance attracts administrative sanctions under Decree No. 88/2019/ND-CP, including fines up to VND 300 million for late or non-repatriation, temporary suspension of foreign-exchange activity, and mandatory rectification, with aggravating factors applied where crypto settlement rails are used to bypass the FCD process.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV / Ngan hang Nha nuoc Viet Nam) acting through the Foreign Exchange Administration Department (Vu Quan ly ngoai hoi), exercising authority under the Law on the State Bank of Vietnam No. 46/2010/QH12, the Law on Credit Institutions No. 47/2010/QH13 (as amended), the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange No. 28/2005/PL-UBTVQH11 (as amended by Law No. 04/2022/QH15), and implementing instruments including Decree No. 70/2014/ND-CP and Circulars No. 02/2021/TT-NHNN, 05/2021/TT-NHNN and 10/2022/TT-NHNN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM-LOW for direct offshore digital EMI onboarding; HIGH for bank-partnered Banking-as-a-Service (BaaS) rails. Vietnam does not recognize an offshore digital EMI licence (e.g., FCA-EMI, MAS-Major Payment Institution, Lithuania-EMI) as an authorized payment institution. Under Decree 101/2012/ND-CP (as amended by Decree 80/2016/ND-CP), only SBV-licensed non-bank payment intermediaries (intermediary payment services, to chuc trung gian thanh toan) and licensed credit institutions may legally execute payment transactions for residents. An offshore EMI may legally maintain a treasury account for the Vietnamese LLC only as a foreign intermediary; it cannot open an onshore settlement account or execute VND clearing. Stripe, as an unlicensed foreign acquirer, may onboard the LLC through its Singapore entity for global card acquittance, but the net USD settlement must be wired by MT103 to the onshore FCD account within the 90-day repatriation window; Vietnamese banking compliance will treat the Stripe and Coinbase Commerce settlement balances as the primary source of export proceeds and will demand the corresponding settlement reports. USDC treasury management is legally permissible only if held offshore (in the name of a foreign affiliate) and is not recognized as foreign currency for SBV purposes; direct onshore conversion of USDC into VND is prohibited as an unlicensed foreign-exchange activity and will be treated as a payment-systems violation. Recommended structure: (i) offshore treasury vehicle holds USDC and converts to USD via regulated exchanges (Coinbase, Kraken) after each periodic event; (ii) USD swept SWIFT to onshore Vietcombank FCD; (iii) VND conversion executed onshore at spot. This preserves full legal viability and avoids SBV enforcement action.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VND 30,000,000,000 (₫30 billion) in annual related-party transaction value triggers the mandatory Local File; the Master File is required where the ultimate parent group's global consolidated revenue equals or exceeds VND 2,000,000,000,000 (₫2 trillion), or where the Vietnamese taxpayer is the ultimate parent at that revenue level; the Country-by-Country Report applies at global group consolidated revenue of VND 20,000,000,000,000 (₫20 trillion). Taxpayers with total revenue below VND 50 billion and related-party transaction value below VND 30 billion are exempt from preparing Local and Master Files provided their related-party transactions do not produce losses (Decree 132/2020/ND-CP).

Economic substance scope: Vietnam does not operate a UAE-style stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime, but it vigorously enforces a substantive anti-avoidance and substance-over-form doctrine under the Tax Administration Law 38/2019/QH14 and Decree 132/2020. Transactions with shell, zero-employment, or conduit offshore counterparties are disregarded and re-characterized; preferential CIT rates are revoked retroactively where the qualifying project lacks real personnel, premises, equipment, and business activity in Vietnam; interest deductions are capped at 30% of EBITDA; and DTA benefits are denied where the recipient enterprise lacks beneficial substance. The virtual concert LLC must maintain verifiable Vietnamese business location, local employees, a real bank account, and a documented economic chain for ticket tokenization to resist anti-abuse challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaration of Virtual Ticket Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Law 38/2019/QH14 and Decree 125/2020/ND-CP: administrative fine of 1-3 times the evaded tax, confiscation of the tax shortfall, and late payment interest at 0.03%/day. Where the evaded amount exceeds VND 100 million, Penal Code 100/2015 (Article 200) applies: corporate/personal fines from VND 100 million to VND 4.5 billion and/or imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years; additional sanctions include forfeiture of criminal proceeds and suspension of business activities for up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: The General Director, legal representative, CFO, and responsible tax agent face personal criminal indictment under Article 200 Penal Code, travel bans prohibiting exit from Vietnam until the tax debt is settled, seizure of personal bank accounts and shareholdings, and joint and several civil restitution for the state's fiscal loss; the corporate veil is pierced upon proof of intentional fraud or illegal conduct, making the individual promoters personally liable without benefit of limited liability.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Insufficiency

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance re-characterization by the Vietnamese tax authority: retroactive additional CIT at 20% for the entire incentive period, disallowance of all intercompany deductions, reclassification of disguised payments as dividends, a 20% under-declaration fine, daily late interest, and revocation of the investment incentive certificate; persistent dormancy may trigger forced dissolution under the Enterprise Law 59/2020/QH14 with the entire unpaid tax accelerated.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who certified false substance statements in investment registration and incentive applications are personally jointly liable for the recovered tax shortfall; personal administrative fines of VND 100-200 million apply for false declarations; travel bans are imposed until all retrospective CIT and interest are discharged; foreign parent directors who directed the fictional structure face Vietnamese criminal fraud exposure and asset attachment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP and Decree 125/2020/ND-CP: administrative fine up to VND 25 million per missing TP form; the tax authority may re-determine taxable income at a deemed arm's-length price, assess additional CIT, impose a 20% under-declaration penalty, and charge 0.03% daily late interest; where the adjustment exposes deliberate concealment exceeding VND 100 million, criminal liability under Penal Code Article 200 applies with fines up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, TP officers, and legal representatives who sign false TP documentation or approve intercompany pricing without supporting analyses are personally liable for the resulting tax adjustment; criminal indictment is available against individuals who orchestrated the mispricing; travel bans and exit suspension apply to directors with outstanding TP-related tax debt; the foreign parent's officers can be engaged extra-territorially where the pricing instructions originated overseas.
Anti-Money Laundering / Illegal Crypto Payment Rails (USDC)

Statutory sanction: Under Law 14/2022/QH15 (AML Law effective 1 March 2023) and Decree 87/2019/ND-CP: administrative fines up to VND 3 billion for institutional violations, suspension of ticketing operations, and forced remediation; utilization of virtual assets as a disguised payment instrument violates Decree 101/2012/ND-CP and Circular 23/2019 with fines up to VND 100-200 million; if the USDC flows are linked to money laundering, Penal Code Article 324 imposes imprisonment from 6 to 15 years and confiscation of property.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and AML compliance officer are personally fined and may be banned from holding managerial offices for failure to implement KYC and suspicious-transaction reporting; directors who knowingly directed the acceptance of USDC payments for ticket tokenization face criminal money-laundering prosecution, personal asset confiscation, and an unconditional exit ban; a travel ban is automatically applied during investigation and until full legal clearance.
Unlawful Profit Distribution / Premature Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Law 59/2020/QH14 Article 135: the LLC must claw back the unlawfully distributed amount; directors are obligated to restore the paid-in capital; the tax authority may re-classify the remittance as a constructive dividend, impose 5% personal income tax (if paid to individuals), or treat it as a taxable capital reduction; where the under-distributed profit trace to under-declared revenue, the evasion penalties of 1-3 times the tax plus 0.03% daily interest and criminal liability under Penal Code Article 200 cumulatively apply.
