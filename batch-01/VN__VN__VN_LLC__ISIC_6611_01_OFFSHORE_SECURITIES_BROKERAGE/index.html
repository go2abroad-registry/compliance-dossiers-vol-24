Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (GDT) of the Ministry of Finance, Socialist Republic of Viet Nam. Electronic filing and registration portal: etax.gdt.gov.vn; national electronic invoice database: hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn; export/customs electronic declaration system: edx.customs.gov.vn. Provincial-level tax departments (provincial Tax Departments) administer the local taxpayer files, acceptance of registrations, and field audits.

E-government filing portal

A 100% foreign-invested LLC must first obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) and an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC), issued by the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) or the authorized industrial/export-processing zone authority. Tax registration is integrated with enterprise registration: after ERC issuance, the national business registration database transmits the data to the GDT, which automatically issues a 13-digit tax identification number (TIN) (official name in Vietnamese: Ma so thue) within 3 working days. The TIN is simultaneously the VAT identification number for the LLC. If automatic issuance is not completed, the taxpayer files tax registration form 01-DK-TCT with the managing provincial tax office. For a securities brokerage, the State Securities Commission of Vietnam must additionally issue the securities business license under the Securities Law 54/2019/QH14 before operational services commence; TIN issuance nonetheless begins from the ERC date such that corporate income tax and VAT filing obligations accrue.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a Vietnamese LLC is a resident taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (CIT) at the standard rate of 20% on worldwide income. There is no pass-through or partnership tax-transparent regime at federal/equivalent level; profits are taxed at the LLC level and dividends distributed to the foreign shareholder are not subject to additional dividend withholding tax in Viet Nam. The LLC is not located in a Tax_Exempt_Zone by default, but may qualify for CIT incentives (10% or 15% rates) if operating in eligible high-tech, software, or large-scale investment sectors and if licensed as an export-processing enterprise respectively. — A 100% foreign-invested LLC must first obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) and an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC), issued by the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) or the authorized industrial/export-processing zone authority. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Vietnamese LLC is a resident taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (CIT) at the standard rate of 20% on worldwide income. There is no pass-through or partnership tax-transparent regime at federal/equivalent level; profits are taxed at the LLC level and dividends distributed to the foreign shareholder are not subject to additional dividend withholding tax in Viet Nam. The LLC is not located in a Tax_Exempt_Zone by default, but may qualify for CIT incentives (10% or 15% rates) if operating in eligible high-tech, software, or large-scale investment sectors and if licensed as an export-processing enterprise respectively.

Available election forms

Viet Nam does not grant any check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election; the LLC's entity-level tax classification is statutory, not elective. Available tax elections and administrative mechanisms include: (i) applying for preferential CIT rates (10%, 15% or 20%) under new-investment and high-tech incentive criteria; (ii) electing simplified VAT declaration or submission of consolidated invoice data under the electronic invoice regime; (iii) applying for an advance pricing arrangement (APA) under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP for transfer pricing stabilization; (iv) electing the export-processing enterprise (EPE) status which carries VAT and customs duty privileges; and (v) choosing to be treated as a small and medium enterprise for reduced administrative formalities. No election can convert the LLC into a pass-through vehicle for Vietnamese tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 1,000,000,000 (one billion Vietnamese dong) of annual revenue for mandatory VAT registration by business households and individuals; an LLC is always required to register for and declare VAT without regard to turnover because VAT registration is mandatory upon enterprise registration. Securities brokerage services are statutory VAT-exempt supplies, so the offshore securities brokerage will not charge output VAT at the 10% rate and is generally not entitled to reclaim input VAT attributable to exempt supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Law on VAT and Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC (Article 9), the 0% VAT rate applies to goods physically exported from Viet Nam or sold into non-tariff zones (export-processing zones, bonded warehouses) and to services supplied to foreign organizations/individuals where the services are consumed entirely outside Viet Nam, including international transport. Eligibility requires a documentary chain evidencing the export, delivery abroad, and payment received from abroad in convertible foreign currency. However, Vietnamese securities business services, including equities brokerage, derivatives brokerage, and related security intermediary services, are statutory VAT-exempt items under Article 5(4) of the Law on VAT and therefore cannot receive 0% VAT treatment; the offshore securities brokerage's commission income is VAT-exempt, not zero-rated, and input VAT attributable to these exempt supplies is blocked. Ancillary non-exempt services (for example market data licensing or bespoke cross-border research) may be eligible for 0% if the contract, offshore consumption, and inbound payment documentation meet all conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Export sales contract, commercial invoice, and packing list
  • Bank SWIFT credit advice or bank statement showing receipt of convertible foreign currency in the LLC's Vietnamese account
  • For offshore services: cross-border service agreement, compliant e-invoice, evidence of offshore consumption (client acceptance or service completion certificate), and banking proof of payment from the foreign client

Mandatory nationwide e-invoicing has applied since 1 July 2022 under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. Every LLC must issue e-invoices in standardized XML format, affix a qualified electronic signature, and transmit invoice data to the national electronic invoice portal (hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn) through certified e-invoice service providers. The GDT performs automated business-rule validation, QR-code/2D-code generation, digital stamping, and risk-based real-time fiscal clearance for high-risk refund claimants. E-invoices without a tax authority code are permitted only after successful registration of the invoice form with the tax authority; all export zero-rated or VAT-exempt services must nevertheless be documented on compliant e-invoices matching the customs and banking records.

Viet Nam applies risk-based VAT refund screening by the GDT; export zero-rating and refund claims are priority audit targets, particularly for high-risk financial-sector taxpayers. Failure to satisfy the substantive and documentation conditions exposes the LLC to reversal of the 0% rate and imposition of output VAT at 10%, disallowance of input VAT credits, reassessment of refunded amounts with late-payment interest at 0.02% per day, and administrative fines under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP ranging from 10% to 100% of the underdeclared tax. Fraudulent refund claims, false e-invoicing, or fabricated export documentation may constitute tax evasion under Penal Code 2015 Article 200 and invoicing fraud under Article 202, carrying substantial fines, imprisonment, and confiscation of assets; systemic violations additionally expose the licensed brokerage to State Securities Commission sanctions including license suspension or revocation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Viet Nam maintains a managed convertibility regime. The Vietnamese Dong (VND) is not freely convertible offshore. Current account transactions are substantially convertible through SBV-licensed authorized banks, while capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment equity contributions, shareholder loans, and profit remittances, are generally subject to registration, licensing, or post-verification by the SBV and the relevant ministry. For the VN_LLC offshore securities brokerage, inbound management fees, brokerage commissions, and client settlements are treated as service exports or current account receipts, but every receipt must be repatriated into a licensed foreign currency account at an SBV-authorized bank before any further disposition. Dividend and profit remittance is permitted only after annually audited financial statements, corporate income tax finalization, and full satisfaction of all statutory tax and social obligations. The entity must maintain a valid Investment Registration Certificate and Enterprise Registration Certificate reflecting the securities brokerage activity, and, if it creates a local presence for retail investors, a licence from the Ministry of Finance/State Securities Commission under the Law on Securities; without such a licence, the entity may only conduct cross-border brokerage that does not solicit Vietnamese retail investors through prohibited distribution channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN and the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange, export proceeds arising from goods and services must be repatriated to a foreign currency account at an SBV-authorized licensed bank within 180 days from the date of completing customs clearance for goods, or from the date the service is performed and invoiced for services. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; the entity may retain 100% of qualifying export proceeds in a foreign currency account. Conversion into VND is made voluntarily at the authorized bank's published buying rate when the entity needs VND liquidity. For the offshore securities brokerage, the entire fee, commission, rebate, or other service consideration must be credited to a local foreign currency account within the statutory 180-day window; no netting or offshore retention is permitted unless the transaction qualifies for an exemption issued by the SBV.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as a standalone banking or client-money holding solution. Offshore digital EMI platforms and virtual banking-as-a-service providers (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, or similar unlicensed EMIs) are not SBV-licensed credit institutions and cannot lawfully hold client funds, issue VND IBANs, or participate in Vietnamese domestic clearing. Their viable role is limited to: (i) an offshore multicurrency receiving or settlement application-layer for non-VND transactions; or (ii) a technology/API middleware that connects the licensed local bank to the brokerage's trade platform. All domestic VND flows, sa, and segregated client money movements must be executed through an SBV-authorized bank. An unlicensed EMI may never be used as a liquidity mirror, an escrow holder for retail clients, or a substitute for a securities company's segregated client account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct investment equity or subordinated capital must be subscribed under the Investment Registration Certificate and Enterprise Registration Certificate and wired by SWIFT MT103 into the bank's dedicated FDI Capital Escrow Account. The remitter must use the SBV-prescribed transaction purpose code for direct investment capital contribution and include the IRC or ERC reference number in the SWIFT field 70 remittance information. The receiving bank validates that the contribution is made within the statutory capital contribution period of 90 days unless extended, records the capital inflow on the SBV monitoring system, and issues a receipt confirming the foreign currency converted into VND if required. Offshore shareholder loans over VND 1 million or equivalent must be registered with the SBV as an enterprise foreign borrowing prior to inflow; loans that are unregistered may not be remitted or may be converted into equity only after SBV enforcement action.Outbound trade settlements and operational disbursements must be executed through SWIFT-cleared rails. For routine cross-border fees, commissions, rebates, and trade settlements involving the offshore brokerage, use SWIFT MT103 for single client-facing transactions and MT202COV for interbank cover payments when the beneficiary account is held at a different institution. Any settlement related to equities, CFDs, or exchange-traded instruments should use a corresponding trade reference code and be matched against the prime broker or execution venue's contract notes. Commercial letters of credit are not applicable to the core brokerage activity except where physically settled commodities or documentary trade underlyings are part of a licensed derivatives portfolio; for such goods-based trades, Issuing Bank LCs under UCP600 are permitted. Domestic VND clearing may be routed through NAPAS instant or batch clearing only for local operating expenses; all client money disbursements to overseas retail clients must use SWIFT MT103 and be covered by the segregated client account ledger. Card funding via Visa/Mastercard is permissible only through an SBV-licensed merchant acquirer, with card-settlement proceeds swept into the segregated client account no later than the next business day and then onward transmitted by wire rails under the brokerage's treasury policy.FX forwards with SBV-licensed authorized banks on documented underlying current-account exposure, including service fee receipts and future dividend/profit remittances, pursuant to Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN and Circular 26/2018/TT-NHNN.FX swaps (spot against forward) with licensed local credit institutions for temporary VND/USD liquidity management and repricing of operational cash flows.Cross-currency swaps or cross-currency interest-rate swaps with licensed banks for long-term foreign-currency borrowings, provided the underlying borrowings are registered with the SBV and the hedge does not create an uncovered speculative position.FX options purchased from SBV-licensed credit institutions, including call or put options, when used to hedge a defined and documented economic exposure arising from actual or anticipated current account or capital account transactions.Combination hedging structures such as collars and ratio forwards, but only where the licensed local bank documents the underlying exposure and the hedge tenor does not exceed the underlying transaction liquidity cycle.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a standalone banking or client-money holding solution. Offshore digital EMI platforms and virtual banking-as-a-service providers (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, or similar unlicensed EMIs) are not SBV-licensed credit institutions and cannot lawfully hold client funds, issue VND IBANs, or participate in Vietnamese domestic clearing. Their viable role is limited to: (i) an offshore multicurrency receiving or settlement application-layer for non-VND transactions; or (ii) a technology/API middleware that connects the licensed local bank to the brokerage's trade platform. All domestic VND flows, sa, and segregated client money movements must be executed through an SBV-authorized bank. An unlicensed EMI may never be used as a liquidity mirror, an escrow holder for retail clients, or a substitute for a securities company's segregated client account.

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Vietnam does not impose a separate dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a foreign-invested LLC to its corporate shareholders. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, a taxpayer must prepare and maintain Local File and Master File if: (1) related-party transaction revenue exceeds VND 30 billion per annum; or (2) total revenue from all activities exceeds VND 50 billion; or (3) total assets exceed VND 100 billion; or (4) the taxpayer is a related party in a group with ultimate parent revenue exceeding VND 100 billion (for Master File). The Country-by-Country Report is required if the ultimate parent group's consolidated global revenue is VND 20 trillion or more. Even if below thresholds, the taxpayer must still complete the related-party transaction declaration in the CIT finalization return, and the tax authority may adjust prices.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam applies anti-abuse and economic substance principles primarily through the Law on Tax Administration No. 38/2019/QH14 (Article 7) and Decree 132/2020/ND-CP (GAAR). For foreign-invested enterprises, documentation must prove that the Vietnamese LLC has real business substance, including employees, premises, business records, and actual performance of the offshore securities brokerage activity. The Vietnamese LLC cannot be used as a shell booking entity. The State Securities Commission must issue a securities brokerage license, and the entity must meet capital and operational substance requirements under the Law on Securities 2019. A letter-box entity without substance in Vietnam will have its income adjusted to a deemed taxable amount, and its transfer pricing documentation will be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Trốn Thuế)

Statutory sanction: Under Law on Tax Administration 38/2019/QH14 and Penal Code 100/2015/QH13 (as amended), tax evasion is punishable by a fine of 1.5 to 3 times the evaded tax amount, plus late interest. Criminal liability: imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine up to VND 1 billion for evading tax of VND 100 million or more; up to 7 years for larger amounts; prohibited from holding certain offices for 1–3 years.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives, directors, and managers who knowingly order or approve false returns are personally liable. Courts may impose travel bans, passport revocation, and restriction on leaving Viet Nam. Directors face criminal imprisonment under Article 200 of the Penal Code. Civil restitution to the state from personal assets is enforced if the corporate entity lacks funds (piercing the corporate veil).
Shell Company Artificiality / Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may apply general anti-avoidance under Decree 132/2020 by disregarding the entity or transaction, re-characterizing income, imposing deemed profits, and charging additional CIT at 20% plus late interest and penalties. The relevant entity may lose access to tax incentives. The securities license can be revoked by the State Securities Commission. Criminal liability for fraud under Article 174 may arise if used to defraud investors or tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for restitution of taxes evaded, monetary penalties up to VND 1 billion, and imprisonment under tax evasion or fraud charges. Travel bans and asset freezes are used to secure collection; Taiwanese-style shell splits are not tolerated.
Tax Loss Carryforward Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority re-adjusts taxable income and rejects the loss carryforward. Penalties under Article 14 of Decree 125/2020 for tax return inaccuracies: up to VND 20 million if no underpayment, or up to VND 50 million for serious errors; if underpayment arises, penalty 20% of unpaid tax plus late interest at 0.03% per day and criminal prosecution for evasion if intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fabricated loss schedules are jointly liable for underpaid CIT, interest, and penalties. Fraudulent tax loss certification is a criminal act subject to travel bans and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Unjustified Offshore Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Decree 132/2020 and Law on Tax Administration, tax authority may reassess profits and impose additional CIT with a penalty of up to 20% of underpaid tax plus late interest (0.03%/day). Refusal to provide documentation shifts the burden of proof; tax authority may apply deemed pricing. Administrative fines up to VND 75 million can be imposed under Decree 125. Under repeated or aggravated circumstances, criminal tax evasion liability applies.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers who approve non-arm’s length cross-border payments are personally exposed for tax collection deficiencies. In egregious cases, travel bans, bank account freezes, and direct garnishment of personal assets. Criminal prosecution under Article 202 of the Penal Code (violation of tax regulations) may be pursued.
Foreign Contractor Withholding Tax (FCT) Remittance Failure

Statutory sanction: If not withheld/remitted, the LLC is liable for the unpaid FCT amount, plus late interest, and is denied deduction for such expenses. Administrative fines equal 100% of unpaid withholding taxes if disclosed too late, or up to 2-4 times if concealed. Criminal tax evasion penalties may apply where the failure is deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who knowingly disburse foreign payments without FCT withholding are personally liable for the underpaid tax. Travel restrictions are routinely used to compel payment; imprisonment up to 7 years for deliberate evasion.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Decree on AML administrative sanctions, fines can reach VND 2 billion for serious violations; repeat violations can require suspension of operations or revocation of license. For criminal money laundering (Article 251 Penal Code), imprisonment up to 15 years, significant corporate fines, and asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally fined (VND 100-500 million), imprisoned, or subject to travel bans and professional bans. Inadequate AML oversight is sufficient for personal prosecution; the corporate veil does not protect gross negligent directors.
