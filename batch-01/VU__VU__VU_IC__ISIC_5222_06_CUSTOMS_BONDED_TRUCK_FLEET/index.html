Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR) – https://www.customs.gov.vu; Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) – https://www.vfsc.vu

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned International Company (IC) is incorporated with VFSC under the International Companies Act Cap 222, obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation and a Tax Exemption Certificate confirming zero income tax. No domestic tax TIN is required because the IC is tax-exempt. For customs operations, the entity must apply separately to DCIR for a Customs Business Code (CBC) and a bonded transporter/warehouse licence under the Customs Act Cap 42; this application requires the certificate of incorporation, board resolution, identity documents of officers, and proof of a registered local agent/physical address in Vanuatu.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None applicable. Vanuatu levies no national income tax, and ICs are automatically exempt without any check-the-box election. No hybrid mismatch or flow-through election exists under Vanuatu law. The only optional elections relate to customs duty treatment (e.g., temporary importation, bonded transit) for which a bond or security must be lodged with DCIR.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST regime in force; indirect taxation is limited to customs import duties and stamp duty under the Customs Act Cap 42 and the Stamp Duties Act Cap 117.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – Vanuatu has no VAT/GST and therefore no turnover threshold. A Customs Business Code (CBC) is mandatory for any bonded or transit cargo operation irrespective of revenue or transaction volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

N/A because Vanuatu does not levy VAT/GST. No zero-rating claim exists. For customs duty purposes, goods transported by bonded truck feeder services that are exported directly from Vanuatu are free of export duties provided the export declaration is lodged and the goods are shown to have left Vanuatu permanently.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) lodged in ASYCUDA World
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or CMR for road feeder movement)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bonded Transport Authorization / Transshipment Permit issued by DCIR
  • Continuous Customs Bond or Bank Security covering the bonded truck fleet

None. Vanuatu has no national e-invoicing or fiscal clearance platform. Customs declarations are lodged electronically through the ASYCUDA World system or on approved paper forms; there is no digital stamping or real-time tax clearance API requirement.

Vanuatu has no exchange control and imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline. However, DCIR may audit bonded trucking operators intensively; false export declarations, misdescription of destinations, or diversion of bonded cargo into the local market attract penalties of up to 10–20 times the duty evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, forfeiture of the bonded fleet, and suspension or cancellation of the customs transporter licence. VFSC may also revoke the IC's tax exemption certificate for serious customs offences, resulting in retrospective loss of exemption status and disqualification of directors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates as an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction with no formal exchange controls on current payments or capital transfers. The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is fully convertible for trade-related receipts, dividends, loan repayments and capital returns. Profit remittance hurdles are not statutory, but each bank applies AML/CFT-source-of-funds due diligence before releasing outward payments. For a Vanuatu International Company, income and capital distributions are tax-exempt, and no Vanuatu withholding tax is imposed on outbound dividends, interest, royalties or service fees; the practical constraint is documentary verification and sanctions screening by the correspondent banking chain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio apply in Vanuatu. Exporters and customs bonded operators may retain foreign currency earnings in external accounts or sell them into VUV at the free interbank market rate. The receiving bank must still record inward remittances for RBV statistical purposes and, for customs bonded aviation trucking, the operator should retain transport documents, customs receipts and airport cargo manifests to evidence the trade payment.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act 1980, with full responsibility for monetary policy, the issue of the Vanuatu vatu, exchange rate administration, and banking supervision under the Banking Act 2018 and the Financial Institutions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail. Vanuatu does not maintain a separate electronic money institution licensing category, and any deposit-taking institution must hold a banking license from RBV under the Banking Act 2018. An offshore digital EMI cannot open a resident operating account unless it becomes a licensed Vanuatu branch or negotiates a correspondent banking relationship with an ANZ/BSP-type institution. Such an arrangement adds intermediary risk, restricted account segmentation, and is structurally unsuitable for capital escrow or customs bonded payment flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in Vanuatu — the IC law does not prescribe Local File or Master File thresholds. For the customs bonded truck fleet, no VAT or CIT return therefore no transfer-pricing filing. Nevertheless, a VFSC substance declaration may require controlled transaction data when the annual foreign-related turnover exceeds VUV 30,000,000 (administrative guidance; not a tax obligation).

Economic substance scope: The Vanuatu statutory regime (International Companies Act and subsidiary regulations and the Beneficial Ownership Act 2019) applies economic substance as a precondition of the IC exemption. The IC must, at all times, maintain a registered office, a licensed agent and records. For an ISIC 5222 bonded truck fleet, the VFSC and the Customs Commissioner expect: a physical fleet management office in Vanuatu; at least one director or senior manager actually managing the feeder operation; GPS-linked fleet data and customs-bonded evidence; and local employees/expenditure sufficient to demonstrate that the profitable freight activity is not mere offshore booking. Failure to satisfy this doctrine when a licence is required leads to cancellation and retroactive taxation through a different entity form.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Illicit use of zero-tax IC to evade customs duty on bonded vehicles and freight.

Statutory sanction: Under the Vanuatu Customs Act, the Customs Commissioner recovers 200% of the assessed duty as a penalty, seizes the truck, bond, and fuel, cancels the Customs Bonded Carrier Licence and imposes an additional VUV one million administrative fine per violation. The Vanuatu tax and customs authorities are also entitled to treat the IC as a local carrier for the whole fiscal year, removing its zero-tax status.

Director personal liability: Where directors instruct routing of duty-exempt bonded cargo into domestic circulation or allow GPS seal tampering, the corporate veil is pierced: directors are jointly liable for the unpaid duties and penalties, may be prohibited from leaving Vanuatu by court order pending payment, and face imprisonment not exceeding five years for deliberate customs fraud.
Shell company / non-operating International Company.

Statutory sanction: The VFSC can strike the IC off the registry, declare its corporate existence void with retroactive effect, and impose a fine of VUV 2,000,000 against the company or its authorised officer for obtaining VFSC registration through materially false asset/substance representations. If the IC carries on business in Vanuatu through a domestic depot without a transport licence, the Ministry of Infrastructure will revoke operating permits.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed a substance declaration that overstated fleet ownership or employment are subject to disqualification as directors for a period of five years, personally liable for all wages owed, charges, and fines of up to VUV 500,000 incurred during the period of illegality, and can be sentenced to up to two years' imprisonment under the International Companies Act offence provisions.
AML/CFT failure for beneficial ownership and licensed financial transport operations.

Statutory sanction: Under Vanuatu's AML/CFT Act (as amended), the financial/corporate compliance supervisor issues a penalty of up to VUV 10,000,000 for a first offence and suspends the VFSC licence for a bonded transport operator when suspicious transaction reporting is omitted.

Director personal liability: Directors with actual or constructive knowledge of the concealed beneficial ownership face personal fines up to VUV 2,000,000, a freeze of their own bank accounts, and imprisonment for seven years for money laundering facilitation; in addition, they may remain personally liable for the entire funds flowing through the bonded trucking contracts as restitution to the government.
False annual return / false certification to VFSC.

Statutory sanction: Providing false documents or information under the International Companies Act is a criminal offence: fine of up to VUV 5,000,000, disqualification from participation in any Vanuatu licensed entity for ten years, and immediate revocation of all customs transport permits.

Director personal liability: Every director who approved the false return is vicariously liable for VUV 1,000,000 in restitution to the VFSC; in egregious cases, a court may impose a travel ban and order the director's detention for contempt until the false filing is corrected. This cannot be the subject of company-wide indemnity. The judicial consequence may be imprisonment not exceeding fifteen years under general fraud provisions if the false return is a predicate for obtaining money or goods by deception.
