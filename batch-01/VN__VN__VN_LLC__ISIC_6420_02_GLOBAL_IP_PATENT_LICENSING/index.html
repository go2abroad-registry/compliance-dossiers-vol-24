Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Viet Nam imposes no general statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC, and ISIC 6420 IP holding and patent sub-licensing is not a conditional business line under the Law on Investment 2020 or Decree No. 31/2021/ND-CP. The LLC must declare its charter capital in the Company Charter and must pay in 100% of the subscribed charter capital within 90 days after the Enterprise Registration Certificate is issued, as required by Article 47 of the Law on Enterprises. For a foreign-invested entity, the capital contribution schedule is recorded in the Investment Registration Certificate and enforced at company incorporation. Vietnamese law does not require an escrow account; the capital contribution is paid in cash into an ordinary corporate bank account or into the direct investment capital account (DICA) maintained at a commercial bank licensed by the State Bank of Viet Nam.

Funding & unlock path: The lessor/building owner of the registered office must hold a valid fire safety design approval and fire safety acceptance certificate under Decree No. 136/2020/ND-CP and the Law on Fire Prevention and Rescue and Prevention of Fire-Related Costs shall be inspected upon practical use by the Fire Prevention and Rescue Police of the provincial Public Security. The Company must carry out occupational risk assessment, maintain first aid equipment, and comply with the Law on Occupational Safety and Health No. 84/2015/QH13; the lessor must provide evidence that the building meets structural load standards (TCVN). Under Viet Nam's civil defense obligations, the Company is required to cooperate with the local People's Committee and the competent police authority on emergency and civil defense notification plans; however, no separate civil defense license is issued to private enterprises.

Paid-in capital rule: Viet Nam imposes no general statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC, and ISIC 6420 IP holding and patent sub-licensing is not a conditional business line under the Law on Investment 2020 or Decree No. 31/2021/ND-CP. The LLC must declare its charter capital in the Company Charter and must pay in 100% of the subscribed charter capital within 90 days after the Enterprise Registration Certificate is issued, as required by Article 47 of the Law on Enterprises. For a foreign-invested entity, the capital contribution schedule is recorded in the Investment Registration Certificate and enforced at company incorporation. Vietnamese law does not require an escrow account; the capital contribution is paid in cash into an ordinary corporate bank account or into the direct investment capital account (DICA) maintained at a commercial bank licensed by the State Bank of Viet Nam.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế) under the Ministry of Finance of Viet Nam; national e-tax portal: https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn; national e-invoice portal: https://hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested LLC must first obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) where required and an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment; upon ERC issuance the tax authority automatically grants a 13-digit Tax Identification Number (Mã số thuế). The company must then complete VAT registration, register for mandatory e-invoicing, and file electronic tax declarations through the national e-tax portal. A foreign contractor without a permanent establishment must procure a tax code by submitting Form 01/DNT-XN (foreign contractor tax registration) on the e-tax portal before executing any royalty or licensing transaction in or with Vietnam.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident Vietnamese LLC is taxed as a separate juridical person on worldwide income at the standard 20% CIT rate; no pass-through or partnership flow-through treatment exists. Territorial scope: worldwide income for tax-resident enterprises. Base regime is not a tax-exempt zone; export processing zones, industrial parks, and high-tech parks merely confer CIT and VAT incentive regimes within the same entity-level taxation framework.

Available election forms

Viet Nam has no check-the-box election and does not recognize hybrid entity classification elections. Available statutory elections include: (i) the Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) deemed taxation method versus the actual computation method under Circular 103/2014/TT-BTC for non-resident licensors and service providers; (ii) election of CIT incentive regimes for qualifying high-tech, supporting-industry, export-processing and large-scale investment projects; (iii) related-party interest limitation and transfer-pricing elections under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, including the 30% EBITDA interest deduction cap; treaty relief is obtained through Vietnamese tax treaty adjustment procedures rather than hybrid mismatch elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 100,000,000 in annual taxable turnover; this threshold principally exempts small households and individual business households. LLCs and foreign-invested enterprises are mandatorily required to register for VAT upon issuance of the ERC regardless of projected turnover. Foreign contractors without a local entity must complete FCT registration before supplying licensable transactions or services in Vietnam.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to exported goods and exported services, including services consumed outside Vietnam, where: (i) an export contract with a foreign customer is duly signed; (ii) goods physically pass customs on a verified export declaration, or services are documented as supplied to a non-resident and consumed entirely outside Vietnamese territory; (iii) payment is received from abroad in freely convertible foreign currency, supported by an inward remittance certificate or foreign-currency bank statement; and (iv) compliant e-invoices and underlying transactional and shipping/licensing dossiers are retained. Patent and trademark sub-licenses granted to foreign licensees may qualify as exported services if the licensed intellectual property is exploited entirely outside Vietnam and the transaction creates no local consumption or nexus. Certified technology transfers may instead qualify for VAT exemption under the Law on Technology Transfer. Licenses used or commercially exploited within Vietnam remain subject to 10% VAT and may trigger Foreign Contractor Tax withholding obligations for the Vietnamese payer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronically cleared export customs record or Customs Outward Manifest)
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Sales Contract
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal/international transport document evidencing physical movement of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Vietnamese commercial bank confirming foreign-currency receipt
  • IP sub-license, trademark, or patent licensing agreement with governing law, royalty, and territorial exploitation provisions
  • Insurance certificate for CIF/CIP export shipments
  • Certificate of origin where applicable for tariff or treaty benefit claims
  • E-invoice transmitted to the national e-invoice portal showing the 0% VAT line

E-invoicing is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses since 1 July 2022. E-invoices must contain a digital signature and be transmitted in near real-time to the General Department of Taxation through the national e-invoice portal using GDT-approved APIs or the GDT e-invoice service provider network. Cash-register-generated e-invoices must forward transaction data to the tax authority within one hour. There is no physical fiscal stamp; invoices generated with tax-authority invoice codes constitute real-time fiscal clearance and primary audit evidence.

The General Department of Taxation and the General Department of Customs conduct post-clearance audits, export-verification interviews, and cross-border data matching against customs declarations, e-invoices, and banking records. Fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims are retrospectively clawed back with interest at 0.03% per day on any incorrectly refunded or credited input VAT. Administrative fines range from penalties for incorrect declarations up to three times the evaded tax, together with suspension of export preferences and revocation of export-processing-enterprise status. Serious violations trigger criminal prosecution under the Penal Code, including tax evasion (Article 200), document forgery (Article 341), and smuggling (Article 188), along with blacklisting of the entity and its legal representatives. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day statutory deadline results in reversal of the zero rate, late-payment interest, denial of refunds, and exchange-control sanctions by the State Bank of Vietnam.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Viet Nam operates a managed-float regime for the Vietnamese Dong (VND). Current-account convertibility is substantially liberalized for enterprises: cross-border royalty and IP license revenues received by a Vietnamese IP Holding Company are treated as export-of-services proceeds and enjoy unrestricted repatriation into onshore foreign-currency accounts at SBV-licensed banks, provided the underlying license agreements, invoices, tax certificates, and transaction-purpose documents are compliant. Capital-account transactions remain strictly controlled: inbound FDI charter-capital contributions must flow through a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at a licensed bank under an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC); foreign shareholder loans require separate SBV registration; and outbound portfolio investments, guarantees, and loans by VN entities require prior approval or registration. Profit and dividend remittance hurdles are manageable but documentary: a foreign-invested enterprise may remit profits and dividends abroad only after filing audited financial statements, completing annual corporate income tax finalization, settling all tax liabilities, and submitting a profit-remittance confirmation through the licensed bank; no separate 'profit extraction tax' applies beyond standard withholding taxes. Practical convertibility frictions include the SBV requirement to evidence economic substance of IP operations, strict use of regulated transaction-purpose codes, the prohibition on onshore banks extending VND credit or deposit facilities to non-residents except under statutory exceptions, monitoring of disguised capital outflows via excessive royalty payments, and the thin offshore VND market; onshore licensed banks are otherwise permitted to convert current-account proceeds to VND at prevailing interbank rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular 02/2021/TT-NHNN (amending and supplementing Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN), all export proceeds, including services and royalty income from cross-border IP licensing, must be received into a foreign-currency account at an SBV-licensed credit institution. For exports of goods, the statutory collection window is 90 days from the date of export, unless an extension is approved in writing by the enterprise's licensed bank for justified commercial reasons; proceeds from services and IP/technology licensing may be collected under the contractual payment term agreed between the parties, but the funds must ultimately be credited to an onshore foreign-currency account of the Vietnamese licensing entity and must not be retained offshore beyond the commercial term. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds: enterprises may retain 100% of exported-service/royalty receipts in foreign currency in their accounts, and conversion into VND is required only when the enterprise needs VND for domestic expenditures, such conversion being executed through authorized banks at the prevailing interbank exchange rate. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the applicable statutory or contract-based deadline exposes the enterprise to administrative fines and enhanced regulatory scrutiny under Decree 88/2019/ND-CP as amended by Decree 143/2021/ND-CP. All foreign-currency receipts must be reported by the servicing bank to the SBV under the periodic foreign-exchange statistical regime.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Viet Nam (SBV) (Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam), which exercises exclusive foreign-exchange administration powers under the Law on the State Bank of Viet Nam (Law 46/2010/QH12), the Law on Foreign Exchange Administration (Ordinance 28/2005/PL-UBTVQH11 as amended by Ordinance 06/2013/UBTVQH13), Decree 70/2014/ND-CP on the implementation of the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange, and implementing instruments including Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN on foreign-exchange transactions between authorized credit institutions and clients and Circular 02/2021/TT-NHNN guiding foreign-exchange management for foreign direct investment activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Payoneer) are rated LOW-FEASIBILITY as substitutes for licensed onshore banking in Viet Nam. They are not authorized by the SBV and cannot perform Vietnamese domestic payment settlement in VND, cannot hold a DICA, and cannot issue statutory Vietnamese banking attestations required for IRC/ERC compliance, dividend remittance, or foreign-loan registration. They may serve only a supplementary treasury function: an offshore entity in the group may use an EMI to aggregate small foreign-currency royalty payments from non-Vietnamese sub-licensees, provided (i) the EMI is not used as the payment beneficiary of record under a Vietnamese contract requiring legal invoices, (ii) the funds are periodically repatriated to the licensed onshore bank within the applicable statutory window, and (iii) all contractual documentation, tax filings, and SBV transaction-purpose codes remain executed through the onshore licensed bank. The SBV does not recognize EMI statements as evidence for inward FDI capital registration, and use of an EMI as an intermediary for outbound payments of royalties, taxes, or dividends is prohibited because those outflows require bank verification of underlying agreements, withholding tax certificates, and purpose codes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Decree No. 132/2020/ND-CP, a Vietnamese enterprise must prepare and maintain a Local File and Master File when it has related-party transactions and (a) annual total revenue exceeds VND 50 billion, or (b) the total value of related-party transactions exceeds VND 30 billion for the fiscal year. In addition, a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory if the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds VND 18,000 billion (approx. USD 800 million). The VN LLC, as an IP HoldCo with cross-border licensing to affiliates, will almost certainly satisfy one or both thresholds and accordingly must prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation with audited benchmarking analysis.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam has not enacted standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) similar to the UAE or Cayman Islands. However, the Vietnamese tax administration applies the substance-over-form doctrine, a statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Law on Tax Administration No. 38/2019/QH14, and the beneficial-ownership clauses in tax treaties. For an IP HoldCo, economic substance is evaluated through the DEMPE framework (Development, Enhancement, Maintenance, Protection, and Exploitation). The VN LLC must employ qualified local personnel, maintain board minutes and decision-making records, incur substantive operating expenditures in Vietnam, control the risk profile of the patents, and actually generate revenue from licensing. If tax authorities conclude that the Vietnamese entity is a shell or lacks the capacity to own and exploit the IP, they may recharacterize the royalty income, disallow deductible licensing costs, increase CIT liability, deny treaty benefits on outbound payments, and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent CIT Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine equal to 1–3 times the evaded tax amount under Article 17 of Decree 125/2020/ND-CP; for criminal tax evasion, a fine of up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment of up to 7 years under Article 200 of the Penal Code (No. 100/2015/QH13, as amended). Offending assets may be seized, and the CTA or customs authorities can issue protective seizure orders.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and chief accountants may be personally liable for signing false returns. The corporate veil can be pierced where the LLC is operated as an alter ego or fraud instrument; the individual faces civil restitution, travel ban and exit-immigration suspension, loss of limited liability for the unpaid tax, and potential imprisonment as joint offender.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration and Shell IP Company

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust revenue, disallow excessive royalties, impose the difference in CIT, apply late payment interest at 0.03% per day, and levy administrative penalties of up to 20% of the underpaid tax for incorrect transfer pricing declarations. Failure to submit documentation can trigger fixed fines up to VND 80 million and evidentiary burden reversal, allowing the tax office to apply its own estimates.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or knowingly implement sham TP arrangements can be held personally liable to the tax authority. Thailand? The Vietnamese court or tax administration may pierce the corporate veil if the IP HoldCo is a mere shell; officers may receive travel bans and be barred from leaving Vietnam pending tax debt settlement, and criminal charges for intentional violation of tax law can result in imprisonment.
Abusive Profit Repatriation and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The Vietnam tax authority will deny treaty relief, impose the full FCT rates (10% CIT + 5% VAT on royalties), reclassify the excessive royalty as a dividend or capital contribution refund, and assess penalties plus late payment interest of 0.03% per day. For serious cross-border profit stripping, criminal prosecution for tax evasion may apply, together with asset seizures and sanctions on offshore bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Board members and controlling shareholders who orchestrate phantom royalty structures may have the corporate veil pierced in restitution proceedings; they are personally responsible for unpaid corporate taxes and interest. Personal travel restrictions and entry bans may be issued by the tax authority; if the transaction is found to be in bad faith, civil fraud damages and imprisonment of up to 7 years can be imposed.
Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing via License Payments

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the AML law and its implementing decrees, license suspension or revocation of the certificate of investment, and criminal liability under Articles 324 of the Penal Code for money laundering, with punishments up to 15 years' imprisonment and fines up to VND 5 billion. The State Bank of Vietnam and tax inspection teams can freeze accounts and halt remittances.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally sanctioned for failure to report suspicious transactions. The corporate veil is disregarded where the LLC is used as a conduit for money laundering; individuals face confiscation of assets, civil restitution to the State, travel bans for suspect persons, and extended terms of imprisonment for systemic AML violations within the enterprise.
