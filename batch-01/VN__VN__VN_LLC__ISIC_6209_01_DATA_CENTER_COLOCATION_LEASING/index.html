Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices are not permissible as the sole statutory registered address of a foreign-invested limited liability company. The registered address must be a physical, rentable premises in Viet Nam where the statutory legal representative, corporate records, and accounting documents can be inspected. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a standard limited liability company in the information technology service sector. Charter capital must be a positive amount approved by the members and must be fully contributed within 90 days after the Enterprise Registration Certificate is issued. There is no escrow account requirement; contribution is made by bank transfer to the company's designated corporate bank account and evidenced by a capital contribution statement. For data-center colocation projects, the registered capital must be consistent with the project scale and investment feasibility, and the DPI may reject the application if the capital is manifestly inadequate.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory prerequisites: (i) Fire-Safety Certificate issued by the Fire Police Department (C07) after an on-site inspection; (ii) construction permit and completion inspection for new/renovated buildings; (iii) structural load certification for server floors and raised floors; (iv) workplace occupational-health and safety clearance covering electric safety, back-up generator ventilation, confined-space protocols, and emergency evacuation drills; and (v) electrical engineering inspection and commissioning certificate by a licensed electrical authority. No additional civil-defence clearance is triggered by the data-center activity itself.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a standard limited liability company in the information technology service sector. Charter capital must be a positive amount approved by the members and must be fully contributed within 90 days after the Enterprise Registration Certificate is issued. There is no escrow account requirement; contribution is made by bank transfer to the company's designated corporate bank account and evidenced by a capital contribution statement. For data-center colocation projects, the registered capital must be consistent with the project scale and investment feasibility, and the DPI may reject the application if the capital is manifestly inadequate.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

Ministry of Information and Communications (MIC) is the sector policy authority, but a pure data-center colocation service under ISIC 6209 does not require a telecommunications or Internet service licence. The operative industrial authorisations are issued by: Department of Construction (construction permit), Fire Police Department C07 (fire-safety certificate), and Provincial Department of Natural Resources and Environment (environmental registration).

Category: No separate 'data-center operating license' is required for pure colocation and bare-metal hosting activities under current Vietnamese law; the Enterprise Registration Certificate together with the Fire-Safety Certificate and Environmental Registration constitute the statutory operating authorisation. If the company later offers value-added telecommunications or internet content services, a telecommunications service licence under Decree 72/2013/ND-CP as amended would become mandatory.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (GDT; Tổng cục Thuế) under the Ministry of Finance; primary e-services portal: National Electronic Tax Portal (eTax) at https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn; e-invoice portal: https://hoadon.gdt.gov.vn.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested LLC: (1) obtain Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) or the Industrial/Export Processing Zone Authority; (2) obtain Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) from DPI; (3) the Tax Identification Number (Mã số thuế – MST) is automatically generated and unified with the enterprise code upon ERC issuance; (4) complete electronic tax registration (Form 05/ĐK-THT) on the eTax portal, declaring VAT, CIT declaration method, and fiscal year; (5) procure a certified digital signature (USB token) for e-declarations and e-invoicing; (6) register the e-invoice template and e-invoice service provider with GDT before issuing invoices. Foreign contractors without a Vietnamese legal entity must separately register Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) with the local tax office using Form 01/ĐK-NTL.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Vietnam does not operate U.S.-style check-the-box elections or hybrid-mismatch elections; LLCs are mandatorily taxed at entity level (CIT at 20%). Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT calculation method election – credit (input-deduction) method is compulsory for enterprises with annual revenue above VND 1 billion; direct (output-minus-input or add-on) method is available for qualified small taxpayers with revenue below VND 1 billion; (ii) Foreign Contractor Tax method election (deemed/knock-off method vs. normal accounting method) for eligible foreign contractors; (iii) election of CIT incentive regimes and preferential rates (high-tech zone, export processing zone, supporting industry certificates) subject to meeting statutory conditions; (iv) election to keep books and declare tax in a foreign currency with GDT approval. Elections are made by submission of prescribed forms via the eTax portal and, once adopted, generally apply for the whole tax period unless amended with GDT consent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for a corporate LLC: VAT registration is compulsory before commencing business, immediately upon ERC issuance; VND 100,000,000/year turnover threshold applies only to households, individual entrepreneurs, and small non-corporate business households under the direct VAT method. Foreign-invested LLCs must register for VAT regardless of projected revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT rate applies to (a) exported goods physically shipped abroad; (b) services exported or supplied to foreign organizations or individuals and consumed entirely outside Vietnam, including international transport; and (c) services directly connected to exported goods. To claim 0%, taxpayers must evidence the export with: a signed export sales contract; for goods, a full customs file (electronic customs declaration, customs-sealed commercial invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, certificate of origin where required); for services, a written contract with the foreign party, proof that the services are performed and consumed outside Vietnam with benefits accruing outside Vietnam, and non-cash cross-border payment evidence. Important limitation: data-center colocation and rack-space leasing deliverables are physically supplied and consumed inside Vietnam (server racks located in Vietnam); such services have place of supply in Vietnam and are taxed at 10% VAT even if billed to a foreign customer. 0% treatment is not available merely because the customer is offshore; only genuine offshore services (e.g., remote technical services consumed abroad and not benefiting any Vietnamese party) may qualify, and the burden of proof lies with the taxpayer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic customs export declaration with customs clearance confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or equivalent international transport document (evidencing physical export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Swift MT103 / FX repatriation proof (foreign-currency remittance received)
  • Export Sales Contract (signed with foreign buyer, stating Incoterms and payment terms)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (customs-sealed)
  • Service Performance / Consumption Certificate (for offshore services, attesting consumption outside Vietnam with no Vietnamese beneficiary)
  • Insurance Certificate (where export insurance applies)

Mandatory e-invoicing under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC, effective 1 July 2022. All e-invoices must be generated in standardized XML format containing a mandatory QR code and must be registered on GDT's national e-invoice portal or with a GDT-certified e-invoice service provider. Coded e-invoices require API-transmitted XML data to the GDT portal at issuance to receive the tax-authority code before forwarding to customers; retail and cash-register invoices are subject to real-time (near-immediate) transmission to GDT. Uncoded e-invoices must be transmitted to GDT within the declaratory period (monthly or quarterly). Corporate digital signatures (HiSign) are compulsory; the real-time cash-register invoice clearance architecture applies to on-premises retail payments, not to pure B2B colocation billing.

GDT applies risk-based pre-refund audits and tax inspections to export zero-rating claims; missing bills of lading, customs declarations, or FX remittance files trigger automatic disallowance. Fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims constitute tax evasion under the Law on Tax Administration No. 38/2019/QH14 and Decree 125/2020/ND-CP: administrative penalty of 20% of the evaded or unduly refunded VAT, plus late-payment interest at 0.03% per day calculated retrospectively from the refund or offset date; the refund is clawed back in full with interest; the taxpayer may be barred from VAT refund treatment for up to 12 months. Customs false-export penalties under Decree 128/2020/ND-CP apply in addition, including fines up to VND 200 million per violation and confiscation in serious cases. Criminal tax evasion under Penal Code 2015 (Article 200) exposes individuals (CEO, CFO, chief accountant) to fines up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment up to 7 years; legal entities may be fined up to VND 15 billion and/or suspended from operations for up to 3 years. The statute of limitations is 5 years for tax declaration inspection and 10 years for tax evasion.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam maintains de facto current-account convertibility while retaining strict capital-account controls. For a foreign-invested LLC engaged in data center colocation leasing to offshore tenants, USD-denominated service export revenue is freely receivable into a locally held foreign currency account but must be repatriated to Vietnam within the statutory window; conversion of VND to foreign currency for profit remittance is permitted only after annual external audit completion, corporate income tax finalization, and a declaration that all taxes, social insurance premiums, and labor obligations have been settled. Dividend and profit remittances are effected in the original foreign currency through the entity's Direct Foreign Investment Capital Account. Offshore convertibility of the Vietnamese dong remains absent: VND is non-deliverable outside Vietnam, offshore NDF markets referencing VND are not legally recognized by SBV, and the onshore interbank FX market is managed within a daily trading band (currently plus/minus 5% around the SBV reference rate). Capital account items, including charter capital increases, foreign shareholder loans, equity transfers, and liquidation proceeds, all require prior SBV registration or licensing; early or unreported equity repatriation is treated as a foreign exchange breach subject to administrative fines and potential criminal exposure under Decree 88/2019/ND-CP and Penal Code provisions on currency smuggling.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN (implementing Ordinance on Foreign Exchange), export proceeds, including cross-border service export receipts from data center colocation and bare-metal server hosting supplied to non-resident customers, must be credited to a licensed foreign currency account located in Vietnam within 90 days from the date the export service is performed/perfection or the invoice becomes due. The statutory rule does not impose an automatic conversion ratio for private service exporters: the full 100% of export proceeds must be repatriated to the local foreign currency account, after which the enterprise may retain USD to fund permissible foreign currency obligations (payments for imported bandwidth, power infrastructure, equipment, and overseas vendor invoices) or may sell USD to the authorized bank for VND. Mandatory conversion to VND applies narrowly, e.g., to certain petroleum exports and state-owned trading houses, and does not apply to a private data center operator. The enterprise must submit a quarterly export-proceeds status report to the PBX branch where the account is held if requested; non-repatriation beyond 90 days triggers administraive penalties up to VND 200,000,000 and potential revocation of the foreign currency account license, and the underreported or unrepatriated amount may be treated as a foreign exchange violation requiring remedial sale of the receivable at the central bank rate.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam), Department of Foreign Exchange Management (Vụ Quản lý Ngoại hối), 49 Ly Thai To Street, Hoan Kiem District, Hanoi, Vietnam. SBV exercises exclusive statutory authority over foreign exchange administration, authorizes commercial banks to act as authorized dealers in foreign currency, and regulates all foreign borrowing, capital account transactions, cross-border payments, and profit remittances under the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange No. 04/2003/PL-UBTVQH11 as amended by Ordinance No. 06/2013/UBTVQH13, together with Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN and Circular 12/2022/TT-NHNN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

LOW feasibility rating for offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie, Intergiro) as primary treasury or clearing rails. Vietnamese foreign exchange law and Decree 70/2014/ND-CP restrict resident enterprises from holding or using offshore payment accounts for transactions that should be settled through SBV-supervised channels; Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN mandates that cross-border service export proceeds be credited to an authorized bank in Vietnam, making an offshore EMI account the legal equivalent of unrepatriated export proceeds if funds remain offshore beyond 90 days. Offshore EMIs are neither licensed nor regulated by SBV for dong clearing, cannot open VND nostro accounts without a local partner, and generally cannot originate compliant MT103 payments bearing Vietnamese purpose codes. A narrow permissible use exists: low-value recurring international invoices (e.g., domain renewal, software subscriptions) may be paid via a licensed international payment intermediary only if the intermediary holds a Vietnamese e-wallet/payment intermediary license under Decree 101/2012/ND-CP and Circular 39/2014/TT-NHNN. The sanctioned architecture is full onshore banking with SBV-licensed authorized banks, supplemented, at most, by a licensed foreign payment intermediary for micro-payments; treasury concentration offshore or use of an unlicensed EMI is classified as a foreign exchange offense.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial owner is a corporate entity in a high-risk or tax-haven jurisdiction (BVI, Cayman, Seychelles) or an opaque multi-tier holding chain without clear attribution of natural persons

Root cause: Beneficial owner is a corporate entity in a high-risk or tax-haven jurisdiction (BVI, Cayman, Seychelles) or an opaque multi-tier holding chain without clear attribution of natural persons

Defense evidence: SBV-prescribed Ultimate Beneficial Owner declaration identifying all natural persons holding 25% or more of the chain; notarized Vietnamese translations of foreign corporate registry extracts (Certificate of Good Standing); full organizational chart from the ultimate individual down to the Vietnamese LLC; source of wealth dossier comprising audited financial statements of the shareholder and bank reference letters from the shareholder's home financial institution; notarized passports of UBOs; statement of no adverse media and PEP check results from a reputable screening database.
Mismatch between declared business scope (data center colocation/leasing) and expected transaction profile dominated by frequent cross-border USD credits from unrelated foreign tenants

Root cause: Mismatch between declared business scope (data center colocation/leasing) and expected transaction profile dominated by frequent cross-border USD credits from unrelated foreign tenants

Defense evidence: Executed master colocation agreements and per-rack service contracts with foreign enterprise customers; tenant KYC file including customer registry extract and passport copies of tenant signatories; invoice schedule with monthly recurring charge pattern; proof of physical operations comprising the data center lease agreement, power purchase agreement (PPA), and utilities billing; SBV-compliant FX transaction declaration forms; bank documentary evidence of the underlying service performance (billing records, SLA reports).
Large charter capital injection or shareholder loan disbursement occurring within weeks of incorporation without proportionate evidence of funding capacity

Root cause: Large charter capital injection or shareholder loan disbursement occurring within weeks of incorporation without proportionate evidence of funding capacity

Defense evidence: Board resolution approving the capital call and the exact timing; executed capital contribution agreement filed with the Department of Planning and Investment; SBV Foreign Loan Registration Certificate for any shareholder loan issued under Circular 12/2022/TT-NHNN; SWIFT MT103 credit messages matched to the FDI capital account; certified source of funds comprising shareholder audited financial statements, dividend distribution minutes, or asset disposition deeds; tax identification certificate of the Vietnamese LLC; statement from the authorized bank confirming the FDI account number.
Early-stage outbound trade payments (prepayment to overseas equipment suppliers for servers, UPS, cooling, and racks) submitted before import documentation exists

Root cause: Early-stage outbound trade payments (prepayment to overseas equipment suppliers for servers, UPS, cooling, and racks) submitted before import documentation exists

Defense evidence: Pro-Forma Invoice and purchase order from each supplier; executed import contract referencing Incoterms 2020; board resolution authorizing the trade prepayment; bank's LC or T/T application signed and stamped by the authorized legal representative; prior customs declaration history (or declaration number if goods already in transit); Equivalence import/export code assigned by customs; supplier due-diligence file including supplier registry and contact verification; sworn attestation that goods are for operational use in the licensed data center facility.
Use of a Power of Attorney signatory rather than the registered legal representative, particularly for high-value FX transactions

Root cause: Use of a Power of Attorney signatory rather than the registered legal representative, particularly for high-value FX transactions

Defense evidence: Notarized Power of Attorney executed in compliance with Vietnamese Civil Code 2015 and Notarization Law, expressly listing the transactional scope (opening accounts, signing FX contracts, issuing L/Cs); legalized translation into Vietnamese if executed abroad; passport and residency documents of both the grantor (statutory legal representative) and the attorney-in-fact; specimen signature card registered at the bank with identity verification; corporation seal used on the PoA matched with the seal registration certificate at the Department of Planning and Investment; board resolution ratifying the delegation.
Unusually high volume of round-tripped funds or frequent recourse to third-party payments (asking the bank to credit amounts to unrelated beneficiaries) inconsistent with a colocation fee stream

Root cause: Unusually high volume of round-tripped funds or frequent recourse to third-party payments (asking the bank to credit amounts to unrelated beneficiaries) inconsistent with a colocation fee stream

Defense evidence: Legal-purpose letter (Cam kết mục đích sử dụng vốn) stating the commercial rationale for each third-party payment; end-to-end invoice trail from tenant invoice to the eventual supplier/bank settlement; service verification certificates from data center management systems; an explanatory memorandum from the CFO identifying the treasury topology; updated UBO declarations confirming no informal shareholders; commitment to implement quarterly transaction-profile reporting to the bank's compliance unit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity and shareholder-loan funding must be routed through the Direct Foreign Investment Capital Account maintained at the primary authorized dealer bank (FDI Capital Account). The LLC must open this account after issuance of the IRC/ERC and register every capital call with the bank before receipt. For charter capital: credit via SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code 'Investment Capital Contribution – FDI Account' and documentary support comprising the certificate of investment, enterprise registration certificate, board resolution on capital call, and a receipt-purpose letter. For intercompany shareholder loans: obtain an SBV Foreign Loan Registration Certificate (Form D-1, issued under Circular 12/2022/TT-NHNN) before disbursement; loan proceeds must be received into the registered borrowing account or the FDI account and may not be credited to any private offshore account. VND-equivalent and USD-equivalent reporting thresholds apply: all transactions equal or exceeding USD 10,000 must be individually justified with a transaction purpose certificate (Giấy đề nghị xác nhận mục đích chuyển tiền) and the bank will file the report to SBV's Foreign Exchange Information System. Non-compliance before receipt (e.g., upfront utilization of funds from an unregistered loan) invalidates the future tax deductibility of interest and triggers fines under Decree 88/2019/ND-CP. For robust capital deployment, structure the initial capital contribution in two to three tranches matching build-out milestones; each tranche must be fully evidenced with board minutes and a revised capital utilization schedule.Outbound settlements for imported servers, networking equipment, environmental control systems, software licenses, and cloud interconnect fees use the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for direct telegraphic transfers (T/T) to overseas suppliers under 30-90 day terms; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or deferred payment, UCP600-compliant) issued by the Vietnamese bank in favor of the supplier, particularly for high-value hardware import batches; (iii) standby L/Cs or bank guarantees for equipment lease and data center infrastructure financing; (iv) direct debits and domestic NAPAS clearing for local vendor and utility payments in VND. Before any FX outward payment, the bank requires underlying trade documents: an import contract or agreement, supplier invoice or packing list, customs declaration (or proof of service receipt for intangible expenses), and a board resolution for payments exceeding VND 500 million. Recurring cross-border O&M expenses (bandwidth, interconnect, vendor-remote-hand) may be aggregated in a quarterly settlement statement with a schedule of services rendered. USD vendor payments are paid from the USD operational account; VND limitations are avoided for foreign beneficiaries except for a narrowly defined list of local-currency remittance cases permitted under Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN. Settlement to countries under OFAC/UN sanctions is automatically blocked by correspondent bank filters; screen all counterparties and include standard denial-of-service sanctions language in supplier terms.Onshore USD/VND outright forward contracts with licensed commercial banks, tenor up to 180 days, governed by Circular 02/2018/TT-NHNN; physically settled only, requiring an underlying trade or investment transaction for hedging eligibility.Onshore FX swaps (spot vs. forward USD/VND) with an authorized credit institution for treasury liquidity management and matching of receivable/liability maturities.Cross-currency swaps (USD/VND) combined with an underlying foreign currency loan, subject to SBV approval and only with a licensed counterparty executing the derivative as part of a documented borrowing facility.Foreign currency interest-rate swaps or options permissible only when ancillary to a registered cross-border loan and executed inside Vietnam with SBV-authorized dealing room supervision.NO offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing the Vietnamese dong are permissible or enforceable in Vietnam. VND is non-convertible offshore; attempting to place NDF positions through an offshore parent is illegal and exposes the Vietnamese entity to FX sanction - all hedging must be executed onshore for physical USD/VND settlement.Internal natural hedging: manage net USD positions tightly by denominating receivables and payable in USD with matched monthly settlement to reduce derivative counterparty reliance.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: LOW feasibility rating for offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currenxie, Intergiro) as primary treasury or clearing rails. Vietnamese foreign exchange law and Decree 70/2014/ND-CP restrict resident enterprises from holding or using offshore payment accounts for transactions that should be settled through SBV-supervised channels; Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN mandates that cross-border service export proceeds be credited to an authorized bank in Vietnam, making an offshore EMI account the legal equivalent of unrepatriated export proceeds if funds remain offshore beyond 90 days. Offshore EMIs are neither licensed nor regulated by SBV for dong clearing, cannot open VND nostro accounts without a local partner, and generally cannot originate compliant MT103 payments bearing Vietnamese purpose codes. A narrow permissible use exists: low-value recurring international invoices (e.g., domain renewal, software subscriptions) may be paid via a licensed international payment intermediary only if the intermediary holds a Vietnamese e-wallet/payment intermediary license under Decree 101/2012/ND-CP and Circular 39/2014/TT-NHNN. The sanctioned architecture is full onshore banking with SBV-licensed authorized banks, supplemented, at most, by a licensed foreign payment intermediary for micro-payments; treasury concentration offshore or use of an unlicensed EMI is classified as a foreign exchange offense.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File threshold: total revenue of at least VND 50 billion in a fiscal year OR total related-party transaction value of at least VND 30 billion. Mandatory Master File threshold: ultimate parent's consolidated global revenue of at least VND 18,000 billion. Below these thresholds, taxpayers must still file an annual related-party transaction declaration and maintain a sufficiently documented pricing policy.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) framework similar to the UAE or BVI, but its General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the transfer pricing rules in Decree 132/2020/ND-CP require commercial arrangements to reflect actual economic substance. For a data center colocation LLC, the entity must demonstrate real decision-making, qualified personnel, physical control over the facility, assumption of operating and market risk, and the capacity to generate profits independent of offshore affiliate instructions. The tax authority may recharacterize routing structures, deny treaty benefits, and disregard the LLC as a façade if substance is absent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal)

Statutory sanction: The taxpayer is liable for back-taxes for up to 10 years, an administrative penalty of up to 25% of under-declared tax, late-payment interest at 0.03% per day, and criminal penalties under Article 200 of the Penal Code: fines from VND 100 million to VND 4.5 billion, imprisonment from 6 months to 20 years for tax evasion exceeding VND 100 million, and confiscation of instrumentalities.

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers, and chief accountants who authorize fraudulent filings face personal criminal prosecution, civil restitution of unpaid taxes and penalties, travel bans, exit suspension, and seizure of personal assets when the corporate veil is pierced as a vehicle for criminal tax evasion.
Aggressive / Non-Documented Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: The General Department of Taxation may adjust taxable income to the arm's length level, impose additional CIT, apply late-payment interest at 0.03% per day, and levy administrative fines for absent documentation under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP. Willful gross mispricing can escalate to criminal tax prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors may be individually called into tax audits, personally liable for tax shortfalls if they directed the intercompany pricing, subjected to prohibition from leaving Vietnam until outstanding amounts are settled, and criminally liable under tax evasion or fraud indictments where the filing was deliberately falsified.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may apply the GAAR and substance-over-form doctrine to deny CIT incentives, disallow dividend treaty relief, reclassify revenue as taxable Vietnamese-sourced income, and impose penalties up to 25% of the underpaid tax together with late-payment interest. In egregious cases, business registration and data-center operating approvals may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors can be held personally responsible for all unpaid taxation and penalties when the LLC is treated as a sham; civil restitution, asset freezing, travel bans, and criminal fraud convictions may follow. Regulators may pierce the corporate veil to recover unlawful distributions.
AML / Foreign Currency and Professional Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Law on Anti-Money Laundering and Decree 116/2013/ND-CP/amended decrees include monetary fines up to VND 3 billion, revocation of payment operation agreements, confiscation of illicit proceeds, and criminal penalties under relevant penal provisions. Non-resident remittances outside approved bank channels are also subject to exchange-control fines.

Director personal liability: The data center's board and chief compliance officer face direct AML liability, personal fines, travel bans, disqualification from company management, and imprisonment where omissions facilitated money laundering or terrorist financing through the colocation infrastructure.
