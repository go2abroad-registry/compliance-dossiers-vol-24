Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum charter capital applies to an ordinary LLC under the Law on Enterprises; however, to lawfully render discretionary portfolio management for multi-family clients, the company must be licensed as a fund management company by the State Securities Commission and maintain charter capital of at least VND 25 billion (≈ USD 1,000,000) subscribed and fully paid-in. All contributed capital must be transferred to the company's Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) within 90 days of the ERC (Article 75, Law on Enterprises), with the first 90-day phase held in a blocked escrow/capital-modality account at a licensed custodian bank and released only after the fund-management license and capital-dedicated verification by the SSC; late contributions subject the defaulting member to loss of voting rights and statutory default interest (Article 76).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (GDT), Ministry of Finance, Socialist Republic of Viet Nam; nationwide e-tax filing and e-invoice portal: https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn; enterprise registration portal: https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investor obtains an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) where required by the Law on Investment and an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment. Step 2: The tax authority automatically issues the statutory 10-digit tax identification number (MST) upon ERC issuance; no separate TIN application is required. Step 3: The taxpayer activates an electronic tax account on the GDT portal at https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn and registers a tax digital signature (USB token or cloud-based e-signature) for filing and e-invoice use. Step 4: A foreign contractor operating without a local legal entity that derives Vietnamese-source income must register directly for Foreign Contractor Tax using the applicable GDT form and obtain a tax code for withholding and compliance. Step 5: The LLC must register a physical tax administration place of business with the provincial tax office and file the standard taxpayer registration dossier for CIT, VAT, and withholdings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A foreign-invested LLC is a separate Vietnamese tax resident by incorporation and place of management. It is subject to Corporate Income Tax on worldwide income and is treated as a distinct taxable person for VAT and Foreign Contractor Tax purposes. The LLC cannot be classified as a pass-through entity under Vietnamese tax law; profits distributed to foreign owners attract dividend withholding tax in accordance with the applicable double tax agreement.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch pass-through election exists. Statutory elections and options available include: (i) VAT calculation method — the credit/deduction method is the default; eligible small taxpayers or foreign contractors may elect the direct turnover method; (ii) Foreign Contractor Tax election — a covered foreign contractor may elect to declare and pay itself or be subject to withholding; (iii) CIT incentive registration — the LLC must register qualifying investment activities with the tax authority to claim preferential CIT rates and exemptions; (iv) loss carryforward claims of up to five years are exercised in the annual CIT finalization; (v) treaty relief — a foreign resident contractor may invoke an applicable double tax agreement by filing Vietnamese domestic returns and providing a valid tax residency certificate. Hybrid mismatch conversion is not available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold applies to incorporated LLCs: every LLC with taxable supplies in Viet Nam must be VAT-registered before starting activity. Small household businesses with annual turnover from taxable goods and services not exceeding VND 100 million are outside the VAT net and need not register unless they voluntarily register. A foreign contractor deriving Vietnamese-source revenue becomes liable under the Foreign Contractor Tax regime from the first invoice, with no minimum economic threshold.

Electronic invoicing is mandatory under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. All sales invoices must be issued as e-invoices in standardized XML format, digitally signed, containing QR codes, and transmitted to the General Department of Taxation through the national e-invoice portal. The regime uses both e-invoices with tax-authority-generated invoice codes and e-invoices without pre-allocated codes, but all invoice data must be submitted to the GDT through certified software or authorized intermediation APIs in the prescribed post-issuance period. There is no physical stamping requirement; fiscal compliance is achieved through schema validation, digital signature integrity, QR code, and near-real-time electronic transmission to the GDT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vietnamese dong (VND) is a managed-float, partially convertible currency under the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange and Decree 70/2014/ND-CP. Current-account items, genuinely evidenced by invoices and contracts, are freely transferable through licensed banks using the correct balance-of-payments (BOP) purpose codes: trade in goods and services, interest, royalties, management fees and profit remittances may be routed without prior SBV approval, subject to underlying documentation and to tax withholding clearance. Capital-account items are open but tightly administratively controlled: inward FDI equity must enter through a licensed bank's Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) against an executed Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) and Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); intercompany loans and offshore borrowing require a PCL/FX-coded foreign-loan registration certificate; portfolio investments by the MFO in Vietnamese securities require a securities custody and investor code if the LLC is a foreign institutional investor. Profit remittance hurdles for the foreign investor are sequential: audited annual financial statements of the LLC, corporate income tax (CIT) and foreign contractor tax (FCT) finalization, resolution declaring distributable after-tax profit, documentary proof that the registered charter capital has been contributed, and bank verification of the DICA outflow. In practice, tier-1 Vietnamese banks apply high-level EDD to discretionary portfolio-advisory flows, and the LLC should be prepared for source-of-funds review on every material outbound profit transfer. Convertibility is therefore unrestricted on the current account but gated on the capital account, and both directions require purpose codes and full bankable documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 32 of Decree 70/2014/ND-CP and Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN, exporters of goods and services must repatriate export proceeds into a foreign-currency demand deposit account (FCA) held at a licensed Vietnamese bank within 90 days from completion of the export customs declaration (or within the contractually agreed payment term for deferred-payment exports, but never beyond the statutory limit). Repatriation must be in the invoiced convertible currency or another freely convertible currency acceptable to the SBV. The SBV publishes an annual Decision setting the mandatory conversion ratio for each foreign-currency receipt category; from the 2016 cycle through the most recently published Decisions, the applicable ratio for general merchandise and commercial export earnings has been 0%, meaning exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds in FCAs and convert voluntarily at onshore licence bank rates. If the MFO treasury generates any exported service income, the same 90-day repatriation rule applies; such receipts are not stranded abroad. Mandatory surrender can be reintroduced at any time by SBV Decision, and the treasury must monitor in force Decisions rather than assume permanent 0% retention.

Central bank supervision: The State Bank of Vietnam (SBV; Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam), established under the Law on the State Bank of Vietnam No. 46/2010/QH12, is the sole monetary and foreign-exchange authority in the Socialist Republic of Viet Nam. FX administration is governed by the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange No. 06/2005/PL-UBTVQH11 (as amended by Ordinance No. 16/2012/UBTVQH13), Government Decree No. 70/2014/ND-CP detailing foreign-exchange management, and SBV Circular No. 15/2015/TT-NHNN on foreign-exchange transactions with residents and non-residents (as amended by Circular No. 05/2021/TT-NHNN). The SBV grants banking licences, authorizes approved banks to deal in foreign currencies and derivatives, supervises correspondent banking and cross-border payment systems, operates the interbank foreign-exchange market, fixes the official reference exchange rate and daily band, and annually determines any mandatory sale ratio on foreign-currency export receipts. For an MFO treasury, SBV licenses are not directly held, but the operating Vietnamese LLC must act exclusively through SBV-licensed credit institutions.
