Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a substantially liberalised capital-account regime for non-resident-owned International Companies (ICs). Current-account payments for dental-block raw material imports and finished-good export receipts are unrestricted; there is no pre-shipment verification, no tariff-linked FX licensing, and no profit-remittance permit requirement. Capital-account transactions, including paid-up share capital injections, intercompany loans, and return of equity, are freely convertible when executed through an RBV-licensed commercial bank solely in foreign currency. Domestic commercial banks require only standard anti-money-laundering documentation and SWIFT-compliant purpose codes. The residual friction is not statutory but correspondent-driven: as a jurisdiction historically exposed to APG/FATF enhanced monitoring, VU-domiciled payment flows attract enhanced screening at beneficiary banks in the United States, Australia, and Europe.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender rule applies to VU International Companies. The Export Proceeds facet of the Exchange Control Act [Cap 54] is disapplied to ICs operating exclusively in foreign currency with non-residents; 100% of export proceeds from zirconia-block sales may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, AUD, or NZD accounts without conversion into Vanuatu Vatu (VUV), and there is no statutory time limit for repatriation into the VU banking system.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap 125]; exchange-control and foreign-currency administration powers are exercised by the RBV under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 54], while supervision of International Companies is vested in the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 222].

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM with legal and operational constraints. Offshore digital EMIs regulated in Singapore, the UK, or the EU (for example Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Wise Business) may on-board a VU International Company that presents a VFSC registered agent certificate, certificate of incumbency, and standard beneficial-ownership declarations; such EMIs provide efficient USD/EUR/CNH payment rails for sub-USD 250,000 operational disbursements. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not licensed by the RBV and cannot open VUV accounts; (ii) prominent EMIs apply policy exclusion or enhanced due diligence to VU entities given Vanuatu's historical APG/FATF grey-list episodes, so approval is discretionary and account limits may be capped; (iii) EMI accounts are inadvisable as primary inbound capital-injection rails because they do not issue a local SWIFT branch address acceptable to correspondent banks for high-value FDI wires. Recommended structure: Tier-1 domestic bank for capital, trade settlements, and LC, plus one EMI as a low-value disbursement overlay.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all share-capital injections exclusively through the segregated Capital Escrow/Deposit account held at ANZ or BSP in Port Vila. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable; Vanuatu national tax law imposes no transfer-pricing documentation, Local File, Master File, or benchmark study requirements

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu's Economic Substance Regulations target geographically mobile financial activities such as banking, insurance, financing, shipping, intellectual property, and headquarters services; a manufacturing entity holding inventory and conducting dental zirconia block production outside Vanuatu is generally outside that scope. Nevertheless, adequate commercial substance is advisable to prevent foreign CFC recharacterisation or anti-tax-haven measures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authorities may raise additional assessments, impose penalties of up to 100–200% of the avoided tax, recharacterise the entity as a CFC, and compel production of transactional documents; Vanuatu authorities may strike off the company for filing false information with the Registrar

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute sham arrangements face foreign civil restitution, personal assessment, criminal fraud sanctions, and potential imprisonment in the taxing jurisdiction; Vanuatu itself does not imprison directors for foreign tax evasion but may disbar them from acting as directors
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies Act, the VFSC may suspend or revoke the company's registration, order compulsory deregistration, and vest its assets; the company loses the legal capacity to contract and its bank accounts may be frozen

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified from managing any Vanuatu company, subject to civil penalty orders, and foreign courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose criminal liability for sham transactions, and issue travel bans pending asset recovery
AML / Counter-Financing of Terrorism Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act, penalties include fines up to VT 75,000,000 per contravention, freezing orders, confiscation of proceeds, and suspension or revocation of the company's licence by the VFSC

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally liable for AML failures; sanctions include imprisonment of up to 10 years, personal monetary penalties, travel bans, and disqualification from corporate management
