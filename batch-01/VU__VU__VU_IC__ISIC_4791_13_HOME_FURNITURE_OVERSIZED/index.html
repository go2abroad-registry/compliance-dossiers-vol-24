Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Government of Vanuatu; official portal: https://customsinlandrevenue.gov.vu

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) with no local taxable supplies is not issued a Tax Identification Number; the IC relies on its statutory exemption under the International Companies Act (Cap. 222) and is not required to register for tax. If the IC makes or intends to make taxable supplies in Vanuatu with a 12-month turnover exceeding VT 5,000,000, the foreign entity must apply to the DCIR for VAT registration on the prescribed form (VAT-100), submitting the certificate of incorporation, registered agent confirmation, registered office address, business description, projected turnover, and banking details. The DCIR then issues a VAT/TIN number. A non-resident IC may apply for voluntary VAT registration without a permanent establishment in Vanuatu if it demonstrably makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu or makes supplies that can be zero-rated as exports.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime because there is no corporate income tax. Available elections under the Value Added Tax Act 2014 are voluntary VAT registration, group VAT registration, and election to zero-rate export supplies where the statutory evidentiary conditions are met. The International Companies Act also permits conversion to a domestic company, which would end the tax-exempt status, pending DCIR confirmation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Vatu (VT) 5,000,000 annual value of taxable supplies in any 12-month period under section 8 of the Value Added Tax Act 2014

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Value Added Tax Act 2014 applies to goods exported from Vanuatu and to services supplied to non-residents where the benefit is enjoyed outside Vanuatu. The supplier must hold documentary proof that the goods physically left Vanuatu within the prescribed period (ordinarily 3 months from the time of supply), and for services, that the recipient is a non-resident and the service is not consumed in Vanuatu. An International Company that entirely sells oversized furniture and home decor through online platforms to foreign customers can zero-rate those export sales, provided the statutory invoices and export evidence are maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Vanuatu does not mandate real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance. VAT invoices must contain the prescribed particulars set out in the VAT rules and be retained for at least 7 years. Electronic records and returns are accepted through DCIR’s digital filing platform, but there is no API-based live tax clearance, digital stamping, or continuous validation requirement.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims attract a clawback of VAT at the standard rate, an additional penalty of up to 100% of the tax under-claimed, and interest from the date of the improper claim. Where the DCIR finds wilful evasion or false documentation, fines of up to VT 2,000,000 and imprisonment may be imposed under the VAT Act 2014 and the Revenue Authority Administration Act. Exporters are subject to periodic DCIR verification of customs declarations, bills of lading, and foreign remittance evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) is pegged to the SDR basket; current-account and capital-account convertibility are formally unrestricted for International Companies. There are no foreign-exchange licences, no capital-inflow approvals, and no restrictions on dividend, loan-interest, fee, or disposal-proceeds repatriation. The operational hurdle is convertibility liquidity: VUV has a shallow interbank market and no global clearing network, so the treasury should denominate in USD, AUD, or EUR. Profit remittance requires only a board resolution and, where requested by the clearing bank, a management or audited financial statement confirming distributable profits; Vanuatu levies zero withholding tax on outgoing remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Vanuatu maintains no statutory requirement for an International Company to repatriate or surrender export proceeds. The exchange-control exemption under the International Companies Act means the entity may freely retain, convert, or transmit export earnings in any currency. No conversion ratio, statutory timeline, or mandatory surrender obligation applies to the online retail of oversized furniture and home decor; the only residual obligation is documentary reconciliation of export proceeds against customs declarations for statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) – the statutory central bank and foreign-exchange regulator under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [CAP 271]; offshore International Companies (ICs) are contractually exempt from the RBV exchange-control regime under the International Companies Act [CAP 222].

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 from the UBO's home bank to the IC's nominated USD account at ANZ or AUD account at BSP Port Vila. Recommended SWIFT purpose codes: 'BE08 – Capital Contribution' for equity injections and 'GDDS – Intra-Group Transfer' for shareholder loans, always accompanied by a signed capital contribution or loan agreement. Because the IC is exchange-control-exempt, no RBV approval or exchange information form is required; the receiving bank's verification package includes the board resolution approving issuance, the share-registry entry, a source-of-wealth questionnaire, and a signed capital contribution agreement. To reduce clearing friction, each injection should exceed USD 50,000, use the full remitter legal entity name, and reference 'VU_IC capital injection'; personal-savings narrative descriptors must be avoided as they trigger AML review.Settlement rails are USD and AUD denominated to avoid VUV illiquidity. Supplier payments to furniture manufacturers in Asia are executed via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'SUPP – Payment for Imported Goods' matched against proforma invoices and packing lists; larger inventory batches use irrevocable at-sight or 60-day documentary Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) to mitigate shipment risk. Freight and oversized-logistics disbursements for ocean roll-on/roll-off, full-container transport, and last-mile white-glove delivery are paid via MT103 to freight-forwarder collection accounts with reference to the bill of lading. Intra-group liquidity between the Vanuatu IC and Hong Kong/Singapore distribution entities is moved via MT103 with purpose code 'INTC – Intra-Group Payments' and signed intercompany agreements retained on file. All disbursements tolerate a one-to-two-business-day clearing delay through the Port Vila correspondent corridors; a pre-funded USD working-capital buffer equal to 15 days of cost of goods sold is recommended for time-critical shipping windows.USD/VUV non-deliverable forward (NDF) executed offshore – permitted because Vanuatu imposes no exchange-control approval on ICs and VUV is non-deliverable outside Vanuatu; used to lock VUV costs on local agent fees and Port Vila freight handling.AUD/USD deliverable forward or NDF – hedges AUD operating balances at BSP when settling Australian logistics subcontractors.EUR/USD outright forward – hedges European supplier invoices routed through EU payment aggregators.USD/AUD cross-currency swap – manages treasury efficiency between the IC's USD settlement account and AUD-denominated local operating costs.Plain-vanilla matched FX spot transactions – a non-derivative treasury alternative executed under the board-approved derivatives policy; speculative positions are strictly prohibited for the Vanuatu IC treasury mandate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None — Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing legislation and no statutory Local File or Master File threshold in VUV. Defensive OECD-aligned transfer-pricing documentation is nevertheless advisable when the VU IC transacts with affiliates in high-tax countries and the consolidated group exceeds a foreign audit threshold such as AUD 50 million in Australia or EUR 750 million in the European Union.

Economic substance scope: The Vanuatu Economic Substance Regulations, administered by the VFSC, apply to relevant entities carrying on geographically mobile relevant activities, including distribution and service-centre activity. An IC engaged in online retail of oversized furniture that claims tax residence in Vanuatu must prove: board meetings held physically in Vanuatu, a local registered office, adequate local employees and premises, and core income-generating activities directed from Vanuatu. An IC that is managed and controlled from abroad is treated as non-resident and excluded from ESR reporting, but such management-and-control abroad exposes shareholders to foreign CFC and permanent establishment recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authorities may reattribute 100% of the e-commerce profit to the jurisdiction of effective management, with reassessment interest, penalties of 25% to 50% of the underpaid tax, and denial of input-credit or export zero-rating claims

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false certificates of offshore control face personal assessment for penalty debts, court-ordered travel bans, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion in the jurisdiction where effective control is located
Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: VFSC warning letter, financial penalty of up to VUV 5,000,000, compulsory disclosure of all foreign activities, and deregistration of the IC from the VU registry

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under s.111 of the IC Act; directors and the registered agent become jointly liable for unpaid default fees, civil restitution of misappropriated profits, and disqualification from managing any VFSC-regulated company
Shell Company / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Vanuatu AML/CFT Act 2020 and the Penal Code: fines up to VUV 100,000,000, imprisonment up to 20 years on conviction, VFSC strike-off, and seizure of funds held by the registered agent

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, asset-freezing orders, a 10-year prohibition on acting as officers, and denial of entry or exit by court order during investigation
Failure to File Annual Return / Pay Annual Fee

Statutory sanction: Monthly default fee of VUV 5,000 compounded, followed by VFSC strike-off; the IC loses legal capacity and its assets vest in the Public Revenue

Director personal liability: Directors cannot enforce contracts on behalf of a struck-off company, become personally liable for post-strike-off trading debts, and are prohibited from acting as directors of any Vanuatu company without a court restoration order
VAT and Indirect Tax Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Default assessment, penalty of 25% of the tax evaded, monthly statutory interest, and criminal fines with imprisonment up to 5 years for deliberate false export declarations

Director personal liability: The Commissioner of VAT may pierce the corporate entity where the IC is a mere facade; directors face joint liability for the VAT debt, mandatory departure prohibition orders issued by the Minister of Finance, and disqualification orders
