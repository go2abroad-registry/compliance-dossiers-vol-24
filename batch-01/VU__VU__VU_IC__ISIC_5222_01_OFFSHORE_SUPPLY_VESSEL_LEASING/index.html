Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Customs and Inland Revenue, Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila; customs and VAT e-filing through ASYCUDA World and VRC's electronic services portal; corporate onboarding through the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Vanuatu International Company (IC) engaged in offshore supply vessel leasing: (1) engage a licensed VFSC registered agent to incorporate the IC and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Non-Residence, and tax exemption endorsement; (2) no VRC TIN is required for purely offshore activities, but if bank or charterer requires a TIN, file the VRC TIN/VAT registration form in the name of the IC, attaching the VFSC certificate, constitutional documents, registered office address, beneficial-ownership declaration, and registered agent letter; (3) state on the form that the IC is a non-resident, tax-exempt entity with no Vanuatu-source taxable activity; VRC assigns a TIN within 10 business days after validated lodgment; (4) separately apply through ASYCUDA World for a Customs Exporter Code to lodge export declarations; and (5) maintain annual VFSC licensing fees to preserve the exempt status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election is available or necessary under Vanuatu law. The IC's classification is fixed by statute as a non-resident tax-exempt corporation outside the Vanuatu VAT and income tax net. An IC may elect the precise scope of its offshore charter activities in its constitutional documents and may obtain a formal Tax Exemption Certificate from VRC; conversion to a domestic company would automatically subject it to Vanuatu domestic tax and VAT rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration threshold: VUV 10,000,000 annual taxable turnover in Vanuatu; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. An IC with no Vanuatu-source taxable supplies cannot be compulsorily registered; a non-resident VAT/TIN registration may be made only for administrative or counter-party facilitation purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when actually exported from Vanuatu and the supplier retains proof of export. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Vanuatu at the time of performance and the benefit is enjoyed entirely outside Vanuatu. For ISIC 5222 offshore supply vessel leasing, zero-rating applies where the charter-party is with a non-resident, the vessel is stationed or navigates outside Vanuatu territorial waters, and any call at a Vanuatu port is limited to bunkering, stores, or force majeure without loading or discharging cargo for Vanuatu. Intrastate or inter-island use in Vanuatu would be taxable at 12.5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged in ASYCUDA World
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Original Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or vessel charter-party
  • Master's final voyage report and GPS track evidencing offshore operation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit advice evidencing payment

Vanuatu currently imposes no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API submission mandate for VAT invoices. VAT invoices must be sequential and contain supplier TIN, invoice date, description, consideration, and VAT entry. Customs declarations must be lodged electronically through ASYCUDA World; no additional machine-readable fiscal signature is required.

VRC may audit zero-rated export claims within the applicable statutory assessment period. Incomplete documentation reverses zero-rating, making output VAT payable at 12.5%, with interest and statutory penalties; input VAT credits previously refunded on the export are clawed back immediately. Fraudulent export declarations, false charter-parties, or false evidence of non-residence attract criminal fines and possible imprisonment under the VAT and Customs Acts, potential three-times-value customs penalties, cancellation of the IC tax-exemption certificate, and deregistration by VFSC.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains full current-account convertibility and an open capital account for International Companies. There are no statutory prohibitions on inward equity contributions, offshore borrowings, or outward profit, dividend, or charter-hire remittances, and the Vatu (VUV) is freely usable within the domestic banking system. Practical hurdles arise not from exchange-control legislation but from the shallowness of VUV/USD dealing: USD clearing for VUV is intermediated through regional correspondent banks, non-USD settlement windows are thin, and profit remittances require the IC to demonstrate genuine economic substance so that the RBV-licensed bank does not reclassify the transaction as suspicious. Dividend remittances are tax-free under the International Companies Act, but the paying bank will require a current Certificate of Good Standing, a life-certificate of the directors and ultimate beneficial owners, and an APG/CFATF-style compliance declaration to evidence that the entity is not a de-risked shell.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Vanuatu imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign-currency export or vessel-leasing receipts, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation. A Vanuatu International Company may retain charter-hire income indefinitely in USD or any permitted foreign currency in its offshore or domestic accounts, subject only to the RBV's standard reporting thresholds for suspicious transaction monitoring and the bank's internal commercial credit policies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), supported by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) for International Company licensing and the Vanuatu Maritime Services Limited (VMSL) for vessel registration and maritime supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Vanuatu has no domestic Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category; an offshore digital EMI must separately hold an International Banking Licence from the VFSC under the International Banking Act. Such an entity is prohibited from taking VUV deposits or providing settlement accounts to a Vanuatu IC without an RBV banking licence and is practically de-risked by global correspondent banks because of the offshore-marine chartering and offshore supply vessel (OSV) exposure of this account profile. The use of an unlicensed offshore EMI for treasury flows should be treated as an automatic compliance red flag and is inadvisable for capital escrow or operational disbursement functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections must route via SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's bank into the Vanuatu International Company's designated capital escrow account at a licensed Vanuatu commercial bank. The intended payment purpose code should be the equity / capital contribution code recognised by the receiving bank (commonly '310' or the bank-specific 'EQUITY' business code), while charter-related funds should be coded as 'SI' (ship and air transport services) or 'FS' (operational leasing) where permitted; a generic 'INTR' or missing code will be rejected or suspended by compliance. Each wiring instruction must be accompanied by a board resolution approving the capital call, a subscribed share schedule, the subscription agreement, and source-of-wealth documentation for the transferring entity. The bank will also require a current VFSC agent confirmation, the VMSL shipping licence or certificate for the vessel subject to the OSV leasing scope, and matching legal beneficiary name on the account. Funds must be fully cleared and verified against the sanctioned-party list before any deployment, and any third-party same-day onward transfer will trigger enhanced due diligence.Trade disbursements for offshore supply vessel acquisition, charterer deposits, and operational costs will move over three clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for operational charter-hire payments, crew wages, P&I insurance premiums, and bunker settlement, with MT202 COV for interbank reimbursement legs; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT720 for vessel purchase, dry-dock instalments, and equipment import, with presentation of bills of lading, commercial invoices, class certificates, and survey reports at the counters of the confirming bank; and (iii) maritime escrow release instructions executed conditionally upon vessel redelivery certificates, bunker survey reconciliation, or class-survey approval. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in Vatu. Vanuatu has no corporate income tax and therefore no statutory Local File or Master File transfer pricing documentation thresholds. The arm's-length principle is nevertheless relevant for foreign counterparties seeking deductibility of charter hire or fees paid to the Vanuatu IC in their own jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The International Companies (Amendment) Act 2019 and the Vanuatu Economic Substance Regulations require every IC conducting a 'relevant activity' to maintain economic substance in Vanuatu. Vessel chartering and offshore marine transport services (ISIC 5222) fall within the 'shipping' and 'transport' relevant activity categories. The IC must annually demonstrate: (a) management and control exercised in Vanuatu; (b) adequate physical premises and employees in Vanuatu proportionate to the vessel chartering activity; (c) operating expenditure incurred in Vanuatu; and (d) core income-generating activities performed in Vanuatu, including crew employment, vessel maintenance decision-making, and charter negotiation. Annual ESR notification and the retention of contemporaneous substance files are mandatory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Vanuatu's Penal Code [Cap. 135] for fraud and perjury; fines up to VT 100 million or asset confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime Act; international exchange of information under the Tax Information Exchange Agreements (TIEA) network may expose the underlying beneficial owner to foreign criminal tax proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, civil restitution orders, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 7 years for knowingly authorising false declarations.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations penalties: initial fine of VT 1,000,000, escalating to VT 5,000,000 for continued non-compliance; automatic information exchange to the foreign parent jurisdiction; revocation of the IC's certificate of incorporation; strike-off from the VFSC register.

Director personal liability: Statutory directors are personally disqualified from acting as directors for up to 5 years, subjected to restoration surcharges, and held jointly and severally liable for the company's ESR penalties and unpaid charges.
AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Enforcement action under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act [Cap. 288]: fines of VT 5,000,000 per violation; forfeiture of the escrowed funds; freezing orders on all Vanuatu bank accounts; revocation of the IC's registered agent services leading to deregistration.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines, travel bans, confiscation of personal assets, and imprisonment for up to 10 years for wilful AML non-compliance or money laundering.
Prohibited Domestic Business

Statutory sanction: The Vanuatu International Companies Act renders the offending contract void; the IC is subject to deregistration, a penalty of VT 10,000 per prohibited transaction, and potential civil suits by counterparties.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the prohibited domestic business are personally liable to indemnify the injured counterparty, face exclusion from reincorporation for 3 years, and may be compelled to pay the company's penalties out of personal funds.
