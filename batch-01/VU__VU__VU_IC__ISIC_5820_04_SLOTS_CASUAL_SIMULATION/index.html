Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Authority (VRCA) — https://www.vrca.gov.vu — responsible solely for customs and excise administration; Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) — https://www.vfsc.vu — statutory regulator for International Company incorporation, annual returns, and prescribed fees. No national income-tax administration exists because Vanuatu levies no income, corporate, capital-gains, or inheritance tax.

E-government filing portal

No TIN or VAT identification number exists in Vanuatu law. A foreign-owned entity registers as an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] through a VFSC-licensed Corporate Service Provider by lodging the memorandum and articles of association, registered-agent consent, registered-office address, and particulars of directors and shareholders. The VFSC then issues the Certificate of Incorporation and a unique Company Number, which is the sole statutory identifier. Post-incorporation obligations are: (a) maintain a registered agent and registered office in Vanuatu; (b) file an annual return on each anniversary date with the VFSC; and (c) pay the prescribed annual government fee. No tax registration, tax clearance, or fiscal identification is required for the IC to invoice foreign counterparties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None applicable. Vanuatu has no check-the-box, pass-through election, CFC attribution, or hybrid-mismatch rules. Tax exemption is mandatory and automatic under section 89 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222], which exempts the IC from all present and future Vanuatu taxes, duties, and stamp duties on its income, shares, securities, and operations. The IC may not elect to be taxable. A shareholder's home jurisdiction may independently classify the IC as a controlled foreign corporation or look-through entity, and may apply its own hybrid-mismatch or anti-deferral rules under foreign domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Vanuatu imposes no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax. The Value Added Tax Act No. 26 of 1998 (12.5% standard rate) was repealed effective 1 July 2018. Indirect levies are limited to customs duties on physical imports and a narrowly scoped fiscal stamp duty on specified domestic instruments; neither applies to exported digital software services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no indirect-tax registration threshold exists; there is no VAT/GST statute, no turnover threshold, and no de minimis supply rule for digital services. The IC's sale of simulated social casino software through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing is entirely outside Vanuatu's indirect-tax perimeter.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — with no VAT regime, there is no 0% export rate to claim and no VAT refund to substantiate. Exportation of software-publishing services by the IC is simply outside Vanuatu revenue jurisdiction. The International Companies Act prohibits ICs from trading with Vanuatu residents, which further confirms the wholly offshore export nexus. Should the IC ever ship physical promotional merchandise, no Vanuatu export duty applies to the majority of goods and no refund mechanism is triggered because no indirect tax is collected. The 0-day value of the repatriation deadline reflects the complete absence of exchange-control or mandatory-repatriation legislation in Vanuatu; export proceeds may remain offshore indefinitely.

Not applicable — Vanuatu has no electronic-invoicing clearance system, no real-time fiscal API, and no digital stamping mandate. The VRCA operates ASYCUDA World solely for customs declarations. The IC may issue ordinary commercial invoices to Apple Inc. and Google LLC without any Vanuatu fiscal format, QR coding, or clearance step.

Vanuatu law provides no VAT or income-tax clawback mechanism, so export zero-rating fraud is legally impossible. Enforcement exposure is limited to: (i) VFSC administrative penalties and late fees for failure to file the annual return; (ii) striking-off of the IC and forfeiture of its property to the Government if annual fees remain unpaid; (iii) criminal liability for false statements or forgery in documents lodged with the VFSC, punishable by fine or imprisonment under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]; and (iv) civil liability to third parties for misrepresentation. Foreign tax claims against the IC would be adjudicated under the relevant foreign taxing statute, not under Vanuatu law.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and maintains a fully liberalized exchange system for both current and capital account transactions. Foreign exchange is freely obtainable from authorized dealers (commercial banks) for bona fide trade, services, investments, and profit remittances. There are no prior RBV approval requirements for current international transfers, dividend distributions, or capital repatriations by an International Company, provided the remitting bank has completed standard source-of-funds and AML/CTF checks. The VUV is convertible to all major currencies (USD, AUD, EUR, GBP, NZD) at market-determined rates, though onshore liquidity for minor currencies may be limited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu does not impose any statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement. An International Company (IC) may retain export or service revenue in any freely convertible currency in offshore or onshore accounts without time limits or conversion obligations. The only applicable duties are record-keeping obligations under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 2002 and the AML/CTF Regulations, requiring transaction documentation and customer due diligence records to be maintained for at least six years.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV); the RBV administers the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 100], issues the national currency (Vatu, VUV), manages foreign exchange reserves, and supervises licensed banks. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) regulates the International Company registry and non-bank financial services.

Stripe Fit: High

Most established EMIs decline Vanuatu-registered corporate accounts due to increased country risk, beneficial ownership opacity, and the gaming-adjacent business model. Some EMI products may be available for payment collection (e.g., Stripe, PayPal) but are subject to contractual gambling restrictions and risk-based underwriting. Legal constraints include the need for the EMI to hold a valid e-money license in its home jurisdiction, cross-border KYC/AML equivalency, and compliance with VFSC anti-money-laundering expectations if the IC is a higher-risk customer. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into a Vanuatu International Company should be routed via SWIFT MT103 transfer from the shareholder's bank to the company's USD (or VUV) account at a tier-1 Vanuatu commercial bank. The payment narrative must clearly state the purpose as 'Equity capital contribution to VU_IC' or 'Shareholder loan facility to VU_IC'. No prior RBV approval is required for inbound foreign investment; however, the receiving bank will apply its domestic transaction reporting threshold (typically USD 10,000 equivalent) and may request supporting source-of-funds documents such as the share subscription agreement, a board resolution authorizing capital increase, and a certificate of deposit for the beneficiary account. For amounts above USD 1,000,000, banks may require enhanced due diligence including a written explanation of the economic substance and a signed declaration from the director.Approved clearing rails for trade settlements and operational disbursements include: (1) SWIFT MT103 for standard vendor payments, licensing fees, and intercompany transfers; (2) SWIFT MT202 COV for interbank buy-funds transfers when moving liquidity between banks; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary L/C) for physical goods-based software distribution or hardware purchases, using ICC UCP 600 rules; (4) Documentary Collections (URC 522) for low-to-moderate value transactions with trusted counterparties. For digital-content revenues from Apple and Google, funds are settled automatically via ACH/SEPA/direct deposit into the company's nominated bank account; no separate clearing rail is needed. Legal constraints include the need for the EMI to hold a valid e-money license in its home jurisdiction, cross-border KYC/AML equivalency, and compliance with VFSC anti-money-laundering expectations if the IC is a higher-risk customer. For treasury resilience, the IC should rely primarily on tier-1 onshore commercial banks and use EMIs only as secondary settlement channels, not as primary repositories of liquidity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on foreign-source profits of an International Company (IC); a 10% Corporate Income Tax rate applies exclusively to profits derived from onshore/Vanuatu-source business activity under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 (effective 1 January 2025) and does not affect exempt ICs that refrain from domestic business.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% domestic withholding tax on dividends paid by a Vanuatu International Company to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Vanuatu does not yet prescribe a statutory Local File or Master File threshold in Vatu; the prudent compliance threshold is VT 10,000,000 (approximately USD 82,000) of annual cross-border controlled transactions, above which contemporaneous master file, local file, and intercompany agreements aligned with OECD transfer pricing guidance should be maintained.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Requirements apply through the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019. The relevant activities are banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service-centre business, and holding-company business. A pure publisher of simulated social casino and virtual slots content under ISIC 5820, monetised solely through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, is not itself a scheduled relevant activity. However, any arrangement with a Vanuatu distribution or service-centre function, in-country operating staff, or a Vanuatu IP holding and licensing entity will invoke the ESR substance test: local board management, adequate physical premises, proportionate staff, and operating expenditure commensurate with the relevant income. Vanuatu remains subject to EU monitoring for tax transparency, and failure of ESR reporting can lead to denial of the tax-exempt status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2019: fines per offence of up to VT 10,000,000 and daily default penalties of up to VT 1,000,000 for continuing contravention; the VFSC may strike off the company, impose reporting restrictions, and refer the matter for prosecution under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2016.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and beneficial owners may be held jointly and severally liable for recoverable penalties and taxes, subject to civil recovery orders, asset freezes, travel bans, disqualification orders, and imprisonment for up to 12 years.
Tax Evasion / Prohibited Domestic Activity

Statutory sanction: Loss of the IC zero-tax exemption with retrospective assessment of profits; Value Added Tax Act assessments at 12.5% with penalty interest of up to 200% of the under-declared amount; and criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines up to VT 15,000,000.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil: directors, officers, and the offshore principal face personal liability for assessed amounts, restitution of concealed profits, forfeiture of foreign assets, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.
AML/CFT Deficiency and Unlicensed Payment Intermediation

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act enforcement: corporate penalties up to VT 20,000,000 for each violation, forfeiture and confiscation of unlawful proceeds, revocation of the IC registration, and prohibition from holding a VFSC licence; serious breaches are referred to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Executives and ultimate beneficial owners face personal criminal fines up to VT 5,000,000, disqualification, travel bans, confiscation of personal property, and imprisonment for up to 10 years for money laundering or knowingly operating without the required authorisation.
