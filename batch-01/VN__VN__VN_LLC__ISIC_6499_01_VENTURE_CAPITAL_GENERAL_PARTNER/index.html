Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No general statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a limited liability company under the Law on Enterprises 2020. Founding members must fully contribute their subscribed capital within 90 days from the date of issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế - GDT) of the Ministry of Finance, Socialist Republic of Viet Nam. Primary digital channels: the unified national electronic tax portal Etaxvn (https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn), the e-invoice portal (https://hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn), and the National Business Registration Portal (https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn). The GDT also operates the dedicated foreign-supplier tax portal for cross-border digital/e-commerce suppliers without a Vietnamese establishment, which became the mandatory registration and filing channel on 1 May 2025 under Law No. 56/2024/QH15 amending the Law on Tax Administration. All VAT, CIT, license-fee, and e-invoice administration is exercised at the national level; there are no provincial or municipal VAT regimes.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Investment registration: the foreign-invested LLC must first obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) or, for export-processing enterprise (EPE) projects, from the relevant Export Processing and Industrial Zone Authority. Step 2 - Enterprise registration: file the dossier on the National Business Registration Portal to obtain the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); under the one-stop-shop mechanism (Articles 30 and 34, Law on Tax Administration No. 38/2019/QH14), the statutory tax identification number (Mã số thuế - MST) is granted automatically and simultaneously with the ERC, so no separate tax-registration application is required. Step 3 - Electronic account activation: activate the Etaxvn tax account using a recognized legal-representative digital signature certificate (USB token or certified e-signature), register the LLC's bank accounts, and enroll in the e-invoice, VAT, CIT, and license-fee (môn bài) filing calendars. Step 4 - Sectoral licensing: because the scope ISIC 6499 requires a statutory license for fund management activity, the relevant State Securities Commission license or concession must be in place before fee-earning services commence; the MST remains the single integrated identifier for all tax accounts. Step 5 - Withholding registration: register the LLC's obligations to withhold foreign contractor tax and personal income tax on payments to non-resident advisers and employees. Where the VC fund itself is a non-resident vehicle with no Vietnamese permanent establishment, no separate VAT ID is required for the fund; the GP invoices under its own MST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Viet Nam does not operate a US-style check-the-box or partnership pass-through election; the LLC is per se a resident entity-level CIT taxpayer at the standard 20% rate. Available statutory elections include: (i) investment-incentive elections - CIT exemption (up to 2 years) and 50% reduction (up to 4 years), or a 10% preferential CIT rate, for qualifying projects under the Law on Investment and Decree 218/2013/ND-CP; (ii) loss carry-forward - CIT losses are carried forward up to 5 consecutive years with no carry-back, applied by the taxpayer in the return rather than by separate filing; (iii) science and technology fund election - appropriation of up to 10% of taxable income to the Technology Development Fund is deductible within statutory limits; (iv) transfer-pricing certainty election - voluntary application for a unilateral or bilateral Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) under Circular 45/2021/TT-BTC; (v) e-invoice modality election - coded e-invoices cleared in real time by the GDT versus non-coded e-invoices transmitted within 24 hours of issuance; and (vi) tax-deferral and installment-payment elections for qualifying restructuring or force-majeure circumstances under the Tax Administration Law. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable; Decree 132/2020/ND-CP applies transfer-pricing documentation rules and a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) that may recharacterize hybrid instrument returns or interposed offshore fund structures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam operates a bifurcated convertibility regime. Current-account transactions are generally freely convertible: cross-border payments for services, management fees, advisory fees, royalties, profit remittances, and other current items may be executed through licensed banks with only documentary verification and SBV balance-of-payments reporting; no prior SBV approval is required for recurring profit remittances once the Corporate Income Tax finalization and audited accounts are completed. Capital-account transactions are controlled and require prior registration or approval: inbound charter capital contributions must be effected through a registered Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at a licensed Vietnamese commercial bank; offshore shareholder loans must be registered with the SBV before drawdown under Circular No. 15/2015/TT-NHNN and related foreign-loan regulations; outbound investments by the Venture Capital General Partner require an Investment Registration Certificate / Decision on Outbound Investment under the Law on Investment No. 61/2020/QH14. Profit remittance hurdles are practical rather than approval-based: the remitting bank requires the annual audited financial statements, CIT finalization dossier, tax clearance documents, shareholders' resolution on distribution, and a board resolution on dividend/management-fee remittance. Capital repatriation upon dissolution or partial withdrawal is permitted only after full settlement of Vietnamese tax and commercial liabilities, completion of liquidation procedures, and bank verification of the liquidation plan and audited accounts. The regime therefore allows full convertibility of current earnings but strictly gates capital movements through DICA mechanics, SBV loan registration, and outbound-investment licensing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vietnam currently imposes no mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds; exporters may retain 100% of foreign-currency export earnings in foreign currency accounts without forced sale to the banking system. However, statutory repatriation applies: all export proceeds must be received into a foreign-currency account held with a licensed credit institution in Vietnam within 15 days from the date the proceeds are credited to an account abroad (or from the contractual payment due date in cases specified by the SBV), in accordance with Circular No. 15/2015/TT-NHNN and the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange. Temporary retention offshore beyond 15 days is permitted only under SBV-approved exceptions, such as payment of costs directly related to the export transaction or repayment of foreign loans, and must be substantiated to the supervising bank. For the Venture Capital General Partner (ISIC 6499), export-proceeds surrender rules are not the primary operational mode; nevertheless, any management fees, fund-mandate advisory fees, milestone payments, or co-investment carried-interest related service income invoiced to offshore funds constitute service-export current-account receipts and must be repatriated to Vietnam within the same 15-day window and credited to the company's operational foreign-currency account or DICA as applicable. The bank will require the underlying service agreement, tax invoice, SWIFT credit advice, and a service-performance certificate for SBV reporting. No mandatory surrender or conversion to VND is currently in force.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam, 'SBV') - the sole central bank and foreign exchange administration authority of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam. FX policy is administered by the SBV Foreign Exchange Administration Department under the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange No. 06/2013/UBTVQH13 (as amended and supplemented), Decree No. 70/2014/ND-CP detailing the ordinance, Circular No. 15/2015/TT-NHNN governing foreign exchange control for foreign direct investment activities, and Circular No. 02/2016/TT-NHNN governing foreign exchange derivatives. All licensed commercial banks must report foreign exchange transactions to the SBV through the balance-of-payments and foreign-exchange supervision reporting system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary statutory clearing rail; MEDIUM only as a supplementary collection-and-distribution overlay that immediately settles into a licensed onshore bank account. Legal constraints: (i) offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer, and Neat are not licensed credit institutions in Vietnam and cannot hold a Vietnamese DICA nor open domiciled VND transaction accounts; (ii) any cross-border payment intermediary service acting for Vietnamese-resident customers requires an SBV intermediary payment service licence under Decree No. 101/2012/ND-CP (as amended by Decree No. 80/2016/ND-CP), and an unlicensed offshore EMI is therefore prohibited from actively marketing or providing domestic VND-corridor services; (iii) statutory capital contributions, shareholder loans, and outbound-investment disbursements must route exclusively through a DICA at a licensed Vietnamese commercial bank, so an EMI cannot satisfy capital-account compliance; (iv) fund balances held by an offshore EMI are commingled with the EMI's operational treasury and are not protected by Vietnamese deposit insurance; (v) the GP may lawfully use an offshore EMI only as a payment-acceptance convenience for offshore limited partners, with simultaneous onward SWIFT MT103 settlement into the Vietnamese licensed bank, and must retain the EMI's settlement statements, underlying invoices, and LP capital-call notices as evidence for the receiving bank's AML/KYC file. The practical recommendation is to treat the EMI as a non-account collection portal and not as the bank of record for capital-account or liquidity-clearing purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from offshore investors into the Vietnamese LLC must be settled through the licensed bank via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for intermediary-value moves), with the ordering customer being the legal investor and the beneficiary being the LLC. The payment-reference/purpose field must read as: 'Capital contribution - [investor legal name] - [Enterprise Code]/[IRC No.] - DICA'. The receiving bank will apply the credit to the LLC's Direct Investment Capital Account only after verification of: (i) the IRC and Enterprise Registration Certificate; (ii) the capital-contribution schedule annexed to the investment constitution; (iii) a board resolution approving the capital call; and (iv) investor identification. The transaction is then reported by the bank to the SBV in the foreign-exchange supervision and balance-of-payments portal. If the inbound remittance is an offshore shareholder loan to the GP rather than equity, the loan agreement must be registered with the SBV under Circular No. 15/2015/TT-NHNN and the foreign-loan registration regime before drawdown, and the wire must cite the SBV loan-registration code. If the inbound remittance is a management fee or advisory fee earned from an offshore fund, it is a current-account receipt and may credit an operational account rather than the DICA, supported by the service agreement, tax invoice, and GP's relevant tax filings. All inbound FX can be retained in USD if the bank permits a USD operational account, or converted into VND at the bank's quoted rate with an MT300 confirmation. The GP should also obtain a Foreign Investor Certificate/registration where required by securities law or by the bank's own domestic institutional policy, and must maintain a DICA-reconciliation workbook matching every inbound SWIFT credit advice to the capital-call schedule.The primary clearing rail for outbound venture-capital disbursements is SWIFT MT103 executed via the licensed local bank from the LLC's operational or designated investment account. For investments into a Vietnamese portfolio company, the bank executes a local settlement: FX conversion (if required) plus a domestic IA/WA transfer credited to the target's DICA or operational account. For outbound investments into offshore SPVs or portfolio companies abroad, the GP must first obtain a Decision on Outbound Investment / amended IRC under the Law on Investment No. 61/2020/QH14 and complete the SBV outward-investment registration; the MT103 reference must identify the recipient, the investment agreement date and number, and the total disbursement. Supporting documentation for the bank file: executed share-purchase or subscription agreement, board resolution approving the deployment, valuation/cap-table substantiation, investor-side commitment letter, and, for loan disbursements to portfolio companies, the loan agreement and interest-payment schedule. A Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary LC under UCP 600) is a permissible clearing instrument but is not the standard rail for equity deployment; it may be used only where a portfolio company's trade settlement involves document-based goods transactions. All outbound disbursements must be recorded in the SBV foreign-exchange supervision report and tagged with the correct outward direct investment or operating-payment purpose code; the GP must retain the full transaction file for audit and for the annual foreign-exchange inspection by the SBV. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary statutory clearing rail; MEDIUM only as a supplementary collection-and-distribution overlay that immediately settles into a licensed onshore bank account. Legal constraints: (i) offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer, and Neat are not licensed credit institutions in Vietnam and cannot hold a Vietnamese DICA nor open domiciled VND transaction accounts; (ii) any cross-border payment intermediary service acting for Vietnamese-resident customers requires an SBV intermediary payment service licence under Decree No. 101/2012/ND-CP (as amended by Decree No. 80/2016/ND-CP), and an unlicensed offshore EMI is therefore prohibited from actively marketing or providing domestic VND-corridor services; (iii) statutory capital contributions, shareholder loans, and outbound-investment disbursements must route exclusively through a DICA at a licensed Vietnamese commercial bank, so an EMI cannot satisfy capital-account compliance; (iv) fund balances held by an offshore EMI are commingled with the EMI's operational treasury and are not protected by Vietnamese deposit insurance; (v) the GP may lawfully use an offshore EMI only as a payment-acceptance convenience for offshore limited partners, with simultaneous onward SWIFT MT103 settlement into the Vietnamese licensed bank, and must retain the EMI's settlement statements, underlying invoices, and LP capital-call notices as evidence for the receiving bank's AML/KYC file. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% standard Corporate Income Tax (CIT) rate as set by the Vietnam CIT Law (Law No. 14/2008/QH12, as amended by Law No. 32/2013/QH13, Decree No. 218/2013/ND-CP)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid by a Vietnamese limited liability company to a foreign corporate shareholder. Vietnamese domestic law does not impose a withholding tax on after-tax profit distributions to foreign corporate investors; the profit remittance tax of the former foreign investment law was abolished with effect from 1 July 2015. Individual foreign shareholders, however, are subject to a 5% personal income tax on dividend income collected through the LLC's withholding obligation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly provisional returns and payments are due no later than the 30th day after the end of each quarter (30 April, 30 July, 30 October, 30 January). The annual finalization return and full settlement of any remaining CIT balance are due within 90 days after the end of the fiscal year, i.e., no later than 31 March for a calendar-year taxpayer. Quarterly provisional payments must in aggregate reach at least 80% of the final annual CIT liability; any shortfall below that threshold is subject to late-payment interest of 0.03% per day from the relevant quarterly deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the annual value of related-party transactions exceeds VND 30 billion. Master File and Local File are mandatory when the related-party revenue attributable to Vietnam exceeds VND 50 billion and the ultimate parent's consolidated global revenue exceeds VND 18,000 billion. A Country-by-Country Report is required when the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue in the preceding fiscal year exceeds VND 18,000 billion. Enterprises below the documentation thresholds are still required to file Form 01/QLGN and maintain contemporaneous evidence of arm's-length pricing if they engage in related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Viet Nam does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the form used in Caribbean jurisdictions. Nevertheless, the economic substance doctrine is embedded in Decree No. 132/2020/ND-CP through the requirement that related-party transactions be priced on arm's-length substance, and in General Rule 18 of the Tax Administration Law requiring that the actual economic substance control transactions over their legal form. The Vietnamese GP of an offshore venture capital fund must be able to demonstrate local substance: a licensed office, substantive employment of fund managers, board decision-making within Vietnam, risk management, and local books and records. Decree No. 66/2025/ND-CP and Resolution No. 107/2023/QH15 implementing the OECD Global Minimum Tax impose a 15% top-up tax on multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more from fiscal year 2024; the GP is expected to reflect local substance in determining where economic profits are created. A GP operating as a shell with no Vietnamese substance will face re-characterization, denial of treaty benefits, and transfer pricing adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Civil administrative penalty of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax amount under Article 107 of Law No. 38/2019/QH14. Criminal prosecution under Articles 200, 201, and 202 of the Penal Code No. 100/2015/QH13 (as amended): legal entities may be fined up to VND 20 billion, suspended from operations for up to 36 months, prohibited from raising capital and issuing securities, and ordered to forfeit assets. All evaded tax, late-payment interest at 0.03% per day, and fines are collectible through seizure of bank accounts and assets.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, CFO, and chief accountant can be personally criminally liable for corporate tax evasion. Individual sentences range from fines and prohibition from holding office to imprisonment of up to 20 years for aggravated evasion exceeding VND 4.5 billion. Directors who sign false CIT returns or approve fake invoices are subject to travel bans (temporary exit suspension), administrative summons, and disqualification from corporate management. Under Article 13 of the Law on Enterprise No. 59/2020/QH14, directors who cause tax losses through unlawful declarations must compensate the LLC in full.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the legal form, disallow intercompany deductions, re-determine taxable income at a 20% CIT rate, impose a 20% under-declaration penalty on any shortage, and charge 0.03% per day interest. Failure to prepare or submit Local File, Master File, or CbC Report is separately sanctionable by administrative fines up to VND 25 million under Decree No. 125/2020/ND-CP, in addition to the right of the tax office to apply ex officio TP adjustments by re-characterizing the shell entity's income as attributable to a permanent establishment in Vietnam. Under the OECD Global Minimum Tax framework, profits allocated to a substance-free shell remain exposed to top-up taxation at the parent level.

Director personal liability: Directors and nominees of the shell entity may have the corporate veil pierced: they are jointly and severally liable for the LLC's unpaid CIT, VAT, and social insurance debts. Vietnamese courts have upheld personal liability for debts incurred without genuine board approval. Directors face exit bans, freezing of personal assets, and criminal charges under Article 200 of the Penal Code if the shell is used to route undisclosed profits. Foreign directors are equally subject to extradition and mutual legal assistance under Vietnam's international cooperation treaties.
Money Laundering and AML Breaches

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Law on Prevention and Combat of Money Laundering No. 14/2022/QH12 may reach VND 5 billion for reporting failures. Criminal liability for money laundering under Article 324 of the Penal Code: individuals face imprisonment of 1 to 15 years; legal entities are fined up to VND 15 billion, forced to dissolve, or suspended for up to 36 months. Unlicensed venture capital activity additionally invokes administrative sanctions under the Law on Securities and the State Securities Commission, including fines up to VND 1.5 billion and compulsory return of investor funds.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers of the GP bear personal criminal exposure for deliberate avoidance of the AML framework; the AML Law expressly extends liability to the beneficial owner and the director responsible for the reporting obligation. Sanctions include travel bans, seizure of passports for pending investigations, personal fines, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment for up to 10 years for those organizing or materially assisting the laundering activity. Civil liability allows investors to sue directors directly for losses caused by non-compliance.

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will perform a comparability adjustment, increase the GP's taxable income to the arm's-length level, impose CIT at 20% on the adjustment, apply a 20% penalty on the under-declared tax, and charge late-payment interest of 0.03% per day. Penalties for failure to produce TP documentation are up to VND 25 million per document and up to VND 50 million for persistent refusal, without limiting the tax office's power to adjust the income on an estimated basis. For large MNEs, failure to align TP outcomes with economic substance may also trigger Global Minimum Tax top-up of up to 15% on excess profits.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve TP policies without contemporaneous benchmarking are personally liable for civil damages to the company if the subsequent tax adjustment reduces distributable reserves. In flagrant cases of TP fraud, directors are treated as accomplices to tax evasion and can be prosecuted under Article 200 of the Penal Code, with imprisonment of up to 20 years for orchestrating artificial profit stripping. Travel bans and asset freezes are routinely imposed on directors of entities under TP criminal investigation.
