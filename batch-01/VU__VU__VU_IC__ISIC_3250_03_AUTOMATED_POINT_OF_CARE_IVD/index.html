Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), operating as the Customs and Inland Revenue Department under the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official taxpayer portal and procedures administered through VRC Port Vila (e-services via the Government of Vanuatu online platform and the VRC tax office, Port Vila).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Confirm nexus — a VU International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] is non-resident and statutorily exempt from Vanuatu income tax and VAT; no tax ID is required for pure offshore activity. Step 2: If the IC establishes a physical Vanuatu presence or makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu exceeding VUV 5,000,000 in any 12-month period, it must apply to VRC for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration within 21 days. Step 3: File the standard VAT registration application (Form VAT 001) with certified constitutional documents, the VFSC Certificate of Incorporation, registered office and agent details, and a local representative/agent appointment letter. Step 4: VRC issues the TIN certificate referencing the statutory VAT number within 7–14 business days; the applicant must file a VAT return and maintain a local agent for service of process. Step 5: Foreign entities without a Vanuatu bank/tax nexus may alternatively obtain a TIN for customs purposes only when transacting physical imports/exports through Vanuatu Customs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Vanuatu domestic law. The IC enjoys an automatic, non-electable statutory tax exemption under section 8 of the International Companies Act [Cap. 222], covering all Vanuatu taxes, stamp duties, and exchange-control restrictions for non-resident operations. The only elective feature is in the IC's constitution (e.g., election of redenomination currency or share capital currency for VFSC registration purposes); no entity-classification election is required or available because the IC is deemed a non-resident exempt vehicle. A corporate 'opt-in' to local tax liability occurs only if the IC engages in prohibited domestic business in Vanuatu without a licence, in which case it may lose statutory exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover per VAT Act No. 13 of 2019 (income-tax-exempt ICs manufacturing offshore with no Vanuatu supplies remain unregistered and outside the VAT net).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods: a supply of goods is zero-rated under the VAT Act No. 13 of 2019 where the goods are physically exported from Vanuatu by or on behalf of the supplier within the prescribed period (customs-controlled export) and the supplier retains commercial and transport evidence. Offshore services: services supplied to a non-resident person are zero-rated where they are performed for use and enjoyment entirely outside Vanuatu and do not relate to land or goods in Vanuatu; supplies to international carriers engaged in international transport are also zero-rated. An exempt VU_IC manufacturing microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers offshore for foreign distributors generally has no Vanuatu VAT exposure, provided no supply is made to a Vanuatu resident and no goods physically enter Vanuatu.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Vanuatu Single Administrative Document) — proof of customs clearance at the port of export
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill — independent carrier evidence that goods left Vanuatu and were delivered to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 foreign-currency credit advice — proof of payment receipt and FX repatriation
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List — matching description, quantity, and value of the zero-rated supply

None currently in force: Vanuatu has not introduced mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping for VAT invoices. Tax invoices under the VAT Act 2019 must contain the prescribed particulars (supplier name and TIN/VAT number, customer identification where above threshold, serial number, date, description, quantity, consideration, and VAT charged), but paper/PDF issuance is accepted. VRC retains audit rights to require certified invoice summaries and performs system-based verification through the VAT return filing portal.

The VRC Commissioner is empowered under the VAT Act 2019 to audit all zero-rated export claims; where export documentation is missing, incomplete, or inconsistent, the zero-rating is retrospectively reversed, output VAT at 12.5% is clawed back with statutory interest, and input VAT credits are disallowed. Fraudulent or reckless zero-rating claims (false declarations, collusive over-invoicing, use of fake customs or bill-of-lading documents) constitute criminal offences under both the VAT Act and Customs Act, punishable on conviction with substantial fines and/or imprisonment; the Customs Act additionally permits seizure of goods, forfeiture of vehicles and vessels, and monetary penalties up to a multiple of the duty evaded, while the VAT Act imposes penalty tax (up to 100% of the tax understatement) and suspension or cancellation of the trader's VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a liberalised exchange-control regime for non-resident entities. The Vatu (VUV) is a managed-float currency administered by the RBV, with active trading through authorized dealers; the VUV is not a global offshore-deliverable currency, so substantive international transactions should be denominated in AUD, USD, EUR or NZD. For International Companies (ICs) classified as non-resident for exchange-control purposes, capital-account transactions such as equity injection and shareholder loans may be freely routed through licensed banks without prior RBV approval. Current-account payments, including trade settlement and profit remittance, are equally unrestricted for non-residents; however, all payments are subject to the delegated oversight of authorized dealers under the Exchange Control Act and to Anti-Money Laundering/Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) reporting through the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit. Vanuatu imposes no income tax, dividend withholding tax, or capital gains tax on ICs or their non-resident shareholders; therefore, profit and dividend remittances face minimal statutory tax hurdles but require robust provenance documentation to satisfy bank compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender regime. An International Company in the IVD manufacturing segment may retain all export and trade-related foreign currency proceeds in offshore or onshore foreign currency accounts without any statutory requirement to convert VUV within a prescribed timeline. The RBV Exchange Control Act delegates custody and transfer control to licensed authorized dealers; in practice, where VUV conversion is needed for local expenses, conversion is executed at prevailing market rates. The entity should, however, maintain a transaction file for each export receipt, including contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration and source-of-funds records, because banks may require such evidence under delegated exchange-control and AML/CFT reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium. Fully digital offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not authorized to provide domestic payment services to Vanuatu International Companies unless they obtain a deposit-taking or e-money license under the RBV Banking Act. Unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot open a Vanuatu settlement account nor issue a Vanuatu IBAN, and Vanuatu banks generally de-risk from fintech aggregators because of FATF and correspondent-banking sensitivity. A viable structuring solution is the use of an EMU- or UK-regulated EMI merely as a pre-fundedP payment-collection vehicle for inbound third-party customer payments from markets where fast card/SWIFT acceptance is required, while the principal treasury settlement remains with an RBV-licensed commercial bank. Legal constraints include the absence of deposit insurance for foreign corporates, restricted VUV denomiation management, and the need to pass through the responsible-persons and AML/CFT requirements under the Vanuatu Financial Institutions Supervision Framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant liquidity-injection route is a direct SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder’s audited bank account to the IC’s operational foreign-currency account at an RBV-licensed commercial bank. The purpose-of-remittance field must clearly state 'Equity contribution to Vanuatu International Company for working capital, IVD equipment trading and trade finance.' For accounting and regulatory mapping, the bank will align the post to an international balance-of-payments capital-account category; the entity should consistently record the transaction as paid-in capital or shareholder loan with a corresponding entry in its IFRS financial statements. The remitting bank will require a board resolution, the IC registration number, the UBO declaration, a certificate of good standing, and a source-of-funds letter. All inbound wires should be designated in USD, AUD, EUR, or NZD, and not in VUV, to preserve exchange-control clarity and to avoid unnecessary conversion spread. Payments from third parties or crypto-linked money-service businesses are prohibited and will trigger automatic compliance refusal.For outbound trade settlements, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments and Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary LC). For open-account supplier payments, the IC must issue a MT103 payment instruction referencing the production invoice number, commodity description, Incoterms, and end-to-end reference ID through its banking portal. For LC-based procurement, the Vanuatu issuing bank will transmit the documentary credit via SWIFT MT700; the exporting supplier’s bank presents complying documents, then settlement occurs through reimbursement in the LC currency. The IC treasury should maintain a segregated USD/AUD trade account and use no VUV conversion unless a local expense is involved. Acceptable outbound settlement documentation set includes the commercial invoice, comprehensive packing list, full bill of lading, certificate of origin, inspection certificate, and the LC negotiated draft. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - Vanuatu has no general transfer-pricing legislation, no Local File or Master File regime, and no controlled-transaction documentation threshold for International Companies. The zero-tax regime eliminates any Vanuatu arm's-length adjustment. Intra-group pricing of IVD cartridges, analyzer hardware, and IP royalties remains material to foreign tax authorities through CFC, permanent establishment, and base-erosion rules.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu's Economic Substance (Companies) Act applies to VFSC-license-holding entities conducting relevant activities (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing, leasing, headquarters, distribution, or service centres). A pure IC manufacturing microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers outside Vanuatu falls outside the domestic economic substance verification net; however, genuine foreign substance must still be demonstrable to prevent foreign CFC attribution, permanent-establishment risk, and EU/G20 listing countermeasures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / sham arrangement

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: strike-off and dissolution by VFSC for failure to maintain an agent and records; the exemption does not shield beneficial owners. Foreign: recharacterization of the IC as a sham, full reassessment, penalties of 100-200% of understated tax, debanking, and inclusion on CFC registries.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false compliance declarations are criminally liable under the IC Act (fine and imprisonment); in foreign proceedings, directors face civil restitution for diverted profits, travel bans, and extradition or conviction for tax fraud.
Prohibited domestic business (shell company abuse)

Statutory sanction: Loss of the IC's exempt status, VFSC deregistration, fines per offence, voiding of prohibited contracts, and forfeiture of assets connected to the unlawful domestic business.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally and jointly liable for the company's debts arising from prohibited domestic contracts; the corporate veil is pierced and personal assets are exposed to civil enforcement.
AML / beneficial ownership breach

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties under the International Companies (Amendment) Act and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act, referral to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit, publication of non-compliance, and deregistration for repeat breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors of nominee structures face personal fines, confiscation of unexplained wealth, and imprisonment for money laundering or proceeds-of-crime offences.
Unlicensed regulated activity

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the IC registration, monetary penalties per unlicensed operation, seizure of medical devices, and freezing of operational bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unlicensed trading debts and may be disqualified; criminal liability attaches to those who knowingly operate an unlicensed IVD-manufacturing or financial business.
Fraudulent misrepresentation in filings

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under the IC Act: fines of up to three times the advantage obtained, mandatory deregistration, and referral to the Public Prosecutor for prosecution.

Director personal liability: Imprisonment for fraud-related offences, disqualification as director of Vanuatu companies, and civil liability to third parties who relied on the false filing.
