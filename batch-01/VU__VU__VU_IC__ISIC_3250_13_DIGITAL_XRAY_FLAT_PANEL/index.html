Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs Authority (VRCA), operating under the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; e-implementation via ASYCUDA World and the Vanuatu Trade Portal.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned International Companies do not require a domestic Tax Identification Number for income tax purposes because they are statutorily exempt. The entity obtains a certificate of incorporation and a VFSC registration number from the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission. For customs/export operations, the IC registers as an exporter with the VRCA Customs Division and obtains a VAT registration only if it makes taxable supplies in Vanuatu that exceed VT 3,000,000 per annum or if it opts to register. The physical process: submit Form IC-1 to VFSC, pay government fees, then apply to VRCA for an exporter code via ASYCUDA World.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist in Vanuatu. The International Companies Act provides an irrevocable election to convert an IC to a Domestic Company, which subjects it to the standard Vanuatu tax regime. Additionally, any person, including an IC, may voluntarily elect to register for VAT under the VAT Act, notwithstanding its exempt status, provided it satisfies the registration requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 3,000,000 (Vanuatu Vatu) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated where the exporter proves that the goods were removed from Vanuatu within 28 days of the supply. Services supplied to a non-resident outside Vanuatu and performed outside Vanuatu also qualify for zero-rating. The International Company must retain outward customs declaration, shipping evidence, and payment vouchers. Medical imaging hardware OEM sales to foreign distributors will qualify for the 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World)
  • Bill of Lading / House Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank SWIFT/TT credit advice
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (if requested by buyer)

No domestic e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate. Export declarations are lodged electronically via ASYCUDA World; no digital stamping or real-time tax authority API clearance applies to VAT invoices. Customs export entries require an approved exporter code and electronic cargo manifest.

False or fraudulent zero-rating claims are audited by the VRCA Customs/VAT division and may result in a penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded, interest at the statutory rate, and criminal fines not exceeding VT 5,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years. Input VAT credits may be clawed back retrospectively with a 4-year assessment period. The Commissioner may also require security from exporters suspected of non-compliance. Since the International Company is otherwise tax-exempt, an improper VAT refund claim for exports will trigger revocation of exempt status and liability to the full VAT law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a fully liberalised foreign exchange environment. The Vatu is freely convertible for current and capital account transactions; there is no exchange control legislation, no prior RBV approval requirement for profit or capital remittances, and no restriction on foreign ownership or dividend repatriation. The practical hurdle is bank-level documentation and correspondent-bank due diligence, given the small local banking system. The International Company should maintain clear underlying contracts, board resolutions and invoice files to facilitate frictionless settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation in Vanuatu. No mandatory conversion ratio exists, and export receipts may be retained in Vatu or foreign currency accounts at licensed commercial banks without any time-limited surrender requirement. The IC is free to use export proceeds for trade settlements, intra-group payments, or treasury purposes subject only to standard bank record-keeping and AML declarations.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), acting as the national monetary authority and foreign exchange regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-Medium. Vanuatu has no dedicated fintech or EMI licensing regime for non-bank payment institutions. Offshore digital EMIs may be used for receivables collection or auxiliary multi-currency settlement, but they cannot legally substitute for a licensed Vanuatu commercial bank as the primary treasury rail. The RBV requires any deposit-taking or payment-system activity to be conducted through a licensed financial institution. The IC should therefore use a licensed commercial bank in Vanuatu as the settlement anchor, with an offshore EMI (e.g., Hong Kong/Singapore-licensed) only as an ancillary collection channel.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax; International Companies (ICs) registered under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] are expressly tax-exempt on worldwide income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no dividend withholding tax; an IC may declare and pay dividends to non-resident shareholders free of source tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None — Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing statute, no thin-capitalisation rules, and no Local File/Master File thresholds. Controlled-transaction pricing is not reviewable under domestic tax law; however, foreign revenue authorities may re-characterise inter-company pricing of the OEM hardware, so contemporaneous OECD-aligned documentation is recommended to protect the customer's cross-border deductions.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations Act No. 22 of 2019 — manufacturing of tangible X-ray flat-panel detectors is not a listed 'relevant activity'; targeted categories that may capture the group's income include 'IP business' (if the IC owns, licences or commercially exploits patents/trade secrets) and 'distribution and service centre' (if the IC takes procurement or warehousing functions). To remain compliant, the IC must demonstrate in Vanuatu a physical office, at least one locally engaged director or manager, board meetings held in Vanuatu at least twice per financial year, and proportionate core-income-generating expenditure. Substantial fines and strike-off exposure arise only if the IC in fact performs a relevant activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Offshore Structuring

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Penal Code [Cap. 135] for fraud (imprisonment up to 12 years) and under the VAT Act for evasion (fine up to three times any evaded VAT); civil penalty assessments by the VRCA; disqualification of the registered agent.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto controllers are criminally liable where they knew or ought to have known of the false invoicing; personal civil restitution is recoverable by the VRCA; travel bans may be imposed pending extradition or asset-freezing orders.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: VFSC administrative strike-off under s. 101 of the International Companies Act; ESR penalties up to VUV 2,000,000 per relevant activity for entities caught by the 2019 regime; restoration refused without filing of nil returns and agent confirmation.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face fines for false declarations; courts may pierce the veil to impose restitution on the ultimate beneficial owner; directors may be barred from managing any Vanuatu company for up to 5 years.
AML / CFT Breach (Beneficial Ownership and STR)

Statutory sanction: Enforcement by the Vanuatu FIU under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 7 of 2014: administrative penalties up to VUV 20,000,000 for body corporates and imprisonment for individuals up to 15 years; asset-confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act [Cap. 279].

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly omit an STR or authorise a sanctions-listed wire transfer incur personal criminal liability; the VFSC may delegate the beneficial-ownership register to the agent and freeze directors' travel through court order.
False Statements and Filing Offences

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under the International Companies Act for false statements (fine within the VFSC discretionary band and imprisonment up to 2 years); in aggravated cross-border fraud, charges escalate to Penal Code [Cap. 135] fraud offences (imprisonment up to 12 years); restoration of a struck-off IC is denied.

Director personal liability: Officers who signed the false declaration are personally prosecuted even after resignation; they are jointly and severally liable for third-party losses, with civil restitution and punitive costs recoverable.
Unlicensed Medical-Device Operation

Statutory sanction: Refusal of export permits, seizure of goods by customs, fines under the relevant trade licensing regulations, and cancellation of the IC's registration; foreign regulators will also block imports of non-conforming devices under CE/FDA regimes.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed product technical files or import-export declarations are personally liable for false conformity statements; penalties include director disqualification, personal civil damages for end-user harm, and travel restrictions in enforcement actions.
