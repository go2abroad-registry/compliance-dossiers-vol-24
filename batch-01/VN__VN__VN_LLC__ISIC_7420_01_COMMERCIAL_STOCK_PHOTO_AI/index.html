Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Vietnam Law on Enterprises imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for an ordinary LLC (non-licensed sector); the charter capital is declared at the member's discretion and must be contributed within 90 days after the Enterprise Registration Certificate date. No escrow account or bank freeze is required under national law for an LLC unless the project is subject to investment conditions listed in the Investment Law. A foreign-invested entity must maintain the total investment capital as stated in the Investment Registration Certificate; paid-up capital is disclosed in the dossier but no minimum threshold applies for ISIC 7420.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế), Ministry of Finance, Socialist Republic of Viet Nam — National electronic tax portal: etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn; e-invoice portal: hoahoadon.gdt.gov.vn; enterprise registration portal: dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested LLC must first obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) and Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment or the applicable industrial zone authority. The enterprise registration dossier concurrently serves as a tax registration application, and the tax authority automatically issues the statutory 10-digit tax identification number (Mã số thuế, MST). The foreign corporate shareholder must appoint a legal representative in Viet Nam, submit the ERC/IRC applications, charter, list of members, notarized constitutional documents, power-of-attorney, and passport evidence. After ERC issuance, the LLC completes the initial tax declaration through the e-tax portal using its MST, activates digital signatures, and files all subsequent corporate income tax and VAT returns under that single national TIN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-invested Limited Liability Company is a resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) at the statutory headline rate of 20% on worldwide taxable income. There is no pass-through treatment under Vietnamese law. Territorial scope is not limited to Vietnamese-source income for resident enterprises, though foreign-source income may be credited for foreign tax paid. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-invested Limited Liability Company is a resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) at the statutory headline rate of 20% on worldwide taxable income. There is no pass-through treatment under Vietnamese law. Territorial scope is not limited to Vietnamese-source income for resident enterprises, though foreign-source income may be credited for foreign tax paid. A standard LLC without special incentive certification is not deemed tax-exempt; only enterprises established as Export Processing Enterprises (EPE), high-tech enterprises, or in designated investment incentive zones may qualify for temporary/partial tax reductions or exemptions.

Available election forms

No IRC Section 7701-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Vietnamese tax law. The LLC is a fixed entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections and designations include: (i) election to apply the export processing enterprise (EPE) regime for duty/VAT-suspension and export VAT benefits; (ii) application for high-tech enterprise certification or investment incentive certificates for hi-tech/geographic priority zones; (iii) selection of VAT computation method — the credit/deduction method is mandatory for an LLC maintaining complete accounting records; the direct/indirect method is not available to a standard LLC. No separate hybrid-mismatch election or sub-entity classification is permissible.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold applies to a corporate LLC: VAT registration and invoicing are mandatory from the date of enterprise registration regardless of turnover. Separate to that, an annual revenue threshold of VND 100,000,000 applies only to business households and individual traders, below which they may be exempt from VAT. This household exemption does not extend to a commercial stock-photo LLC operating as a limited liability company.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rate eligibility applies to exported goods and offshore services delivered and consumed outside Viet Nam. A stock-photo marketplace LLC may apply 0% VAT on digital visual assets and platform services sold to a foreign customer located abroad, provided the supply is not consumed in Viet Nam and the LLC can substantiate an export contract/offshore service agreement, separate invoicing in foreign currency, and settlement through bank remittance from a non-Vietnamese account. Sales to Vietnamese resident customers, even through the same platform, are subject to standard 10% VAT and cannot be zero-rated. If the documentary conditions are not satisfied, the tax authority re-classifies the turnover as domestic taxable supply and imposes 10% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Tờ khai Hàng hóa Xuất khẩu) — mandatory where physical media or goods enter the export customs process
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / multimodal transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Giấy báo Có ngoại tệ) or bank/FinTech statement evidencing foreign currency settlement
  • Export sales contract or offshore service agreement with the foreign customer
  • Electronic VAT invoice (HĐĐT) with tax authority code
  • Stripe/PayPal payout statements and interfund remittance records linking foreign receipts to the specific zero-rated invoice

All enterprises must use the national e-invoice regime (Hóa đơn điện tử, HĐĐT) under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. Every VAT invoice must be issued with a tax authority code, transmitted in real time to the General Department of Taxation through the e-invoice portal at hoahoadon.gdt.gov.vn using digitally signed XML/JSON API templates, and confirmed by the tax authority's digital code/verification number before it is legally usable. The LLC's billing and payment rails (Stripe, PayPal) must be integrated so that each sales transaction generates the prescribed XML schema, applies the correct VAT rate, and transmits invoice clearance data to the tax authority without manual intervention. No paper invoice or unstamped HĐĐT is fiscally valid for a corporate exporter.

The tax authority may retrospectively verify export zero-rating claims up to five years after the tax filing period. Where an invoice has been zero-rated without full documentary evidence, the authority will revoke the 0% VAT treatment, re-assess turnover at 10%, impose late-payment interest, and recover any unlawfully refunded input VAT. Administrative penalties for false invoice/declaration or tax evasion may range up to three times the evaded tax amount for serious violations, plus statutory penalty interest. In cases involving fabricated customs declarations, false invoices, or proceeds above criminal thresholds, directors may incur criminal liability for tax evasion or smuggling under the Vietnamese Penal Code, including fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment. Clawback immunity is not available after the statutory audit period begins without complete compliance substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Viet Nam maintains a substantially liberalized current-account convertibility regime: cross-border royalties, stock-photo licensing fees, subscription revenue, digital-content download fees, and other service-export receipts may be transferred into Vietnam and repatriated abroad without prior SBV approval, provided the underlying transaction is supported by a genuine commercial contract, invoices, tax records, and is settled through an authorized bank. The capital account remains controlled and licensing-based: foreign equity contributions must be routed through a registered Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA); foreign loans require registration with the State Bank in accordance with applicable thresholds; derivative and hedging transactions require a demonstrable underlying commercial foreign-currency exposure; and no free convertibility exists for speculative, portfolio, or non-documented flows attributable to a non-listed Vietnamese LLC. Profit and dividend remittance by the foreign-invested LLC is not subject to a quantitative cap, but is conditional upon audited annual financial statements, completed enterprise income tax finalization, settlement of all tax and social-insurance liabilities, and bank verification before each outward transfer. Liquidation-capital repatriation additionally requires DICA closure confirmation and a tax clearance certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export and service-licensing proceeds arising from foreign customers, including stock-photo download fees, platform subscription receipts, and royalty payments for digital visual-asset licenses, must be repatriated in full into an authorized foreign-currency account or the Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) maintained with an SBV-licensed bank. The statutory baseline requires goods-export proceeds to be credited no later than 90 days from the date of exportation, with possible extension under deferred-payment terms up to 180 days; service-export and royalty proceeds must be credited within 180 days from the date the service is completed or the licensing period closes, as represented in the underlying agreement, and the SBV or the servicing bank may grant extensions in meritorious or documented force-majeure cases. The mandatory foreign-currency surrender (compulsory conversion) ratio is currently dormant at zero, meaning exporters are entitled to retain USD, EUR, and other eligible foreign currencies in permitted FX accounts without any State-imposed conversion quota; however, the Government retains the statutory authority to activate mandatory conversion ratios during urgent balance-of-payments emergencies, and the LLC's treasury policy should therefore maintain the ability to demonstrate the source and timing of all foreign-currency inflows.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV / Ngan hang Nha nuoc Viet Nam), functioning as the sole central bank and foreign exchange control authority of the Socialist Republic of Viet Nam. FX regulation is exercised primarily through the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange No. 28/2005/PL-UBTVQH11, as amended by Ordinance No. 06/2013/UBTVQH13, Decree No. 70/2014/ND-CP, and Circular No. 06/2019/TT-NHNN, as amended by Circular No. 03/2022/TT-NHNN and Circular No. 23/2023/TT-NHNN. The SBV exclusively authorizes licensed credit institutions to conduct foreign-currency activities; all external treasury operations of VN_LLC must be executed through an SBV-licensed bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where total annual revenue exceeds VND 50 billion or aggregate related-party transaction value exceeds VND 30 billion. Master File is required where total annual revenue exceeds VND 200 billion and related-party transaction value exceeds VND 50 billion. All taxpayers with related-party transactions must file TP declaration Forms 01 and 02 with the annual CIT return regardless of the documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam does not maintain a standalone ESR regime for operating companies, but anti-avoidance and substance-over-form rules under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP allow tax authorities to disregard transactions without genuine commercial objectives, real personnel, assets, functions and risk assumption. Treaty-shopping arrangements can be denied relief under the principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / CIT False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 20% of the under-declared tax (or 2-3 times the tax evaded in serious cases), execution on unpaid tax with 0.03% daily interest, and criminal prosecution under Article 200 of the Penal Code for evasion over VND 100 million, carrying fines up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, approve or direct false CIT returns are jointly and severally liable; they may face civil restitution, travel bans, asset freezing, and personal imprisonment if their intent is proven.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The GDT may re-characterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, reassess income using deemed profits, and levy additional CIT plus 20% penalty and interest; in egregious abuse, fines can reach triple the avoided tax and the entity may be subject to license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as de facto managers may be personally assessed for the enterprise’s resulting tax liabilities and may face travel bans and confiscation orders.
AML / Money Laundering via Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to VND 200 million for violations of the Law on Anti-Money Laundering 2022; criminal liability under Article 324 of the Penal Code carries imprisonment up to 15 years, fines, and confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or fail to prevent laundering transactions are principals; they can be individually fined, imprisoned, banned from leaving the jurisdiction, and ordered to make civil restitution.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment re-adjusting profits to arm's-length results, an additional penalty of 20% of the underpaid tax, and 0.03% daily interest; failure to file documentation may attract fines up to VND 25 million and can elevate to criminal tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve group pricing policies and sign legal declarations in Vietnam risk personal liability, including restitution, travel restraints, and criminal investigation if deliberate mispricing is established.
Illegal Profit Repatriation / Premature Distribution

Statutory sanction: The distribution is legally ineffective; regulators can impose administrative penalties up to VND 25 million, require restoration of the distributed amounts, and collect any outstanding CIT plus interest that the distribution was meant to shield.

Director personal liability: Directors must recover distributions for the estate and may be personally liable for the company’s unpaid taxes and creditors if they authorized unlawful dividends.
