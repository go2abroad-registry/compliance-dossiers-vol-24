Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue Authority (VRA) – Customs and Inland Revenue Division, Port Vila; official web portal: https://www.vra.gov.vu. Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) is the statutory registrar and regulator of International Companies (ICs) under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222]; portal: https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

Vanuatu does not issue a general income tax TIN because it imposes no income tax on International Companies. The IC obtains its sole tax-identity instrument from the VFSC via the Certificate of Incorporation (registration number). If the IC makes or intends to make taxable supplies in Vanuatu exceeding VUV 10,000,000 in any 12-month period, it must apply separately to the VRA for a VAT registration number using Form VAT-01, supported by the VFSC Certificate, bank references, and projected turnover statement. A pure offshore brokerage operation under ISIC 4631 supplying services wholly to non-residents is outside the scope of Vanuatu VAT and is not required to register. An IC that does not carry on business in Vanuatu is also exempt from business license requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu law contains no elective entity classification mechanism (no check-the-box regime) and no hybrid mismatch election, because Vanuatu has no income tax and no dividend, interest, or royalty withholding tax system. All International Companies are statutorily classified as tax-exempt offshore entities. A voluntary VAT registration election is available under the Value Added Tax Act No. 6 of 2022, but once registered, full VAT compliance obligations apply and no partial-exemption election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 10,000,000 annual taxable turnover; non-resident suppliers without a fixed establishment in Vanuatu are registrable where they make supplies of goods situated in Vanuatu or services performed in Vanuatu.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply qualifies for zero-rating (0% VAT) if it constitutes an export of goods from Vanuatu, evidenced by physical departure of the goods; or an export of services supplied to a non-resident recipient who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply, where the services are performed entirely outside Vanuatu or the benefit is enjoyed wholly outside Vanuatu. A bulk grain brokerage commission (for arranging international soybean/corn shipments among foreign counterparties) satisfies the exported-services test and is zero-rated or outside the scope of Vanuatu VAT, provided the broker makes no taxable supply to a Vanuatu resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration approved through ASYCUDA World (where goods are physically shipped from Vanuatu)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing carriage from a Vanuatu port to the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and complete packing list for the exported consignment
  • Charter party or sale/purchase confirmation for the shipment
  • Brokerage agreement and commission invoice issued by the Vanuatu International Company
  • SWIFT / MT103 payment confirmations evidencing commission settlement from the non-resident principal
  • VFSC Certificate of Incorporation confirming the International Company's tax-exempt status

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime has been enacted for VAT purposes. The VRA operates the ASYCUDA World customs platform for all import/export declarations and automated risk assessment. VAT invoices may be issued in paper or PDF format and must be serially numbered, stating the supplier's legal name, address, VAT registration number, date of supply, description, consideration, and VAT amount.

Vanuatu imposes no statutory FX surrender or repatriation deadline; commercial contracts typically fix settlement terms at 30–180 days. The VRA can retrospectively claw back zero-rated VAT for unpaid VAT, plus interest at 2% per month from the due date and late-payment penalties of up to 10% of the unpaid amount; fraudulent or reckless evasion of VAT attracts an administrative penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded, criminal fines, imprisonment under the VAT Act No. 6 of 2022, and the Customs Act [Cap. 72], including forfeiture penalties for false export declarations. VRA audit scrutiny concentrates on documentary proof of physical export, offshore-benefit test for services, and sufficient non-resident counterparty evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is generally convertible on both current and capital account transactions. There is no statutory exchange control regime restricting inward or outward remittances for non-resident shareholders of an International Company. Profit remittances and dividend distributions are subject only to standard KYC/AML verification and bank transfer documentation; no central bank approval or conversion license is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratios in Vanuatu. Export receipts may be held, credited, and repatriated freely in foreign currency. Banks may nevertheless require supporting export documentation (commercial invoices, bills of lading, and LC settlement advices) to evidence the underlying trade for deferred payment reporting and AML screening.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) acting as the central monetary authority and the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) as the licensed currency and corporate conduct supervisor for this International Company.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium. Offshore fintech and Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may provide onboarding convenience, but they generally cannot open VUV-denominated settlement accounts or issue local payment instruments through RBV-regulated clearing systems. Vanuatu’s AML/CFT Act demands effective physical or registered local agent oversight for a licensed IC. Thus, the primary corporate treasury accounts should be maintained with an RBV-licensed domestic bank or a fully authorized foreign-owned bank branch; EMIs can only be used for limited USD auxiliary flows and must not replace the primary settlement rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax; there is no Income Tax Act in force and Vanuatu operates a pure zero-tax regime for offshore International Companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. The exemption is absolute under the International Companies Act; no local income tax is deducted at source.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the last day of the month containing the anniversary of registration, e.g., incorporated 15 May, renewal due by 31 May. No corporate income tax return is required because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly returns due by the 20th day of the following month. A compliant IC does not reach the VAT registration threshold because all offshore brokerage services are zero-rated exports.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — Vanuatu has no domestic transfer pricing statute and no Local File or Master File regime. De facto international threshold: any related-party transaction, including brokerage fees on bulk soybean and corn shipments between an IC and a foreign affiliate, should be supported by OECD-aligned contemporaneous TP documentation (functional analysis, benchmarking study, intercompany agreement) in the counterparty's tax jurisdiction, where documentation thresholds commonly range from EUR 4,000,000 to EUR 10,000,000 (approximately VUV 470,000,000 to VUV 1,175,000,000).

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the BVI Economic Substances (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act 2018. However: (a) the International Companies Act requires a physical registered agent and registered office in Vanuatu; (b) an IC is prohibited from carrying on business with Vanuatu residents (ICA s.15); and (c) foreign tax authorities and EU/FATF monitoring require the IC to demonstrate genuine economic substance — board meetings, executed contracts, independent bank account, and a functioning local agent — to withstand CFC re-attribution of trading income to non-resident shareholders. Guidance: maintain contemporaneous minutes, service agreements, and complete corporate records; do not backdate substance documents.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Foreign Tax Fraud Conducted Through IC

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu imposes no domestic income tax sanction; however, the foreign jurisdiction may recharacterize the IC as a sham, levy penalties of up to 150% of the evaded foreign tax, and impose interest. VFSC may strike off the IC for carrying on business contrary to public interest, and the registered agent may be fined or deregistered under the AML Act 2017 for facilitating a shell structure.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations face prosecution in the foreign jurisdiction despite Vanuatu confidentiality laws; Vanuatu cooperates through AEOI and TIEA information-exchange requests, and courts may make personal restitution and costs orders against directors.
Shell Company / Prohibited Domestic Operations (ICA s.15 Breach)

Statutory sanction: Conviction under the International Companies Act renders the IC liable to a fine of up to VUV 1,000,000, automatic loss of tax-exempt status, and strike-off from the VFSC register; contracts made in breach of the prohibition become unenforceable in Vanuatu.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid duties and VFSC penalties; courts may order restitution against directors who knowingly permitted the domestic trade, and directors may be disqualified from managing a Vanuatu company for up to 5 years.
AML/CFT Breach (Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2017)

Statutory sanction: VFSC may fine the company and its registered agent up to VUV 10,000,000, suspend or revoke the agent's license, and freeze the IC's accounts; criminal conviction for money laundering carries imprisonment of up to 15 years and asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors with actual knowledge of suspicious flows face personal criminal liability; courts may impose travel bans, asset restraint orders, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud, exposing non-resident shareholders to civil forfeiture.
Fraudulent Trading and Piercing the Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: Under the residual application of the Vanuatu Companies Act 2009 and general insolvency principles, the court may order winding-up, void asset transfers, and require directors to make personal contributions to the company's assets; restoration of a struck-off company requires a court application and full payment of arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil liability for the full amount of fraudulent trading losses, disqualification orders, and criminal imprisonment where dishonesty is proved; courts may issue bench warrants and travel bans pending final determination.
