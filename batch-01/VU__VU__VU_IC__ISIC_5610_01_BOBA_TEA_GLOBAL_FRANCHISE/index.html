Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Service (VCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official portal: https://www.customsinlandrevenue.gov.vu; offshore entity registry administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) at https://www.vfsc.vu

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act 1992 (Cap 222) via the VFSC and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, which carries an automatic statutory tax exemption for offshore activities. Step 2: A Vanuatu TIN is not issued or required for an IC that derives no income from within Vanuatu, as it is statutorily exempt from national income tax, corporate tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty. Step 3: If the IC carries on a taxable activity inside Vanuatu or elects voluntary VAT registration, submit the VAT registration application (Form VAT 1) to the VCIR and receive a VAT identification number (VAT ID) for filing and invoicing. Step 4: Obtain a business license where local physical presence exists through the Vanuatu Investment Promotion Authority (VIPA) or municipal/ provincial councils under the Business Licences Act 2018. Step 5: Maintain a registered agent in Vanuatu and file the annual VFSC confirmation fee; no income tax return is required for the tax-exempt IC unless it becomes VAT-registered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu does not maintain a US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election system. The IC is automatically classified as a non-resident offshore tax-exempt entity under the International Companies Act 1992 (Cap 222) and the Income Tax Act is structured such that no national income tax exists for offshore companies. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) voluntary VAT registration election under the Value Added Tax Act 1998 (Cap 245) to enable input VAT recovery on local purchases; (ii) election to obtain a formal Tax Exemption Certificate from the VFSC for banking counter-party evidence; (iii) election to redomicile into or out of Vanuatu under Part IVA of the International Companies Act; (iv) no OECD Pillar Two, CFC, or hybrid mismatch elections are operative or available for a purely offshore franchise holding company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover; registration is mandatory for any person, including a non-resident IC, making taxable supplies in Vanuatu above this threshold; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold with VCIR approval

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimable under the Value Added Tax Act 1998 (Cap 245) on: (a) goods physically exported from Vanuatu, including syrup concentrates, flavour powders, packaging, and franchise equipment, provided export occurs within the prescribed period and is evidenced by customs documentation; (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient for use wholly outside Vanuatu, including franchise royalty income, system licensing fees, training services, and global brand management services rendered by the IC to overseas boba tea franchisees; (c) supplies to designated international organizations. The franchise must hold contemporaneous evidence that the benefit of the service is enjoyed outside Vanuatu and that the royalty payer is a non-resident not making local use of the service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CEF/DEC Form filed with the Vanuatu Customs Division)
  • Bill of Lading or House Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Vanuatu
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of payment from overseas
  • Commercial Invoice linking goods to the IC VAT ID and export entry
  • Franchise Agreement, Royalty Statement, or Service Contract proving the offshore recipient and offshore use of the service

Vanuatu has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing clearance regime. The VCIR operates an electronic tax administration portal for online VAT return filing and tax payments, but taxpayers remain entitled to issue conventional sequentially numbered paper or PDF tax invoices. Every VAT invoice must contain the supplier VAT ID, customer details, invoice date, description of supply, tax rate, taxable value, and VAT amount. Franchise royalty invoices and export sales invoices must be retained in original form for five years to support zero-rating claims during VCIR audits.

Vanuatu imposes strict documentation-based scrutiny of zero-rating claims. Penalties and clawback consequences include: (i) under s. 44 of the VAT Act 1998 (Cap 245), a civil penalty of up to the greater of 200% of the tax evaded or VUV 200,000 per offence, plus default interest at the prevailing statutory rate; (ii) retrospective VAT assessments clawing back zero-rated output tax together with input VAT disallowance where export documentation is missing, forged, or does not show physical departure within the required period; (iii) under the Customs Act 1981, false or fraudulent export declarations attract fines up to VUV 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; (iv) the VCIR may cancel the VAT ID and refer egregious abuse to the VFSC for revocation of the IC Tax Exemption Certificate; (v) directors and franchise principals may be personally liable for fraudulent misuse of zero-rating where franchise royalties are artificially structured as offshore services while the economic benefit occurs inside Vanuatu; (vi) no statutory FX repatriation deadline applies in Vanuatu, as International Companies enjoy unrestricted capital and payment flows pursuant to the International Companies Act 1992, but royalty remittance flows should, as a prudent benchmark, be received within 180 days to maintain clean audit evidence and support transfer-pricing substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is effectively convertible on both current and capital account; the exchange control framework is liberalized and does not restrict profit remittance, dividend distribution, or royalty outflows for non-resident international companies (ICs). Since the 2021 RBV reform, the VUV is anchored to the U.S. dollar (approximately VUV 112.5 per USD), which substantially reduces USD translation risk for royalty and franchise fee inflows. A Vanuatu IC is statutorily treated as a non-resident for exchange control purposes and is therefore exempt from prior RBV approval for offshore current and capital transactions. Vanuatu levies no income tax, capital gains tax, or withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or trade payments, creating a frictionless remittance corridor. Residual hurdles are limited to bank-level AML/CFT due diligence, FATF increased monitoring (grey-list) scrutiny, and correspondent bank risk appetite rather than statutory convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Vanuatu imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Exporters and non-resident ICs may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in onshore or offshore foreign-currency accounts denominated in USD, AUD, EUR, or CNH. Banks require only transactional evidence (commercial invoice, bill of lading, signed franchise agreement, or LC documents) to demonstrate the underlying genuine trade or service flow.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), acting under the Foreign Exchange Control Act [Cap 118], the International Companies Act [Cap 222], and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2014, as the central bank and designated exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Vanuatu has no domestic EMI licensing regime; an offshore or global EMI (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) will generally decline a Vanuatu IC due to grey-list risk appetite, lack of a VUV-denominated IBAN, and the entity's offshore classification. A fintech EMI may supplement, not replace, the primary banking rail for FX conversion or low-value disbursements if it accepts Vanuatu corporate clients; legally, any deposit-taking or payment-instrument activity targeting Vanuatu residents requires an RBV banking licence. Onshore licensed banks (ANZ and BSP) must remain the treasury anchor.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold or Local File / Master File obligation under Vanuatu law; recommended administrative threshold of VUV 100,000,000 in related-party royalty and supply-chain revenue to evidence arm's-length conduct and satisfy foreign tax authority audits.

Economic substance scope: No dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime exists in Vanuatu; however the International Companies (Amendment) Act 2022, VFSC beneficial-ownership rules, and EU/OECD transparency screening effectively require demonstrable offshore substance: licensed registered agent, board governance, accounting records, and management decisions outside Vanuatu. The boba tea franchise IC must maintain a functional treasury and licensing contract archive to avoid characterization as a tax-evasion shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax-Exemption Claims

Statutory sanction: Revocation of International Company status; retrospective reassessment at the standard corporate tax rate (0% statutory but loss of exemption exposes fees and penalties); VFSC strike-off; fines up to VUV 500,000 per false declaration plus VUV 5,000 daily continuing penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of all unpaid taxes and penalties; courts may issue asset-freezing orders, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 2 years for false statutory declaration.
Shell Company / Sham Substance and AML Failure

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act No. 30 of 2017: corporate fines up to VUV 10,000,000 per contravention; VFSC cancellation of registration and freezing of royalty collection bank accounts; referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit for suspicious transactions exceeding VUV 1,000,000 without disclosure.

Director personal liability: Individual directors face personal fines up to VUV 10,000,000, imprisonment up to 15 years, confiscation of personal assets under proceeds-of-crime legislation, and criminal travel bans pending investigation.
International Tax Transparency Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu-side administrative strike-off and director disqualification for failure to furnish records to VFSC; foreign-side penalty taxes of 20%-40% on gross boba tea royalties, retroactive transfer-pricing adjustments, and reputational listing on EU blacklists or OECD harmful-tax reviews.

Director personal liability: Foreign courts may pierce the corporate veil, subjecting directors and controllers to personal tax assessments equal to unpaid withholding taxes plus interest, civil restitution of the full royalty stream, and extradition or international arrest warrants for tax fraud where criminal intent is proven.
Franchise Licensing and Consumer Protection Fraud

Statutory sanction: Civil liability under Vanuatu consumer protection and trade licensing frameworks; VFSC licence suspension; clawback of franchise fee payments; criminal prosecution for obtaining financial advantage by deception with corporate fines up to VUV 2,000,000 and full restitution orders.

Director personal liability: Directors liable in personam for franchisee contractual damages and fraudulent misrepresentation torts; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 10 years, director disqualification for 7 years, and non-dischargeable restitution obligations.
