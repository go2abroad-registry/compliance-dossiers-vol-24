Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

General rule: an LLC has no statutory minimum charter capital, but the registered charter capital must be paid in full within 90 days after issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate and the payment date and method must be recorded in the National Business Registration database. Special rule for a virtual card issuing and spend-management fintech: the State Bank of Vietnam imposes a minimum legal capital of VND 50 billion (approximately US$ 2,000,000) for licensed intermediary payment services covering payment instruments and card-related issuing/acquiring support; the capital must be fully disbursed, deposited in the company's corporate bank account, and evidenced by an independent audit certificate of capital disbursement attached to the payment-license application.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Appraisal and Acceptance Certificate under Decree 136/2020/ND-CP (including the server room and UPS room design with approved fire-extinguishing systems); the building landlord must provide the base building fire-safety acceptance certificate; the company itself must obtain the premises acceptance certificate for its leasehold floors. Civil-defense compliance includes a business continuity and incident-response plan registered with the fire police, closed-circuit security recording, and employee safety drills. Occupational clearance: DOLISA registration of the hazardous machinery/electrical inventory (server equipment, UPS systems), mandatory safety-training program, appointment of a safety officer, and annual medical examinations for IT and facilities staff.

Paid-in capital rule: General rule: an LLC has no statutory minimum charter capital, but the registered charter capital must be paid in full within 90 days after issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate and the payment date and method must be recorded in the National Business Registration database. Special rule for a virtual card issuing and spend-management fintech: the State Bank of Vietnam imposes a minimum legal capital of VND 50 billion (approximately US$ 2,000,000) for licensed intermediary payment services covering payment instruments and card-related issuing/acquiring support; the capital must be fully disbursed, deposited in the company's corporate bank account, and evidenced by an independent audit certificate of capital disbursement attached to the payment-license application.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (GDT) under the Ministry of Finance of Viet Nam; official portal: https://thuedt.gdt.gov.vn

E-government filing portal

Foreign-invested LLC obtains a Tax Identification Number (MST) automatically upon issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate by the Department of Planning and Investment. Thereafter, the enterprise must complete tax registration with the local tax authority (Cục Thuế) to activate its tax file, register the method of VAT accounting, obtain an electronic invoice registration number, and provide supporting documents including the investment registration certificate, company charter, and legal representative identification. For entities engaged in virtual card issuing and payment intermediary activities, a separate license from the State Bank of Vietnam is required before carrying on operations; the tax ID remains contingent on lawful establishment and licensing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box elections are available under Vietnamese tax law. The entity is automatically classified as a taxable resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax on worldwide income. Available mechanisms include election to apply CIT preferential rates based on sector and location (e.g., high-tech, supporting industries, economic zones) and election of the VAT calculation method (deduction method versus direct method) where eligible.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 1 billion (annual revenue threshold for mandatory VAT registration under the deduction method; enterprises below the threshold may voluntarily register or be subject to the direct method)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, and services supplied to foreign organizations/individuals used outside Vietnam, are eligible for 0% VAT. This includes offshore card processing, financial switching, and B2B services provided by a Vietnamese entity to non-resident clients, provided the service is consumed outside Vietnam, contracts are with non-residents, and supporting documents evidence the offshore delivery. Zero-rating is also available for goods sold to export processing enterprises (EPE) and for goods/services used in export processing or bonded warehouses.

  • Customs Export Declaration (validated)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill, or international transport documents)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of foreign currency receipt
  • Sales contract and commercial invoice
  • Electronic invoice with 0% VAT rate
  • Documents evidencing foreign counterparty and utilization/consumption outside Vietnam (for services)

Since 1 July 2022, Vietnam mandates 100% electronic invoices with mandatory tax authority verification codes for all enterprises (Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC). Invoices must be transmitted to the GDT system in XML format via certified software; each invoice is assigned a unique verification code (mã của cơ quan thuế). No real-time per-transaction clearance is required for standard invoices, but no-coded invoices (hóa đơn không có mã) are only allowed on the direct method. APIs are required for some high-volume business-to-business models, and all invoices must be digitally signed.

The tax authority will claw back VAT previously zero-rated if export documentation is incomplete, payments are not repatriated within 180 days, or the services are found to be consumed in Vietnam. Penalties include: 10% late-payment interest per annum on outstanding tax; administrative fines of 20% of underdeclared tax for incorrect declarations; up to VND 200 million (and confiscation of invoices) for serious violations; and criminal prosecution under the Penal Code for tax evasion, with imprisonment of up to 7 years and penalties up to 1.5 times the evaded amount. VAT inputs corresponding to disallowed zero-ratings are also reversed.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam maintains a managed float regime with the Vietnamese Dong (VND) not fully freely convertible. Current account transactions are generally convertible subject to SBV regulations, while capital account transactions require strict licensing, registration, and underlying economic substance. Foreign-invested enterprises (FIEs) may open VND and foreign currency accounts at authorized banks, but foreign currency lending and settlement are restricted to permitted purposes. Profit remittance abroad is permitted after audited financial statements, completion of annual corporate income tax (CIT) finalization, fulfillment of all tax obligations, and submission of the audited financial report plus remittance application to the tax authority; remittance is made through an authorized bank. Capital contribution must be made fully in accordance with the investment registration certificate (IRC) and enterprise registration certificate (ERC), with the equity capital transfer and loan principals/interest remittance subject to registration with the SBV via the authorized bank. A virtual card issuer's cross-border fees, interchange settlements, program manager fees, and network assessment charges are classified as current-account services that may be remitted abroad upon invoices and underlying contracts; however, any deemed capital export, foreign loan, or intercompany funding must be registered under the Foreign Loan Registration regime. Practical hurdles include documentation-heavy remittance files, SBV's ex-post inspection powers, delays in obtaining tax confirmation for profit remittance, and the requirement that all foreign currency inflows from exports or card scheme settlements in VND be channeled through an authorized bank; the State Bank may impose temporary suspension of foreign currency payments in cases of national foreign reserve stress.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SBV Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN (as amended by Circular 02/2018/TT-NHNN and relevant regulations), exporters are generally not required to compulsorily sell all export proceeds; they are required to remit export proceeds into a foreign currency account at an authorized bank within 90 days of actual export (or within the time limit specified by contract, but not exceeding 180 days, and certain exceptions may extend to 360 days with SBV approval in specific sectors). Since Circular 15/2015 abolished the mandatory surrender ratio for exporters, the effective conversion obligation is limited: exporters must convert foreign currency to VND only for the foreign exchange balance requirement or as otherwise directed by the SBV in emergency situations, or when the exporter elects to sell to the authorized bank. For a virtual card issuer, settlement and clearing flows received from international card networks (Mastercard/Visa) are not classical 'export proceeds' but must still be credited to the entity's foreign currency account and, when converted to VND for operational payments, must be converted through an authorized credit institution using an applicable exchange rate, with proper declaration on the foreign exchange surveillance form and underlying clearing statement retained for six years.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) and the Foreign Exchange Control Department under SBV; delegated administration also exercised by the Ministry of Planning and Investment (MPI) for FDI registration and by licensed commercial banks as authorized foreign exchange banks (authorized banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for a locally licensed principal entity: offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., UK-licensed EMIs, EU EMI-licensed issuers, or Singapore-licensed payment institutions) are not permitted to maintain a branch or provide payment card issuance services in Vietnam without a local license, and they are not authorized banks under SBV law. Offshore EMIs may only serve as unlicensed foreign technical service providers (card program management, BIN sponsorship, processing, compliance software) and may not hold Vietnamese client funds or issue VND-denominated virtual cards as principal. A BIN sponsorship arrangement must be structured so the local LLC remains the principal card issuer under a licensed Vietnamese bank's acquiring/issuing sponsorship or under the fintech's own e-money/payment intermediary license from the SBV; otherwise the activities constitute unlicensed banking/payment intermediation under the Law on Credit Institutions and Decree 52/2024/ND-CP, exposing the entity to criminal and administrative liability. BIN sponsorship by foreign entities is permissible only if the foreign sponsor is itself an authorized payment institution in its home jurisdiction and the actual settlement occurs through a licensed Vietnamese authorized bank; even then, the SBV requires prior notification/approval for cross-border payment services and every VND clearing must occur in-country.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital must be contributed by the foreign shareholders through the capital account held at an authorized bank established in Vietnam, registered with the SBV in the foreign direct investment (FDI) capital account monitoring system. The authorized bank receives the foreign wire (SWIFT MT103) with purpose code addressing 'Direct investment equity contribution' and must match the amount to the IRC-registered charter capital; the LLC must maintain a separate VND current account and a foreign currency account for current-account operational funds. The capital injection must be reported via the e-degree/information system of the SBV using the FDI enterprise's enterprise identification code; the bank issues a capital receipt confirmation (Xác nhận góp vốn) after checking that the remitter matches the shareholder of record, the reference is made to the IRC/ERC, and no money laundering red flags are triggered. It is not permissible to inject equity through third-party or intercompany netting without SBV approval. The loan or equity portion may instead be recorded as a shareholder loan subject to a foreign loan registration and must be routed through the borrower's foreign currency account with a purpose code 'Private sector external borrowing - direct lending'.Permitted clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfer through an authorized Vietnamese bank for service fees, licensing royalties, card scheme settlement, program management fees, and vendor payments, with mandatory decoding into the proper FX purpose code and supporting contract; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) issued via SWIFT MT700 for physical import trade in card hardware if applicable, with bill of lading and documents presented under UCP600; (3) cross-border payment card scheme settlement via Mastercard/Visa clearing messages (ISO 8583 / ISO 20022) submitted through an authorized acquiring/sponsoring bank that nets the VND positions and converts through the interbank FX market; (4) domestic direct debit, NAPAS payment gateways, and QR/ACH rails for VND disbursements to Vietnamese merchants/suppliers; and (5) approved escrow or trust accounts for pass-through settlement only if not constituting deposit-taking. MT103 remittances above USD-equivalent thresholds require the bank to perform EDD, check the transaction against SBV Circular 09/2023/TT-NHNN and the State Bank reporting thresholds, and for payments to tax haven jurisdictions the bank may require enhanced documentation. All outbound trade disbursements must be executed strictly after the corresponding tax withholding obligations (Foreign Contractor Tax, VAT, or CIT on outbound payments) are satisfied or documented as exempt, and the entity's auditor and the bank's compliance officer must approve the underlying economic substance.Onshore USD/VND deliverable forward contracts executed between the LLC and a Vietnamese authorized bank, with underlying, contract term, and trading purpose fully documented and settled by physical delivery of VND against USD.FX swaps between the LLC and an authorized bank in Vietnam, including simultaneous spot and forward trades for working capital management, provided the notional and tenor are matched to actual commercial cash-flow exposure.Cross-currency swaps (USD/VND or USD/foreign currency) with an authorized credit institution where the VND leg is registered as a derivative transaction with the SBV and hedges a documented commercial loan or card settlement exposure.Interest rate and currency swap transactions under master agreements (ISDA/ISEMDA or Vietnam Master Agreement) executed by an authorized bank, subject to bank credit approval and the absence of any speculative (non-hedging) purpose.Onshore purchasing of USD against VND only at the authorized bank's listed buying/selling rates within the official daily band; use of offshore NDF (non-deliverable forward) contracts referencing USD/VND is generally prohibited for Vietnamese resident enterprises unless specifically permitted by SBV, and any offshore hedge that is in substance a forward tied to VND may be void or unenforceable in Vietnam. Treat NDFs as not permissible unless socio-economic underlying necessity and SBV direct authorization are first obtained, in which case the hedge must be routed through an authorized bank or licensed securities broker.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for a locally licensed principal entity: offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., UK-licensed EMIs, EU EMI-licensed issuers, or Singapore-licensed payment institutions) are not permitted to maintain a branch or provide payment card issuance services in Vietnam without a local license, and they are not authorized banks under SBV law. Offshore EMIs may only serve as unlicensed foreign technical service providers (card program management, BIN sponsorship, processing, compliance software) and may not hold Vietnamese client funds or issue VND-denominated virtual cards as principal. A BIN sponsorship arrangement must be structured so the local LLC remains the principal card issuer under a licensed Vietnamese bank's acquiring/issuing sponsorship or under the fintech's own e-money/payment intermediary license from the SBV; otherwise the activities constitute unlicensed banking/payment intermediation under the Law on Credit Institutions and Decree 52/2024/ND-CP, exposing the entity to criminal and administrative liability. 20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Foreign corporate shareholders in a Vietnamese LLC are not subject to a separate dividend withholding tax upon distribution of after-tax profits; dividends are paid out of after-tax CIT earnings without additional withholding. However, the transfer of profits abroad is subject to administrative conditions and creditor protection rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A taxpayer is required to prepare a Local File and Master File (TP Documentation) if the related-party transaction turnover in a year exceeds 50 billion VND or total related-party transaction value exceeds 30 billion VND, and the taxpayer does not fall below the annual revenue and transaction thresholds specified in Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, Article 18. For smaller taxpayers, a TP form (Form 03-1/TNDN) must be submitted with the annual CIT return. Related-party transactions with low-value thresholds are exempt from full documentation if revenue is below 100 billion VND and total related-party transaction value is below 5 billion VND.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam applies an economic-substance-based analysis through the related-party transaction principles of Decree 132/2020/ND-CP; taxpayers must demonstrate that the transaction substance, functions, assets, and risks align with the arm's length result. Although Vietnam does not apply the OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations applicable in UAE/BCI jurisdictions, the General Department of Taxation (GDT) may recharacterize arrangements lacking commercial rationality and substance; for fintech virtual card issuers, substance review focuses on actual card processing activities, talent, risk management, and decision-making functions in VN. The 'substance over form' doctrine also applies under Law on Tax Administration Article 19, allowing the tax authority to disregard transactions without bona fide business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax amount, plus late payment interest at 0.03% per day. Criminal liability under the Penal Code 2015 (amended 2017) Article 200 for tax evasion: from 100 million to 1.5 billion VND evaded, up to 3 years' suspended imprisonment; 1.5 to 4.5 billion VND, 3 to 7 years' imprisonment; above 4.5 billion VND, 7 to 12 years' imprisonment and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors, general directors, chief accountants, and legal representatives can be jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes if they knowingly approved false declarations or diverted assets to frustrate collection. Travel bans are issued under the Tax Administration Law for legal representatives with outstanding tax debts. Criminal conviction exposes directors to imprisonment, fines of up to 5 billion VND and civil restitution of defrauded State revenues.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The GDT may recharacterize or disregard the entity's transactions, deem a permanent establishment of the foreign parent, impose 20% CIT on income deemed earned in VN, and deny deductible intercompany fees; penalties for incorrect transfer pricing documentation can be 1 to 3 times the adjusted tax deficit, plus late payment interest, under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP. The LLC may face license revocation for failing to meet the operating purpose for which it was licensed.

Director personal liability: Directors who are signatories to sham transactions are subject to administrative fines, and if the GDT proves intent to conceal a benefitted foreign principal, they may be prosecuted as accomplices to tax evasion; travel bans are applied for tax arrears, and courts may issue a prohibition from holding management positions.
AML / Unlicensed Money Services (Fintech Red Flag)

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed payment operations are subject to administrative fines under SBV regulations of up to 2 billion VND and shutdown of the operation. Violations of AML obligations carry up to 500 million VND in fines; serious violations are criminally prosecuted as illegal money transmission under Penal Code Article 329, subject to imprisonment of up to 10 years and property confiscation.

Director personal liability: Senior managers can be criminally charged, personally fined, and imprisoned; SBV inspectors may suspend banking relationships and block personal bank accounts of directors involved in layering or undisclosed ownership arrangements; civil liability for customer losses triggered by unlicensed issuance flows to the entity and directors jointly.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP and Decree 126/2020/ND-CP, tax authorities may make an upward adjustment of taxable income, disallow excess interest deductions (with a 30% EBITDA cap), impose penalties of 20% of the under-declared tax amount plus interest, and require updated TP documentation. Fines for non-submission of TP documentation or failure to justify arm's length prices range from 1 to 5 times the tax underpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors and chief financial officers may be personally liable for penalties as control persons in administrative penalty decisions; criminal liability arises if the transfer pricing manipulation is classified as tax evasion; travel bans against directors are common enforcement tools where the LLC is unable to pay the adjusted tax, and directors may be required to personally remit withholding taxes on distributed funds under joint liability provisions.
