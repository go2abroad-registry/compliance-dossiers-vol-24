Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Viet Nam maintains a managed-floating exchange rate regime with full current-account convertibility in principle but strict capital-account controls. The Vietnamese Dong (VND) is not freely convertible offshore; all foreign-currency inflows and outflows must be settled through SBV-licensed commercial banks and reported under Balance of Payments (BoP) statistics. Current-account transactions, including cross-border media-buying service payments, advertising revenue receipts, profit dividends, interest, and royalties, are permitted without prior SBV approval provided the resident enterprise presents authentic underlying contracts, invoices, tax clearance certificates, and corporate authorizations. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI) equity contributions, offshore loans, and equity transfers, require prior registration under the Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) or Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) and, where applicable, a separate foreign-loan/contribution registration with the SBV. Profit remittance by a foreign investor in an LLC is allowed once annually after the audited Vietnamese statutory financial statements are finalized and after corporate income tax (CIT) obligations are settled; no additional remittance withholding tax is imposed, but the remittance will be blocked if tax arrears or an unfiled CIT finalization exist. Practical hurdles include the requirement to open a dedicated capital contribution account, the mandatory provision of audited financial statements, local banks requiring a tax compliance certification, and documentary scrutiny designed to prevent disguised capital flight.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular No. 02/2021/TT-NHNN (Part II, Section 1) and the Law on Foreign Exchange, all export proceeds of the Viet Nam LLC for advertising and media-buying services must be repatriated into a foreign-currency account held at an SBV-licensed bank within 180 days from the date the export service is deemed completed, typically the date of customs clearance for physical goods or the contractual settlement/financial completion date for digital services. There is no fixed compulsory conversion ratio to VND under the current regulatory framework; 100% of the export proceeds must be collected and retained in a foreign-currency account, but the enterprise must sell foreign currency to the licensed bank to satisfy lawful VND-denominated obligations when required or upon request of the bank. Statutory exemptions and extensions are available only with SBV approval in cases of justified overdue receivables from non-resident trading partners, force majeure, or documented foreign insolvency proceedings. Non-compliance triggers administrative penalties under Decree No. 88/2019/ND-CP and Decree No. 86/2024/ND-CP, including fines, suspension of foreign-currency operations, and potential criminal liability for deliberate evasion in egregious cases.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV / Ngan hang Nha nuoc Viet Nam), the sole central bank and foreign exchange administration under the Law on the State Bank of Vietnam No. 46/2010/QH12 and the Law on Credit Institutions as amended, exercising FX regulatory authority through the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange No. 05/2005/PL-UBTVQH11 (as amended by Ordinance No. 06/2013/UBTVQH13), Circular No. 02/2021/TT-NHNN on foreign exchange operations by credit institutions, and Circular No. 05/2021/TT-NHNN on lending to foreigners.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low legal and operational feasibility rating (Low). Offshore digital EMIs such as Mercury, Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed by the State Bank of Vietnam to maintain VND accounts, issue domestic payment accounts, or act as a principal clearing channel for an onshore Vietnamese corporate entity. Under Vietnamese FX and anti-money-laundering rules, all VND and foreign-currency payment transactions of a resident enterprise must be conducted through SBV-licensed credit institutions; routing primary operating cash flows through an unauthorized EMI would constitute illegal foreign-exchange intermediation and payment-services infringement under Decree No. 87/2019/ND-CP and Law on Credit Institutions. An offshore EMI may be used solely as an ancillary receivables/payables tool for settling invoices with foreign ad networks and publishers in USD or EUR, provided the Vietnamese LLC still maintains a compliant local treasury account and ensures that any onshore-to-offshore funding transfers are properly purpose-coded and documented. The principal operational treasury should therefore be a tier-1 local bank account with SWIFT/MT103 capability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Fully compliant FDI inbound capital protocol: (1) The offshore parent contributes equity in accordance with the IRC/ERC and must post a notice of capital contribution to the SBV's Foreign Investment Administration via the local bank; (2) the LLC opens a dedicated Capital Contribution Account (Tai khoan von dau tu truc tiep) at an SBV-licensed authorized bank, denominated in USD and VND, used exclusively for inward FDI funds; (3) the parent bank originates a SWIFT MT103 wire with accurate BPM6 Balance of Payments purpose code for 'Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (code 5.1.1) referencing the IRC number and the Vietnamese company name; (4) the receiving bank verifies the contribution against the registered capital schedule before crediting the capital contribution ledger; (5) the LLC may then convert USD to VND at the prevailing bank TT rate for working capital; (6) quarterly ongoing reporting of disbursements and capital usage is filed with the SBV through the electronic BoP portal; (7) all transactions are subject to the 180-day rule for capital expenditure utilization as documented in the IRC; (8) no secondary foreign-currency loan is permitted without registering through a separate SBV foreign-loan file. Failure to route through the capital account invalidates the contribution for tax and licensing purposes.For affiliate media-buying services, outbound disbursements to foreign ad networks, publishers, and traffic sources are executed through the Vietnamese LLC's licensed bank using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 (cover) for bank-to-bank settlement. Commercial Letters of Credit are not the standard instrument for service-based advertising transactions given the absence of physical goods and are generally inapplicable; documentary collections are used only for the rare tangible prepaid media trade. Each outbound payment must be accompanied by a trade-payment authorization form, the underlying service agreement and publisher invoice, a description of the transaction purpose and BPM6 code, and proof of applicable withholding tax (Foreign Contractor Tax) if the Vietnamese entity is the payer. The bank debits the FCY account, ensures the beneficiary is not OFAC/UN-sanctioned, and clears the transfer on the correspondent banking network (typically via J.P. Morgan, Bank of New York Mellon, or Standard Chartered) in USD. Domestic publisher payments in VND are settled on the National Payment Corporation of Viet Nam (NAPAS) automated clearing house or through real-time gross settlement (RTGS) within the Vietnamese interbank system. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low legal and operational feasibility rating (Low). Offshore digital EMIs such as Mercury, Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed by the State Bank of Vietnam to maintain VND accounts, issue domestic payment accounts, or act as a principal clearing channel for an onshore Vietnamese corporate entity. Under Vietnamese FX and anti-money-laundering rules, all VND and foreign-currency payment transactions of a resident enterprise must be conducted through SBV-licensed credit institutions; routing primary operating cash flows through an unauthorized EMI would constitute illegal foreign-exchange intermediation and payment-services infringement under Decree No. 87/2019/ND-CP and Law on Credit Institutions. An offshore EMI may be used solely as an ancillary receivables/payables tool for settling invoices with foreign ad networks and publishers in USD or EUR, provided the Vietnamese LLC still maintains a compliant local treasury account and ensures that any onshore-to-offshore funding transfers are properly purpose-coded and documented. The principal operational treasury should therefore be a tier-1 local bank account with SWIFT/MT103 capability.
