Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Commission (VCIRC), Ministry of Finance and Treasury, Port Vila; operational contact desks at the VAT and Excise Section; official national portal maintained at gov.vu with no fully automated fiscal e-filing platform as of the current regime.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first incorporate an International Company under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] through a licensed registered agent and obtain a business licence from the Vanuatu Investment Promotion Authority (VIPA). The IC then applies in writing to the Commissioner of VAT on the prescribed VCIRC registration form, submitting the certificate of incorporation, registered office and agent details, projected 12-month turnover, ownership structure, and bank references. The Commission assigns a unique VAT identification number (also used as the customs client code for import/export clearances). Compulsory registration is triggered once annual taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold; voluntary registration is available below threshold. Processing normally completes within 5 to 15 working days, and the VAT number must appear on all tax invoices and customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: a Vanuatu International Company is a non-resident limited liability vehicle classified within the exempt offshore zone. It is outside the Vanuatu income tax base because Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no withholding tax. The IC is nevertheless within the territorial scope of the Value Added Tax Act for supplies sourced in or imported into Vanuatu and must zero-rate its offshore bonded-trade exports to non-resident buyers.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists because Vanuatu imposes no entity-level income tax. Statutorily available elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration under the VAT Act for ICs below the compulsory threshold; (ii) election by the IC to abandon exempt international status and operate as an ordinary resident company by obtaining an in-country business licence from VIPA; and (iii) election of the bonded warehouse deferred-entry regime by which customs duty, excise, and VAT are suspended until goods are withdrawn either for local home consumption or for export.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover (compulsory); voluntary registration is permitted below this threshold for ICs expecting to make only zero-rated export supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 11 of the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 247] to goods physically exported from Vanuatu and to prescribed offshore services supplied to non-residents. For bonded wine and spirits, eligibility requires that the goods are removed from a licensed bonded warehouse solely for export, that the IC holds the contract with the foreign buyer, and that export occurs within the statutory 28-day window from removal from bond. Goods supplied by an IC to residents of Vanuatu are ineligible for zero-rating and would be taxable at 13% if permitted under the IC licensing conditions. Input VAT incurred on bonded storage, importation, and freight can be recovered by the IC through periodic VAT returns where the corresponding supply is zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration cleared by Vanuatu Customs and explicitly flagged as export
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing delivery to a destination outside Vanuatu
  • Bonded warehouse withdrawal order and excise removal certificate showing release of goods from the licensed warehouse
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin (EUR.1 or Form A where preferential treatment is claimed)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 receipt evidencing payment by the foreign buyer
  • VAT tax invoice printed with the IC's VAT registration number and an unambiguous zero-rated annotation

No real-time fiscal clearance regime, e-invoicing API mandate, or digital stamping requirement is currently operational in Vanuatu. Taxpayers must issue sequentially numbered tax invoices in paper or PDF form showing supplier name, VAT identification number, date, description of goods, quantity, and rate. Bonded wine and spirit removals must be supported by customs clearance records in the ASYCUDA environment and the licensed warehouse operator must maintain an excise control ledger; digital fiscal clearance is limited to customs manifest data.

Export substantiation documents must be retained for at least 7 years. Where an IC cannot demonstrate physical export and receipt of foreign currency within the required period, the VCIRC may reassess the supply as taxable at 13%, claw back all input credits previously refunded, and impose interest at 1.5% per month from the date the refund was issued. Civil penalties of up to 50% of the VAT sought to be avoided (minimum VT 50,000) apply for false or incomplete documentation, and fraudulent zero-rating claims are referred for criminal prosecution under the Value Added Tax Act and the Customs Act, carrying fines of up to VT 20,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years. Retrospective assessments may extend over the preceding 5 years in cases involving intentionally misleading export evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Vanuatu has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; there are no exchange controls, no prior approval requirements for inward or outward remittances, and no restrictions on profit or dividend remittances. All foreign exchange receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts either locally or offshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Vanuatu does not impose statutory export proceeds surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirements. Exporters are free to hold trade receipts in VUV or any foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Vanuatu has no dedicated electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing framework. Offshore digital EMIs without a physical licensed banking presence cannot provide regulated treasury clearing or escrow services in-country. An international company engaged in bonded wine and spirits trade must bank through a licensed domestic commercial bank to maintain statutory audit trails and satisfy trade finance counterparties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold exists in Vanuatu in local currency (VUV). Cross-border related-party transactions by International Companies should nevertheless be supported by contemporaneous documentation, including business purpose and arm's-length pricing, to satisfy VFSC substance reviews and anti-money-laundering obligations.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime for International Companies. However, a licensed bonded trade business in wine and spirits must demonstrate genuine local substance, including physical storage premises, local licence possession, and appointed licensed directors/agents, to meet VFSC licensing and AML/CFT standards.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative strike-off and deregistration by VFSC; disqualification of the registered agent; substantial monetary fines under the International Companies Act for false statements; forfeiture of bonds or guarantees associated with the wine/spirits licence.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid fines and third-party losses. Knowingly making false declarations can lead to personal travel bans and imprisonment for related fraud offences.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the International Company status, striking off the corporate registry, revocation of the bonded warehouse licence, freezing of bank accounts, and monetary penalties for breach of the International Companies Act and licensing conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil liability for company debts where the company is adjudged a sham. Travel bans and director disqualification may be ordered, and imprisonment applies to those who operate a shell company to facilitate fraud.
AML / Bonded Trade Violation

Statutory sanction: License suspension or revocation under the Customs Act and VFSC licensing rules; asset forfeiture and fines under the Vanuatu AML/CFT framework; criminal prosecution for money laundering and customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be criminally prosecuted; imprisonment up to 10 years for money-laundering offences; personal fines; prohibition from holding any directorship or licensed position in Vanuatu; potential international travel restrictions while proceedings are pending.
