Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Department of Finance and Treasury, Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Port Vila; taxpayer registration and VAT lodgment are administered through the VRC taxpayer services division.

E-government filing portal

The International Company must first be incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 144] via a VFSC-licensed registered agent, who obtains the IC registration number and certificate of incorporation. For VAT and TIN purposes, the registered agent submits a VRC VAT/TIN registration application together with the certificate of incorporation, certified registered office details, information on directors/shareholders, the entity's Vanuatu business address, and a 12-month forecast of taxable turnover. Non-resident ownership is permitted, no minimum paid-up capital is required, and the TIN is issued once VRC registers the IC as a taxable person.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Vanuatu International Company: statutorily exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty under the International Companies Act; the IC is not subject to entity-level taxation on offshore business activities, although VAT registration obligations apply independently where the IC makes taxable domestic supplies or imports goods into Vanuatu).

Available election forms

Vanuatu does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election mechanism. The International Company's tax-exempt status is automatic and mandatory under the International Companies Act. The only statutory election of practical relevance is voluntary VAT registration under the Value Added Tax Act where annual taxable turnover is below the compulsory threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 4,000,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and VUV 10,000,000 for services under the Value Added Tax Act No. 17 of 1998 (as amended); imported goods are subject to VAT at the border with no threshold exemption for the importer of record.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of goods, including ICU mechanical ventilators, where the goods are physically exported from Vanuatu within the statutory period stipulated by the Director of Customs (generally 28 days from supply) and the supplier retains the required export evidence. Supplies of services to a non-resident performed wholly outside Vanuatu may also qualify. The zero-rate claim is sustainable only if the exporter holds the full statutory export documentation at the time of filing the VAT return and can demonstrate payment receipt from the foreign buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic declaration accepted by VRC Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Vanuatu
  • Commercial Invoice quoting the IC TIN and the VAT zero-rating basis
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/FX credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin supporting the exported goods description

Vanuatu does not currently impose a mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance or fiscal digital stamping regime. Taxable suppliers must issue sequentially numbered conventional invoices (paper or electronic) containing the prescribed VAT Act particulars and retain them for VRC audit inspection; export consignments are processed through the VRC Customs electronic declaration and clearance system with automated documentary checks.

The VRC audits export zero-rating claims by cross-matching the exporter's TIN, customs export entries, shipping manifests, and banking receipts. Where goods are not exported within the statutory period, are reimported into Vanuatu, or the supporting documents are found to be false or materially incomplete, the VAT previously zero-rated is retrospectively clawed back together with default surcharge and interest payable under the Value Added Tax Act. Knowingly submitting fraudulent export declarations constitutes a criminal offence under the VAT Act and the Customs Act, attracting fines and imprisonment, while the VFSC may suspend or strike off the International Company's registration for non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) is freely convertible on both current and capital account. The Exchange Control Act nominally reserves RBV approval powers, but no prior license or approval is required for equity injections, trade payments, dividends, or capital repatriation by a Vanuatu International Company (IC). Profit remittance is unrestricted once standard AML/EDD and sanctions screening are cleared; no minimum-stay or reinvestment rule applies. ICs are treated as non-residents and may hold multi-currency external accounts (USD, AUD, EUR, XPF, VUV) with licensed dealers. The practical constraint is not convertibility but correspondent-bank de-risking sensitivity, which elevates documentary stringency on all USD cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio (0%), and no repatriation deadline for medical-device export earnings. Exporters may retain overseas currency or convert freely. The only obligations are record-keeping and reporting of cross-border transactions under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing framework, including suspicious-matter and threshold reports to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit. Proceeds from ICU ventilator exports invoiced in USD or AUD may be held indefinitely in external currency accounts.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), constituted under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 125], which administers the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 26] and licenses authorized foreign-exchange dealers (all Vanuatu-licensed commercial banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW — not viable as a primary treasury or clearing rail for a Vanuatu International Company. Legal constraints: the International Banking Act 2023 requires any deposit-taking or payment-serving banking business operating in or from Vanuatu to hold an RBV banking license or an international-bank license. Unlicensed offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Dukascopy, and similar neobanks) do not hold Vanuatu licenses; IC use of such an EMI would (i) breach the licensing perimeter if the EMI solicits or receives funds from Vanuatu, (ii) generate name-screening and nested-account red flags for the IC's licensed settlement bank, and (iii) risk immediate de-risking of all Tier-1 Vanuatu bank accounts. The compliant alternative is a licensed-bank multi-currency account with direct SWIFT connectivity; an EMI may be used only as an information overlay in its own licensed jurisdiction and must never touch Vanuatu-sourced funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity for the IC is routed exclusively via SWIFT to a licensed Vanuatu bank. Recommended instruction: MT103 with charge code OUR and purpose code 'Direct investment — subscription of shares in a Vanuatu International Company' (BPM6 direct-investment classification), accompanied by the investor's bank reference and source-of-wealth statement. No RBV exchange-control approval is required; the receiving bank performs transaction monitoring and may file a threshold or suspicious-transaction report with the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit where mandated by law. The IC must first submit a board resolution approving the share issue, the updated share register, and a KYC/UBO pack from a licensed introducer. On receipt, funds are credited within T+1 in USD, AUD, or VUV; conversions to the operating currency occur at spot, with an FX-forward overlay for large committed amounts.Vendor payments for ventilator components and logistics settle by SWIFT MT103 for individual disbursements under the bank's trade-finance threshold, with the payment mandate referencing the purchase order and HS code (HS 9018.19; NAICS 339112). 0% (Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax on International Companies).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in VUV – Vanuatu levies no income tax and has not enacted transfer pricing legislation, so no Local File/Master File statutory thresholds exist. Nevertheless, ESR requires every relevant IC (regardless of revenue) to evidence arm's-length pricing and core income-generating activities (CIGAs) in Vanuatu; recommended documentation is OECD-aligned Master File/Local File plus a CIGA dossier (board minutes, procurement approvals, risk assessment, pricing policy) filed with the ESR report.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu ESR (Economic Substance (Amendment) Act No. 18 of 2019; Economic Substance (Implementation) Regulations 2020) applies to all ICs. A 'non-resident IC' is exempt from tax but must file an ESR notification annually and, where a relevant activity (headquarters, distribution/service centre, holding entity, high-risk IP) is carried on, an ESR report demonstrating: board meetings and decision-making in Vanuatu, adequate employees and premises, and arm's-length active direction. A ventilator manufacturer holding IP, approving contract manufacturers, or assuming product liability in Vanuatu will be treated as conducting a relevant activity; CIGA records must be physically available in Vanuatu. No Vanuatu Business Licence (Cap 246) is required for a non-resident IC with no local physical business presence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Use of Tax-Exempt IC Regime

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: VFSC strike-off with forfeiture of assets to the Government of Vanuatu; fines up to VUV 1,000,000 for trading while struck off. Foreign jurisdiction: reallocation of profits to a permanent establishment, denial of treaty/tax-exempt benefits, 50–100% civil fraud penalties, criminal tax evasion prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for struck-off debts; foreign courts may impose worldwide asset freezes, travel bans and extradition; imprisonment up to 15 years where tax evasion proceeds are laundered through Vanuatu accounts (Proceeds of Crime Act [Cap 274]).
Economic Substance (ESR) Non-Compliance / Shell Company Red Flag

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties: up to VUV 100,000 per late/failed notification; VUV 500,000 for first confirmed substance failure and up to VUV 1,000,000 per month for continuing contravention; up to VUV 10,000,000 for high-risk IP companies; VFSC may apply to the Court for dissolution and may exchange information with foreign tax authorities under TIEAs.

Director personal liability: Officers signing false ESR declarations commit an offence (fine up to VUV 500,000 and/or 12 months' imprisonment); the registered agent will resign on non-compliance, exposing directors to global banking de-risking, public blacklisting as a shell company, and personal civil claims by foreign revenue authorities.
AML / Record-Keeping Failure / Fraudulent Medical Procurement

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT Act No. 35 of 2017: money laundering conviction punishable by fine up to VUV 30,000,000 and/or 15 years' imprisonment for individuals; companies liable to compliance penalties up to VUV 2,000,000; failure to maintain records is a strict-liability offence against the company and each director; VFSC may strike off the IC and notify the Vanuatu FIU. Foreign anti-corruption statutes (FCPA, UKBA) apply to underlying hospital tenders.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for civil restitution of illicit tender proceeds; travel bans, freezing orders, enhanced due diligence blacklisting; extradition risk to the market jurisdiction and imprisonment exceeding 15 years for bribery of public hospital officials where proceeds flow through Vanuatu.
