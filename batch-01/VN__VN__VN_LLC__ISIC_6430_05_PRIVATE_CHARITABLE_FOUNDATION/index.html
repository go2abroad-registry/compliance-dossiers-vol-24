Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under general enterprise law for a private charitable endowment LLC; however, the charter capital must be fully paid within 90 days from issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate, and the company must maintain capital reasonably sufficient for the contemplated grant-making and endowment management activities. There is no escrow account requirement under the Law on Enterprises; capital is paid into a Vietnamese commercial bank account opened in the company's name, and the bank's payment confirmation serves as evidence of contribution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tong cuc Thue) of the Ministry of Finance of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam; national e-tax portal: https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn; e-invoice portal: https://hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested LLC incorporates through the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI), which issues the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); the tax identification number (Ma so thue, MST) is automatically created with the ERC. The enterprise then (1) activates its tax dossier at the local Tax Department; (2) registers for electronic tax filing on the national eTax portal; (3) obtains a digital signature certificate for invoice signing; and (4) registers the electronic invoice template with the General Department of Taxation. A private charitable foundation must also secure its statutory operating license and apply for tax-exemption/charitable status decrees, while grant inflows require only treasury/bank account registration, not a separate VAT registration. Non-resident foreign entities with grant-making operations or contractors deriving Vietnam-source income register for a foreign contractor tax identification number through a Vietnamese paying agent or the local Tax Directorate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident LLC is a standalone taxpayer subject to 20% standard CIT on worldwide income; no pass-through classification; no default tax-exempt zone status.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Vietnamese law. A foreign-invested LLC may elect preferential CIT rates for incentivized sectors (e.g., 10% for high-technology, scientific research and technological development enterprises), reduced small-and-medium-enterprise rates (15% where revenue does not exceed VND 3 billion; 17% where revenue does not exceed VND 5 billion), and the unilateral foreign tax credit election in the annual CIT finalization return. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; anti-hybrid and substance outcomes are imposed through the general anti-avoidance and transfer-pricing rules of Decree 132/2020/ND-CP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory revenue threshold for VAT registration: any entity undertaking Vietnam taxable supplies must register. Only households and individual businesses with annual revenue not exceeding VND 100 million are outside the VAT system. A foreign-invested LLC is therefore always a compulsory VAT registrant irrespective of turnover. Foreign digital and cross-border suppliers without a local establishment must register on the Foreign Supplier E-Portal from the first Vietnam-source supply without a turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies to goods exported from Vietnam, goods/services supplied to export-processing enterprises or non-tariff zones, and services rendered by Vietnamese enterprises to foreign enterprises/organizations for consumption outside Vietnam. Eligibility requires: (i) an international sale or offshore service contract; (ii) the goods having actually left Vietnam evidenced by a customs export declaration; (iii) the service being performed and consumed offshore; (iv) payment received from a foreign non-resident party; and (v) the receipt being shown as a foreign-currency inward remittance into the enterprise's Vietnam bank account. Domestic consumption services and catch-all excluded services (brokerage, tourism, advertising, reinsurance, credit) are not zero-rated. Grant distributions of a charitable endowment are treated as non-taxable fund transfers rather than zero-rated VAT supplies.

  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign-currency payment proof)

Mandatory e-invoicing under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. Businesses must issue XML e-invoices with a digital signature for each supply. E-invoices bearing a tax authority code must be cleared in real time through the General Department of Taxation's secure REST API (digital certificate plus XML/JSON envelope) before transmission to the buyer; e-invoices without a tax authority code, the standard for stable taxpayers, must be transmitted to the GDT data warehouse within one working day (T+1) after issuance and reported monthly. No physical fiscal stamping applies; tax clearance operates through the GDT's eTax and e-Invoice digital platform.

Export VAT refunds receive pre-refund risk-based audit scrutiny by the General Department of Taxation. Failure of the refund documentation test or non-repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days triggers reassessment of the 0% rate to 10% VAT, recovery of the refunded amount plus late-payment interest at 0.03% per day, and an administrative fine of up to 20% of the understated tax under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP. Intentional false-invoice or refund fraud attracts penalties of up to three times the evaded tax and criminal tax-evasion liability under the Vietnamese Penal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vietnamese dong (VND) is a managed currency and is not freely convertible. Viet Nam accepted IMF Article VIII obligations in 2011, so current-account payments and transfers are nominally free from exchange controls, but licensed banks still require underlying documentary proof and statutory declarations at each conversion. Capital-account transactions, including FDI capital contributions, intercompany loans, foreign grants treated as retained endowment capital, and any outbound investment or offshore treasury placements, remain tightly controlled and require either mandatory designation of a specific bank account (the Direct Investment Capital Account or an SBV-approved foreign-currency account), prior SBV registration, or express SBV approval in the individual case. For a non-distributing private charitable foundation, the classical dividend-repatriation hurdle is largely moot because the charter is structured to prohibit profit distributions; nevertheless, any conversion or remittance of endowment funds, whether in or out, must be routed through the licensed bank of record, documented with the correct purpose code, and reported to SBV via the bank's mandatory transaction-reporting platform.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory foreign-currency surrender obligation exists in Viet Nam since Decree 70/2014/ND-CP abolished the former statutory conversion quotas. Export proceeds must still be repatriated and effectively credited to a foreign-currency account maintained at a licensed credit institution within 180 days of the export date, extendable to 365 days for projects expressly approved by SBV. For the subject charitable foundation, export activity would fall outside its constitutional objects; all foreign grant receipts, donations, and endowment contributions must be credited directly to the foundation's designated foreign-currency account at a licensed bank in Viet Nam and may not be netted, pre-credited, or settled through offshore collection accounts, which would constitute a violation of the foreign-currency circulation restrictions.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV; Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam), exercising exclusive authority over foreign exchange administration, cross-border payment approval, capital account surveillance, and the licensing and supervision of credit institutions, foreign bank branches, and licensed payment intermediaries. The Foreign Exchange Control Division of SBV and its provincial branches operate the registration, reporting, and approval windows that apply to continuing foreign-invested treasury operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low, with significant legal constraints. Viet Nam does not license offshore non-bank e-money institutions (EMIs) and does not recognize foreign EMI account numbers as permissible settlement accounts under Decree 52/2024/ND-CP on non-cash payments. A foreign digital EMI such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud may only enter the market through a licensed Vietnamese payment intermediary arrangement, and settlement must ultimately occur on the books of a licensed credit institution. Vietnamese banks will treat routing of charitable grant funds through offshore EMI omnibus accounts as a high-risk arrangement and may reject or suspend the instruction. The compliant architecture therefore mandates that all grant receipts and endowment movements clear directly over SWIFT to and from the foundation's licensed bank accounts in Viet Nam.

Defense evidence: A formal treasury manual approved by the board segregating account classes (endowment custody account, operational expense account, expenditure grant distribution account); a board-approved endowment investment policy defining permissible instruments and counterparties; monthly bank reconciliations tied to management accounts; annual audited financial statements of the foundation; and a standing instruction that all transactions carry a purpose code consistent with the foundation's licensed scope.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For any contribution structured as charter capital of the foreign-invested LLC, the remitter must wire funds exclusively to the Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened by the foundation at a licensed bank under Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN. The capital contribution schedule must align with the IRC and the charter's payment deadline, ordinarily 90 days from the date of the IRC, and each inward SWIFT MT103/MT202 instruction must carry the correct beneficiary account, the foundation's legal name, and the investment purpose code recognized by SBV for FDI capital contributions. The receiving bank will notify SBV through the statutory reporting system and will refuse credit to any non-designated account. For grant-style donations not structured as equity, the foundation must use a separate foreign-currency account approved for charitable receipts under the MHA establishment license; all such receipts must be supported by the underlying donation agreement and reported by the bank to SBV with the appropriate current-transfer purpose code. Any intercompany or shareholder loan intended to be part of the endowment must be first registered with SBV if it exceeds short-term trade credit thresholds, and must flow through the DICA as an eligible borrowing.Outbound treasury rail selection depends on payment class. Programmatic grant disbursements to foreign beneficiaries are executed through ordinary wire transfers via the SWIFT system using MT103 messages, carrying the designated beneficiary bank and account details and a purpose code consistent with a current-account transfer; the foundation must present the executed grant agreement and beneficiary identification to the bank at the time of instruction. For procurement of goods, services, or scientific equipment financed from the endowment, the foundation may use documentary trade instruments, including commercial letters of credit (L/C at sight or usance) and standby L/Cs or bank guarantees where counterparties require on-demand security; the documentation trail for L/Cs must include the underlying purchase contract, invoice, transport documents, and insurance certificate as applicable. All outward FX transfers require the foundation to first convert VND for USD/EUR at its authorized bank or to utilize existing foreign-currency grant balances, and every instruction must be accompanied by a completed purpose-of-payment declaration, otherwise the bank will block the remittance pending supplementary review.Deliverable onshore foreign-exchange forward contracts between the foundation and its licensed custodian bank, with tenors up to a maximum of 365 days, available only where a genuine underlying transaction exposure exists and documented per Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN.FX swaps (VND against USD, EUR, or another freely convertible currency) executed onshore between the foundation and a licensed credit institution for cash-flow management, subject to the maturity and underlying-exposure documentation requirements of Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN.Plain-vanilla foreign-exchange options purchased from a licensed bank by the foundation (beneficial to the foundation as option holder) to cap conversion costs on committed foreign grant disbursements or scheduled endowment conversions.Offshore VND non-deliverable forwards (NDFs), limited to non-resident counterparties transacting with the foundation; onshore entities in Viet Nam are not licensed to access an onshore NDF market, and the foundation may only use this instrument indirectly via a compliant foreign parent or donor if deemed contractually necessary and cleared by its legal counsel.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low, with significant legal constraints. Viet Nam does not license offshore non-bank e-money institutions (EMIs) and does not recognize foreign EMI account numbers as permissible settlement accounts under Decree 52/2024/ND-CP on non-cash payments. A foreign digital EMI such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud may only enter the market through a licensed Vietnamese payment intermediary arrangement, and settlement must ultimately occur on the books of a licensed credit institution. Vietnamese banks will treat routing of charitable grant funds through offshore EMI omnibus accounts as a high-risk arrangement and may reject or suspend the instruction. The compliant architecture therefore mandates that all grant receipts and endowment movements clear directly over SWIFT to and from the foundation's licensed bank accounts in Viet Nam.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File preparation is mandatory if: (i) total revenue exceeds VND 50 billion, or (ii) total related-party transaction value exceeds VND 30 billion, or (iii) the entity has related-party transactions and is not a small or micro enterprise under Vietnamese law. De minimis exemption for SMEs: total revenue < VND 50 billion and total assets < VND 25 billion, with no related-party transactions exceeding the arm's-length threshold. Country-by-Country Report threshold: VND 18,000 billion consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, related-party transactions must reflect actual business substance. For a charitable foundation, economic substance requires: local decision-making, local employees, real office premises, and active grant management. Thin capitalization rule: net interest expenses exceeding 30% of EBITDA are non-deductible. Charitable status is contingent on ongoing operational substance; shell-like foundations risk profit re-characterization, retroactive taxation, and license revocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Penal Code 2015 (as amended): fine from VND 100 million to VND 4.5 billion and/or imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years. Administrative: penalty up to 3 times the evaded tax, late payment interest, and revocation of tax-free status.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives can be held personally criminally liable, subject to travel bans, asset seizure, and extradition risk; civil restitution ordered by court.
Shell Company / Abusive Non-Profit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the charitable foundation registration; retrospective CIT at 20% plus penalties; administrative fines under Decree 128/2020/ND-CP up to VND 100 million; forced dissolution.

Director personal liability: Board members face personal liability for unauthorized distributions and fraudulent grant reporting; travel bans and prohibition from management roles in Vietnam.
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the AML Law 2022 up to VND 1 billion; criminal sanctions under Penal Code Art. 324 (money laundering) with imprisonment up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face criminal prosecution if they knowingly facilitated money laundering; personal fines and seizure of assets; travel bans pending investigation.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment by tax authorities; additional CIT at 20%; penalty 20-30% of additional tax; interest at 0.03%/day; administrative fines up to VND 25,000,000 for non-compliance with documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly liable if they signed false TP documentation; criminal liability for fraudulent records; travel restrictions during TP audits.
Improper Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 100% of the illegally remitted amount; back taxes plus interest; suspension of foreign investment privileges.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unauthorized remittances; criminal liability for illegal capital flight (Penal Code Art. 189); travel bans and asset freezes.
