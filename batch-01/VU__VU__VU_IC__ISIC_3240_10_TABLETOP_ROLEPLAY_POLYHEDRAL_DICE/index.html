Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), a department of the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management of the Republic of Vanuatu; official portal: https://vrc.gov.vu (VAT administration, customs single-window, and taxpayer services). Corporate registry counter-party: Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporation: the foreign beneficial owner instructs a VFSC-licensed registered agent to incorporate the International Company under the International Companies Act [CAP 222]; the VFSC issues the Certificate of Incorporation and an IC registration number, which is the entity’s statutory corporate ID; no minimum capital is required and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted. Step 2 — Tax ID / VAT registration: there is no effective income-tax TIN regime in Vanuatu, so the IC is not issued a general tax ID unless it registers for VAT; export-only manufacturers should voluntarily register for VAT under s.38(5) of the VAT Act [CAP 248] to recover input VAT on resins, moulds and packaging; if the IC makes local taxable supplies in Vanuatu above the statutory threshold, mandatory registration is due within 21 days after the end of the month in which the threshold is exceeded (application: VRC VAT Form 1, accompanied by Certificate of Incorporation, registered agent’s certificate, registered office address, expected turnover and bank details; the VRC issues a VAT certificate and VAT number in the format VAT-XXXX). Step 3 — Customs: the IC obtains an exporter code from the Vanuatu Customs Service division of the VRC for lodging export entries. Constraint: an IC is restricted by s.3(2) of the IC Act [CAP 222] from carrying on domestic business in Vanuatu; export-only sales to non-residents, or contracting with third-country toll manufacturers, are the compliant archetypes; any physical manufacturing footprint inside Vanuatu should be ring-fenced in a separately incorporated domestic subsidiary (also zero-income-tax, but VAT- and customs-exposed).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Vanuatu International Company is statutorily exempt, under ss.49-50 of the International Companies Act [CAP 222] and the absence of any effective income tax law, from income tax, corporation tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, inheritance tax and stamp duty on all profits and gains derived within or outside Vanuatu; it is treated as non-resident for direct-tax purposes, and neither pass-through nor entity-level taxation applies. The only operative national tax is Value Added Tax (12.5%) on a territorial supply basis: local taxable supplies attract VAT, goods exported from Vanuatu are zero-rated, and no VAT burden arises on export revenue.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime because no corporate income tax is in operation. Statutory elections available to the IC: (1) voluntary VAT registration under s.38(5) VAT Act [CAP 248] for input-VAT recovery on export inputs, subject to a minimum registration period and quarterly refund filings; (2) migration/continuation election under the IC Act [CAP 222] to redomicile the company to, or from, another jurisdiction without winding-up; (3) conversion election into a domestic company under the Companies Act [CAP 191], which forfeits IC status and the s.3(2) local-business restriction but creates no direct-tax disadvantage because domestic companies are equally free of income tax; (4) no OECD-global-minimum-tax election is currently exercisable in Vanuatu, as no qualifying income-tax law and no IIR/UTPR mechanism is yet in force.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 4,000,000 (approximately USD 33,000) of taxable supplies in Vanuatu in any rolling 12-month period (VAT Act [CAP 248] s.38; confirm current VRC guidance for a possible uplift under the VAT (Amendment) Act 2022). Nexus is territorial: VAT applies where goods are produced, delivered or made available in Vanuatu or where services are physically performed in Vanuatu, regardless of the supplier’s place of incorporation. A foreign IC selling goods that are manufactured, stored and delivered from Vanuatu to overseas buyers is within the zero-rating regime rather than the local taxable regime, whereas any sale of goods delivered within Vanuatu counts toward the threshold; export-only sellers may register voluntarily to claim input-VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under s.6(2) and the First Schedule of the VAT Act [CAP 248] to (a) consideration for goods physically exported from Vanuatu by the supplier within 30 days of the tax invoice, and (b) services supplied to a non-resident where the service relates to goods to be exported and is consumed wholly outside Vanuatu. For ISIC 3240 epoxy-resin role-play dice: the IC must export finished goods via a Vanuatu port or airport (Port Vila or Santo) under a named foreign buyer’s order; title and risk must pass to the buyer at the FOB export point; samples, replacements and warranty items count as supplies and must be supported by the same export trail; a PO Box delivery or an invoice without physical export is outside zero-rating and is taxable at 12.5%. Exported goods also qualify for input-VAT refunds on resins, moulds and packaging purchased in Vanuatu, but the refund claim must be lodged with the quarterly VAT return and accompanied by the full documentation bundle.

  • Original Customs Export Declaration (Vanuatu Customs Single Administrative Document) endorsed with the clearance stamp and exporter code of the VU_IC
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical dispatch from a Vanuatu port or airport to the overseas buyer’s destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed Vanuatu bank, or payment-gateway payout statement (Stripe/PayPal) matched at invoice level, evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list cross-referencing the customs export declaration (HS Code 9504.90 / toy-and-game classification per ISIC 3240 alignment)
  • Signed export contract or purchase order from the non-resident buyer, plus the VRC VAT export schedule (Form VRC-VAT-EX) where a refund is claimed

Not mandated. Vanuatu has no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping system for ISIC 3240 manufactured goods as of the dossier effective date. Taxpayers must issue and retain VAT invoices in standard VRC format (paper or PDF) with sequential numbering, supplier and buyer names, VAT number, and VAT-exclusive/inclusive amounts; export consignments must be covered by the customs export declaration. The VRC operates a digital portal for VAT return filing and payment (monthly or quarterly), but invoice clearance remains post-audit rather than real-time. Payment-rail evidence (Stripe, PayPal, bank remittance) is accepted as corroborating export documentation when mapped to invoice numbers.

VRC may audit zero-rating claims within the 3-year assessment window (s.72 VAT Act [CAP 248]). If the exporter fails to produce the documentation bundle, or the foreign-exchange proceeds are not traceable within 180 days of the invoice date, the zero-rated output is recharacterised as taxable at 12.5% with interest at 1.5% per month (18% per annum) under s.70, and a clawback assessment is issued for the full unrecovered input-VAT credits. Fraudulent or reckless export declarations attract s.69 penalties of 200% of the tax evaded and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; false customs entries additionally engage the Customs Act [CAP 245] s.255 with fines up to VT 1,000,000 and forfeiture of the goods. For crowdfunding and card payments (Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal), aggregate platform payouts without per-invoice remittance mapping are treated as unsubstantiated and are scrutinised as potential disguised tax evasion; exporters must maintain platform settlement reports, chargeback logs, and invoice-level reconciliation for audit defence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement, thereby ensuring unrestricted current account convertibility and free transfer of payments for current international transactions. Capital account flows are administratively exempt for non-resident International Companies, which are treated as external to the domestic Vatu economy. There are no statutory restrictions on maintaining and settling foreign currency accounts, and no withholding tax or income tax is imposed on profit remittances by an International Company to non-residents. The only practical hurdles arise from bank-side AML/KYC due diligence and the need to demonstrate beneficial ownership and source of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement applies to a Vanuatu International Company. Where goods are physically exported from the Republic of Vanuatu, a licensed trading entity resident in Vanuatu must repatriate proceeds within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless the RBV grants a longer period. An International Company operating purely as a foreign-trading entity outside the domestic customs and exchange-control perimeter is exempt from mandatory conversion of foreign-currency receipts, provided it does not transact in Vatu with domestic residents. The company should maintain non-resident status by keeping all operational accounts in currencies other than the Vatu and by recording export invoices in USD or another external currency.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) acting under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 190] and the Financial Dealers Licensing Act [Cap 225].

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Mainstream multi-currency electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex and Payoneer generally accept Vanuatu-registered companies only as payment-disbursement beneficiaries, not as full banking clients. Legal constraints include EMI policy restrictions on FATF/EU-list or under-defined jurisdictions, insistence on a physical rep office in a recognized PSD2 jurisdiction, and the requirement for the underlying corporation to provide audited financial statements for the last two years. The most effective substitute is to route shelf payments through Stripe's Hong Kong or Singapore entity, or through PayPal's US account with a verified business KYC, while retaining an ANZ Port Vila account for treasury concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's personal or corporate account to the ANZ/BSP foreign-currency escrow account. The SWIFT field 70 must contain the clear remittance purpose 'Capital Subscription—Issue of Shares' and field 50 should match the name on the passport. A share subscription agreement and board resolution must be pre-delivered to the bank's compliance officer. RBV exchange-control notification (Form EC/1) is required only if the injection is denominated in or converted into Vatu; for USD/GBP/EUR no approval is needed. Sums above USD 100,000 trigger a source-of-funds questionnaire and the bank may request a blockchain or card-settlement trail for the funds' provenance.Supplier invoices for epoxy resin, molding silicone, pigments, and polyhedral-dice packaging materials are settled via SWIFT MT103 using purpose code '2230' (imports of goods) or the domestic equivalent '2000', accompanied by the pro-forma invoice and purchase order. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Mainstream multi-currency electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex and Payoneer generally accept Vanuatu-registered companies only as payment-disbursement beneficiaries, not as full banking clients. Legal constraints include EMI policy restrictions on FATF/EU-list or under-defined jurisdictions, insistence on a physical rep office in a recognized PSD2 jurisdiction, and the requirement for the underlying corporation to provide audited financial statements for the last two years. The most effective substitute is to route shelf payments through Stripe's Hong Kong or Singapore entity, or through PayPal's US account with a verified business KYC, while retaining an ANZ Port Vila account for treasury concentration.
