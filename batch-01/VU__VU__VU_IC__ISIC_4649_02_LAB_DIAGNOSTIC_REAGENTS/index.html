E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) is not within the Vanuatu income tax net and has no automatic domestic tax file. To claim zero-rated VAT or conduct VAT-relevant local transactions, the IC must submit a VAT registration application on Form VAT 01 together with the IC certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, register details, director/shareholder identification, proof of a physical or licensed registered office in Vanuatu, and evidence of intended taxable activity. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods (including diagnostic reagents and IVD assays) that are physically exported from Vanuatu to a non-resident recipient, or provided as offshore services, provided the supplier retains contemporaneous proof of export, the goods leave Vanuatu within the statutory timeframe prescribed in the VAT Act, and the supply is evidenced by a valid export sale and transfer of ownership outside Vanuatu. A VAT-registered IC may claim zero-rating if it holds and submits the required export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ASYCUDA export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing overseas destination
  • Commercial Invoice (tax invoice where VAT-registered)
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT or TT credit advice
  • VAT export schedule or exporter declaration substantiating zero-rating
  • Certificate of Free Sale or export permit for human diagnostic products (where applicable)

Vanuatu does not currently mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, digital stamping, or continuous clearance. VAT-registered businesses may issue standard VAT invoices, in paper or electronic form, containing the required prescribed particulars. Export declarations are filed electronically through ASYCUDA World for customs clearance, but there is no tax-authority e-invoicing clearance platform.

The Commissioner may audit export substantiation and re-characterize unproven zero-rated supplies as taxable, resulting in VAT assessed at 12.5%, interest for late payment, penalties for false or negligent declarations, and clawback of any input VAT credits or refunds claimed. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims can attract criminal prosecution under the VAT Act and Customs Act, including monetary penalties and potential forfeiture of goods or profits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) floats under a managed basket administered by the RBV; current-account payments for trade in goods and services are freely processed by authorised dealers under delegated authority using statutory forms (notably Form A2), while capital-account transactions require RBV approval unless a statutory exemption applies. The decisive exemption for the International Company (IC) is the exchange-control exemption in the International Companies Act [Cap. 222]: transactions routed through an IC's foreign-currency account held with an authorised dealer are outside the Exchange Control Act. Consequently, USD-denominated equity injections, trade collections, and dividend remittances by the IC are effectively fully convertible, with no withholding tax on outbound dividends; only VUV transactions with Vanuatu residents require exchange-control clearance. Overall onshore convertibility for this ISIC 4649 IVD distributor is assessed as High for USD treasury flows and Medium for VUV conversion capacity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds (0% surrender requirement). Vanuatu's exchange-control framework requires exports above the prescribed threshold to be declared (goods declaration and exchange-control export form), and export proceeds must be repatriated to Vanuatu within the statutory period (generally six months from shipment, or such extended period approved by the RBV or an authorised dealer). The IC may retain all USD export collections in its foreign-currency account, may offset them against import payments, or may rotate them to an offshore group treasury account where the dealer's conditions permit; there is no statutory obligation to sell VUV into the market.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 123]; exchange control is administered under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 75] and its Regulations, with the RBV delegating daily approval authority to licensed authorised dealers (commercial banks). The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) supervises corporate registries and financial dealers but does not operate the FX regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs. Vanuatu has no dedicated electronic money institution licensing track, and the RBV's exchange-control exemption for ICs operates only through authorised dealers. Global correspondent banks treat Vanuatu ICs clearing through non-bank EMI platforms as an elevated shell-company risk, and EMIs cannot issue the SWIFT MT103 / commercial LC instruments required for clinical-diagnostic reagent trade. EMIs are acceptable only as an auxiliary collection gateway, never as the primary settlement rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (nil). Vanuatu is a zero-direct-tax jurisdiction: no corporate income tax, no profits tax, no capital gains tax, and no payroll tax. An International Company (IC) is statutorily exempt from every present and future Vanuatu tax on income, assets, or transactions under the International Companies Act (Cap 222).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no withholding tax on dividends, distributions, capital gains, or liquidation proceeds paid by an International Company to its non-resident shareholders. Dividends are remitted gross with no deduction at source.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in Vanuatu: domestic law prescribes no mandatory Local File, Master File, or Country-by-Country reporting threshold, no transfer pricing return form, and no advance pricing agreement procedure because the IC is fully tax-exempt. The operative TP documentation obligation arises extraterritorially from the foreign related-party jurisdiction applying OECD-aligned transfer pricing rules once its statutory revenue or related-party transaction thresholds are crossed.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not currently maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute for International Companies. Substance requirements nevertheless operate indirectly: (i) the IC Act ring-fences the entity — no local business with residents, no local land ownership — so the IC must evidence a genuine offshore commercial purpose; (ii) Vanuatu's FATF increased-monitoring status (grey list June 2021) and EU listing cycle (blacklisted 2020–2023, delisted after beneficial-ownership and exchange-of-information reforms) oblige ICs to demonstrate transparent governance; (iii) a letterbox company is exposed to foreign CFC inclusion, permanent-establishment assertions, and banking de-risking. Recommended substance for an IVD reagent distribution IC: physical office in Port Vila, licensed resident registered agent, substantive board minutes, locally maintained books and records, and complete commercial documentation (contracts, purchase orders, invoices, shipping records) proving that only offshore business is transacted.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent concealment abroad

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: criminal fines for false filings under the International Companies Act (up to approximately VT 1,000,000 or the amended ceiling) and imprisonment of responsible officers; AML/CTF prosecution for money laundering connected to tax fraud. Foreign jurisdiction: full reassessment, interest, fraud penalties of 100–400% of tax evaded, asset freezing, and proceeds-of-crime confiscation.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the veil where the IC is a sham or mere façade: directors and beneficial owners become jointly and severally liable for unpaid foreign tax, civil restitution, interest, and penalties; personal travel bans, INTERPOL red notices, extradition requests, and imprisonment terms of 5–10 years are realistic personal consequences.
Shell company / economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu: VFSC administrative penalties, rejection of the annual attestation, and strike-off/deregistration for non-filing or non-payment. International: re-escalation of Vanuatu on the EU/OECD lists triggering defensive measures; foreign tax authorities may recharacterize income as domestically sourced, apply CFC income inclusion, assert a permanent establishment, and impose penalties and interest on the full underpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors resident in the foreign jurisdiction face enforcement orders, director disqualification, and personal liability for corporate tax where the company is disregarded as a separate entity; courts may issue contribution orders requiring directors to fund the company's tax liabilities.
AML/CFT and beneficial ownership breaches

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu AML/CFT framework (post-FATF-grey-list aligned): substantial corporate fines, suspension or revocation of the IC's status, deregistration, and confiscation of proceeds of crime; foreign sanctions enforcement adds asset freezes, correspondent-banking restrictions, and secondary penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and shadow directors are individually prosecutable; sanctions include personal fines, disqualification, imprisonment up to 15 years for serious money-laundering under Vanuatu law, asset-confiscation orders, and travel restrictions pending extradition within the FATF mutual-recognition network.
Unlicensed domestic activity by an International Company

Statutory sanction: The IC commits a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to approximately VT 1,000,000 (or the amended ceiling), may forfeit its statutory tax exemption and separate legal-person protections, and may be struck off by the VFSC; contracts concluded with Vanuatu residents may be void and unenforceable.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined, ordered to compensate the company or third parties, and disqualified from acting as directors; the corporate veil is lifted for the prohibited transactions, rendering directors personally liable on the unauthorized contracts.
