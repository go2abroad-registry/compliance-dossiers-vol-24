Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Government of Vanuatu - VAT and customs administration is exercised through the Commissioner of Inland Revenue and the Director of Customs; International Company registration is administered by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC). Official e-services portal: www.gov.vu (VAT, customs ASYCUDA World, and business license procedures); VFSC portal: www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporation: A foreign-owned International Company (IC) is incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 212] through a VFSC-licensed registered agent; the VFSC company number and certificate of incorporation are the statutory corporate identifiers. Step 2 - No income-tax TIN: Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax and issues no general TIN; no national tax registration is required for an offshore IC whose business is conducted outside Vanuatu. Step 3 - VAT ID for onshore supplies: an IC making taxable supplies in Vanuatu must apply to the Commissioner of Inland Revenue for VAT registration on the prescribed form, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered agent and registered office details, bank account information, and projected turnover; the issued VAT registration number is the only tax identifier. Step 4 - Business license: any onshore business activity requires a municipal business license (Port Vila, Luganville, or other municipality) or a license issued through the DCIR outside municipal boundaries. Step 5 - Annual maintenance: the IC must maintain a licensed registered agent in Vanuatu and pay VFSC annual fees to preserve its exempt status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - A Vanuatu International Company is statutorily exempt from all Vanuatu taxes, duties, levies, and imposts on offshore income, profits, distributions, and capital gains; Vanuatu has no corporate income tax, no withholding tax, and no capital gains tax for any taxpayer. Onshore taxable supplies remain subject to the 12.5% VAT and municipal business license fees, but no entity-level profits tax is imposed. Pass_Through_Eligible is not separately recognized because Vanuatu has no income tax to pass through.

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box, hybrid-mismatch, CFC, thin-capitalization, or entity-classification election exists because Vanuatu imposes no income tax. The relevant statutory elections are: (i) voluntary VAT registration for taxable persons below the VT 10,000,000 compulsory turnover threshold; (ii) selection of the VAT filing cycle (monthly or quarterly) as prescribed by the Commissioner, with a concessional VT 5,000,000 threshold available to tourism-related operators; and (iii) a VFSC-filed election to convert an IC to, or from, a local company, which changes the tax-exempt characterization. The offshore tax exemption itself is automatic and requires no election or filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 (ten million vatu) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; a lower VT 5,000,000 sectoral threshold applies to tourism-related operators; voluntary registration is permitted below the compulsory threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies under the Value Added Tax Act No. 6 of 1998 (as amended) to: (a) goods physically exported from Vanuatu by the supplier, including cross-border DTC consignments of cosmetics delivered to overseas consumers; (b) services supplied to a non-resident located outside Vanuatu at the time of supply, excluding services connected with Vanuatu land or goods; and (c) international transport and related freight and insurance. The supplier must be the exporter of record and must maintain contemporaneous documentary evidence of physical export, sale value, and receipt of consideration in convertible currency. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated supplies is creditable, and any excess credit is refundable.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World export declaration) showing the cosmetics consignment, HS classification, FOB value, and final destination outside Vanuatu.
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or carrier-endorsed courier/sea-freight receipt evidencing physical removal of the goods from Vanuatu.
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list referencing the export declaration and evidencing the overseas buyer and 0% VAT treatment, together with banking evidence of receipt of export proceeds (inward remittance certificate or foreign-currency bank statement).

None - Vanuatu does not mandate e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, government API reporting, or digital stamping. Taxpayers may issue paper or PDF VAT invoices with the prescribed particulars. Customs export formalities are submitted electronically through the ASYCUDA World system and the Vanuatu Trade Portal; no real-time tax-authority invoice clearance applies.

Vanuatu has no foreign-exchange control law, so no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline exists and no clawback is triggered by timing of repatriation. However, the Commissioner of Inland Revenue may audit zero-rating claims within the assessment periods of the VAT Act 1998 and, where export substantiation is false, deficient, or fraudulent, may: (i) issue a default assessment charging VAT at 12.5% on the full sales value; (ii) impose late-payment interest and civil penalties at the statutory rates; (iii) pursue criminal sanctions for fraudulent evasion against the IC and its officers, including fines and imprisonment for natural persons; and (iv) retrospectively claw back the zero-rating benefit, input VAT credits, and refunds previously paid.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates under a broadly liberalised, non-restrictive exchange-control framework. There are no legally imposed exchange controls on current-account transactions, capital transfers, or profit remittances by International Companies. The VUV is convertible in practice, subject to the commercial anti-money-laundering checks of the banking institution executing the transfer. Profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders is generally permitted without central bank prior approval, provided that the bank receives the supporting audited financial statements, board resolution, and tax-clearance or confirmation of the entity's tax-exempt status where requested. The primary hurdles are not statutory but documentary: source-of-funds verification, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and correspondent-bank due diligence on VUV-to-USD transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. A licensed cosmetic-beauty retailer may receive export proceeds directly into its offshore-capable VUV or multi-currency account and may retain, convert, or disburse such proceeds freely. The only reporting obligation arises under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act, under which international wire transfers above the applicable threshold require transaction reporting to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit. Banks may request supporting invoices and customs declarations for compliance verification but do not require conversion of foreign currency into VUV.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium for offshore digital EMI services. Vanuatu's regulatory environment does not currently provide a dedicated local electronic-money-institution licence, and the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission is not a passporting member of the EU/UK EMI regimes. An offshore digital EMI may serve the e-commerce treasury structure only if the EMI relies on a correspondent banking partner in Vanuatu, Australia, or New Zealand and obtains an appropriate payable-through or settlement arrangement. Legal constraints include: the absence of a local EMI licence framework, onboarding requirements for a Vanuatu International Company under the AML/CFT rules, and the heightened risk of account restriction due to the perceived use of digital-only financial institutions. EmiS without tier-1 correspondent banking relationships are not viable as the primary settlement rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: 0 VUV (no domestic transfer pricing statute and no Local File/Master File obligation in Vanuatu). Because Vanuatu has no corporate income tax, the IC has no Vanuatu transfer-pricing compliance duty. However, if the ultimate parent or beneficial owner is tax-resident in a jurisdiction with transfer pricing rules, related-party cross-border transactions of the IC may be subject to that foreign jurisdiction's documentation thresholds, e.g. EUR 750,000 or the equivalent local currency threshold for intercompany services, royalties, and financing.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not currently impose a domestic Economic Substance Return on a non-licensed International Company engaged in online retail. The online sale of cosmetics and personal care products is not a prescribed activity requiring a Vanuatu financial Services licence. ESR-style substance obligations may be triggered only if the IC is managed and controlled in Vanuatu and conducts a regulated financial activity, or if a foreign jurisdiction applies OECD/EU substance requirements to the IC for CFC/treaty purposes. For a pure e-commerce structure, the commercial substance (inventory, employees, logistics, and management decisions) should sit in the operating jurisdiction in which sales are made or in the parent's residence to avoid a sham/shell finding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate suppression of taxable profit in the operating country

Statutory sanction: The operating jurisdiction may assess the full profit on the basis that the Vanuatu IC is a sham, together with interest at statutory rates and penalties of 20% to 100% of the tax understated. In Vanuatu, filing a false declaration with the VFSC is an offence under s.158 of the International Companies Act, subject to a fine up to VUV 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign misleading invoices or tax declarations may be made personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid foreign taxes. Foreign criminal courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and custodial sentences of up to seven years for tax evasion, and the corporate veil will be pierced where fraud or sham conduct is shown.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu may strike off the IC for failing to maintain a registered agent and annual fee compliance. In foreign jurisdictions, anti-avoidance rules can re-attribute the IC's revenue and assets to the beneficial owner, impose CFC taxation, and levy penalties of up to 25% of the tax adjustment. Where a licence or ESR regime applies, fines may be substantial and the IC can be struck off.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil if the IC is a mere facade; directors may be jointly and severally liable for the foreign tax liabilities and may be prohibited from managing companies for up to 15 years. Serious cases may lead to extradition, asset restraining orders, and imprisonment.
AML / CFT compliance failure / unauthorised payment processing

Statutory sanction: Under the Vanuatu Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act [Cap.249], breaches can result in fines up to VUV 20,000,000, forfeiture of transaction proceeds, and suspension of the registered agent. The VFSC may also strike off the company for failure to maintain a lawful registered agent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate money laundering face criminal liability, imprisonment of up to fifteen years, asset confiscation, travel bans, and personal disqualification orders. The corporate veil is pierced for AML offences, and beneficial owners may be named in public enforcement actions.
Abusive tax-residence certificate and false treaty representation

Statutory sanction: Misuse of a residence certificate is treated as fraudulent misrepresentation in the recipient jurisdiction and as an offence in Vanuatu under s.158 of the International Companies Act. The certificate may be revoked retroactively, the entity struck off, and fines imposed in both the foreign jurisdiction and Vanuatu.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly furnish false certificates may be subject to personal restitution obligations, criminal fines, imprisonment, and Interpol red notices where the misrepresentation is used to move funds across borders. Foreign tax authorities may issue a travel ban pending investigation.
Non-arm's-length transfer pricing and margin stripping

Statutory sanction: Although Vanuatu has no TP law, the foreign parent's tax authority can make transfer-pricing adjustments, disallow deductions, impose interest rates of 3% to 5% per annum, and apply penalties of 20% to 40% of the adjusted amount. Aggressive related-party arrangements may also trigger general anti-avoidance and fraud penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers approving false transfer-pricing documentation may incur personal liability for the taxpayer's penalty and interest where they signed declarations or caused the understatement. Courts may pierce the veil, impose civil restitution, issue travel bans, and refer directors for criminal prosecution in serious fraud cases.
