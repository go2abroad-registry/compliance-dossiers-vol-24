Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Government of the Republic of Vanuatu; official portal: https://customs.gov.vu (Vanuatu Revenue and Customs online services)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity must submit a prescribed TIN/VAT registration application together with certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office address, director and shareholder passport copies, and bank reference letter to the Commissioner of Inland Revenue; TIN certificate is typically issued within 7-14 business days. An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act Cap 222 through the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) is automatically classified as tax-exempt and requires no income-tax registration; VAT registration is mandatory only when supplying taxable goods or services consumed in Vanuatu. A pure offshore re-invoicing hub supplying only exported goods or offshore services may register voluntarily to zero-rate exports and recover input VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Vanuatu law; tax-exempt status of an International Company is automatic under the International Companies Act Cap 222 provided the IC does not carry on business in Vanuatu. No elective pass-through regime is available because Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or withholding tax. An IC that commences local business must register as a foreign-investment enterprise and loses exemption for that Vanuatu-source activity; economic substance reporting obligations apply to geographically mobile income but do not create a fiscal election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 (approximately USD 42,000) annual taxable turnover for resident suppliers; mandatory registration applies when the threshold is exceeded or upon taxable imports; non-resident suppliers of services consumed in Vanuatu are registrable without threshold benefit; voluntary registration is permitted for exporters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to (i) goods physically exported from Vanuatu within 28 days of supply, evidenced by a customs export declaration, and (ii) services supplied for direct use and consumption outside Vanuatu to a non-resident recipient. A re-invoicing hub may zero-rate its trading margin on offshore transactions provided no value is consumed in Vanuatu and the supplier holds valid VAT registration; input VAT is recoverable to the extent attributable to zero-rated supplies. Goods and services consumed in Vanuatu remain taxable at 12.5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD approved via ASYCUDA World)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT TT credit advice evidencing foreign currency payment
  • Zero-rated VAT tax invoice
  • Insurance certificate and certificate of origin (where applicable)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is in force in Vanuatu. VAT-registered suppliers must issue a compliant tax invoice (sequential number, supplier TIN, date, description, VAT amount) and retain records for audit; export declarations are processed through the ASYCUDA World electronic single-window system with digital customs records; government e-invoicing reform is at pilot stage and not yet mandatory.

The Commissioner of Inland Revenue may disallow zero rating, claw back input VAT credits, and assess interest at 1.5% per month plus civil penalties up to 200% of evaded VAT where export documentation is deficient or fraudulent (VAT Act Cap 248 ss.70-75); criminal false-declaration offences attract fines up to VUV 1,000,000 per offence and/or imprisonment up to 2 years. Vanuatu imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, so the 180-day figure is a prudent compliance benchmark; inward remittance evidence is nevertheless used by banks and the Financial Intelligence Unit for AML verification of export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Vanuatu maintains a liberal exchange-control regime: there are no prior approvals, quotas, exchange restrictions, or withholding taxes applied to dividend distributions, profit repatriation, or liquidation proceeds of International Companies. The only practical hurdles are bank-level documentary requirements under the CRS/FATCA disclosure regime and AML/CFT source-of-funds verification, which require board resolutions, profit-support schedules, and executed trade contracts. The VUV exchange rate is a managed basket float against USD, AUD, NZD, EUR, and JPY, fixed daily by the RBV; no meaningful offshore VUV market exists, so USD-denominated clearing is commercially essential for re-invoicing operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. The VU_IC may retain re-invoicing and trading receipts in foreign currency indefinitely within AD-bank Foreign Currency Accounts or external approved accounts. In practice, AD banks enforce an internally prescribed 90-to-180 day document-matching rule: each incoming trade receipt must be reconciled to a signed contract and commercial invoice within that window, otherwise the funds may be frozen pending an AML investigation. This de facto compliance timeline governs the re-invoicing treasury calendar.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), constituted under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 125], is the exclusive monetary authority with statutory powers over Vatu (VUV) issuance, official exchange-rate fixing, exchange-control administration, Authorized Dealer (AD) bank licensing, and bank supervision. The Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) administers the registration and supervision of the International Company (VU_IC), its registered agent, and its beneficial-ownership filings; cross-border banking flows affecting the entity fall under the joint oversight of RBV and the national AML/CFT supervision framework administered with the VFSC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High feasibility strictly as a supplementary settlement utility; offshore EMIs (X-Transfer, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are legally permissible for collecting and disbursing small-ticket trade payments through partner banks in Hong Kong, the United States, or Singapore. Legal constraints: (i) offshore EMIs are not licensed by the RBV and cannot clear or hold VUV; (ii) EMIs independently classify the re-invoicing model as Medium_Risk_Enhanced and mirror AD-bank trade-file requirements; (iii) EMI rails must not be used for counterparties in high-risk or sanction-adjacent jurisdictions because onward-transfer AML oversight is weaker; (iv) primary liquidity and documentary-letter-of-credit capacity must remain with a Vanuatu-licensed AD bank to evidence regulatory substance.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on taxable profits — International Companies are exempt under section 49 of the Vanuatu International Companies Act [Cap 222]; Vanuatu imposes no general corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — no dividend withholding tax is imposed by Vanuatu on distributions paid by an International Company, whether to resident or non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer-pricing documentation threshold is prescribed in Vanuatu; there is no domestic Master File or Local File obligation. For an ISIC 4690 re-invoicing hub, the prudential documentation threshold is set at VUV 30,000,000 (approximately USD 250,000) of aggregate annual related-party cross-border flow-through transactions; above this threshold, the company should maintain a transfer-pricing policy memorandum, transaction file, comparability analysis, intercompany agreements, and contemporaneous evidence of economic substance.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu economic substance rules apply to tax-resident International Companies carrying on a relevant activity. A general re-invoicing and trade intermediary ordinarily falls within the distribution and service centre business category, requiring: a physical office in Vanuatu, full-time employees engaged in managing the trade, key management decisions taken in Vanuatu, operating expenditure commensurate with the value of the cross-border transactions, and an annual substance declaration filed with VFSC. The company should hold regular board meetings in Vanuatu, keep minutes, maintain bank accounts through which re-invoicing settlements actually flow, and avoid functions performed solely by a foreign service provider.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misrepresentation to Tax Authorities

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the VAT Act [Cap 244] or the Penal Code can expose the company to substantial fines, confiscation of goods and bank balances, deregistration, and cancellation of the International Company license. VFSC may also suspend or revoke the registered agent relationship and publish a striking-off notice.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly sign false declarations or orchestrate sham invoicing are personally liable in civil damages to the company or creditors and can be prosecuted criminally. The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is used as a mere instrumentality of fraud; sanctions include personal fines, travel bans, director disqualification, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Failure to Maintain Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with the economic substance regime or the International Companies Act constitutes a regulatory offence; VFSC may impose administrative monetary penalties, require a replacement registered agent, direct corrective filings, and ultimately strike the company from the International Companies Register. Continued default may lead to prosecution and court-imposed fines.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal disqualification and monetary sanctions if they intentionally authorise a shell operation; a court may lift the veil where the company is a facade concealing personal dealings, making directors personally liable for third-party debts and unpaid statutory fees.
Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Violation of Vanuatu anti-money laundering legislation and UN financial sanctions regulations attracts criminal liability, confiscation orders, forfeiture of laundered property, and revocation of the International Company registration. Enhanced AML risk treatment by banks and correspondent institutions can also result in frozen accounts, termination of banking relationships, and regulatory enforcement against the registered agent.

Director personal liability: Company principals and directors may be prosecuted for money laundering and terrorist financing; personal assets are subject to forfeiture, and directors can be denied cross-border travel, arrested on extradition requests, and sentenced to imprisonment for knowing participation or wilful blindness. Beneficial owners hiding behind the International Company structure are not protected by limited liability in such circumstances.
Misreporting of Related-Party Transactions and Transfer-Pricing Artifacts

Statutory sanction: Although Vanuatu has no domestic transfer-pricing penalty, the same documentation deficiencies can trigger severe foreign tax penalties in buyer and seller jurisdictions, including disallowance of deductions, deemed dividend treatment, secondary adjustments, and penalty adjustments of 100% to 200%. VFSC can impose record-keeping penalties under the International Companies Act if books are not maintained in Vanuatu.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the Vanuatu International Company to act as a sham conduit may be held liable for assisting in a foreign tax offence; under foreign anti-avoidance rules and Vanuatu general company law, where the entity is found to be a puppet, courts can impose restitution orders on the directors and remove the benefit of limited liability.
