UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Governing statutes: (1) International Companies Act [Cap 222] as amended, together with the beneficial-ownership amendments adopted 2014-2019 requiring VFSC-licensed Registered Agents to identify, verify and maintain particulars of ultimate beneficial owners; (2) Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 18 of 2022, which imposes risk-based customer due diligence (CDD), enhanced due diligence (EDD), record-keeping and reporting obligations and establishes the VFSC-administered central UBO register; (3) Counter-Terrorism and Transnational Organised Crime Act [Cap 311]; (4) VFSC AML/CFT Rules, Guidance Notes and registered-agent licence conditions; and (5) FATF Recommendations as implemented through Vanuatu's Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering (APG) mutual evaluation commitments. The UBO threshold is set at 25% ownership interest or voting rights, with an additional control-based test (control through other means) and a lower risk-based threshold where the Registered Agent suspects circumvention.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is not permissible as the statutory registered office of a Vanuatu International Company: the International Companies Act [Cap 222] requires a physical registered office in Vanuatu situated at the premises of the licensed Registered Agent, where the statutory registers, UBO records and service-of-process arrangements are maintained. The ISIC 5920 stock-music licensing operation may, however, be managed entirely remotely, including a home-based or virtual operational head office abroad, provided the Vanuatu registered office and Registered Agent filings are maintained; Stripe and PayPal account-opening will in practice rely on the Vanuatu registered address plus the directors' and UBOs' operational addresses.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement under the International Companies Act 1987. Authorized capital may be denominated in any currency and shares may be issued without par value. No escrow, stamp duty deposit, or paid-in capital bank verification is required.

Funding & unlock path: Not physically mandated for a pure digital operator; upon opening a local administrative office, obtain a fire safety certificate from the Vanuatu Fire and Emergency Services Authority, a structural load inspection clearance from the Public Works Department, and an occupational health and safety workplace clearance under the Vanuatu OHS regulations before staff occupation.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital requirement under the International Companies Act 1987. Authorized capital may be denominated in any currency and shares may be issued without par value. No escrow, stamp duty deposit, or paid-in capital bank verification is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Republic of Vanuatu; official e-portal and forms available via www.customs.gov.vu and the Vanuatu Government portal.

E-government filing portal

A Vanuatu International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] is a non-resident, statutorily tax-exempt company. It obtains its corporate registration number through the registered agent at the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC); no income tax registration or TIN is required because the IC is exempt from all Vanuatu tax on income, capital gains, and withholding. If the IC derives any Vanuatu-source taxable supply and its annual taxable turnover reaches VT 4,000,000 (or if it opts for voluntary registration), it must file VAT Registration Form VAT-REG-01 with DCIR and obtain a VAT registration number. For importing equipment or physical media, the IC must also obtain a Customs Client Reference Number from the DCIR Customs Division. All filings are processed by the registered agent; electronic submission is available for VAT registrants.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the IC is a non-resident company entirely exempt from Vanuatu income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and business licence tax on offshore activities; territorial scope excludes Vanuatu-sourced business; the IC is prohibited from trading with Vanuatu residents or owning Vanuatu land unless it obtains a domestic licence, which would trigger a separate tax classification review.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election regime; the IC's tax-exempt status is automatic under section 20 of the International Companies Act and requires no affirmative election. The only statutory election available is voluntary VAT registration under section 8 of the VAT Act No. 16 of 1998, allowing the IC to register below the mandatory threshold to recover input VAT on local startup or operating costs. Because Vanuatu imposes no income tax, no hybrid mismatch or CFC attribution rules apply; however, the OECD Inclusive Framework status and potential pillar rules should be monitored by non-resident shareholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 4,000,000 (approximately USD 35,000) of annual taxable supplies made in Vanuatu; mandatory registration upon exceedance; voluntary registration permitted below the threshold; foreign-sourced digital licensing revenue is outside the VAT net when supplied to non-resident customers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under section 10(1) of the VAT Act, including digital audio files and royalty-free music delivered by electronic download to non-resident customers. Services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply qualify as exported services and are zero-rated; the IC's subscription and licence revenue from the commercial soundscape and audio sample marketplace therefore qualifies for 0% VAT upon proof that the supply was made outside Vanuatu, the customer is a non-resident, and the payment was received from abroad. Input VAT attributable to such exports is creditable and refundable by DCIR through the periodic VAT return; refund claims are subject to documentary verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export entry filed with DCIR Customs for physical consignments; for digital downloads, an electronic delivery log or CDN download report from the licensing platform equivalent to the export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for physical consignments; for digital supplies, the platform's download ledger supplemented by IP address and time-stamp records)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (issued by a Vanuatu licensed bank or recognised payment gateway, proving receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident customer)
  • Commercial invoice and licence agreement evidencing the scope, territory, and consideration of the royalty-free music licence
  • VAT return and input tax credit calculation schedule demonstrating the correspondence between zero-rated export revenue and recoverable input tax

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime is in force in Vanuatu. DCIR accepts paper and PDF invoices; electronic payment gateway records (Stripe, PayPal) and download logs are accepted as supporting audit evidence. Registered taxpayers must issue a tax invoice within 28 days of supply; invoice numbering and VAT-inclusive pricing rules apply, but no government software clearance or continuous transaction control exists.

DCIR may conduct a VAT audit covering a three-year assessment window, extendable to six years in cases of fraud or wilful default. False zero-rating claims expose the IC to reassessment at 12.5%, penalty interest, and administrative penalties of up to 200% of the tax shortfall under the VAT Act and Customs Act, together with retrospective clawback of refunds paid with interest. Criminal prosecution may result in monetary fines and imprisonment for directors for intentional falsification of export or payment records. In addition, breach of the International Companies Act (such as unauthorised domestic trading) can trigger VFSC enforcement, striking-off, and loss of the tax-exempt status of the IC.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is generally convertible for current account transactions, including trade in services and royalty remittances, without exchange control approval. The capital account is liberalised but may require notification or supporting documentation for large cross-border transfers, particularly where the International Company (IC) is treated as a non-resident controlled entity. Profit and royalty remittance hurdles are minimal; however, the IC must present audited financials or management accounts, a tax clearance confirmation (where applicable), and supporting licensing agreements to demonstrate the commercial basis of the outflow. The FIU under the RBV may impose enhanced due diligence on jurisdictions classified as high-risk, but portfolio royalty income from stock music subscriptions is a standard invisible trade remittance and does not engage prohibitive convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu does not impose mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines for export proceeds. The IC may maintain invoice settlement in USD, EUR, or VUV and is not required to convert export receipts into VUV. Nevertheless, funds must pass through a licensed bank in Vanuatu if the IC resident for banking purposes, and supporting invoices and contracts must be retained for a minimum of five years to satisfy the RBV’s anti-money laundering (AML) record-keeping obligations. No percentage surrender ratio applies to non-resident ICs operating under the International Companies Act.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility. Vanuatu does not have a comprehensive local electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime; the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu regulates banks and money transfer operators, but offshore digital EMIs that are unlicensed in Vanuatu cannot maintain a domestic account in VUV or rely on local clearing infrastructure. Cross-border EMIs domiciled in fully compliant jurisdictions (e.g., UK FCA-authorised EMIs or Singapore MPI-licensed entities) may be used as supplementary collection rails for Stripe/PayPal payouts, provided the IC satisfies the EMI’s own KYC and source-of-wealth onboarding requirements. Legal constraint: the EMI must not solicit Vanuatu-domiciled clients by holding itself out as a Vanuatu deposit-taking institution, and funds held at the EMI do not qualify for RBV banking secrecy or depositor protection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder loans or equity injections must be routed through the RBV-regulated banking system rather than crypto intermediaries. The standard protocol is a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 where interbank funds transfers are required) from the parent shareholder’s corporate bank to the IC’s operational account at Bank of South Pacific Vanuatu or another licensed local bank. The wire must clearly state the purpose code corresponding to a capital contribution or director loan, the unique IC registration number, and the shareholder name matching the corporate register. The bank will request supporting documentation comprising a copy of the subscription resolution, confirmation of the registered office and corporate registry extract, a source-of-funds certificate from the remitting bank, and, where the commentary states capital injection, the balance sheet treatment confirming that the funds are booked in an equity reserve rather than trading receipts. For payments received via Stripe/PayPal from global marketplace buyers, the inbound capital injection route is separate: payment aggregators credit the IC’s settlement account directly if the account is held in a connected jurisdiction; otherwise funds are batched by the aggregator and remitted by wire to Vanuatu with the commercial payment purpose code included in the bank reference field.Outbound trade disbursements from the Vanuatu IC follow three clearing paths: (i) licensed music publishers, sub-licensors, and audio production houses receive settlement via SWIFT MT103 message transfer under the standard beneficiary-charges (BEN) or shared-charges (SHA) model; (ii) commission and revenue-share payments to international distributors are executed via commercial letters of credit only when non-payment or documentary performance risk requires a bank undertaking; for digital licensing royalties, an MT103 with irrevocable payment instructions is the predominant and cost-effective rail; and (iii) automated low-value payments to individual artists or AI-generated audio suppliers are routed through embedded cross-border payment APIs integrated with the settlement bank; alternatively, periodic batch transfers through the aggregator platform minimise friction. All outbound wires must carry a matching description linking the beneficiary, the underlying licence agreement reference, and the consideration taxed or tax-exempt status under the Vanuatu International Companies Act. When the counterparty bank is outside the SWIFT network (e.g., local Asian clearing jurisdictions), the Vanuatu bank can use a SWIFT MT103 with a cover payment or an MT205 second-party cover if the beneficiary maintains a nostro account.Vanuatu-domiciled ICs may transact outright spot FX swaps with authorised dealers to convert USD, AUD, and EUR proceeds into VUV or continue to hold foreign currency balances without restriction.Domestic forward FX contracts (formal or informal) are not commonly provided by the small Vanuatu market, but a vanilla deliverable forward may be written between the IC and a Category-2 licensed bank in Vanuatu for a specific receivable if the bank is willing to accept underlying invoice documentation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in the Pacific region can be used only through offshore dealing banks in London, Singapore, Hong Kong, or New York that accept a Vanuatu IC as a non-resident corporate counterparty; local RBV licensed banks do not generally quote NDFs in VUV due to limited market liquidity.Cross-currency swaps are permitted for ICs with large contractual future cash flows, provided the swap does not involve VUV balances and does not create deposit-taking activity in Vanuatu; typical counterparties are international dealer banks under a bilateral ISDA Master Agreement.Plain-vanilla USD/VUV, AUD/VUV, and EUR/VUV over-the-counter options may be structured with approved foreign banks under the IC’s treasury policy; however, such hedging requires substantive underlying royalty receivables to satisfy anti-speculation bank compliance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Annual Return must be lodged within 6 months after the date of incorporation and within 6 months after each anniversary of incorporation. Non-resident ICs are not required to file any tax return in Vanuatu.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VUV 0 - Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing statute, no Local File/Master File requirement, and no statutory threshold triggering transfer-pricing documentation for a tax-exempt IC. Nevertheless, an IC engaging in related-party transactions in connection with a relevant economic substance activity must maintain contemporaneous arm's length evidence. For an offshore music licensing marketplace, the prudent threshold is to document all intercompany royalty flows and service margins regardless of amount, because the absence of a CIT filing does not extinguish substance documentation expectations.

Economic substance scope: The Vanuatu International Companies (Economic Substance) Amendment Act applies to ICs carrying on a 'relevant activity' in Vanuatu. A royalty-free stock music business that commercially licenses audio content and collects subscription revenue may be characterised as an intellectual property (IP) business, to which a full economic substance test applies if the core income-generating activity is attributed to Vanuatu. A pure low-risk IP holding and licensing company that has no Vanuatu management, no local employees, and no local premises must rely on the substance of the foreign principal company to avoid Vanuatu ESR failure; otherwise, significant fines and deregistration may be imposed. The VFSC requires an economic substance declaration and, for relevant activities, the filing of a substance report.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Directed at Foreign Revenue Authorities

Statutory sanction: While Vanuatu has no domestic corporate income tax, the International Companies Act imposes criminal sanctions for false declarations and fraud. A company may be fined, struck off, and dissolved; foreign revenue judgments are not automatically enforced, but the VFSC may disqualify registered agents and directors involved in sham arrangements.

Director personal liability: Courts in Vanuatu and enforcement jurisdictions may pierce the corporate veil where the IC is a mere facade. Directors may face civil restitution orders, asset freezing orders, director disqualification, and imprisonment for fraudulent trading or knowing participation in tax evasion.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike the International Company off the register, causing automatic dissolution. VFSC can impose administrative penalties and monetary fines under the economic substance legislation. Banking and payment processors may freeze settlement proceeds if the entity cannot provide substantive documents.

Director personal liability: Directors who continue to trade after dissolution or who allow the company to be used as a shell may be personally liable for debts incurred after the company ceases to have valid legal status. Personal liability extends to restitution of diverted assets and, in cases of dishonesty, imprisonment.
AML/CTF Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with Vanuatu AML/CTF obligations exposes legal persons to substantial monetary penalties, licence cancellation of the registered agent, seizure of assets, and criminal prosecution of involved officers. Continued non-compliance may result in prohibition orders and revocation of the IC's registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly structure transactions to avoid AML reporting may be prosecuted criminally. Sanctions include substantial personal fines, confiscation orders, travel bans, and imprisonment. The VFSC and the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit may jointly freeze bank and payment rail accounts.
