Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Viet Nam imposes no general statutory minimum paid-up charter capital for an LLC engaged in ISIC 7320 market research and survey platform activities. The charter capital is determined freely by the member(s) in the Company Charter. However, the registered charter capital must be fully contributed within 90 days after the Enterprise Registration Certificate is issued, or within the longer schedule expressly stated in the Investment Registration Certificate. Contributions by a foreign investor must be remitted into a direct investment capital account (DICA) at a bank licensed in Viet Nam. Failure to contribute on time requires an adjustment of the charter capital and member equity percentages through amended Charter and member list filings with the DPI.

Funding & unlock path: The leased office must obtain a Fire Safety Acceptance Certificate under Decree 136/2020/ND-CP and Circular 83/2021/TT-BCA if the premises meet the regulated thresholds for service or office buildings; for tenancies below those thresholds, the building owner’s fire-safety inspection certificate is sufficient. Structural load certificates are not separately required for leased office space; the landlord’s construction completion or building occupancy certificate evidences structural compliance. Under the Labour Code and Decree 145/2020/ND-CP, the company must file a labour utilisation declaration, complete a workplace risk assessment, register employees for social insurance, provide occupational safety training, and comply with all applicable workplace safety and hygiene standards.

Paid-in capital rule: Viet Nam imposes no general statutory minimum paid-up charter capital for an LLC engaged in ISIC 7320 market research and survey platform activities. The charter capital is determined freely by the member(s) in the Company Charter. However, the registered charter capital must be fully contributed within 90 days after the Enterprise Registration Certificate is issued, or within the longer schedule expressly stated in the Investment Registration Certificate. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế) under the Ministry of Finance of Viet Nam, together with provincial Tax Departments and the Department of Planning and Investment in the unified investment-enterprise-tax registration procedure. National e-tax portal: https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn. National electronic invoice system portal: https://hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested Limited Liability Company (LLC), the statutory Tax Identification Number / VAT ID (Mã số thuế, MST) is granted automatically upon issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); no separate application to the tax office is required. Step 1 - Obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment or the industrial-zone / export-processing-zone management authority where the project is located. Step 2 - Submit the enterprise registration dossier through the National Business Registration Portal (dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn); the ERC, together with the MST, is issued within 3 working days. Step 3 - Activate the company's electronic tax account at https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn using the legal representative's digital certificate, appoint the tax-authority bank account, and execute electronic tax filing and payment agreements; the MST is a 10-13 digit code with dependent-entity sub-codes where branches are registered. Step 4 - Register electronic invoice templates and issuance parameters with the tax authority within 30 days of MST issuance in accordance with Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. Step 5 - Where a foreign company without a Vietnamese commercial presence supplies online SaaS / market-research services into Vietnam, the foreign supplier must register and obtain an MST through the dedicated foreign-supplier module of the e-tax portal under Circular 80/2021/TT-BTC and may appoint institutions such as PayPal or Wise as payment intermediaries for tax remittance; however, where the local LLC is the taxable entity, it files monthly/quarterly VAT and CIT declarations on its own MST under the Law on Tax Administration 38/2019/QH13.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The LLC is a Vietnamese tax resident subject to entity-level taxation: Corporate Income Tax (CIT) at 20% on worldwide net taxable income (territorial scope: global profits with foreign tax credit and treaty relief), VAT on domestic and imported consumption, and statutory payroll and contractor withholding obligations. Pass-through tax treatment is not available for LLCs; profits distributed to shareholders are non-deductible distributions. No tax-exempt zone status applies automatically; however, qualified new investment projects in high-tech zones, industrial zones, export processing zones, and eligible sectors may enjoy CIT holidays/reductions. The LLC remains fully subject to ordinary national tax administration and e-invoicing regardless of incentive status.

Available election forms

Vietnam does not apply U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections. Available statutory choices include: (i) VAT method election: the credit/deduction method is mandatory where annual VAT-liable revenue is VND 1 billion or more; smaller or newly formed LLCs may use, in limited circumstances, the direct method on value added or direct method on revenue for the initial period; (ii) Foreign Contractor Tax / Foreign Supplier Tax election: a non-resident vendor without a permanent establishment in Vietnam may apply for treaty benefits and elect the applicable DTA withholding treatment under Circular 80/2021/TT-BTC, or the offshore entity may be structured through a local LLC subject to domestic CIT/VAT; (iii) CIT incentive election: an LLC may elect, through the IRC process and tax filing, investment incentive regimes such as tax holidays or reduced rates for eligible high-tech, industrial-zone, or export-processing projects; and (iv) interest capitalization and related-party treatment elections under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, including the 30% EBITDA interest limitation and transfer-pricing safe harbours. There is no elective pass-through or hybrid-instrument regime under Vietnamese law; general anti-avoidance rules apply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam operates a managed-float foreign-exchange regime with regulated current-account convertibility and restricted capital-account convertibility. The VND is not freely convertible beyond the national jurisdiction; every foreign-currency purchase, transfer, or conversion must trace to an underlying lawful commercial transaction and must be executed through an SBV-authorized credit institution. Resident legal entities may not hold accounts abroad without prior SBV approval. For the VN LLC, current-account service receipts from the cross-border consumer panel survey platform are convertible: the USD revenue collected from foreign clients may be either retained in a USD current account at a licensed bank or sold to an authorized bank for VND at the prevailing interbank central-bank rate for working capital, payroll, NAPAS/IBPS mass payouts and tax settlement. Capital-account items remain heavily controlled: inbound equity must be paid into the Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) under the FDI currency-control framework, while medium and long-term foreign loans require pre-registration with the SBV under Circular 03/2015/TT-NHNN, as amended. Profit-remittance hurdles under SBV FDI regulations (Circular 05/2014/TT-NHNN): a foreign-invested LLC may remit after-tax profits abroad only once per fiscal-year cycle and only after (i) annual audited financial statements are issued by an eligible audit firm; (ii) corporate income tax (CIT), foreign contractor tax, VAT and all other taxes are fully declared, withheld and settled; (iii) the Members' Council adopts a formal profit-distribution resolution; and (iv) the enterprise submits a complete profit-remittance dossier to its designated authorized bank including the distribution resolution, audited financial statements, tax-finalization confirmation, and corporate banking attestations. The bank reviews the dossier and executes the wire once satisfied; the remittable amount cannot exceed cumulative retained after-tax profit less statutory reserve appropriations (5% annual reserve up to 10% of charter capital) and any accumulated loss offsets. Practical hurdles include the single annual remittance cadence, the tax authority's non-owing certificate requirement, strict separation of capital injections from service revenue in cash-flow documentation, and the bank's risk-based discretion to request additional underlying contracts, UBO declarations, and foreign tax-residence certificates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The VN LLC must repatriate all foreign-currency service-export proceeds into its foreign-currency current account at an SBV-licensed credit institution within 180 days from the contractual or actual completion of the cross-border service, in accordance with Circular 02/2021/TT-NHNN amending Circular 32/2013/TT-NHNN on foreign-exchange controls over import and export payment terms. Each receivable must be evidenced by executed statements of work, delivery acceptance certificates, and final invoices carrying a service purpose description. There is currently no fixed mandatory surrender ratio for general export-service receipts: the SBV abolished the standing percentage-based surrender obligation for resident economic organizations, and the enterprise may lawfully retain up to 100% of its export-service revenue in its USD balance at an authorized credit institution. However, all export proceeds must be transferred back into Vietnam; offshore accumulation is prohibited absent specific SBV approval, and when the enterprise elects to expend in VND it must sell the foreign currency to an authorized bank at the prevailing interbank or central-bank rate. The SBV has reserved the legal power to impose emergency conversion requirements or intervene through authorized banks during periods of acute FX pressure, and banks retain prudential discretion in purchasing foreign currency within their limits. For monitoring purposes, the receiving authorized bank reports each incoming cross-border transaction to the SBV through the national international-transaction reporting framework (ITRS-type transmission), identifying the purpose code, counterparty, and contract reference; the enterprise must align these inflows with its CIT, VAT and foreign contractor tax declarations.

Central bank supervision: The State Bank of Vietnam (SBV / Ngan hang Nha nuoc Viet Nam) is the sole central banking, monetary and foreign-exchange control authority for the Socialist Republic of Vietnam. Its Foreign Exchange Administration Department (Vu Quan ly Ngoai hoi), Banking Activities Supervision Agency, and Anti-Money Laundering supervision divisions jointly administer the Law on the State Bank of Vietnam, the Law on Credit Institutions, the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange (Phap lenh Ngoai hoi, as amended by Ordinance 06/2013/UBTVQH13), Decree 70/2014/ND-CP, Decree 10/2022/ND-CP, Decree 101/2012/ND-CP (as amended), and the relevant SBV Circulars including 32/2013/TT-NHNN, 02/2021/TT-NHNN, 05/2014/TT-NHNN, 03/2015/TT-NHNN, 15/2015/TT-NHNN, 21/2018/TT-NHNN, 23/2019/TT-NHNN and 05/2022/TT-NHNN. The Ministry of Planning and Investment (provincial DPI authorities issuing the Investment Registration Certificate) and the Ministry of Finance (tax finalization confirmation supporting profit remittance) execute complementary roles in the foreign-exchange control architecture.

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary or principal banking rail for the VN LLC; MEDIUM-HIGH as a supplementary cross-border payout and collection facilitator. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex and Payoneer are not licensed credit institutions or licensed intermediary payment services in Vietnam. They may not open or maintain local VND accounts for Vietnamese residents, may not originate domestic wire or NAPAS/IBPS transfers inside Vietnam, and may not act as a domestic payment processor unless separately licensed under Decree 101/2012/ND-CP and Circular 23/2019/TT-NHNN. The LLC may lawfully use such platforms for external mass payout distribution and small-value multi-currency settlement only where (i) the platform balance is funded by regular SWIFT MT103 transfers from the LLC's USD account at an authorized Vietnamese bank; (ii) the platform does not hold or intermediate the funds as a VND-denominated local deposit; and (iii) all platform balances are periodically swept back into the authorized bank so that the offshore accumulation does not become a treasury parking point. Every addition to a Wise, PayPal or Payoneer balance must be traceable to an export-service invoice and declared in the annual foreign-exchange reporting file; any attempt to use such platforms as the primary collection account for inbound client revenue would raise SBV cross-border settlement, AML and tax reporting concerns.
