Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Vanuatu International Company. The authorised capital is fixed in the Memorandum of Association (market convention commonly sets USD 1,000 to USD 50,000), and shares may be issued for cash or other consideration in any currency. No escrow account, bank deposit, or physical capital deposit is mandated by VFSC for standard licensing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Vanuatu Fire and Emergency Services (VFES); wharf structural load and crane foundation certification by the Department of Public Works; Occupational Safety and Health (OSH) registration with the Department of Labour under the Workplace Health and Safety Act, including stevedoring risk assessments; inspection and registration of all mobile cranes, conveyor systems, and lifting gear under national equipment registration rules; emergency response and pollution contingency clearance coordinated with the National Disaster Management Office (NDMO) for terminal operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) is the sole registrar and regulator of International Companies (ICs); fiscal and customs administration is vested in Vanuatu Customs & Inland Revenue (VCIR) within the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management (MFEM). No general income-tax administration exists because Vanuatu imposes no national income, profits, capital-gains, or withholding taxes. Statutory e-portal: https://www.vfsc.vu.

E-government filing portal

No statutory TIN or VAT ID is issued to ICs. The VFSC company registration number on the Certificate of Incorporation serves as the sole fiscal identifier. Procedure for a foreign-owned IC: (1) engage a VFSC-licensed registered agent; (2) file the Memorandum and Articles through the VFSC e-portal; (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation bearing a unique company number; (4) file the annual exemption declaration and pay the VFSC annual government fee; (5) if any narrow domestic fiscal obligation arises (employer registration for locally resident staff or a customs account for imported terminal equipment), apply to VCIR for a TIN using the Certificate of Incorporation, a registered-agent letter, and CRS/FATCA self-certification. ICs are not registered with VCIR by default.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — No statutory TIN or VAT ID is issued to ICs. The VFSC company registration number on the Certificate of Incorporation serves as the sole fiscal identifier. Procedure for a foreign-owned IC: (1) engage a VFSC-licensed registered agent; (2) file the Memorandum and Articles through the VFSC e-portal; (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation bearing a unique company number; (4) file the annual exemption declaration and pay the VFSC annual government fee; (5) if any narrow domestic fiscal obligation arises (employer registration for locally resident staff or a customs account for imported terminal equipment), apply to VCIR for a TIN using the Certificate of Incorporation, a registered-agent letter, and CRS/FATCA self-certification. ICs are not registered with VCIR by default.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Exempt-status election is made at incorporation under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222]: all profits, income, capital gains, dividends, and distributions of an IC are exempt from all present and future Vanuatu taxes, duties, and stamp duty, provided the IC does not carry on business with Vanuatu residents or transact in Vatu currency. No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections exist in Vanuatu law. Optional statutory conversions are available: IC-to-local-company and local-company-to-IC (Part 12 of the IC Act), each requiring VFSC approval. No distribution tax, no withholding tax, and no thin-capitalization or interest-disallowance regime applies to ICs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No National VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use Tax (import customs duty and import infrastructure tax arise only at the border)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no consumption-tax regime, no turnover threshold, and no VAT registration requirement exists in Vanuatu; municipal business-license fees apply only to onshore trading activities, from which an IC is statutorily barred.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable by VAT mechanics: Vanuatu has no VAT/GST, so there is no 0% export rate and no input-tax refund pathway. The operative relief is the IC statutory exemption itself: all earnings of an IC, including stevedoring revenue from international bulk vessels, are fully exempt from Vanuatu taxation. Exemption is preserved only where stevedoring services are contracted by, invoiced to, and paid by non-resident shipowners or charterers in foreign currency (offshore business). Services rendered to Vanuatu resident principals or paid in Vatu breach the domestic-business prohibition and destroy exempt status.

  • Customs export declaration/export entry for the underlying bulk cargo
  • Bill of Lading evidencing carriage on an international voyage
  • Port/terminal stevedoring service contract with a non-resident carrier, charterer, or P&I-appointed surveyor
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit voucher for overseas freight and handling fees
  • Cargo manifest and tally sheets endorsed by the Vanuatu Ports Authority
  • VFSC annual return with tax-exemption declaration and payment receipt

None — Vanuatu imposes no e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control obligation. Stevedoring invoices may be issued in any agreed commercial form; SWIFT wire and Port Authority clearing rails are the operative settlement evidence.

No VAT clawback can arise because no consumption tax is in force. Vanuatu maintains no exchange controls, so the 0-day entry denotes the absence of any statutory FX-repatriation deadline rather than a same-day remittance requirement. Enforcement risk concentrates on the IC exempt-status conditions: knowingly carrying on domestic (onshore) business in Vatu with Vanuatu residents exposes the company to fines, loss of the statutory tax exemption, retrospective assessment of customs and municipal liabilities, VFSC deregistration, and mandatory re-registration as a local company; false declarations to VFSC are prosecutable offenses against both the company and its officers. Commercial export documentation (bill of lading, inward remittance, manifests) remains the standard audit trail for VFSC, VCIR, and bank AML review of port-terminal cash flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates an open foreign exchange regime with no exchange controls or surrender requirements on current or capital account transactions. The VUV is a managed-float currency linked to a trade-weighted SDR/partner-currency basket. There are no restrictions on repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital for bona fide trade or investment transactions. All cross-border settlements are subject to anti-money laundering (AML) due diligence and reporting of international transfers by the servicing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export receipts. Foreign-currency earnings from stevedoring and port services may be held offshore or in designated foreign-currency accounts at Vanuatu banks. There is no statutory timeline or minimum conversion ratio; exporters may convert at their discretion.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) – The national monetary authority established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [CAP 125]; regulates foreign exchange dealing, payment and settlement systems, and licensed banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. Vanuatu law does not provide for a dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) or digital bank licence class. Offshore EMIs can receive funds from foreign counterparties but cannot clear VUV-denominated payments or settle through the local Automated Transfer System (ATS). Use of unregulated EMIs as the primary banking rail is not advisable due to VFSC registration requirements under the Financial Dealers Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound investor capital must be routed through a SWIFT MT103 credit message to the bank account registered in the name of VU_IC. The payment should reference purpose code 'GIT' (General Investment) or 'CAPG' (Capital Goods and Materials) and include the Unique Investor Code issued by VIPA if the underlying project is classified as a foreign investment. The bank will require a board resolution from the IC authorising the account opening and deposit, an updated share register evidencing issued shares, and a source-of-funds declaration from the remitting shareholder. Funds originating from sanctioned or high-risk countries may require an additional legal letter and RBV notification.Outbound commitments (vessel agency fees, port handling royalties, equipment lease payments, and supplier invoices) are settled via: (i) standard SWIFT MT103 – for fiat transfers under USD/VUV 1,000,000 transaction threshold; (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit – for large capital imports of cranes/terminal machinery, with accepted currencies USD, EUR, AUD, NZD and JPY; and (iii) interbank VUV clearing through the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu's Deferred Net Settlement (DNS) system for domestic payables. All MT103 messages must be accompanied by underlying trade documentation (invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration) to satisfy RBV AML reporting requirements.Plain-vanilla forward foreign-exchange contracts with a licensed authorised dealer, allowing fixed-rate locking of up to 12 months for genuine trade or investment exposure.Cross-currency swaps between VUV and major trade-partner currencies (USD, AUD, NZD, EUR) executed with the prior approval of the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu.FX swaps with the same counterparty for rollover of short-term trade financing, subject to RBV spot/forward split confirmation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable; Vanuatu does not impose corporate income tax and has not enacted transfer-pricing documentation thresholds in Vanuatu vatu (VUV). No Local File, Master File, or Country-by-Country Report is required of an International Company.

Economic substance scope: No Economic Substance Regulations are currently in force in Vanuatu. However, an International Company must maintain a licensed registered agent and registered office in Vanuatu, and should retain corporate minutes and records to demonstrate separate legal personality and validity of the offshore structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed Domestic Business by an International Company

Statutory sanction: Striking-off or deregistration by VFSC, forfeiture of exemption benefits, cessation orders by the Ports Authority, and penalties under the International Companies Act and Vanuatu business licensing legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent may be subject to enforcement proceedings; a court may pierce the corporate veil if the International Company is used as a sham to conduct domestic trade, exposing directors to civil liability for debts and losses.
VAT and Customs Duty Evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid VAT/customs duty, statutory interest, monetary penalties, seizure and forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution under the VAT Act and Customs Act.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors can be prosecuted personally; failure to account for public money may trigger personal restitution orders, asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: VFSC administrative penalties, suspension or revocation of licenses, reporting to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit, and criminal fines or imprisonment under Vanuatu's AML/CFT legislation.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors who knowingly facilitate laundering may face criminal liability, personal fines, travel bans, and court-ordered confiscation of assets.
Fraudulent Trading and False Statements to VFSC

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties for false statements, deregistration, winding-up proceedings, disqualification orders against directors, and compulsory restoration fees.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fraudulent trading and losses suffered by creditors; the corporate veil may be pierced where the International Company is simply a façade, leading to personal fines, disqualification, and imprisonment.
Abusive Offshore Arrangement for Foreign Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu cooperates through tax information exchange agreements and mutual legal assistance mechanisms; domestic tax exemption does not protect the arrangement from lawful foreign tax investigations, freezing orders, or enforcement requests.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and directors remain liable under applicable foreign tax law; Vanuatu courts may assist with asset freezing, evidence production, and enforcement where the arrangement is fraudulent.
