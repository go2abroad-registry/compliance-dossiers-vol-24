Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR) — administrative contact via the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) for International Company registration and licensing; no separate national income-tax portal exists because Vanuatu levies no direct taxation.

E-government filing portal

A Vanuatu International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] is registered with VFSC and receives a Certificate of Incorporation bearing a unique company number that serves as its national identifier; no VAT/TIN number is issued. The IC must file an annual return with VFSC but is not required to register with DCIR for income-tax purposes. If the IC physically imports goods into Vanuatu, it must separately obtain a Customs Importer Code from DCIR under the Customs Act [Cap 195].

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Vanuatu International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] is registered with VFSC and receives a Certificate of Incorporation bearing a unique company number that serves as its national identifier; no VAT/TIN number is issued. The IC must file an annual return with VFSC but is not required to register with DCIR for income-tax purposes. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not_Applicable_as_a_VAT_concept. Vanuatu does not levy VAT, GST, exporter sales tax, or any zero-rated export mechanism. Exports of jewelry, watches, and fashion merchandise are outside any indirect tax charge; no refund claim is possible or required. Physical exports leaving Vanuatu are subject only to customs export clearance formalities, with no ad valorem export duty on finished jewelry or timepieces.

  • Customs Export Declaration (approved form under the Customs Act [Cap 195])
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (to evidence the external sale for anti-money-laundering and VFSC recordkeeping, given Stripe/PayPal settlement flows)

Not_Applicable_No_electronic_invoicing_fiscal_clearance_stamping_or_API_clearance_mandate_exists_for_digital_or_physical_goods_exports

No VAT clawback is applicable because no indirect tax on exports exists. However, penalties apply for misuse of IC status: an International Company engaging in prohibited domestic business with Vanuatu residents, or carrying on business without maintaining a registered agent, forfeits its statutory tax exemption and is liable under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] to fines of up to VUV 500,000. False customs export declarations are prosecuted under the Customs Act [Cap 195] with confiscation of goods, monetary penalties, and imprisonment on conviction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu has no exchange controls and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The legal tender is the Vanuatu Vatu (VUV), but foreign currency accounts are freely permitted. There are no profit-repatriation hurdles, no withholding taxes on dividends of an International Company, no exchange control licences, and no requirement for government permission for outward capital flows. For an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act (Cap. 259), remittance of capital, dividends, loan repayments, and trade payments can be executed freely; banking partners will nonetheless apply ordinary anti-money-laundering (AML) and know-your-client (KYC) filters to verify source of wealth, transaction purpose, and absence of sanctioned parties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Vanuatu imposes no statutory export-receipt surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any currency in local or foreign bank accounts, used directly for trade settlement, or repatriated without central bank notification. The only applicable requirement is standard reporting by banks to the RBV for macro-prudential statistics and suspicious-transaction reporting under the AML/CFT Act, not individual trade-flow approval.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act (Cap. 125); the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) is the non-bank financial services regulator but the RBV retains prudential regulation of banking and foreign exchange.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility, with important legal constraints. Vanuatu does not issue a distinct 'Electronic Money Institution' license. A non-bank accepting customer moneys or executing payment transactions must hold a banking license under the Banking Act (Cap. 63) or be registered as a Financial Dealer under the Financial Dealers Act (Cap. 60) and be regulated by the VFSC/RBV. Offshore digital EMIs licensed in the EU, UK, Australia, or Singapore may service the Vanuatu entity from abroad and provide accessible multi-currency settlement, but they generally cannot open local VUV clearing accounts without an RBV-approved bank acting as agent. For this e-commerce jewelry/watch operation, global PSP rails (Stripe, PayPal) are commercially permitted for customer collections, but the core operating liquidity, supplier disbursement, and capital escrow should remain in a tier-1 Vanuatu financial institution to reduce settlement and regulatory risk.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial owner is a non-resident, offshore-domiciled individual with no prior Vanuatu bank relationship.

Root cause: Beneficial owner is a non-resident, offshore-domiciled individual with no prior Vanuatu bank relationship.

Defense evidence: Bundle consisting of a current passport (notarized and apostilled), a board resolution authorising account opening, a certificate of incumbency from the registered agent, a VFSC extract of the International Company, a source-of-wealth statement supported by audited accounts or regulated-bank statements, and an official regulatory reference letter from any existing foreign regulated bank showing no adverse history.
High-value goods (fine jewelry and timepieces) triggering enhanced due diligence due to elevated money-laundering typology risk.

Root cause: High-value goods (fine jewelry and timepieces) triggering enhanced due diligence due to elevated money-laundering typology risk.

Defense evidence: Detailed commercial documentation: product catalogue with photos and serial numbers (for timepieces), supplier invoices, dealer resale/consignment agreements, transport and insurance certificates, and a written AML/PEP/sanctions compliance policy for the e-commerce platform. Add a client-risk assessment matrix and a copy-calculation showing that average transaction values and volumes are consistent with ordinary online luxury trade.
Inbound capital injection originated from a non-regulated vehicle or aggregated payment-service-provider account.

Root cause: Inbound capital injection originated from a non-regulated vehicle or aggregated payment-service-provider account.

Defense evidence: Provide a structured source-of-funds chain: equity declaration, audited financial statements or accountancy report, a notarized letter from the beneficial owner describing the funds, and bank statements from the original remitting regulated account. Confirm that no client funds from the Stripe/PayPal settlement are commingled before the capital injection; arrange a direct remittance from an ordinary regulated bank account.
Outbound wire transfers to suppliers in high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., China, UAE, Hong Kong) or to finance vendors that manufacture replicas/counterfeit goods.

Root cause: Outbound wire transfers to suppliers in high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., China, UAE, Hong Kong) or to finance vendors that manufacture replicas/counterfeit goods.

Defense evidence: Deliver commercial purchase orders, supply agreements, letters of credit, shipping bill of lading or air waybill, inward invoice matching, and the supplier's own business license and directorship list. Include a screenshot of the supplier's online presence and the eBay/Amazon store registration link for the e-commerce affiliate model. Provide transaction narrative in the SWIFT field 72 showing the goods description and the unique invoice number.
Frequent cross-border FX conversions at inconsistent rates or unusual use of third-party intermediary currencies.

Root cause: Frequent cross-border FX conversions at inconsistent rates or unusual use of third-party intermediary currencies.

Defense evidence: Create and maintain a treasury cadence showing purchase/settlement currency needs; provide invoices denominated in USD/EUR/CAD/AUD; attach FX rate confirmations, and clearly state that no pass-through financing or third-party payment arrangement exists. If a local third party attempts to settle on behalf of the company, the bank will require a foreign-exchange service-provider agreement and ensure the beneficiary name remains the final commercial supplier.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Vanuatu International Company, inbound equity capital is accepted through SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's regulated bank account or, for larger rounds, via MT202COV where the intermediary bank needs cover. Use the BPM6/ISO 20022 purpose code B011 for contributed equity capital (or B013 for intercompany loan); in the SWIFT remittance information field (FT 72) state 'Share Capital Contribution / Loan per Facility Agreement'. Route the wire through established correspondents (ANZ Melbourne/Sydney, Bred Bank's Paris/Luxembourg correspondent, or BSP Asia Pacific) to avoid intermediary fees. The bank will verify the beneficiary name exactly as it appears on the VFSC registration; no RBV approval or foreign-investor permit is required for an International Company. Retain a copy of the board resolution and shareholder register for the capital injection for the customer-due-diligence file.Trade payments for jewelry and watch inventory should be cleared primarily by SWIFT MT103 straight-through transfers for orders under USD 100,000, and by confirmed irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) for larger consignments or when supplier terms demand documentary security. For recurring procurements from Hong Kong, Singapore, UAE, and India, use a designated beneficiary name exactly matching the invoice; include the purchase order number, invoice reference, and goods classification in the SWIFT narrative. For LCs, a senior domestic bank can issue on the basis of the client's credit or cash collateral; this provides supplier-side confidence without earlier payment at risk. Cross-currency settlement is free of exchange-control restrictions; the account bank may quote VUV/USD for televised transfers, but it is advisable to retain USD/EUR/AUD clearing lines to reduce two-step conversion exposure. For outbound PayPal/Stripe remittances to the Vanuatu operational account, settlement occurs in USD/EUR/AUD and requires linking to a regulated bank account; all PSP settlement funds must be traceable to the original customer sale to satisfy AML audits.FX Forward Contracts: Vanuatu-licensed ADI banks may write deliverable forwards on VUV/USD, AUD/USD, EUR/USD, and GBP/USD to lock trade settlement rates, provided there is an underlying commercial transaction and the bank does not take an uncovered speculative position.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): permitted on currencies with restricted onshore convertibility (e.g., CNY, INR, PHP, KRW, IDR, and AED/AED-related pairs) through international counterparties; NDF cash settlement is executed in USD, not in VUV, and is subject to standard ISDA/IFX master agreements.Cross-Currency Swaps: vanilla interest rate and principal swaps between VUV, USD, AUD, and EUR are available through treasury teams of major internationally-licensed banks where the Vanuatu entity maintains a global-liquidity account; these are permissible when used to convert foreign-currency funding or hedge balance-sheet risk, but must be documented under an ISDA agreement with a qualified-rated counterparty to avoid regulatory re-characterization as a speculative exchange contract.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (nil) — Vanuatu levies no national corporate income tax; an International Company is statutorily exempt from all income, profit, and capital-gains taxes under the tax-exemption provisions of the International Companies Act [Cap. 222].

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — dividends paid by a Vanuatu International Company to resident or non-resident shareholders are wholly exempt from Vanuatu withholding tax. No dividend declaration duty, no gross-up, and no exchange-control approval is required for outward remittance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 4 months after each anniversary of incorporation; the annual government fee is payable at the same time. Failure to file for two consecutive years triggers administrative strike-off and dissolution by the Registrar of International Companies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Vanuatu transfer-pricing statute or domestic documentation threshold exists. Market practice under OECD standards: prepare contemporaneous local-file-equivalent documentation when annual related-party transactions exceed VUV 10,000,000 (approximately USD 83,000); full master file plus country-by-country reporting readiness is prudent when group turnover approaches EUR 750,000,000 due to OECD Inclusive Framework GloBE rules.

Economic substance scope: Economic-substance requirements apply under the International Companies (Economic Substance) Amendment Act 2022/2023, implementing EU and OECD/G20 commitments. An IC conducting a relevant activity, including 'distribution and service centre' (e-commerce sales, logistics, procurement) or 'holding entity' business, must demonstrate in Vanuatu: (1) direction and management (board meetings held in Vanuatu); (2) adequate physical premises; (3) full-time qualified employees; (4) adequate annual operating expenditure. A jewelry-and-watches e-commerce IC that operates as a Stripe/PayPal shell with substance located elsewhere is in breach. Penalties reach VUV 10,000,000, with deregistration and information exchange to the beneficial owner's jurisdiction as consequences.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Offshore Tax Evasion and Permanent Establishment Exposure

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu side: VFSC strike-off and fines up to VUV 100,000 for false declarations. Foreign side: full tax reassessment with penalties (up to 75% accuracy-related penalty in the United States; up to 100% base penalty in Australia), VAT/GST back-claims, and enforcement against the company's bank accounts and payment-processor balances.

Director personal liability: Directors face director penalty notices in Australia and analogous personal-liability regimes elsewhere, making them personally liable for unpaid company taxes. Criminal tax-evasion convictions carry imprisonment (United States: up to 5 years per count under 26 U.S.C. § 7201; Australia: up to 10 years under Criminal Code s 134.2), alongside travel bans, extradition, and seizure of personal assets.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: Economic-substance financial penalty up to VUV 10,000,000; daily default fines; compulsory deregistration with asset forfeiture; adverse referral through TIEA channels to the beneficial owner's tax authority, triggering CFC and foreign-entity-classification rules that eliminate the zero-tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for ESR civil penalties and may be disqualified from acting as directors. Beneficial owners face reclassification of the entity as a shell under EU ATAD Annex V rules, resulting in tax-residence reallocation and look-through taxation in their country of tax residence.
Anti-Money Laundering / CFT and Payment-Rails Violations

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu AML/CFT Act No. 21 of 2016: civil penalties up to VUV 20,000,000 for reporting-entity failures; money-laundering penalties under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2005 include imprisonment up to 20 years; VFSC suspension of the registered agent; Stripe and PayPal permanent account termination, rolling-reserve confiscation, and referral to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Officers knowingly involved face personal fines up to VUV 5,000,000 and imprisonment for money-laundering offences. Directors may be named in global AML enforcement actions, triggering Interpol red notices, extradition requests, and civil asset-recovery orders extending to personal and family property.
Fraudulent Trading, False Declarations, and Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Officer-level fines up to VUV 100,000 under the International Companies Act; fraudulent-trading provisions of the Companies Act [Cap. 191] render the wrongdoer personally liable without limitation; the Supreme Court may order winding up, confiscation, and disqualification of directors.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for all company debts to creditors and revenue authorities; criminal fraud prosecution under the Penal Code [Cap. 135] may result in imprisonment, confiscation orders, a 10-year directorship ban, and a travel ban imposed by the Supreme Court of Vanuatu.
Unlicensed Financial / Escrow Activity

Statutory sanction: Carrying on regulated financial business without a VFSC license is a criminal offence punishable by fines up to VUV 10,000,000 and imprisonment; the Supreme Court may wind up the company and order restitution of client funds; affected contracts are void and unenforceable.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are criminally liable for unlicensed financial-services activity and are jointly and severally ordered to repay customer funds personally; continued operation after a VFSC cease-and-desist notice results in custodial sentences and asset-freezing orders.
