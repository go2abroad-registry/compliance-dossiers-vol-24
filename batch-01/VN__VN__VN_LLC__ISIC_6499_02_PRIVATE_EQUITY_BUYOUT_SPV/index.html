Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Business Registration Office, Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) of the province or centrally administered city where the SPV's registered head office is located, using the National Business Registration Portal (https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn) and the National Investment Portal as the electronic one-stop windows. If the head office is in an economic/industrial/export-processing zone, the relevant Management Board replaces the DPI as the receiving authority for the investment registration certificate.

Governing statute

Law on Enterprises No. 59/2020/QH14 of the Socialist Republic of Viet Nam, in particular Articles 26 through 31 (enterprise registration) and Articles 46 through 81 (limited liability companies); Law on Investment No. 61/2020/QH14, in particular Articles 22 (market access), 33 (investment project dossier), 38 (procedures for establishment of an economic organization by a foreign investor), and 39 (time limit for issuance of an Investment Registration Certificate); Decree No. 01/2021/NĐ-CP on enterprise registration; Decree No. 31/2021/NĐ-CP detailing the Law on Investment. The SPV is not subject to the Law on Credit Institutions because it does not take deposits or provide payment services; it will not engage in regulated fund management or securities dealing unless separately licensed by the State Securities Commission.

Formation / constitutional filings

Enterprise Registration Application (Giấy đề nghị đăng ký doanh nghiệp) prescribed by Decree No. 01/2021/NĐ-CP, submitted together with the company charter, list of members, legal-status documents of the founding members and legal representatives, and the Investment Registration Certificate previously granted to the foreign investors; the combined IRC/ERC dossier may in some provinces be submitted to a one-stop administrative office of the DPI.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital amount is prescribed for a limited liability company under the Law on Enterprises 2020, and ISIC 6499 private-equity buyout SPVs are not listed in any conditional market-access schedule requiring a mandatory capital floor in Viet Nam. The founding members must nevertheless declare a charter capital in the enterprise registration dossier; that capital may be in cash or lawful assets and must be contributed in full within 90 days after the enterprise registration certificate is issued. There is no mandatory notarial escrow account; however, foreign investors must channel contributed capital and inward loan proceeds through a direct investment capital account opened at a licensed bank in Viet Nam under State Bank of Vietnam Circular No. 03/2015/TT-NHNN, and the capital amount must be consistent with the project scale described in the Investment Registration Certificate.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (GDT) of Viet Nam under the Ministry of Finance (Bao Tai Chinh / Tong Cuc Thue). Digital infrastructure comprises the e-Tax and e-Invoice portal at https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn, the Foreign Supplier Tax Portal (etaxvn) at https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn, and the National Business Registration Portal at https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn; provincial Departments of Planning and Investment (DPI) and provincial tax departments act as integrated issuing and collection agencies.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial DPI or the Export Processing Zone/Industrial Zone Authority (for EPE-located projects). Step 2: Obtain an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); under the integrated registration regime the enterprise code on the ERC is automatically the 10-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN / Ma so thue) granted by the GDT. Step 3: File the supplementary tax registration dossier (Form 01-DK-TCT prescribed by Circular 105/2020/TT-BTC) with the managing tax office within 15 working days of ERC issuance, confirming accounting regime, business lines, bank accounts, and legal representative details. Step 4: The GDT activates the VAT, CIT, and withholding tax profiles under the single TIN. For fraudulent, incomplete, or inactive registration the GDT may suspend invoice use, impose penalties under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP, or invalidate the TIN; the LLC must display the TIN on all invoices, contracts, and correspondence.

Pass-through eligibility does not exist in Vietnam; a domestic LLC has no transparent or flow-through alternative. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The foreign-invested limited liability company is a CIT-resident enterprise taxed at the standard corporate income tax rate of 20% on worldwide income under the Law on Corporate Income Tax No. 14/2008/QH12 as amended. Pass-through eligibility does not exist in Vietnam; a domestic LLC has no transparent or flow-through alternative. Territorial_Scope: profits are recognized at entity level; dividends remitted to foreign shareholders are not subject to additional dividend withholding after final CIT, but offshore interest, royalties, technical service fees, and cross-border service payments to non-residents are within the Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) regime, and capital gains from transfers of the LLC's shares by foreign investors are subject to 20% CIT on net gains (or 0.1% of gross transfer price at the investor's election). Tax_Exempt_Zone: not automatic; concessional rates (10%, 15%, 17%) or tax holidays arise only through an investment incentive certificate, high-tech/industrial zone location, or Export Processing Enterprise (EPE) status, and are obtained separately from entity classification.

Available election forms

Vietnam does not adopt a US-style check-the-box election, nor does it permit hybrid fiscal-transparent elections or treaty override elections that would reclassify the LLC for domestic tax purposes; the LLC is a CIT taxpayer by automatic operation of law. Available statutory elections: (i) selection of the VAT deduction method versus the direct method (deduction method is mandatory in practice for exporters claiming 0% VAT and input VAT refunds, and is compulsory above the VND 50 billion turnover threshold); (ii) election to apply investment tax incentives (tax holidays, preferential rates) by filing the required incentive registrations with the tax office; (iii) election of depreciation method (straight-line is default, accelerated is available on satisfaction of Circular 45/2013/TT-BTC criteria); (iv) election of transfer pricing method (comparable uncontrolled price, resale minus, cost plus, transactional net margin, profit split) and option to file a benchmarking study under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP; (v) no hybrid mismatch election is available; anti-hybrid consequences and CFC rules under Decree 132/2020 and the Law on Tax Administration override any contractual or accounting characterizations aimed at achieving double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 100,000,000 (one hundred million dong) of annual turnover per Article 3 of Decree 209/2013/ND-CP is the VAT registration and liability threshold for households and individual businesses. For corporate taxpayers (including the subject foreign-invested LLC) there is no VAT registration threshold: the LLC is automatically registered for VAT upon issuance of the ERC/TIN, regardless of turnover. The VND 100 million benchmark only affects eligibility for simplified compliance regimes and does not exempt any corporate entity from VAT registration, e-invoicing, or filing obligations. Turnover of the private-equity SPV (management fees, interest, dividends, disposal gains, and incidental income) is aggregated for all VAT and CIT threshold tests.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate is claimable on goods physically exported outside Viet Nam (including goods sold to export processing enterprises (EPE), non-tariff zones, and foreign-bound bonded warehouses) and on services supplied to foreign persons for consumption outside Viet Nam, provided the conditions of Article 9 of Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC are met. Core conditions: (i) an export contract with a foreign party transferring ownership and risk of loss outside Viet Nam; (ii) actual export evidenced by a customs-cleared export declaration; (iii) transport documentation (B/L, AWB, CMR, or multimodal bill) proving dispatch abroad; (iv) a commercial invoice and bank evidence of receipt of export proceeds in a convertible currency; (v) settlement and foreign-exchange repatriation through the banking system within 180 days, except for legally approved set-offs or State Bank of Vietnam dispensation; (vi) the LLC must use the invoice deduction method and maintain complete separate books for export activities. The private-equity SPV may claim 0% VAT only on genuine exports of goods or offshore services it itself supplies; passive financial services (loan origination, dividend flows, share trading) are generally VAT-exempt or FCT-covered rather than zero-rated, so the SPV must not mix zero-rated export lines with exempt financial income in a way that distorts input credit computation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic e-declaration via the VNACCS/VCIS system with 'cleared / da thong quan' status)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean/multimodal), Air Waybill, CMR, or rail consignment note evidencing dispatch outside Viet Nam
  • Export contract signed by the LLC and the foreign buyer stating Incoterms, value, currency, and delivery terms
  • Commercial invoice generated from the e-invoice system matching the customs declaration in quantity, unit price, and total value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit confirmation (SWIFT MT103/MT950 or equivalent) proving receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Foreign exchange settlement authorizations and offsetting documentation (Circular 39/2014/TT-NHNN) where proceeds are not directly repatriated
  • Certificate of Origin (Form E, Form D, or other) where preferential tariff treatment or FTA benefits are claimed in the importing jurisdiction
  • Inspection certificates, phytosanitary certificates, or conditional-export licenses required for goods subject to regulatory approval

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for all enterprises under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. Each e-invoice must be an XML-structured invoice with a tax authority code or a self-issued invoice code, signed with a digital certificate (CA), and transmitted through the GDT e-invoice system (hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn) or through certified intermediary service providers using prescribed API endpoints. Invoices with tax authority codes undergo real-time transactional clearance: the GDT issues a verification code at the moment of issuance, validating the invoice as legally compliant. Self-issued e-invoices (no pre-assigned GDT code) must be transmitted to the GDT data repository within 10 days from the invoice date (distinct schedules apply to high-volume issuers). The e-invoice must contain the TIN, buyer and seller identities, EPP/EPE status where applicable, line-item description, VAT rate, and segregated zero-rated export lines. Real-time clearance does not involve physical fiscal stamps but statutory validity requires successful electronic acceptance with an official confirmation status; failure to transmit or any alteration after transmission provides grounds for the tax authority to deny input VAT credits, impose penalties, and open de facto assessment proceedings.

The GDT applies heightened post-clearance scrutiny to zero-rated exports and VAT refund applications, auditing (i) invoice-against-customs-declaration congruence, (ii) physical reality of export logistics, (iii) offshore residency and substance of the buyer, (iv) use of back-to-back SPVs to fabricate offshore consumption, and (v) repeated reliance on 180-day extensions or set-off mechanisms. If export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days (unless a lawful State Bank of Vietnam set-off/extension is obtained), the zero-rated treatment is retrospectively withdrawn: the LLC must re-output VAT at 8%/10%, refund any input VAT credit previously refunded or credited, and pay late-payment interest at 0.03% per day on the underpaid tax from the original due date to full settlement. Under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP (as amended by Decree 102/2021/ND-CP), false e-invoices, inaccurate customs declarations, fraudulent refund claims, and tax evasion carry administrative fines from 20% to 100% of the understated tax amount (with a fine cap of VND 500 million for organizational misconduct) plus forcible recovery of the refund and interest. Criminal liability may attach under Article 200 of the Penal Code 2015/2017: commercial legal persons found guilty of tax evasion face fines of VND 500 million to VND 20 billion, suspension of operations, or a 1-3 year prohibition on doing business; responsible managers may face imprisonment of up to 7 years, and the tax authority may publish the enterprise on its compliance blacklist, revoke the e-invoice authorization, and delegate the case to the Economic Police for criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam maintains a managed partial convertibility regime. Current account transactions (trade payments, service fees, interest, and profit remittances) are generally convertible without pre-approval, subject to documentation and tax compliance. Capital account transactions (FDI capital contributions, shareholder loans, acquisitions, divestment proceeds) require prior registration with the SBV and are settled only through licensed banks via a foreign investment capital account (DICA). Profit remittance is permitted after audited financial statements and corporate income tax finalization; a tax clearance certificate may be required. The VND is not freely floatable; the SBV sets daily reference exchange rates within a trading band.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vietnam imposes a mandatory export proceeds surrender: exporters must sell 80% of eligible export foreign currency receipts to a licensed bank within 15 days of receipt. The remaining 20% may be retained in a foreign currency account. For a PE buyout SPV with no direct export operations, any non-trade or incidental foreign receipts should be classified as capital/current-account remittances, not export proceeds; however, any indirect export-related inflows must comply with Circular 03/2016/TT-NHNN and be surrendered within the statutory 15-day window.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) — the central bank and foreign exchange administration authority; all foreign exchange activities are regulated by the SBV and the Foreign Exchange Control Department, whose authority is exercised through licensed commercial banks as authorized foreign exchange institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorized banks in Vietnam and cannot hold a DICA or corporate operational account directly. They may provide payment facilitation or IBAN-style services upstream, but the SPV must maintain its primary settlement and DICA accounts with an SBV-licensed bank. Use of an offshore EMI for VND clearing would violate SBV regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI capital must be wired from the investor's overseas bank to the SPV's DICA at a licensed Vietnamese bank. SWIFT payment instructions must cite the FDI capital contribution purpose and be supported by the IRC, SBV registration code, and the bank's DICA account mandate. The bank will identify the transfer as capital injection and maintain the DICA balance until the SPV redeploys it. The capital must be used only for lawful investment purposes and must be recorded in the company's charter capital ledger.Outbound disbursements for acquisition consideration, syndicated loan repayment, dealer fees, and operating costs should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 / MT202 using VND or foreign exchange settlement through the operating account. For trade-related payables (e.g., equipment or services), commercial letters of credit can be used, but for a PE SPV, direct telegraphic transfers are the prevalent clearing rail. All outbound payments must be supported by underlying contracts, approved by the bank's compliance team, and reported to the SBV under the balance of payments or foreign loan monitoring system.Onshore deliverable FX forwards between a licensed Vietnamese bank and the SPV for genuine VND/USD exposure, maximum tenor one year.FX swaps (spot/forward) with an authorized bank.Cross-currency interest rate swaps for the LBO/syndicated facility.NDFs permitted only off-balance-sheet / offshore on an exceptional basis; not recommended.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital EMIs are not authorized banks in Vietnam and cannot hold a DICA or corporate operational account directly. They may provide payment facilitation or IBAN-style services upstream, but the SPV must maintain its primary settlement and DICA accounts with an SBV-licensed bank. Use of an offshore EMI for VND clearing would violate SBV regulations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File where annual revenue exceeds VND 50 billion OR related-party transaction value exceeds VND 30 billion; Master File is required where the consolidated worldwide revenue of the ultimate parent entity exceeds VND 500 billion. Vietnamese TP reporting also requires a Country-by-Country notification for consolidated groups above VND 18,000 billion (approx. USD 685 million); enterprises below these thresholds must still maintain contemporaneous TP documentation and perform an arm’s length analysis.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam does not codify an ESR regime equivalent to the Gulf Cooperation Council’s Economic Substance Regulations. However, an Economic Substance Doctrine is applied through the general anti-abuse rules in Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, the test of ‘business purpose’ in tax-treaty interpretation, and the constitutional requirement that an enterprise be a genuine operating entity with headquarters, managerial decision-making, payroll, and accounting records in Vietnam. A private equity buyout SPV must demonstrate real substance by maintaining a physical office, local management, transaction controls, and contemporaneous records of the investment and acquisition decisions; absence of substance can justify recharacterisation of the SPV’s roles, denial of treaty benefits, and a full 20% CIT recalculation on the allocable profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent invoicing

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of 1 to 3 times the evaded CIT for underdeclared income; late-payment interest at 0.03% per day; criminal prosecution for tax evasion where the unpaid amount is VND 100,000,000 or more, with imprisonment of up to 3 years, or up to 10 years when the amount exceeds VND 500,000,000.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is available where directors knowingly sign fraudulent returns or direct the concealment of revenue; directors and legal representatives are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution, suffer travel bans, and can be sentenced to imprisonment under the Penal Code for tax evasion.
Shell-company misuse and sham SPV

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the shell entity under the general anti-abuse rule, reattribute income to the underlying beneficial owner, deny preferential rates, impose a reassessment at the 20% CIT rate, add late-payment interest at 0.03% per day, and apply a non-compliance penalty of up to 3 times the underpaid amount; substantial violations also risk enterprise licence suspension under commercial law.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly establish or maintain a sham SPV expose themselves to personal liability for the deficient tax and interest, a travel ban, and criminal charges for the use of false commercial documents, notwithstanding the general limited-liability protection of the legal entity.
AML/CFT non-compliance in cross-border financial flows

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines in the range of VND 50,000,000 to VND 200,000,000 for non-reporting; repeat violations can lead to suspension of the entity’s payment or investment licences; wilful concealment of the economic nature of a wire constitutes money laundering with a criminal penalty of up to 10 years’ imprisonment and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who circumvent AML controls, falsify beneficial-ownership declarations, or knowingly route funds to avoid detection are personally liable, risk civil forfeiture of personal assets, may be banned from overseas travel, and may be prosecuted for money laundering.
Transfer-pricing documentation failure and mispricing of related-entity services

Statutory sanction: Assessment of deemed turnover and recharacterisation of the SPV’s transaction, recalculation of the CIT deficiency at 20%, late-payment interest at 0.03% per day, and an administrative penalty ranging from 20% to 100% of the transfer-pricing adjustment amount; treaty benefits may also be disallowed under Limitation-on-Benefits provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors are held personally liable when they knowingly sign false TP declarations or deliberately destroy contemporaneous documents; such conduct constitutes wilful negligence, leading to joint restitution obligations, travel restrictions, and possible imprisonment for fraudulent tax administration.
