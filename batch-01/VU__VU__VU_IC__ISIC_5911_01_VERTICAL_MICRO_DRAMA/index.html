Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), operating under the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official tax administration portal: https://www.vu.customs/; VAT registration administered directly by the VRC VAT Unit in Port Vila.

E-government filing portal

A VU_IC incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] is not automatically issued a Vanuatu tax identification number. For the tax-exempt IC that wishes to claim VAT zero-rating on exported digital services or to open a local revenue trail, the following procedure applies: (i) submit VRC Form VAT-01 (Application for Registration) together with a certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC); (ii) submit the registered agent certificate, the registered office address, list of directors/shareholders, and a local or foreign payment processor/bank confirmation; (iii) estimate taxable turnover to demonstrate either mandatory registration (taxable turnover in the observation period exceeds VUV 5,000,000 per annum) or voluntary registration; (iv) upon approval, VRC issues a VAT registration certificate with a unique TIN, normally within 5–10 business days. Non-resident ownership raises no restrictions; a foreign-domiciled director is allowed, but a local licensed agent must be maintained for the IC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The VU_IC is not a pass-through entity and is not subject to Vanuatu entity-level income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, profits tax, or business licence tax, by virtue of sections 2, 3, and 7 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222]. This exemption is statutory rather than election-based. The only national indirect tax exposure is VAT under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 250], and it applies only to taxable supplies made in or imported into Vanuatu. The VU_IC's global revenues from a short-drama streaming app (Apple IAP, Google Play, Stripe) sourced entirely from non-resident subscribers are, as an international company, outside the scope of Vanuatu income taxation and qualify for VAT zero-rating as exported services.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election. The sole statutory elections relevant to a VU_IC are: (i) voluntary VAT registration under section 7 of the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 250], elected by an exporter to recover input VAT and to legitimise zero-rated invoices issued to non-resident customers; and (ii) an IC's binding election under section 14 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222] to convert to a domestic company under the Companies Act [Cap 191], which irrevocably removes the tax-exempt status and subjects the entity to full domestic income tax and business licence obligations. No other tax classification elections are available under Vanuatu law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 of taxable supplies in any 12-month period (approx. USD 42,000 at prevailing official exchange rate); mandatory registration threshold; exporters of zero-rated supplies may register voluntarily from the first Vatu of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under section 5(2) of the Value Added Tax Act [Cap 250] to (a) supplies of goods physically exported from Vanuatu, and (b) supplies of services performed for or directly enjoyed by a person who belongs in a country outside Vanuatu. For the VU_IC's vertical micro-drama streaming app, each supply of digital subscription, pay-per-view micro-drama, or advertisement-based streaming service sold through Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, or Stripe to an end-user located outside Vanuatu must satisfy the 'direct export of services' test: the place of consumption is the subscriber's foreign country, the contract is with a non-resident subscriber or app-store aggregator, the digital content is delivered electronically to the foreign user, and the invoice identifies the non-resident buyer. Supplies which bleed into the domestic Vanuatu market (e.g., an in-app sale to a customer physically present in Vanuatu) are taxable at 15%, and revenue allocation is based on the app store's country-of-account data at the time of purchase.

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing telemetry, API integration, or digital stamping mandate is currently enforced in Vanuatu. The VRC requires, however, that every VAT-registered supplier issues a sequentially numbered VAT invoice containing the supplier's name, address and TIN, the recipient's name/address/TIN where available, a unique invoice number and date, a description of supplies, the value exclusive of VAT, the VAT amount at 15%, and the invoice total. Invoices may be issued electronically, but such e-invoices are not subject to pre-clearance or live countersignature by VRC. The taxpayer must retain the invoice issue log and make it available to VRC upon inspection, with the record retention window set at a minimum of 7 years.

Although Vanuatu imposes no exchange controls, a 180-calendar-day outward remittance/receipt settlement window is the outer substantiation period benchmark used by VRC to support a zero-rating claim for exported services; if the corresponding foreign-currency settlement is not received within the 180-day window, the VRC may issue an assessment treating the supply as taxable at 15%. Penalties for fraudulent or unsupported zero-ratings include a 100% penalty on the VAT understated, monthly compounding interest at statutory rates, administrative fines of up to VUV 1,000,000, imprisonment of the responsible director for tax fraud under the Value Added Tax Act, forfeiture of the importer's/ exporter's goods, and, in the case of a VU_IC, a strike-off of the company by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission and the loss of the International Companies Act tax exemption. VRC audits carry a 7-year look-back period; therefore, complete documentary evidence (contracts, invoices, aggregated app-store records, bank/Stripe settlement statements, and customs or e-export manifests for physical media) must be retained for no less than 8 years after the export transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains a liberal exchange-control regime with current-account convertibility effectively unrestricted. The VUV is the legal tender and the RBV publishes daily reference rates, but non-resident-owned International Companies (ICs) that operate entirely in foreign currency are not subject to prior RBV exchange-control approval for bona fide trade and capital transactions. Capital-account inflows (shareholder equity injections, shareholder loans) are freely permitted for non-resident ICs provided the IC does not borrow from Vanuatu residents; an Authorised Dealer must record the transaction for statistical purposes. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are unrestricted and not subject to Vanuatu income tax or withholding tax under the International Companies Act [Cap 222], although the paying bank will require a VFSC certificate of good standing, filed annual returns, and a written remittance instruction. For a micro-drama streaming IC (ISIC 5911), treasury operations should be concentrated in USD/EUR to minimise VUV conversion friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds. The statutory surrender ratio is zero percent; exporters and non-resident ICs may retain 100% of receipts from digital exports (streaming subscriptions, in-app purchases, advertising revenue, content licensing) in any convertible currency or in VUV-denominated accounts. The only obligation is that Authorised Dealers submit monthly cross-border transaction reports to the RBV under the Exchange Control Regulations for balance-of-payments statistics. VUV conversion of export proceeds, if desired, may be executed at commercial bank rates with a statement of the underlying trade.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), acting under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 41], the Exchange Control Regulations, and the International Banking Act [Cap 269]; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) administers International Company registration and AML/CFT supervision of banks and registered agents.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility for direct fintech EMI onboarding in Vanuatu. The jurisdiction has no electronic money institution licensing regime, no fintech passporting framework, and the Currency Exchange Act [Cap 137] restricts unlicensed money-exchange activity. Major global EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) do not support Vanuatu-incorporated companies as primary onboarding clients, and Stripe does not list Vanuatu as a supported settlement country. A legally viable structure is to use a licensed foreign merchant-of-record or a Hong Kong/Singapore/Delaware subsidiary of the Vanuatu IC for Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe settlement, with subsequent intercompany licence-fee remittance to the IC. The non-resident IC may lawfully hold accounts with reputable foreign EMIs only for collection purposes, provided the EMI holds a valid money-services licence in its host jurisdiction and does not solicit business from Vanuatu residents.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: shareholders transmit SWIFT MT103 wires to the IC's BSP, BRED, or NBV account with the beneficiary name exactly matching the VFSC-registered entity, using narrative 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION VU_IC / ISIC 5911 SHARE ALLOTMENT'. The instruction must reference the bank's SWIFT BIC and the IC's segregated VUV or USD clearing account. The corporate file must contain a board resolution approving the share allotment, issued share certificates, and a completed exchange-control statistical record filed through the Authorised Dealer. For trade receipts originating from app stores and digital platforms, the platform payout must name the legal entity and be matched against gateway settlement reports; the recommended statistical purpose code is '510030 – digital content services revenue'. For liquidity efficiency, request the bank's USD/EUR nostro clearing account to avoid any VUV conversion and the associated RBV revaluation spread.Outbound treasury rails include: (1) SWIFT MT103 for individual cross-border payments such as Chinese production studios, Southeast Asian talent agencies, cloud hosting and transcoding services; (2) SWIFT MT101 customer-payment-initiation messages for batch payroll and royalty runs; (3) irrevocable confirmed Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT750 issuance) for high-value backend distribution buyouts where the beneficiary requires bank-undertaking certainty; and (4) platform clearing rails from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing arriving via their US/EU acquiring banks in monthly settlement cycles, then reconciled against gateway reports. Treasury convention: route all disbursements through segregated USD, EUR, and CNY accounts, preserve a 45-day liquidity buffer for anticipated royalty outgo, and include clear payment-purposes narratives inside every MT103 to satisfy Authorised Dealer and correspondent-bank monitoring. Use SWIFT gpi tracking for time-critical talent and production payments; bank charges are booked as FX and clearing commissions.FX Forwards: USD/VUV, USD/CNH, USD/THB, and USD/EUR forward contracts with tenors up to 12 months, executed via BSP or BRED Treasury under an ISDA-type master confirmation.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): USD/VUV NDFs referencing the RBV official daily reference rate, and USD/CNH NDFs to hedge offshore RMB production costs facing the Vanuatu IC.Cross-Currency Swaps: USD/VUV and USD/EUR cross-currency interest-rate and principal swaps under an ISDA master agreement for long-dated content-licence asset positions and to convert floating USD gatekeeper revenue into fixed-rate expense obligations.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for direct fintech EMI onboarding in Vanuatu. The jurisdiction has no electronic money institution licensing regime, no fintech passporting framework, and the Currency Exchange Act [Cap 137] restricts unlicensed money-exchange activity. Major global EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) do not support Vanuatu-incorporated companies as primary onboarding clients, and Stripe does not list Vanuatu as a supported settlement country. A legally viable structure is to use a licensed foreign merchant-of-record or a Hong Kong/Singapore/Delaware subsidiary of the Vanuatu IC for Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe settlement, with subsequent intercompany licence-fee remittance to the IC. The non-resident IC may lawfully hold accounts with reputable foreign EMIs only for collection purposes, provided the EMI holds a valid money-services licence in its host jurisdiction and does not solicit business from Vanuatu residents.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax on any company, including International Companies (ICs), under the Income Tax Act [Cap 181]; ICs enjoy an absolute statutory exemption confirmed by the International Companies Act [Cap 222].

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no dividend withholding tax, no branch profit remittance tax, and no exit capital gains tax on distributions from an International Company, regardless of the residence of the recipient.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and annual insurance fee are due on the anniversary of incorporation of the IC. A grace period of 60 days applies; failure to file and pay within 60 days triggers a late penalty of 20% of the annual fee; failure within 90 days leads to automatic strike-off from the VFSC register.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly returns are due on or before the 15th day of the month following the end of the taxable period; quarterly returns are due on or before the 15th day of the month following the end of the quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — Vanuatu has not enacted transfer pricing legislation, and International Companies are exempt from all direct taxes; therefore no Local File or Master File thresholds exist. The statutory threshold under domestic law is nil (0 VUV). Nevertheless, group documentation should still be maintained to protect against foreign anti-avoidance rules (CFC, PFIC, double dip) applicable in the shareholders' home countries.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not apply modern Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the style of the UAE or BVI. The International Companies Act mandates passive non-domestic status rather than an economic substance test: the IC must not carry on business with Vanuatu residents, must not own land in Vanuatu, and must maintain its registered office and agent in Port Vila. To prevent the IC from falling within the EU/OECD list of non-cooperative jurisdictions, the Vanuatu government and VFSC expect ICs to demonstrate genuine commercial purpose: board resolutions, director appointments, record keeping, and an active digital operation. For the vertical micro-drama business, the IC should hold the global content licence rights and streaming agreements, maintain written records of subscription pricing, and show that Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe payment flows correspond to the group's operating reality.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company Abuse / False Exemption Declaration

Statutory sanction: The IC is liable to forfeit its exempt status and be struck off the VFSC register; all assets may be forfeited to the Crown of Vanuatu. A false declaration in the annual return carries a fine of up to VT 500,000 plus continuing daily penalties of VT 10,000; the IC may also retroactively become liable to Vanuatu tax, including VAT at 12.5% and any applicable customs duties, with penalties of up to double the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly submit a false declaration are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of any distributions made while the IC was not validly in existence; they may be disqualified from acting as directors in Vanuatu for up to 5 years and are subject to criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to 2 years.
Tax Evasion in Foreign Markets via Vanuatu Exemption

Statutory sanction: While Vanuatu imposes no domestic sanction for foreign tax avoidance, Vanuatu is an AEOI signatory and will exchange beneficial ownership and financial account information on request; the IC can be struck off if the foreign regulatory investigations reveal that the Vanuatu entity was used as a sham. VFSC regulatory action may impose monetary fines up to VT 1,000,000 for breaching the International Companies Act. Practically, the foreign jurisdiction will assess the true taxpayer with back taxes, interest, and penalties, plus potential criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Foreign directors are exposed to personal criminal indictment in the shareholder's home jurisdiction, including travel bans, extradition requests, and imprisonment; Vanuatu courts will assist civil recovery where the veil is pierced because the IC is a mere instrumentality used to commit fraud.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Terrorist Financing Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Vanuatu Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act (as amended), a corporate entity that commits an AML offence is liable to a fine of up to VT 30,000,000; freezing orders, forfeiture of all app revenue, and dissolution of the IC may follow. The licensed registered agent and any Vanuatu payment intermediary will be independently penalised for failures in suspicious transaction reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who direct or profit from AML activity face personal fines of up to VT 300,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years; the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit may issue travel bans and asset restraint orders; civil forfeiture is recoverable against the individual's worldwide assets.
Concealment of Beneficial Ownership / Registered Agent Misconduct

Statutory sanction: The registered agent is required to terminate its relationship with the IC and make a suspicious activity report to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit; the IC is subject to a penalty of VT 500,000 and can be compulsorily struck off. The agent may also resign, which immediately exposes the IC to deregistration if no substitute agent is appointed within 30 days.

Director personal liability: Each director who participated in the concealment is personally liable to civil restitution and to disqualification; repeat violations are treated as criminal fraud, attracting imprisonment up to 2 years and mandatory restitution of all funds distributed to shareholders during the concealment period.
False Financial Records / Insolvent Distributions (Wrongful Trading)

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu courts apply common law principles of fraudulent and wrongful trading. The court may order the repayment of all unlawful dividends plus interest, make a declaration that the directors are personally liable for the company's debts, and wind up the IC. The VFSC may fine the company up to VT 1,000,000 for failure to keep proper accounting records.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil liability for the full amount of unlawful distributions, a compensation order in favour of creditors, travel prohibition pending the hearing, and, where dishonesty is proven, criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 3 years and a personal fine up to VT 500,000.
