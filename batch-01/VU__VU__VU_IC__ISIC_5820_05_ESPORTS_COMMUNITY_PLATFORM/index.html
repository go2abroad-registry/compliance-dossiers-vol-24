Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Customs and Inland Revenue (DCIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Vanuatu; portal: https://customs.gov.vu

E-government filing portal

As a tax-exempt International Company (IC), the VU_IC is not required to obtain a TIN for income tax purposes. If it voluntarily registers for VAT or becomes liable due to local taxable activity, the entity must submit Form VAT-01 to the Commissioner of Customs and Inland Revenue, accompanied by the VFSC certificate of incorporation, IC particulars, local registered agent confirmation, director identification, and estimated annual taxable turnover. DCIR issues a VAT registration number within approximately ten working days; for customs purposes, the VFSC company number serves as the de facto reference identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box, hybrid mismatch, or corporate tax election regime applies; the International Companies Act automatically grants full income tax exemption. The IC may elect voluntary VAT registration under the VAT Act [Cap 156] s.8, but for an export-only digital eSports platform such registration is generally unnecessary because export supplies are zero-rated and create no input VAT recovery need unless domestic taxable purchases occur.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated where they are physically exported from Vanuatu by the supplier or on its behalf within 28 days of supply and the supplier retains sufficient export evidence. Services are zero-rated where they are supplied to a non-resident person located outside Vanuatu and are not used or consumed inside Vanuatu; international transport services are also zero-rated. The eSports tournament and matchmaking services supplied to foreign users qualify as offshore services, provided the platform can demonstrate that customers are non-resident and the service is delivered from Vanuatu to servers or persons outside Vanuatu.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA Entry accepted by Vanuatu Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill covering the exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit evidence proving export proceeds were received in Vanuatu
  • VAT invoice issued for the export supply
  • Commercial contract and payment settlement records for offshore service exports

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing is mandated. VAT invoices are issued in paper or PDF format and must state supplier name, address and VAT number; invoice number and date; customer particulars; taxable value; VAT amount; and a clear description of supplies. Customs export declarations are processed through the ASYCUDA World system at the border.

DCIR may conduct export verifications and VAT audits for up to four years from the relevant period. Where zero-rating is claimed without adequate export documentation or funds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Vanuatu and assessed to VAT at 12.5%. Penalties for false zero-rating claims include a penalty of 100% of the tax evaded, repayment of any refund with interest, and criminal prosecution attracting fines of up to VUV 2,000,000 and imprisonment of up to seven years under the VAT Act and Criminal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu operates a liberalised convertible-currency regime. Current account transactions in Vatu (VUV) clear through authorised dealer banks without prior RBV approval; import/export settlements, service fees, and profit remittances are permitted freely if supported by commercial invoices or audited financial evidence. Capital account transactions by residents require authorised-dealer or RBV consent above prescribed de facto thresholds, while non-resident inward investment into International Companies is unrestricted. The VU_IC (tax-exempt International Company) is structurally excluded from the Exchange Control Act for offshore activities, allowing unrestricted holding of USD, EUR, or other foreign currency and free dividend distribution. Profit remittance barriers are minimal: there is no dividend withholding tax in the IC tax-exempt zone, but the paying bank requires a current certificate of incorporation, paid annual VFSC fees, and a registered-agent compliance letter confirming non-resident offshore status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Vanuatu-resident exporters of goods or services, the Exchange Control Regulations 1982 require export proceeds to be repatriated to an authorised dealer within 6 months of shipment and, upon receipt, surrendered or sold to the authorised dealer within 30 days; the standard mandatory conversion ratio is 100% into VUV unless the RBV has granted a foreign-currency retention account exemption. The VU_IC is treated as a non-resident entity for exchange control purposes when it complies with the International Companies Act [Cap 223] restrictions (no local business activity with Vanuatu residents except licensed professionals), and esports-platform revenues derived from global tournament entry fees, subscriptions, and digital licensing are therefore eligible for retention in USD/EUR operational accounts without VUV surrender. This exemption must be evidenced by a registered-agent certificate of non-resident status and maintained by ensuring all commercial counterparties are offshore.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap 282], exercising delegated exchange control powers under the Exchange Control Act [Cap 63] and the Exchange Control Regulations 1982.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for foreign-currency treasury, Low for VUV-denominated settlement. Vanuatu has no domestic e-money/EMI licensing regime, and offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not regulated by the RBV or VFSC; they may not clear VUV payments or provide Vanuatu local settlement. They are nonetheless legally useable as offshore treasury sub-accounts for receiving aggregated Stripe/PayPal settlements (USD/EUR/GBP/AUD) and executing SEPA/ACH/MT103-equivalent payouts, subject to proof of corporate substance, source of funds, and the EMI's acceptance of Vanuatu ICs. Binding constraints: (i) any disbursement in VUV to Vanuatu residents or suppliers must be routed through an RBV-licensed authorised dealer; (ii) onshore transfer of proceeds must be accompanied by the underlying commercial contract; (iii) e-money balances are unsecured claims on the EMI, not bank deposits; and (iv) exclusive reliance on an EMI without a local Vanuatu bank relationship will be treated as a substance deficiency during VFSC annual renewal or director due diligence.

All initial and subsequent equity injections must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank account to the VU_IC's capital escrow or operational account at an authorised dealer in Vanuatu. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None. Vanuatu has no corporate income tax, no transfer pricing statute, and no local file or master file threshold for International Companies.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not apply Economic Substance Regulations to an ordinary International Company conducting an offshore esports community platform. However, the company must maintain credible operational substance if it seeks to rely on tax-exempt offshore status in foreign jurisdictions, and the registered agent must ensure the company does not engage in licensed insurance, banking, or trustee business without VFSC authorization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion in Source Jurisdiction

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authority reassessment of the attributable profits; penalties of 25%–100% of the underpaid tax plus non-deductible enforcement interest; criminal tax fraud fines where non-disclosure is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid foreign tax; personal asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment may result from deliberate cross-border tax evasion or fraudulent trading.
Shell Company Misuse and Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Vanuatu International Companies Act, the VFSC may order production of books and records and dissolve the company for non-compliance. Internationally, tax authorities may disregard the entity, deny treaty benefits, and apply anti-avoidance sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for fraudulent or sham trading, including mandatory contribution to outstanding liabilities; in foreign civil/criminal proceedings, directors may face restitution orders, travel bans, and custodial sentences.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act No. 13 of 2018, fines of up to VUV 10 million per offence and imprisonment of up to 10 years for serious violations; the VFSC may also strike off the International Company.

Director personal liability: Each director and compliance officer is individually liable; assets may be confiscated by court order, civil forfeiture proceedings may be brought, and travel bans may be imposed during investigation.
Payment Processor Fraud and Unlicensed Money Transmission

Statutory sanction: Permanent account termination, card-scheme fines, and in the United States or EU, unlicensed money services business exposure with daily civil penalties and consumer restitution orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly make false statements to payment processors may face wire fraud statutes, private creditor suits, asset seizure, and extradition in serious cross-border fraud cases.
