Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế, GDT) under the Ministry of Finance of the Socialist Republic of Viet Nam; electronic administration is centralized on the eTax portal at https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn and the national e-invoice portal at https://hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn; customs-side VAT and export declarations are administered by the General Department of Viet Nam Customs under the Ministry of Finance via the VNACCS/VCIS e-clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - file an Investment Registration Certificate application with the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) or the Export Processing and Industrial Zone Authority; no statutory minimum capital applies to the LLC, but the project and factoring/credit-extension authority must be expressly included in the investment objective. Step 2 - file enterprise registration with the same DPI under the Enterprise Law 2020; upon issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate, the National Enterprise Registration Database automatically allocates the permanent 13-digit Tax Identification Number (MST), so no separate GDT application is required at incorporation. Step 3 - complete tax registration activation at the sub-department of taxation by filing the GDT e-form 01-DK-TCT on eTax, uploading the IRC, ERC, and legal representative identification; the tax office activates the MST for VAT, CIT, license fee (LPT) and PIT-withholding accounts. Step 4 - register the e-invoice template and bank account number(s) with the GDT, obtain a digital signature certificate from an accredited CA for e-invoicing, and register optional customs-clearance broker mandates. A foreign contractor carrying on business in Viet Nam without a PE uses a separate registration code under the FCT regime rather than an MST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a foreign-invested LLC is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to CIT at 20% on worldwide net business income computed under the Enterprise Law and CIT Law 2018-recodified; there is no pass-through of income or losses to foreign shareholders; dividends and interest remittances to foreign shareholders are additionally subject to a 5% tax on dividend distributions (offset mechanism for dividends paid from after-tax profit) and other WHT only on cross-border payments. Territorial scope: income arising in Viet Nam is taxable; foreign-source income is taxable in full with a foreign tax credit capped at the Vietnamese CIT amount on that income. Losses may be carried forward up to 5 years. A factoring entity within the credit-institution scope remains a CIT taxpayer at 20%.

Available election forms

Viet Nam does not have a check-the-box classification regime; entity classification is hardwired under the Law on Enterprises. The principal elective mechanisms available are: (i) filing an application with the DPI for CIT incentive eligibility (high-technology, supporting industry, large-scale industrial projects, socialized sectors, or zone-based incentives) that triggers a preferential 10% or 15% rate, tax holidays of up to 4 years and 50% rate reductions of up to 9 years; (ii) the irrevocable election, upon satisfying accounting and revenue tests, between the VAT credit method and the direct method; (iii) for inbound cross-border service suppliers without a PE, the deemed Foreign Contractor Tax method (VAT deemed and CIT deemed assessed on gross receipts) versus a tax-permanent-establishment computation; (iv) requesting an advance tax ruling from the GDT under Article 41 of the Law on Tax Administration No. 38/2019/QH14 for novel factoring structures; (v) applying the qualified domestic minimum top-up tax at 15% for constituent entities of MNEs in scope of Resolution 107/2023/QH15; (vi) no hybrid or check-the-box elections exist, and anti-hybrid/mismatch rules are enforced under general anti-avoidance, CFC rules and BEPS-aligned transfer-pricing decrees (Decree 132/2020/ND-CP).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 1,000,000,000 (one billion Vietnamese Dong) of annual revenue from VAT-liable goods and services in the preceding twelve months is the mandatory full VAT registration threshold (Article 5 Decree 209/2013/ND-CP; Article 1 Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC). Businesses below the threshold may voluntarily register; a taxpayer whose activity consists exclusively of VAT-exempt revenue (e.g., pure factoring credit services by a licensed non-bank credit institution) is not required to register unless it also carries on VAT-liable activities. Foreign contractors in Vietnam are registered for deemed VAT assessment regardless of revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate is available only where the goods are physically exported from Viet Nam under a customs export declaration and the payment is remitted in a freely convertible currency (or in VND accepted for cross-border trade), and where the sale is recorded under an export contract and commercial invoice. For export services (including revenue from the target trade-finance/factoring scope), 0% VAT applies where the service is supplied to a foreign organization or individual with no establishment in Viet Nam and the service is consumed entirely outside Vietnamese territory; commission and factoring fee income earned by a Vietnamese LLC from a non-resident bank or factor to finance an overseas seller-buyer chain may qualify as export services if the beneficial consumption test is met, while pure credit extension (interest and discounts) remains VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated. The zero-rating claim requires a complete substantiation file evidencing the offshore character of the service and the non-resident status of the counterparty. Input VAT on costs attributable to zero-rated exports is fully creditable and, if not offset, is refundable in cash under Articles 71-72 of the Law on Tax Administration No. 38/2019/QH14 and Circular 80/2021/TT-BTC, subject to the statutory refund pre-audit for newly established exporters in their first 12 months.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Tờ khai Hải quan) conclusively cleared by the General Department of Viet Nam Customs (including electronic clearance message from VNACCS)
  • Signed export contract or Sales Agreement evidencing Incoterms, parties, description, quantity and price
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the exporting LLC
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill showing the discharge port and consignee outside Viet Nam
  • Bank payment dossier: SWIFT MT103, inward foreign-currency remittance certificate, or a telegraphic-transfer credit advice from the VN servicing bank proving funds inbound within the statutory deadline
  • Certificate of Origin (C/O) where preferential origin is claimed or customs requires it
  • For export services: offshore consumption dossier including the foreign customer’s certificate of tax residence or registry extract, the service delivery report, underlying trade documents in a reverse-factoring chain, and evidence that the service benefit was realized outside Viet Nam

Mandatory e-invoicing for all enterprises effective 1 July 2022 (Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC). Every VAT invoice must be generated through the GDT-approved e-invoice format, digitally signed with a business digital certificate, and transmitted in near-real-time to the GDT national e-invoice portal (hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn). The tax authority validates the file and, for the class of taxpayers subject to the authentication-code regime, returns a 12-digit digital authentication code affixed to the invoice as a condition of lawfulness; taxpayers meeting transparency conditions may use the self-authentication method. There is no physical fiscal stamping; the GDT publishes structured JSON/XML API specifications for software suppliers, and import-VAT clearance runs through the VNACCS customs platform. Non-transmission of invoice data to the GDT within the statutory deadline triggers invoice-use suspension and administrative fines under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP (up to VND 20 million for late transmission; up to VND 40 million for illegal invoices). Cash-sale outlets must issue e-invoices per invoice at the point of sale.

The GDT performs desk and field audits that reconcile e-invoice data, customs declarations, bank remittances and CIT/VAT returns. Any refund claimed on fraudulent or fabricated exports is clawed back for the entire refunded amount, plus late payment interest at 0.03% per day from the date the refund was credited, plus a penalty of up to 100% of the understated or evaded tax amount (tiers 20%, 30%, 40%, 60% and 100% set out in Article 16 of Decree 125/2020/ND-CP). Where a taxpayer knowingly issues or uses illegal e-invoices to substantiate zero-rating, the illegally issued invoice fine can reach VND 40,000,000, the invoice use is suspended for 1-3 months, and the VAT refund claim is barred for up to 12 months. Repeated or large-scale schemes transit into criminal liability under the Penal Code 2015/2017: Article 200 (tax evasion) permits imprisonment of up to 10 years (severe tier) and disqualification or suspension of the LLC's tax-relevant activities; Article 188 (smuggling) and Article 221 (false accounting) carry imprisonment of up to 15 years and confiscation of assets. Corporate fines and suspension of tax registration numbers are imposed in addition to personal criminal liability. Directors, CFOs and compliance officers of the Vietnamese LLC may personally be held criminally liable; mitigating evidence includes voluntary self-declaration under Article 148 of the Law on Tax Administration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime. Current-account transactions (trade in goods, services, interest, fees) are generally convertible, subject to documentary review by an authorized bank. Capital-account transactions remain restricted and require registration or prior approval: direct share capital contributions, foreign loans, and security issuance must be registered with the SBV or an authorized bank as a Capital Account (Form 03/NHNN for loans). Profit remittance is permitted under current-account treatment: a foreign-invested enterprise may remit annual profits abroad after the audited financial statements are approved, corporate income tax is fully settled, all statutory reserve requirements are satisfied, and a profit-distribution resolution is passed by the members' meeting; remittance is made through an authorized bank using the 'profit remittance abroad' purpose code. Dividends and interest on shareholder loans are subject to tax-finalization checks and bank-level AML review. Legal capital must be fully contributed within 90 days (modified by IRC timeline) and certified by the bank before credit facilities are fully utilized. The Vietnamese dong (VND) is not fully convertible for speculative capital purposes; NDF trading domestically is not permitted for residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SBV regulations (principally Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN and the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange), exporters must repatriate export proceeds into Vietnam within 90 days from the date of export (extendable with SBV approval in case of force majeure or letters of credit tenor). Unless the exporter is an Export Processing Enterprise (EPE) or has obtained an SBV retention approval, export proceeds are subject to mandatory sale to an authorized commercial bank: at present, export receipts must be converted into VND or held under an approved foreign-currency account with a fixed retention period (typically up to 180 days extendable). The 30% mandatory conversion ratio has been effectively suspended; however, authorized banks are instructed to ensure exporters sell their receipts to meet VND obligations and to comply with each bank's internal surrender/conversion policy. For a factoring entity, the underlying exporter's repatriation and conversion obligation remains a compliance condition precedent: the factor must obtain evidence of the exporter's FX surrender/conversion certificate and assign the export proceeds to the factoring company via an irrevocable assignment notice accepted by the importer. The 90-day timeline runs from the customs export clearance date unless the trade agreement has a longer credit period approved by the bank.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam); the Foreign Exchange Administration Department under the SBV exercises day-to-day FX control, with the Ministry of Planning and Investment (MPI) and the General Department of Vietnam Customs (GDVC) coordinating on investment capital and export-import trade data.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Rapyd, Currencycloud, Revolut Business, MultiPass) cannot act as the primary account bank for a Vietnamese entity because they lack SBBank/SBV payment-service licenses in Vietnam. An offshore EMI may be used by the offshore parent or foreign factoring counterparty as an upstream collection/payment vehicle, but it cannot lawfully open a VND/USD account for the local LLC in the LLC's own name. Cross-border funding must ultimately clear through a licensed Vietnamese commercial bank with an SBV-approved capital account. Shareholder capital contributions must be remitted via international wire (SWIFT MT103) from the investor's own bank account to the LLC's Capital Contribution Account at a licensed Vietnamese bank, with the remittance purpose code (e.g., 'Investment capital — direct equity contribution') and the LLC's IRC number. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, mandatory Local File/Master File documentation is required where annual related-party transaction turnover exceeds VND 30 billion or annual total enterprise revenue exceeds VND 50 billion. Every taxpayer with related-party transactions must attach the related-party disclosure appendix (Appendix III) to the annual CIT finalisation return even when the documentation threshold is not met. A Country-by-Country Report is required where the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue reaches VND 18,000 billion or the foreign parent jurisdiction applies a CbCR threshold satisfied by the group.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to the UAE; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and beneficial-ownership provisions in every Vietnamese DTA operate as an economic-substance doctrine. A factoring company must maintain genuine physical presence, competent employees, substantive board oversight, capital at risk, arm's-length decision-making, and a valid State Bank of Vietnam licence if engaged in receivables factoring. A shell company lacking business substance will be denied treaty benefits and exposed to domestic CIT recharacterisation, penalties and potential criminal tax charges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False CIT Declaration

Statutory sanction: Penal Code Article 200: tax evasion from VND 100 million or more is punishable by a fine of VND 100 million to VND 4.5 billion and/or imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years. Administrative consequences include full collection of evaded tax, late-payment interest at 0.03% per day and a 20% deficiency penalty; serious cases can also carry supplementary fines and prohibition from holding management positions.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and chief accountants who sign false returns face criminal prosecution, travel bans, asset freezes, civil restitution and disqualification from corporate management upon conviction.
Unlicensed Factoring / Unauthorised Credit Activity

Statutory sanction: Unauthorised banking and credit activity may be administratively fined up to VND 3 billion per violation under Decree 88/2019/ND-CP; illegal banking operations are criminally sanctionable under Penal Code Article 203, with fines up to VND 3 billion and imprisonment up to 5 years. Underlying factoring agreements may be declared void, leaving the offshore investor without enforceable security.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or approve unlicensed factoring are personally prosecuted, may be ordered to disgorge unlawful fees, face travel bans during investigation, and are barred from future financial-sector management.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Vietnam tax authorities may re-assess intercompany transactions at arm's length, impose additional CIT, apply a 20% penalty on the under-declared tax and charge 0.03% daily late-payment interest. Non-production of TP documentation or the related-party appendix leads to administrative fines; fraudulent TP manipulation can be escalated to criminal tax-evasion proceedings under Article 200.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute intercompany agreements and TP documentation knowable as sham face personal tax liability to the extent of the shortfall, civil recovery, travel restrictions and imprisonment if criminal intent is established.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Compliance Failure in Factoring

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanctions under Vietnam's Law on AML (2023) for due-diligence or reporting breaches reach hundreds of millions of VND per violation; criminal money laundering under Penal Code Article 324 carries imprisonment up to 10 years, pecuniary fines, confiscation of laundered assets and revocation of operating licences.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and directors who knowingly permit money movement through factoring structures are personally criminally liable, subject to travel bans, civil restitution and a permanent prohibition from directing financial institutions.
Treaty Shopping / Shell Company Abuse Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Vietnam will deny DTA benefits and apply domestic statutory rates: 5% FCT on interest and 15% aggregate FCT on royalties; the adjustment attracts a 20% deficiency penalty and 0.03% daily interest. False document submission constitutes tax fraud and may be prosecuted; the foreign shell can also be reported to the treaty partner's tax authorities for bilateral administrative assistance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure and sign sham shell arrangements are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties, face travel bans during tax collection, and may be prosecuted for tax evasion where economic substance is falsely certified.
