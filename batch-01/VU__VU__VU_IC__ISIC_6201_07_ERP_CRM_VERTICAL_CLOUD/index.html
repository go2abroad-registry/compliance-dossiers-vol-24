Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Authority (VCIRA), Port Vila, Republic of Vanuatu; created by the Tax and Customs (Administration) Reforms Act 2019 as a merger of the Department of Customs and the former Inland Revenue Department. Principal portal: www.vcira.vu (VCIRA Online Services) for VAT registration, VAT return submission, and customs entries; customs declarations are processed through the ASYCUDA World system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A foreign beneficial owner engages a licensed registered agent in Vanuatu to incorporate an International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222]. Step 2: The agent files the constitution, memorandum and articles, consent of first director, and registered office address with the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC); the Certificate of Incorporation and unique IC registration number are usually issued within 1-2 business days. Step 3: Because an IC is statutorily exempt from all national direct and indirect taxes on international business and is prohibited from carrying on domestic business with Vanuatu residents, no separate TIN or VAT ID is automatically created; the VFSC registration number serves as the corporate identity for all tax-exempt offshore activities. Step 4: If domestic VAT-taxable activity is ever undertaken, compulsory VAT registration applies within 21 days after the turnover threshold is exceeded, using VAT Form 100 submitted to VCIRA; VCIRA then issues a VAT registration number and certificate. Step 5: The IC must maintain a licensed registered agent in Vanuatu, file an annual return with the VFSC, and, if foreign-owned, hold a Foreign Investment Registration approval issued by the Vanuatu Investment Promotion Authority.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: an International Company is wholly exempt from all Vanuatu national taxes on foreign-source business - no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no dividend, interest, royalty, or management-fee withholding tax, no inheritance or gift tax, and no stamp duty on offshore instruments. The exemption is conditioned on the IC carrying on business exclusively with non-residents and not holding an interest in land or engaging in domestic trade in Vanuatu. Pass-through concepts and entity-level income tax are inapplicable because Vanuatu imposes no income tax at either the entity or shareholder level; the territorial scope of the exemption is limited to offshore-source activities.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Vanuatu because the jurisdiction has no income tax and therefore generates no deduction, credit, or mismatch computations. Available elections under the International Companies Act: (1) conversion of an IC into a local 'ordinary company' under the Companies Act [Cap. 191] by special resolution and VFSC consent, which prospectively terminates the tax-exempt status and exposes the company to VAT, customs duty, and other obligations on local supplies; (2) an election in the constitutional documents to restrict the corporate objects to 'international business', which affirmatively preserves the exemption; and (3) optional voluntary VAT registration by an exporter to claim input VAT credits when below the compulsory threshold. None of these elections interact with hybrid mismatch rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 4,000,000 (Vanuatu Vatu, approximately USD 33,000): compulsory registration for any person carrying on a taxable activity in Vanuatu where the value of taxable supplies, including zero-rated supplies, reaches or exceeds VT 4,000,000 in any 12-month period; the VAT registration application (Form 100) must be submitted to VCIRA within 21 days of crossing the threshold. The International Company conducting pure offshore business is outside the domestic VAT net and is not required to register; voluntary registration is available to claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supplier may claim the 0% VAT rate where the supply qualifies as an export of goods (removed from Vanuatu within 28 days of the date of invoice) or a supply of services to a non-resident customer for use and enjoyment wholly outside Vanuatu, per Section 6 and the Second Schedule of the Value Added Tax Act. For the vertical ERP/CRM cloud (SaaS) business, zero-rating is available when: (a) the customer is a non-resident; (b) the software is delivered electronically and accessed from servers or devices outside Vanuatu; (c) no Vanuatu-resident user obtains economic enjoyment of the service; and (d) payment is received in foreign currency through a licensed bank. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export supplies is fully creditable, and excess credits are refundable through the periodic VAT return (Form 200). An International Company of Vanuatu is entirely outside the VAT charge on international business and may render services without charging Vanuatu VAT at all.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED) validated in ASYCUDA World for physical goods or software media
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (or electronic delivery acknowledgement for digital services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed Vanuatu bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated VAT invoice issued to the non-resident buyer
  • Signed software license, SaaS subscription, or master services agreement evidencing offshore use, service delivery, and payment terms
  • Evidence of server location and offshore service delivery for cloud-based supplies

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing regime is currently in force in Vanuatu. VAT-registered suppliers must issue a tax invoice containing the statutory particulars: supplier name and VAT registration number, recipient name, invoice date, description of the supply, VAT-exclusive price, VAT amount, and applicable rate. VAT returns are filed electronically through the VCIRA Online Services portal, and customs entries are lodged via ASYCUDA World. Fiscal digitalisation, including future e-invoicing standards for services suppliers, is under government review but has not been legislated for ERP/CRM cloud service providers.

VCIRA conducts documentary and risk-based audits of zero-rated export claims and may recharacterize a supply as taxable in Vanuatu where contract performance, server usage, or customer enjoyment indicates local economic use. A false or unsupported zero-rating claim triggers a retrospective VAT assessment at 15% plus statutory late-payment interest, a 25% uplift for unreported tax understatement, and an accuracy or civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded for deliberate conduct; knowingly issuing a false export invoice is a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment under the Value Added Tax Act. Refunded input VAT is clawed back with interest upon audit reversal. In addition, export proceeds must be repatriated through a licensed Vanuatu bank within 180 days of shipment or invoice date; failure to repatriate may result in disallowance of the zero-rating, exchange-control review, and anti-money-laundering referral.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains a broadly liberal exchange control regime. The Vatu (VUV) is fully convertible for current account transactions and, for registered International Companies, capital account transactions are effectively unrestricted. An International Company is classified as a non-resident entity; it therefore may hold VUV or any foreign currency without Central Bank approval. Profit, capital and liquidation remittances to non-resident shareholders are freely payable, are free of withholding tax, and are not subject to dividend-blocking regulations, provided that all underlying revenue arises from non-resident business. Only residents or local trading companies are required to seek formal exchange control approval for certain foreign borrowings and large remissions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or statutory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds earned by an International Company. Vanuatu does not impose repatriation deadlines or percentage-based conversion requirements on ICs operating with non-resident status. If the principal software sales are executed through a Vanuatu resident operating entity, however, the resident exporter must follow the standard RBV dealer rules and repatriate proceeds through a licensed commercial bank within the applicable periods for resident exporters.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) acting pursuant to the Exchange Control Act [Cap 83] and operating through the Ministry of Finance and the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission for International Company remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold; Vanuatu has no transfer pricing rules and no Local File/Master File regime. For substance-risk management, an International Company should maintain internal records demonstrating that the ERP/CRM cloud business is managed and directed from a jurisdiction where it has real operational substance.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not administer Economic Substance Regulations similar to BVI/Cayman; no statutory economic substance test under the International Companies Act. However, entities that are managed from or that transact with entities in other jurisdictions (e.g., EU member states, OECD members) may face foreign substance requirements under the foreign entity's CFC/GILTI/controlled-foreign-company rules. To support a non-resident tax treatment, the IC must preserve board minutes, contracts, bank records, and service agreements demonstrating that core revenue-generating activities occur outside Vanuatu.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Vanuatu law, a company that is struck off for default can be dissolved and its assets vested in the Registrar or Crown (as applicable); knowingly providing false declarations to the VFSC attracts fines up to VT 100,000 or imprisonment. More critically, foreign tax authorities (IRS CFC, UK CFC, EU exit taxation, BEPS Action 5) may recharacterize the IC as a purely artificial offshore arrangement, imposing punitive anti-avoidance taxes and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations or allow the company to be used for tax evasion may be personally liable for fraudulent trading, disqualified from managing companies, prohibited from re-entering Vanuatu, and subject to criminal prosecution in the home jurisdiction for perjury and tax evasion.
Failure to File Annual Return / Administrative Default

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty not exceeding VT 100,000; automatic strike-off and dissolution after continued default, with property of the company forfeited to the Crown.

Director personal liability: Directors may be named in the strike-off notice, personally responsible for penalty arrears, banned from acting as directors in Vanuatu for up to five years, and if assets are concealed, subject to criminal confiscation orders.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Crimes

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act imposes fines up to VT 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 15 years for non-compliance by financial institutions; beneficial-ownership reporting to the VFSC can be enforced and false filings penalized. International sanctions (FATF, UN, OFAC) may freeze assets and blacklist the corporate group.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally liable for criminal penalties, including imprisonment; civil forfeiture of funds; travel bans from Vanuatu; and extradition risks for money laundering predicates.
Misrepresentation / False Tax Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: The tax exemption is void; the company becomes subject to any Vanuatu business license fees, import duties, VAT (12.5% where applicable), and may lose its ICC status. For the exporter of services, foreign jurisdictions may impose withholding tax collections and penalty tax of 50% or more on gross revenue.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly make false tax-exemption claims are liable for misrepresentation, fraudulent trading, and civil restitution to affected customers/investors; they may be criminally prosecuted and imprisoned for up to 7 years; regulatory travel bans may be issued by the VFSC and foreign enforcement agencies.
