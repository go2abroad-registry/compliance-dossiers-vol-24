Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Department of Finance and Treasury / Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC); the tax administration portal is operated by the Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC) under the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management. Official registration and filing portal: https://www.vrctax.gov.vu/ and corporate registry portal: https://www.vfsc.vu/

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing in Vanuatu must first incorporate or register as an International Company (IC) through the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) and obtain a Certificate of Incorporation. Following incorporation, the IC must apply for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) via the Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC) online portal or in-person at the VRC office, submitting the Certificate of Incorporation, certified passport copies of directors/shareholders, proof of registered agent appointment in Vanuatu, a registered office address, and the completed TIN application form. Approval of the TIN is generally issued within 3 to 5 business days. Where the IC engages in taxable supplies under the Value Added Tax (VAT) regime, a separate VAT registration number must be obtained under the VAT Act [Cap. 363] before making taxable supplies, with the VAT registration threshold and application procedures administered by the VRC. Foreign entities not maintaining a physical presence may still obtain a TIN solely for income tax administration purposes; however, no domestic income tax is imposed on qualifying International Companies. The statutory fee schedule and annual renewal requirements are published by VFSC and VRC, and the IC must complete an annual license renewal with VFSC regardless of tax-exempt status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] and the VAT Act, an International Company may elect to be treated as tax-exempt only if it does not conduct domestic trading activity in Vanuatu. No check-the-box election exists because Vanuatu does not impose income tax on International Companies; however, an IC may voluntarily register for VAT under Section 7 of the VAT Act if the VRC determines that the entity is carrying on a taxable activity in Vanuatu. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available in Vanuatu. A hybrid entity election may arise under foreign jurisdictions' rules only where Vanuatu law classifies the IC as opaque for foreign tax purposes; Vanuatu generally issues a Certificate of Tax Residence or Certificate of Non-Residence only upon application, and the fiscal status of an IC is determined by its place of incorporation and compliance with the International Companies Act. For VAT purposes, a person may elect to register for VAT where turnover is below the threshold solely at the discretion of the Commissioner of Revenue, and such election is binding for at least 24 months.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 (Vanuatu vatu) annual taxable turnover, as prescribed under the VAT Act and VRC regulations. A person is required to register for VAT if the value of taxable supplies exceeds or is expected to exceed VUV 5,000,000 in any 12-month period. Registration is mandatory once the threshold is met; voluntary registration is available below the threshold upon application and is subject to the Commissioner's discretion. International Companies engaged solely in export of goods or services may register for VAT to recover input VAT on exported supplies, but input VAT recovery is available only for taxable activities and complies with the zero-rating provisions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Section 10 of the VAT Act, a supply of goods is zero-rated where the goods are exported by the supplier from Vanuatu within a prescribed time frame (generally 28 days from the time of supply or as otherwise approved by the Commissioner) and the supplier obtains and retains evidence of export. A supply of services, including cold chain logistics and refrigerated storage services for perishable pharmaceuticals, is zero-rated when the services are supplied for consumption outside Vanuatu or are directly connected with movable goods that are exported, provided the recipient is not in Vanuatu and the services are of a type that the Commissioner determines to be exported. Refrigerated logistics services physically performed in Vanuatu are not zero-rated if the recipient of the service is a Vanuatu resident and the service is enjoyed in Vanuatu. The zero-rating of ancillary transport and handling services requires that the service is performed directly in respect of goods being exported and is separately identified on the customs declaration. Input tax on costs attributable to zero-rated exports may be recovered via a VAT refund, subject to the documentary substantiation requirements, provided the VAT is not blocked by input tax restriction rules under Section 17 of the VAT Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Asycuda format, with unique declaration number and customs officer clearance)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the exporter as the consignor and a foreign consignee outside Vanuatu
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign currency credit evidence from a bank licensed in Vanuatu showing the export proceeds received from outside Vanuatu
  • Commercial Invoice referencing the VAT invoice and zero-rated supply narrative
  • Packing List matching the export cargo quantities
  • Insurance certificate or transport document evidencing movement from Vanuatu to an overseas destination
  • VAT tax invoice marked 'Zero-rated export' and containing the supplier's VAT registration number
  • Copy of the registered agent's declaration of no domestic market supply, where applicable for an International Company

Vanuatu does not currently mandate a fully automated real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance system for all VAT taxpayers. The VRC operates a manual and semi-electronic filing system through its portal, with e-filing available for VAT returns and customs declarations using the Asycuda World system for customs import/export clearances. For taxable supplies, VAT invoices must be issued within 28 days of the supply and must contain prescribed particulars, including the word 'tax invoice', supplier's name, address and VAT registration number, recipient details, description, quantity, taxable value, VAT amount, and date. Digital stamping is not required in Vanuatu. A real-time clearance mechanism applies only to customs export and import declarations through Asycuda, with electronic filing and automated risk assessment; export declarations must be lodged through the Asycuda portal and cleared before goods are exported. API integration with VRC systems is not currently mandated but is permitted for high-volume filers upon VRC approval.

Where a VAT-registered person zero-rates an export without proper documentation or where the goods/services are not actually exported, the VRC may assess VAT at 12.5% on the full taxable value, with interest accruing at a rate published by the Commissioner. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract penalties under the VAT Act including an amount equal to twice the tax sought to be avoided, imprisonment for up to 10 years for knowingly supplying false information or for forgery, and a monetary fine not exceeding VUV 1,000,000 for each false declaration. The VRC may also invoke the Tax Administration Act provisions for failure to register, failure to preserve records, and failure to file returns, with fines ranging from VUV 5,000 to VUV 500,000 and up to 2 years' imprisonment. A refund or credit may be clawed back (with interest computed from the date of refund) where the VRC subsequently establishes that the export documentation is false, forged, or non-compliant. In addition, under the International Companies Act, an International Company that breaches its offshore-only condition may forfeit its tax-exempt status and become liable to tax, if any, under the Income Tax Act (currently nil), but would be denied the protection of the IC regime and may be struck off, with director liability arising for any unpaid VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu Vatu (VUV) trades under a trade-weighted managed float administered by the RBV. Vanuatu has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, so current-account payments (warehousing fees, refrigeration logistics invoices, insurance, freight, and operating disbursements) are freely convertible without prior approval. Capital-account transactions (share capital subscriptions, shareholder loans, loan repayments above VUV 5 million) require standard filing via Form EX1/EX2 with an authorized dealer, but no prior consent is required for a non-resident International Company. Profit remittance is unrestricted for the Tax-Exempt IC: zero withholding tax, zero capital gains tax, and no obligation to convert VUV receipts into VUV. The IC may maintain External Accounts with authorized dealers and may hold USD, AUD, NZD, or EUR balances offshore free of exchange control, provided the underlying commercial documentation substantiates the flows and no speculative FX dealing is undertaken.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender ratio or mandatory conversion timeline to VUV applies to export receipts. Resident exporters must repatriate proceeds to Vanuatu within 180 days of shipment and submit declaration forms to the RBV through an authorized dealer, but may retain proceeds in an approved foreign-currency account. A non-resident International Company (VU_IC) is exempt from surrender: receivables from cold-storage and temperature-controlled warehousing services may be credited directly to an External Account or a foreign-currency account at an authorized dealer, or to an offshore USD/AUD account, without conversion, as long as the invoices and service contracts precisely match the beneficiary name and reference the scope 'ISIC 5210 Refrigerated Logistics Warehousing'.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), operating under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap 125] and the Exchange Control Act [Cap 58] as the delegated exchange-control authority of the Minister of Finance; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) regulates the International Company under the International Companies Act [Cap 222], but holds no mandate over currency, FX, or clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for mainstream offshore EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Currencycloud, Payoneer) because Vanuatu is on the FATF grey list and the International Company structure triggers automated KYC rejection engines. Medium for specialized neo-banks and EMI issuers whose compliance teams accept grey-listed jurisdictions after enhanced due diligence, a physical-substance dossier (cold-storage lease deed, utility and payroll invoices, staff contracts), and a board resolution explaining the treasury function. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Vanuatu levies no corporate income tax at the national level; an International Company is statutorily exempt under the International Companies Act, Cap. 231.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Vanuatu imposes no dividend withholding tax. Dividends paid by an International Company to any shareholder, whether resident or non-resident, are exempt from all Vanuatu taxes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None — Vanuatu has no domestic transfer pricing legislation and prescribes no Local File or Master File thresholds in VUV. The ESR regime nevertheless requires contemporaneous substance records for any entity carrying on a relevant activity. For prudence, an IC engaged in related-party cold-chain logistics should prepare Master File documentation where group turnover exceeds EUR 50 million equivalent and a Local File with benchmarking study where annual related-party service revenue exceeds VUV 20 million (approximately USD 170,000) or intra-group cost recharges exceed VUV 10 million (approximately USD 85,000).

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The Vanuatu Economic Substance Act (Act No. 22 of 2018, as amended by the Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2019) and the Economic Substance Regulations 2019 require any Vanuatu-resident entity carrying on a relevant activity to demonstrate adequate economic presence in Vanuatu: a physical office, full-time employees proportionate to the income, local management and decision-making, sufficient local expenditure, and board meetings held in Vanuatu. A third-party cold storage business serving unrelated pharmaceutical shippers is not a listed relevant activity; however, if the IC provides warehousing or distribution services to related parties within a multinational group, the activity falls within 'distribution and service centre business' and becomes subject to the full substance test. An IC that merely holds the equity of a foreign warehouse-owning subsidiary is classified as a pure equity holding company, a relevant activity subject to a reduced substance standard satisfied through Companies Act compliance, filing of accounts, and BO reporting.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unauthorised Domestic Business in Vanuatu / Breach of International Company Foreign-Operations Mandate

Statutory sanction: Offence against the International Companies Act: the IC is liable to a fine of up to VUV 1,000,000; VFSC may refuse renewal, apply for court-ordered winding-up, or strike the company off the register. The Business Licence fee becomes retroactively payable with a 25% penalty surcharge, and the domestic business is subject to immediate closure orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorised domestic trading are jointly and severally liable for the company's monetary penalties, may be disqualified from managing any Vanuatu entity for up to 10 years, and are subject to criminal prosecution carrying imprisonment of up to 2 years. Civil restitution to affected counterparties may be ordered by the Supreme Court of Vanuatu.
Tax Evasion / Offshore Sham Structure Sheltering Foreign Operating Income

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu has no income tax to apply, but the VFSC and Vanuatu authorities cooperate through exchange-of-information agreements. Foreign revenue authorities applying CFC and GAAR rules will reallocate the income, impose tax, interest, and penalties of up to 200% of the avoided tax, and may blacklist top-up invoices issued by the IC. Under the Proceeds of Crime Act, fraudulent evasion of a foreign tax debt can be prosecuted as an indictable offence where the unpaid foreign liability is treated as property obtained through criminal conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false invoices, back-dated contracts, or certified sham board minutes can be held personally liable for the foreign tax liabilities under piercing doctrines, subjected to travel bans by the foreign revenue authority where the debt exceeds approximately USD 50,000, and prosecuted in the foreign jurisdiction with extradition risk through Vanuatu mutual assistance channels.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance or Pure Equity Holding SPV Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act, the VFSC may impose administrative penalties of up to VUV 10,000,000 per serious breach, publish the IC's name on the non-compliance list, and apply to court for strike-off. The IC's foreign counterparties may recharacterise the entity as fiscally transparent, collapsing the intended tax benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for the VUV 10,000,000 penalty, and repeat violations trigger automatic disqualification as directors of any Vanuatu company for 5 years and imprisonment of up to 2 years on conviction. Creditors may bring civil claims against directors for losses caused by false solvency or substance declarations.
AML/CTF and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies and Trust Service Providers Act and Vanuatu's Anti-Money Laundering Regulations, the IC and its registered agent face fines of up to VUV 5,000,000 per breach, suspension or disqualification of the agent, and mandatory disclosure of suspicious transactions to the Vanuatu Financial Intelligence Unit. The IC's registration may be suspended and its bank accounts frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors who conceal beneficial ownership face personal fines of up to VUV 2,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years. The FIU, through the Public Prosecutor, may obtain civil forfeiture of warehouse assets shown to be instrumentalities of crime and may seek preventive travel bans against directors pending investigation.
Money Laundering and Sanctions Evasion Through the Cold Chain

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act (Act No. 28 of 2002, as amended), money laundering is a serious offence punishable by a fine of up to VUV 100,000,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 15 years. The court may confiscate the IC's bank balances, receivables, and the foreign warehouse assets legally owned by the IC, and may order the winding-up of the IC in the public interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners convicted of money laundering are subject to imprisonment of up to 15 years, confiscation orders against personal assets, and disqualification from holding any directorial role in Vanuatu for life. Mutual legal assistance processes permit the execution of foreign travel bans and extradition requests against the individuals involved.
