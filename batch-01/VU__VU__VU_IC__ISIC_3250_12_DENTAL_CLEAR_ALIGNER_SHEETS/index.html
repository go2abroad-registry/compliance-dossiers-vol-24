Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% VAT) applies where dental clear aligner sheets are sold to a non-resident customer, the goods are physically exported from Vanuatu within 30 days of supply, title and risk pass outside Vanuatu, and payment is received in foreign currency from non-Vanuatu sources. Offshore services qualify only if the recipient is non-resident and the service benefit is located wholly outside Vanuatu.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice (VAT export invoice)

No electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance API, or digital stamping mandate. Vanuatu operates a manual/PDF tax invoice regime; a valid VAT invoice under the Value Added Tax Act [Cap. 250] may be issued on paper or in standard PDF format without filing with VRC before issuance.

VRC audits match the Customs Export Declaration date, the Bill of Lading journey, and the Inward Remittance Certificate; any missing or inconsistent document triggers reversal of the zero-rated refund, a default assessment, and interest. Knowingly false export-refund claims expose the exporter to retrospective clawback of the refunded amount, a civil penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded, and on conviction a fine or imprisonment (Value Added Tax Act [Cap. 250], ss. 25-34). The limitation period is open-ended for fraud or wilful default. Vanuatu imposes no exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is the expedient evidentiary safe harbor for refund substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) is fully convertible for current-account transactions without balance-of-payments restrictions, and Vanuatu maintains a liberalised offshore regime: capital-account convertibility is also available to non-resident International Companies (ICs), with unrestricted profit and capital repatriation subject only to standard documentary verification of source of funds and beneficial ownership. Practical liquidity constraints exist because VUV is a low-volume offshore currency; therefore cross-border treasury operations are typically denominated in USD, AUD, NZD or EUR. Remittance hurdles are minimal but banks retain discretionary compliance screening under AML/CTF rules and correspondent-bank due-diligence policies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export proceeds in Vanuatu. Under the liberalised exchange-control framework and the International Companies Act [Cap. 222], an IC may retain and deploy 100% of foreign-currency receipts in multi-currency onshore or offshore accounts without any surrender obligation or conversion deadline. The only requirements are transaction-level record-keeping and submission of supporting commercial documents (customs entries, shipping bills, packing lists and purchase orders) to the collecting bank for verification and audit trail purposes.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 292], acting as the central bank and monetary authority; the Minister of Finance retains residual exchange-control powers, while the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) administers licensing and AML/CFT supervision under the Financial Dealers Licensing Act [Cap. 70] and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act [Cap. 282].

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, and cross-border payment aggregators) may offer convenient USD/EUR settlement rails, but they cannot be passport-licensed into Vanuatu and would require supplementary local authorisation under the Financial Dealers Licensing Act [Cap. 70] as a Category A or B financial dealer. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold expressed in Vanuatu Vatu. Vanuatu currently imposes no transfer pricing documentation rules due to the absence of corporate income tax. Nevertheless, ICs engaged in relevant activities under the Economic Substance Regulations must maintain contemporaneous records evidencing core income-generating activities, physical presence, adequate employees, and board management in Vanuatu. A de facto documentation expectation applies irrespective of turnover for entities claiming substance-based exemptions.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu's International Companies (Economic Substance) Act and supporting ESR require ICs undertaking relevant activities to satisfy real economic presence such as physical office, local employees, board meetings in Vanuatu, and decisions made in Vanuatu. The dental clear aligner sheet manufacture under ISIC 3250 may fall outside the classic pure holding or IP high-risk categories if the IC executes real operations overseas; however, any distribution, procurement, or IP-holding arrangement integrated with the IC must be assessed to avoid the shell company classification. ICs must file an annual economic substance statement with the VFSC where a relevant activity is identified.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax-Exemption Declaration

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the International Companies Act and Vanuatu criminal law; monetary penalties, deregistration, confiscation of proceeds, and nullification of the tax exemption. The entity may also be retrospectively disqualified from IC status.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution, disgorgement of profits, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraud where the corporate veil is pierced.
Shell Company / Absent Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Economic Substance Regulations; VFSC-imposed penalties, possible strike-off, and a foreign tax authority may reclassify the entity as taxable in the actual management jurisdiction with back taxes, interest, and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for outstanding penalties and foreign-imposed taxes if the IC is determined to be a newly incorporated shell; personal asset freezes and travel bans may apply.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) Failures

Statutory sanction: Breach of Vanuatu's AML/CFT legal framework and Financial Intelligence Unit directives; monetary penalties, suspension of the entity's registration, seizure of suspicious proceeds, and referral for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Individuals knowingly involved in laundering proceeds face imprisonment, fines, travel restrictions, and confiscation of personal assets. Directors cannot rely on the IC's corporate form to shield personal criminal conduct.
Failure to Maintain Beneficial Ownership Information

Statutory sanction: VFSC daily default fines, refusal to issue a Certificate of Good Standing, and administrative strike-off. Non-compliance undermines the company's ability to bank, renew licenses, and execute cross-border contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents are personally subject to fines and may be disqualified from acting as officers of any Vanuatu international company.
Prohibited Local Trading / False Local Business Declaration

Statutory sanction: Revocation of IC status, monetary penalties, loss of all statutory tax exemptions, and retrospective liability. The VFSC may order deregistration and refer criminal violations to the Public Prosecutor.

Director personal liability: Directors who order or knowingly permit prohibited local dealings are personally liable for unpaid government fees, fines, and third-party claims; the corporate veil is pierced and the directors may be imprisoned.
