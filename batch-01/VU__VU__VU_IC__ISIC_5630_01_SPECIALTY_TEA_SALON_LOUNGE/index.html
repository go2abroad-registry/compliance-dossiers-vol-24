Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Department within the Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official portal https://vurc.gov.vu; customs processing via VRC Customs ASYCUDA World single window; entity incorporation registry is the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC), with foreign investment clearance by the Vanuatu Foreign Investment Promotion Agency (VFIPA).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the International Company (IC) under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] at VFSC and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and registered agent confirmation. 2) Obtain a Foreign Investment Certificate from VFIPA if any natural or juridical foreign investor holds a beneficial interest. 3) For non-offshore domestic activity (specialty tea salon), obtain a municipal Business Licence (Port Vila or Luganville Municipality) and, if foreign owned, apply for the standard local trading conversion procedure, because a pure IC cannot trade with Vanuatu residents. 4) Apply to VRC on VAT Form REG-01 for compulsory VAT registration once taxable turnover exceeds VT 10,000,000 in any 12-month period, or voluntarily below the threshold; VRC then issues the VAT Registration Certificate and VAT registration number, which functions as the de facto national TIN. 5) Register for customs importer/exporter code under the Customs Act 2004 and open an Authorised Dealer relationship with a licensed bank for exchange control approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box, flow-through, or hybrid mismatch election exists under Vanuatu law. The IC automatically enjoys statutory tax exemption under s.104 of the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] (exempt from income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, stamp duty and estate duty) conditioned on the IC not carrying on business in Vanuatu and deriving no Vanuatu-sourced income; local trading activity (on-premise tea service) forfeits the exempt status and attracts VAT and municipal licensing obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 (ten million Vatu) of taxable turnover in any 12-month period above which VAT registration is compulsory; voluntary registration is available below the threshold; a foreign IC whose only supplies are exported goods or offshore services is generally outside the VAT charge but must still register if it makes taxable domestic supplies (e.g., salon retail sales) exceeding the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under s.11 of the VAT Act, (i) a supply of goods is zero-rated when the goods are exported by the supplier and leave Vanuatu within 30 days of supply; and (ii) a supply of services is zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident for use wholly outside Vanuatu. For the specialty tea salon and lounge, zero-rating is available for (a) loose-leaf tea, ceramics, and merchandise sold as export consignments that physically leave Vanuatu within the 30-day rule, and (b) offshore consulting, wholesaling or cultural-service fees supplied to non-resident clients for use abroad. On-premise tea service, retail takeaway consumed in Vanuatu, and catering remain taxable at 12.5%.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged electronically via VRC Customs ASYCUDA World (approved exporter code required)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the tea salon goods from Vanuatu within 30 days of supply
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the goods, quantity, and zero-rated value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice showing receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident purchaser
  • VAT zero-rating schedule and export register maintained and retained for VRC audit inspection

None. Vanuatu has no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate. Standard VAT invoice rules apply: consecutive serial number, date, supplier name and VAT number, recipient name, description of supply, tax-exclusive amount, VAT amount at 12.5%, and total. The tea salon's local POS and card rails must generate compliant VAT receipts; export transactions must be separately evidenced for zero-rating.

VRC may audit export zero-rating claims and, where the 30-day export rule, documentary bundle, or offshore-use requirement is not satisfied, assess the uncollected 12.5% VAT as output tax together with penalty tax and monthly interest (penalty tax and interest apply per the VAT Act late-payment rules). Fraudulent zero-rating claims or false declarations constitute tax evasion under the VAT Act, exposing the IC, its registered agent, and its directors to criminal fine and imprisonment (up to 2 years) and to retrospective clawback of all improperly claimed refunds plus penalties. All supporting customs, banking, and sales records must be retained for at least 7 years to withstand a VRC audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vanuatu vatu (VUV) is freely convertible for current account transactions (trade in goods and services, freight, insurance, worker remittances) and capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, loan disbursements, portfolio-related transfers), with no mandatory prior RBV approval for payments made through an RBV-authorized dealer bank. Profit and dividend remittance require standard bank AML/KYC verification, tax-clearance evidence where applicable, and a Board resolution; no separate exchange-control permit, dividend block, or surrender threshold applies. However, the International Company (Cap 222) is tax-exempt and may not conduct on-island domestic commerce, so profit remittance from a physical specialty tea salon must run through the resident operating company's accounts rather than through the IC offshore treasury layer. Market depth is thin for VUV, and commercial banks determine effective convertibility of large or exotic currency pairs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio into VUV. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in an RBV-authorized dealer bank account. For the tea-salon cross-border trade, export proceeds from high-end loose-leaf tea and cultural ceramics should be reported to the commercial bank as export proceeds for BPM6 balance-of-payments statistics; shipping documentation, commercial invoices, customs entries, and evidence of delivery must be retained to verify the underlying trade and satisfy AML source-of-funds obligations. There is no statutory timeline for conversion or repatriation beyond standard bank record-keeping rules.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), Port Vila

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. Offshore digital EMI providers, including EU/UK e-money institutions and non-regulated international payment processors, are not licensed by the RBV, do not have direct access to the Vanuatu domestic clearing system, and cannot act as the acquiring bank for local POS or on-island credit-card terminals under RBV payment-system rules. Such providers may be used only as a secondary non-VUV supplier-payment tool after the primary on-island bank relationship is established; all Vanuatu-source retail revenue must be settled through a resident licensed bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% - Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax. International Companies (ICs) registered under the International Companies Act (Cap. 222) are statutorily exempt from all taxes, duties, and statutory charges of Vanuatu, including corporate income tax, capital gains tax, and dividend tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Vanuatu does not impose withholding tax on dividends declared, paid, or distributed by an International Company. ICA s. 6(1)-(2) expressly exempts distributions, and there are no exchange controls or dividends-tax reporting obligations.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The registered agent exclusively controls the filing timeline; directors and beneficial owners must provide updated KYC, shareholding, and officer data to the agent no later than 14 days before the anniversary deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Vanuatu VUV transfer-pricing documentation threshold applies because IC income is exempt. Nevertheless, the Economic Substance Regulations and general record-keeping rules require every IC to maintain contemporaneous documentation evidencing arm's length remuneration of intra-group transactions. Recommended minimum: full Local File style documentation where annual related-party revenue exceeds VUV 100 million (approximately USD 850,000) or where the IC owns high-value intangible property or cash-treasury assets; a Master File style overview is prudent for any group with cross-border intra-group services.

Economic substance scope: Applicable - Vanuatu enacted the International Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations No. 21 of 2022 (as amended), effective 1 July 2022. Any IC carrying on a relevant activity must satisfy the management and control test in Vanuatu, maintain physical premises, employ adequate full-time staff, incur adequate local operating expenditure, and perform core income-generating activities in Vanuatu. For the Specialty Tea Salon Lounge held by the IC, two compliant structures exist: (i) the IC itself operates a genuine high-end tea salon with a Vanuatu storefront, local staff, and local POS acquiring, justifying full substance; or (ii) the IC is a pure equity-holding parent of foreign operating tea-salon entities, in which case the passive holding exemption applies with reduced substance but still requires annual ESR notification and beneficial ownership filings. A nil-activity IC may file only a notification. Asserting pure holding status while trading in tea, executing franchise agreements, or conducting treasury functions destroys the exemption and triggers strict-liability penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misrepresentation of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: VFSC strike-off and dissolution; referral to the Public Prosecutor for unlicensed local business activity under the Business License Act, carrying fines of up to VUV 100,000 and custodial sentence; offences of false declaration render the Tax Exemption Certificate void ab initio; the Penal Code imposes imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent tax-related misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors and beneficial owners who signed or procured the false compliance certificate are jointly and severally liable for unpaid penalties, civil restitution to the Revenue, and damages to the State; the Supreme Court may issue disqualification orders and departure prohibition orders (travel bans) pending settlement.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Strict-liability penalties under the Economic Substance Regulations: fines up to VUV 10 million (approximately USD 85,000) for the initial offence; continuing non-compliance accrues a daily penalty of up to VUV 250,000 per day; the VFSC may compulsorily strike off the IC and notify foreign tax authorities through TIEA channels.

Director personal liability: Directors, the registered agent, and the local compliance officer are jointly liable for fines; the Supreme Court may impose a travel ban on the directors until all ESR penalties are discharged and may disqualify them from directing any Vanuatu legal entity for up to 15 years.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines per day of default under the Companies and International Companies (Beneficial Ownership) regime; criminal prosecution for money-laundering concealment under the Proceeds of Crime Act, with penalties including imprisonment up to 5 years, forfeiture of the salon's assets, and account freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto controllers face personal criminal liability, confiscation orders, and disqualification; movement across Vanuatu ports may be restricted by court order, and extradition pursuant to mutual legal assistance treaties is available for predicate money-laundering offences.
Fraudulent Trading and Investor Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Court-ordered restitution, equitable freezing injunctions, and winding-up for just and equitable grounds; fraudulent trading triggers penal fines and imprisonment up to 7 years; section 6 exemption status may be revoked and the IC dissolved with retrospective effect.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered personally to contribute the entirety of the company's assets to creditors for fraudulent trading; civil recovery and disgorgement of hidden profits; imprisonment; international travel restraint; and surrender of passports pending final judgment.
