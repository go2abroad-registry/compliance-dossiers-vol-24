Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Business Registration Office, provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office, operating the National Business Registration Portal (dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn) under the Ministry of Planning and Investment; foreign-investment approval is equally centralized before the same DPI through the National Investment Registration System. The statutory filing fee is effectively USD 0.00 because online enterprise registration is free of charge; nominal paper-dossier notarization and gazette-publication costs are payable to third-party notary offices and the national portal operator.

Governing statute

Law on Enterprises No. 59/2020/QH14 (effective 1 January 2021), in particular Articles 26, 27, 34, 46-79 and 75-76 applicable to limited liability companies, Article 74 on legal representation, and Chapter VII on enterprise registration; Law on Investment No. 61/2020/QH14, Articles 38-39 and the negative list as implemented by Decree No. 31/2021/ND-CP; Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP on Enterprise Registration, Forms II.1 and II.2; Circular No. 47/2019/TT-BTC on registration fees (online filing exempt); Law on Securities No. 54/2019/QH14 and Decree No. 86/2016/ND-CP (secondary applicability where securities-linked M&A advice is rendered); Law on Anti-Money Laundering No. 04/2024/QH15 (effective 1 July 2025); Law on Occupational Safety and Health No. 84/2015/QH13; Law on Environmental Protection No. 72/2020/QH14 and Decree No. 08/2022/ND-CP.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Investment Registration (Form A.1 per Decree No. 31/2021/ND-CP) resulting in the Investment Registration Certificate (Giay chung nhan dang ky dau tu); Application for Enterprise Registration (Form II.1 per Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP) resulting in the Certificate of Enterprise Registration (Giay chung nhan dang ky doanh nghiep), which issues simultaneously the Enterprise Code and Tax Identification Number and constitutes the operating business license for non-conditional ISIC 7020 advisory services.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Viet Nam imposes no statutory minimum charter capital for a management-consultancy LLC under ISIC 7020; the foreign investor must nevertheless demonstrate financial capacity, and the charter capital must be reasonably proportional to the scale of the M&A advisory project. The registered charter capital must be fully contributed in cash within 90 days from the Certificate of Enterprise Registration, paid via an inward transfer into the Company's Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at a licensed commercial bank. The DICA operates as a monitored capital escrow because subsequent foreign-currency inflows, including M&A success-fee wires, must be received through it and are subject to foreign-exchange and anti-money-laundering verification by the custodian bank.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under Decree No. 136/2020/ND-CP: for a leased class II or III commercial office, the lessor's fire-acceptance certificate and full building PCCC maintenance records must cover the boutique's floor; the boutique itself must undergo inspection of fire extinguishers, automatic alarm points, evacuation directional signage, emergency exit capacity, and employee fire-warden training. A standalone boutique with net floor area exceeding the Decree thresholds requires the Certificate of Eligibility for Fire Prevention and Fighting issued by the provincial Fire Police before personnel occupancy. Structural-load and safe-escape clearance is evidenced by the building's occupancy certificate under QCVN 03:2009/BXD, the Law on Fire Prevention and Fighting No. 27/2001/QH10 (as amended by Law No. 40/2013/QH12), and the Law on Civil Defense No. 23/2023/QH16 (effective 1 July 2024). Occupational workplace clearance under the Law on Occupational Safety and Health No. 84/2015/QH13 requires a documented workplace risk assessment covering fire drills, electrical safety, workstation ergonomics per TCVN 7114:2002, first-aid equipment, and pre-employment and annual medical examinations for all employees; the General Director must carry the labour-safety statement and training records at the registered office for inspection by the provincial labour inspectorate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế), Ministry of Finance of the Socialist Republic of Viet Nam; Electronic tax filing portal: etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn; National e-invoice clearance and registration portal: hoadon.dichvucong.gov.vn

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain the Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment or through the National Foreign Investment Portal (dautugocp.gov.vn) for the licensed foreign-invested enterprise. Step 2: Obtain the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); the tax identification number (Mã số thuế - MST) is automatically and concurrently granted upon ERC issuance, so no separate TIN application is required for the domestic LLC. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Foreign-Invested LLC is a Viet Nam tax resident taxed at the 20% standard CIT rate on worldwide income; there is no pass-through eligibility and no tax-exempt zone status in the ordinary course; Territorial_Scope is global for resident LLCs, with offshore passive income subject to attribution and controlled-foreign-company type rules under Vietnamese tax administration and transfer-pricing frameworks.

Available election forms

Viet Nam does not operate a check-the-box election or OECD hybrid-mismatch election regime. Available statutory elections: (i) mandatory VAT credit method for companies with annual turnover above VND 1 billion; (ii) optional direct method on value-added for taxpayers below the statutory turnover ceiling; (iii) election to apply CIT incentive schedules (e.g., 10% preferential rate, tax holidays and reductions) for qualifying investment projects by sector and location; (iv) election to enter into Advance Pricing Agreements (APA) with the General Department of Taxation under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP; (v) election of quarterly vs monthly tax filing for CIT/VAT; (vi) no entity-level hybrid-mismatch elections — intra-group financing, royalties and management fees are instead constrained by thin-capitalization and related-party transaction rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for corporate entities: all LLCs must register for VAT upon enterprise registration. Only non-corporate household and individual business activities with annual taxable turnover not exceeding VND 100,000,000 are outside the VAT net; an LLC operating as an M&A Advisory Boutique has an unconditional VAT registration obligation regardless of expected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero-rated (0%) VAT supply is available for cross-border services, including M&A advisory services, only where all conditions of Article 9 of the Law on VAT (No. 13/2008/QH12 as amended), Decree 219/2013/ND-CP and Circular 72/2013/TT-BTC are met: (i) the advisory service is provided to a foreign customer (an organization or individual resident abroad) or to an export-processing entity; (ii) the service is actually consumed outside Viet Nam (advisory deliverables, offshore decision-making and closing activities occurring abroad); (iii) there is a signed service contract documenting the foreign recipient and place of performance; (iv) payment is received from abroad in a convertible foreign currency through an authorized Vietnamese bank; and (v) the enterprise holds complete accounting vouchers proving the offshore supply. Services rendered to onshore parties or consumed in Viet Nam are not zero-rated and remain subject to output VAT at 10%; failure to satisfy any condition transmutes the supply into a domestic taxable service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (required only for embedded physical deliverables/goods; a pure advisory service does not generate a customs declaration)
  • Export Service Contract / engagement letter identifying the offshore client, scope of work, and place of consumption
  • Zero-rated VAT e-Invoice with tax-authority code issued and cleared under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Giấy báo Có) or foreign-currency bank statement evidencing that the success fee was remitted from abroad
  • Statement of Work, time sheets, closing schedules and delivery memos evidencing performance and consumption outside Viet Nam
  • Retained correspondence and deal documentation substantiating offshore consumption (retention period: 10 years under Vietnamese tax record-keeping rules)

Mandatory e-invoicing has applied to all enterprises since 1 July 2022 under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. Invoices with tax-authority codes must be cleared in real time through the national portal hoadon.dichvucong.gov.vn before issuance; the enterprise transmits digitally signed XML/SOAP messages to the tax authority's infrastructure through certified e-invoice service providers (TMS API integration). Self-issued e-invoices without tax-authority codes must be transferred to the tax authority within one working day of issuance. Every e-invoice requires a Vietnamese digital signature token and a QR code for fiscal authentication; failure to clear electronically renders the invoice invalid for input VAT credits and for export zero-rating claims.

The General Department of Taxation scrutinizes export zero-rating claims through dedicated desks and post-refund audits. Where a zero-rating claim is found fraudulent: the VAT refund is clawed back retrospectively, the 0% rate is re-characterized as 10% output VAT, and the enterprise is liable for the full underdeclared output VAT plus late-payment interest at 0.03% per day and penalties under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP ranging from 1 to 3 times the evaded tax for material VAT misdeclaration, and up to 20%-50% of the tax plus administrative fines for erroneous declarations. For criminal tax evasion under Article 200 of the Penal Code, penalties include fines up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment up to 7 years. The tax-administration limitation period for fraud cases is 10 years, during which the General Department of Taxation may reopen returns, disallow all related input credits, sanction responsible tax practitioners, and blacklist the entity for future VAT refund clearances.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

VND operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime with limited, condition-based convertibility. Current-account transactions — including cross-border advisory service fees, success fees, interest, royalties, and after-tax profit remittances — are freely convertible through SBV-licensed intermediaries without prior SBV licensing approval, subject to underlying documentation, tax clearance, and bank-level due diligence. The capital account remains restricted: all foreign direct investment equity inflows must enter through a registered Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA); external commercial borrowings require a Foreign Loan Registration Certificate; outbound investments and capital repatriation require investment licence backing; and derivative transactions must be executed with SBV-licensed banks in respect of a verified real commercial exposure. For the M&A advisory boutique, foreign-currency success fees earned from offshore clients are characterized as service-export receipts enjoying current-account convertibility, but profit distribution to the foreign shareholder remains a capital-account operation and must satisfy cumulative conditions: (i) audited annual financial statements filed, (ii) full payment of corporate income tax and any Foreign Contractor Tax obligations, (iii) prior-year accumulated losses fully absorbed, and (iv) lodgement of a profit-remittance declaration and supporting tax certificates with the DICA-holding bank. No quantitative annual remittance cap is imposed on compliant after-tax profits, though banks apply strict documentary scrutiny and report all remittances to SBV in the periodic FX statistics return.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN, as amended, all export proceeds — including cross-border service fees and success fees invoiced by the M&A advisory boutique to non-resident clients — must be repatriated to a foreign-currency account held at an SBV-licensed commercial bank within 90 calendar days from the date of service completion or the contractual payment due date, whichever is later; an extension of up to 180 days may be granted by the bank on documented cash-flow justifications. Mandatory statutory conversion ratios for export receipts were definitively abolished in 2012; the current surrender ratio is 0%, meaning exporters are not required to convert foreign-currency receipts into VND and may retain and redeploy foreign currency through their licensed foreign-currency accounts. However, SBV reserves a residual power, in extraordinary balance-of-payments stress, to issue temporary administrative directives requiring selected exporters to convert a stated percentage of their foreign-currency balances; banks will then enforce such directive. The 90-day repatriation window is a hard compliance point for the boutique's offshore fee collections, and failure to repatriate exposes the entity to administrative sanctions under the FX-control legal framework.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam, SBV) — the Republic of Viet Nam's sole central bank and foreign exchange administration. SBV operates under the Law on the State Bank of Vietnam 2010, the Law on Credit Institutions 2024, and the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange 2005, as amended by Law No. 75/2019/QH14. The SBV sets the daily central reference rate for VND, administers the permitted intraday VND/USD trading band, issues all FX-management decrees and circulars (including Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN on FX activities of credit institutions and Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN on foreign exchange management for foreign direct investment), and chairs national Balance of Payments and monetary stabilization policy.
