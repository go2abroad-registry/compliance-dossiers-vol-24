Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Agency for Business Registration (ABR) under the Ministry of Planning and Investment, operating the National Business Registration Portal (Cổng thông tin quốc gia về đăng ký doanh nghiệp) at dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn; locally competent authority is the Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) of the province or centrally governed city in which the registered office of the Limited Liability Company is situated. If the company is established inside an industrial park, export processing zone, economic zone, or high-tech park, the competent authority is the Management Board of that zone. For investment registration, the competent authority is the DPI or the relevant Zone Management Board acting under the Law on Investment 2020 and Decree 31/2021/ND-CP.

Governing statute

Law on Enterprises (Luật Doanh nghiệp) No. 59/2020/QH14, dated 17 June 2020, effective 1 January 2021, as amended and supplemented, in particular Articles 25, 26, 28, 35, 36, 47, 48, 49, 50, 67, 73, 74, and 75; Law on Investment (Luật Đầu tư) No. 61/2020/QH14, in particular Articles 13, 22, 23, 24, 36, 37, 38, 39 and 40; Decree No. 01/2021/ND-CP on Enterprise Registration, in particular Articles 2, 3, 4, 5, 21, 22, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29 and 32; Decree No. 31/2021/ND-CP detailing the Law on Investment, in particular Articles 15, 16, 17, 20, 21, 22 and 38; Decree No. 136/2020/ND-CP on Fire Safety and Fire Fighting; Law on Environmental Protection No. 72/2020/QH14 and Decree No. 08/2022/ND-CP.

Formation / constitutional filings

For a foreign-invested limited liability company: (i) Application for Investment Registration Certificate (Form I-01) and the enclosed investment project proposal for issuance of the Investment Registration Certificate (IRC); and (ii) Application for Enterprise Registration (Form A-01) together with the company Charter (Điều lệ công ty), the List of Members (Form A-07), the written statement of the legal representative, and the capital contribution schedule, which collectively constitute the enterprise registration dossier leading to the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Viet Nam does not impose a general statutory minimum charter capital, nor any mandatory escrow or blocked-account rule, for a foreign-invested limited liability company engaged in a non-conditional digital web fiction subscription and interactive reading platform business. The charter capital is freely determined by the founding members and must be declared truthfully and fully in the company Charter. Under Article 47(4) and Article 75(1) of the Law on Enterprises 2020, each member must contribute the subscribed capital in full within 90 days from the date of issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate, by transferring the monetary contribution into a corporate bank account opened in the name of the company with a licensed commercial bank in Viet Nam, or by completing registration of ownership transfer for non-cash contributions. The company must issue a capital contribution certificate to each contributing member. If, exceptionally, the company later enters a licensed or conditional industry requiring a higher charter capital (e.g., banking, insurance, telecommunication, education), a statutory minimum paid-up capital threshold may apply, and a deposit or escrow may be required by sector-specific legislation, but such threshold does not apply to the present ISIC 9000 digital content platform activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế) of the Ministry of Finance of Viet Nam; National e-Tax portal (thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn) and National Business Registration Portal (dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested LLC obtains its Enterprise Registration Certificate through the National Business Registration Portal; the tax registration and issuance of the Tax Identification Number (Mã số thuế, MST) occur automatically through the administrative single-window mechanism. The LLC must additionally complete a VAT registration dossier with the competent local tax sub-department within 15 days of the Enterprise Registration Certificate date and register for the e-invoice system under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP. The MST serves as both the corporate income tax account number and the VAT identification number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a foreign-invested LLC is a resident enterprise taxable at the corporate level; no pass-through election is available. Territorial_Scope — resident enterprises are taxed on worldwide income, with double-tax relief for foreign-source income under applicable tax treaties and domestic unilateral credit rules. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a foreign-invested LLC is a resident enterprise taxable at the corporate level; no pass-through election is available. Territorial_Scope — resident enterprises are taxed on worldwide income, with double-tax relief for foreign-source income under applicable tax treaties and domestic unilateral credit rules. No Tax_Exempt_Zone applies to the ordinary LLC; only Export Processing Enterprises (EPE) located in recognized export processing zones or industrial zones would benefit from special fiscal zone treatment, which is not assumed here.

Available election forms

Viet Nam has no check-the-box entity classification election analogous to the U.S. regime. Available elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration under the credit (input-deduction) method instead of the direct method, which is mandatory once annual turnover exceeds VND 1 billion; (ii) election to file consolidated withholding-tax settlements for related-party cross-border transactions; (iii) election to apply a simplified VAT declaration for micro entities where statutorily eligible; and (iv) application of the Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) regime only for non-resident contractors, not for the domestic LLC. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Decree 132/2020/ND-CP adopts hybrid-mismatch anti-avoidance rules without elective features.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to a resident LLC: VAT registration is mandatory from the date of enterprise registration irrespective of revenue. For non-resident digital suppliers with no place of business in Viet Nam, the threshold is VND 100,000,000 per calendar year under Circular 80/2021/TT-BTC; this foreign-supplier threshold is inapplicable to the resident VN_LLC but establishes the domestic nexus baseline for cross-border platform payment flows.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods and services are subject to 0% VAT under Article 9 of Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC. A resident LLC may apply 0% VAT to web-novel subscription and interactive reading services only where the services are supplied under a written contractual agreement to a foreign customer (non-resident), are consumed wholly outside Viet Nam, and are settled in foreign currency through a licensed Vietnamese bank. Services rendered to foreign principals but consumed in Viet Nam, and services evidenced by domestic top-up or App-Store settlement flows, remain standard-rated at 10%. Consequently, Apple IAP and Google Play Billing flows require re-invoicing to the offshore platform operator together with a contract and offshore-consumption evidence to sustain 0% treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (not applicable to pure digital services; replaced by a duly executed Service Export Contract identifying the offshore recipient and containing an offshore-consumption clause)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (not required for digital services; substituted by the web-novel platform's delivery and usage logs evidencing offshore subscriber consumption)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds through a licensed Vietnamese bank within the statutory repatriation window

Mandatory electronic invoicing applies under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. From 1 July 2022, all taxpayers must issue e-invoices with a certified digital signature of the e-invoice service provider and transmit invoice data to the General Department of Taxation via the National e-Tax portal. Retail sales to consumers effected through the app must be documented by cash-register e-invoices (hóa đơn điện tử khởi tạo từ máy tính tiền) transmitted in near real time to the tax authority. The issuer must adopt an API-integrated clearance or periodic XML upload satisfying the GDT data format, and e-invoices must carry the national fiscal serial number and QR code issued by licensed Viet Nam e-invoice service providers.

Unfounded zero-rating claims are scrutinized by GDT audits using transfer-pricing and offshore-consumption tests. Consequences include: retrospective clawback of input VAT credits; imposition of 10% VAT (or 20% where fraudulent misclassification is established) on the full gross consideration; late-payment interest of 0.03% per day on the underdeclared tax under Article 59 of the Law on Tax Administration; administrative fines up to VND 500,000,000 under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP; and criminal sanctions for tax evasion under Article 200 of the Penal Code, including fines up to VND 4,500,000,000 or imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years, with additional prohibition on tax-refund claims and denial of input VAT offsets for up to 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vietnamese đồng (VND) is not fully freely convertible. Current account transactions, including remittances for trade in services and software/content licensing, are generally permitted and may be freely effected through authorized credit institutions, subject to underlying documentation and MT103/RCD purpose codes. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections for a foreign-invested LLC and outbound remittances of dividends or liquidation proceeds after annual audit and tax clearance, require registration with the SBV (e.g., Foreign Investment Registration Certificate / Investment Registration Certificate, registration of capital account via the enterprise's direct investment capital account (DICA) at a licensed bank). Profit remittance hurdles: annual financial statements must be audited, all corporate income tax and VAT obligations settled, and the remittance supported by audited statement, tax finalization proof, and a legalized corporate resolution. Capital contribution must be fully paid within the timeframe and registered with the SBV via the DICA. Foreign loan disbursements require SBV registration under Circular 12/2022/TT-NHNN for medium to long-term foreign loans. No general capital controls beyond transaction-level documentary compliance; dividends on foreign investment may be repatriated annually after satisfying all financial obligations and tax certifications.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vietnam has substantially liberalized surrender requirements. As of recent regimes, exporters are not broadly mandated to surrender 100% of export proceeds to the SBV; however, authorized dealers must receive export earnings into a foreign currency account domiciled in Vietnam within the statutory period (generally not exceeding 180 days from customs clearance, unless extended under Circular 05/2021/TT-NHNN or successor regulations). Mandatory conversion exists only for certain state-enterprise or special categories; for a private LLC, the requirement is to repatriate and deposit export receipts into a licensed bank's foreign currency account, convert to VND as needed for operational/tax obligations. The statutory timeline for reporting and supporting documentation includes submitting the B2 declaration and underlying invoices through the bank's electronic system, with a general 180-day collection horizon for services/digital exports unless a SBV/Treasury-approved longer term is obtained.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) – Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam, together with the Foreign Exchange Control Ordinance and Circular No. 15/2015/TT-NHNN (and successor amendments) guiding foreign exchange activities, including current account and capital account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium -- Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Aspire) are not licensed banks in Vietnam and cannot provide local VND settlement, DICA custody, or SBV compliance services. They may be used only as auxiliary receivable collection tools for low-risk digital subscription receipts in foreign currency, provided funds are swept promptly into a licensed Vietnamese bank account and transferwise-style peer-to-peer settlement does not violate Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN. The digital platform's direct debit from Apple/Google via IAP and Play Billing generally settles in USD or local App Store currencies into the EMI, but Vietnamese foreign exchange control requires the operating company to repatriate export earnings to a licensed bank in Vietnam and document the cross-border service sale. Full reliance on EMIs is legally non-viable as the primary treasury rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

In general, dividends paid by a Vietnamese limited liability company to its foreign corporate shareholders are subject to Corporate Income Tax at the rate of 10% of the dividend distribution amount, unless the foreign shareholder qualifies for a treaty rate reduction under an applicable Double Taxation Agreement (DTA). While Vietnam does not impose a separate final dividend withholding tax under the Law on CIT, the 10% is effectively collected as CIT on dividend income of the foreign corporate recipient. Dividends distributed to foreign individuals are subject to a final personal income tax rate of 5% (not applicable to intercompany repatriation).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Vietnam's Decree 132/2020/ND-CP requires the preparation and maintenance of transfer pricing documentation (TP file), including the Master File, Local File, and Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) thresholds. The Local File is mandatory for a taxpayer that is a related-party transaction participant and satisfies at least one of the following thresholds: (i) total related-party turnover for the period exceeding VND 50 billion (approximately US$2.1 million); or (ii) total related-party transactions in the tax year exceeding VND 30 billion (approximately US$1.3 million); or (iii) the taxpayer is a party to any transaction qualifying for the profit-based method and has applied the DTA benefit. The Master File must be prepared where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds VND 50 trillion (VND 50,000 billion, approximately US$2.1 billion) and any Vietnamese entity is the ultimate parent or is required to file CbCR. The CbCR is required where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds VND 18,000 billion (approximately US$775 million) and the group operates in more than one country. However, even where thresholds are not met, the taxpayer must still maintain sufficient documentation to demonstrate arm's length pricing, and the General Department of Taxation may request contemporaneous documentation in a TP audit. Starting with assessment periods from 2025 tax year onwards (under Decree 132 as amended and official guidance), certain high-risk taxpayers or taxpayers above the safe-harbor thresholds, including those carrying out digital business activities with related parties, may be placed into mandatory TP documentation requirements. A safe-harbor exists for taxpayers with low related-party turnover and limited related-party transactions (under VND 10 billion turnover and normal profit margins) allowing exemption from preparing the formal TP file, but the exemption is not a bar to adjustment if the return is materially incorrect.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam does not have a formal standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime equivalent to the UAE ESR. However, the following doctrines impose economic substance and anti-avoidance requirements: (i) the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under the Law on Tax Administration 38/2019/QH14 and Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, which empowers the tax authority to re-determine tax obligations where a transaction is not economically substantive and is conducted mainly to avoid CIT; (ii) the beneficial ownership requirement embedded in each applicable DTA and Circular 80/2021/TT-BTC's anti-treaty-abuse provisions; and (iii) the substance-over-form principle embedded in the application of Circular 103/2014/TT-BTC for foreign contractor tax, and in the transfer pricing rules under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP regarding related-party transactions lacking commercial substance. In practice, a webnovel serial platform LLC with only a narrow profit margin, low payroll, and substantial remuneration paid abroad to a related marketing or licensing company may be challenged as lacking economic substance; the tax authority may apply the GAAR to ignore the transaction or re-characterize the arrangement, impose VAT and CIT on deemed revenue, and impose late payment interest and penalties. The protective response is to maintain real substantive operations in Vietnam: a physical production office in an industrial park or licensed digital content company, payroll and social insurance contributions, board meetings held in Vietnam, local service agreements and staff performing content curation, actual payment of Vietnamese suppliers, and a documented commercial rationale for all trademark, content-library, and marketing-related-party service arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Declaration of Revenue

Statutory sanction: Administrative taxation penalties: additional tax assessment, payment of late payment interest at 0.03% per day, and penalties ranging from 20% to 100% of the under-declared tax amount. Criminal exposure under Article 200 of the Penal Code 2015 (amended 2017): fine of VND 100,000,000 to VND 4,500,000,000 or imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years for tax evasion of between VND 100,000,000 and VND 4,500,000,000, with the upper thresholds rising to imprisonment of up to 20 years for amounts exceeding VND 4,500,000,000 in severe cases. The corporate entity is also subject to a fine of up to VND 3,000,000,000 and prohibition of business activities for a fixed period, depending on the aggregated amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives of the LLC are personally liable for tax offenses where the crime is committed on their orders or with their consent. The individual penal sanction can include imprisonment of up to 7 years for tax evasion plus monetary fines. Administrative restitution obligations attach to the legal representative; the director may be subject to a travel ban, exit suspension (cannot depart Vietnam until the tax debt and penalty are resolved), seizure of personal assets and bank accounts, and in cases of corporate veil piercing proof (fraudulent intent, abuse of limited liability, gross undercapitalization), the personal assets of shareholders and directors can be attached to satisfy tax liabilities. Where the evasion is proven to be organized at the director level, the director can face a criminal conviction, imprisonment, and a separate personal fine; additionally, the court may order the director to pay civil restitution to the state treasury.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP, the tax authority will apply an arm's length re-determination: recharacterize the transactions, disallow the related-party deductions, impute profit to the Vietnamese LLC, and impose the resulting CIT (at 20% or preferential rate if applicable) plus late payment interest at 0.03% per day and a 20% or 40% administrative fine for mis-declaration. The foreign entity may simultaneously be subject to Foreign Contractor Tax (CIT + VAT) on its entire deemed Vietnamese-source income under Circular 103/2014/TT-BTC, creating a double economic burden. Where the arrangement is assessed as abusive tax avoidance, the GAAR under Article 19 of the Tax Administration Law empowers the tax authority to disregard the transaction and impose the tax that would have been paid had the substance been real.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly establish or maintain a shell-like structure and cause the Vietnamese LLC to sign non-arm's length contracts with related entities may be jointly responsible for the assessed tax shortages to the extent the legal entity is dissolved or unable to pay. Under the Enterprise Law 2020 and the Civil Code, the director who illegally transfers corporate assets or uses the corporate shell to evade tax obligations can be personally ordered to restore the company's assets or compensate the company. Immigration control: a legal representative with an outstanding tax assessment is prohibited from exiting Vietnam, and forward travel suspensions may be applied to shareholders who are also directors. Criminal liability arises under Article 213 of the Penal Code for releasing from settlement obligations or deliberately performing contrary to law in tax collection ("for-profit crimes") only if an official colludes; for the corporate director, the more likely exposure is Article 200 tax evasion and Article 196 of the Penal Code (intentionally violating state economic management regulations causing serious consequences).
Money Laundering and Cross-Border Payment Route Offenses (AML)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the AML regime: reporting entities that fail to identify customers or report suspicious transactions face fines up to VND 200,000,000; failure to comply with internal control rules attracts fines up to VND 500,000,000; non-cooperation in a transaction freeze may result in additional fines. Foreign exchange violations under Ordinance on Foreign Exchange and Decree 88/2019/ND-CP can result in fines from VND 100,000,000 to VND 300,000,000, plus forced repatriation of funds. Where laundering of criminal proceeds is proven, criminal sanction under Article 324 of the Penal Code: imprisonment from 1 to 10 years for laundering money, with aggravating factors up to 15 years. The corporate entity can be subject to penal fines and dissolution/prohibition.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit the payment rails to be used for laundering, or who help structure transactions to obscure more than VND 300,000,000 in a single or multiple linked operations, face criminal imprisonment under Article 324 as principals or accomplices. Directors are additionally exposed to asset seizure, exit bans, and personal confiscation orders. Vietnamese courts and the State Bank may order the director to pay restitution, are permitted to freeze bank accounts and personal property of the director, and can impose a travel ban to prevent flight. The termination of a director from the legal entity does not extinguish the personal criminal liability. For a foreign-owned LLC, the offshore ultimate beneficial owner may be denied entry or extradition cooperation under treaty provisions; directors resident in Vietnam are the primary target of criminal prosecution.
Dividend Distribution and Repatriation Non-Compliance / Unlawful Capital Withdrawal

Statutory sanction: The company is liable for the unpaid dividend withholding tax (10% CIT for corporate shareholders) and subject to late payment interest of 0.03% per day. Penalty for late or non-filing of withholding returns and non-payment of withheld taxes ranges up to VND 20,000,000 for each form and additional fines. Dividend distributions made in violation of the Enterprise Law 2020 (Article 135) render the shareholder liable to return any profits received from illegal distributions plus damages; the company's legal representative can be fined from VND 5,000,000 to VND 15,000,000 under the Enterprise Law administrative sanctions. If the profit repatriation is disguised as a royalty or interest payment to a foreign related party fabricated without substance, the entire amount is disallowed as a deduction and treated as a deemed dividend, triggering the additional 10% dividend CIT with penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and Directors who approve an unlawful dividend distribution can be jointly and severally liable to the company and to creditors for the value of the distributed amount and consequential losses under Article 135, Section 4, and Article 136 of the Enterprise Law 2020; the Civil Code allows affected creditors to pursue claims directly against the director. Travel bans and asset freezes apply for unsatisfied tax liabilities related to the distribution; directors can also face criminal prosecution under Article 200 of the Penal Code if the distribution goes hand in hand with tax under-declaration or the failure to remit withholding taxes constitutes misappropriation of state money (Article 221 - appropriating state revenues in the form of non-remitted withholding tax, potentially imprisonment for up to 10 years).
