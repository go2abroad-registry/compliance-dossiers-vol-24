Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For a virtual office and mail scanning LLC, no industry-specific legal capital (minimum charter capital) is imposed; the company must nevertheless declare its charter capital in the ERC and fully contribute it within 90 days after issuance of the ERC in accordance with Article 47 of the Law on Enterprises 2020. Capital is paid into a corporate bank account opened in the company's name; no escrow or blocked account is statutorily required, but the DPI and tax authorities may require contribution evidence, and any later change in contributed capital must be registered by a supplementary filing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Acceptance Certificate (Giấy chứng nhận nghiệm thu về PCCC) obtained from the provincial Fire Police must be displayed at the registered office; annual checks on evacuation routes and fire-fighting equipment are mandatory. For structural clearance, the lessor must present the completed-construction record and building function certificate demonstrating an office function. Occupational safety: conduct a workplace hazard assessment for screen-equipment workstations, provide compulsory occupational safety training under Law on Occupational Safety and Hygiene No. 84/2015/QH13, maintain an occupational safety inspection file for computers and scanning devices, and keep labor and insurance records. Civil defense: the virtual office must have no hazardous storage beyond office supplies, display an evacuation map, and suspend operations during fire guard inspection if the fire safety acceptance is not revalidated.

Paid-in capital rule: For a virtual office and mail scanning LLC, no industry-specific legal capital (minimum charter capital) is imposed; the company must nevertheless declare its charter capital in the ERC and fully contribute it within 90 days after issuance of the ERC in accordance with Article 47 of the Law on Enterprises 2020. Capital is paid into a corporate bank account opened in the company's name; no escrow or blocked account is statutorily required, but the DPI and tax authorities may require contribution evidence, and any later change in contributed capital must be registered by a supplementary filing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (GDT), Ministry of Finance, Socialist Republic of Viet Nam. Official transaction portals: EtaxVN tax filing and TIN administration at https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn; national e-invoice portal at https://hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn; National Business Registration Portal at https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested LLC obtains its statutory enterprise tax identification number (MST/TIN) automatically upon issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) through the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI). The foreign investor must first complete investment registration and enterprise registration via the National Business Registration Portal; no separate TIN application is required for the LLC. A foreign company operating in Viet Nam without a locally incorporated entity must instead obtain a contractor TIN and register for the Foreign Contractor Tax regime with the GDT before issuing invoices or receiving Vietnam-source payments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity status. Viet Nam does not recognize check-the-box elections, transparent/partnership classifications, or elective hybrid-mismatch treatment; the LLC is per se a corporate income tax (CIT) taxpayer. Available administrative elections are limited to VAT accounting method options (credit-invoice deduction method versus direct turnover-based method) and, for qualifying non-resident foreign contractors, election between the deemed Foreign Contractor Tax and ordinary CIT registration, in each case subject to GDT approval and statutory documentation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 100,000,000 annual revenue threshold for business households and individuals below which VAT registration and output VAT are not required; no turnover threshold applies to an LLC, which must register for VAT immediately upon enterprise registration and before any taxable supply activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to goods and services genuinely supplied to non-resident persons and consumed outside Viet Nam. The claim requires a signed cross-border export or service contract, evidence that performance and consumption occurred outside Vietnam, foreign-currency payment received through an authorized Vietnamese commercial bank, and, for goods, completion of electronic customs export procedures. A virtual-office/mail-scanning service qualifies as an exported service only where the recipient obtains the benefit entirely outside Viet Nam, the contract and payment trail identify the non-resident recipient, and the service has no connected Vietnam-located property or local use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic customs export entry) for physical exports; not required for pure cross-border digital service exports
  • Signed international sales or service contract with the non-resident counterparty
  • Commercial invoice and GDT-coded export e-invoice
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or transport document for goods; for services, signed delivery report or performance log proving overseas consumption
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC), bank foreign-payment confirmation, or authorized commercial bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency
  • Packing list, certificate of origin, and export insurance documents where applicable

Mandatory e-invoicing applies to all enterprises from 1 July 2022. Invoices must be issued as electronic invoices bearing a tax-authority code (hoá đơn điện tử có mã của cơ quan thuế) in standardized XML/JSON format, transmitted to the GDT e-invoice portal in near real time through a registered service-provider API. The GDT validates each invoice and returns a digital tax-authority code and QR stamp before the invoice is legally usable; invoice records and underlying transaction data must be retained for at least 10 years.

GDT and Customs cross-check zero-rating claims against customs declarations, e-invoice data, annual CIT filings, bank settlement records, and IRC documentation. An erroneous or fraudulent export zero-rating claim is reopened within the statutory assessment period; the VAT input credit and refund are retrospectively clawed back, the due VAT is reassessed, and late-payment interest applies at the statutory rate (0.03% per day). Administrative penalties range from fines to 1–3 times the evaded VAT, and deliberate export VAT refund fraud, e-invoice forgery, or customs evasion may attract criminal prosecution, suspension of the taxpayer's e-invoice account, cancellation of tax-clearance status, and denial of future zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam operates a managed float regime with tight capital controls and partial convertibility. The Vietnamese dong (VND) is not freely convertible offshore; current account transactions—including trade-related transfers and operating expenses—are generally permitted and bankable, subject to verification of underlying documentation. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, shareholder loans, and the issuance of foreign securities, require compliance with the Law on Enterprises, the Law on Investment, Decree 31/2021/ND-CP, SBV Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN, and Circular 02/2021/TT-NHNN. Profit and dividend remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted after the completion of audited annual financial statements and payment of all corporate income tax obligations, subject to an SBV-registered Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA). The SBV sets the daily central reference exchange rate and permits commercial banks to quote rates within a narrow band, limiting arbitrage flexibility. Virtual office and mail scanning businesses are not considered a priority sector; therefore, the entity will undergo enhanced FX compliance scrutiny for remittances, and profit repatriation may be deferred until the audited finalization is approved and prior year deficits are cleared.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SBV Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN (as amended by Circular 02/2021/TT-NHNN), resident entities that directly export goods or services are required to sell a statutory percentage of export foreign-exchange proceeds to a licensed bank. For most exporters, at least 80% of the export proceeds must be converted into VND within 15 working days of receipt, while the remaining 20% may be retained in the exporter's foreign currency account to fund permissible current-account spending. Certain categories—namely Export Processing Enterprises (EPE), entities operating in or for export-processing zones, and entities that are parties to approved Commercial Concession Agreements—may retain a higher proportion of export proceeds, subject to an SBV-provincial approval. For the CMRA / mail scanning entity whose primary revenue is subscription or recurring fee income rather than merchandise exports, the general 80% surrender rule applies to any export of professional services (classified as current-account receipt). The bank will apply a mandatory sale ratio based on the underlying transaction code and will require a qualified Export Title (e.g., Service Contract, Commercial Invoice, Bill of Exchange) to determine the exact removal timeline.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV / Ngan Hang Nha Nuoc Viet Nam), including the Foreign Exchange Administration Department (FXD) and provincial SBV provincial branches, with the Government of Vietnam issuing FX ordinances and circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Stripe, Wise, Airwallex, Rapyd, or similar platforms are not authorized to open deposit accounts or provide banking services to legal entities within Vietnam. SBV and the Payment Directive (Decree 52/2013/ND-CP, amended by Decree 27/2022/ND-CP) confer only upon licensed commercial banks and licensed non-bank payment institutions (e.g., VNPAY, MoMo) the right to process payment transactions for residents. Offshore EMIs may act as a foreign treasury aggregation layer only if (i) the Vietnamese LLC uses them as a non-resident receivable-channel merchant processor, (ii) all settled funds are routed to a licensed Vietnamese commercial bank within the SBV regulatory perimeter, and (iii) the EMI obtains a partnership with an approved foreign-owned bank branch to secure SWIFT pass-through. The feasibility rating is LOW for using an EMI as the primary Vietnamese bank account substitute, but MEDIUM for using an EMI as an invoice-billing and receivable collection facilitator that is contractually backed by a local commercial bank (e.g., via Stripe's Local Payment Method enabling VND-currency settlement). Legal constraints include: EMIs cannot open a DICA or accept FDI capital injection in Vietnam, cannot issue FX surrender certificates, and cannot support SBV-mandated annual balance and transaction reporting. Therefore, the blueprint requires a local tier-1 bank presence as the sole repository for statutory capital and operating funds, with the EMI only integrated as a secondary front-end collection code for credit-card recurring fees.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment is routed exclusively through a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at a licensed Vietnamese commercial bank. The remitting foreign shareholder must instruct its offshore bank to transmit the funds via SWIFT/MT103 in USD (or another convertible currency) to the DICA's account at the local depository bank. The payment must include a payment narrative citing the purpose code 'DIRECT INVESTMENT CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' (code 4.2) or 'SHARE CAPITAL PAYMENT' under the Vietnam Balance of Payments classification, along with the Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) of the Vietnamese LLC and the Investment Registration Certificate number. The bank will verify the remitting shareholder's identity, match the remittance amount to the capital schedule in the Investment Registration Certificate, and submit a Foreign Investment Supervision Notification to the State Bank of Vietnam within 48 hours. The funds are not available for operating expenditure until the bank confirms the receipt and the SBV's electronic portal flags the GIA consent, after which the bank may reclassify the funds from 'Escrowed, Pending Verification' to 'Available Current Account'. If capital is injected in tranches, each tranche requires a separate SWIFT/MT103 and a corresponding revised certificate or the company must file an adjustment notice with the provincial Department of Planning and Investment and the bank before the transfer.Outbound trade and service payments are executed through the Vietnam LLC's transactional foreign-currency account or domestic VND account, using SWIFT MT103 for cross-border fund transfers originating from a licensed bank. For trade settlement, the beneficiary is an overseas supplier; the bank will require presentation of the commercial invoice, shipping bill (for goods), service contract (for intangible services), and the corresponding 'IG' trade code to confirm the import obligation. The LLC may also settle trade obligations via an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC) opened by a Vietnamese commercial bank; such LC are routed through SWIFT MT700 and honoured against presenting banks' documents, payable at sight or by acceptance. The clearing rails used will be: (1) VND debit from the LLC's account converted to USD at the bank's daily published rate, followed by an MT103; (2) direct USD transfer from the FX account if retained funds are compliant with the surrender rules; and (3) a Commercial LC import line tied to the DICA. Where the offline CMRA payment flows are in the form of a commercial LC to the overseas IT or logistics vendor, the bank must verify the underlying contract to ensure that the LC is not a front for capital flight. All SWIFT payments are subject to a Foreign Currency Business Permit verification and the SBV's Electronic Foreign Exchange Reporting Module must be updated no later than the next working day.FX Forward Contracts: bilateral contracts between the licensed bank and the LLC for a fixed exchange rate settled on a future date, requiring a demonstrated commercial-hedging underlying obligation (e.g., on expected monthly USD inflows from subscribers or a planned USD disbursement to a foreign AI provider), and subject to SBV's Daily Rate Band.Currency Swaps: periodic swap of VND and USD cash flows (or USD/EUR) between the LLC and a licensed bank, provided the bank applies the SBV's maximum tenor limits and includes a required COP (Compliance and Operational Procedure) to ensure no speculative position is built up.FX Options (Premium/Zero-Premium): limited to vanilla European-style options purchased from licensed credit institutions; covered-call or covered-put strategies require prior SBV approval; these instruments must be used consecutively with named trade-documentation.Interest Rate Swap Coupled to a Cross-Currency Facility: used where the LLC's capital injection is a USD-denominated shareholder loan converted to VND; the swap is permissible if matched to the underlying loan agreement and the DICA, with clearing executed via an ISDA schedule.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) with an SBV-Approved Local Bank: residents are not permitted to enter NDFs offshore, but licensed domestic banks may structure an onshore NDF if the hedge is wholly on account of a fully-bilateral current-account exposure and the NDF is settled in VND at maturity, with foreign-currency difference delivered offshore outside SBV jurisdiction avoided; we do not recommend using an offshore NDF provider due to enforcement risk and pure outside clearability.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Stripe, Wise, Airwallex, Rapyd, or similar platforms are not authorized to open deposit accounts or provide banking services to legal entities within Vietnam. SBV and the Payment Directive (Decree 52/2013/ND-CP, amended by Decree 27/2022/ND-CP) confer only upon licensed commercial banks and licensed non-bank payment institutions (e.g., VNPAY, MoMo) the right to process payment transactions for residents. Offshore EMIs may act as a foreign treasury aggregation layer only if (i) the Vietnamese LLC uses them as a non-resident receivable-channel merchant processor, (ii) all settled funds are routed to a licensed Vietnamese commercial bank within the SBV regulatory perimeter, and (iii) the EMI obtains a partnership with an approved foreign-owned bank branch to secure SWIFT pass-through. The feasibility rating is LOW for using an EMI as the primary Vietnamese bank account substitute, but MEDIUM for using an EMI as an invoice-billing and receivable collection facilitator that is contractually backed by a local commercial bank (e.g., via Stripe's Local Payment Method enabling VND-currency settlement). Legal constraints include: EMIs cannot open a DICA or accept FDI capital injection in Vietnam, cannot issue FX surrender certificates, and cannot support SBV-mandated annual balance and transaction reporting. Therefore, the blueprint requires a local tier-1 bank presence as the sole repository for statutory capital and operating funds, with the EMI only integrated as a secondary front-end collection code for credit-card recurring fees.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (Form 02) and Master File (Form 01) are mandatory if the LLC's total related-party transaction value in the year exceeds VND 30 billion, or if total revenue exceeds VND 50 billion and related-party transaction value exceeds VND 10 billion; Country-by-Country Report (Form 03) is required when the ultimate consolidated group revenue reaches VND 18,000 billion. A CMRA LLC typically operates below these thresholds but must still apply arm's-length pricing and keep contemporaneous documentation on demand.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam does not have a UAE-style standalone ESR regime, but Decree 132/2020/ND-CP fully incorporates the OECD BEPS substance-over-form principle and a general anti-avoidance rule. Transactions lacking commercial substance are recharacterized by the tax authority; thin capitalisation limits interest deductions to 30% of EBITDA; controlled-foreign-company rules apply; and a CMRA LLC with only a virtual mailbox, no local employees, and no genuine activity is treated as a shell and highly vulnerable to being recharacterized as a pure tax vehicle.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion under Penal Code Article 200

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to VND 4.5 billion; imprisonment of individuals from 6 months up to 7 years for the basic offence, extending up to 20 years for extraordinary amounts; legal entities face fine up to VND 4.5 billion, suspension of operations, or forced dissolution; plus administrative penalties equal to the evaded tax and 0.03% daily late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and day-to-day managers who order or participate in tax evasion face personal criminal prosecution, passport confiscation, travel bans, asset freezes, and civil restitution orders for the evaded CIT; piercing of the corporate veil is applied so the personal assets of the culpable director answer for the unpaid tax.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority re-determines taxable income using the best-fit arm's-length methodology and imposes additional CIT of up to 30% of the recalculated income, plus 0.03% daily late-payment interest; documentation deficiency fines reach VND 15,000,000 under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP; investigation files may be criminally referred under Tax Administration Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false TP documentation or approve fictitious cross-border service contracts are personally liable for the penalty uplift; the law permits director disqualification, travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution for signing fraudulent subsidy? (i.e., fraudulent expense) declarations.
Shell Company and Abuse of Limited Liability

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the Investment Registration Certificate and Enterprise Registration Certificate; forced dissolution; denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of all related-party revenue as deemed dividends; administrative fines for false licensing declarations; criminal prosecution for document forgery and fraud with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil of the shell, rendering the foreign parent and its directors jointly and severally liable for all unpaid corporate taxes and creditor claims; directors face entry bans, travel bans, confiscation of local assets, and criminal sentences for abuse of legal personality.
Anti-Money Laundering / KYC Violations in CMRA Operations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to VND 5 billion under Decree 87/2023/ND-CP on anti-money laundering; criminal liability for money laundering under Penal Code Article 324 with imprisonment of 1-10 years for individuals and up to 15 years for aggravated organized conduct; revocation of the CMRA licence and exclusion from payment-processing channels.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal fines, asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment; the court may impose a management ban and order personal restitution to defrauded third parties when the director knowingly allowed the platform to facilitate laundering.
