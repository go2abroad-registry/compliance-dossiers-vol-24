Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs (VRC), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management, Government of Vanuatu; taxpayer portal: VRC online services and e-filing portal accessible through the official MFEM/Vanuatu Government portal at gov.vu

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) that carries on no Vanuatu taxable activity is automatically outside the income tax and VAT net and is not required to obtain a TIN; company identification is the VFSC-registered IC number. If the IC begins or intends to carry on a taxable activity with actual/expected turnover of at least VUV 5,000,000 in any 12-month period, it must file the VAT registration application with VRC, attaching the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, register of directors/shareholders, identification of beneficial owners, proof of business address, and a brief description of the activity. VRC issues the VAT registration number (VRN) and certificate. Customs will assign a separate importer/customs reference for physical imports even in the absence of a TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Vanuatu; the IC's exemption from income tax is mandatory and non-waivable. An IC can voluntarily register for VAT under the VAT Act if it carries on taxable supplies below the compulsory threshold. Vanuatu has no hybrid mismatch or entity classification elections because there is no corporate income tax; the VAT registration election does not alter the IC's tax-exempt status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 5,000,000 (Vanuatu vatu) of taxable turnover in any 12 consecutive months (mandatory); voluntary registration is available for taxable supply businesses below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Vanuatu in the course of a commercial sale to a non-resident qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier is VAT-registered and obtains and keeps evidence of physical export. Services are zero-rated if supplied to a non-resident and consumed wholly outside Vanuatu. For the electric cargo e-bike manufacturing scope, component exports and finished e-bike sales to offshore distributors or buyers qualify for zero-rating when shipment is made from Vanuatu under normal commercial terms and the goods are delivered to the foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (lodged electronically with Vanuatu Customs at the port of export)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Vanuatu to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice containing contract terms, Incoterms, and a zero-rated export endorsement
  • Packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing collection of the export proceeds
  • Customs-stamped export documentation/outward landing card used by Vanuatu Customs

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate currently applies in Vanuatu. Registered businesses must issue valid VAT invoices in paper or digital PDF form containing prescribed particulars (VRN, date, supplier/customer names and addresses, description, quantity, VAT-exclusive price, VAT amount and total) and retain them for VAT return and audit purposes.

Vanuatu Revenue and Customs may verify the goods left Vanuatu and that title and payment passed offshore. If zero-rating is claimed without genuine export, the VAT shortfall becomes immediately assessable at 12.5% together with statutory interest. Additional penalties are imposed for incomplete or false returns, including a civil penalty of up to the tax shortfall and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, which can extend to imprisonment. Erroneous export refunds are clawed back by amended assessment, interest, penalties, and set-off against any future refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Legal tender is the Vanuatu Vatu (VUV), managed by the RBV against a Special Drawing Right (SDR) peg. Current-account transactions are fully convertible with no quantitative limits; the capital account is substantially liberalized for International Companies, which are statutorily free to hold and deal in any foreign currency and maintain offshore bank accounts absent restrictive Articles. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders faces no statutory hurdles: the IC is exempt from income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax under the International Companies Act, and no RBV approval or dividend-blocking mechanism applies. The practical convertibility constraint is correspondent-bank due diligence, not domestic regulation; settlement in USD or AUD is standard.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Vanuatu does not impose any statutory timeline for surrender of export proceeds, any mandatory conversion ratio into VUV, or any RBV-directed repatriation of foreign-currency earnings. A Vanuatu IC exporting electric cargo e-bikes may retain 100% of export receipts in designated multi-currency accounts and redeploy those funds freely for component imports and supplier settlement.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), which administers the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 66] and the Financial Dealers Licensing Act; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) exercises supplementary AML/CFT supervision over licensed banks and registers International Companies under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222].

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Ramp Fit: High

Low feasibility for primary treasury use. Vanuatu has no statutory Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category; VFSC does not issue EMI charters and no digital-bank passporting regime applies. Offshore EMIs (UK, EU, Lithuania, Australia) may provide on-ramp collection and fee aggregation, but they cannot issue VUV-denominated accounts, originate Commercial Letters of Credit, or act as a settlement bank for the IC's Asian OEM supply chain. They may be used as a secondary collection rail for small-value B2B payments, provided that the IC maintains its principal USD liquidity and trade settlement rails with a licensed Vanuatu commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity is transmitted by SWIFT MT103 (T/T) in USD directly to the IC's USD-denominated account at ANZ Vanuatu or BSP Vanuatu. The remittance narrative must state 'Equity capital contribution / share subscription' without ambiguous terms; the remitting bank will map the payment to its own balance-of-payments equity code. The receiving bank verifies the credit against a board resolution, the updated VFSC share register, and directors' funds-confirmation letter. No RBV exchange-control approval is required, although the receiving bank will record the credit for statistical purposes and may ask for the underlying board resolution. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Foreign-Source Opaque Structure

Statutory sanction: Within Vanuatu, VFSC may suspend or strike off the IC and impose fines for false annual-return declarations. In the shareholder's home jurisdiction, the structure is vulnerable to sham/GAAR recharacterization, CFC attribution, reallocation of the full OEM profit margin, interest, penalties typically ranging from 20% to 200% of the understatement, and criminal tax-fraud prosecution where deliberate concealment is proven.

Director personal liability: Signing false VFSC declarations exposes directors to fines up to VUV 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years under the International Companies Act; foreign courts may pierce the corporate veil to impose civil restitution of diverted profits, director disqualification, seizure of Vanuatu bank accounts, cross-border travel bans, and extradition-based imprisonment for tax fraud.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: VFSC may issue an ESR compliance notice, impose monetary penalty assessments, require a corrected return, and ultimately strike the company off the register; knowing inclusion of false information in an ESR filing is a criminal offense carrying fines up to VUV 1,000,000 per offense and custodial sanctions, with collateral effects including FATF/EU listing countermeasures, correspondent-bank account closure, and denial of banking services.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent acting with knowledge attract joint personal exposure: administrative penalties, disqualification orders, restoration cost recovery, and foreign judicial piercing of the veil reallocating manufacturing income to the true economic owner with civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraudulent misrepresentation.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Record Failure

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2022 and VFSC AML directives: monetary penalties, suspension or revocation of the registered agent's license, strike-off of the IC, freezing of the Vanuatu account, and disqualification of the compliance officer; where the underlying funds are criminal proceeds, the directors and any facilitating agent become subject to money-laundering prosecution with asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines and confiscation orders; involvement in a laundering network triggers travel bans, mutual legal assistance arrests, and imprisonment in Vanuatu or the foreign prosecuting jurisdiction; the corporate veil is pierced as the IC is treated as a mere instrumentality of the undisclosed principal.
Cross-Border Mispricing / CFC Profit-Attribution Conduit

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu imposes no domestic transfer-pricing penalty because it has no TP statute; the sanction is imposed by trading-partner revenue authorities through transfer-pricing adjustments, controlled-foreign-company attribution, and general anti-abuse rules, producing additional tax, interest, and penalties of 20% to 40% of the recharacterized profit, plus in severe cases criminal tax-evasion charges; the bank executing the wire flows may also terminate the IC relationship for lack of economic purpose.
