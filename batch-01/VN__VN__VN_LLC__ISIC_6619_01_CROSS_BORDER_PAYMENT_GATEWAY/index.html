Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Law on Enterprises 59/2020/QH14 imposes no universal minimum charter capital for an LLC. However, because the entity will operate as a non-bank payment intermediary, the State Bank of Viet Nam (SBV) regime requires a paid-up charter capital of at least VND 50 billion (approx. USD 2,050,000) for the license to provide intermediary payment services, including payment gateway services. The capital must be maintained in a blocked bank account opened at a licensed commercial bank in Viet Nam, fully contributed within 90 days from the date the Enterprise Registration Certificate is issued, and evidenced by a bank certificate of capital availability in the SBV licensing dossier.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (GDT) of the Ministry of Finance, Socialist Republic of Viet Nam; taxpayer portal at etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn; National Business Registration Portal at dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn; electronic invoice portal at hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested limited liability company must first obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial investment authority or export processing zone authority, and thereafter an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) through the National Business Registration Portal. The enterprise code stated on the ERC serves as the automatic tax identification number (MST) and is simultaneously registered with the GDT for corporate income tax and VAT. A separate GDT tax-code registration is required for any branch, additional establishment, or VAT registration for non-resident foreign contractor services. A non-resident foreign supplier of cross-border digital services must register through the GDT eTax portal and be issued a foreign-supplier tax identity before filing returns; if no direct registration is feasible, a local withholding agent must be appointed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Viet Nam does not operate a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch classification regime. An LLC may elect its constitutive ownership structure (single-member or multi-member), its applicable accounting framework (Vietnamese Accounting Standards, with IFRS only for group consolidation or statutory banking/licensed payment-services preparers), and its investment incentive status, including optional certification as an export processing enterprise (EPE). The Foreign Contractor Tax regime provides a deemed-output tax mechanism for non-resident suppliers but does not create pass-through entity classification for Viet Nam domestic LLCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VND 100,000,000 annual turnover from taxable supplies. Domestic enterprises, including foreign-invested LLCs, are VAT-registered without a turnover threshold, whereas the VND 100 million low-turnover threshold applies to household businesses and, critically, to non-resident foreign digital-service suppliers in the GDT foreign-supplier registration process for cross-border digital supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Viet Nam-based LLC may apply the 0% VAT rate to exported goods if the goods physically leave Viet Nam customs territory, are sold under a written export contract, have an accepted customs export declaration, and are transported to a foreign buyer or to an export processing enterprise under EPE-qualifying conditions. For the cross-border payment-gateway scope, a zero rating is available only for services supplied to a foreign organisation or individual that are consumed entirely outside Viet Nam; international acquiring, routing, clearing, and settlement services supplied to a non-resident merchant for card transactions occurring wholly outside Viet Nam can qualify, if the Vietnam-based PSP maintains contractual evidence, non-resident invoicing, and operational records demonstrating offshore consumption. Domestic acquiring fees, gateway fees on Vietnam-origin transactions, merchant setup fees, and processing margins attributable to Viet Nam remain subject to 10% VAT (or the reduced rate for currently eligible sectors); VAT-exempt supplies cannot be zero-rated and VAT refunds are allowed only where input VAT exceeds output VAT and the taxpayer has qualifying export turnover.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic VNACCS acknowledgment with accepted status)
  • Bill of Lading (or airway bill/multimodal transport document showing physical egress from Vietnamese customs territory)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a licensed bank in Viet Nam evidencing foreign-currency repatriation of export proceeds

Electronic invoicing became mandatory in Viet Nam on 1 July 2022. Every VAT invoice generated by an established business must be transmitted to the GDT electronic-invoice system in XML format within one working day of issue; GDT either validates the invoice or assigns a live invoice serial code and corporate hash code. Point-of-sale or cash-register-connected transactions, and specific supervised transactions, require real-time transmission to GDT before delivery/completion using authorised fiscal-cash-register data connections. An e-invoice must include a machine-readable QR code and satisfy the XML data layout in Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC. For payment-processing service providers, invoice issuance must be integrated through the GDT e-invoice application API or a certified third-party e-invoice service provider; customs exports are cleared electronically through the VNACCS/VCIS data interchange, not on paper.

GDT and customs authorities cross-match e-invoice data, VNACCS export declarations, bank SWIFT/FX payment records, and Inward Remittance Certificates during post-clearance audits. Fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims trigger clawback of the VAT refund, imposition of late-payment interest at 0.02% per day, and administrative penalties from one to three times the under-refunded tax amount under the Law on Tax Administration 38/2019/QH14, as amended. Knowingly issuing false customs declarations, using fraudulent transport documents, or abusing export-claim arrangements to illegally obtain tax refunds may constitute tax evasion under Penal Code Article 200, with individual criminal liability of up to 7 years' imprisonment and corporate entity-level fines up to VND 4.5 billion, suspension of operations, and revocation of the payment-service licence. No statutory limitation applies where fraud is demonstrated, and retrospective clawbacks may extend to the entire VAT refund principal plus statutory interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam operates a partial-convertibility regime. Current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, profit remittances, interest, royalties, scheme fees and operating expenses) are generally convertible and may be settled freely through SBV-licensed banks without a prior exchange-control permit, provided the underlying documentation is presented and the transaction is economically real. Capital-account transactions remain controlled: FDI equity must be injected through a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at a licensed bank; cross-border borrowings by the VN_LLC require SBV registration under Circular 08/2022/TT-NHNN; portfolio investment requires an Indirect Investment Capital Account; and outward investment by Vietnamese entities requires the relevant investment registration certificate. Profit remittance hurdles for the foreign-invested PSP are material: annual after-tax profits may be remitted abroad in USD or EUR through the DICA only after (i) the annual audited financial statements have been prepared, (ii) corporate income tax finalization has been filed with the tax authority, (iii) the statutory profit-remittance notice has been lodged with the competent tax office, and (iv) all conditions relating to registered foreign loans and outstanding SBV registrations have been satisfied. In practice, the receiving bank requires a tax-compliance confirmation, an unqualified audit opinion, and evidence of accumulated distributable retained earnings before executing the dividend remittance; the absence of any one of these items is the most frequent operational blocker.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a Vietnamese exporting entity, all foreign-currency proceeds from the sale of goods or services must be repatriated into a foreign-currency account held with an SBV-licensed authorized bank within 90 days from the date of customs clearance of exports or, for service exports, within 90 days from the date of service completion and invoicing (Circular 03/2019/TT-NHNN, as amended). No general mandatory conversion ratio applies to commercial exporters today: the historical export-proceeds surrender obligations have been abolished, and the VN_LLC is entitled to retain 100% of its export proceeds in its onshore foreign-currency account and to convert them at negotiated bank rates. Mandatory sale to the SBV is limited to designated state budget revenues, crude oil, and natural gas receipts. For this payment gateway, cross-border acquiring, clearing, scheme and processing fees earned from foreign merchants constitute service exports; 100% of those earnings must be credited to the PSP's onshore foreign-currency account at a licensed bank and may not be netted, offset, or retained offshore.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) — Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam; the FX regulator is the Foreign Exchange Administration Department under the SBV, exercising delegated authority pursuant to the Ordinance on Foreign Exchange No. 06/2005/PL-UBTVQH11, as amended by Ordinance No. 06/2013/UBTVQH13.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required when annual related-party transaction value equals or exceeds VND 50 billion. Master File: required when the ultimate parent group’s annual consolidated global turnover exceeds VND 18 trillion. Country-by-Country reporting applies at the VND 18 trillion group turnover threshold.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam has no separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP and the anti-abuse provisions of Law on Tax Administration 38/2019/QH14, the tax authority will disregard or recharacterize offshore entities lacking real economic substance. For a cross-border payment gateway, substance is determined by the presence of Vietnamese employees conducting merchant due diligence, transaction monitoring, board decision-making in-country, and operational control. A shell company incorporated only to own IP or booking fees, without functions and risks, will be treated as a façade and profits will be re-attributed to Vietnam.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False CIT Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP, an administrative fine of 1–3 times the evaded tax amount; interest at 0.03%/day on underpaid amounts until payment. Criminal liability under Penal Code (amended 2017) Article 200 for tax evasion: fines up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment of up to 5 years, depending on the amount evaded and previous violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers are jointly and severally liable for the full evaded amount if they signed or consciously permitted false returns; Vietnamese courts can order personal civil restitution, a 12-month exit ban, freezing of personal bank accounts, and criminal detention during investigation.
Shell Company / DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will reject the treaty benefit, reclassify the income as a taxable permanent establishment in Vietnam, impose the 20% CIT and FCT deficiencies, add 20%–100% late-payment penalties and 0.03%/day interest. Penal Code tax evasion provisions can also apply if the structure is found to be a sham.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Vietnamese LLC that knowingly interposed a shell entity can be stripped of limited liability protection; personal liability attaches for unpaid corporate taxes, travel restrictions are imposed, and criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years may be sought for fraudulent bankruptcy or tax evasion.
Unlicensed Payment Intermediary Activity

Statutory sanction: The State Bank of Vietnam may issue license-revocation orders, administrative fines under the Non-Cash Payment Law 2023 (No. 23/2023/QH15) and Decree 52/2024/ND-CP, confiscation of illegal remuneration, and suspension of operations for up to 12 months. If criminal threshold is met, prosecution for illegal business under Penal Code Article 200 can result in fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Officers who directed unlicensed payment processing are individual offenders: they can be fined , obligated for restitution of merchant losses, barred from holding senior positions in Vietnamese payment institutions, and, in cases involving multiple merchants, subject to imprisonment from 3 to 5 years.
AML/KYC Failures for Cross-Border Merchants

Statutory sanction: Fines under Vietnam Anti-Money Laundering Law 2022 (effective 2023) and State Bank guidance up to VND 500 million for licensees; suspension of the PSP license for repeated failure; fully acquitted disgorgement of laundered funds. Criminal liability under Penal Code Article 324 (money laundering) applies up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the PSP are subject to criminal proceedings if they knowingly processed tainted funds or failed to file Suspicious Transaction Reports; personal fines up to VND 500 million, travel bans, and imprisonment from 5 to 10 years are statutory ceilings. The corporate veil is pierced where AML compliance was deliberately not implemented.
