Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to the establishment of a non-conditional programmatic advertising exchange and SSP platform LLC. Under Articles 112-113 of Law on Enterprises No. 59/2020/QH14, the charter capital is determined freely by the members, and every member must contribute the full amount of registered capital within ninety days from the date of issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate. No state escrow or prescription of a special contribution bank account is mandated, but capital contributions are evidenced by bank transfer records and a capital contribution schedule appended to the charter.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the provincial fire police must be obtained if the leased office is located in a building classified as a group II or higher danger facility or if the workforce exceeds the applicable headcount threshold (typically 200 persons or more). The Company must also secure a structural load clearance from the building owner/certified engineer if any server rack or heavy equipment is installed in the premises, as well as a workplace inspection certificate confirming adequate egress, emergency lighting, first-aid availability, and occupational health compliance under Circular 19/2016/TT-BYT. In practice, for a conventional office lease in a certified commercial tower, the Company obtains a letter of confirmation from the building management confirming that the existing fire certificate, structural load rating, and evacuation plans cover the leased floor.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to the establishment of a non-conditional programmatic advertising exchange and SSP platform LLC. Under Articles 112-113 of Law on Enterprises No. 59/2020/QH14, the charter capital is determined freely by the members, and every member must contribute the full amount of registered capital within ninety days from the date of issuance of the Enterprise Registration Certificate. No state escrow or prescription of a special contribution bank account is mandated, but capital contributions are evidenced by bank transfer records and a capital contribution schedule appended to the charter.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế, GDT), Ministry of Finance of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam; day-to-day administration is exercised by the provincial Department of Taxation of Hanoi/Ho Chi Minh City where the LLC is headquartered. Official e-portals: https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn (domestic e-tax filing and e-invoice subsystem) and https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn (national tax web portal used for Foreign Contractor Tax, cross-border supplier registration, and payment settlement).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested LLC must complete the following sequence: (1) obtain the Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) or the Industrial/Export-Processing Zone Authority after filing the investment project application (15 working days statutory review); (2) register the enterprise with the provincial Business Registration Office to obtain the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC); (3) the 10-digit/13-digit Tax Identification Number (MST, mã số thuế) is automatically assigned by the National Business Registration Database upon issuance of the ERC, with data electronically shared to the GDT, thereby completing CIT/VAT registration (an LLC does not file a separate paper tax-registration application); (4) using the MST, obtain a GDT-certified digital signature (USB token), register the e-invoice account and VAT declaration schedule on https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn; (5) open a direct investment capital account and a current VND/FX bank account at a licensed commercial bank, and notify the tax office of the account; (6) for any payment to a non-resident ad-exchange/DSP/SSP counterparty, the local LLC must first register the foreign party under the Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) regime via https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn so that withholding VAT and CIT can be remitted correctly.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Vietnamese LLC is a resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax (CIT) at the standard 20% rate on worldwide income; there is no pass-through or S-corporation style classification; dividends distributed by the LLC to foreign corporate shareholders are exempt from dividend withholding tax (the prior remittance tax was abolished in 2014), while a 5% personal income tax applies to distributions to resident individual shareholders; non-resident persons without an LLC establishment are subject to Vietnam-source taxation through Foreign Contractor Tax and are taxed on a territorial basis.

Available election forms

Vietnam has no U.S.-style check-the-box election and no elective OECD hybrid-mismatch rule. Statutory elections available to an LLC include: (1) VAT method election — the credit (input-deduction) method on a contractual/turnover basis instead of the direct/imputed-ratio method (the credit method is mandatory for enterprises with full accounting records and revenue above 10 billion VND, and optional for smaller firms); (2) CIT incentive election — selecting the investment regime (high-tech zone, geographic investment support areas, or large-scale projects) that is evidenced in the IRC to obtain a 10% or 15% CIT rate for 10–30 years, with a 4–9-year tax holiday; (3) Foreign Tax Credit election — under Circular 151/2014/TT-BTC, claiming a credit for foreign income tax paid on foreign-source income instead of a deduction; (4) group loss-offset is not elective; there is no consolidated tax-payroll regime, except statutory tax-loss carry-forward upon merger/consolidation (5 years). Hybrid arrangement deductions are denied or recharacterized by the transfer-pricing substance rules of Decree 132/2020/ND-CP; no separate election exists to legitimately use a hybrid mismatch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero for corporate enterprises: a foreign-invested LLC must be VAT-registered from its first day of legal existence irrespective of turnover, and must file VAT declarations monthly (turnover above 50 billion VND/year) or quarterly (turnover at or below 50 billion VND/year), including nil returns during the pre-revenue stage. Non-resident suppliers of digital/ad-tech services without a domestic establishment must register for Foreign Contractor Tax via https://etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn from the first Vietnamese-source receipt; the turnover exemptions (100,000,000 VND/year for households/individuals, raised to 200,000,000 VND/year under Law 48/2024/QH15) do not apply to LLCs or registered enterprises.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT on exported ad-tech services is claimable under Article 9 of Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC only when all of the following are cumulatively established: (i) the programmatic advertisement/SSP/DSP platform services are supplied to a foreign contracting party (foreign advertiser, foreign ad agency, or foreign ad exchange) under a written service contract; (ii) the services are physically consumed outside Vietnam — evidenced by ad-server logs, publisher geolocation data, campaign delivery reports, and the absence of Vietnamese user impressions; (iii) payment is received from abroad via a licensed Vietnamese bank in convertible foreign currency and is repatriated within the statutory deadline; and (iv) the services are not on the prohibited list and are not considered domestic intermediary advertising (advertising services that sell goods/services to Vietnamese audiences are domestic supply at 10%). If the platform sells impressions to Vietnamese publishers or local advertisers, the fees attributable to that Vietnamese inventory are domestic revenue at 10% and must be recorded in separate invoices from the zero-rated offshore revenue. When 0% applies, the LLC is entitled to input VAT credits and refunds for VAT incurred on costs directly attributable to the export turnover, and refund claims are prioritized semi-annually/annually under Article 72 of Law 38/2019/QH14.

  • Export-service e-invoice (0% VAT rate line; distinctive export invoice notation)
  • Written service contract with the foreign ad exchange/SSP/DSP/agency partner containing the export-consumption clause and payment terms
  • Service delivery certificate / ad-server campaign performance logs evidencing the audience impressions and usage outside Vietnam
  • Foreign buyer's certificate of tax residence or business registration / trade registry excerpt
  • Bank inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MTN / foreign exchange voucher) showing receipt of payment from the foreign buyer through a licensed Vietnamese bank
  • Customs export declaration and bill of lading — required only if tangible goods (e.g., ad-tech hardware/media kits) are bundled; for pure digital services, these are not applicable

All enterprises must issue electronic invoices (hóa đơn điện tử) under Decree 123/2020/ND-CP effective 1 July 2022, using GDT-certified software or certified e-invoice service providers integrated via API to the GDT e-invoice system at https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn. Every invoice data record must be transmitted to the GDT within 24 hours of issuance. Standard B2B invoices do not require a physical seal, but must contain a digital signature and QR code, and the GDT validates the record and returns an acceptance/error acknowledgment. Taxpayers classified as high-risk, newly formed, or using point-of-sale/cash-register e-invoices must obtain a pre-issued tax-authority code (mã của cơ quan thuế) generated in near-real-time via the API before the invoice is legally delivered. POS/retail transaction data is required to be transmitted synchronously to the GDT in phases from 1 September 2023 through 2024. Failure to transmit within the mandated window is treated as failure to issue an invoice, with penalty up to 20,000,000 VND per occurrence and forced use of pre-coded invoices.

The GDT and the State Bank of Vietnam cross-check e-invoice zero-rated declarations with FX inflow records and CIT returns. If an auditor recharacterizes the invoice as a domestic advertising service (e.g., the campaign actually reached Vietnamese users, or the foreign contract is found to be artificial or back-to-back), the full turnover is re-taxed at 10% VAT, input VAT refunds already obtained are clawed back, and penalties apply: (1) an administrative penalty of 1 to 3 times the evaded tax amount (or 20% of the understated tax for non-fraudulent bookkeeping errors); (2) late-payment interest at 0.03% per day retroactive to the original due date; (3) forfeiture or suspension of CIT/VAT incentive treatment for the audited period; (4) criminal liability under Penal Code Article 200 for tax evasion where the evaded/fraudulently refunded amount exceeds 100,000,000 VND — individuals face fines up to 4,500,000,000 VND and/or imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years; the commercial legal entity faces fines up to 10,000,000,000 VND and suspension of operations for up to 3 years; and (5) administrative coercion measures, forced disclosure of the violation on the GDT portal, and mandatory use of pre-coded tax-authority invoices for 12–24 months.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Viet Nam maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with a partially convertible capital account. Current account transactions, including cross-border payments for software, advertising, royalties, management fees and short-term trade credit, are convertible in practice through SBV-licensed credit institutions, provided the underlying transaction is supported by documentation and the account-holding bank is able to record the transaction under the balance-of-payments reporting framework. Capital account transactions are not freely convertible: inbound equity/debt contributions, outbound portfolio investment, intercompany loans and any remittance affecting the assets or liabilities of an FDI enterprise require registration with the SBV or the account-holding bank, a valid Investment Registration Certificate (IRC), Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) and a capital contribution schedule. Profit remittance is permitted only after the annual financial statements are audited, corporate income tax and other fiscal liabilities are settled, and the lawful distributable profit has been determined. Remittance may be made in one or several instalments during the fiscal year, but each remittance must be effected through the foreign currency account and cannot exceed the audited distributable profit net of statutory appropriations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a foreign-invested LLC, there is no mandatory percentage sale of export proceeds to a local bank under current law. However, 100% of export and service export proceeds, including advertising and programmatic platform receipts invoiced to foreign clients, must be repatriated to the entity's foreign currency account held with an SBV-licensed authorised credit institution within 180 days from the date of customs clearance for goods, or from the service completion/collection date for advertising services. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency and used for lawful foreign currency payments without prior conversion, but retaining such proceeds in a bank account outside Viet Nam requires individual SBV approval. Non-compliance can lead to administrative sanctions, suspension of foreign exchange purchase rights and adverse treatment in the bank's compliance rating.

20%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic corporate income tax of 20% already applies at the LLC level; dividends paid by a Vietnamese LLC to foreign shareholders are generally not subject to an additional Vietnamese dividend withholding tax where the dividend is derived from after-CIT profits. However, branch remittance profits of foreign-owned branches are subject to an additional 0.0% to 5.0% branch profit remittance tax, and constructed dividend rules may apply in certain limited circumstances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Prepared Local File and Master File are mandatory if the taxpayer has related-party transactions above VND 30 billion per year, or if the taxpayer's related-party transaction value exceeds 50% of total revenue, or where the total related-party revenue exceeds 50% of total operating expenses. Even below those thresholds, a Vietnamese TP declaration form and contemporaneous documentation may be required on a risk-based basis. Country-by-Country Report thresholds follow Decree 132/2020/ND-CP for groups with consolidated revenue above VND 18 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam applies Economic Substance rules via Decree 132/2020/ND-CP requiring that controlled transactions reflect actual commercial substance, that the entity bears genuine risks and functions, and that profits are commensurate with the value-creation activities undertaken in Vietnam. A Vietnamese LLC acting as a commissionaire or low-risk distributor for group ad-tech functions, with minimal employees, offices, and decision-making, is exposed to recharacterisation, profit adjustment back to Vietnamese substance, and a mandatory 8% interest levy on any tax adjustment. Formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as applied in UAE/Caribbean jurisdictions do not exist under Vietnamese law, but the doctrine of substance is embedded in TP audit practice and anti-avoidance legislation in Decree 132.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Offshore Revenue Diversion

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to 3 times the evaded tax amount under the Tax Administration Law 38/2019/QH14, plus late-payment interest at 0.03% per day; criminal liability for tax evasion exceeding VND 100 million (or VND 300 million with aggravating factors) may result in imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years, or up to 7 years where the evaded amount is exceptionally large.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives may be jointly and severally liable for tax debts incurred during their tenure; deliberate evasion can trigger personal criminal liability, civil restitution of the corporate veil, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and up to 7 years of imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment with upward taxable income correction plus interest at 0.03% per day; administrative penalty of 20% of the adjusted tax underpayment plus late-payment interest. Failure to submit documentation may trigger a penalty of 20% of the tax due and a fixed fine of up to VND 25 million.

Director personal liability: Vietnamese tax authorities may issue demands on directors and legal representatives where the company is dissolved or insolvent without paying adjusted TP liabilities; deliberate TP manipulation with intent may be pursued as tax evasion with imprisonment up to 7 years.
Shell Company Structure and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP with profit adjustment; Interest on adjusted tax at 0.03% per day; administrative fines up to VND 35 million for accounting irregularities; potential criminal tax fraud where manipulated substance facilitates revenue concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal disqualification from serving as legal representatives, travel restriction orders, and criminal prosecution for intentional falsification of corporate records; the corporate veil may be pierced to recover unpaid tax from personal accounts in a deliberate evasion scheme.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanctions under AML Law 2022: fines up to VND 300 million for AML compliance failures and a temporary suspension of corporate registration up to 3 months for serious violations; transfers to sanctioned jurisdictions can trigger trade control penalties and designated entity freezing, custom cooperation sanctions and referral to the Ministry of Public Security for criminal notices.

Director personal liability: Managers and compliance officers face personal fines, travel bans, and criminal liability for willful participation in laundering criminal property or violating international sanctions with penalties including imprisonment up to 10 years and personal financial restitution.
