Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department (CIR), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official e-services portal: https://www.customs.gov.vu

E-government filing portal

Vanuatu imposes no corporate income tax. A foreign entity or International Company (IC) wishing to transact in Vanuatu or to claim input VAT recovery must obtain a VAT registration number by submitting the VAT 1 Application for Registration form to the Department of Customs and Inland Revenue, together with certified constitutional documents, proof of registered agent address in Vanuatu, and a VAT-compliant invoice/record system. An IC incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] is statutorily tax-exempt and prohibited from carrying on business with Vanuatu residents; registration is mandatory only when annual taxable turnover equals or exceeds VUV 10,000,000 or when the IC elects to register to claim export-related input VAT refunds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election exist under Vanuatu law. The International Company (IC) operates under a statutory absolute income-tax exemption; no entity classification election is available or required. A de facto election exists for VAT purposes only: a person may voluntarily register for VAT below the VUV 10,000,000 threshold to obtain input tax credits on export procurement, with registration becoming compulsory upon exceeding the threshold.

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from Vanuatu are zero-rated under Item 1 of the First Schedule to the VAT Act, provided the goods are physically exported within the statutory timeframe, the supplier retains documentary proof of export, and the supply is made to a recipient outside Vanuatu. For an offshore IC engaged in off-price wholesale liquidation of branded overstock garments, zero-rating applies where finished garments are routed through Vanuatu customs for re-export; where goods are sold and shipped directly between third countries without entering Vanuatu, the transaction falls entirely outside the Vanuatu VAT net and no VAT is chargeable. Input VAT on procurement attributable to exported goods is refundable to a registered person.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Vanuatu Customs Form CED)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Vanuatu
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit advice (SWIFT) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and itemized Packing List
  • Original Purchase Order and Supply Contract with the overseas buyer

None. Vanuatu currently imposes no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API data transmission, or digital stamping mandate. Taxpayers must issue paper or PDF tax invoices containing the issuer's name and address, the VAT registration number, the invoice issue date, a description of the supply, the VAT-exclusive amount, the VAT amount at 12.5%, and the total due. Invoices and supporting records must be retained for at least seven years for audit purposes.

Exporters are subject to targeted audits comparing customs export declarations, shipping manifests, and banking records. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to a 25% tax penalty on the understated VAT, interest accruing, and a retrospective clawback of input VAT refunds with a limitation period of up to seven years where fraud or deliberate evasion is established. Criminal prosecution may result in fines of up to VUV 2,000,000 and/or imprisonment for responsible officers. The Commissioner of Customs and Inland Revenue may also cancel the IC's VAT registration and impose deregistration penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vanuatu maintains a liberal but formally regulated convertibility regime under the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 90], administered by the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu through authorised dealer banks. Current account transactions (trade, services, repatriation of profits) are freely convertible without prior approval; capital account injections and liquidation proceeds also move freely with only statistical reporting, no licence and no surrender charge. The Vatu is not an internationally traded currency offshore, yet the elimination of corporate/income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax and remittance tax for International Companies means profit remittance hurdles arise exclusively at bank compliance level: documentation of beneficial ownership, source of funds and underlying trade. Practical convertibility risk exists in correspondent banking de-risking rather than in statutory exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu has no statutory export-proceeds surrender or conversion deadline. There is no requirement for wholesale exporters or re-export traders to convert USD/EUR/AUD receipts into Vatu, nor to repatriate proceeds within a specified window; receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at a licensed authorised dealer. The only obligations are passive: accurate labelling of receipts in the RBV balance-of-payments statistical reporting and, where goods physically pass through Port Vila/Havannah Harbour, submission of the approved customs declaration and the standard offshore goods documentation. As the off-price apparel liquidation is likely conducted on a re-invoicing or transit-trade basis with goods moving from third-country suppliers to third-country buyers, Vanuatu customs and surrender rules will not be triggered.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) - the central bank and foreign exchange administration, operating under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act and the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 90].

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low. Vanuatu-domiciled International Companies are generally rejected by regulated offshore digital EMIs and fintech banking platforms (including UK FCA e-money firms, EU PSD2 payment institutions and US money-transmitter challenger banks) because the jurisdiction carries an elevated FATF/EU monitoring history and the IC structure typically lacks a public UBO register. The VFSC does not issue a cross-border EMI licence of international standing, and the local National Payment Switch enables only domestic VUV card/payment transfers, not outbound trade treasury. EMIs are usable only where the parent group is resident in a top-tier OECD jurisdiction and acts as the contracting treasury entity, with the Vanuatu IC settling via intercompany allocations. This disqualifies EMIs as a primary treasury rail and requires a conventional bank-led architecture.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are executed by SWIFT MT103 from the home-country bank of the shareholder/parent to the IC's VUV/USD operational or escrow account at BRED or ANZ in Port Vila. Remittance must be coded as 'Capital Contribution / Issue of Share Capital' (or 'Shareholder Loan' for intercompany debt) in the purpose field; the bank will record it on the RBV balance-of-payments statistical return. The remitter must match the certificate of incumbency shareholder register, and the IC must supply board resolutions, share certificates, and — for amounts above the bank's EDD threshold (commonly USD 50,000) — source-of-wealth affidavits and the underlying purchase/funding agreement. Since an IC is tax-exempt, there is no stamp duty on the equity issue and no FX surrender; nevertheless, the bank will verify that the funds were not routed through anonymous wallet structures or jurisdictions with active sanctions.Primary payment rail is SWIFT MT103 / MT202 cover for direct supplier remittances from the IC's foreign-currency settlement account; documentary trades are serviced by irrevocable, unconfirmed or confirmed letters of credit (MT700 issue / MT707 amendment / MT752 acknowledgement / MT740 reimbursement) arranged with ANZ or BRED and routed through top-tier correspondent banks in Australia, Singapore or Hong Kong. An IC with no Vanuatu-resident customers and no local delivery has no taxable supplies and is not required to register for VAT (registration threshold: annual turnover exceeding VUV 4,000,000 from Vanuatu supplies). Late lodgement attracts penalty tax of up to 25% of the VAT shortfall plus interest; habitual default allows DCIR to issue a default assessment and initiate customs enforcement proceedings.
Field B - Zero-Rated Exports Value of exported goods evidenced by shipping and customs documentation. Cross-border overstock wholesale shipments are zero-rated where title and risk transfer outside Vanuatu; retain bills of lading, invoices, and payment records. Late lodgement attracts penalty tax of up to 25% of the VAT shortfall plus interest; habitual default allows DCIR to issue a default assessment and initiate customs enforcement proceedings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — Vanuatu has no transfer-pricing legislation, no Local File / Master File obligation, and no statutory threshold expressed in VUV. Best practice nonetheless requires documented arm's-length pricing for intercompany inventory purchases, management fees, and cost recharges to withstand foreign tax authority audits.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu has endorsed the OECD/G20 BEPS standard but has not enacted a comprehensive Economic Substance Regulations statute applicable to ordinary International Companies. The International Companies Act deems an IC non-resident and prohibits it from carrying on business in Vanuatu, holding local real property, or soliciting local investments. Therefore, local substance is typically confined to the registered agent, registered office, and board records. An IC performing 'relevant activities' (notably distribution and service-centre functions in the garment-liquidation trade) must demonstrate genuine management, employees, and premises in its jurisdiction of tax residence to foreign revenue authorities; a letter-box conclusion may result in the foreign jurisdiction treating the IC as transparent and re-attributing the taxable profit to the ultimate shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Foreign-Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Vanuatu criminal liability for dishonest misrepresentation to public authorities; in the owner's home jurisdiction (e.g., US, UK, AU), criminal tax-fraud prosecution, back taxes, interest, and penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false records are personally liable for civil restitution of diverted profits and penalties, may be disqualified from acting as directors, and face criminal prosecution with custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Local remedy: VFSC strike-off or deregistration and disqualification of the registered agent. Foreign remedy: re-characterisation of the IC as a sham, denial of the 0% effective rate, and application of GAAR, CFC, or offshore-fund rules to re-attribute profits.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil; nominee directors face disqualification orders, freezing of personal assets, and secondary liability for the company's unpaid foreign taxes and penalties.
AML / CFT Breach and Undisclosed Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under Vanuatu's Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act No. 1 of 2020: on conviction, a fine up to VUV 100,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 15 years for money laundering; licensed agents are also individually fined for deficient filings.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are criminally liable if knowingly involved; courts may issue travel bans, seizure orders, proceeds-of-crime confiscation, and orders for civil restitution to affected parties.
Breach of International Company Statutory Restrictions

Statutory sanction: Offence under the International Companies Act [Cap 222]: fines imposed on the company and every defaulting officer, revocation of the exempt status, and strike-off by the VFSC with possible re-assessment of waived taxes.

Director personal liability: Each director who authorises the prohibited activity is jointly and severally liable for fines and for taxes re-assessed on the company; repeat offenders face disqualification and, where fraud is proven, imprisonment.
False Filings and Misrepresentation to VFSC

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties for false declarations under the International Companies Act, including fines and imprisonment; cancellation of the company's registration without prejudice to third-party claims.

Director personal liability: The signatory director and the registered agent are personally liable to the VFSC for investigation costs and penalties; individuals may be prohibited from holding corporate offices in Vanuatu and are subject to criminal prosecution.
