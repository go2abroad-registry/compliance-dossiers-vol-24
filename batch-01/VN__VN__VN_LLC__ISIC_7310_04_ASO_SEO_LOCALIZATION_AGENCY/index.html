Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (Tổng cục Thuế) under the Ministry of Finance of the Socialist Republic of Viet Nam. Primary digital portals: eTax electronic tax declaration portal (https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn), National Electronic Invoice Portal (https://hoadondientu.gdt.gov.vn), National Business Registration Portal (https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn), and the Viet Nam Automated Cargo and Port Consolidated System (VNACCS/VCIS) for customs. Provincial Departments of Taxation (Cục Thuế) administer local LLC files; the Large Taxpayers Department manages enterprises classified as large.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI) under the Law on Investment 2020 (Form A.1/A.2). For the ISIC 7310 advertising/agency sector, no conditional investment license is required. Step 2: Submit the enterprise registration dossier through the National Business Registration Portal; the DPI issues the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) within 3-15 working days. The 13-digit enterprise code printed on the ERC is automatically the statutory tax identification number (TIN/MST) and VAT identification number; no separate TIN application is required. Step 3: Within 10 working days of obtaining the ERC, file electronic tax registration records and the VAT method declaration on the eTax portal (Form 01-ĐK-TCT for the taxpayer, Form 02-ĐK-TCT for any branch or permanent establishment in Viet Nam, and Form 05/ĐK-HT for the VAT calculation method election). Step 4: Acquire a digital signature (USB token or remote e-signature) qualified by the Ministry of Information and Communications; register the e-invoice template and e-invoice issuance parameters with the General Department of Taxation. Step 5: Open a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) at a Vietnamese licensed credit institution in the name of the LLC to record equity and loan inflows and to evidence repatriated export earnings. Failure to register within statutory deadlines results in penalties up to VND 25,000,000 for late tax registration plus a provisional tax assessment at the highest applicable rate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. A foreign-invested LLC is a Vietnamese resident enterprise (incorporated in Viet Nam, central management in Viet Nam) and is subject to Corporate Income Tax (CIT) at entity level on its worldwide income at the statutory rate of 20%. The LLC is not a pass-through for Vietnamese tax purposes; there is no check-the-box regime and no taxation at the hand of the foreign members. Dividend distributions to foreign shareholders are generally exempt from further Vietnamese CIT withholding, but payments of interest, royalties, and service fees to offshore related parties remain subject to Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) provisions. Non-resident enterprises deriving Vietnam-source income without a permanent establishment are taxed on a territorial basis by way of FCT withholding; a resident LLC is not eligible for that territorial regime for its own income.

Available election forms

No entity classification election (no pass-through/check-the-box) exists in Vietnamese domestic law; tax personality is determined by the Enterprise Law. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT method election between credit method (compulsory for enterprises with annual revenue from sales and services of VND 1 billion or more, or opting voluntarily if adequate accounting) and direct method on value-added for smaller taxpayers; (ii) CIT incentives: the LLC may elect to claim preferential CIT rates (10% or 15% for up to 10-15 years) and tax holiday incentives if the project is located in an industrial park, economic zone, high-tech zone, or meets investment-scale criteria, subject to DPI/GDC registration; (iii) Double Tax Agreement (DTA) relief: the LLC can elect to claim foreign tax credit under the applicable DTA on its foreign-source income instead of a mere deduction; (iv) loss carry-forward: up to 5 consecutive years, applied in chronological order; (v) transfer pricing documentation: election between the simplified transfer pricing documentation regime for small/medium taxpayers with related-party transactions below VND 30 billion and the full three-tier documentation (master file, local file, country-by-country report) for multinational groups meeting the threshold; (vi) Global Minimum Tax and Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT) under Resolution 107/2023/QH15 for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, effective fiscal year 2024. The ADC/SEO localization agency with typical revenue profile will not normally be captured by Pillar Two unless it belongs to a large international group.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vietnamese dong (VND) is a managed, not fully convertible currency for capital-account purposes. Current-account transactions (payments for services, royalties, trade-related charges) are generally unrestricted and may be settled through licensed banks; capital inflows and outflows are subject to foreign-exchange controls and must be routed through a resident entity’s direct investment capital account (DICA). Profit remittance after corporate income tax, annual foreign-exchange reporting, and audited financials is permissible, but requires a bank-approved remittance dossier, tax clearance certificate, and evidence of capital contribution completion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from services and goods must be repatriated to a foreign-currency account at a licensed Vietnamese credit institution within 180 days from the payment due date (or the date the receivable falls due under the export/service contract if earlier). Under Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN, the current mandatory conversion ratio is 0% — exporters may hold 100% of export proceeds in VND or foreign currency; however, the SBV retains the legal power to impose a temporary mandatory conversion ratio (up to 100%) through official instructions in periods of balance-of-payment stress. The LLC must still file currency purchase/remittance reports with the SBV-derived bank reporting system.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) (Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam) is the sole central bank, money-issuing authority and foreign-exchange regulator. The SBV’s Department of Foreign Exchange Management licenses Vietnamese banks to deal in foreign exchange, issues guidance on current and capital account transactions, and supervises reporting from authorized credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for settlement-only use; Low feasibility as a primary banking or capital-tracking rail. Offshore EMIs such as Wise Business (Wise B2B) and Payoneer are not licensed credit institutions, deposit-takers, or foreign-exchange dealers in Vietnam. They may be used to collect foreign-currency service fees from overseas app-store operators and SEO/ASO clients (e.g., US/EU/SG receiving accounts), then onward-sweep the proceeds by SWIFT/ACH to the LLC’s licensed Vietnamese bank account. Legal constraints: (i) no VND-denominated receiving account may be provided without a local license; (ii) an EMI rail cannot be used for capital injections or profit repatriation, which are reserved for licensed bank DICA/processing; (iii) the flow-through must be supported by the same contract and invoice trail used for underlying revenue; (iv) if an EMI receives payments that originate from Vietnamese resident clients, it would require an intermediary payment-services license under Vietnamese e-payment rules, which is not granted to offshore EMI providers. Accordingly, custody and settlement records should remain at a Vietnamese licensed bank while EMI routes are used only as value-concentration tools.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign parent investors must remit equity contributions from a bank account in the investor’s direct name to the LLC’s Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) at a licensed Vietnamese bank via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for correspondent settlement). The payment must be tagged with the purpose code for 'Foreign Direct Investment Capital Contribution' and must cite the ERC/IRC number and the LLC’s registered capital line item. Before the bank executes, the LLC must present: (i) a notarized board/shareholder resolution on capital contribution; (ii) investor bank account statement showing the remitting account; (iii) the capital schedule in the ERC/IRC; (iv) tax registration certificate; (v) if the contribution exceeds registered capital, a new investment-registration amendment. The DICA then becomes the source of record for local currency conversion into VND at the interbank exchange rate. The bank will require that the purpose code and beneficiary legal name exactly match the LLC’s records; the use of Payoneer/Wise routes for capital contribution is not compliant and will be rejected. After capital is received, the LLC must notify the Department of Planning and Investment within 30 days and update the General Department of Taxation’s registration database.Operational outflows for contractor fees, advertising purchases, SEO tool subscriptions, cloud/hosting, and app-store networking settlements should be cleared through the licensed Vietnamese bank using SWIFT MT103 messages for direct outgoing wire transfers; SWIFT MT202 (COV) should be used where correspondent banks execute back-to-back covers. For physical-goods procurement, commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) or documentary collections remain permissible, but the LLC’s service-based disbursements will rely almost entirely on bank-to-bank wires. The supporting treasury dossier includes a signed service agreement, a valid foreign-currency invoice, proof of performance (deliverable/status report), and, when required, a Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT) withholding calculation (typically 1%–5% VAT and 5%–10% CIT equivalents depending on service classification; treaty relief may be available under Vietnam’s double-taxation agreements). Payments for services performed by Vietnamese resident subcontractors should be made in VND through the local clearing system (NAPAS/ACH), preserving FX for genuine cross-border commitments.FX forward contracts (delivery-based) executed with an SBV-licensed credit institution on a documented commercial exposureFX swaps (spot/forward combinations) for managing liquidity mismatches in USD/VND and other major trade currenciesCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) where a real underlying foreign-currency liability or receivables portfolio existsFX options (purchased put/call, and risk-reversal structures) with a licensed bank, provided the notional is supported by the approved annual treasury risk policyNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs): not permissible as a domestic Vietnamese banking product for VND; offshore NDF hedges may be considered only outside the SBV supervisory perimeter and, because of regulatory and settlement ambiguity, are not recommended as the primary hedging rail for this LLC

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for settlement-only use; Low feasibility as a primary banking or capital-tracking rail. Offshore EMIs such as Wise Business (Wise B2B) and Payoneer are not licensed credit institutions, deposit-takers, or foreign-exchange dealers in Vietnam. They may be used to collect foreign-currency service fees from overseas app-store operators and SEO/ASO clients (e.g., US/EU/SG receiving accounts), then onward-sweep the proceeds by SWIFT/ACH to the LLC’s licensed Vietnamese bank account. Legal constraints: (i) no VND-denominated receiving account may be provided without a local license; (ii) an EMI rail cannot be used for capital injections or profit repatriation, which are reserved for licensed bank DICA/processing; (iii) the flow-through must be supported by the same contract and invoice trail used for underlying revenue; (iv) if an EMI receives payments that originate from Vietnamese resident clients, it would require an intermediary payment-services license under Vietnamese e-payment rules, which is not granted to offshore EMI providers. Accordingly, custody and settlement records should remain at a Vietnamese licensed bank while EMI routes are used only as value-concentration tools.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: VND 50 billion (approx. USD 2.1 million) in annual related-party revenue triggers the mandatory Local File requirement. The Master File requirement applies when the consolidated group revenue exceeds VND 75,000 billion (approx. USD 3.2 billion). Country-by-Country Report is required when the ultimate parent group is required to file CbCR and the Vietnamese entity meets the VND 75,000 billion consolidated revenue threshold. Even below VND 50 billion, TP documentation may be requested if the enterprise has sustained losses for two consecutive years or related-party transactions are material.

Economic substance scope: Vietnam applies the substance-over-form doctrine via Decree 132/2018/ND-CP and Circular 132/2018/TT-BTC. Transactions lacking economic substance and primarily for profit shifting may be disregarded or recharacterized. The Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the MLI denies treaty benefits to arrangements with a principal purpose of treaty shopping. For an ASO/SEO agency, economic substance requires actual local personnel, office premises, operational evidence (campaign reports, client communications, subcontractor agreements), and arm's-length pricing. Vietnam does not have an ESR law analogous to the UAE, but substance checks are embedded in TP audits and CIT examinations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Article 200 of the Penal Code 2015 (as amended), tax evasion of VND 100 million or more is punishable by 3 months to 7 years' imprisonment. Administrative fines range from 20% to 300% of evaded tax under Law on Tax Administration No. 38/2019/QH14, plus 0.03% daily late payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and chief accountants who falsify returns face criminal prosecution, imprisonment, joint and several civil liability for tax arrears and fines, travel bans, and asset seizure. The corporate veil may be pierced where the LLC is an instrument of fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Upward CIT adjustments, disallowance of interest deductions exceeding 30% of EBITDA (related-party loans), 20% to 200% administrative fines on understated tax, 0.03% daily interest, and non-deductibility of undeclared payments. Fines for TP documentation failure range from VND 12M–30M.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for tax debts if pricing manipulation constitutes intentional evasion, and may face civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment if the TP manipulation is criminalized.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may recharacterize the entity, reattribute income to the offshore principal, impose full CIT at 20% plus late interest, levy administrative fines under Decree 88/2019/ND-CP, suspended tax code, and AML fines up to VND 300 million under Law No. 02/2022/QH15.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face money laundering charges under Article 324 Penal Code with imprisonment of 1–10 years, asset forfeiture, travel bans, and personal civil liability.
Foreign Contractor Tax / Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The LLC is liable for unpaid FCT, fines up to the withheld amount, 0.03% daily interest, fines up to VND 24M under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP, and potential CIT gross-up.

Director personal liability: Willful non-withholding may be prosecuted as tax evasion; directors may face imprisonment, civil liability, and travel bans.
AML / Payment Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines of VND 50M–300M per AML violation, State Bank of Vietnam enforcement, freezing of accounts, criminal liability under Article 324 Penal Code with imprisonment of 1–10 years and fines up to VND 100M.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in structuring payments or concealing transactions face personal criminal liability, asset forfeiture, travel bans, disqualification from management roles, and extradition risks.
