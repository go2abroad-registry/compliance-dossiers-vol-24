Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Department of Taxation (GDT) under the Ministry of Finance of the Socialist Republic of Viet Nam; national tax administration portal: https://thuedientu.gdt.gov.vn (Electronic Tax Portal for e-filing/e-payment; corporate tax registration via https://dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn for enterprise registration integration).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested limited liability company must first obtain an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment (DPI). Following ERC issuance, the tax authority automatically issues a 10-digit or 13-digit Tax Identification Number (MST) through the business registration database. The company must then complete tax registration dossiers, including the registration of the head office and any dependent branches/sub-offices, via the electronic tax portal or directly at the provincial tax office. For value-added tax (VAT) purposes, the same MST serves as the VAT identification number; no separate VAT ID is issued. Foreign entities without a permanent establishment that derive Vietnamese-source income may need a tax registration code through the withholding agent or must follow the contractor withholding tax regime, depending on the structure. A licensed electronic money institution must also update its tax registration file to reflect the State Bank of Viet Nam (SBV) e-money license and any licensed payment intermediary activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Viet Nam does not maintain a check-the-box pass-through election comparable to the U.S. regime. An LLC is always subject to entity-level corporate income tax (CIT) at 20% on worldwide taxable income if resident, or on Vietnamese-source income if non-resident. There are no hybrid mismatch elections for entities. Taxpayers may elect the VAT calculation method (deduction method or direct method) in accordance with Law on Value-Added Tax, subject to statutory conditions: the deduction method is generally mandatory for licensed electronic money institutions, as they are deemed to be engaged in taxable banking and financial ancillary services. Certain preferential regimes are available by election where the taxpayer qualifies for CIT incentives (e.g., high-technology enterprises, new investment projects), but this is a concession, not a classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to enterprises: every entity established under the Law on Enterprises, including foreign-invested LLCs, is required to register, declare, and pay VAT regardless of turnover level. Foreign suppliers of digital services and other cross-border electronic services without a Vietnamese establishment are required to register, declare, and pay VAT under the foreign supplier e-registration portal (etaxvn.gdt.gov.vn) once they derive taxable turnover from Vietnam; specific threshold rules may apply under official guidance, but the general rule is mandatory registration for any taxable activity by an incorporated Vietnamese entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods and services supplied for export from Viet Nam, the 0% VAT rate applies if the following cumulative conditions are met: (i) the goods are actually exported out of Vietnam, or services are supplied to and consumed outside Vietnam (including services directly used for export production); (ii) the taxpayer holds a VAT invoice for the exported goods or services; and (iii) the taxpayer possesses a complete customs dossier, including the customs export declaration, a bill of lading or equivalent transport document, and an export payment/remittance document proving the consideration was received from a foreign party in a convertible foreign currency. For offshore services provided by an electronic money institution (e.g., virtual IBAN issuance or payment processing to non-resident merchants), the service must be consumed outside Vietnam and supplied to a foreign customer; however, banking, financial and payment intermediary services are expressly excluded from the zero-rating entitlement under the Law on VAT and are instead VAT-exempt (subject to output-input VAT treatment), meaning no 0% VAT is available for core e-money issuance or wallet management services unless the service falls within a specific export-service category approved by the tax authority and is performed for a foreign party with foreign consumption (e.g., certain software licensing or technical services, if separately carried out and properly documented). The general rule for financial services in Vietnam is exemption, not zero-rating; the electronic money institution must conduct a detailed service-by-service analysis and apply separate accounting between exempt financial services and potentially zero-rated export services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Tờ khai hải quan điện tử) duly lodged and processed by Customs Authority of Viet Nam, with valid confirmation of export clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / other transport document evidencing physical export movement of goods
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the Vietnamese exporting company
  • Sales contract / purchase order with the foreign customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign currency bank statement proving the receipt of export proceeds from abroad; for service exports, an offshore payment receipt and written documents evidencing foreign consumption (e.g., service agreement and certificate of use by foreign party)
  • For goods exported through export processing enterprises (EPEs) or CMT/processing arrangements, additional documentation under Circular 219/2013/TT-BTC and customs transit documents as required

Viet Nam mandates electronic invoicing with a mandatory invoice issuance code (mã của cơ quan thuế) for businesses, including licensed financial institutions. The electronic invoice data must be transmitted in real time (or within one business day, depending on the invoice type) to the General Department of Taxation through the E-Invoice system. The system applies a digital stamping/clearance process: before an e-invoice can be legally used, the tax authority issues a tax authority invoice code after validating the invoice data via API integration. The invoice data format is XML per the official e-invoice technical specification. Electronic money institutions issuing virtual IBANs must integrate their invoicing systems with the tax administration's API and ensure compliance with Decree 123/2020/ND-CP and Circular 78/2021/TT-BTC on e-invoices, including authentication by digital signature. Fiscal data clearance is not a continuous transaction-level real-time control as in Italy or Hungary, but pre-clearance coding is a statutory condition for validity.

For an electronic money institution, the General Department of Taxation and customs authorities perform post-clearance audits focused on fraudulent export VAT zero-rating claims. Penalties include: (i) retroactive VAT reassessment at the 10% standard rate (or non-deductible treatment for exempt financial services) for any improperly claimed zero-rated export treatment; (ii) interest on underpaid tax at the rate applicable to overdue tax obligations (currently 0.03% per day on the late-payment amount); (iii) administrative fines up to 20 times the tax under-declared amount in egregious cases, or up to VND 200 million per violation for non-compliant invoicing or failure to use tax-authority-coded e-invoices; (iv) criminal prosecution for tax evasion or smuggling if fraud is established, with penalties including fines up to VND 4.5 billion or imprisonment under the Penal Code for the statutory responsible officers; (v) clawback of refunds already paid, with mandatory interest, and suspension or revocation of VAT refund eligibility for 12 months in certain cases; (vi) for VAT refund fraud involving counterfeit export dossiers, the enterprise may be blacklisted on the tax authority risk management system and subjected to enhanced pre-refund audits for up to five years. The written authorization of the Viet Nam Chamber of Commerce? No, the GDT expressly prohibits refund claims based solely on bank statements, requiring authenticated customs declarations and FX remittance certificates; any mismatch among the customs declaration, bill of lading, and inward remittance certificate leads to automatic rejection and penalty.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Vietnam has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, hence VND is convertible for all current-account transactions, including profit remittance, royalty payments, management fees, and service imports, subject to presentation of supporting documentation to a licensed bank (invoices, tax clearance certificates, audited financial statements). Capital-account convertibility remains restricted. FDI equity injections must flow exclusively through a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) opened at an SBV-licensed bank pursuant to Circular 15/2015/TT-NHNN as amended by Circular 02/2023/TT-NHNN. Foreign commercial borrowings require SBV registration under Decree 96/2024/ND-CP and must be drawn, serviced, and repaid through the DICA. Outbound portfolio investment by a Vietnamese EMI is severely restricted and effectively requires case-by-case SBV/MoF authorization. Dividend and profit remittance to the foreign corporate shareholder is lawful once annually after final CIT settlement, based on legally audited financial statements and retained earnings available for distribution; there is no dividend withholding tax on distributions to foreign corporate shareholders, but distributions to foreign individual shareholders attract a 5% final withholding rate after CIT.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Decree 70/2014/ND-CP (as amended) and the SBV's prevailing FX management directives, all export receipts of goods and services, including the EMI's cross-border service fees and e-money settlement commissions, must be repatriated to Vietnam and fully credited to a foreign currency account at an SBV-licensed bank within 30 calendar days of the contractual payment due date, or of the actual receipt date if earlier. The mandatory conversion-to-VND surrender ratio applicable to exporters is currently set at 0% by SBV's periodic FX policy decisions, but the DICA holder must still sell foreign currency to the licensed bank at the prevailing interbank rate whenever required by an SBV re-imposition of a positive surrender ratio (historically 30% under earlier regimes, with conversion mandated within 15 working days of a re-imposition order). The statutory timeline is not automatically waived for processing-zone enterprises; only documentary relaxation applies to EPEs, while the EMI is treated as an ordinary services exporter for these rules.

Central bank supervision: State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) - Ngân hàng Nhà nước Việt Nam, operating under the Law on the State Bank of Vietnam No. 46/2010/QH12, the Foreign Exchange Ordinance No. 28/2005/PL-UBTVQH11, Decree 70/2014/ND-CP as amended by Decree 89/2019/ND-CP, and Decree 96/2024/ND-CP on external borrowing and lending. SBV is the sole licensing authority for banks and non-bank payment intermediaries under Decree 52/2024/ND-CP on non-cash payments and exercises exclusive FX administration, balance-of-payments statistics, and regulatory reporting authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM for direct offshore digital EMI operation without local licensing, and HIGH only for a locally incorporated and SBV-licensed non-bank payment intermediary. Legal constraints: no EU-style passporting exists in Vietnam; an offshore EMI (e.g., an EU/Singapore/U.K. digital bank) cannot issue virtual IBANs or hold VND e-money on behalf of Vietnamese customers without a subsidiary and an SBV license under Decree 52/2024/ND-CP. Foreign equity participation in a licensed payment intermediary service provider is capped at 49% of charter capital absent an international treaty exception. The offshore EMI may only act as a correspondent bank, a white-label technology vendor, or an authorized agent of the local licensee, provided it has no direct customer relationship and no domiciled VND liabilities. Any cross-border multi-currency virtual IBAN product must be conducted through the local licensee's foreign currency account and clear in the offshore EMI's jurisdiction, not through the domestic customer's e-wallet.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing follows a six-stage protocol: (1) obtain the Enterprise Registration Certificate and Investment Registration Certificate from the provincial Department of Planning and Investment or Export Processing Zone authority; (2) open a Direct Investment Capital Account (DICA) at an SBV-licensed Tier-1 bank under Circular 02/2023/TT-NHNN; (3) the foreign investor remits equity in USD/EUR via SWIFT MT103 using the standard payment narrative 'Direct investment capital contribution to [Company Name] under IRC No. [ID]'; (4) the Vietnamese receiving bank applies the SBV Balance-of-Payments purpose code for direct investment equity (capital contribution) and credits the DICA in foreign currency; (5) upon each drawdown, the EMI submits a utilization plan, and foreign currency may be converted to VND at the licensed bank's corporate exchange rate or held in the DICA for permitted foreign currency expenditures including import settlement, offshore loan repayment, and staff payments; (6) all subsequent changes to capital or shareholders require that the DICA be credited before any further disbursement, and no inbound equity wire may be credited to a general operational account. Third-party finance from non-shareholder offshore lenders must be preceded by SBV registration under Decree 96/2024/ND-CP and must use the DICA loan disbursement sub-account.Outbound trade and service settlements are executed over the following clearing rails: (i) domestic VND disbursements to merchants and e-wallet counterparties settle real-time on NAPAS (National Payment Corporation of Vietnam) ACH rail, either via the acquiring bank's direct participation or through a Tier-1 partner bank; (ii) cross-border EUR/USD service fee payments and virtual IBAN payouts use SWIFT MT103 messages through the EMI's NOSTRO accounts at correspondent banks, with each instruction carrying the correct local FX purpose narrative and value-date discipline; (iii) high-value import settlement for infrastructure procurement (e.g., hardware wallets, data center services) is executed via documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600, with issuance on MT700, amendments on MT707, and reimbursement between the Vietnamese issuing bank and the beneficiary's confirming bank over SWIFT; (iv) SEPA Credit transfers for European merchant payouts are originated by the EMI via the banking partner's pain.001 ISO 20022 message converted to an SCT in the EU/EEA clearing area, clearing through TARGET2/EBA CLEARING infrastructure; and (v) repatriation of profits, foreign loan repayment, and dividend distributions debited from the DICA with outward MT103 and supporting tax/audit documentation.Spot FX transaction (delivery in VND or foreign currency within T+2 per Circular 02/2021/TT-NHNN)FX Forward contract with licensed bank, maximum tenor 365 days, requiring documentary evidence of underlying current or capital transaction exposureFX Swap combining spot and forward legs between VND and USD/EUR, permitted tenor per SBV market convention and circular limitsCross-currency swap (CCS) between VND and foreign currency executed with an SBV-licensed credit institution, subject to underlying loan or project cash flow documentationFX Option (European-style call/put) with licensed bank, permitted only where the notional, strike, and tenor correspond to a genuine commercial or financial exposure of the EMI and are backed by formal hedging policy approved by the Board of MembersNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are expressly NOT permitted for domestic corporates under the current SBV regulatory framework; all hedging must be deliverable and settled within Vietnam

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM for direct offshore digital EMI operation without local licensing, and HIGH only for a locally incorporated and SBV-licensed non-bank payment intermediary. Legal constraints: no EU-style passporting exists in Vietnam; an offshore EMI (e.g., an EU/Singapore/U.K. digital bank) cannot issue virtual IBANs or hold VND e-money on behalf of Vietnamese customers without a subsidiary and an SBV license under Decree 52/2024/ND-CP. Foreign equity participation in a licensed payment intermediary service provider is capped at 49% of charter capital absent an international treaty exception. The offshore EMI may only act as a correspondent bank, a white-label technology vendor, or an authorized agent of the local licensee, provided it has no direct customer relationship and no domiciled VND liabilities. Any cross-border multi-currency virtual IBAN product must be conducted through the local licensee's foreign currency account and clear in the offshore EMI's jurisdiction, not through the domestic customer's e-wallet.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File (Form 01, 02, 03/TK TNCN is not applicable; the relevant forms are 01/QLGN and 02/QLGN under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP) are required if the taxpayer has related-party transactions and its total related-party revenue is above VND 50 billion (approximately USD 2 million) and/or total related-party transaction value is above VND 30 billion. Additionally, the safe-harbour threshold: taxpayers with related-party revenue below VND 30 billion or with no related-party transactions must still prepare a TP risk assessment. For commonly controlled payments such as management fees, royalties, and interest, the standard country-by-country reporting threshold applies where the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals at least VND 18,000 billion (about EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: Viet Nam applies Economic Substance Regulations through Decree 132/2020/ND-CP: transactions are recharacterized when the arrangement lacks commercial rationale and economic substance, especially in the EMI segment where intragroup SaaS fees, platform royalties, and digital wallet reserve management fees are subject to audit. For an electronic money institution, the absence of local decision-making, technology development, and risk management staff may lead to adjustments. Specifically, the QD 132 regulations require the taxpayer's business plan, employment of personnel, and asset ownership to be aligned with functions performed, risks assumed, and assets used. The State Bank of Viet Nam also imposes a physical license requirement and prudent reserve requirements; an EMI that merely collects funds and channels them to a related treasury company will be recharacterized as a conduit. Consequently, such a company may lose the benefit of deductions for intercompany commissions and royalties and may face additional CIT at 20% plus penalty interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP and the Law on Tax Administration 2019, additional CIT assessed at 20%; a penalty up to 1.5 times the evaded tax for failure to apply arm's length pricing; late payment interest at 0.03% per day. In cases of intentionally mispriced IP royalties exceeding VND 100 billion, criminal tax evasion under Penal Code 100/2015/QH13 (Article 200) carries fines up to VND 4.5 billion and imprisonment for up to 7 years for responsible individuals.

Director personal liability: General director and legal representative can be held personally liable for filing certified tax returns containing false entries; civil restitution to the tax authority; travel ban (exit suspension) applies to directors of delinquent taxpayers across the entire Vietnamese border; imprisonment up to 7 years for intentional evasion when the amount exceeds VND 100 million or where both large acts and complicity are proven.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may recharacterize the entire payment base and deny all intercompany deductions, impose a deemed profit margin under Decree 132/2020/ND-CP causing effective CIT of up to 20% on gross revenue, and revoke the enterprise registration through the licensing authority. The State Bank of Viet Nam may revoke the instant-payment/e-money license for failure to satisfy organizational and legal conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors face administrative fines from VND 20 million to VND 100 million under Decree 98/2021/ND-CP for corporate governance failures; the corporate veil is pierced if the entity is shown to be an agent of the foreign parent: personal restitution for the full underpaid tax, prohibition from holding managerial positions in financial institutions for up to 3 years, and a travel ban until all liabilities are settled.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Anti-Money Laundering Law 2022 (effective 1 March 2023), administrative sanctions include fines up to VND 200 million for failing to report STRs; criminal money-laundering under Penal Code Article 324 applies when the underlying funds are the proceeds of crime: fines up to VND 3 billion and imprisonment up to 15 years for responsible persons. The entity's license is revoked for serious or repeated violations.

Director personal liability: The AML compliance officer and the general director are personally liable for failure to implement an internal AML program; can be subject to criminal imprisonment for money-laundering complicity, voidance of employment contracts, restitution of the laundered amounts seized via criminal confiscation, and entry into the public registry of prohibited financial-services managers. Personal travel bans apply to the chairman and directors pending the criminal investigation.
Foreign Exchange Control / Illegal Capital Movement

Statutory sanction: Under the Law on Foreign Exchange Administration 2005 and Decree 88/2019/ND-CP, fines range from VND 100 million to VND 200 million for operating a payment service without an SBV license; the transaction is void and funds may be confiscated; repeated violations lead to criminal penalty for illegal foreign-exchange brokerage under Article 207 of the Penal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to personal criminal prosecution for unlawful cross-border transfers, travel bans, confiscation of personal bank accounts, civil restitution for exchange losses, and imprisonment up to 10 years for organizing currency trading without permission.
Pillar Two GloBE Non-Compliance (for covered multinational EMI groups)

Statutory sanction: Under Resolution 107/2023/QH15 and Decree 132/2024/ND-CP, penalties for non-filing of the qualified domestic minimum top-up tax return can reach VND 40 million plus 0.03% daily interest on unpaid top-up tax; for false statements to reduce the top-up, an administrative penalty of up to 1.0 times the omitted tax; the general tax administration penalty regime applies.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Vietnamese constituent entity are responsible for true and accurate GloBE filings; a deliberate false certification exposes the legal representative to a warning or fines up to VND 20 million, and where it generates a false refund claim, criminal tax-evasion liability under Penal Code Article 200 may attach with imprisonment up to 3 years. Travel bans are applied until the top-up tax is settled.
