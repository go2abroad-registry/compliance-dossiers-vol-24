Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Vanuatu Customs and Inland Revenue Department (VIRD), Ministry of Finance and Economic Management; official e-portal: www.gov.vu / VIRD e-Services portal (tax administration services), with company registration administered separately by the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC).

E-government filing portal

An International Company (IC) incorporated under the International Companies Act [Cap 222] is wholly tax-exempt and is not issued a domestic income-tax TIN; its juridical identifier is the VFSC Certificate of Incorporation number. If the IC voluntarily registers for VAT (to recover input VAT on local studio leases, equipment, utilities, or contracted VFX labor), the foreign beneficial owner must file Form VAT-01 with VIRD together with the certificate of incorporation, board resolution authorizing registration, registered office address, and a statutory declaration confirming non-resident ownership and the absence of Vanuatu-source domestic activity. VIRD issues a VAT Registration Certificate bearing a 9-digit VAT ID. Foreign entities (non-IC) engaged in taxable activity in Vanuatu must register for VAT once taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold, and must also obtain a Business Licence from the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission / municipal licensing authority.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone: The IC is ring-fenced as 100% exempt from all Vanuatu taxes, including corporate income tax (none exists), capital gains tax (none exists), dividend/interest/royalty withholding tax (none exists), and stamp duty on share transfers. The IC is prohibited from trading with Vanuatu residents, holding land interests, and carrying on business from a physical Vanuatu office; all substantive commercial activity for 3D animation/VFX export must occur outside Vanuatu or with non-resident counterparties. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The IC is ring-fenced as 100% exempt from all Vanuatu taxes, including corporate income tax (none exists), capital gains tax (none exists), dividend/interest/royalty withholding tax (none exists), and stamp duty on share transfers. The IC is prohibited from trading with Vanuatu residents, holding land interests, and carrying on business from a physical Vanuatu office; all substantive commercial activity for 3D animation/VFX export must occur outside Vanuatu or with non-resident counterparties. This is an entity-level permanent statutory exemption, not a pass-through election, and it coexists with the fact that Vanuatu's ordinary domestic tax system also imposes no corporate income tax on resident companies.

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no US-style check-the-box or OECD hybrid-mismatch election regime. Available elections: (1) election to incorporate as an International Company under ss. 5-10 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222], conferring statutory tax-exempt status; (2) voluntary VAT registration election under the VAT Act (Form VAT-01) allowing an exporter to register below the compulsory threshold to claim input credits; (3) election under the VAT Act No. 11 of 2024 for a non-established/supplier-of-imported-services to self-assess reverse-charge VAT where applicable; (4) election under s. 47 of the IC Act to maintain books, records, and share capital in any foreign currency; (5) no hybrid mismatch election is recognized, but a 0% effective tax rate eliminates any hybrid duduction/offset outcome in practice.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VUV 2,500,000 (Vanuatu Vatu) of annual taxable turnover within any consecutive 12-month period (legacy Cap 246 threshold, carried forward under commencement regulations of the 2024 VAT Act); a person commencing business must register if expected first-3-month turnover exceeds VUV 2,000,000. An offshore IC that exclusively supplies zero-rated animation/VFX services to non-resident clients is not compulsorily registrable, but may elect voluntary registration for input credit recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to (a) exported goods supported by customs export documentation; (b) services supplied to a non-resident person who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply and where the benefit of the service is not enjoyed in Vanuatu (export of services); and (c) services physically performed entirely outside Vanuatu. Commercial 3D animation and CGI/VFX production for overseas studios qualifies as a zero-rated export of services where: (i) the contracting client is a non-resident and not a Vanuatu VAT-registered person; (ii) the final rendered media files are delivered digitally to a foreign recipient; (iii) the intellectual property and commercial exploitation rights are held offshore; and (iv) consideration is payable from outside Vanuatu in foreign currency. No customs clearance is required for service zero-rating; substantiation is purely documentary and must be retained for the statutory audit retention period.

  • Goods (if any physical media shipped): Validated Vanuatu Customs Export Declaration (Form CED-01) with electronic lodgement reference, commercial invoice, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, packing list
  • Services: Executed international service contract identifying the non-Vanuatu principal place of business and ISIC 5911 scope; itemized zero-rated invoice bearing the IC VAT ID (if registered); inward payment evidence (SWIFT MT103 or Wise B2B statement) proving foreign-source settlement; digital delivery log with file hashes and geolocation metadata of the 3D renders delivered to the overseas studio; VIRD zero-rated supply schedule (Form VAT-21) where applicable
  • Supporting corporate evidence: Certificate of Incorporation, register of non-resident shareholders/beneficial owners, and annual return confirming no Vanuatu domestic trading activity

The VAT Act No. 11 of 2024 mandates a phased electronic invoicing regime. VIRD may designate 'prescribed taxpayers' (generally by annual turnover class) who must issue invoices exclusively through VIRD-approved electronic billing systems or fiscal devices integrated via certified APIs, transmitting invoice data to the VIRD central fiscal clearance platform and receiving a digital stamp/QR-code token for each transaction in near-real-time (within 24 hours of issuance). Non-prescribed taxpayers may issue manually numbered PDF/paper invoices but must report digital VAT returns via the VIRD e-portal monthly or quarterly. Detailed API specifications and the phased go-live timetable are set out in the Tax Administration (Electronic Invoicing) Regulation 2025; cross-border service suppliers outside Vanuatu are outside the domestic e-invoicing cleanse unless reverse-charge registration applies.

Vanuatu's Exchange Control Act [Cap 45] imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation obligation; the 180-day figure serves only as the firms internal compliance benchmark for invoicing-to-settlement discipline. VIRD audit scrutiny on zero-rated export claims focuses on proof of non-resident recipient status, actual offshore exploitation, and full traceability of inward FX. Under the VAT Act No. 11 of 2024, a false zero-rating claim triggers a tax shortfall penalty of up to 200% of the VAT under-claimed, a further late-payment penalty of up to 50%, and statutory interest on the unpaid amount. Criminal sanctions include fines of up to VUV 10,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 10 years for knowingly furnishing false export documents to VIRD. Retrospective reassessment may be made for up to 5 years (extended to 7 years for fraudulent conduct), and directors/beneficial owners of an IC are jointly and severally liable where the IC knowingly misrepresented its zero-rated export status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) is convertible for current international transactions under Vanuatu's IMF Article VIII obligations, and domestic law does not impose a formal distinction between capital-account and current-account convertibility. Profit remittance by non-resident shareholders of an International Company is not statutorily restricted; however, in practice the remitting bank requires a VFSC-issued tax clearance or tax-exempt status certificate, a certificate of incumbency, a board resolution authorizing the dividend, and proof that the underlying profits were earned from non-Vanuatu sources. Deep offshore convertibility is limited by the thin domestic interbank market, the lack of a deliverable offshore VUV market, and the global correspondent-bank de-risking environment; therefore the recommended treasury currency is USD, with VUV conversion reserved for local operating expenses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Vanuatu imposes no statutory surrender regime and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Proceeds from exported commercial 3D animation services may be retained indefinitely in VUV or any foreign currency in an account at an authorized dealer. The Exchange Control Act [Cap. 92] only requires declarative reporting or licensing approval for specific capital transactions by residents, and the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] expressly exempts non-resident-owned International Companies from exchange-control licensing for offshore receipts, provided the funds flow through an RBV-authorized banking channel.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV), established under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act [Cap. 125], is the sole central monetary authority. The RBV administers the exchange-control framework through the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 92] and supervises all licensed commercial banks, foreign exchange dealers, and money remitters operating in Vanuatu, while the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) separately regulates the International Company registry.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital should be routed as a SWIFT MT103 wire credit or Wise_B2B transfer to the beneficiary Vanuatu bank, using ISO 20022 purpose category CAPC for capital payments and the free-text narrative 'Share capital contribution to Vanuatu International Company'. Funds are accepted in USD or other foreign currency and are credited after the bank completes source-of-funds verification. First-time inbound transactions above VUV 1,000,000 are subject to enhanced transaction monitoring and a standard one-to-three business-day verification hold. The receiving bank requires the share subscription agreement, a board resolution evidencing the allotment, and a remitting-bank reference before releasing funds for discretionary use.Primary clearing rails for outbound trade settlements are SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers routed through the correspondent network of ANZ, BSP, or Bred, and the Wise_B2B rail for sub-USD 50,000 disbursements to non-bank vendors. USD outflows clear through the New York correspondent channel, while AUD/EUR outflows clear through Sydney or European correspondent banks. Commercial Letters of Credit are permissible but unusual for animation services; for larger milestone-based production projects, an unconfirmed or confirmed LC may be issued to a visual effects subcontractor. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in VUV – Vanuatu has no statutory transfer pricing regime, no Local File or Master File obligation, and no VUV-denominated documentation threshold. Nevertheless, if the IC is inserted into an international group, foreign parent and subsidiary tax authorities may require OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation under CbC or local country rules; a prudent IC should prepare contemporaneous TP documentation for related-party animation services, licensing of IP, cost-sharing, and management fee flows.

Economic substance scope: Vanuatu does not currently impose ESR-reviewed Economic Substance Regulations on a 3D animation IC as a domestic tax filing condition. However, the IC must still demonstrate genuine economic substance for anti-avoidance, anti-money laundering, and global CFC/GAAR purposes. Recommended commercial substance includes a physical or serviced office, documented decision-making for production contracts, real staffing or subcontracted animation capacity, complete accounting records, operating bank accounts, and actual expenditures attributable to the animation business. An offshore invoicing-only shell with no operational footprint is vulnerable to challenge by foreign taxing authorities and by VFSC under the IC Act's fit-and-proper standards.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Representation

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Vanuatu's criminal and AML/CFT laws, including fines, confiscation of all assets derived from the offending invoices, and imprisonment. The IC may be struck off; foreign tax authorities may impose unpaid tax, interest, and evasion penalties up to 100% or more of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who sign false declarations or order the evasion scheme are personally liable for civil restitution, subject to travel bans and disqualification, and may be imprisoned together with the beneficial owner.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: VFSC may refuse renewal, impose administrative penalties, and strike the company off the register. Foreign courts may pierce the corporate veil, recharacterize the IC as an agent or conduit, and assess tax, penalties, and interest against the parent or beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for fraudulent or reckless trading, disqualified from managing ICs, and restrained from leaving Vanuatu while an investigation is pending.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Monetary administrative penalties under Vanuatu AML/CFT regulations, freezing orders, suspension of the registered agent, and criminal penalties for money laundering if the animation revenues are the proceeds of predicate offences.

Director personal liability: Company officers and compliance officers face criminal fines, imprisonment, personal confiscation orders, and prohibition from acting as directors of any Vanuatu regulated entity.
Invalid Domestic Business Activity / IC Prohibited Activities

Statutory sanction: Contracts made in breach of the IC Act may be void or unenforceable; the IC is liable to penalties, deregistration, and the loss of its tax-exempt offshore status; taxes may become payable under ordinary Vanuatu domestic law.

Director personal liability: Directors authorizing prohibited domestic business may be jointly and severally liable for the company's obligations arising from that business, and may be prosecuted for breach of the IC Act.
VAT Fraud and Incorrect Invoicing

Statutory sanction: VAT evasion assessments, penalty tax up to twice the evaded VAT, criminal fines, and restriction orders; the tax authority may also audit related companies and impose joint assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in the preparation or approval of false invoices face personal criminal liability, civil penalties, and disqualification orders; foreign tax authorities may additionally recover withholding and profit taxes from the directors as responsible persons.
Transfer Pricing and Cross-Border Anti-Abuse

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authorities may impose transfer pricing adjustments, secondary adjustments, withholding tax recharacterization, interest, and penalties up to 100% of the adjustment; Vanuatu itself has no penalty, but the adjustment may expose the IC to anti-money laundering scrutiny if artificial round-tripping is used.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or implement non-arm's length agreements may be personally assessed under foreign director-liability and fraudulent conveyance rules; travel bans may issue in serious cross-border tax fraud cases.
