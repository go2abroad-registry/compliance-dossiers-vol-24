Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Vanuatu has no check-the-box, entity-classification, or OECD-style hybrid mismatch election regime. The IC exemption under s.38 of the International Companies Act [Cap 222] is statutory, automatic, and non-waivable. Available elections relevant to the tax identity: (i) voluntary VAT registration under s.4 of the VAT Act 2021 for a person carrying on a taxable activity, permitting input VAT recovery; (ii) election in the company constitution for no-par-value share capital; (iii) election to operate as an International Company versus a domestic company — a domestic election triggers full Vanuatu taxation and is practically irreversible without dissolution; (iv) for multi-country groups, Vanuatu does not attribute deemed permanent establishments to ICs, and no hybrid mismatch adjustment is available or required. Elections are effected by written notice to VRCD or by constitutional amendment filed with VFSC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VT 10,000,000 (ten million vatu) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period for mandatory registration; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. The exempt International Company does not count its offshore export sales toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the zero-rating provisions of the VAT Act 2021, a supply of goods by a registered supplier is zero-rated where the supplier exports the goods from Vanuatu within 30 days of the date of supply (or such extended period as the Commissioner allows) and retains evidence of export. Zero-rating also covers services supplied to a non-resident who is outside Vanuatu at the time of supply, where the services are not supplied to a Vanuatu-resident recipient and the benefit is consumed offshore. For the ISIC 3250 sterile examination nitrile glove project, the International Company's offshore sales fall entirely outside the Vanuatu VAT net by reason of the s.38 IC Act exemption; if a domestic VAT-registered manufacturer were used, the same export invoices would be zero-rated and input VAT on production inputs would be refundable through the VAT return within the statutory processing period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CusDec) lodged in ASYCUDA World at the port of exit
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / TT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • VAT zero-rated tax invoice carrying the prescribed zero-rating notation
  • Commercial invoice matched to the tax invoice
  • Packing list
  • Certificate of Origin (where tariff preference is claimed in the destination market)
  • Sterility and quality certification (ISO 13485 / EN 455) for the medical glove shipment

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate exists in Vanuatu as of 2025. VAT-registered suppliers must issue a standard electronic or paper tax invoice with prescribed particulars: supplier name, address, and VAT registration number; recipient name and address; invoice date; description; quantity; unit price; VAT amount; and the notation 'zero-rated' for exported goods. Invoices and source documents must be retained for at least five years and produced on VRCD audit demand. Customs import-export declarations are lodged electronically through ASYCUDA World, which is not a fiscal invoice clearance platform.

VRCD may audit and re-characterise zero-rated exports within five years of the relevant return period. Failure to export within 30 days, or absence of the CusDec, transport, and payment evidence, converts the supply to a taxable supply at 12.5% VAT, plus statutory interest at 20% per annum on any shortfall and monetary penalties determined by the Commissioner; wilful evasion attracts criminal fines and imprisonment under the VAT Act 2021 and the Penal Code. Vanuatu imposes no exchange-control or statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark above is the trade-finance due-diligence standard applied by correspondent banks to Swift_LC/TT_Wire settlement and by VRCD in substance-over-form export audits — unexplained exceedance may trigger clawback of the zero-rating claim as trade-based money-laundering. For the International Company, breach of the IC Act restrictions (trading with Vanuatu residents, holding freehold land, or unlicensed banking/trust/insurance activity) voids the s.38 exemption, exposing the company to clawback of exempt status, VFSC penalties up to VT 5,000,000, strike-off, and officer and registered agent liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Vatu (VUV) is a managed float against a trade-weighted basket, freely usable for non-residents. Section 37 of the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] statutorily exempts an International Company (IC) and all its transactions from every exchange control provision. Consequently, current account trade settlements, capital account injections, dividend distributions, and full liquidation repatriation are freely convertible into USD, AUD, NZD, or EUR without prior RBV approval. The effective practical barrier is not convertibility but correspondent-bank de-risking: USD clearing is executed through offshore correspondents (ANZ Melbourne, BRED Paris/Port Vila treasury links), and each conversion requires the bank's internal FX spread and an underlying commercial or capital document. No profits accrual or deferral restrictions exist for the non-resident IC, but the bank will typically require audited or management-prepared financial statements evidencing arm's length earnings before processing large outward remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Vanuatu resident exporters, the Exchange Control (Authorized Dealers) Regulations impose a statutory timeline: export proceeds must be received within 180 days from the bill of lading date, and 100% of those proceeds must be surrendered to an authorized dealer in VUV within 30 days of receipt. An International Company, however, is classified as non-resident for exchange control purposes under Section 37 of the International Companies Act [Cap. 222] and is entirely exempt from the surrender timeline and from mandatory conversion into VUV. The IC should still maintain the export evidence trail — outward customs entry, commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and, where applicable, a Ministry of Health or Department of Customs permit — to satisfy bank and correspondent anti-money-laundering (AML) due diligence, even though no statutory conversion into VUV applies.

Central bank supervision: Reserve Bank of Vanuatu (RBV) — sole central bank and exchange control administrator under the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu Act 1981 [Cap. 125] and the Exchange Control Act [Cap. 126]; the Vanuatu Financial Services Commission (VFSC) separately regulates International Company incorporation, bank licensing under the Banks Act [Cap. 64], and offshore banking under the International Banking Act 2005.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Vanuatu has no domestic Electronic Money Institution (EMI) prudential licence; only banks chartered under the Banks Act [Cap. 64] and international banks licensed under the International Banking Act 2005 may lawfully hold client funds or issue VUV payment instruments. Foreign-headquartered digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex) are not RBV-authorized and may not serve as the IC's primary liquidity or escrow holder. Their appropriate role is limited to ancillary collection gateways and FX-efficient low-value vendor payments, always backed by a settlement account at an RBV-licensed bank. The IC must ensure the foreign EMI is itself regulated in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction, carries no sanctioned counterparty, and is not used to transit medical-device receivables from institutional buyers, as that would trigger correspondent-bank de-risking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses a SWIFT MT103 from the investor's account to the IC's account at an RBV-licensed authorised dealer, with the 28-character narrative stating 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION — IC ACT CAP 222' and a value date matching the board resolution. The receiving bank requires a board resolution approving the share issuance, an updated Share Register, the VFSC annual return listing the shareholder, and source-of-wealth evidence (bank statement, sale agreement, or audited financial statements). For transfers exceeding USD 50,000, the sending bank must use IMF BPM6 classification code E1 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital — Shares in International Company' in the MT103 purpose field, and the Vanuatu bank files a Cross-Border Transaction Report with RBV/VRU. Because Section 37 of the International Companies Act exempts the IC from exchange control, no prior RBV approval is required, but the bank may demand a non-resident status confirmation letter issued by the VFSC-licensed registered agent.Trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers to verified supplier accounts — the dominant rail for raw nitrile latex, accelerators, packaging, and freight — and SWIFT MT700/MT707 for commercial documentary Letters of Credit on high-value OEM or medical tender orders. For Chinese and Southeast Asian suppliers, an MT700 LC with tolerance and transhipment clauses is preferred; for US/EU procurement, MT103 with 1–3 day value dating reduces cost. Each disbursement instruction must pass a three-way match against purchase order, supplier invoice, and goods-receipt note, plus OFAC/RBV sanctions screening; the MT103 narrative must identify the purchase order and supply contract. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None – Vanuatu has no corporate income tax and no codified transfer pricing law; therefore no Local File or Master File threshold applies (VUV 0). Nevertheless, under the ESR, an IC that operates as the principal in the nitrile glove supply chain and earns 'relevant activity' income must maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation evidencing arm's length pricing, even though no tax return is filed.

Economic substance scope: The International Companies (Economic Substance) Act (as amended) adopts OECD/Inclusive Framework standards. A Vanuatu IC that concludes offshore purchase and resale contracts for sterile nitrile gloves – while manufacturing is tolled by a third-party factory in Malaysia or China – is treated as carrying on the 'distribution and service centre' or 'headquarters' relevant activity. The IC must prove: (i) board meetings held at quorum in Vanuatu; (ii) local employees commensurate with the volume of trade; (iii) adequate physical premises; (iv) key decision-making actually performed in Vanuatu; and (v) a valid CIGA analysis retained. Failure is a substance offence punishable by VFSC penalty and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance Evasion (Shell Company)

Statutory sanction: VFSC penalty of up to VT 2,000,000, compliance directions, daily default penalties of VT 50,000, court-ordered strike-off and winding up, and automatic loss of the tax exemption.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident registered agent are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution and penalties; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezing orders, disqualification for up to 5 years, and imprisonment of up to 2 years for false substance declarations.
Tax Evasion by Certificate Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Retroactive revocation of the exemption, voidance of the IC's international status, strike-off by the Registrar, financial penalty up to VT 3,000,000, and restitution of any fees or economic benefit derived from the misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false declarations face personal liability for the company's liabilities without the cap of limited liability, court-ordered restitution with statutory interest, disqualification from managing any corporate entity for 5 years, and criminal prosecution for perjury leading to imprisonment.
AML/CFT Violation and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the AMLCFT Act and amendments, fines up to VT 100,000,000 or 15 years' imprisonment, confiscation of the trade proceeds and bank accounts, suspension of the registered agent's licence, and red-flag referral to the Vanuatu FIU.

Director personal liability: Directors, company secretaries, and compliance officers are criminally liable on a personal basis; courts impose asset freezing, travel bans, extradition cooperation, and confiscation orders spanning shell entities. Foreign tax evasion underlying the laundered funds is an indictable predicate offence.
Breach of IC Domestic Restrictions

Statutory sanction: Transactions in breach are voidable at the counterparty's election; the IC forfeits its exempt status, is liable to a penalty not exceeding VT 1,000,000, is struck off the International Companies Register, and all contracts become unenforceable in Vanuatu courts.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed the offending contracts become personally liable for all debts arising from those transactions without limited liability protection; the Court may impose a travel ban, restitution, and disqualification, and the company's veil is pierced as a matter of statute.
